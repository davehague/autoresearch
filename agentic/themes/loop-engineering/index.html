Loop engineering — map

Coined 2026-07-07 during the nightly radar — first evidence in the previously-empty "Loop engineering" group (the standing priority gap in vault/index.md). Moved to consolidating 2026-07-08 on cross-org production replication (OpenAI Symphony). Downgraded to contested 2026-07-10: a T1 controlled scaling study (Google Research + DeepMind, 260 configurations) finds multi-agent coordination degrades sequential and tool-heavy tasks by 39–70% and saturates once the single-agent baseline passes ~45% — the map's headline generalized from decomposable benchmarks to "long-horizon work," and those are not the same thing. Status moves both ways. Flagged for David's review. 2026-07-12: a second fleet-side T1 (DeLM) refines the substrate tension — the operative variable looks like unit size × validation × routing, not shared-vs-private; stays contested. 2026-07-14: a paired noise-floor protocol adds a measurement precondition to the decisive experiment — architecture deltas of the magnitude this map argues over may not be separable from run-to-run variance unless a same-model paired floor is measured first. No evidence here is refuted; the instrument every side reads its evidence with is now in question. Stays contested. 2026-07-22: a second limb opens — everything above is coordination between agents; VRR-Stop measures the within-agent verify-repair loop and finds it destroying correct work under a weak verifier (validity peaking at round 2, then collapsing below the no-repair baseline in six settings of eight). It confirms the map's central variable (validation quality, not iteration) from a new direction rather than contesting anything, so the status is unchanged. 2026-07-24: the within-agent limb takes two more T1 arrivals and widens. Binding drift shows the same lesson holding for persisted state rather than rewritten candidates — the intuitive "resolve once, carry forward" fix eliminates drift and multiplies wrong actions 3.0× — and supplies the limb's first controlled measurement of who should check: a cheap second model re-deriving from the original request beats the same model checking itself, at near-oracle recovery. CodeRescue adds that the repair action every harness ships as its default is the weakest of three post-failure options. All three extend; nothing here touches the coordination contest, so the status is unchanged. 2026-07-25: the coordination limb gains its largest production instance and the within-agent limb gains an installability axis — and the status still holds. Anthropic's migration practice runs hundreds of agents over 1M LOC on a substrate whose admission check is the compiler and the test suite and whose queue is derived from disk state, with no orchestrator routing peer discoveries — the purest case yet of hypothesis (A)'s validated arm, and the first where verification costs no model call at all. It is T2 practice with no control arm on a task class that hands you three oracles for free, so it tilts (A) rather than settling the contest. Separately, a CUA repair study finds that how installable a correction is tracks how constrained the action is (a fixed stop token at 0.97, open-ended corrections 0.14–0.71 with seed spreads wider than their effects) and that a verified frame-level repair reaches task success only when the corrected step is the sole remaining blocker (8/20 vs 0/15; 0/5 vs 0/5 when it is one of several). Both extend; neither touches the coordination question.

Status: contested

What we believe now:

  • "Independent verifier" names four different properties, and the map's conflicting results stop conflicting once they are separated. (Named 2026-07-29 by the monthly connect sweep — no new source; a reconciliation of claims already carried across this map and agentic-code-review.) The map holds that a cheap call to a different model re-deriving the answer recovers substantially more injected error than self-checking (an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself) — and simultaneously that a cross-family verifier was the worst of three (verifier-diversity-is-not-verifier-independence), that a narration-blind verifier still passed 68 of 76 wrong answers (a-verifier-sharing-the-interpretation-shares-the-error), and that a weak independent reviewer on a strong draft is a significant net loss (reviewer-value-tracks-capability-headroom). Four axes: independent weights (a different model), independent narration (inspecting the artifact, not the agent's account of it), independent errors (statistically uncorrelated failures — measurable only as error correlation at your acceptance operating point, never inferable from a vendor diff), and independent capability (enough headroom to add anything). The discriminator the results imply: what a cheap different model buys is re-derivation from an external anchor, not independence — which is why a much smaller model suffices where an anchor exists (the original instruction, a test suite, program state) and fails where the verifier must supply judgment with nothing to re-derive from, and there headroom sets the sign. Practical rule: anchor present → buy cheap independent weights; anchor absent → you are buying capability, and a weaker reviewer is negative value. Stated here for the first time and untested as a discriminator; it does not resolve the map's contested status, it localizes what would. [No new evidence; reconciliation of 4 existing T1 claims]
  • The within-agent limb gains a second failure that a good verifier does not fix: the loop finds the correct patch and then destroys it, and the metric everyone reports cannot see that happening. (Added 2026-07-29, T1, strong.) Over 900 trajectories / 2,700 revisions under forced revision, ever-correct rises 0.820 → 0.847 → 0.853 while current correctness falls 0.820 → 0.673 and recovers only to 0.693 — at revision 3, 16.0% of trajectories had produced a correct patch and lost it. Pass@k and best-of-k report the first curve. Depth is not the variable: the shape is evidence-specific (current-binary 0.700/0.700/0.720, stale-trace 0.687/0.713/0.660, none 0.687/0.687/0.707), and "loop depth changes the number of transition opportunities; the transition kernel determines their sign." The kernel makes the stopping rule explicit — with α = P(wrong→right) and β = P(right→wrong), continuation has negative expected value once q > α/(α+β), so the licence for another round is a property of your current state, not your budget — which is the same shape as the map's 2026-07-22 marginal-gain rule arrived at from retention rather than from validity (stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain, repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum). The dominant identified mechanism is evidence whose payload is true and whose envelope is stale: a report generated from the previous revision reverts a fixed Caesar shift in 5/5 seeds, and under a common-state design that freezes one wrong and one correct program before assigning conditions — removing post-treatment risk-set bias over 2,430 branches — stale traces harm 34/135 correct starts against 4/135 for current ones (+22.2 points, task-cluster 95% CI [8.9, 37.0], exact Holm p = 0.0337). Harm is clustered, not diffuse: 4 of 27 tasks at 7B, 12 of 27 at 14B. The fix is a hash: carry evidence as (source-hash, suite-hash, payload) and let it drive an edit only on a match (bind-evidence-to-the-state-it-describes). Read with 2026-07-24's binding-drift result, the operative variable is the same one context-engineering named for files — pinned versus re-derived — now applied to verdicts. Read the remedies honestly: state binding intercepts every stale-condition harm in replay (19 → 2 and 69 → 33 total harm with typed actions), and prospectively the same bundle eliminates correct-start harm (13/135 → 0/135) while costing seven repairs and failing its pre-registered joint criterion; the repository arm removes every unsafe completion and the only sound one. Safety and liveness trade, and the authors claim an auditable specification of obligations, not a net win. One free win: all five 7B current-trace harms on one task were replies containing only STATUS: DONE and no code, so a fail-closed parser mapping an unparseable action to "keep" retains the checkpoint in every recorded branch — a harness edit, not a model edit. [T1, strong for non-absorption and the stale-evidence mechanism; suggestive for the verifier ordering. Bounds: HumanEval function repair plausibly in training data, 27 tasks selected for observed repairability, and only one of six pre-registered contrasts survives Holm correction — the cross-coder interaction does not, so no capacity claim is supported; repository arms are floor-limited at 0–2.1% baseline sound completion across four stacks]
  • Isolate the routing term and it dominates: with workers held perfectly constant, orchestration quality is whether the plan declared the handoff, not how many agents it used. (Added 2026-07-29, T1, strong inside the simulator.) Every prior data point in this limb comes from end-to-end runs that mix plan quality with worker capability, tool flakiness and environment noise. Here the planner emits only an assignment plus an explicit information transfer per cross-agent edge, and a deterministic simulator executes it — same plan, same result, every time. Across 240 DAGs at 10–100 subtasks, agent count's correlation with quality falls to −0.021 at n = 100 while transfer coverage holds 0.614–0.952 at every scale, and average missing transfers per plan widen from a [0.00, 0.43] range across models to [0.07, 22.70] (transfer-coverage-beats-agent-count-in-orchestration). At extreme scale the behaviour is divergence, not degradation: from 500 to 1,000 subtasks two frontier models fall from 0.981 transfer coverage to 0.441 and 0.398 — ~900 missing transfers each — while a third holds complete coverage, and no model leads at every scale. This isolates the routing term of the map's unit-size × validation × routing hypothesis (verified-shared-state-beats-orchestrator-routing) and supplies a mechanism for the 2026-07-10 saturation result (coordination-saturates-as-the-single-agent-improves): raising the agent cap from 16 to 64 more than doubles the agents and leaves the score flat, because each added agent adds cross-agent edges, and each edge is another handoff that can go undeclared. Parallelism saturates at the rate coordination failures accumulate, not at the rate tasks run out of independence. And at 1.3% of the tokens and 10.3% of the wall-clock of real execution, correlating with real Claude Code quality at r = 0.816, this is the first affordable way to run the map's still-unrun three-armed raw-pool / event-passing / verified-gist sweep with σ_eval zero by construction — the strongest form of the frozen-shared-state discipline the map adopted on 2026-07-28 (simulate-the-plan-to-price-a-coordination-question). [T1, strong for the internal contrasts, suggestive for sim-to-real. Bounds that matter: no worker ever runs, so nothing here says a fleet beats a single agent; the sim-to-real correlation rests on six model-level points and one leave-one-out configuration collapses it to r = 0.421, p = 0.50; and the authors' own ablation shows the benchmark's discriminative power is almost entirely the missing-transfer penalty (a 0.247 quality gap becomes 0.011 without it), so this measures declared information routing under an assumed loss model and very little else. Task decomposition is a fixed input, and 34.3% of sampled generated DAGs were rejected by a judge on semantic faithfulness]
  • A swarm's coordination cost is set by unit size, admission validation and routing — and one vendor has now moved all three at once on a fixed task, with the telemetry the hypothesis predicts. (Added 2026-07-26, T2, suggestive.) Hundreds of agents implementing SQLite from the 835-page manual, old substrate versus new. The old one is hypothesis (A)'s worst case built out: finest possible unit (a git commit), no admission check, no hierarchy — 68,000 commits in two hours, 70,000+ merge conflicts and accelerating, one file contested by 1,173 agents, and three separate SQL implementations inside 54 crates. The new one coarsens the unit (~1,000 commits in the same window, ~68× fewer), adds an admission check that costs no model call (shared design docs whose references the compiler validates, so contention surfaces as a build error), and adds routing (planner agents hold the task tree, worker agents hold one subtask). Four-hour score 73–85% against 11–77%; every new configuration eventually passed 100% of the suite at roughly one seventh the code (4,645 lines at 100% vs 19,013 at 97%). Two mechanisms sit outside the hypothesis's vocabulary and should be named: neutral third-party agents resolve merge collisions, and a stigmergic field guide the agents curate for their successors, injected at startup (agents-curate-a-field-guide-for-the-agents-behind-them). This is the second production substrate in six days whose validation is a compiler rather than a model call, and it tilts (A) rather than testing it — five mechanisms changed together, no arm isolates any of them, no paired floor, and the losing arm is a system its own authors had already decided to replace (hierarchical-swarm-beats-flat-swarm-on-the-same-task). [T2, suggestive — vendor's own product, single task, no ablation, no repeated runs]
  • When a mistake recurs, repair the generator, not the artifact — and buy your validation from a compiler wherever one exists. (2026-07-25, T2 practice.) Two completed migrations at a scale nothing else here touches — 1,000,000 LOC Zig→Rust in under two weeks (19 post-merge regressions, ≈$165k in tokens) and 165,000 lines Python→TypeScript in one weekend across hundreds of agents and 8 phase gates — run on one stated principle: you fix the process that produced the code. Four mechanisms carry it: a work queue derived from disk state with "done" = the output file exists (build-the-work-queue-from-disk-state); compiler errors, test failures and behaviour diffs as the only ground truth, never an agent's report; adversarial reviewers in separate contexts with a third agent breaking ties; and rulebook-driven regeneration — on a recurring error, amend the rule and regenerate the batch. The last of those is a third answer to the map's own repair question, alongside iterate (repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum) and escalate (recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder): move the repair up a level. And the routing is inverted from instinct — small models implement in parallel, the largest models review and write the rules. Load-bearing caveat: no control arm, and a language port supplies a total type checker, a working reference implementation, and a human-written suite that ordinary feature work does not have (fix-the-loop-not-the-artifact). [T2, suggestive — two case studies, no counterfactual]
  • How installable a correction is depends on how constrained it is, and a correct repair buys nothing unless the defect it fixes is the binding constraint. (2026-07-25.) On held-out frames against a ≈0 base, a single fixed stop token installs at 0.97 ± 0.06 while open-ended corrections do not — spatial coordinates 0.53–0.71 at ±0.26–0.35 (one seed at 0.00), a generative fill 0.14 ± 0.04 — and it is not the base model's prior doing the work: the most installable correction has by far the lowest base log-prob. Ordering replicates on a second architecture. The transfer boundary is the operational half: the repair reaches oracle-graded task success only where the corrected action is the sole remaining blocker (8/20 vs 0/15, p = 0.006) and is a flat null where it is one of several steps (0/5 vs 0/5). So the licence for a repair loop narrows twice — the verifier must discriminate and the defect must be binding — which no default retry policy checks. This is also why stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain is the reachable intervention: stopping is a single discrete token, the one correction that installs reliably (repair-installs-only-constrained-actions). [T3, single-study — computer-use agents, not coding; carried for structure, not magnitudes]
  • The iteration loop has an interior optimum, and the verifier's discrimination decides where it sits — below a threshold the loop is worth less than not looping at all. Everything else in this map is coordination between agents; this is the loop inside one. When verifier and repairer are both noisy, true validity peaks at a small number of rounds (K* = 2 in the measured stress setting) and then declines while verifier acceptance keeps rising: 55% of correct plans get repaired into incorrect ones and 24% of those damaging repairs win majority acceptance. An independent-resampling control at the same budget shows no such decline, so the damage is path-dependent — from rewriting the same candidate — not sampling noise. A stronger verifier reduces but does not remove it (with a process-reward-model verifier at J = 0.805, five fixed rounds still drive validity 0.727 → 0.097). The fix is to stop on the sign of the marginal gain rather than on acceptance votes: +60.6 pp of final validity at 0.72 rounds instead of 5, slightly beating a policy given the ground-truth parameters — so the fixed round budget in every harness is a category error, not a tuning error. This is the same lesson as the coordination limb (the coordinator's value is validation, not scheduling) restated for a single agent: an unvalidated loop is negative value. Note the counterweight the map already holds — a reviewer scored against executable tests does lift a weak generator (review-revise-loop-lifts-resolve-most-on-weak-generators) — so the operative rule is measure your verifier's discrimination before granting the loop a budget, and stop early when you cannot. [T1, suggestive — small open-weight models, adversarial stress setting]
  • The same lesson holds for state the loop carries as for candidates the loop rewrites: persistence is not validation, and removing variance is not removing error. (2026-07-24.) A multi-step agent resolves an entity once and then acts on it through steps that only say "it." The obvious defense — pin the first binding and reuse it — is measured on disjoint workflow sets so the trade cannot hide inside one task-success number, and it eliminates drift to 0% while amplifying wrong actions from 907 to 2,746 (3.0×, bootstrap CI [2.8, 3.3]) under controlled error injection, taking strict workflow success from 29.2% to 0.0%. Two structural details transfer better than the ratio. The amplification rises with model capability — 1.8× on the smallest backend to 8.5× on Claude Opus 4.5 — the plausible mechanism being that stronger models treat carried workflow state as authoritative, which is the exact property that becomes a liability when the state is wrong. And the dependent-chain pattern amplifies 16.6× because the failure becomes self-consistent: mis-resolve the account, and "that account's owner" returns a perfectly coherent wrong person with no local signal that anything is off. Naturally, without injection, agents drift on 18% of eligible workflows and the per-step wrong-entity rate rises ~8% → 28% by action step 3. The rule for any harness holding session state: carry it, and re-derive it before anything irreversible (persisted-state-without-revalidation-amplifies-errors). [T1, suggestive — synthetic entity stores, 3–8 step workflows]
  • Who checks is an active ingredient with a price tag, and the price is one cheap call. (2026-07-24 — the third arrival in nine days, by three methods on three objects.) Same protocol, same instruction, same correction rule, only the verifier's identity varied: self-verification recovers 71% of injected errors (0.29×), a cross-model verifier recovers 79% (0.21×, CI [0.18, 0.25]), and a ground-truth oracle 80% (0.20×) — the practical cross-verifier landing within a point of the oracle on all eight backends. The winning verifier is smaller than what it checks, because the job is not to be smarter but to re-derive the target from the original request without inheriting the acting model's committed state; self-reflection cannot detect a reference error, since internal state is entirely consistent. This composes with the map's own stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain (stopping reliability governed by verifier discrimination, not by the repairer) and with structure-outweighs-the-verification-step (moving observe/compare onto the generating model cut rescues 6 → 2). The honest cost is over-clarification — clarifying steps roughly triple and strict success dips 29.2% → 25.2% — so the accounting turns on reversibility (an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself). [T1, suggestive]
  • After a failure, "repair it" is the weakest thing you can do, and a third of failures are out of reach of any in-loop action. (2026-07-24.) Given execution feedback, three recovery actions — reflect (repair from the feedback), replan (fresh cheap attempt), escalate (stronger model) — solve 27.5% / 45.3% / 68.6% as fixed policies. Reflect, the default every coding harness ships as "iterate on the error," is the worst, below naive discard-and-retry. The actions are also not a ladder: 28% of recoverable failures are cheap-only, 45% escalation-only, 27% both. And roughly 30% of attempted problems fail initially and are solved by none of the three — a ceiling on what any within-agent loop can reach, however well validated. Primary map: model-routing-token-economics; see recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder. [T1, suggestive — competitive-programming and function-level benchmarks, not repo-scale SWE]
  • The coordination tax may really be a substrate tax — and as of 2026-07-12 the substrate variable has three components: unit size × validation × routing, not shared-vs-private. Two T1 counterexamples now beat strong single agents on SWE tasks with different substrates: icat-agent (2026-07-11) coordinates via event-based message passing, no shared pool, from a 59.1% baseline, and wins by 8.3 points on SWE-bench Pro at lower cost; DeLM (2026-07-12) coordinates via a shared context — but one holding only verified, ~100-token gists, written directly without an orchestrator router — and wins by up to 10.5 points on SWE-bench Verified at half the cost (65.7% vs 56.4%/54.7%, $0.12 vs $0.25). What the degrading systems in the scaling sweep share is neither topology nor shared-ness: it is raw, unvalidated state moving through a serializing controller. DeLM's ablation puts a number on the discriminating property — remove admission-time verification and it drops 4.9 points. icat-agent's messages and DeLM's gists are near-duals: small, validated units of state transfer. Three T1s, reconcilable under one variable nobody has manipulated directly — the map stays contested, but the question is now sharp enough to test. [T1 vs T1]
  • The criterion is decomposability, not horizon length — and the two come apart exactly in software engineering. A controlled sweep (260 configurations, six agentic benchmarks, five architectures, three LLM families, standardized tools/prompts/token budgets) finds coordination's sign flips with task shape: +80.9% for centralized coordination over a single agent on decomposable financial reasoning, and −39.0% to −70.0% for every multi-agent topology on strict-ordering planning. Tool-heavy work is penalized further — the paper's own worst case is a 16-tool software-engineering task, and the efficiency–tools interaction (β = −0.330, p < 0.001) is the dominant inhibitor in its scaling model. Long-horizon SWE work is simultaneously sequential, dependency-ordered, and tool-dense. [T1]
  • The multi-agent premium is largest where the base model is weakest, and it inverts. Coordination yields "diminishing or negative returns once single-agent baselines exceed an empirical threshold of ∼45%." A fleet architecture validated against last year's model can be negative value against this year's — the loop layer goes stale the way the harness does (harness-model-assumptions-go-stale). [T1]
  • When you do coordinate, topology decides how badly errors compound. Independent agents amplify errors 17.2× through unchecked propagation; a validating central orchestrator contains it to 4.4×. The coordinator's value is validation, not scheduling — a dispatcher that merges without checking is buying the 17.2× number. This is the quantitative case for the stitch step below, and it sharpens it: the reconcile step must verify, not merely merge. [T1]
  • Where a fleet is warranted, the architecture is settled and the load-bearing detail is the stitch across the partial-observability boundary: each isolated worker sees only its slice, so their work must be reconciled explicitly. Three variants attested — the manager forwards each node's distilled result to dependents (MACU), workers reconcile through git branch-and-merge in isolated worktrees (CAID: +26.7% PaperBench, +14.3% Commit0), or a deterministic orchestrator daemon rides the existing ticket-state + git/CI substrate (Symphony). Get the reconcile step wrong and isolated agents clobber each other. [T1]
  • The constraint a ticket-level fleet removes is human attention, not model capability: interactive supervision caps at ~3–5 concurrent sessions per engineer; making the issue tracker the control plane moves humans to reviewing results. Production evidence is three-org — OpenAI (landed PRs +500% on some teams), Cursor (40%+ of internal PRs from cloud agents at 50M+ actions/day). Note what this evidence is: an output count, not a quality measure. [T2]
  • Constrain the loop's scheduling and isolation, not the worker's procedure: rigid state-machine roles under-use the model — give workers ticket-level objectives plus tools and let them choose steps. Cross-org (Symphony; Cursor's progressive-autonomy arc). [T2, cross-org]

The live tension (what "contested" means here): There are now two candidate reconciliations, and as of 2026-07-11 the second one has displaced the first as the more promising. The vault should not pretend the conflict is resolved in either direction.

(A) The substrate hypothesis (2026-07-11; REFINED 2026-07-12 — the sharper one). The scaling study's degradation may not be a tax on coordination but on the substrate. First cut (icat-agent): the tax is shared context — messages carry what a peer needs and nothing else leaks. Second cut (DeLM): that can't be the whole story, because a shared substrate wins too, provided what's shared is verified at admission, compact (~100-token gists), and written directly rather than routed through a serializing orchestrator — and its ablation shows verification alone is worth 4.9 points. The refined hypothesis: the tax is raw, unvalidated state moving through a serializing router, and both event-passing and verified-gist blackboards avoid it. This retro-explains the sweep's own internal gradient (its worst performer never validates the pool; 17.2× vs 4.4× error amplification says the orchestrator's value was validation, not scheduling) and why git branch-and-merge worked so well for CAID (isolate-and-merge-parallel-agent-work): a worktree is event-passing with a filesystem for a mailbox, and the merge gate is the validation. The decisive test is cheap and unrun: re-run the sweep with a raw-shared-pool arm, an event-passing arm, and a verified-gist arm, everything else fixed. Prediction if (A) holds: the last two tie, and both beat the first.

(B) The regime hypothesis (2026-07-10, still live but weaker). Intra-task coordination — sub-agents splitting one problem (MACU, CAID) — is what the Google sweep measures. Inter-ticket fleets — one whole agent per independent ticket (Symphony, Cursor) — parallelize across units a human already decomposed, which under the scaling study's own theory is the decomposable regime where coordination should help. This is a rescue that saves the production numbers without explaining icat-agent, which is intra-task, SWE-shaped, and wins anyway. (A) explains more of the evidence with fewer moving parts.

If (A) holds, the map's advice changes concretely and for the better: coordinate freely, but never through a shared window. If (B) holds and (A) fails, fleets pay off because a human did the decomposition, not because agents coordinate well. If neither holds, the +500% and 40% numbers are throughput on work a single strong agent would have done better serially — unchecked, because the production evidence has no quality criterion behind it (evaluation-integrity).

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-07-30 — blind-resampling-placebo-controlled-retry (Yuvraj Verma, independent, arXiv, "Try Again, Don't Look Back: Blind Resampling Outperforms Self-Repair in Small Code Models", 2026-07-28, T1, strong at ≤7B): the within-agent limb gains a placebo arm, and it lands upstream of everything the limb has measured so far. Every prior result here concerns what the loop does with the candidate — validity peaking at round 2 under a noisy verifier (repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum), the stopping rule that fixes it (stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain), the loop destroying correct work it already had (report-current-correctness-not-only-ever-correct). This asks whether the loop's input carries value at all. Four conditions at k_max = 8 on MBPP+ (378 tasks) with Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B/3B/7B: blind resample (the failed attempt is never shown), placebo (the failed program plus a content-free failure notice), feedback (plus real test output), reflect (plus a prose diagnosis). The informational payload of execution feedback — feedback minus placebo — is +0.000 / +0.026 / +0.008, none significant at any scale. Meanwhile merely seeing your own failed attempt costs −0.061 pass@1 at 1.5B (p = 0.006) and −0.069 at 3B (p < 0.001). Blind resampling wins outright at 1.5B and 3B and ties at 7B, at 2.5–5.5× fewer tokens. The mechanism is measured, not inferred: with its own output in view the model regenerates a near-identical program (similarity > 0.95) in 33–68% of retries against 2–14% blind, and the size of the penalty is predicted by baseline pass@1 alone at r = 0.96 — so it is the cost of committing to a bad first attempt, and it shrinks as first attempts get good (showing-a-failed-attempt-anchors-the-retry). Two consequences for this map. The limb's rule has been measure your verifier's discrimination before granting the loop a budget; it now also reads measure whether your feedback beats a blank retry, because the extra sample may be the whole effect. And it supplies the mechanism for recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder's ranking — the default post-failure action every harness ships was the worst of three at 27.5%, and what distinguishes it is precisely the self-exposure measured here; the cheap harness change is to make discard-and-resample an available recovery action. Caveats, and they bound this tightly: 1.5–7B open-weight models of one family, quantized, function-level synthesis on one public benchmark family, single unfunded author, one reflection-prompt wording, feedback drawn from the scoring suite. No claim about agentic harnesses or frontier models, and the r = 0.96 fit predicts the penalty vanishing as baseline quality rises — so 7B is already non-significant and the 1.5B figure must not travel. Nothing here touches the coordination limb; status unchanged. See a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm.
  • 2026-07-29 — interaction edge from harness-heavy-lifting-residual-llm (Jung & Son, independent / RLWRLD.AI, arXiv, "How Much Heavy Lifting Can an Agent Harness Do?", 2026-04-08, T1, suggestive for this edge; planning domain, not software engineering): the typed, fail-closed revision action this map adopted hours earlier from looping-is-not-reliability-state-bound-evidence — implemented, gated on machine-checkable preconditions, and measured. The revise step is a guarded action, legal at a turn only if four runtime-computed predicates hold: model confidence below threshold, a sustained low-confidence streak, a cooldown counter at zero, and a counterfactual one-step preview clearing a minimum delta against the current policy. None requires a model to evaluate, so "should I revise" is answered by the loop rather than by the thing being revised (give-agents-objectives-not-transitions, enforce-invariants-not-implementations, separate-the-generator-from-the-evaluator).   • 2026-07-10 — scaling-agent-systems-science (Kim et al., Google Research + Google DeepMind, arXiv, "Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems", 2025-12-09 (v3 2026-04-08), T1): the first controlled scaling law for coordination — 260 configurations, six benchmarks, five architectures, three model families. Coordination helps decomposable tasks (+80.9%, Finance-Agent) and degrades sequential ones (Independent −70.0%, Centralized −50.4%, Decentralized −41.4%, Hybrid −39.0% on PlanCraft); tool-heavy SWE-shaped tasks suffer most (β = −0.330); benefits saturate past a ~45% single-agent baseline; independent topologies amplify errors 17.2× against centralized coordination's 4.4×; an architecture selector predicts the best topology for 87% of held-out configurations. Moves the map consolidating → contested. It also answers, in the negative and by analogy, this map's own standing open question about unpriced coordination overhead. See multi-agent-helps-only-decomposable-tasks, coordination-saturates-as-the-single-agent-improves.
  • 2026-07-09 — cursor-cloud-agent-lessons (Cursor, Josh Ma, "What we've learned building cloud agents", 2026-06-02, T2): a third org's production fleet numbers — 50M+ actions/day, 7M+ workflows, 40%+ of Cursor's internal monorepo PRs generated by cloud agents; sub-agents spawn across pod types on a durable workflow substrate; and the give-objectives-not-transitions claim replicates as progressive autonomy (scaffolding stripped as models improved). Still an output-count grade — no quality measure. Primary map: unattended-operation. See durable-workflow-engine-runs-the-agent-loop.
  • 2026-07-08 — openai-symphony-orchestration (OpenAI, Kotliarskyi/Zhu/Brock, "An open-source spec for Codex orchestration: Symphony", 2026-04-27, T2): the cross-org production replication this map's open questions named as the priority follow-up — an always-on orchestrator makes the issue tracker the control plane, one agent per ticket in an isolated per-issue workspace, dependency-ordered tickets executing as a parallel DAG, restart recovery, PRs shepherded through CI; landed PRs +500% on some teams in three weeks; humans previously capped at 3–5 interactive sessions. Moved the map emerging → consolidating (since contested). See make-the-issue-tracker-the-control-plane, give-agents-objectives-not-transitions.
  • 2026-07-07 — multi-agent-computer-use (Koh/Salakhutdinov/Fried, CMU, arXiv, "Multi-Agent Computer Use", 2026-06-01, T1): a manager decomposes a computer-use task into a DAG and dispatches sub-agents to parallel nodes, forwarding distilled results across the partial-observability boundary; +3.4–25.5% over single-agent on OSWorld/web nav, ~1.5× faster on Odysseys long-horizon tasks, better test-time scaling; code released. Re-read 2026-07-10: OSWorld/web-nav tasks are highly decomposable, which is now the suspected source of the effect rather than the horizon length the map generalized from. See decompose-and-dispatch-to-parallel-subagents.
  • 2026-07-07 — async-swe-agents-caid (Geng/Neubig, CMU, arXiv, "Effective Strategies for Asynchronous Software Engineering Agents", 2026-03-23, T1): CAID (Centralized Asynchronous Isolated Delegation) reaches the same loop architecture in the SWE domain — centralized delegation, concurrent execution, isolated git worktree workspaces — and finds branch-and-merge is the critical coordination mechanism (+26.7% PaperBench, +14.3% Commit0). Its centralized topology is also the low-amplification one (4.4× vs 17.2×) in the 2026-07-10 scaling study — consistent, and a partial explanation of why branch-and-merge mattered so much. See isolate-and-merge-parallel-agent-work.

Open questions:

  • NEW 2026-07-30 — every architecture result in this map is missing a placebo arm, and the arm is free. The placebo design decomposes a retry result into the value of the information and the value of another sample, and at ≤7B the information contributed nothing while the packaging cost points (a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm). The map's own limbs are full of claims with exactly this shape — verified-gist admission, event passing, cross-model re-derivation, typed revision gates — all scored against a baseline that gets fewer attempts, not the same budget with the payload blanked. Two specific asks. First, the SWE version at frontier scale: a real fail-to-pass suite as the oracle, a content-free failure notice as the placebo arm, and the sign of the marginal gain per round measured against it — which also answers the 2026-07-22 question below, since the anchoring penalty's r = 0.96 scaling with baseline quality predicts it should vanish there and nobody has checked. Second, the cheapest harness experiment in the map: make discard-and-resample an available recovery action and measure it against repair-from-feedback on the same trajectories (recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder).
  • NEW 2026-07-29 — the three-armed sweep is now affordable, and the remaining excuse is the loss model. A deterministic orchestration simulator runs at 1.3% of the token cost with σ_eval zero by construction, so the raw-pool / event-passing / verified-gist comparison this map has been deferring since 2026-07-12 could be run at every scale this week. What stops it being decisive is that the simulator's degradation kernel — a geometric mean of parent qualities raised to a per-subtask compression sensitivity, with a chosen penalty for a missing transfer — is an assumption nobody has validated against real degradation. The cheap fix is to calibrate the kernel on a handful of real executions at one scale, then simulate the rest. Nobody has.
  • NEW 2026-07-29 — how often does an unforced loop destroy correct work, and does any shipped harness measure it? The 16.0% correctness-loss figure comes from forced revision, which the authors are explicit is not an adaptive policy. Every shipped coding agent chooses when to stop, so the field quantity is unknown — and unknowable from the metric everyone reports, since pass@k and best-of-k are discovery curves. The measurement costs nothing beyond logging: record the correctness of the state at every revision, not just the best one seen, and report current beside ever-correct (report-current-correctness-not-only-ever-correct).
  • NEW 2026-07-25 — the largest fleet result in the map has no counterfactual, and the cheapest version costs one weekend. Anthropic's migrations are the strongest evidence hypothesis (A) has, and they are unfalsifiable as reported: nobody ran the same rulebook and the same suite through one strong agent serially, or through a fleet with the compiler-gate removed, so the attribution between "the fleet helped" and "the mechanical oracle helped" is unmade. A migration is unusually cheap to arm this way — the rulebook, the judge and the file dependency map are all reusable across arms, so a second arm is a compute cost, not a research programme. Until someone runs it, this map's best production evidence supports (A) only by consistency, which is the same footing the two unfloored SWE deltas sit on. Related and sharper: the practice's active ingredient may be the rulebook, not the topology — a stress-tested external contract regenerating batches would plausibly help a single agent just as much (fix-the-loop-not-the-artifact, track-the-spec-in-external-state).
  • NEW 2026-07-25 — does the sole-blocker transfer boundary hold for coding repair? A verified, correctly-identified fix bought nothing end-to-end in two of three cells because it was not the binding constraint (repair-installs-only-constrained-actions). If that transfers, then every reported "our repair loop fixes X% of failures at the step level" is an upper bound with an unstated discount, and the discount is the fraction of failures with more than one blocker — which recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder's ~30%-unrecoverable figure suggests is large. Measured on CUA tasks with 5 rollouts per arm; a SWE version needs only existing agent trajectories plus a count of independent failure causes per instance.
  • NEW 2026-07-22 — does the within-agent repair-damage result survive a frontier agent with an executable oracle? The damage rates (0.615–0.938 per round) are measured on 3–8B open models with deliberately degraded verifiers on math tasks. The SWE-shaped question is the one this map needs and nobody has run: take a frontier coding agent, a real fail-to-pass suite as the verifier, and measure the sign of the marginal gain per additional repair round — does an executable oracle raise discrimination far enough that unbounded iteration is safe, or does it merely move the interior optimum outward? Cheap: the trajectories already exist on every SWE-bench leaderboard submission. Note the two loop results the map now holds point opposite ways on the same structure, and only the verifier's discrimination separates them.
  • The decisive experiment changed shape on 2026-07-11, gained a third arm on 2026-07-12, and gained a measurement precondition on 2026-07-14. It is no longer "fleet vs single agent" — icat-agent and DeLM have each run a version of that and the fleet won both times. It is raw shared pool vs event-passing vs verified-gist blackboard: re-run the scaling sweep with the coordination substrate as the manipulated variable, topology and task held fixed. The refined hypothesis predicts the two validated substrates tie and both beat the raw pool — if instead the raw pool matches them, the whole substrate story collapses and the regime hypothesis (B) inherits the evidence. Nobody has done it, and it requires no new benchmark. What is new: it must now be run paired, with a same-model configuration-equivalent noise-floor arm (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor) — on τ²-bench that floor reached ≲15 pp, and while it explicitly does not transfer to SWE benchmarks, nobody has measured what it is there. Run the sweep unpaired and a 5–10 point result means nothing.
  • The map's two counterexamples sit in the danger band, and this is now the cheapest thing to check. icat-agent's +8.3 pts and DeLM's +9.3–10.5 pts are exactly the magnitudes a floor of the measured order would swallow. Both were single-configuration comparisons, not paired same-model replications across seeds. Neither is refuted — the floor is local to τ²-bench/Haiku and does not transfer — but neither has been floored, and the cost of flooring them is a re-run, not a new benchmark. Until someone does, the substrate hypothesis rests on two unfloored deltas, and that is a weaker footing than the map has been treating it as. This now sits alongside the contamination confound (benchmark-scores-inflate-when-agents-can-retrieve-the-fix) as the second independent reason the SWE-bench evidence may not mean what it appears to.
  • DeLM vs icat-agent is itself an unrun comparison. Both beat single agents on SWE benchmarks, one on Verified, one on Pro, different models, different baselines — near-duals in design but never on the same field. Whoever runs the three-arm sweep gets this for free.
  • The prior decisive experiment is still unrun and still worth running: a fleet versus a strong single agent on a dependency-ordered SWE testbed (RoadmapBench, version-upgrades-defeat-frontier-agents; or SaaSBench, failures-concentrate-before-business-logic), holding model and token budget fixed. Note that SWE-bench Pro is not a substitute: it is contaminated in a way that flatters exploratory scaffolds (benchmark-scores-inflate-when-agents-can-retrieve-the-fix), which is exactly what icat-agent is.
  • Does the ~45% saturation threshold survive icat-agent at all? A scaffold beating a 59.1% baseline is a direct violation of it. Either the threshold is benchmark-specific, or it is a property of shared-context topologies only, or the SWE-bench Pro win is a retrieval artifact. These are distinguishable, and which one is true decides whether fleet architectures have the shelf life the map has been warning about since 2026-07-10.
  • Does the intra-task / inter-ticket distinction actually carve the evidence, or is it a rescue? A falsifiable version: Symphony-style ticket fleets should show the coordination penalty on tickets whose dependency graph is deep rather than wide. Nobody reports per-ticket-graph-shape outcomes.
  • The ~45% saturation threshold implies fleet value is decreasing over time as base models improve. If it holds for SWE tasks, today's fleet architectures have a shelf life, and the +500% and 40% production numbers are partly artifacts of the model generation they were measured on. Untested, and cheap to test on any two adjacent model versions.
  • The three stitch mechanisms (manager-forwarded distilled state, git branch-and-merge, deterministic daemon over ticket states) still haven't been compared head-to-head — but the error-amplification result reframes the comparison: score them on how much they verify, not on how they transport.
  • Production evidence remains output-count grade: Symphony's 500% is a landed-PR count on self-selected teams; Cursor's 40% is a share of PRs. No defect-escape, rework, or quality measure for fleet-produced code exists anywhere yet. See evaluation-integrity for why that is a load-bearing hole and not a footnote.
  • Failure mode of the reconcile step itself (bad merges, forwarded-but-stale state) is named as critical but not quantified.
