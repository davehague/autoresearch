What we believe now:

  • A procedure delivered as prose is re-derived every run and about half-executed; the determined half can be moved into program structure, and the reliability that buys does not improve with the model. (Added 2026-07-31, T1, strong.) This map has carried move-task-difficulty-from-the-model-to-the-harness and declarative-structure-outperforms-the-model-it-replaces as design claims without a compliance number on the substrate itself. Here is one, with the authoring surface held fixed so the comparison isolates compiling the coordination rather than a difference in writing: across 30 skills × 3 tasks × 3 repeats × 2 model generations, a prose agent performs 56% of the steps its own skill mandates against a compiled harness at 86%, completes the full prescribed procedure in 28% of runs against 65% (2.3×), and costs more — the harness runs at 0.58× median tokens, cheaper on 24 of 32 skills (compile-the-procedure-into-the-harness). On 11 of the 30 a prose agent executes fewer than half its mandates and on one it executes none. The failures are procedural rather than linguistic: the model restates the mandate accurately, narrates an intention to perform it, and does not — and the dropped steps concentrate on the mechanical ones (the fetch, the check, the commit), while the open-ended authoring steps survive. That gives the split a criterion the map has been circling without one, the Owner test: is this step's output a function of its inputs? If yes it can lower to structure that fires on node entry with the model never consulted; if not, over-constraining it is as damaging as under-constraining mechanism, and the enforceable share is a property of the skill (37% overall, 32–44% by family) rather than of the compiler. The capability axis is the consequential half. Prose improves 56% → 68% from the weaker model to the stronger; the compiled harness holds 86% → 86%, because the graph carries the procedure and the graph is the same artifact in both sweeps (structural-guarantees-do-not-improve-with-the-model). So the advantage narrows 30 points → 17, structure substitutes for capability exactly where capability is cheapest, and any "agents follow instruction X in N% of runs" figure — including this vault's own — is a model-version snapshot that expires (re-examine-the-harness-when-the-model-changes, harness-model-assumptions-go-stale). Two honest boundaries: on the stronger model the harness loses on 4 of 30 skills, all judgment-heavy; and where it costs more it is running an adaptive loop the prose agent skipped, so part of prose's economy is unperformed work. [T1 — mandate statuses are judge-assigned, nine runs per skill per arm, two models from one provider, 30 skills weighted toward document / process / compliance work, artifact quality deliberately not scored as a separate axis]
  • The heavy-lifting harness layer can have no model in it at all — and the LLM's residual is measurable rather than assumed. (Added 2026-07-29, T1, suggestive; planning domain, not software engineering — taken for the mechanism and the method, not the magnitude.) Four layers over one shared belief backend, each lifted into a separately callable runtime object: adding declarative planning moves win rate 50.0% → 74.1% with zero LLM calls, the only contrast whose Wilson 95% intervals do not overlap, and the unique heavy-lifting layer under a pre-registered criterion. The comparison that gives it teeth: LM-only captains calling the model on every turn fall below the no-LLM baseline when they lack inference-time reasoning, and this holds to 109B parameters (0.353 F1, zero wins, against belief-only's 0.522 / 50.0%) — "model size is not the missing ingredient, inference-time reasoning is." A mid-reasoning model does reach the planner's neighbourhood (77.8% vs 74.1%, overlapping CIs) at 100% LLM invocation against structure's 0%, which prices move-task-difficulty-from-the-model-to-the-harness. Where the model enters it enters thinly: a gate with four runtime-computable preconditions fires on 4.3% of turns for +0.005 F1 and the lowest win rate in its family — LLM revisions sharpen local targeting while spending a finite question budget, improving the metric and losing the game (declarative-structure-outperforms-the-model-it-replaces). This is an independent, code-released replication of the ordering structure-outweighs-the-verification-step found in a vendor self-evaluation. [T1, suggestive — n=54, single MCMC-friendly domain the authors admit favours declarative planning, one 9B model for the residual]
  • Per-layer attribution is not identifiable while the layers share one prompt loop — and an aggregate null is not an absent mechanism. (Added 2026-07-29, T1, suggestive.) The map's whole literature moves along one axis, search over whole harnesses ranked by end-to-end pass rate. The orthogonal axis is decomposition: hold the harness fixed, lift each internal layer into an object with declared preconditions and effects, measure its marginal. Why it must be structural rather than a prompt ablation: deleting one role from a bundled prompt perturbs the others through the shared generation distribution, so the marginals are not recoverable — the fix is a typed state record, side-effect-free computed properties, and a non-Turing-complete DSL in which toggling one layer's flag cannot reach another's logic (enforce-invariants-not-implementations pointed at the measuring apparatus). Three things it buys that a pass rate destroys: an aggregate Δ of −0.001 F1 resolving into +0.140 to −0.148 per-case signed effects in two opposed regimes (a trigger-calibration diagnosis, not a dead mechanism — "a mechanism that did nothing would produce a flat band at zero"); LLM invocation rate as a measured dependent variable rather than a configured quota; and a −16.7pp confound surfaced and published as an open question instead of being absorbed into the layer's score. This is the constructive form of benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives's demand for per-component evaluation (decompose-the-harness-into-separately-liftable-layers). [T1, suggestive — transfers to harnesses someone will rebuild declaratively, not to a prompt-orchestrated production loop]
  • The most valuable harness edits keep turning out to be subtractions, and the two largest this run were performed with no model in the path at all. (Added 2026-07-28, two T1s.) The map's 2026-07-22 note was that removal is an under-used edit and that where a well-defined mechanical operation exists, taking the model out of the path is the reliability win. Both land again. (a) The append-only trajectory — a default nobody chose, arising from appending each tool call beside its result — stores a file's bytes as an immutable history entry, which is false for every file the agent edits. Replacing it with a registry plus a deterministic re-read before each cycle cuts input tokens 9.3–50.4%, reasoning cycles 4.9–37.3% and cost 8.3–50.3% across four models on 573 instances at comparable pass@1 — no summarizer, no relevance judgment, nothing to be miscalibrated (prevent-context-growth-upstream-not-downstream, re-derive-file-state-instead-of-storing-snapshots). It is the fourth member of a sequence: strip 80% of a mature system prompt (strip-the-prompt-when-the-model-improves), strip a third of a patch by trajectory search with no model reading the trajectory (patch-minimization-is-search-not-generation), collapse the tool surface to one execution channel (restrict-the-tool-surface-to-cut-cost-not-capability), and now delete the accumulated history of file contents. The design question a harness author should ask first is therefore not "how do I compress this history" but "what is this history storing that it should be querying?" Unusually, this one's value grows with the model (−37.3% cycles on the strongest backbone against −15.2% on the weakest, because stronger models sustain longer trajectories), which makes it durable capability rather than model compensation (separate-durable-scaffolding-from-model-compensation). (b) A fifth dependent variable for the pair thesis, measured cleanly: holding one backbone fixed, a state-first harness for long-horizon computer use — the main agent acting on files, application backends and the DOM through code, with a GUI subagent reserved for the irreducibly visual (28 of 108 tasks, 1.1% of main-agent steps) — moves binary success 20.6% → 26.9% and mean partial 54.8% → 61.6% at roughly 9× lower cost ($72 → $7.8/task), and lifts a weaker backbone too (8.3 → 11.1 binary), so it is not one model's quirk (ground-the-agent-in-state-not-its-rendering). Its ablations are the useful part: removing act-on-state is the largest drop and lands below the reference harness (18.5/51.3 vs 54.8 partial), a bash-only configuration also lands below it (15.7/45.9), and flat delegation beat both recursive variants (61.6 vs 57.6 / 57.9). And it states the scope condition harness papers usually omit: on a short-horizon benchmark the same harness and the reference are within a point (78.4 vs 77.3), so the entire advantage is a long-horizon effect. [Two T1s; strong for the efficiency and ablation results, suggestive for the headline deltas — single runs per configuration, an unoptimized append-only baseline on one side, and computer-use rather than software engineering on the other]
  • Part of the harness term decomposes: some of it is task-shape fit, which is why no harness ordering is portable across benchmarks either. (Added 2026-07-31, T1, single-study.) A third grid, and the smallest — four models × two model-agnostic harnesses, one run per cell — so it adds nothing to the magnitudes below. What it adds is a named mechanism for one swap, which neither better-powered study has. Every model scores lower under Aider than under OpenCode, by up to 0.34 (GLM-4.6 0.78 → 0.44), and the reason given is that Aider is a code-editing harness applied to an answer-writing task — the benchmark asks an agent to read a task file and write a solution file, while the harness's native loop is propose-a-diff-to-existing-source. The failures are overwhelmingly genuine wrong answers rather than format errors, so this is not the grader artifact benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives warns about (part-of-the-harness-term-is-task-shape-fit). That splits the term usefully: some is capability the scaffold adds or destroys, and some is fit between the harness's native interaction shape and the task's — which is not a property of the harness at all but of its pairing with the workload. It also predicts the bullet below: if orderings do not transfer across models because the term is an interaction, they will not transfer across benchmarks either, since a benchmark fixes a task shape and the shape is half the term. So the map's rule sharpens from "evaluate a candidate model on your own harness" to evaluate the pair on your own task shape, because a harness winning on someone else's benchmark may be winning for a reason that does not apply to you. A smaller observation rides along and is worth keeping: under one harness the four accessible models span 0.66–0.93 against a reference frontier at 0.95–0.97, with ordering tracking more than parameter count (two 32B-active models differ by 0.15), and the author frames evaluation as a deployment filter for the cheap, local or open models a practitioner tries first — the tier with the widest spread and the least published measurement. [T1, single-study — one run per cell, four models, two harnesses, a single unaffiliated author; the task-shape reading is the author's interpretation rather than a manipulation, since nobody ran the same harnesses on a code-editing task to complete the 2×2; electric power engineering, so the numbers are not portable and the decomposition is]
  • The distribution replicates from outside, and the harness term is an interaction — there is nothing to correct for. (Added 2026-07-31, T1, strong.) The bullet below carried two caveats the vault wrote down at the time: two harnesses only, one of them the publishing vendor's own product. Both are now answered by an independent group on a different benchmark in a different task domain, with four harnesses and none of them the authors' — 67 tasks, 18 model×harness pairs, shared 1,800-second timeout and one evaluation protocol. Every model run under ≥2 harnesses spreads ≥0.08 Pass@1: Claude Opus 4.8 0.209 on a single swap (0.921 ClaudeCode → 0.712 OpenClaw), Qwen3.6-27B 0.157 across three, DeepSeek-v4-Flash 0.099 across four, the authors' own summary of the range being 8–21 points. Two independent groups, two benchmarks, six harnesses between them, one conclusion. The part that is new is structural and it closes a loophole the map had left open. The ordering of harnesses is not model-invariant — ClaudeCode is best or tied-best for every model where it was run, including the open-weight ones (which is what rules out a vendor pairing effect), but below the top Codex beats Hermes for DeepSeek-v4-Flash while Hermes is competitive with OpenClaw only for Qwen models (no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models). If the harness term were a per-harness offset the field could ship a correction table and carry on; being an interaction between harness and model, a score measured on someone else's scaffold is not adjustable into a score on yours, it is a different measurement. That is harness-interchangeability-is-asserted-not-measured with the non-equivalence measured across four scaffolds instead of asserted, and it is why attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model remains the only safe attribution. The paper's own response is the one to copy: publish the full model×harness matrix and organize every table by harness so model comparisons are read inside a fixed scaffold — the first time the vault has seen agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness's remedy followed by a benchmark author rather than recommended to one. [T1, strong — but the grid is partially crossed (Codex one entry, the Claude family never run under Codex or Hermes), nruns varies 1–3 with no intervals, and the domain is enterprise workflow work rather than repository maintenance: the ordinal claim transfers, the magnitudes are that benchmark's]
  • The pair thesis now has a distribution, and the harness term is the size of the model term. (Added 2026-07-26, T1, strong.) Seven models × 260 tasks × three runs under two harnesses: mean absolute shift 8.6 points on one subset, rank order changing, one cell moving 13.5 points with nothing varied but the harness — the authors' own conclusion being "the harness is visibly not a neutral measurement instrument." On the same benchmark's Code subset frontier models separate by 2–14 points, so the harness is not a confound to disclose but a term of the same magnitude as the effect. It is also non-uniform — one subset shifts almost everyone upward, another reverses orderings, a third spreads — which forbids a correction factor and is what "the pair is the unit" means concretely (the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order). Two rules: treat a single-harness leaderboard gap under ~10 points as unresolved, and evaluate a candidate model on your own harness or you have not evaluated it for your system (re-examine-the-harness-when-the-model-changes). A fifth dependent variable arrives the same run and resolves the other way — across three commercial products the harness contributes little to malicious-instruction refusal and the model carries it (framework-guardrails-add-little-over-model-refusal) — which is the thesis's strongest form: neither component's contribution is estimable without the other, and which dominates depends on the question. [T1, strong — two harnesses, one the publishing vendor's own; three runs per cell, no intervals]
  • The unit that fails is the harness-model PAIR — now on three dependent variables by three methods, and it is an accountability boundary too. (Generalized 2026-07-20.) Security detection reverses when only the harness is swapped (controlled ablation); 255 field-reported bugs exist only in the reaction between a specific LLM response and the harness parsing it, with the same trigger crashing 79% of the time in one framework and 2% in a CLI product (issue-report study); and an industry cost comparison could not separate model capacity from harness design at all, its authors concluding results must be read as deployable harness+model bundles. Practical rule: a harness test suite that mocks the model tests a system that does not exist, and a security review or benchmark result does not transfer across a swap of either component. The unresolved half: when the defect needs both components and the components have different owners, no fix path is well-defined — 66% of user action targets the harness and only 38% of user-filed harness PRs merge, while maintainers attribute 15% upward to the model vendor. [T1 ×2 + T3, converging]
  • The harness-model pairing is a security boundary, not only a performance one — and neither harness dominates. The vault has carried same-model harness swings as benchmark-score effects (8.3-point scaffold topology, 53-point grader/scaffold). This is the first time the dependent variable is a security outcome, and it reverses in both directions. Holding the model and a byte-identical repository fixed and swapping only the harness: Opus 4.8 on an untrusted-localhost- registry attack catches 10/10 under Claude Code vs 9/30 under Copilot CLI (Fisher's exact p=1.1×10⁻⁴) — then Sonnet 4.6 on the external-HTTPS variant catches 0/10 under Claude Code vs 10/10 under Copilot CLI (p=1.1×10⁻⁵), and a config-poisoning attack flips it back again (10/10 vs 0/10). There is no safer harness to pick; the safeguard that fires is a property of the pairing and the attack together. Practical consequence: a harness security review does not transfer across a model swap, and a model's safety record does not transfer across a harness swap. [T1, single-study]
  • Decouple the reasoning loop from its execution sandbox behind a stable tool-call interface (execute(name, input) → string) rather than running the harness inside the same container as its tools — the decoupling itself has been measured to cut latency substantially (p50 TTFT ~-60%, p95 ~-90%). [T2]
  • More structure is not always better structure — sometimes the highest-leverage harness edit is removing tool surface. A controlled three-arm ablation (all-tools / bash-only / a single execute_code channel), crossed over two task regimes and two agent designs with the model fixed, finds restricting the tool surface is pass-rate-tied in every cell and cheaper in three of four — the extra affordances bought no capability at current model sizes and usually cost tokens, because one general execution channel caches and composes better than many narrow schemas. The cheapest surface depends jointly on task regime and agent design, decided by cache-adjusted cost. Methodological catch worth keeping: separate cost-on-success from cost-of-failure, since the one adverse cell was entirely doomed-run failure cost, not a per-edit tax. [T1, suggestive]
  • Don't harden a harness against a specific model's current limitations — those workarounds become technical debt (or active friction) once a newer model no longer has the limitation; re-examine and strip the harness on each model upgrade. Now cross-org: shown across two Anthropic systems (the Sonnet→Opus "context anxiety" anecdote, Opus 4.5→4.6 deleting sprints) and independently by Cursor, which re-tunes edit format, prompting, and tool provisioning per model and re-iterates for weeks on every new model. This cross-org replication is what moves the map to consolidating. [T2]
  • The harness is customized per model and per version, not model-agnostic — matching each model's training (OpenAI patch-based edits vs Anthropic string-replacement; literal vs intuitive prompting), with mid-chat model switching needing explicit architectural support. A focused reliability sprint at Cursor drove all tool calls to 2–3 nines. [T2] 2026-07-25 — the claim finally has a magnitude, and it points at deletion. Anthropic removed over 80% of Claude Code's system prompt for Claude Opus 5 and Claude Fable 5, reported as "no measurable loss on our coding evaluations," on the diagnosis that a mature harness's instruction context is accumulated compensation for weaknesses the current model no longer has — with the named cost being conflict rather than tokens (the prompt carried "leave documentation as appropriate" and "DO NOT add comments," so every run spent capability reconciling a contradiction). The prescribed replacements move content off the documentation plane entirely: rules → a relative standard, examples → tool schema design, repeated instructions → tool descriptions, depth → references fetched at point of use. So per-model tuning, normally imagined as adding scaffolding for a new model's quirks, has its largest reported edit as subtraction — the same operation as codeslop-is-the-search-surviving-into-the-patch one layer up, and neither is reachable by generation. Bounded hard: vendor post about the vendor's own harness, and the headline names no eval, delta or interval (agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness from the vendor side); the claim is model-generation-specific by construction, so a team on a smaller model deleting its scaffolding is contradicting it, not applying it (strip-the-prompt-when-the-model-improves). [T2, single-study]
  • For multi-hour autonomous builds, split roles into planner / generator / evaluator — the evaluator being a separate skeptical agent (drives the running app with Playwright, grades against hard thresholds) because an agent asked to grade its own output confidently praises mediocre work. Caveat (new this run): a separate LLM evaluator is still gameable once something optimizes against it — see agentic-code-review and llm-judges-have-exploitable-biases. [T2]
  • The thing outside the agent that decides it is not done can itself be learned — and its correct policy differs per agent. A cost-penalized contextual bandit choosing {generate, analyze-then- generate, stop} from cheap static + dynamic features beats a fixed iteration budget on quality and price (82.8% line / 74.7% branch / 64.7% mutation at $0.693 vs 77.3 / 70.2 / 59.6 at $0.797) — and learns opposite policies for two agents: stop-early-on-95%-of-tasks against a strong-zero-shot agent, iterate-and-analyze against a weaker one. This is separate-the-generator-from-the-evaluator with an economic policy attached: the evaluator does not just grade, it decides how much more to spend. The fixed iteration cap in most harnesses is wrong in both directions at once. [T1, single-study]
  • The harness extends into the repository itself: in an agent-first codebase, knowledge layout (a ~100-line AGENTS.md table of contents over a structured, CI-linted docs/ system of record), layer-dependency linters, structural tests, and "golden principle" cleanup agents are harness components on equal footing with tools — enforce invariants mechanically, leave implementations free (~1M LOC / ~1,500 PRs / 3.5 PRs-per-engineer-day in 5 months with zero hand-written code; custom lint errors inject remediation instructions into agent context; recurring GC agents replaced the 20%-of-week manual slop cleanup). Third org converging on harness engineering as the core discipline. [T2]
  • Below the role split sits a session-lifecycle split: an initializer agent seeds the environment once (init script, feature list all-marked-failing, progress file, git), then each session re-enters through a fixed ritual (read artifacts → pick ONE incomplete feature → baseline test → incremental progress → clean artifacts out) — killing the two unguided cross-window failure modes: build-everything-at-once and premature "done". [T2]
  • When the uplift is decomposed by layer, structure carries it and the verification checkpoint is the smallest term — invest in that order, and do not skip the last one. (2026-07-22.) The map's instruction to tune structure over prompts has never had an interior. A production system measured against three benchmarks stressing unrelated failure modes supplies one: on SpreadsheetBench, scaffolding and prompting account for +9.5 pp of a +11.0 pp total and the deterministic verification loop adds the final +1.5 pp; on GAIA the planner–executor split and routing carry ~60% → ~74% and the loop ~+1 pp. The checkpoint is small but positionally decisive, converting the residual tasks that good scaffolding has already failed. What makes per-step verification affordable is stage staffing: 0.5–4B post-trained specialists at 0.02–0.1× frontier per-call cost, which is move-task-difficulty-from-the-model-to-the-harness applied to loop stages rather than to harness adaptation. Vendor self-evaluation with proprietary components and no matched-budget scaffold ablation — take the decomposition shape, not the magnitudes. [T3, single-study]
  • The harness-model pair now has a fourth dependent variable: context interventions. The map holds that a result does not transfer across a component swap — security (install-time-security-is-a-harness-model-pair-property), defects (agent-reactive-bugs-need-both-components-to-explain), cost, and the eval-side complement (attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model). Add retrieval: a packaged progressive-disclosure context pack lifts weak-navigator harnesses double digits and is worth exactly zero under one whose bare agent already greps for the entities a question names — model held constant. So a context-engineering result reported without naming the harness is not portable, and the corollary for builders is separate-durable-scaffolding-from-model-compensation read one level down: a retrieval layer may be compensating for your harness's navigation weakness rather than supplying durable capability. [T1, suggestive]
  • Removal is an under-used class of harness edit, and the trajectory is a durable artifact worth computing over. restrict-the-tool-surface-to-cut-cost-not-capability established that deleting tool surface can be the highest-leverage change; TRIM adds a second instance at the output end — a post-processing stage that strips 17.9–32.9% of an agent's patch by counterfactual search over the repair trajectory, preserving the oracle on 99.1% of repairs. Two design lessons transfer past the application. First, the signal consumed is the trajectory's structure (which edits were grouped in time), not its content, so no model is in the path — a deterministic search beats prompting the agent by 1.6–3.1× and, unlike it, never returns an invalid result. Second, the same hierarchical search matches exhaustive delta-debugging quality at 1.9× fewer validations with a bounded worst case, which is what makes it safe to put in a pipeline at all. [T1]

Self-evolving harnesses are the pattern this map is most bullish on, and TTHE measures the ceiling they hit: 20 points of attainable performance lost to a miscalibrated judge, most of it in selection rather than search. The standard remedy is unavailable in the unlabeled setting by construction. Open, and now urgent: does the selection gap widen across generations as proposer and judge share blind spots (the correlated-variant finding says yes); is there any label-free in-flight detector of judge miscalibration; and does a periodic gold-labeled audit — the only obvious fix — reintroduce the labeled-development-set dependency the whole method exists to escape? 2026-07-16 adds the second cap and a remedy with a catch: the optimizer can fabricate the failures it fixes (harness-optimizers-fabricate-failures-to-fix), and warrant-aware acceptance eliminates fabrication in the lab — but the lab has a byte-exact oracle to confirm warrants against, and the unlabeled setting's defining condition is that no such oracle exists. Whether a warrant requirement is implementable label-free is now the sharpest open sub-question in this thread.
  • How expensive is self-evolution? Both self-evolving-harness sources report gains and neither reports a full cost. The Fudan work at least measures token efficiency of the evolved harness (−12%); TTHE reports no cost figure at all for the evolution process — while maintaining a population of candidate programs, running an agentic proposer over their traces, and re-executing candidates for a judge. A method whose price is unstated cannot be compared against the compute-matched baselines the authors themselves say are missing (best-of-N execution selection; development-time optimizers). Until someone prices it, "the harness improves itself" is a capability claim, not an engineering recommendation.
  • If ~95.6% of from-scratch failure mass sits in setup, configuration, and integration, then the highest-return harness component is the environment, not the role split — and none of the planner/generator/evaluator numbers control for environment quality. Nobody has ablated a harness against a fixed, pre-built environment to separate the two contributions.
