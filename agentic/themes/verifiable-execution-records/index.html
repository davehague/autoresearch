Verifiable execution records — map

Carved from observability on 2026-08-26 (bounded auto-fork by the unattended run; flagged for David's review and one git revert away). The parent's own sub-thread declaration had been holding this strand open by name since 2026-08-22 — "the static half of auditability (configuration as a governed versioned object rather than the run as a trace) is still the strongest third candidate and still awaits a second source" — and tonight two independent T1s supplied it from opposite ends of the stack: a two-class survey of 47 delivery platforms finding that not one emits a content-addressed identity of the behavioral tuple by default, and a source-level reading of three agent harnesses finding external verifiability absent from all three. The boundary between parent and child is a question, not a topic. observability asks what happened in this run and where did it first go wrong — it reads a trajectory. This map asks can anyone other than the runtime establish what ran — it reads the record layer itself: whether the identity of the thing that executed is expressible, whether the evidence binds to that identity, whether an approval names a judgement, and whether any of it survives being checked by someone who does not trust the process that wrote it. The parent's through-line is append-only history and stays intact; three claims move across by re-topicing, nothing rewritten.

Status: consolidating (opened at consolidating rather than emerging, on the same reasoning verifier-independence was opened: two independent T1s from different orgs, on different objects (vendor documentation versus harness source), by different methods (a blind-double-graded documentation survey with pinned pages versus a pinned-commit multi-case source reading), converging on the same negative — the record class that would carry external verifiability does not exist by default anywhere either of them looked — plus three prior T1s already in the vault reaching it from the configuration-governance, approval- artifact and provenance-identifier directions. Not consensus: every source here is a bounded absence, and an absence measured at one moment in a fast-moving layer is the most perishable finding this vault holds. One of the two headline sources says so itself and attaches a date — the survey expires rather than fails when a named major platform ships a content-addressed tuple identity as a default, with a re-run committed no later than 2027-02. Not contested either: nothing here pushes back on anything, and the two arrivals corroborate rather than collide. What would move this map is a positive: one platform, or one harness, shipping the record and someone measuring whether it holds.)

Sub-threads: none — one question, "can anyone other than the runtime establish what ran": identity of the executing tuple, binding of evidence to that identity, authority naming a judgement rather than a click, and outsider-checkability are four rungs of one ladder rather than separable objects, and every source here is measured against the same ladder. What we believe now:

  • A governance property that holds because of how a system happens to be wired, rather than because a written rule forbids the wiring, is not a property — and no artifact the system produces distinguishes the two cases. (Added 2026-08-28, T1 — single-study.) A pre-separation build was probed against exactly the property its successor architecture was designed to guarantee — that a freely-editable persona cannot reach governed execution — and passed: 5/5 identical on tool sequence, state-transition path and structural contract fields, directed instructions 0/3 honoured, with a positive control on the general execution surface confirming the probe worked. Then the author wired the persona into the governed path explicitly and execution changed 2/2 on both models tested (an-isolation-nobody-wrote-down-is-decoupling-by-omission). His own gloss is the claim: the path was "decoupled from the persona by omission, not by construction — an unwritten implementation fact that a later wiring change could reverse," and the architecture's contribution is to make the isolation "a written, audited rule: the restrictive domain is faceless by rule, not by default." Note what he refuses: "This is not a measured win … We report no paired contrast." This adds a rung beneath the four already on this map's ladder — whether the record can distinguish an enforced property from one that merely obtains. The audit log, approval chain and structural contract fields are identical in the passing and the injectable configurations; the only separator is a rule someone wrote down, and rules-as-artifacts is precisely the object govern-the-agents-configuration-as-a-versioned-object found represented nowhere and the-approval-artifact-never-recorded-that-anyone-judged found missing at the approval boundary. Two details worth keeping: once connected, injected text changed how the artifact was phrased, not the orchestration or approval count; and an A/A probe with no persona change produced the same class of free-text divergence, so any trace-isolation claim over unstructured output is unfalsifiable without an A/A arm — only hard-asserted structural fields are checkable. One development/pilot deployment operated by the author's own team, no comparative evaluation, and R = 0 is a designed outcome rather than a discovery. [T1, single-study]
  • The record that would let anyone check what ran does not ship — not on 47 delivery platforms by default, and not in any of three agent harnesses read at source — so a promotion gate that depends on that identity cannot refuse a transition, whatever its configuration declares. (Added 2026-08-26, T1 — strong.) Two instruments, both public-material-only, reaching the same negative from opposite ends. The survey: 47 platforms (20 CI/CD, 27 model-serving/agent), four dimensions, one fixed three-label protocol, every cell graded twice with the second pass blind, every consulted page pinned by content hash and fetch date. Across 188 double-graded cells, no platform's default record emits a content-addressed identity of the behavioral tuple — model version, instructions, tool definitions, retrieval and runtime configuration — and the blind pass alone grades that column default on zero of 47 (no-delivery-platform-emits-the-identity-its-assurance-claim-needs). The harness reading: across deepagents, pi and dsh at pinned commits, external verifiability is not merely divergent but absent; the strongest of the three is event-sourced with a runtime "visible ⇒ logged" invariant and still aims at operational reconstructability rather than outsider-checkability, its own documentation conceding that a tool restriction is "a visibility composition, not a permission boundary" (no-harness-produces-a-record-an-outsider-can-check). The dependency both expose is the same one: behavior depends on a tuple whose components vary independently of the source, so artifact continuity is necessary and not sufficient — identical code under a different model version breaks behavioral continuity while preserving artifact continuity. The escape hatch is narrow and stated: where models are pinned, prompts are in-tree, retrieval is static and tools do not change, the tuple collapses onto the source digest and ordinary artifact provenance suffices. [T1, strong] · [T1, suggestive]
  • What is arriving in that space is immutable nominal versioning — a counter or a name in front of a pointer that can move — which is the layer the artifact supply chain and the model registry each already found insufficient, being resolved nominally a third time one layer up. (Added 2026-08-26, T1 — strong.) 16 of 27 agent and model-serving platforms now record the tuple, or most of it, by default as an immutable named or numbered version, against 0 emitting a content-addressed one (nominal-versioning-is-arriving-where-content-addressing-was-needed). The gloss cells are each a specific way a version can be true and useless: a revision that is immutable by documentation while its versioned fields hold storage URIs, so it pins pointers rather than bytes; a deployment that is immutable while a team-level routing rule rewrites which model actually serves with no redeployment, drifting the tuple under an unchanged deployment identity; immutability as stated policy with no digest to check the policy against; a publish history that permits deleting old versions. One layer down the failure mode is already a measurement: on the largest model registry, name-based versioning left 40.87% of model-weight changes unrepresented in either the name or the documentation. The two largest vendors are moving in opposite directions inside the observation window — one retiring server-side tuple versioning outright (2026-11-30) and routing the record to the customer's own repository, the other introducing it as a counter. [T1, strong]
  • Every trace this vault argues for is a record of a run against a system nobody wrote down: the tooling versions the prompt and traces the execution, and represents the configuration — agents, tools, skills, models, policies, workflows and their dependencies — nowhere. (Added 2026-08-14, T1, single-study. Moved here from observability 2026-08-26, unchanged.) A capability audit across five platforms practitioners actually use — LangSmith, Langfuse, Helicone, Braintrust, PromptLayer — finds prompt versioning, tracing, observability, evaluation and lineage supported everywhere, and framework-independent configuration model, configuration graph, immutable baseline, configuration lifecycle, dependency model, governed revisions, state propagation and governance semantics marked "not provided as a first-class capability" in all five columns (govern-the-agents-configuration-as-a-versioned-object). Four design moves survive the paper. Configuration–runtime separation — runtime observations may enrich governance information and may not modify governed configuration, with provenance mapping every runtime entity back to the revision that produced it. Immutable revisions with stable logical identity, so "the reviewer agent" and "the reviewer agent as it was on Tuesday" are both nameable — agent-specifications-are-versioned-engineering-artifacts taken past commit the markdown too into an addressing scheme. Four dimensions kept apart (quality, assurance, impact, eligibility) so absence of evidence is not read as poor quality. And a measured reason to compute impact transitively: one-hop traversal found 6 of 9 affected items where fixed-point propagation found 9 of 9 (one-hop-dependency-inspection-misses-transitive-config-impact). [T1 venue, design with a conformance test]
  • A provenance field that always returns the same answer is indistinguishable from one that works, and the cheap test is to count distinct identifiers against distinct events. (Added 2026-08-20, T1 — suggestive. Moved here from observability 2026-08-26, unchanged.) A governed decision record identified the write a repair drew from by hashing that write's key and value — so two writes of the same value are one identifier, and 3,478 real substitution occurrences resolved to 62 unique ids with the field present, well-formed and schema-valid on every line. Found by independent review, not by the schema; repaired with a durable run-scoped write-history log (an-audit-line-must-resolve-to-the-write-it-used). The rule: a content-derived identifier answers "what", never "when" or "which occurrence", so any trail that must support trace this decision back to the event that produced the value it used needs an event log beside the hash. And the scoping discipline that came with it is this map's most load-bearing distinction: the repaired record promises an identity commitment verifiable against a content-addressed store, not a reconstruction of the original bytes — a stronger reading claimed in an earlier draft, refuted by review, and retracted. [T1, suggestive]
  • The record layer is populated where a vendor needs authorisation and empty where accountability would need it: an approval is stored as the submission of a review, the same event type as a comment. (Added 2026-08-18, T1 — suggestive; documentation analysis, not repository data. Moved here from observability 2026-08-26, unchanged.) The artifact carries that a party with authority acted and never that anyone formed a judgement. The audit log carries fields for whether an actor is an agent, whether an actor is a bot, and an agent session identifier — and defines none of them. No standardised trailer exists for machine authorship; one provider repurposes the co-authorship trailer, documented elsewhere purely as a multiple-human mechanism, and that attribution is disableable. Attribution runs in opposite directions across providers (the-approval-artifact-never-recorded-that-anyone-judged). Tonight's survey measures the same hole from the platform side and finds it wider in the agent class than in CI/CD: approval records are the most uniformly default rung in CI/CD (13 of 20) and absent on 14 of 27 agent platforms, typically because the deploy act itself is the only gate — with the class's most complete deployment-event record shipping toggled off by default and unrecoverable for the period it was off. [T1, suggestive]
  • The seams an autonomous system cannot walk back are the ones that break, and none of the harnesses records the destructive action it took. (Added 2026-08-26, T1 — suggestive.) Reproduced defects across two harnesses of opposing philosophy and different languages land on four recurring seams, and three of four specimens end in unrecoverable loss — a delete deny-rule failing open on a path-normalisation mismatch, and conversation history permanently overwritten by a destructive compaction fired by a transient rate-limit a regex misread as context overflow (the-unrecoverable-seam-is-where-agent-harness-defects-land). The generalisation the author draws is this map's constructive half: every automated destructive action — compaction, eviction, deletion — should be recorded and, where possible, reversible, which is precisely what none of the three provides. It arrives at the same requirement as the record-layer results above from the failure side rather than the audit side. [T1, suggestive]

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-08-28 — the map's first source about a record that passes, and why that is not reassuring. persona-execution-separation (Yisen Xi, arXiv, 2026-08-27, T1, single development/pilot case) probed a recovered pre-separation build for persona influence on a governed execution path, found none (5/5 structurally identical, 0/3 directed instructions honoured), and then established by explicit injection that the isolation was an accident of wiring rather than a rule — connect the persona to the same path and execution changes 2/2 on both models. Nothing in the audit chain, approval records or contract fields separated the two configurations. The rung this adds sits under the map's existing ladder: a record that cannot say whether a property is enforced leaves an auditor crediting the architecture for something a refactor can silently remove. The author declines the available win and reports no paired contrast, which is the reason to trust the negative. The self-evolution reading — persona text and execution semantics being one substrate to any governor — sits in harness-self-evolution, and the cross-model advancement finding in unattended-operation.
  • 2026-08-26 — the map is carved out of observability on the night its strand finally got a second source, and gets two of them. traceability-to-justifiability (Rashid Azarang, independent researcher, arXiv, 2026-08-21, T1) surveys 47 delivery platforms in two classes under one fixed three-label protocol, every cell graded twice with the second pass blind and every consulted page pinned by content hash — and across 188 double-graded cells finds no platform whose default record emits a content-addressed identity of the behavioral tuple, while 16 of 27 agent platforms are institutionalizing immutable nominal versioning in its place. Its second instrument computes realized assurance depth from a pipeline's published exhaust alone, from a hashed 1,116-response archive over a frozen 30-repository frame, and finds a verifiability hole: seven of fifteen attestation adopters publish source-only releases, so the binding their workflows declare has no artifact surface where they declare it — while five of the seven whose surface can be checked realize it end to end (the-declaration-and-the-evidence-live-on-different-surfaces, which sits primarily in ci-cd-for-agents). And harness-architectural-convergence (Jiahong Dai, NTU, arXiv, 2026-08-25, T1) reaches the same absence from the implementation side: three harnesses converged on five architectural elements and not one built a tamper-evident record an outsider can check without trusting the runtime. The two are independent in object and method and agree on the negative, which is why the map opens consolidating. The reliability detail worth recording: on the survey's load-bearing column, all four grader divergences ran against the paper's own claim — the hypothesis-aware pass graded default, the blind pass graded stricter — and in no tuple cell in either class did both passes agree on default.
  • 2026-08-20 — (inherited from observability) a governed decision record whose provenance field was present, well-formed and schema-valid on every line resolved 3,478 substitution occurrences to 62 unique ids, because a content-derived hash cannot distinguish two writes of the same value. Found by independent review rather than by the schema. The retraction that came with the repair is this map's sharpest boundary: what such a record promises is an identity commitment verifiable against a content-addressed store, not a reconstruction of the original bytes.
  • 2026-08-18 — (inherited from observability) the commit-boundary record is thinner than anyone assumed: an approval is stored as the submission of a review — the same event type as a comment — so the artifact carries that a party with authority acted and never that anyone formed a judgement, and the audit log defines none of the agent/bot/session fields it carries.
  • 2026-08-14 — (inherited from observability) five practitioner observability platforms version and trace the run and represent the configuration nowhere: eight governance capabilities marked "not provided as a first-class capability" in all five columns, with a conformance test showing one-hop dependency inspection finds 6 of 9 affected items where fixed-point propagation finds 9 of 9.

Open questions:

  • Every finding here is a bounded absence, and the strongest evidence available for this map's claim is therefore the weakest kind of evidence there is. What would move it is a positive: a platform or harness that ships the record, and an independent measurement of whether the binding holds. The survey's own falsifier is designed for exactly that — name one page that describes the capability.
  • The behavioral-rung gap is unmeasured anywhere, and the reason is the finding folded back on the method: the agent class emits no public exhaust surface on which a depth study could run, so realized assurance depth is measurable today only at the artifact layer of conventional pipelines. Nobody knows the prevalence of the problem this map is about.
  • Does external verifiability actually change an outcome, or only an argument? Every source here measures whether the record can express the claim. None measures a case where a checkable record caught something an operational one missed. The closest thing in the vault is the negative-gap repository — evidence flowing with no gate consuming it — which suggests the record and the decision are decoupled in both directions.
  • The registry-side half of the verifiability seam is unexamined: the artifacts whose bindings could not be checked on the release surface live on public package registries, and whether the binding holds there is unknown because the study did not look.
  • What does the temporal-order limb cost to compute? An authorization that covers the deployed thing but was granted before the evidence it rests on is a broken promotion, the check is a formal comparison over two records that already carry timestamps, and by the survey's account no platform or framework computes it.

See also:

  • observability — the parent. It asks what happened in a run and where it first went wrong; this map asks whether anyone outside the runtime can establish what ran at all. Sources routinely serve both, and the provenance-identifier and approval-artifact results are shared history rather than a clean partition.
  • verifier-independence — the closest constructive neighbour. That map's arrangement thesis assumes the checker can read a trustworthy record; this map measures whether the record is trustworthy to anyone but the process that wrote it. a-deterministic-terminal-check-beat-every-model-based-one sits at exactly the join: its verifier is independent of the agent's interpretation and not of the runtime that recorded the evidence, which is this map's line.
  • ci-cd-for-agents — where the promotion gate lives. The depth instrument's declared-versus-realized comparison is a CI/CD measurement; the record-expressiveness ceiling it runs into is this map's.
  • runtime-action-governance — an approval record that names an actor but not a judgement is an authority-continuity failure, and that map holds the enforcement side of the same act.
  • agent-harness-architecture — the harness is where the record is or is not written; the verifiability ladder is a harness design dimension before it is a governance one.
  • unattended-operation — the operator's stake. A record you can only reconstruct by trusting the process that wrote it is least useful in exactly the run where that process misbehaved.
