What we believe now:

  • Externalize execution status the same way you externalize a specification: a deterministic layer that tracks what has been observed, modified and attempted — and enforces it, not just informs of it — beats leaving the agent to infer status from raw history. (Added 2026-08-30, T1 — strong.) Ledger wraps an unmodified coding agent with two boundaries and zero model calls of its own: an inform path appends a compact state view before the agent acts, a govern path checks a proposed command against the ledger before it runs and can substitute a still-valid earlier result for re-execution, or nudge on likely repetition. All 500 SWE-bench Verified instances: GPT-5 mini 56.2%→64.2% Pass@1 at -28.9% cost, MiniMax M2.5 75.8%→81.0% at -31.8% cost (McNemar p<0.001 / p<0.01), and +3.4 points at -24.4% cost attached to OpenAI Codex through its hooks with model, tools and policy untouched (p<0.05) (explicit-execution-state-beats-implicit-history-inference). The ablation cleanly separates what each path buys: govern (enforcement) carries most of the resolution gain, inform (a summary) carries most of the efficiency gain — informing the model is not the same lever as constraining it, the same distinction this map's task-anchoring findings draw between graded and prose channels. Adjacent to, not redundant with, gate-the-edit-on-evidence-observed-not-claimed in context-retrieval-and-acquisition: that layer gates on whether required evidence was gathered at all, this one gates on whether an already-gathered result is still valid — haven't-looked and looked-but-stale are separate failure modes with the same shape of fix (a deterministic check with no model in the decision path).

  • A single context-management policy — pruning older tool output as the window fills, holding the model fixed — moves fail-to-pass fraction 21 points under a tight window, and the effect turns off once the window no longer needs pruning. (Added 2026-08-29, T1 — strong. Interaction edge; primary map harness-as-score-confound.) SWE-bench Verified: 28%→49% fail-to-pass fraction at a 20,480-token window, transferring to three other model families with no retuning; 69% vs 68.7% at a wide 262,144-token window (a-context-pruning-policy-changes-what-fixed-weights-can-solve). For sub-thread (2) this is a cleanly isolated, single-variable version of the pruning mechanism the map's decay/compaction thread has mostly measured under harder-to-separate conditions — direct evidence the mechanism works, conditioned on context pressure actually binding.

  • The first population-scale evidence that the durable context file is worth writing — and it shows up in the repository's condition months later, not in whether the run passed. Agents accelerate every team equally; the quality cost lands almost entirely on teams that commit no AI configuration at all. (Added 2026-08-27, T1 — strong, observational.) Re-estimating an existing staggered difference-in-differences panel of agent adopters inside strata defined by a script-readable property — does the repository commit rules, coding standards or tool configuration — splits a reported average into two populations. Agent-first stratum: cognitive complexity +52.7% unconfigured against +26.7% configured (2.0×), static-analysis warnings +24.1% against +14.0% (1.7×), with the four-level decomposition monotonic and the event-study gap widening after adoption rather than closing. Velocity shows no such penalty in either direction. Two things keep this honest: maturity is not randomly assigned, and unobserved model capability is named by the authors as a live alternative explanation; and even the configured stratum still takes a 27% complexity increase, so a rules file halves the damage and does not remove it. What the study measures is the presence of artifacts, never their content. See a-few-pages-of-committed-markdown-halve-the-quality-cost.

  • A constraint can survive a handoff as a sentence and stop being a constraint. Ordinary compression — not an aggressive setting, the normal one — turns a blocker that must be resolved before execution into a caveat that merely informs it, and the risk language is usually what survives while the prerequisite, the named authority or the fallback is what disappears. (Added 2026-08-27, T1 — strong.) 1,296 controlled episodes, every one conditioned on the upstream agent having identified the blocker correctly, so nothing here is a detection failure: direct-handoff controls preserve 100.0% with 0.0% forbidden action; normal handoff compression reaches 100.0% deactivation and 54.2% forbidden action, and deactivation is already at its ceiling there — going more aggressive adds nothing. Four other everyday transformations do the same thing (ownership deferral is worst at 23.3% preservation and +60.8 points of forbidden action). This names the variable compaction-drops-the-safety-rule-at-the-rate-it-drops-the-log and a-loaded-skill-is-not-a-retained-requirement were both circling: semantic availability ⇏ operational preservation. Restoring all four state fields recovers it completely; a downstream gate does not (containment-at-the-endpoint-leaves-the-artifact-broken). See a-handoff-can-keep-the-sentence-and-drop-the-stop.

  • How much context to carry across a boundary has no single right answer — it inverts with who is receiving. Dropping the sender's trajectory while keeping its edits is the BEST interface when a weak model hands to a strong one and the WORST when a strong model hands to a weak one, in both model families and at every difficulty level. (Added 2026-08-27, T1 — strong.) Escalation, trajectory-drop against full transfer: quality recovery 47% → 64% (Claude) and 36% → 84% (GPT). Downshift, same move: 50% → 28% and 79% → 53%. The mechanism separates cleanly and is what makes it portable — carrying a weak model's trajectory into a strong one inflates each receiver call 2.2× at unchanged step count (reading tax), while dropping a strong model's trajectory makes the weak receiver take 1.6–2.0× more steps at unchanged cost per call (rework). This is a scope condition on prune-and-summarize-beats-full-history, which established that less context wins — measured throughout with one model reading its own history. A compaction step that runs identically in both directions is optimizing one of them by accident. See what-crosses-at-a-handoff-should-flip-with-the-direction.

  • Code context compression needs two properties prose compression does not — extractive, because an editing agent needs exact strings, and intent-conditioned, because what matters changes turn to turn — and a 4B model with both compresses roughly twice as hard as a frontier prompt compressor without a proportional quality loss. (Added 2026-08-26, T1 — strong.) Extractiveness is measured, not asserted: 96.0% of emitted identifiers, paths and numbers already appear in the input, holding at 96.2% off the training distribution (compress-agent-context-extractively-and-condition-it-on-intent). And the intent-conditioning result is more specific than "task-aware compression helps": told the current task, the model retains differently rather than more, choosing which lines survive inside a segment it was going to keep anyway (retained lines +0.067 more intent-relevant, paired 95% CI [+0.056, +0.078]) — which is how twice the compression avoids costing twice the quality, since the extra aggression lands on stale context the current intent does not touch. On all 300 SWE-bench Lite instances: 25.7% of original size against 50.2% and 61.9% for the two GPT compressors, retaining 86.5% of uncompressed solve quality (27.8% / 89.3% on the line-numbered input real agents produce). The paired statement is the strong one — 122/300 resolved uncompressed against 109 compressed, 47 discordant pairs, exact McNemar p = 0.079 — and the authors refuse to over-read it in either direction: "not significant is not the same as no effect," the point estimate is still a net loss of 13 instances. This extends goal-hinted-pruning-beats-generic-compression with a named mechanism and a recoverability property this map has wanted since compaction-selection-is-all-or-nothing: [SEG]-structured output lets the surrounding gateway pull any original span back byte-exact, so the compression is lossy on the wire and recoverable when the agent needs the untouched bytes. Two operator-facing limits travel with it. Identifier retention is a tendency, not a guarantee — roughly 60% of identifier-like tokens in a segment do not survive, by design — so run a presence check for the current target identifier on the compressed output and fall back to the original segment on failure. And the dominant known gap is under-dropping (drop recall 0.24 against an always-keep policy's 0.59), so there is compression still on the table rather than too much being thrown away. Single-shot harness, one API call, no tools, no turns — it bounds comprehension under compression and says nothing about multi-turn cost; Python-heavy training distribution; four importance levels collapse to two effective bands, so a deployment cannot dial compression per level. [T1, strong]

  • Compaction is type-blind: your safety rules decay at the same rate as your debugging transcript, and asking the compactor to keep them does not work — the fix is to classify each item before compressing and verify afterwards. (Added 2026-08-25, T1 — strong.) Under Claude Code's production /compact on Sonnet 4.6, with a prompt that says "keep every safety rule and procedural command verbatim", constraint recall is 0.53 after one round and 0.10 after five; across all eight type-blind strategies tested the best single-round recall is 0.53 at 50% compression, 0.39 at 25%, 0.24 at 10%, and the decay appears in every LLM compactor family and every structural baseline (compaction-drops-the-safety-rule-at-the-rate-it-drops-the-log). Two analyses make this structural rather than a bad sample: a five-type model covers 97% of instructions across 396,934 real agent-configuration artifacts from 54,628 repositories, so there is no large uncovered class where the cliff could hide; and real safety text is declarative — 49.8% of openFDA boxed warnings and 61.1% of LegalBench safety clauses, against ~22% imperative in both (second-judge κ = 0.92) — which is why it reads to a compressor like a fact and to a regex like nothing. The remedy is a decision made earlier, not a better prompt. Pin constraints and procedures by type at indexing time and recall holds at 1.00 / 0.95 / 0.80 at 50/25/10% and 0.96 across five rounds, at zero model tokens; re-inserting constraints with a regex after compression closes only about half the gap, and only the pre-compression label closes the rest. On τ-bench retail the pinned policy beat not only truncation but the full uncompacted policy — 37.7% against 29.2% and 28.6%, paired McNemar p = 0.005 / 0.003 over 329 pairs — with the gain concentrated on the smallest model, which is the mechanism: a weaker model benefits most from not having to locate the governing rule inside a wall of policy (pin-the-rule-before-you-compress-not-after). And the compaction step cannot be its own receipt: on the configurations too dense to compress safely, the same runs without a post-compaction verifier reported apparent 1.00 recall while silently dropping a mean 57% of the constraints (a-compactor-that-audits-itself-reports-recall-it-did-not-achieve). Three practice lines fall out: write rules in imperative form, decide the type before compaction rather than after, and run a deterministic presence check over the compacted output. Ceiling, stated by the authors first: per-type retention preserves no more than the classifier detects — the residual 4.5-point gap on the medical benchmark is exactly the nine declarative items it missed at 0.93 recall.

  • Delivery is not the last stage: with the fact provably in the window, the largest remaining failure is the agent acting on a superseded version of it — and the fix is bookkeeping about validity, delivered at answer time, not a bigger window or a bigger memory system. (Added 2026-08-21, T1 — strong.) Measured on an oracle split where recall is 1.0 by construction, so no residual failure can be retrieval in disguise, judges from two model families both assign 44.4% of what remains to state drift, and it concentrates where a decision integrates multiple sources — multi-session questions drift at 71.4% against 25.0% for single-fact updates. Turning on the reasoning trace does not help (84.0% → 76.0%, McNemar p=0.34), so this is not a shortfall the model thinks its way out of (state-drift-survives-perfect-retrieval). And more context is not the remedy: on a benchmark built to isolate the mechanism, holding the entire transcript scores 0.149 on both backbones — level with BM25, below dense retrieval — which is the cleanest counterexample this map has to the standing intuition that a large enough window dissolves memory problems. What pays is narrow and cheap. In a controlled ablation, extraction of facts alone recovers almost nothing (0.174); marking that a value was superseded is the single largest step (0.174 → 0.298); instructing the answerer to recompute rather than quote a derived quantity is worth another 6 points; and aggressive dependency propagation over-fires, costing 12.5 points on scenarios where the anchored answer was still right, such that removing it leaves the system marginally better (mark-the-supersession-not-just-the-fact). The deployable form carries none of the machinery: a single answer-time transformation that replaces the answer call the backend was making anyway, writes the value chain for every slot the question touches before committing to an answer — which blocks post-hoc justification, the same move as anchor-a-verification-claim-to-the-content-it-was-checked-against — and resolves under four precedence rules. It improves all six backends by +31.7 to +66.6 points, of which a length-, cost- and call-matched generic-extraction control attributes +15 to +32 to the structure rather than the extra text; and it beats the full persistent store on the same scenarios, which says the store earns its keep only when the transcript does not fit. One condition governs all of it: on the weaker of two backbones every method drifts at 61–66% — full context, Mem0, BM25 and the state tracker alike — and only the stronger one converts an explicit state representation into correct answers (the-memory-layer-only-separates-on-a-capable-answerer). So a memory design's benchmark score is a property of the pair, and a low drift rate can also mean the system rarely produced a plausible answer at all — which is why in-pool engagement has to be reported beside it.

  • Availability at the moment of the edit decides the outcome; distance from the edit does not — and this map has never separated the two. (Added 2026-08-18, T1, strong — and the reason this map is now contested.) Model a task as a graph of coupled facts: at each edit a required fact arrives from recent context or from parametric memory, and whatever neither covers is coherence debt. Withhold facts by construction and damage adds rather than compounds — passed tests fall 32 / 24 / 16 / 8 / 0 against a linear prediction of 32 / 24 / 16 / 8 / 0, maximum deviation zero tests across nine cells and two removal modes. Vary only a supplied fact's distance and success is flat out to 128,000 characters, while withholding the same fact floors the same task. State sixteen invariants once and they hold at every position across up to 96 sequentially revealed tasks and ~140,000 accumulated tokens, never reread (availability-decides-the-edit-and-distance-does-not). The substitution half of this map is confirmed and stated as settled: 154 of 154 novel closed-book trials score 0/12 (Wilson 95% upper bound 2.4%), and putting the same rules in the prompt lifts 299 of 300 matched trials to ≥9/12. What is in doubt is the mechanism the map has been attributing effects to. The authors decline the strong reading themselves and say why: presence and residency coincide here because nothing evicts, so they measure presence and bound residency rather than confirming it. Until one design manipulates both, an intervention that improves where a fact sits and one that improves whether it is there have been priced against each other exactly once, and only the second moved.

  • A stale convention file costs more than no convention file — deleting an out-of-date rules file beats leaving it in place. (Added 2026-08-18, T1, strong — the sharpest practice-facing result this map has had.) Where a written standard and working code in the same repository state the same fact differently, the agent follows the document, in all 39 trials, across three model tiers and two harnesses (Wilson 95% [0.91, 1.00]) — and inverting the workload rules out "it just writes sensible code": with the document demanding the worse practice the agent divides money into floats and calls the naive clock. Priced over 3,385 scored decisions, ten trials per condition: a correct standard yields 100% better-form decisions, code alone 33%, and a standard demanding the worse form 0% — both endpoints with zero variance (a-stale-convention-file-costs-more-than-no-file). So a rules file is not advice the agent weighs; it is an override whose authority does not decay with its accuracy, and a wrong one destroys the 33% floor a repository has for free. This is what the-project-rule-file-is-written-once-and-abandoned costs (67.3% never edited after creation), and the reason deletion-hazard-falls-because-the-rationale-decayed matters operationally rather than aesthetically. Open: the cells do not separate authority from modality or read order, which decides whether the fix is "keep the convention file current" or "stop shipping prose conventions at all".

  • A skill works by anchoring a procedure, not by supplying knowledge — and the same distillation that makes it portable installs an applicability decision the agent gets wrong ten times more often than it gets anything else wrong. (Added 2026-08-17, T1, strong — the first study to hold the prior experience byte-fixed and vary only its packaging.) The census told us how many skill files exist; this tells us what one does to a run. Same trajectories, distilled into a SKILL.md or delivered as cleaned workflow traces: the skill wins by +6.06 points (95% CI [+0.76, +11.36]) on identical source material, and two controls kill the "any compact hint would do" reading (an instruction-derived plan 47.7%, a test-first template 59.2%, the skill 79.2% on the same 26 tasks). The mechanism is the surprise. Over 528 paired triples, procedural anchoring accounts for 65.7% of skill effects and explicit knowledge injection for 4.5% — and the failures that vanish are exactly the operational ones: environment and infrastructure failures 5.3% → 0.2%, output-format mismatches 7.4% → 3.2%, service-lifecycle failures 2.7% → 0.8%, while algorithmic logic errors (8.3% → 7.4%) and static-verification-without-runtime (12.5% → 11.7%) do not move at all (skills-work-by-anchoring-procedure-not-by-adding-knowledge). So the write rule is narrow: put the setup dance and the always-wrong quoting in a skill, and expect nothing from it on the part of the task that needs reformulation. The cost of that abstraction is a new failure class, not merely fewer old ones — misapplied-or-ignored guidance appears in 10.0% of skill runs against 0.8% with no skill at all, and invocation/applicability failures rise to 78 of 528 triples against 19, because a skill is not self-executing and something has to decide whether it applies (the-skill-adds-an-applicability-decision-the-agent-gets-wrong). Raw traces fail the opposite way, on budget exhaustion (10.6% against 1.7%), so distillation is the choice to trade a volume problem for a judgment problem — and the mitigation is a condition line, not more content. One construction detail with teeth for anyone planning to mine failed runs: withholding the success/failure labels while distilling barely matters on an all-success pool and is worth 0.7462 against 0.4000 once failures are in it.   • In the wild the project rule file is not maintained — two thirds are never edited after the commit that created them, so the surface this map has been measuring as load-bearing is, for most repositories, scaffolding nobody returns to. (Added 2026-08-14, T1, strong.)   • A skill distilled from a developer's own history helps no more than one distilled from a stranger's; the pooled skill is the one that works, and personalization only turns on at roughly six relevant sessions. (Added 2026-08-14, T1, suggestive, null-effect.) Four conditions on the same held-out sessions with everything else fixed: no skill 65.02, the developer's own skill 65.99 (+0.97, p = .399), a randomly chosen other developer's skill 65.94 (+0.92, p = .451), the skill pooled across all thirteen developers 68.80 (+3.78, p = .063, the only win rate above half).   • Which surface a rule is written on decides whether it survives contradiction, and the ordering is not prompt depth — the project file ties with the system prompt, while skill and tool descriptions lose the conflicts they are easiest to obey. (Added 2026-08-13, T1, strong.)   • The context file has no steady state. Instruction count more than triples over a file's own lifetime, the only deletion event that happens at scale is a wholesale rewrite, and growth resumes faster afterwards — because deleting an instruction requires knowing why it was added, and that reason decays faster than the instruction does. (Added 2026-08-12, T1, strong.) Everything this map has said about authored context has been a claim about content at a moment: what belongs in the file (agents-md-is-a-map-not-a-manual), what a bad one costs (generic-context-files-do-not-move-correctness, generated-context-files-cost-more-than-they-help), how a good one loses force inside a run (a-standing-policy-decays-into-one-more-retrieved-source, a-loaded-skill-is-not-a-retained-requirement). This is the first measurement of the trajectory, on 247,694 individual instruction lifetimes across 1,867 repositories: +226% in count over a file's lifetime at +4.9 net instructions per commit, median file ending at 39 instructions — past the size at which instruction-following is independently known to degrade — with 77.3% of instruction deaths arriving inside a bulldozing rewrite, after which files regrow at 4.9%/commit against 4.1% before (an-agentic-context-file-grows-until-someone-bulldozes-it). The mechanism is identified rather than asserted, by the sign of a hazard: staleness predicts deletion becoming more likely with age, and the measured log-hazard is −0.032/commit [−0.047, −0.019], falling further with the number of maintainers who have touched the file (β = −0.021, z = −11.7) — a prediction only imperfect recall makes, and a gamma-frailty refit absorbs just 30.8% of it (deletion-hazard-falls-because-the-rationale-decayed). The economics are the point: adding is O(1) and diffuse, and an honest deletion audit is O(2^n) subset probes because redundancy defeats one-at-a-time testing — so append-only is the correct behaviour for a maintainer whose rationale has evaporated, not a lazy one. The fix is a second channel the executor never sees: an inline comment recording the failure, the hypothesis and the outcome cuts excess size +211.3% → +1.4% at parity satisfaction and lifts real-world instruction-following +11.6 pp (23.1% relative), while comment-shaped noise does nothing and a narrative of attempts without their outcomes is the worst arm of all at +70.0% (write-the-rationale-beside-the-instruction). Two readings this map should carry. It is prevent-context-growth-upstream-not-downstream with a mechanism and a price — the upstream lever is provenance, and it costs one line at write time. And the capability sweep inverts the usual expectation: the uncommented arm's excess rises +67.7% → +571.9% across three maintainer tiers, so a more capable maintainer ratchets harder, which is the relevant fact for anyone whose context files are now maintained by agents. The controlled arm runs on covers of two or three instructions against a real median of 39, so nothing here shows the ratchet halted at real-file scale; and the author's own warning is the operating rule — writing a rationale is safe, automating deletions from one is not.

  • A context artifact that works here is not an artifact that works there: a frozen playbook carries assumptions about workflow, target capability, decoding regime and runtime budget, and where the runtime assumption breaks it keeps its task heuristics and loses its stopping rule — accuracy holds while cost triples. (Added 2026-08-09, T1, strong.) This map has spent a month on what to write down — persist the contract not the conversation, abstract trajectories rather than replaying them, expect a tax on generated artifacts (persist-the-contract-not-the-conversation, abstract-past-trajectories-raw-ones-do-not-transfer, generated-context-files-cost-more-than-they-help, skills-regress-as-much-as-they-gain). This is the next question and the one teams actually face: the artifact exists and works — does it move? Under matched conditions, clearly yes: greedy decoding on ALFWorld is positive on every target, up to +23.6 pp, with shorter trajectories, and it beats five canonical few-shot demonstrations by +10.8 pp [+2.7, +18.9] at near-matched prompt budget. In a heterogeneous service-agent setting the same protocol yields a prespecified +1.72 pp [+0.25, +2.97] average matched-domain advantage and almost nothing else — global Holm over 135 route hypotheses retains one (a-playbook-transfers-only-as-far-as-its-assumptions, correct-a-transfer-grid-for-multiplicity-before-reading-it). Three things this changes here. The robustness surface is three-way — decoding regime × target capability × artifact specificity — and the sign flips inside it: at t=0.3 the detailed artifact turns harmful on the 7B target while staying robust on the 32B, where the compact one collapses instead. Nobody validating a context artifact varies temperature, and this says the sign of the effect lives there. The failure is a false premise, not decay — distinct from a-standing-policy-decays-into-one-more-retrieved-source and a-loaded-skill-is-not-a-retained-requirement: the instruction retains full force and is simply wrong for where it landed. And stopping breaks before accuracy does: moving one playbook from a 32K to a 128K runtime leaves Pass@1 flat while search calls go 5.9 → 15.7, tool calls 10.4 → 24.7 and the overlong-trajectory rate 8% → 25%, with Pass@3 down 16 points (an-imported-artifact-breaks-stopping-before-it-breaks-accuracy). The last two are the ones nobody checks and the ones that fail first. Worth noting the study also runs the control this literature usually skips: a same-length generic placebo stays at baseline while the real playbook reproduces its direction, so the transferred content is doing the work (a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm).   • Compaction now has a price as well as a fidelity problem, measured on 13.5M production sessions — and it lands on exactly the sessions this map worries about most. (Added 2026-08-08, T1, strong.) Every compaction result here has been about what survives the rewrite (compaction-selection-is-all-or-nothing, compaction-can-launder-killed-process-output, context-renewal-does-not-discharge-a-completed-obligation).   • Seventh clause: the re-derive rule reaches what the agent produced, not only what it read — a verification result has to carry the content it was checked against, or it survives the change that falsifies it. (Added 2026-08-06, T1, strong.) Since 2026-07-28 this map's rule has ended re-derive state from its source rather than carrying your reading of it (re-derive-file-state-instead-of-storing-snapshots) — established on files the agent reads, where the source sits on disk and re-reading costs no model call. The claims an agent makes have no such source. A prose handoff note records that a behaviour was verified and where, never what it was verified against, and after an upstream change every import still resolves, the project still compiles, and one entry in a lookup table now holds a different value, so the claim is false and nothing in the workspace says so. What is missing announces itself; what is stale does not. Binding each claim to a content hash of the specific sub-path artifact used to establish it — the table entry, the config key, the re-exported constant, resolved through alias chains to its leaf definition rather than to its containing file — turns that into a query. Measured on generated repositories with behaviour-to-artifact ground truth known by construction (42 sessions, seven worlds of 96 behaviours, three memory conditions all seeded with complete correct records so only record content differs): on the smaller model, anchored memory beats both prose notes and no memory in all seven worlds, exact paired Wilcoxon p = 0.0156 each — the floor the design can reach — at median F1 1.000 against 0.270 and 0.286, with prose and no memory statistically indistinguishable from each other (anchor-a-verification-claim-to-the-content-it-was-checked-against). Granularity is the mechanism, not a detail: flagging every behaviour that imports from a changed file marks 81–91 of 96 where the true answer is 14–18, a 5.1× over-invalidation nobody will work through. Two design rules ride along and both are this vault's own findings arriving as types rather than habits: no model output may enter at PROVEN — every LLM-proposed edge is provisional and promotion requires deterministic re-extraction (treat-agent-output-as-a-claim-not-lifecycle-state) — and unprovable is a terminal state, not low confidence, kept separate from what to do with the artifact, so an agent can say it cannot verify something without deleting working code (unprovable-is-a-terminal-state-not-low-confidence). Across 42 sessions with three definitions deliberately withheld per world, no session fabricated withheld content. The exploratory half is the one to watch: the stronger model's controls reconstructed the artifact baseline inside the session — parsing the reference, resolving aliases, diffing against the port — and the smaller model's controls attempted the same and mostly failed to finish it cleanly, which suggests anchored memory is buying an externalized re-derivation rather than a new capability. Held as the authors hold it: a hypothesis, not a result.   • The delivery rule applies to the codebase, not just the contract — and it separates a variable this map had confounded: the problem was never persistence, it was pinning. (Added 2026-07-28, T1, strong.) Every production coding agent stores a file read and its observation together in an append-only trajectory, so the file's bytes at the moment of reading become an immutable history entry. Files are mutable; histories are not. Two measured consequences: 26% of one model's file reads (834 of 3,207) were re-reads of a file already in the trajectory, each appending another full copy and compounding the growth that caused the re-read; and edits fail against text that has already changed, costing a re-read plus a diagnosis cycle. Replacing the read tool with a registry plus a re-read before every reasoning cycle — at most one copy of each file, always current, by construction — cuts input tokens 9.3–50.4%, reasoning cycles 4.9–37.3%, prompt length 15–32%, cost 8.3–50.3% ($5.27 → $2.62 per instance on the strongest model) and wall-clock up to 40%, at comparable pass@1 across four models and 573 instances (re-derive-file-state-instead-of-storing-snapshots). The sync is nearly free — the working set averages 2.66–7.81 files and a local read is milliseconds against an LLM call. The reconciliation matters more than the numbers. On 2026-07-24 this map recorded that pinning a resolved reference — the intuitive "resolve once, carry it forward" fix — eliminates drift and amplifies wrong actions 3.0× (up to 8.5×), worst on the strongest model, taking strict workflow success from 29.2% to 0.0% (persisted-state-without-revalidation-amplifies-errors). Read alone that indicts persistence. Read together, the operative variable is pinned versus re-derived: an append-only trajectory is a pinned resolution, and re-deriving from the authoritative source each cycle wins on every axis measured. So the rule gains a fifth clause: persist the contract, deliver it, re-deliver it at the decision it governs, price what the resident half does when unwanted — and re-derive state from its source rather than carrying your reading of it. Where the source is a file on disk, that re-derivation costs no model call at all. Two corollaries: this is upstream of every compression method rather than a competitor (combining it with sliding windows, summarization, observation masking or trajectory reduction beats either alone on all four — 1,453K → 796K input tokens alone, 644K–685K combined), and the gain is largest on the strongest model because stronger models run longer trajectories.   • Added context is not free even when it is right: every skill library measured broke tasks the bare agent already solved, and the resident half acts whether or not it is invoked. (Added 2026-07-27, T1 — the cost side of the map's delivery rule.) 5,832 paired task-condition runs (486 tasks × 4 conditions × 3 model-harness stacks, only the skill library varying) produce 553 gains against 324 regressions — regressions offset 59% of gross gains — with no condition reaching zero regressions and per-condition counts running 2 to 41. Two mechanisms matter for practice. (1) Osmosis — a skill's triggering description stays in the system prompt at every step and changes behaviour with the body never opened: one worked case has an agent answer correctly with no skills and incorrectly, identically, under three libraries none of which it invoked, with the shift tracking words in two descriptions. It accounts for 14 of 81 and 70 of 243 regressions on the two benchmarks — and it helps more than it harms (84–100% of gains on low-invocation stacks are invocation-free), so the point is that presence is an unconditional intervention in both directions (a-skill-description-acts-without-being-invoked). Open questions:

  • Does a stale rule file hurt, or merely fail to help? (Opened 2026-08-14.) The census establishes that most project rule files are frozen at repository-scaffolding time (the-project-rule-file-is-written-once-and-abandoned) and the precedence work establishes that this surface wins conflicts (a-skill-description-loses-the-conflicts-it-wins-alone). Nobody has joined them: no study measures an agent working a repository whose rule file describes an earlier version of that repository. The plausible failure — a high-precedence instruction actively overriding correct current behaviour — is strictly worse than the inert-file case generic-context-files-do-not-move-correctness measured, and it is the more common situation in the wild by the census's own numbers.
  • Where is the crossover between a pooled skill and a personal one? (Opened 2026-08-14.) pool-skills-across-developers-instead-of-personalizing-them finds pooling ahead overall and personalization ahead by +5.67 once six task-relevant sessions have accumulated (n = 12). The obvious design — a pooled base with a thin personal overlay, or retrieval of the relevant personal rules only — is proposed by the authors as future work and tested by nobody. The threshold also has to be re-measured as interaction histories lengthen, since the null here is explicitly a data-volume null and not a design one.
  • UPDATED 2026-07-30 — the middle arm ran, and now the third arm is the only one left. The question posed 2026-07-29 was a three-arm test: policy resident only; policy re-injected at the decisions its clauses govern; policy compiled into a deterministic pre-dispatch guard. RepoComplianceBench ran the first two on 106 real instances across four shipped agent–harness pairs, and split the answer by rule type — re-delivery takes procedural obligations from 17–40% to 76–100% and leaves prohibitions at 0%, recovering only to 0–23% even when an oracle names the violated clause (re-delivery-governs-procedure-not-permission). So the clause survives with a scope condition. What is still unrun is the arm that matters for the prohibition half: compile the same clause into a guard evaluated before dispatch, and measure both sides — violation rate and the cost, in blocked legitimate work and in operator interruptions, that every prior gate in this vault has bought its safety with and none has priced (permission-prompts-fail-open-under-fatigue). The instance set now exists, which makes this the cheapest decisive experiment in the map.
  • NEW 2026-07-30 — does better-served context fix more bugs, or only cost less? CodeNib cuts trajectory tokens 50–87% at preserved localization and reports no resolve-rate arm at all; Agent Retrieval Bench measured a 27–35% acquisition miss rate and explicitly declined to connect it to repair outcomes. Two well-measured results a week apart, both stopping at the same line. The map now has a large, quantified upstream improvement with no established downstream value, and the experiment that would close it is not expensive — run one agent on one SWE benchmark with the serving tier as the only variable and report resolve rate beside the token saving. Until someone does, "better context engineering" is a cost claim in this map, not a capability claim.
  • NEW 2026-07-29 — how much of the 27–35% retrieval miss rate survives a good context engine, and does closing it move patch success at all? Agent Retrieval Bench deliberately refuses to answer the second half — it reports no correlation with repair outcomes, because no mapping from its workflow-derived rows to executable instances exists, and its own proxy-validity check puts budgeted context yield against agent gold-touch at Spearman 0.129–0.237. So this map has a large, well-measured upstream failure with no established downstream cost. The oracle arm (+0.3115 File F1, every miss rescued) bounds the headroom for context acquisition; nothing bounds the headroom for the patch.
  • The single-stack caveat is now largely closed (Anthropic + GPT-5 + Cursor all converge), but the effect sizes stay domain-specific — the 47-point decay is data-analysis agents, the +20.6-point prune/summarize win is one enterprise expense workflow. The direction replicates cross-org; a single general effect-size for "good context engineering" still doesn't exist.
  • No head-to-head on when to reach for compaction vs note-taking vs sub-agents beyond qualitative selection guidance — the Less Context sweep tests pruning ± summarization but not the sub-agent or external-note variants against each other. This gap got sharper and more urgent on 2026-07-11: it is no longer a matter of optimization but of sign. One T1 says memory scaffolds hurt all 10 models; another says a spec-state tracker recovers 90% of a faithfulness gap. Run a spec-tracking layer against a history-memory scaffold on one task set, and the map either gains its most useful distinction or loses one of its headline recommendations. Nothing else in this map is worth measuring first. 2026-07-15 gets closer than anything before it — one architecture measures contract-memory (96%), full-history (71%), and no-memory (79%) in the same experiment, and the contract-vs-history gap is exactly the map's hypothesis. What keeps it from closing the question: it is one group's enterprise data-artifact workload, not a long-horizon SWE benchmark, and the retained/discarded sets are bundled (four contract categories vs "session reasoning") rather than isolated to the single spec-vs-history variable. The remaining ask is that same split on code repair, with the categories ablated.
  • NEW 2026-07-14 — the tension may not be about memory at all; it may be about scoring. Five data points now share one shape, and the newest reframes the other four. If agents honor context in proportion to how task-anchored it is, then "structured contract-state helps, unstructured self-recall hurts" might be a special case of "context that bears on the scored objective is used; context that doesn't is ignored or is pure overhead." That is a sharper and more falsifiable hypothesis, and it makes a prediction the existing studies can almost test: the same instruction should be obeyed when it is in the acceptance criteria and ignored when it is not. Put a logging requirement in the graded task spec versus in AGENTS.md, hold everything else fixed, and measure compliance. That experiment is cheaper than the spec-vs-history head-to-head below and would tell us whether this map has been describing memory or describing incentives.
  • How much of "context rot" is actually human interaction pattern? SLUMP holds requirements constant and varies only whether they arrive whole or across ~60 turns, and the incremental delivery loses on 16/20 tasks. That is a degradation this map has been attributing to window mechanics, and part of it may be attributable to how the developer talks. Nobody has decomposed the two.
