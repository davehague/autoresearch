The harness as score confound — map

Forked from evaluation-integrity on 2026-08-06 (bounded auto-fork; the parent was at 56 dated bullets with three declared sub-threads, and had carried [fork-due] since 2026-08-03). The parent asks whether a number describes the agent — is the benchmark clean, is the label right, is the judge honest, does the proxy rank the thing it claims to rank. This map asks the separable question of whether the number describes the agent or the apparatus it ran inside: the scaffold, the container, the timeouts and retry policy, the wrappers on the plumbing between the runner and the provider, and — as of tonight — the inference backend that renders the chat template and calls the kernel. The boundary is is the measurement of the right object versus is the measurement of the object valid. A benchmark can be uncontaminated, correctly labelled and honestly judged, and still report a number that is 8 to 21 points of somebody else's scaffold. Four claims and two dated arrivals moved across intact; two phrases were repointed where the moved text referred to the parent's own catalogue, and three arrivals that carry substantial non-harness content stayed in the parent and are cross-referenced here.

Status: consolidating (inherited from the parent and independently supportable: five groups — a benchmark vendor publishing a two-harness grid, a benchmark author publishing a four-harness matrix unprompted, a single-author study auditing its own plumbing after a false positive, a disclosure audit of twelve benchmark papers, and now a fully crossed inference-backend study — agree that the apparatus is a term of the order of the effect being reported, with no live objection to how any of it was measured. Held below consensus because the two largest grids are two and four harnesses respectively on one benchmark each, the inference-backend result is at ~1B scale on non-agentic tasks, and the field's practice has not moved: this vault is still ingesting single-harness leaderboard comparisons weekly. Held again 2026-08-07 on four more arrivals — three of them measuring the term on quantities other than a resolve rate (a security detection rate, a monitor's AUROC, a long-horizon obligation score) and one, DCAS, reaching a layer the disclosure remedy cannot repair, since a scaffold baked into fine-tuning weights is not a reporting failure. That is the first evidence here that the map's own prescription is incomplete, and it is one study. Held again 2026-08-16 on the strongest single confirmation the map has received — the first design that attributes the apparatus term to a named layer instead of detecting it by swapping a scaffold. It deepens the core claim without disturbing it, and it argues against promotion rather than for it: by showing that a whole-scaffold swap cannot attribute a reversal to any mechanism, it puts the map's own dominant method in question, and consensus here requires the instrument to have survived scrutiny rather than the result to have been repeated. Held again 2026-08-19 on the first arrival that set out to measure the apparatus term deliberately rather than stumbling on it — and found it five times the model-generation term on the same benchmark. It confirms the core claim from the constructive side and argues against promotion on the same ground as the last hold: the measured object is a runtime bundled with an evolved benchmark-adapted profile, with no compute-matched control and no decomposition inside the layer, so the size of the term is confirmed and its attribution is not. Held again 2026-08-20 on the first arrival to measure the apparatus term on the stability of a score rather than on its level: robustness to a semantics-preserving rewrite of the codebase ranks models differently under every scaffold and every benchmark, while the capability ranking is untouched. It extends the claim to a second quantity without putting the existing measurements in doubt, and its own bounds — two scaffolds, a deliberately non-adversarial sampler, 54 instances chosen to be solvable — are exactly why it does not promote the map. Held again 2026-08-22 on the first arrival to catch the term travelling through post-training: a released post-trained coding model gains 3.4 points under the harness its authors report and loses ground under another, where the untuned newer base model it was built from scores higher. That is the map's core claim in its most consequential form — a published model-selection result reversing sign with the apparatus — and it is also, unusually, produced by a group measuring a competitor's artifact rather than its own. It confirms rather than promotes: the authors explicitly decline to isolate a cause, it is one policy family on one benchmark under one training recipe, and the mechanism question the map has been asking since the attribution result of 2026-08-16 is once again left open. Held again 2026-08-24 on three arrivals, one of which is the first credible limit on where this map's term lives. Six root-cause-analysis frameworks under a fixed model span 37 points — the largest apparatus term this map has recorded — but the three that run an open-ended adaptive investigation span only 2.0 points among themselves, while swapping the backbone model on one of them moves it 10.8. So the term is not distributed across scaffold details; it is concentrated at one architectural boundary, and once a design is on the adaptive side of it a harness swap resolves almost nothing. That is a scope condition on the map's comparison advice rather than on its claim — gridding four adaptive harnesses will report a small term and be read as evidence the term is small — and it converges with the optimizer-capability result already here, which found the model moving gain 1.8× more than the harness inside a similarly narrow band. It does not promote or demote: every framework number is a single run the authors themselves label indicative, on one topology, with the two backbones differing 22× in cost per case. The second arrival is the map's first constructive result — an intervention that shrinks the apparatus term rather than detecting it. Compiling a prose procedure into declared per-step read/write artifacts halves the cross-executor spread (32 points → 16), takes all-six-executors-agree from 48% → 80%, takes all-ten-runs-correct from 16% → 72%, and holds 100% of previously-passing cases under injected request errors and context compaction where prose drops to 80% — with a stated mechanism (per-agent input tokens −63%) that is the first partial account here of why the term exists. The third extends the term to a layer nobody had checked, the input artifact: the same specification is worth 0.73 Token F1 to the agent that wrote it and 0.035 to another, with the reverse transfer improving, so knowledge preservation does not imply operational-interpretation preservation. It is carried as an existence proof — no intervals, no significance tests, near-zero runnability throughout — and does not bear weight. Held again 2026-08-25, on the crossed direction this map has almost no evidence in: fix the apparatus, swap the model. A harness discovered by automatic search under one model keeps +5.6 pp of its +9.0 pp when the task agent's model is replaced by a substantially weaker one from the same family. Read one way that is reassuring — a harness is a designable artifact rather than a per-model fitting. Read as measurement, it is the harder version of the map's disclosure argument, not the softer one: an undisclosed harness contributes a systematic, transferable offset to every row it appears in, so it does not average out across a leaderboard. It confirms rather than promotes — one benchmark, one model family, one direction, an appendix figure with no interval — and it leaves the arm that would decide the matter unrun: a harness tuned against a weak model, applied to a strong one. Held again 2026-08-29 on the map's first fully single-variable demonstration: one model, weights untouched, only the context-management policy changed. A harness that mechanically prunes older tool output as the window fills moves fail-to-pass fraction 28%→49% on the same benchmark cell where the control harness scores 28% — and the effect transfers unmodified to three other model families (+20, +6, +15pp). It confirms rather than promotes because it also supplies the map's cleanest scope condition yet: at a wide-enough window the gap collapses to noise (69% vs 68.7%), so the term is conditional on context pressure being the binding constraint, not a constant tax on every run. Held again 2026-08-31 on an industry system paper that supplies the map's cleanest recent case of the unattributed apparatus term: a harness beats the strongest same-model prior systems by 3.4pp (SWE-bench Verified) and 3.39pp (Terminal-Bench 2.1), names five distinct runtime mechanisms as candidate causes, and — by the authors' own admission — ships zero ablation isolating any one of them or comparing against itself with adaptivity disabled. It does not move the status: it adds a fresh instance of the disclosure gap this map already tracks, not a new quantification or a new layer. Held again 2026-09-04 on the map's first source that set out from the position-paper stage to measure the apparatus term as a ratio rather than stumbling on it mid-experiment: a purpose-built factorial (three models × three harnesses, 100 SWE-bench Verified tasks) reports harness-induced variance exceeding model-induced variance by 7.80×, with 6 of 9 model-pair/harness-pair comparisons reversing ranking. It confirms rather than promotes — "comparable frontier models" is informally defined and the authors call the definition circular, and no principled harness-distance metric exists to say whether three harnesses was a representative draw — but it names the thesis this map has been assembling piecemeal (the Binding Constraint Thesis), supplies a control-theoretic mechanism (harness as controller, model as open-loop policy), and proposes the most comprehensive disclosure schema this map has seen (seven-layer ETCSOVG), a candidate answer to this map's own open question about what a harness card needs to contain.)

Sub-threads: none — one claim measured at successively lower layers of one stack (loop implementation, agent scaffold, serving wrapper, inference backend, the training trajectories in the weights, and the transport between reply and shell), with the same remedy at every layer: disclose the layer and run at least one arm outside it. The layers are instances of the argument, not separable arguments, and three have one source each. How a layer is measured — swap detection, fixed-output replay attribution, frozen-artifact transfer — cuts across all of them, so it is a method axis rather than a strand. What we believe now:

  • A purpose-built factorial crossing three frontier models with three harness configurations finds harness-induced score variance exceeds model-induced variance by 7.80x, with 6 of 9 model-pair/harness-pair comparisons reversing ranking — the first source on this map to measure the apparatus term as a ratio rather than an absolute point spread. (Added 2026-09-04, T1 — strong.) Naming itself the Binding Constraint Thesis, the paper frames the harness as a controller in a closed-loop dynamical system and the model as an open-loop policy conditioned only on what the controller constructs, operationalized through three trajectory-level diagnostics (Recovery Rate, Context Retention, Control Lag). Leaderboard corroboration: SWE-bench Pro +9.5pp (Opus 4.5 fixed, SEAL scaffold → Claude Code harness), SWE-bench Verified +14–16pp (Grok 4 fixed, scaffold swap), Terminal-Bench 2.0 +13.7pp (infrastructure optimization alone), and a single subagent addition flipping the MiniMax 2.5 vs Claude Opus 4.6 ranking on SWE-bench Pro. Proposes the map's most comprehensive disclosure schema yet — seven layers (Execution, Tool, Context, Scheduling, Observability, Verification, Governance) — a candidate answer to this map's open question on what a harness card must contain, though "nothing establishes completeness" still applies (harness-induced-variance-exceeds-model-induced-variance-7x). Confirms rather than promotes: "comparable frontier models" is informally defined and self-flagged as circular, and no harness-distance metric exists to say three configurations were a representative draw. [T1, strong]
  • A harness reporting a leaderboard-beating, model-fixed score with no ablation isolating which of its own mechanisms produced the gain cannot have that gain attributed to its design — even when the model-fixed check that usually rules out a relabeled model-generation effect is satisfied. (Added 2026-08-31, T1 — single-study.) openJiuwen beats the strongest same-model prior systems by +3.4pp on SWE-bench Verified (82.6% vs 79.2%, Claude 4.5 Opus held fixed) and +3.39pp on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (87.19% vs 83.8%), across five named runtime mechanisms (Rail-based capability composition, a semantic Goal Mode stop signal, LSP-driven passive diagnostic feedback, progressive context management, cross-task self-reflection) — and reports zero ablation removing any one of them, and no run of itself with adaptivity disabled as a fixed-harness control (a-harness-win-with-no-ablation-cannot-be-attributed-to-the-harness). The duration-band breakdown is suggestive (52.38% at 1–4 hours vs mini-swe-agent's 35.71% high-water mark in that band) but is not a substitute for the removal the authors themselves say is missing. A useful negative case for the map: satisfying "hold the model fixed" is necessary but not sufficient to attribute a harness score to a specific harness mechanism. [T1, single-study]
  • A metric can rise under a better specification format while the agent's self-validation behavior stays at zero — a compliance score improving is not evidence the agent checked its own work. (Added 2026-08-31, T1 — single-study. Interaction edge; primary map spec-driven-development.) On the weakest model in a six-model, five-format architecture-specification study, route coverage rises 33%→100% switching prose to typed interface contracts, while that same model ran its own demo/verification script in 0% of 15 trials across every format tested — it produced code and stopped, unverified, regardless of which specification format it was given (spec-format-matters-most-to-the-weakest-model). A concrete instance of this map's core caution: an automated compliance number moving is a fact about the number, not automatically a fact about whether the work behind it was checked.
  • Holding one model's weights exactly fixed, a single context-management policy — mechanically pruning older tool output as the window fills — moves fail-to-pass fraction 21 points, and the effect turns off once the window is wide enough that nothing needs pruning. (Added 2026-08-29, T1 — strong.) SWE-bench Verified at a tight 20,480-token window: 28%→49% fail-to-pass fraction, 43→72 complete solutions (exact paired sign test p<0.0001); SWE-bench Pro 15%→33%; FeatureBench 11%→20%. The same harness change, unmodified, lifts three transfer models with no retuning (Devstral +20pp, Nemotron +6pp, Qwen3.8 +15pp) — evidence the mechanism, not just this model, generalizes. At a wide 262,144-token window the gap collapses to noise (69% vs 68.7%) (a-context-pruning-policy-changes-what-fixed-weights-can-solve). This is the map's first fully single-variable isolation of the apparatus term, and its first demonstration that the term is conditional on a measurable precondition (context pressure) rather than a fixed tax every comparison pays. [T1, strong]
  • The harness term is now measured at 20–46 points on a frozen artifact with the model held fixed, in three stateful enterprise environments and across two model families — and the same artifact moves a weak model further than a strong one. (Added 2026-08-26, T1 — strong. Interaction edge; primary map harness-self-evolution.) Twelve transfer rows, every one positive, from +10.7 to +46.3 pp, from a single harness artifact evolved once against one model and then applied frozen with no re-evolution (stratify-the-evolution-pool-and-hide-the-selection-set). For this map the interesting structure is not the size of the term but its shape: on two of three benchmarks the gain shrinks monotonically as the model gets stronger (ITBench +44.4 for the weakest against +27.9 for the strongest; EnterpriseOps +20.6 against +10.7), which is what a model-environment-mismatch account predicts and a general-capability account does not. The third benchmark does not follow it, so the pattern is suggestive. Read against this map's standing claim, a leaderboard on any of these three benchmarks with the harness undisclosed would be reporting a number whose harness component exceeds the entire spread between the models compared — Qwen3.5-27B with the evolved harness (70.0%) beats GPT-5.5 without it (50.8%) on ITBench, which is the confound stated as a row rather than as an argument. [T1, strong]
  • The apparatus term travels across a model swap, so an undisclosed harness is a persistent per-row constant rather than noise that averages out. (Added 2026-08-25, T1 — single-study.) Almost every result in this map detects the term by swapping the apparatus at a fixed model. This runs the crossed direction: a harness found by automatic search under Claude Opus 4.6 retains +5.6 pp over the base harness when the task agent's model is replaced by Claude Haiku 4.5, against +9.0 pp in the cell it was optimized in — roughly 60% of the gain crossing a model boundary the optimizer never saw (an-optimized-harness-survives-a-model-swap). Supporting portability arms in the same source: optimization on a different training universe still yields +5.9 pp, an independent rerun reaches 58.6%, and all splits are cross-repository. The reading that matters for this map is the pessimistic one. If the apparatus contribution were model-specific noise, a reader could argue it washes out over a leaderboard. It does not — it is a systematic offset carried into every cell the harness is used in, which means a table whose rows were each produced under a different undisclosed harness is comparing models plus a persistent several-point per-row constant. The unrun arm that would bound this is the reverse transfer, where an-evolved-harness-compensates-defects-it-does-not-raise-the-ceiling predicts failure: a harness tuned to compensate for defects a stronger policy no longer commits should have nothing to do. One benchmark, one model family, one direction, no interval reported.
  • The apparatus term is not uniform across the design space — it is concentrated at the boundary between a constrained framework and an adaptive one, and inside the adaptive band the model is the dominant lever. (Added 2026-08-24, T1 — strong. The map's first credible scope condition.) Six frameworks at a fixed model span 37 points of accuracy, and they sort by exactly one property: whether the agent runs an open-ended investigation that requeries as evidence emerges. The three that do hold 77.6–79.6%; the three that do not trail in the order of their constraint — a largely fixed plan at 65.2%, commit-as-soon- as-you-can at 46.6%, no iteration over evidence at all at 42.6%. Within the top band the spread is 2.0 points, while a backbone swap on one of those frameworks moves it 10.8 — over five times the framework term (the-apparatus-term-collapses-inside-the-adaptive-band). Two details argue this is saturation rather than a ceiling artifact: none of the top three was built for the task (general-purpose agents repurposed, so the band is not convergent task-specific tuning), and the model gap widens with difficulty — from causal-chain depth 2 to 5 every framework on the weaker backbone loses 28–44 points while the stronger holds flat. The attribution is behavioural, not architectural: the stronger backbone wins by a broader diagnostic intent repertoire (one probe type in 82.8% of its trajectories against 16.6%) and by acting on what it retrieved on 54.3% of rounds against 24.9% — neither of which the framework supplies. What this changes is the map's comparison advice, not its claim. 37 points is this map's thesis in its strongest form; what the result conditions is the sampling: grid the design space, not the vendors, because four adaptive harnesses will report a small term and be misread as evidence the term is small. Bounds are real — every framework number is a single run the authors label indicative, one topology, one benchmark, and the two backbones differ 22× in cost per case, so "the model dominates" is a capability statement and not a budget-matched one. [T1, strong]
  • The apparatus term can be deliberately shrunk by changing the artifact the apparatus consumes — and the same intervention moves level, cross-model spread and repeat-consistency in the same direction at once. (Added 2026-08-24, T1 — strong. The map's first constructive result.) Every prior source here treats the term as a quantity to detect: swap the scaffold, swap the backend, audit the disclosure. Compiling a prose procedure into a form with declared per-step read/write artifacts and explicit control transfers reduces it. Across six executor models on one domain the score range goes 28–60% → 72–88% (spread halved); per-instance agreement, defined as all six executors passing or all six failing the same instance, goes 48% → 80%; pass^10 goes 16% → 72% with the k=1→10 decay falling from 35 points to 16; and under injected transient request errors and context compaction the compiled form preserves 100% of cases both representations originally passed while prose drops to 80% (an-explicit-artifact-boundary-narrows-the-cross-model-spread). The mechanism is stated and is the one this vault already believes: per-agent input tokens fall 63%, so if the term is substantially a term in how much irrelevant state each model must select from, compressing the per-step context should compress the between-model spread — which is what happens. The consistency metric is worth adopting on its own terms: it answers how often a development-time observation transfers to a different executor, and under prose the answer was half the time. Scope: all six executors run inside one harness, so what is demonstrably compressed is the executor-model term and not the scaffold term this map is mostly about — whether an artifact-driven representation also compresses a harness swap is untested and is the obvious next arm. [T1, strong]
  • The term reaches the input artifact: a specification's operational value is a joint property of the document and the agent consuming it, and transfer damage is directional rather than a general penalty for foreignness. (Added 2026-08-24, T1 — suggestive, carried as an existence proof.) In a controlled Oracle-to-PostgreSQL migration where the knowledge content is not in doubt, one agent consuming another's specification produced Token F1 0.035 and SQL syntax validity 2.33% against 0.73 and 45.5% on its own — a collapse the authors reran independently because it looked anomalous, and it reproduced. But the same foreign specification consumed by a third agent stayed at its native level, and the reverse transfer between the same pair improved: C(i→j) ≠ C(j→i), so "foreign specifications are worse" is refuted in the table that establishes the effect (a-specification-is-not-an-agent-neutral-artifact). The mechanism test is clean: asking the consumer to rewrite the foreign specification into its own idiom — leaving the knowledge source unchanged and transforming only the representation — recovers it almost fully, which isolates representational compatibility from missing information. And specification size predicts nothing: the longer document (≈1,597 lines against ≈193) did not produce better code, a third independent route to this vault's standing null on specification notation and completeness. The authors' decomposition is the durable part — a specification is ⟨knowledge, representation, dependency structure, granularity⟩, and knowledge preservation does not imply operational-interpretation preservation. Bounds are severe and this claim should not bear weight: one task family, no inferential statistics at all, an incomplete transfer matrix, one internally-inconsistent baseline row, and runnability of 0–2.38% throughout the cross-agent stage, so nearly all separation lives in lexical and structural proxies rather than in working code. [T1, suggestive]
  • The apparatus term travels through post-training, and it takes the sign of the effect with it: a released post-trained model gains under the harness its authors report and loses under one they do not. (Added 2026-08-22, T1 — strong.) All numbers from one group, identical harness versions and evaluation protocol (SWE-bench Verified, temperature 0.7, 200 turns, 200k context), with the harness the only thing varying. The bare confound, on an untrained checkpoint: 64.0 / 62.4 / 57.2 under OpenHands SDK / Claude Code / OpenCode — a 6.8-point spread on one model. For calibration on the same three harnesses, a full base-model generation is worth +3.4 / +1.0 / +3.4: the apparatus outweighs the generation again, on a third independent setup. The treatment interacts with it rather than cancelling: the same RL recipe on the same checkpoint gains +6.4 / +5.8 / +9.4, so a paired design does not rescue you. And the transfer failure: KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev, post-trained elsewhere from the newer base, reads +3.4 under Claude Code (the harness its authors report), +4.2 under OpenCode, and −0.4 under OpenHands SDK — where the untuned base beats it, 67.4 to 67.0 (a-post-training-gain-is-scored-in-the-harness-it-was-tuned-in). Read beside fine-tuning-installs-the-training-scaffolds-conventions, which measured levels collapsing by up to 29 points across scaffolds, this measures the gain flipping sign — together: a post-training claim is scoped to the harness its training data was collected in, and the sign is inside that scope. The paper adopts the map's own remedy as a house rule without being asked: because step-0 differs by scaffold, improvements are measured against each scaffold's own baseline and never compared across scaffolds. A fourth number belongs here because it is the same term at the infrastructure layer: the same sandbox stack excludes 2.4% / 7.1% / 6.4% of trajectories as infrastructure failures under the three harnesses, with wall-clock timeouts dominating under one and environment-setup failures under another — score those as zeros and the apparatus term acquires a noise component too. Neither this nor the DCAS result can attribute a mechanism, and both say so. [T1, strong]
  • The apparatus term also governs how much the score moves, and that second term does not rank the same way the first one does. (Added 2026-08-20, T1 — strong.) Every result on this map so far detects the apparatus by swapping it and watching the number change. This one holds the apparatus fixed and perturbs the workload — rewriting a repository into a semantically equivalent form, tests excluded so the oracle is byte-identical — and finds that how much a model degrades is a property of the model, the scaffold and the benchmark jointly. No ordering of models by robustness survives a change of scaffold or of benchmark: one model degrades 0.2 points under the simpler scaffold and 5.5 under the richer one, same benchmark, same tasks; another takes the study's largest drop (6.7) in one cell and is the least degraded of four in the next. Every model is among the most robust in at least one cell and the most brittle in another. The one term that holds is the scaffold: the simpler scaffold degrades less on both benchmarks (1.34 vs 1.88 and 1.88 vs 3.65) — and it holds through a capability flip, since the simpler scaffold is the stronger one on one benchmark and the weaker on the other. That flip is what rules out "the better scaffold is also the steadier one" (robustness-ranks-differently-under-every-scaffold). Two consequences. A harness card owes a robustness row, not just a version string, because the disclosure remedy this map prescribes does not currently ask for the quantity that varies most. And brittleness does not attach to models or repositories: no instance degrades under all eight configurations, and three instances carry two-thirds of the largest configuration's mean — it attaches to instance × configuration cells, which is the smallest unit this map has yet had to name. [T1, strong]
  • Somebody finally went looking for the apparatus term on purpose, and it is not merely the order of the model term — it is five times it. (Added 2026-08-19, T1, strong.) Every other result on this map found the harness term by accident: a study auditing its own plumbing after a false positive, a benchmark author publishing an unrequested grid, a disclosure audit counting what twelve papers failed to print. This one names the axis (harness scaling) and designs three progressively stronger tests for it. Hold GPT-5.5 fixed and put a YAML state-machine runbook around it: Terminal-Bench 2.1 goes 83.1% → 92.1%, +9.0 points. Freeze that profile before any next-generation outcome is observed and apply it to GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh: 84.9% → 95.28% raw, +10.4, with all 89 tasks solved at least once. Under the reference harness the same generation step is +1.8 points (a-runbook-is-worth-more-than-a-model-generation). The cheap-tier cell is the one an operator should read twice: the same frozen profile lifts GPT-5.6 Luna 76.7% → 85.4%, above the expensive tier's own reference score. And nothing in the largest task-level movers is a new capability — configure-git-webserver goes 0/5 → 5/5 because final handoff is gated on a materialized clone–commit–push–curl path rather than on the agent's say-so, which is attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model with the stack's contribution itemized. Two things keep this from arguing for promotion. The measured object is the runtime plus an evolved benchmark-adapted control profile and the authors say outright it does not isolate the state-machine abstraction — a rung below the fixed-output replay that transport-damage-hides-inside-a-matched-score achieved. And the reference row is a model-matched public row on a different agent version, not a rerun A/B.   • The apparatus term is finally attributable, because someone froze the model's output and moved one parser — and the first thing attribution shows is that a matched score is the sum of two terms each an order of magnitude larger than itself. (Added 2026-08-16, T1, strong.) Every other result on this map swaps a whole scaffold and reports that the leaderboard moved, which proves a confound exists and cannot say which layer produced it; the authors say so about the prior work in as many words — replacing the harness wholesale "measures variance but cannot attribute a reversal to any one mechanism." Here the reply is held byte-identical and exactly one downstream parser is added to the path it executes on. Success falls 55.4 to 73.2 points in all eight configurations, in every leave-one-family-out slice, and — the detail that makes it a general hazard rather than an adversarial one — the 14 benign control tasks alone lose 28.6 to 57.1 points, because models emit double-quote-active characters when writing ordinary commands. Re-prompt with one sentence disclosing the boundary, giving no quoting advice, and six of eight configurations recover 30.4 to 60.7 points. The two contrasts sum exactly to what a conventional evaluation prints: GPT-5.6-sol's matched gap of −3.6 points is −64.3 of damage plus +60.7 of compensation (transport-damage-hides-inside-a-matched-score). This map has held that the harness term is the order of the reported effect; here it is an order of magnitude larger and arithmetically invisible. Familiar consequences arrive with it — Kendall τ between the raw and deployed orderings is 0.57, the top rank flips, and the compensating ability spans +60.7 to −5.4 across the same eight configurations, so once again there is nothing to subtract (no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models). Two new ones do not. The compensation is conditioned on the declared grammar rather than on caution: disclose a single-quote wrapper and the top model passes 53/56 under that wrapper and 10/56 under the other, a +80.4-point diagonal, while the same boundary-aware replies lose 28.6 to 64.3 points on the path with no boundary — so an over-declared boundary is as wrong as an undeclared one, and the component owning the interpolation is the only one that can tell the truth about it (models-adapt-to-the-declared-boundary-not-a-fixed-defense). And effort buys the score without buying portability: across 26 crossover points the nested-replay pass rate of raw-conditioned replies moves at most 5.4 points within any ladder, and Opus-4.8's matched gap improves from −48.2 to −3.6 while its damage grows from −58.9 to −67.9. [T1 — one mechanism, one shell family, one-shot generation with no multi-turn recovery; 14 purposively constructed families supporting mechanism attribution and explicitly not prevalence, so the intervals quantify variation across those families rather than sampling; effort-rung cells rest on a single stored generation each. Against that: the nested boundary reproduces a real ssh localhost "R" path to the decimal for 7 of 8 configurations, the effect replicates on 42 held-out private payloads and three further draws, and the decomposition transfers off the shell — a naive JSON string embedding costs 51.8–66.1 points where a correct round-trip serializer costs zero]

Open questions:

  • NEW 2026-08-24 — where exactly is the boundary the term concentrates at, and does it exist outside root- cause analysis? One study found six frameworks sorting cleanly into "requeries as evidence emerges" and "does not", with 37 points between the classes and 2.0 within the top one. Nobody has tested whether the same binary explains the spread on issue resolution, where this map's largest prior grids were run — and if it does, several published harness comparisons were sampling entirely inside one class and reporting the residual as the term. The arm: re-run an existing multi-harness grid with the harnesses labelled by adaptivity before the scores are read.
  • Can an artifact-driven representation compress a harness swap, or only an executor-model swap? The constructive result arrived under a single harness with six models varied inside it, so it demonstrably compresses the model term and says nothing about the scaffold term this map is mostly about. If declaring per-step artifacts also narrows the spread across scaffolds, this map acquires a remedy rather than only a disclosure rule — and if it does not, that is itself evidence the two terms have different causes.
  • Which scaffold property makes a scaffold robust? The 2026-08-20 arrival establishes that the simpler of two scaffolds degrades less under an equivalent rewrite, on both benchmarks, through a capability flip. Two scaffolds cannot say why. The candidate mechanisms — how much of the repository is pulled per turn, whether localization goes through keyword search, how much inert context the agent tolerates before it notices — are all separable in principle and none has been isolated.
  • Should a harness card carry a robustness row? This map's standing remedy is disclosure: name the apparatus layer and run one arm outside it. Disclosure does not capture the quantity that varies most, because the capability ranking is stable under perturbation and the robustness ranking is not. What a useful robustness disclosure would even be — a perturbation family, a magnitude, a per-instance tail — is unspecified, and the first person to propose one will be defining the instrument.
  • Fixed-output replay is now available and has been used on exactly one layer. The design that finally attributed a harness effect — freeze the model's output, change one component, replay — is not specific to shell quoting. It applies wherever the apparatus transforms the model's output before acting on it: a tool-call serializer, a patch applier, a diff parser, a test-harness wrapper. Nobody has run it on any of those, so it is unknown how much of every other layer on this map is transport damage rather than a difference in what the model produced.
  • Nobody has run the two grids together. Every harness grid varies the agent scaffold with the serving stack unstated; the one backend study varies the serving stack with no agent at all. The crossed experiment — same benchmark, two scaffolds × two backends — has never been run, so it is unknown whether the two terms add, interact, or whether one dominates. Given that both are model-dependent interactions rather than offsets, an interaction is the likelier prior and would mean neither can be reported alone.
  • The backend result has never been measured on an agentic task. Its own authors exclude code, multi-turn dialogue and agentic workflows, which is where output length, stopping behaviour and tool-call formatting interact with the serving stack most. A long agent trajectory has hundreds of generations, each a chance for a template or stopping difference to compound — so the ~1B, single-turn magnitudes are a floor with no evidence about the ceiling.
  • What does a harness card have to contain to be sufficient? The disclosure argument has proposed a 5-field schema (2026-07-13), a container spec, the backend name/version/generation configuration, and now (2026-09-04) a seven-layer ETCSOVG schema — the most comprehensive candidate yet. Nothing establishes that any of these lists is complete, and the plumbing incident (2026-07-28) was a wrapper-level output cap that no proposed schema, ETCSOVG included, names explicitly as its own field. The honest position is that disclosure is necessary, demonstrably incomplete, and has no known sufficient form.
  • Is any of this correctable after the fact? Two independent grids now say the harness term is an interaction rather than an offset, so there is no correction table — but nobody has tested whether a partial adjustment (rank-preserving within a harness, say) recovers anything usable, or whether cross-harness comparison is simply void.
