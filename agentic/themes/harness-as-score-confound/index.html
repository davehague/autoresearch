The harness as score confound — map

Forked from evaluation-integrity on 2026-08-06 (bounded auto-fork; the parent was at 56 dated bullets with three declared sub-threads, and had carried [fork-due] since 2026-08-03). The parent asks whether a number describes the agent — is the benchmark clean, is the label right, is the judge honest, does the proxy rank the thing it claims to rank. This map asks the separable question of whether the number describes the agent or the apparatus it ran inside: the scaffold, the container, the timeouts and retry policy, the wrappers on the plumbing between the runner and the provider, and — as of tonight — the inference backend that renders the chat template and calls the kernel. The boundary is is the measurement of the right object versus is the measurement of the object valid. A benchmark can be uncontaminated, correctly labelled and honestly judged, and still report a number that is 8 to 21 points of somebody else's scaffold. Four claims and two dated arrivals moved across intact; two phrases were repointed where the moved text referred to the parent's own catalogue, and three arrivals that carry substantial non-harness content stayed in the parent and are cross-referenced here.

Status: consolidating (inherited from the parent and independently supportable: five groups — a benchmark vendor publishing a two-harness grid, a benchmark author publishing a four-harness matrix unprompted, a single-author study auditing its own plumbing after a false positive, a disclosure audit of twelve benchmark papers, and now a fully crossed inference-backend study — agree that the apparatus is a term of the order of the effect being reported, with no live objection to how any of it was measured. Held below consensus because the two largest grids are two and four harnesses respectively on one benchmark each, the inference-backend result is at ~1B scale on non-agentic tasks, and the field's practice has not moved: this vault is still ingesting single-harness leaderboard comparisons weekly. Held again 2026-08-07 on four more arrivals — three of them measuring the term on quantities other than a resolve rate (a security detection rate, a monitor's AUROC, a long-horizon obligation score) and one, DCAS, reaching a layer the disclosure remedy cannot repair, since a scaffold baked into fine-tuning weights is not a reporting failure. That is the first evidence here that the map's own prescription is incomplete, and it is one study.)

Sub-threads: none — one claim measured at successively lower layers of one stack (loop implementation, agent scaffold, serving wrapper, inference backend, and now the training trajectories baked into the weights), with the same remedy at every layer: disclose the layer and run at least one arm outside it. The layers are instances of the argument rather than separable arguments, and no layer has enough sources to stand alone — the deepest two have one each. [reviewed 2026-08-07 @ 10 bullets]

What we believe now:

  • The apparatus term can change the sign of an intervention, not only its size — and the gap between two harnesses can exceed every intervention being compared against them. (Added 2026-08-08, T1, strong.) This map's best instances hold an artifact byte-identical and vary the harness: a context pack worth double digits under one and zero under another, a skill library read on 99–100% of tasks under one stack and 0–1% under another (progressive-disclosure-buys-context-not-intelligence, skill-invocation-rate-is-a-harness-property). Here the effect flips. Seventeen skills authored by six senior quality engineers, dropped into two coding agents with tools, environment, base LLM and per-task budget held fixed on the same held-out task set: OpenHands F1 0.08 → 0.16 (doubled), mini-SWE-agent 0.28 → 0.26 (worse). The stated mechanism generalizes — human skills supply high-level workflow guidance the stronger harness already provides, so on that harness they are displacement rather than addition, which is strip-the-prompt-when-the-model-improves one layer out and puts a cause under generic-context-files-do-not-move-correctness. The scale is the alarming part: the two base agents, identical task, identical model, identical budget, differ 0.08 against 0.28 F1 — a gap wider than every skill technique's improvement over its own base — and each technique's effect is far larger on the weaker harness (+0.14 vs +0.01, +0.11 vs +0.01, +0.23 vs +0.10). Anyone reporting one of these methods on one harness could publish anything from +0.01 to +0.23 for the same artifact purely by choice of substrate (a-skill-set-helps-the-weaker-harness-and-hurts-the-stronger). A second, independent instance the same week separates two layers this map usually fuses: under graded security policy, cost follows the model (one model stays costlier under either harness at every policy) while success follows the bundle (crossing either endpoint model to the other harness lowers success at every policy) — so a cost comparison and a capability comparison do not belong to the same layer (price-the-security-policy-as-an-evaluation-variable). [T1 ×2 — for the sign flip: one task (the paper's second task shows human skills helping both harnesses), one LLM, 108 cases, no seeds or intervals, and mini-SWE-agent had no native skill support so the authors added a loading interface, which may itself carry part of the difference. For the policy result: one benchmark, three trials per cell, no intervals]
  • The apparatus term reaches all the way into the weights: single-scaffold fine-tuning installs the training scaffold's conventions, and the same model degrades by up to 29 points when deployed under another — while the untrained base it came from does not. (Added 2026-08-07, T1, strong.) Every prior arrival here varies something outside the model. This one does not, and it is the first on this map that cannot be disclosed away. The open coding-agent ecosystem collects its fine-tuning trajectories almost entirely under one scaffold (SWE-Gym, Nebius, SWE-Lego and CoderForge all under OpenHands), and models trained on them carry that scaffold wherever they go: Nebius-SWE-Rebench-30B reads 49.7 / 46.0 / 35.8 / 20.4 across OpenHands / Claude Code / OpenCode / mini-swe-agent, SWE-Lego-Qwen3-32B reads 52.6 / 44.2 / 8.4 / 37.6 (the 8.4 a tool-call format mismatch leaving most trajectories with zero parseable actions), SERA-32B 54.2 / 33.4 / 35.8 / 26.4. The base rows are the control: Qwen3-32B spreads 29.0 / 23.2 / 18.4 / 8.0 and, within each family, the base spread is smaller than the spread of any model fine-tuned from it — so the divergence is installed by training rather than being a property of the model (fine-tuning-installs-the-training-scaffolds-conventions). Mixing scaffolds in training is the obvious fix and has been tried: a model trained on diverse multi-format trajectories still falls 50.5 → 26.2 and 15.6. The named locus is planning structure in two separable senses, and the paper demonstrates the leverage by isolating it — executor, scaffold, benchmark and turn budget fixed, plan source varied, 42.8% → 57.8% Pass@1, a gain larger than the cross-scaffold drops it explains (plan-quality-is-a-separable-lever-and-calibration-beats-capability). The authors state this map's thesis in their own words — "single-scaffold evaluation is a methodological confound", and a claim of the form "model X achieves Y% on SWE-bench Verified" is "methodologically incomplete without specifying the scaffold" — and propose the sharpest disclosure minimum yet: report at least one scaffold outside the training distribution, ideally a vector. The systemic version is worse than the per-model one: if almost every open model with strong CLI-agent performance is trained under one scaffold, and that scaffold is also where evaluations are reported, the published record is measuring convention-familiarity and calling it capability. [T1 — four fine-tuned models plus two bases, one benchmark, single runs per cell with no seeds or intervals; four scaffolds, one of them the study's own primary environment; two of the three degradation categories are integration mismatches (format, context ceiling) that any smoke test would catch, which puts the weight on the third. Independently corroborated by Qwen3-Coder-Next's multi-scaffold reporting and by SWE-Gym's differing profiles under OpenHands versus MoatlessTools]
  • Above the harness sits the loop, and it is a term of the same size as four generations of model. (Added 2026-08-07, T1, strong.) The layer that carries objectives, progress criteria and residual work across an extended run has been an unstated constant in every long-horizon number this vault holds. Crossed both ways on one benchmark: hold the model at gpt-5.4 and vary only the loop and resolve rate spans 18.75% (Codex) → 7.14% (mini-swe-agent), 11.6 points and a 2.6× ratio; hold the loop at Claude Code and vary the model across eight backends and it spans 25.00% → 4.46%. The two spreads are the same order (the-loop-implementation-is-a-larger-term-than-the-model-tier). Two details make it an interaction rather than a scalar. A single boolean in the runner — an outer continuation loop — is worth +3.6 to +8.0 points in every cell, larger than most published model-to-model gaps in this vault. And the loops differ in shape: closed-source loops recover more of the prerequisite graph (Edge F1 0.71/0.67/0.58), organize execution as a tree and slightly over-parallelize, while open-source loops driven by linear control flow form a near-chain (critical-path ratio 0.85–0.97) and emit patches 2.3–2.5× gold length against 1.6–1.8×. So there is no loop correction table either. The cross-benchmark gap is the disclosure argument in one figure: the strongest configuration resolves 25.0% here against top solve rates of 95.0, 83.4, 80.0 and 64.4 elsewhere — partly difficulty, and partly that end-state instruments never ask whether the run kept its earlier promises. Third field for the harness card, and the one nobody prints: the loop implementation and its continuation policy. [T1 — one benchmark, single configurations per cell in the main tables (pass@K appears only as an appendix monotonicity check), so the spreads carry no intervals; the fixed-model loop sweep is the crossed arm and it uses one model]
  • The apparatus term reaches below the agent scaffold, into the software that loads the weights and emits the tokens. (Added 2026-08-06, T1, strong.) Everything else on this map varies the agent harness — which tools, which container, which retry policy. Hold all of that fixed, hold the weights, the prompts, the hardware and the decoding fixed, and change only the serving framework, and the score still moves. Fully crossed at 3 models × 5 backends × 6 benchmarks × 4 generation modes: under greedy decoding, where no sampling noise exists, hf_pipeline (the same in-process model object as the reference) diverges by 0.000 on every metric, while Ollama diverges 0.055 on Llama-3.2-1B (9 of 11 per-item metrics significant), LangChain 0.049 on identical weights and engine — the mechanism there being prompt-template rendering alone — and vLLM 0.025 on one model of three, significant on 10 of 11, from nothing but a different attention kernel under non-associative arithmetic (the-inference-backend-is-a-term-in-the-score). Two properties carry the same shape as the scaffold layer. It is an interaction, not an offset: Gemma-3-1B is near-immune (at most 4 of 16 severe configurations anywhere) while Llama is severe on 14 of 16 across four benchmarks, so there is no per-backend correction table — exactly no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models one layer down. And the mean understates the churn: almost every severe cell sits above the y = x line relating score divergence to per-item disagreement, which reaches 48%, so a score stable in the mean can be composed of different correct answers. The remedy splits the term usefully: structural effect 0.00074, plus sampling 0.00112, plus each framework's own default hyper-parameters 0.00190 — so roughly 39% is structural and 61% is avoidable by decoding greedily and stating the generation configuration, which extends the harness card by three fields rather than requiring new research (match-the-generation-config-before-comparing-backends). [T1 — but ~1B instruction-tuned models on English multiple-choice and short-form generation; the authors name code, mathematical reasoning, multi-turn dialogue and agentic tasks as explicitly uncovered, which is where this vault's evidence lives. One GPU, one CUDA version, one seed, five generations per prompt in the stochastic arms, and the reference backend is a reference rather than ground truth. The existence result and the avoidable/structural split transfer; the magnitudes do not]
  • A benchmark's own paper can publish the disclosure this map demands, and when it does, the hidden terms turn out to be interactions rather than offsets — nothing you can learn once and subtract. (Added 2026-07-31, T1, strong.) Every disclosure argument on this map so far has been made about benchmark papers (agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness, 0 of 8 disclosing a container spec) or by a study correcting its own apparatus after the fact (harness-asymmetry-manufactures-model-differences). Here a benchmark author publishes the full model×harness matrix unprompted, organizing every table by harness so comparisons are read inside a fixed scaffold, and the resulting grid answers both caveats the vault attached to the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order — two harnesses only, one of them the publishing vendor's. Four harnesses, none the authors', different benchmark, different domain: every model run under ≥2 harnesses spreads ≥0.08 Pass@1, Opus 4.8 spans 0.209 on one swap (0.921 → 0.712), the authors' own range is 8–21 points (no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models). The new half is structural: the ordering of harnesses is not model-invariant — Codex beats Hermes for one model and not another — so there is no harness correction table to publish, and a score measured on someone else's scaffold is not adjustable into a score on yours. Two more hidden terms surface from the same matrix. The noise floor is not constant down the leaderboard: best-of-3 recovers +0.007 for the saturated leader and +0.10 to +0.21 for mid-tier entries, growing monotonically as capability drops (resampling-headroom-is-inverse-to-capability) — so a fixed "gaps under N points are unresolved" rule is too strict at the top and far too lenient in the middle, and it prices a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm's missing null at 10–21 points for exactly the models loop papers study. And the flat average hides the axis that decides deployability: hold the agent fixed and it keeps 0.85–0.90 where partial credit is available while collapsing to 0.50–0.63 on the domain where one arithmetic or schema error voids the deliverable, with per-domain leaders reshuffling column to column (all-or-nothing-domains-collapse-under-a-flat-average) — the task-category cut of the duration divergence in reliability-and-capability-rankings-diverge. Read together the three say the same thing about aggregation: the harness, the cell's variance, and the task category are each terms of the order of the reported effect, and averaging is what conceals them. [T1 — one benchmark, 67 hand-authored tasks from one team, a partially crossed harness grid, nruns varying 1–3 with no intervals; enterprise workflow work rather than repository maintenance, so the ordinal claims transfer and the magnitudes do not]
  • Closing the model-judgment channel does not make a comparison neutral: the apparatus feeding the two models is itself a term, and an asymmetry in it manufactures a model difference with nothing in the number to announce it. (Added 2026-07-28, T1, strong.) evaluation-integrity's catalogue has been a catalogue of bad proxies — merge labels, pass@1, LLM judges, resolved rate, fail-to-pass. Here the proxy was fine. Every verdict was a rule-injected mutant and a failing pytest run, so no model ever scored another model's output. The bias entered one level out, through an undetected 16,000-token output cap on one provider's wrapper that truncated replies and logged them under an unrelated-sounding rejection note. The cap applied to all calls on that wrapper but only bit on verbose replies, and verbosity is a model property — so the failure was correlated with the arm under test and produced a pooled p = 9.5 × 10⁻⁶⁶. Corrected: p = 0.0625, not supported (harness-asymmetry-manufactures-model-differences). Generalized, the hazard is any dimension on which the apparatus treats two models differently and which correlates with the outcome — a timeout, a token budget, a retry policy, a parser that tolerates one model's formatting quirks, a serving-stack difference. This map already holds that the harness is a term in a model's score (the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order, attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model); this says it is a term in the comparison's plumbing too, one layer further out than anyone reports. Three practices follow, and the study argues for each by having needed it. Fail-loud instrumentation: three earlier silent corruptions in the same study were caught mechanically before any number was written, and the one that reached the counted data was the one that emitted no crash and no error (fail-loud-instrumentation-over-plausible-substitutes). Pre-registration enforced by tooling, so the runner refuses to execute unless the committed protocol is byte-identical to HEAD and every number recomputes from receipts. And adversarial review above the code — the defect survived every runtime gate and was caught by an outside-the-family review of the completed analysis (independent-review-catches-bugs-in-the-measurement-code-itself). The parent map's 2026-07-25 rule was validate the grader in both directions before the run (validate-the-judge-before-you-trust-the-migration); the clause it was missing is validate the plumbing around the grader, and require it to halt rather than substitute. [T1, strong — but one receipted incident on a four-subject study, not a prevalence estimate]
  • The harness term in a published model comparison is the same size as the model differences being compared. (Added 2026-07-26, T1, strong.) Seven models, 260 tasks, three runs per cell, run under two harnesses: mean absolute shift 8.6 points on the Security subset, rank order changing, one cell moving 13.5 points (GPT-5.5 Security 64.39 → 77.91) with nothing varied but the harness. The authors say it themselves — "the harness is visibly not a neutral measurement instrument." Frontier models separate by roughly 2–14 points on the Code subset of the same benchmark, so the unreported term is the order of the reported effect. This converts agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness from a hygiene complaint into arithmetic, and it is the systematic sibling of the paired coordination noise floor (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor) — not run-to-run variance but a fixed unmeasured factor nobody discloses. Two rules: reading, treat a single-harness leaderboard gap under ~10 points as unresolved; building, a candidate model evaluated on someone else's harness has not been evaluated for your system. The shift is not uniform — Code moves mostly upward for everyone, Web reverses orderings, Security spreads — so there is no offset to subtract (the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order). [T1, strong — two harnesses only, one of them the publishing vendor's own product; three runs per cell with no intervals; two of four subsets LLM-judged, though the largest shift is on the deterministically-scored one]
  • The score is unfalsifiable for a reason independent of contamination: nobody publishes the harness. A pilot audit of 12 benchmark papers finds agent benchmarks disclose roughly half of what classical static ones do (mean 0.38 vs 0.66 on a 5-field schema); 0 of 8 disclose inference cost, 0 of 8 fully specify the container. Contamination explains levels; harness under-specification explains spread — two papers running an under-specified harness on the same benchmark and model will disagree even with perfect test-set hygiene. Unlike contamination, this channel is fixable by authors unilaterally: ship a harness card with every score. [T1, single-study]

  • 2026-08-12 — interaction edge from opencodereview-determinism-injection (Li, Zhang, Wu, Zhuang, Xu, Wang, Zhu, Wang, Zhao, Zheng & Rong, Alibaba Group / Nanjing University / Peking University, arXiv, "OpenCodeReview", 2026-08-10, T1, strong for this edge): the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order was established on issue resolution, where a long horizon and heavy tool use give a scaffold plenty of room. The open question was whether it generalizes to short, bounded tasks. It does, and more severely. Six backends from four providers on 200 real pull requests under identical sampling parameters: Claude-4.6-Opus is first of six under one review system and near-bottom under the other (25.10% vs 11.57% SEM-F1), while Claude-4.8-Opus does the reverse — the strongest backend under Claude Code and among the weakest under OpenCodeReview. Two models from one vendor one generation apart swap places when the pipeline changes. The margin that settles it: the weakest configuration of the better system beats the strongest configuration of the worse one, so the system term dominates the model term across the whole model set. Precision regimes are system-owned and model-unstable — 25–38% under one system for all six backends, 7–16% under another — as is cost, 352K–743K tokens per PR against 1.2M–5.7M for the same models on the same diffs (the-review-harness-reorders-the-model-leaderboard). The practical corollary runs re-examine-the-harness-when-the-model-changes backwards: re-run your model selection when the harness changes, because the previous ranking was a joint measurement and only one term moved. Caveat this map should apply to itself: the benchmark shares authors with the winning system, though the rank-instability result is a within-system comparison and largely insulated from that.   • 2026-08-10 — interaction edge from agentchaos-fault-injection-agent-systems (Tan et al., Sun Yat-sen University / Singapore Management University, ASE 2026, arXiv, "AgentChaos", 2026-08-07, T1, suggestive for this edge): a layer of the stack this map has not enumerated, and the only one that is not under the evaluator's control at all — the provider response path. Every layer here so far is something a team chose and could disclose (loop implementation, scaffold, wrapper, inference backend, review protocol, monitor calibration, training trajectories). This one is the API's health at the moment the benchmark ran. Injecting realistic provider faults — HTTP errors, timeouts, empty bodies, truncated replies, malformed tool-call payloads — moves pass@1 by 0.87 to 49.66 points depending on the system, with a persistent degradation regime worth 47.64–62.39 points on multi-agent systems and an intermittent one at p = 0.3 worth almost nothing. Two consequences for reading a published number. A pass@1 is implicitly conditioned on an undisclosed and unrecorded provider-health term, and nobody reports it — no leaderboard entry in this vault states its error or truncation rate during the run, which makes provider health a candidate explanation for irreproducible cells alongside the seven layers already here (agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness). And the sensitivity is architecture-specific (rankings invariant across four backbones, the pipeline pattern losing 83.87% to a fault on its first call), so provider degradation does not scale all entries equally — it reorders them, which is the defining property of a confound on this map rather than of noise (the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order, robustness-to-provider-faults-is-an-architecture-property). Carried as suggestive because it is an injection study, not a measurement of how often real providers degrade during real evaluation runs — the prevalence half is exactly what nobody has, and it is the number that would turn this into a correction rather than a caution. Primary map: agent-harness-architecture.
  • 2026-08-08 — interaction edge from gse-global-skill-evolution (Yang, Tian, Wang, Liu, Ye & Chen, Tianjin University / Tsinghua University, arXiv, "Learning Globally Reusable Skills for Coding Agents", 2026-08-06, T1, strong for this edge): the apparatus term with a sign change, which this map has not had before. Seventeen skills written by six senior software-quality engineers (6–10 years each), independently authored then jointly consolidated, evaluated on 108 manually verified focal methods under a held-out protocol with tools, environment, base LLM and per-task budget fixed. OpenHands F1 0.08 → 0.16; mini-SWE-agent 0.28 → 0.26. Precision moves both ways too (0.17 → 0.33 and 0.48 → 0.42). The authors' reading — human skills mainly supply workflow guidance the stronger harness already implements — makes this strip-the-prompt-when-the-model-improves applied to a skill bank, and supplies a mechanism for generic-context-files-do-not-move-correctness and generated-context-files-cost-more-than-they-help: what matters is not whether the artifact is good but whether the substrate already does what it describes. The confound dwarfs the treatments — the two base agents differ 0.08 vs 0.28 F1 on the identical task with the identical model, and every technique's effect is roughly an order of magnitude larger on the weaker harness (+0.14/+0.01, +0.11/+0.01, +0.23/+0.10), so single-harness reporting of a skill method is a free choice of headline (attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model, no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models, a-skill-set-helps-the-weaker-harness-and-hurts-the-stronger). Credit where due: the paper runs both harnesses precisely to control for this. One caution against over-reading — mini-SWE-agent has no native skill support and was extended with a loading interface by the authors, so part of the gap may be that interface rather than intrinsic scaffolding; and the paper's second task shows human skills helping both harnesses, so the sign flip is one task deep. No seeds, variance or intervals. Primary map: harness-self-evolution.
  • 2026-08-08 — interaction edge from boundary-bench-policy-graded-hardening (Davidovich, Amar, Rozencwajg & Hiltch, Accomplish AI, arXiv, "Permission Denied", 2026-08-02, T1, strong for this edge): a new layer for this map's stack — the execution environment's security posture — and an ablation that separates two attributions usually fused.   • 2026-08-07 — dcas-scaffold-internalized-planning (Thangarajah, Chen & Hassan, Centre for Software Excellence Huawei Canada / Queen's University, ASE '26, arXiv, "DCAS: Decoupling CLI Agent Scaffolding to Internalize Planning across Scaffolds", 2026-08-06, T1, strong): the deepest layer this map has reached — the apparatus is inside the weights. Four 30B–32B open models plus the two untrained bases they were fine-tuned from, on SWE-bench Verified at 100 turns, across four scaffolds; every fine-tuned model degrades on at least one non-training scaffold and the within-family base spread is smaller than any fine-tuned row's, so the divergence is training-installed (fine-tuning-installs-the-training-scaffolds-conventions). DCAS itself is the instrument the field was missing: an interception layer routing API traffic between any CLI scaffold and any backend model without modifying the scaffold, which is what makes controlled cross-scaffold evaluation possible at all — and its own failure is instructive, Codex CLI being excluded after the binary went permanently unresponsive at 10–18 round trips despite HTTP 200 on every call. The controlled plan-source intervention (executor, scaffold, benchmark and turn budget fixed) runs 42.8 → 48.2 (self) → 49.2 (open-weight 480B) → 54.2–56.0 (Opus 4.5) → 57.8 (Sonnet 4.5), with the mid-tier planter beating the stronger one (plan-quality-is-a-separable-lever-and-calibration-beats-capability). And the transfer result is why it belongs here rather than only in loop-engineering: 576 planning-aware trajectories collected under one scaffold produce gains on a newer release of that scaffold (57.2%, exceeding the training version) and on two scaffolds never seen (OpenCode 49.4%, mini-swe-agent 42.4%), and the PlanOnly/Plan+Exec contrast cleanly separates the scaffold's turn-by-turn implicit conventions from the productive use of an explicit plan — training on planning turns alone produces its largest gain in the no-plan condition. Also, against the field's collection-scale assumption: 576 trajectories against the thousands-to-tens-of-thousands of the OpenHands corpora, with collection scaffold argued as the more consequential choice. Ripples: loop-engineering (plan quality as a separable 15-point lever and planner–executor calibration as a design variable), agent-harness-architecture (the pair thesis gaining a training-history term), evaluation-integrity (single-scaffold leaderboard reporting named as a confound by a benchmark-adjacent group). Bounded: one executor model for every controlled arm, one benchmark, single-attempt Pass@1 with no seeds, and a planning phase budgeted up to 500 exploration turns so plan quality and extra exploration are not fully separated.

Open questions:

  • Nobody has run the two grids together. Every harness grid varies the agent scaffold with the serving stack unstated; the one backend study varies the serving stack with no agent at all. The crossed experiment — same benchmark, two scaffolds × two backends — has never been run, so it is unknown whether the two terms add, interact, or whether one dominates. Given that both are model-dependent interactions rather than offsets, an interaction is the likelier prior and would mean neither can be reported alone.
  • The backend result has never been measured on an agentic task. Its own authors exclude code, multi-turn dialogue and agentic workflows, which is where output length, stopping behaviour and tool-call formatting interact with the serving stack most. A long agent trajectory has hundreds of generations, each a chance for a template or stopping difference to compound — so the ~1B, single-turn magnitudes are a floor with no evidence about the ceiling.
  • What does a harness card have to contain to be sufficient? The disclosure argument has proposed a 5-field schema (2026-07-13), a container spec, and now the backend name, version and generation configuration. Nothing establishes that any of these lists is complete, and the plumbing incident (2026-07-28) was a wrapper-level output cap that no proposed schema would have caught. The honest position is that disclosure is necessary, demonstrably incomplete, and has no known sufficient form.
  • Is any of this correctable after the fact? Two independent grids now say the harness term is an interaction rather than an offset, so there is no correction table — but nobody has tested whether a partial adjustment (rank-preserving within a harness, say) recovers anything usable, or whether cross-harness comparison is simply void.
