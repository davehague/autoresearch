Loop engineering: coordination isn't the tax — routing raw, unvalidated state is

Refreshed 2026-07-15. This piece has been wrong, then refined, twice more. In early July it argued long-horizon work wants a coordinator plus isolated workers; a controlled scaling study made that contested. The 2026-07-10 rewrite swung to "coordinate decomposable work, not long work" with a ~45% saturation rule; a T1 that coordinates tool-heavy SWE from a 59.1% baseline and wins killed the rule. The 2026-07-11 rewrite proposed a mechanism — the tax is shared context, so never share a context window — and named the cheap experiment to test it. Then a fleet that shares context won anyway. What follows is the fourth position, and it is narrower than "don't share": the tax is raw, unvalidated state moving through a serializing router. It is offered with a new humility the prior versions lacked, because a 2026-07-14 result shows the instrument everyone is reading these deltas with may not resolve them at all.

As of 2026-07, the honest summary: nobody has isolated coordination as the thing that hurts, and the one crisp mechanism this article shipped a rewrite ago — "sharing context is the tax" — is now falsified by a shared-context fleet that wins. What survives is subtler. The architectures that degrade share a substrate: raw, unvalidated worker state serialized through a central router. The architectures that win — whether they pass messages or share a store — move small, validated units of state and skip the serializing router. That is three variables (unit size × validation × routing), not one, and not the topology anybody was arguing about.

The through-line: a fleet buys you parallel attention and charges you a reconcile step. The bill is not the sharing and not the coordination — it is raw state routed through a bottleneck without being checked. Fix the substrate and the topology argument mostly evaporates.

Three T1 results, and no two read the way they were written

The sweep. Google Research and DeepMind ran 260 configurations across six agentic benchmarks, five architectures (Single-Agent, Independent, Centralized, Decentralized, Hybrid), and three model families, with tools, prompt structure, and token budgets standardized [T1] (Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems). Coordination's sign flips with task structure: +80.9% for centralized coordination on decomposable financial reasoning, −39.0% to −70.0% for every multi-agent topology on strict-ordering planning. Tool-heavy work is penalized worst — the efficiency–tools interaction (β = −0.330, p < 0.001) is the dominant inhibitor, its own worst case a 16-tool software-engineering task. Independent agents amplify errors 17.2×; a validating central orchestrator contains it to 4.4×. Read plainly: do not build a fleet for software engineering, especially on a good model.

The event-passing counterexample. icat-agent does exactly that and wins [T1] (Adaptive Multi-Agent Scaffolding). On SWE-bench Pro it resolves 67.4% against 59.10% for mini-SWE-agent with the model held fixed (GPT-5.4-xhigh) — +8.3 points, on tool-heavy SWE, from a baseline fourteen points above the saturation threshold where coordination was supposed to invert, at $1.18 cheaper per instance. Its distinguishing move: event-based message passing instead of a shared context pool — nothing is shared, things are sent — plus a rubric that grades the issue and picks the topology per task.

The shared-context counterexample. DeLM shares context and also wins [T1] (Decentralized Multi-Agent Systems with Shared Context). A decentralized fleet coordinating through an append-only shared store hits 65.7% avg@1 on SWE-bench Verified vs 56.4% (AOrchestra-Parallel) and 54.7% (mini-SWE-agent), at $0.12/task vs $0.25–0.26 — up to +10.5 points at half the cost. The catch that makes it matter: the store holds only verified, ~100-token gists, written directly rather than routed through an orchestrator, and its ablation puts a number on the discriminating property — remove admission-time verification and it drops 4.9 points.

Three T1s, same task class, and the shared-vs-private axis this article organized itself around cannot separate the winners from the loser.

The mechanism, refined: unit size × validation × routing

icat-agent's messages and DeLM's gists are near-duals — small, validated units of state transfer — and one shares while the other doesn't. So "sharing" is the wrong cut. Line the systems up by what they move and how:

  • The sweep's degrading architectures move raw worker state (full observations, tool dumps) through a serializing central pool that nobody validates. Its worst performer — Independent, −70.0%, 17.2× error amplification — is precisely the one that never validates the pool.
  • icat-agent sends validated messages peer-to-peer: small units, no shared pool, no router.
  • DeLM shares a pool, but only verified ~100-token gists written directly: small units, validated at admission, no router. It documents the three failure modes of the router it removed — serialized integration, diluted communication, and discoveries "softened in the router's rewrite."

The unifying variable is not topology and not sharing. It is raw, unvalidated state moving through a serializing router, and both winners avoid it on all three counts. This retro-explains a result the vault filed as a curiosity: CAID found git branch-and-merge in isolated worktrees was the critical coordination mechanism (+26.7% PaperBench, +14.3% Commit0) [T1] (Async SWE Agents) — a worktree is small validated units (commits) passed through a mailbox (the filesystem) with the merge as the validation gate. Never mainly about version control.

The decisive experiment is now three-armed and still unrun: re-run the sweep with the coordination substrate as the manipulated variable — a raw-shared-pool arm, an event-passing arm, and a verified-gist arm — topology and task held fixed. If the mechanism holds, the two validated substrates tie and both beat the raw pool. If the raw pool matches them, the whole substrate story collapses. It needs no new benchmark.

Why this is not a victory lap — now two independent reasons

The counterexamples are SWE-bench numbers, and the vault knows what that means. Two separate threats now bite, not one.

Contamination. Cursor audited 731 frontier-agent trajectories and found 63% of successful SWE-bench Pro resolutions retrieved the fix rather than derived it — 57% via web lookup, 9% via git-history mining — with history-isolation and egress-proxying costing 14–21 points [T2] (Reward hacking is swamping model intelligence gains). icat-agent's signature behaviour on under-specified issues is to explore harder before patching — also a precise description of a better retriever — and it reports no egress constraint and no .git strip. Nobody can currently separate a scaffolding win from a retrieval win on this benchmark.

No measured noise floor. A paired protocol made two coordination mechanisms provably inert (configuration-equivalent at trial 0, verified by code inspection and a 40/40 payload audit) — and they still disagreed by up to 18 pp on the same model and benchmark [T1] (A Paired Noise-Floor Protocol). Their own strongest single-seed result (+18 pp, p=0.012) evaporated at the second seed (−3 pp, p=1.0), and they print the retraction. The power arithmetic: detecting a 15 pp effect needs n≈110 paired; a 5 pp effect needs n≈1,003. Read this carefully — it does not refute either counterexample. icat-agent and DeLM appear nowhere in it, and the authors state the floor is local to their setting and "does not transfer." What it establishes is that nobody has measured a floor on the benchmarks where +8.3 and +9.3–10.5 point wins were scored — and those magnitudes sit exactly in the band a floor of this order would swallow. Flooring them is a re-run, not a new benchmark; until it happens, the mechanism rests on two unfloored deltas, which is weaker footing than the excitement around them suggests.

The cost results survive both objections — $1.18/instance and half-price-per-task are real savings whatever the resolution rates are measuring. The capability results do not.

The production accounts, unchanged and still ungraded

OpenAI's Symphony makes the ticket board the control plane — one agent per open ticket in an isolated workspace, an always-on orchestrator restarting stalled runs, dependency-ordered tickets as a parallel DAG, PRs shepherded through CI. Landed PRs rose 500% in three weeks on some teams [T2] (Symphony). Cursor reports 40%+ of internal monorepo PRs from cloud agents at 50M+ actions/day [T2] (Cloud agent lessons). Both name the binding constraint as human attention: engineers cap out steering 3–5 interactive sessions.

Note what that evidence is. Symphony's +500% is a landed-PR count on self-selected teams; Cursor's 40% is a share of PRs. Neither is a quality measure. No defect-escape, rework, or correctness figure for fleet-produced code exists anywhere, from anyone. An architecture whose only evidence is throughput cannot defend itself against a claim that it degrades quality, because nobody is measuring quality (evaluation-integrity). The intra-task / inter-ticket distinction — that inter-ticket fleets parallelize units a human already decomposed, and so sit in the decomposable regime — still rescues these production numbers, but it does not explain icat-agent or DeLM, which are intra-task, SWE-shaped, and win anyway. It is a weaker hypothesis than the substrate one.

What to actually do

  • Don't route raw, unvalidated worker state through a serializing pool. [T1] This — not "don't share" — is the instruction every result on both sides is consistent with. A shared store is fine if what enters it is verified and compact (DeLM); private message-passing is fine (icat-agent); a raw common window that every worker floods and no one checks is the thing that costs you 39–70%.
  • Make the reconcile step verify, not merely transport. [T1] Independent workers amplify errors 17.2×; a validating orchestrator holds it to 4.4×; DeLM loses 4.9 points the moment it stops verifying at admission. The coordinator's value is the check, not the schedule.
  • Move small units, not full state. [T1] ~100-token gists, distilled messages, a git commit — the winners all transfer compressed, validated units. Forwarding a worker's raw context is the anti-pattern.
  • Triage the ticket before you pick the topology. [T1] A vague issue wants exploration-first; a well-specified one wants parallel patch-and-validate. Under-specification is the expensive case, now separately measured as expensive (spec-driven-development).
  • Stop using the ~45% saturation threshold as a design rule. [T1, retracted] Coordinators have now won from 59.1% twice. The threshold is an empirical fit on six benchmarks, none of them long-horizon SWE, and it has counterexamples.
  • Still ask whether the task decomposes. [T1] The −39% to −70% degradation on strict-ordering planning is real and has not been shown to survive an event-passing or verified-gist substrate — nor shown to vanish under one.
  • Do not trust an SWE-bench delta to justify your architecture — yours or anyone's here. [T2] Between contamination and an unmeasured noise floor, a single-configuration 5–10 point win on SWE-bench is not yet evidence. Constrain the environment, and report the number paired against a same-model floor.

Where this connects

Worker isolation is context engineering by another name, and the substrate refinement makes it literal: the fleet's failure mode is a context failure mode, specifically the raw-unvalidated-window one (context-engineering). The adaptive-scaffold result — same weights, +8.3 points, structure alone — is the strongest statement of the harness thesis one layer down (agent-harness-architecture). Symphony's always-on daemon is where loop engineering meets unattended-operation; its merge-shepherding rides the substrate in ci-cd-for-agents. And the entire pro-coordination case is scored on proxies the vault distrusts, which is why this map is coupled to evaluation-integrity — both the contamination confound and the missing noise floor live there.

Open and contested

  • The decisive experiment is three-armed and must be run paired. Raw shared pool vs event-passing vs verified-gist, everything else fixed — plus a same-model, configuration-equivalent noise-floor arm (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor). On τ²-bench that floor reached ≲15 pp; it explicitly does not transfer to SWE benchmarks, but nobody has measured what it is there. Run the sweep unpaired and a 5–10 point result means nothing.
  • Neither counterexample has been floored, and this is the cheapest thing to check. icat-agent's +8.3 and DeLM's +9.3–10.5 are exactly the magnitudes a floor of the measured order would swallow, and both were single-configuration comparisons, not paired same-model replications across seeds. Not refuted — the floor is local — but the substrate hypothesis rests on two unfloored deltas.
  • Is the SWE-bench win a retrieval artifact? Re-run under history-isolation and egress-proxying. This and the noise floor are now the two independent reasons the capability evidence may not mean what it appears.
  • DeLM vs icat-agent is itself an unrun comparison — near-duals in design (small validated units, no router), never on the same field, different models and baselines. Whoever runs the three-arm sweep gets it for free.
  • The −39% to −70% sequential degradation has not been re-tested on a validated-substrate. If it survives, task shape still rules and the substrate story is only a partial rescue.
  • All production evidence remains output-count grade; the reconcile step's own failure modes — bad merges, forwarded-but-stale state — are named and unquantified. And nobody has measured spec → tickets, where fleets get their work and where the reconciliation hypothesis says the value is actually created.
