Can the harness improve itself? Everything is settled except the one thing you'd buy it for

As of 2026-08, the mechanics of automated harness improvement are largely worked out. We know what a self-improving harness has to be able to read (raw execution traces, not summaries of them). We know which parts of it are worth editing (structure — tools, middleware, memory; not prompt wording). We know the two ways its acceptance step fails, and we know the fix for each. We know a step upstream of the whole loop is weak enough to confound the results. And there is now a product category selling the finished idea.

What we do not know is the one thing the pitch rests on:

Does a search over harness configurations beat spending the same budget on the agent?

On coding — the domain this vault exists for — that question has been asked once under controlled conditions, answered no, and never re-run. Everything else here is either evidence about the mechanism or evidence from another domain. That is why the map's status is contested rather than emerging, and why it has stayed contested through five subsequent arrivals, including a strong positive one on 2026-08-08.

This map forked out of the agent harness architecture map on 2026-08-05. That map asks how a harness should be built; this one asks whether it can build itself. The split is a boundary between a design claim and an optimization claim: every hand-engineered lever over there is judged by whether it improves an agent, and every claim here is judged by whether a search over levers beats spending the same budget elsewhere. The parent's contested status was carried entirely on this limb, so the downgrade moved here with the evidence and the parent returned to consolidating.

Part 1 — What is settled

These four hold regardless of how the contest resolves. Three of them are things to do; one is a thing to stop assuming.

Make the harness observable before you try to evolve it

The founding result [T1] — Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution — automated what this vault had treated as manual work. Represent every editable component explicitly, distill trajectories into an evidence corpus, and pair each proposed change with a prediction verified against outcome. Harness evolution stops being hand-tuning and becomes a falsifiable control loop: Terminal-Bench 2 69.7% → 77.0% over ten iterations, transferring to SWE-bench Verified at 12% fewer tokens and +5.1–10.1 points cross-family.

A second, apparently independent lineage reached the same design without citing the first. TTHE: Test-Time Harness Evolution [T1] maintains a population of candidate harnesses — arbitrary Python control programs — refined by a proposer reading their execution traces, with solver, proposer and judge all the same frozen model in different roles and gold labels used only for reporting. SWE-bench Verified 20 → 35%, LiveCodeBench 30 → 38.3%, DS-1000 38 → 44%. The convergence is the point: two groups reached "distill the traces, let the harness rewrite itself, verify against outcome" independently.

Spend the effort on structure, not on prompt wording. Both ablations say the same thing: structural components — tools, middleware, memory — drive the gains, and system-prompt edits contribute minimally.

And the harness can be re-tuned per test distribution, not only per model. TTHE runs at test time on unlabeled execution traces with the model frozen. Keep the caveat attached: it is transductive, which is exactly what the contest below turns on.

Give the optimizer raw traces — summarizing the feedback is worse than not compressing it

This is the most transferable finding on the map, and it inverts a habit. Meta-Harness [T1] ran an information ablation holding proposer, search procedure and budget fixed, varying only what the proposer may read:

What the proposer seesMedianBest
Scores only34.641.3
Scores plus an LLM summary34.938.7
Full execution-trace access50.056.7

The summarized condition is worse than raw scores on best-candidate accuracy. It spent tokens and removed signal.

What makes trace access practical is the interface: a filesystem the proposer queries with grep and cat rather than a prompt it is handed — 10.0 MTok/iteration of available diagnostics against 0.002–0.026 for every prior text optimizer, a median 82 files read per iteration, single evaluations emitting up to 10M tokens.

The mechanism is horizon. A storage or retrieval decision surfaces as a failure many steps later, so compression removes exactly the evidence needed to trace back to it. This does not contradict the vault's summarization findings, which measure an agent executing. The rule that reconciles them: compress what the actor carries forward; preserve what the diagnostician can reach back for.

Worth noting alongside: the winner beats ACE (Agentic Context Engineering) by 7.7 points at 4.5× fewer context tokens, with a Pareto frontier reported rather than a single operating point.

Gate every accepted edit — the acceptance step is where self-evolution actually breaks

Two failure modes, both measured, both with cheap fixes. This is the part of the map with the least tension and the most immediate value.

The judge is a ceiling, and it is measurable. In TTHE's unlabeled setting the committed harness reaches 50.0% where an oracle over the candidates the judge actually inspected reaches 64.0%, and an oracle over all candidates ever generated reaches 70.0%. Fourteen points lost to selection, six more to coverage. The judge prefers outputs that execute cleanly but are wrong while "claiming near-perfect correctness" — and because proposer and judge share the frozen model, extra search budget produces correlated variants repeating the same blind spots, so more rounds can make it worse. The usual defence against a gameable evaluator — pair it with a non-LLM ground-truth gate — is definitionally unavailable when there is no gold. If you ship self-evolution, the proxy's false-positive profile is a first-class engineering artifact.

The optimizer can invent the failure it fixes. Phantom Guardrails [T1] built a deterministic micro-lab where the correct self-edit is no action. Proposers fabricated guardrails for nonexistent rules in 15/60 runs when all-legal input carried a rule-shaped pattern (0/60 on featureless input; 0/180 for other pattern shapes). Three conditions were each necessary: a rule-shaped pattern in the data, an unstated rule-set completeness, and failure-presupposing instructions. Under suppression-only acceptance the phantom enters monotonically and can never be demerited, because a no-op guard is invisible to the metric. That is a new failure shape — not reward hacking, not over-refusal, but unwarranted complexity the score cannot see.

The fix is cheap and ablation-clean: warrant-aware acceptance, where a self-edit lands only with oracle-confirmed failing-episode citations. Fabrication 0/60, all rounds.

As of 2026-08-08 the acceptance gate has a second form and a price. Learning Globally Reusable Skills for Coding Agents [T1] attacks the same defect one level out — not "did this edit cite a real failure" but "does this edit break something else." The diagnosis is that skill evolution is normally a sequence of local updates: one failure, one patch, admitted unverified. Two things go wrong. A proposal generated in isolation invalidates assumptions sibling skills rely on — their example is tightening a repository-exploration skill to truncate file output, which silently breaks the downstream analysis skills that assumed complete contents. And a per-failure edit overfits to the case that produced it.

Three mechanisms answer it: an explicit skill relation graph co-evolved with the bank, cluster-based consolidation that abstracts reusable capability out of local proposals, and replay-driven verification that rejects an update which regresses prior episodes. The ablation is what matters:

RemovedTest generation F1False-positive filtering F1
Skill relation graph0.31 → 0.270.70 → 0.59
Consolidation + replay0.31 → 0.270.70 → 0.54

Four to sixteen F1 points, and neither mechanism dominates. This vault already carried the damage empirically — an evolved skill set regresses about as much as it gains. What is new is that the regression is not intrinsic to accumulating skills. It is the price of admitting updates without replaying what they might break.

Two riders worth taking even if you never run the method. Consolidation is a token intervention as well as a quality one: every skill method raised downstream execution cost over the bare agent by roughly a quarter, and the consolidated bank was the cheapest of four to carry (+21.2% against +25.4% to +26.8%). And the implementation detail is worth stealing wholesale — the skill bank is versioned with a git worktree, with traces, replay outcomes and update states in SQLite, so concurrent evolution runs cannot interfere and every accepted version is reproducible. A self-modifying artifact needs a rollback story.

Stop assuming the loop can find the edit site

Harness Handbook [T1] measures a step upstream of everything above. Modification requests describe behavior; repositories are organized by files. Unaided, a strong planner reaches 38.3% symbol-level localization F1 on a 2,267-file production harness. A generated behavior-centric index whose every candidate is re-verified against live source lifts that to 57.1%, file-level 46.6% → 61.8%, at 8.6–12.7% fewer planner tokens, with gains concentrated on search-hostile (+33.3 pp) and cross-file (+20.0 pp) requests.

Why this matters for the contest: an evolution loop that cannot find the edit site is not a fair test of whether evolutionary search works. It does not un-contest anything — nothing here demonstrates a durable generalizing gain — but it is the first evidence the two halves are separable, and it names a cheap experiment nobody has run: rerun the matched-budget comparison with localization held fixed.

Carry it at single-study strength and read the asterisk. The dependent variable is an LLM-judged edit plan — nothing applied, compiled or tested — the two judges disagree by roughly 2× on one system's magnitude, and the only baseline is unaided browsing.

Part 2 — The contest, stated honestly

The case against

Two T1s landed together on 2026-07-18 and the map downgraded on both.

Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents [T1] makes an argument that is methodological and hard to escape: harness evolution is itself a search procedure that repeatedly runs and revises candidates, so crediting its gain over a single-shot baseline conflates the design discovered with the compute spent discovering it. Match feedback and inference budget across arms on Terminal-Bench 2.1:

ArmScore
Harness evolution67.4
Direct sampling baseline68.2
Harness scaling71.8
Parallel sampling72.3

With unit tests the gap widens further. And the decisive number is generalization: held-out tasks gain +0.6 points, and +0.0 on one of the three models. The evolution fitted the tasks it searched.

Independently, the human version fares no better. How Scaffolding Evolution Shapes Coding Agent Quality [T1] froze the model and tracked 35 sequential releases of one production CLI scaffolding against 50 stratified SWE-bench Verified tasks: resolve rate swings 23.0%–39.0% with no significant trend (ρ = 0.208, p = 0.231), while average tokens rise 391K → 668K (+70%) monotonically. Regression risk concentrates in the LLM-provider layer and context management.

Together: no version of harness improvement, automated or human, has demonstrated a durable generalizing gain under controlled measurement on coding.

Note carefully what survives. Harness scaling still beat the baseline (71.8 vs 68.2), so structural search is not worthless. The refuted claim is narrower and sharper: the evolutionary loop does not beat a dumber use of the same compute.

The case for — and its exact boundary

Meta-Harness answers both halves of that objection, in two of three domains.

Matched budget: a compute-matched Best-of-N control (34.0 median / 44.2 best) against a searched harness (50.0 / 56.7). Structure in the search bought what the same compute spent dumbly did not.

Held-out transfer: nine unseen classification datasets at 73.1% against 70.2% for the strongest hand-designed baseline (best on 6/9 — and naively adding few-shot examples hurt on 7/9), plus a single discovered retrieval harness lifting 200 previously-unseen IMO-level problems by +4.7 points across five models never used during search. That last result is the important one: transfer to models the search never saw is durable scaffolding rather than model compensation, and it is the first time this map has that distinction measured rather than argued.

And on coding it answers nothing. The Terminal-Bench-2 arm (76.4 with one frontier model; 37.6 with a small one, ranking first among agents built on it) searches and scores on the same 89 tasks, framed deliberately as a "discovery problem," with leakage audited by manual inspection and regex — which catches copied strings, not a harness shaped to the task distribution. Those are search-set numbers. The limb stays contested exactly where it was contested.

What 2026-08-08 changed, and what it did not

The skill-evolution arrival is the first coding-domain result on this map whose only reported numbers are held-out. Evaluation is one-project-held-out: all cases from one project are withheld while the remaining projects evolve the bank, so no evaluated focal method or bug report is ever seen during evolution. It runs across two independently-implemented harnesses with tools, environment, base model and per-task budget held fixed, on 108 manually verified triggerable focal methods across 9 Java projects and 500 committer-labelled Go bug reports from 8 production repositories, plus a deployment on an industrial internal agent that already beat both open-source agents (F1 0.43 → 0.71).

That answers the transfer half of the 2026-07-18 objection on a coding-adjacent domain, for the first time, against a prior of +0.6 points.

It does not answer the other half. There is no compute-matched control anywhere in it. The comparisons are against other evolution frameworks and against expert-authored skills — never against spending the same evolution budget on test-time sampling, which is the precise comparison that produced the downgrade. Two further boundaries: both tasks are quality-assurance shaped (write a failing test; classify a report) rather than repository-modification, so nothing here speaks to resolve rates; and there are no seeds, variance or intervals in any cell.

So the honest position as of 2026-08 is unchanged in structure and narrower in scope. The strongest positive result and the strongest negative result are on different task families, and neither group has run the other's arm.

The operative rule

Until someone re-runs this properly, one question does most of the work when you read any harness-search result, including a good one:

Ask which distribution the number was measured on, and demand the compute-matched control. A gain measured on the tasks the search optimized against is a search-set number, whatever else it is.

And one practice: pin your scaffolding version like you pin your model. Thirty-five releases with a frozen model swung resolve rate 16 points with no trend — a range exceeding most claimed model-to-model gaps in this vault.

Part 3 — The product category arrived first

Mid-2026 saw a meta-harness layer ship from unrelated vendors: an orchestration tier above Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Hermes and Pi — Omnigent [T2], Apache 2.0, alpha — plus a metaharness generator [T3] that mints bespoke per-repo harnesses across six runtimes on the thesis "the model is replaceable; the harness is the product."

The load-bearing promise is composition: combine multiple models, harnesses, and techniques without rewriting code. Set that against what this vault's best-replicated finding says results do when a harness swaps: 8.6-point mean shift with rank-order change, a security outcome reversing 10/10 → 9/30, a context pack worth double digits under one harness and zero under another, a byte-identical skill library read on 99–100% of tasks under one stack and 0–1% under another.

A uniform API makes harnesses substitutable at the call site, not equivalent in outcome — and no product in the category measures the gap. Their own per-runtime adapter tables are tacit evidence they know it exists.

Two consequences, and one thing that survives untouched.

Treat harness portability as a migration, not a configuration change, and re-run your evaluation on the target pair. The settling experiment is cheap and nobody has run it: one workload, two harnesses, one mediating layer, publish the delta.

Demand the matched-budget held-out number and the mutation-acceptance warrant before adopting a self-improving harness. The generator ships the self-evolution loop this map cannot validate with no acceptance criterion, no held-out split and no warrant published — the last being exactly the intervention that drove fabrication 15/60 → 0/60.

The governance tier survives the pair thesis. Policy at three scopes, spend caps that pause rather than kill, mandatory OS sandboxing — all enforced at the runner, below the prompt, on the dispatch path the harness already mediates. That does not require harnesses to be equivalent, only wrapped.

What would move this

The map is contested and the resolving experiments are all cheap, which is the frustrating part.

  • The matched-budget held-out arm, on coding. Run harness evolution against compute-matched Best-of-N with disjoint search and evaluation task sets, and report the held-out number. One group has done this outside coding and found for; one group has done it on coding and found against. Nobody has done both.
  • The same comparison with localization held fixed, since a loop that reaches 38.3% F1 at finding the edit site may be failing at retrieval while wearing a search failure's clothes.
  • A compute-matched arm for the replay-gated skill result — it is the strongest held-out coding-adjacent evidence on this map and it is missing the one control that would let it move the status.
  • The interchangeability delta: one workload, two harnesses, one meta-harness layer, publish the number.

Until then, the buildable parts stand on their own: make the harness observable, hand the optimizer raw traces rather than summaries, edit structure rather than prompt wording, gate every accepted change on a real cited failure and a replay of what it might break, and version the artifact so you can roll it back. Those are worth doing whether or not the search beats the sampler.
