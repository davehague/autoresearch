Measure the null: what your agent's improvement would have been anyway

As of 2026-08, thirteen independent results say the same thing about agent evaluation, and none of them is about contamination, benchmark quality, or judge bias. Those are the parent map's subject: is this number a valid measurement of the agent. This is the separable question underneath most published agentic-engineering claims: is the difference between two numbers a result at all.

The two come apart. A benchmark can be uncontaminated, its harness fully disclosed, its judge honestly calibrated — and the +7-point win you measured on it can still be noise, arithmetic, a base rate, an implementation you happened to draw, or the extra sample your treatment quietly bought. Every finding below is an instance of one demand:

Find out what the number would be if the mechanism you are claiming did not exist. Subtract that first.

The demand is not new. What is new, and what makes this worth a piece rather than a footnote, is that between 2026-07-11 and 2026-08-05 five independent groups measured that null on five different subjects and it came back large every time — often larger than the effects the field publishes. This is a young map, forked out of evaluation integrity on 2026-08-03, and its status is consolidating rather than consensus for a reason stated plainly at the end: almost nothing here has been replicated on a second benchmark, and the field's published practice has not moved.

Part 1 — The null catalogue

There is no general null. There is a null per subject, and the whole trick is naming the right one. Six are now measured.

For a coordination or architecture delta, the null is a paired same-model floor. Take two coordination protocols. Make them configuration-equivalent at trial 0 — verified by code inspection plus a 40/40 SHA-256 payload audit, so that neither mechanism can possibly have acted yet. They still disagree: paired gaps of +10 pp and 0 pp across two n=100 seeds, an observed envelope of [−3, +18] pp, and a pooled upper Wilson bound of ≲15 pp [T1] (How Much Coordination Gain Is Real?). The power arithmetic is the part to internalize: detecting a 5 pp effect needs n ≈ 1,003 paired; detecting 15 pp needs 110. The field routinely claims 2–10 pp wins on n ≈ 100, once, unpaired. Seven of ten headline effects in that paper's motivating table fall below its own local floor — flagged by its authors as illustrative rather than systematic, and worth carrying that way.

The authors also did the thing this map most wants to see: their own strongest single-seed finding failed to replicate at a second seed, and they retract it inside the paper.

For a coordination claim specifically, there is a second missing arm: one agent, in order. This is the newest member of the family (2026-08-05) and the cheapest to run. "Coordination helps" is silently two claims — beats unconstrained parallel execution, which says the mechanism suppresses interference between concurrent writers, and beats one agent working serially, which says the architecture is worth building. The literature reports the first and is read as the second.

On the one study that instrumented both, they separate completely. 30 frozen CooperBench feature pairs × 4 arms × 3 coder seeds = 360 executions, with the planner's declarations generated once and frozen across every arm and seed. Deterministic pre-write admission beat unconstrained parallel execution by +26.7 pp (23.3% → 50.0% pair pass, 95% cluster bootstrap [9.6, 60.0]) and matched the always-serial arm at 50.0% vs 50.0% — not approximately, but with identical pair-pass rates inside every repository-task cluster, on a longer critical path (188.0 s against 181.4 s) [T1] (Claim Plane). It bought its reliability by serializing 87 of 90 executions.

For a self-repair or retry loop, the null is another attempt at the same budget with the content blanked. Not "no loop" — another sample. Decomposed that way on MBPP+ across three model scales with four conditions at eight attempts on 378 tasks, the entire informational payload of execution feedback (feedback minus a content-free failure notice) is +0.000 / +0.026 / +0.008, none significant at any scale, while merely putting the failed attempt back in context costs −0.061 (p = 0.006) and −0.069 (p < 0.001) [T1 at ≤7B] (Try Again, Don't Look Back).

This one is different in kind from the others, and the difference is worth naming. A paired floor shrinks an effect. A majority-class baseline removes one. This reverses a sign, because the confound (an extra sample, positive) and the treatment (self-exposure, negative) push opposite ways and the conventional design sums them. "Our repair loop adds X points" may be reporting a benefit its own intervention is subtracting from. The arm costs nothing: it is the treatment with the payload deleted.

For a classifier, the null is the majority-class baseline. At a ~91.8% positive base rate, predicting "merged" for everything scores F1 0.957 / accuracy 0.918 / recall 1.000 / AUC 0.500; the best learned model scores F1 0.958, a gain of 0.001. The abstract reports "F1 scores above 0.95" and the discriminating metric, AUC, sits at 0.676 [T1] (Predicting Acceptance and Review Effort).

Carry this as a fair example rather than a snipe: the paper says outright that the baseline's AUC of 0.500 "shows that it has no ability to rank," and recommends triage over automated decisions. The failure is one of emphasis — which number goes in the abstract, and therefore which number travels. The base rate itself is never printed anywhere in the paper and has to be back-inferred from the majority-baseline row.

Reading rule. For any classification result, find the majority-class baseline before reading the headline metric. If it is absent, treat accuracy and F1 as unreported.

For a long-horizon claim, the null is the composed stage product. Four independent repairs at 80% each compose to 0.8⁴ ≈ 41% with no horizon mechanism whatsoever. A benchmark reporting 41% end-to-end has measured multiplication; one reporting 10% has a fourfold shortfall — log(0.41/0.10) ≈ 1.41 nats — and that residual is the finding [T3] (Benchmarking the Residual).

Discount this one properly. It is a position paper with no experiment: the residual has never been computed on a real benchmark by its authors, the product baseline assumes constant stage accuracy and independent failures, and the estimator is unstable exactly where agents currently sit, since a near-zero prediction makes an all-failure outcome diagnostically worthless. It changes what the map asks of evidence rather than what the map believes. Its most useful export is a distinction: a deployment benchmark asks whether the whole system succeeds under realistic budgets; a diagnostic benchmark asks which controlled contrast explains behaviour that local competence does not. Read strictly, most long-horizon evidence in this vault is valid deployment evidence and not yet diagnostic.

For a continuous metric, the null may not be stable enough to be a null. Replaying the official reference patches of three performance-optimization benchmarks across cloud machine types (740 tasks), the references satisfy their own validity rules on only 38% (GSO) / 8% (SWE-Perf) / 82% (SWE-fficiency) of tasks; scoring-rule choice flips 9 of 28 pairwise rankings; and public submissions already match or exceed the reference on 85.3% of replay-valid tasks [T1] (Are Performance-Optimization Benchmarks Reliably Measuring Coding Agents?). A speedup is an environment-conditioned claim. A paired agent-vs-agent delta on single-run cells means nothing when the reference itself varies across hardware.

Part 2 — The variance is not where you are controlling it

Every null above controls a known noise source. The uncomfortable result of 2026-07-31 is that the field's standard reliability move — repeat the run, fix the seed — controls the smallest one.

Freeze a mechanism as an outcome-blind card. Implement it three times in fresh sessions. Replay each artifact byte-identically. Across 312 assignments (13 OpenML tasks × 4 frozen cards × 3 implementations × 2 coding-agent setups), same-artifact rerun variance is 4–6% of within-task variation — zero where the evaluator is deterministic — while implementation choice is 33–45%, and 71–82% in the deterministic arm [T1] (One Run Is Not an Idea).

The number to carry is the decision consequence, not the variance share: leave-one-implementation-out winner reversal of 25.6% and 43.6%, surviving two outcome-blind degeneracy filters and the full penalty grid. One frozen card produced three valid, blind-faithful, exactly-reproducing realizations spanning +2.31% to −1.85%, reversing every one-shot choice against a competitor at +0.46%.

A score is honest evidence about the artifact it measured, and a lottery ticket the moment it is credited to the mechanism — which is what happens whenever a result prunes a branch, licenses transferring an approach, or lands in an agent's persistent memory.

One layer up, the same decomposition on computer-use agents finds the noise living upstream again — in which data you drew. A crossed grid of 3 independent data draws × 8 training seeds, every checkpoint re-scored, yields two invariants in every cell: σ_eval ≈ 0 (re-scoring a frozen checkpoint is deterministic) and σ_train ≤ 10%. The data draw takes ~16–22% where the intervention works reliably and 48%, dominant, on the hardest cell — its share rising as the effect becomes marginal, which is the regime every interesting agent result occupies. Same trainer, same data, same config, run twice: 0.18 vs 0.68 [T3 — method transfers, magnitudes do not] (Teach it to stop, not just to click).

Its corollary is the indictment. Estimating variance from subsamples of one batch instead of independent draws halves it (0.18 vs 0.36) and erases the catastrophic-failure mode entirely — 0/24 runs below 0.10, against 3/10 under independent draws. The cheap version of variance reporting hides the instability it exists to expose.

Three reporting rules fall out, and they are the practical core of this map:

  1. Characterize the distribution's shape before reporting a mean. The hard cell is bimodal (modes ≈0.09 and ≈0.80; Hartigan dip p = 0.07 at k = 10, ΔBIC = 13, and underpowered at k = 5, p = 0.38 — it takes the tenth seed). Mean ± std is structurally wrong there, and a single run has ~30% chance of landing in the failure mode.
  2. Set k from your own σ and the effect you mean to claim. Existence is cheap at k ≤ 5. Ordering two cells Δ ≈ 0.18 apart at σ ≈ 0.3 needs k ≈ 46. A k-seed mean predicts a held-out half 1.3–1.7× better than a single run.
  3. Buy tasks, not repeats. At 17 tasks × 3 repeats, even a true 30 pp effect is detected 57% of the time; 80% power on a 10 pp effect needs 120–200 tasks; and raising repeats 3 → 10 at fixed n moves power only 13% → 58%. Task-level variance dominates [T1] (Do Context Files Help Coding Agents?).

At the improvement magnitudes the field actually publishes (+7.7 points; +0.2 task success), a single-run report has the wrong sign 33–44% of the time in that variance regime.

Part 3 — Denominators, bands, and the checks that cannot see

Three failures that are not about variance at all.

Report the attempt count, not only the run count. In one controlled study only 50.9% of attempts produced a scoreable artifact — 29 valid against 28 excluded — and 20 of the 28 exclusions fell in a single condition, so missingness was condition-correlated and probably not random [T3] (How Agent Skills Fail under Long Contexts). Model failure and operational scoreability are different denominators. Pooling them inflates the apparent failure rate; hiding them puts a silent selection effect on the published comparison. The author's handling is the practice to copy: define a valid run, keep exclusions in a separate operational denominator with a per-cause audit, and say so.

A task set has no fixed informative band. Running the same 15 real tasks on two frontier agents, difficulty transfers (ρ = 0.75, p = 0.001) but the band where an intervention could show an effect does not: 6 of 15 tasks are borderline for exactly one agent and 6 differ in floor/ceiling status, so on roughly 40% of the set the agent that would reveal an effect is not the one under test. That is enough to reconcile two published studies that disagreed about context files without either being wrong.

So a reported null needs the same scrutiny as a reported effect. An apparatus asymmetry manufactures a difference; a task-set/agent mismatch manufactures its absence.

An artifact check is structurally blind to whether the procedure that establishes the artifact's validity ran. Across 30 skills, a prose agent performs 56% (gpt-4o) and 68% (gpt-5) of the steps its own skill mandates while producing artifacts that pass output checks throughout, completing the entire prescribed procedure in only 28% of runs [T1] (SIGIL). The two sharpest cases are the ones where the skipped step was the verification: a skill whose stated Iron Law forbids any completion claim without fresh verification evidence is satisfied 30% of the time, with the agent writing "all tests pass, build succeeds" straight into the deliverable.

This is not a precision gap in the gate. The omission is outside the gate's field of view. The remedy needs no compiler: decide which mandated steps leave machine-checkable evidence, and grade that.

A dating caution rides along from the same study, and it applies to this whole vault: prose compliance is a model-version snapshot (56% → 68% across one generation) while the structural number is flat at 86% → 86%. Figures like the 8.3% context-file logging-compliance result should be read as dated, not constant.

Part 4 — And when the null is what you found

The mirror-image problem is how to publish a zero. The August 2026 example is worth copying wholesale [T1] (Test-time reasoning effort and unauthorized tool use).

Decide before collecting what precision the planned sample buys, and make that the claim. With zero-event arms anticipated, the frozen plan fixed the equivalence margin at ±2U, where U = 3.50% is the exact one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson limit at the planned n = 84 — ±7.01 pp, described as "the resolution available at zero events given the planned sample size." The observed estimand came in at 0.000 with a simultaneous exact interval of ±4.34 pp, and the conclusion is equivalence within a pre-declared margin, not "no significant difference." The bounds are closed-form functions of the arm counts; no model is fitted, and the prespecified mixed-effects logistic is declared unfittable in print because with no outcome variation its coefficients are not identifiable.

Three cheap practices ride along:

  • An adversarial missingness table. Recompute every arm counting every invalid or superseded attempt as if it had been a failure. The worst arm reaches 25.88%, and the conclusion survives. That answers the denominator problem with a bound instead of a caveat.
  • Deterministic judges, validated on both sides. The primary outcome is read from the tool log and terminal state, with final-answer prose contributing only to validity checks — so claims in text neither earn credit nor establish violations. Two audited human rounds of 40 trajectories agreed 40/40 on every field, and synthetic violation fixtures serve as positive controls, proving the instrument can fire before a zero is reported from it.
  • Amendments frozen before the data are seen. Three deviations are logged, each frozen before the corresponding outcomes were viewed, including two whole-scenario reruns carrying a pre-committed acceptance criterion and a pre-committed fallback.

And the reason to hold this up as a model rather than a trophy: the same discipline surfaces the confound the design could not remove. Collection order was fixed — all low-effort trajectories before max-effort ones within each scenario — so every effort contrast is entangled with collection time, and the authors print it. A study that reports its own uncontrolled variable next to a bounded null is telling you exactly how much of its claim to spend.

Two design choices from the coordination study belong here too, and neither depends on its result. Freeze the upstream stage across arms: the 60 planner declarations were generated once under a fixed seed and reused across all four arms and all three coder seeds, so planner variation cannot load onto the policy contrast. Resample the cluster, not the execution: the bootstrap unit is repository + task, because three coder seeds on one pair are correlated observations rather than independent samples. Seven clusters is a thin base and the interval says so — a +26.7 pp point estimate carries [9.6, 60.0]. Resampling the 360 executions would have produced a tight interval around a number the design cannot support.

Part 5 — What is not settled

Three things, stated as open rather than smoothed over.

The floors do not transfer, and their authors say so. The paired coordination floor is explicitly local — its own cross-model probes confirm it — so the number is not portable. The protocol is the contribution. The same applies to the variance decomposition: it is computer-use agents, taken for a domain-general method and explicitly not for magnitudes, on proprietary unreleased environments.

Nobody has run these protocols on the deltas that matter most. Loop engineering's three-armed coordination sweep (raw pool vs event passing vs verified-gist blackboard) is still unrun after seven weeks — and it now needs a fourth arm, several independent implementations per arm because its contested effects are the same order as the 25.6–43.6% reversal rate, and pairing throughout. Two fleet results the field leans on, +8.3 points on SWE-bench Pro and +9.3–10.5 on SWE-bench Verified, report gains over strong single agents and not over a serial execution of the same decomposition under the same discipline. Neither is refuted. Both are missing an arm that has been shown, once, to swallow an entire effect.

Published practice has not moved. This vault is still ingesting single-run, unpaired deltas weekly, from good groups, in good venues.

The checklist

Before you believe a delta — someone else's or your own:

  1. Name the null for this subject. Paired same-model floor for architectures. Always-serial arm for coordination. Content-free placebo for retry loops. Majority-class baseline for classifiers. Composed stage product for long horizons.
  2. Ask what the treatment bought besides its mechanism. An extra sample is the commonest hidden gift, and it can outweigh the intervention.
  3. Check the denominator. How many attempts produced no scoreable run, and were they evenly spread across conditions?
  4. Find the base rate. If it is not printed, the classification metrics are unreported.
  5. Count the implementations, not the seeds. One implementation of an idea is one draw from a distribution wide enough to reverse the winner a quarter to two-fifths of the time.
  6. Look at the distribution's shape before the mean. Bimodality is invisible at k = 5 and structurally breaks mean ± std.
  7. Buy tasks before repeats. Task-level variance dominates; repeats are the expensive way to buy a little power.
  8. Ask what the check cannot see. An output test is blind to a skipped verification step. Grade the steps that leave machine-checkable evidence.
  9. If you found a null, bound it. Fix the margin from the exact limit at your planned n before collecting, publish an adversarial missingness table, and print the confound you could not remove.

None of this is expensive. The placebo arm is the treatment with its payload deleted. The serial arm is the work you were going to do anyway, in order. The majority-class baseline is one line. What they cost is the possibility that the effect goes away — which is the point.
