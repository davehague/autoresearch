The score belongs to the pair: what you actually measured when you measured an agent

As of 2026-08, more than a dozen independent groups have measured the same thing on more than a dozen different subjects, and it comes back large every time. The thing is the apparatus: the loop, the scaffold, the container, the wrapper on the plumbing, the inference backend, the review protocol, the calibration corpus, the sandbox policy, the transport between a model's reply and the shell, the provider's health at the moment the benchmark ran. Not whether the benchmark was clean, not whether the labels were right, not whether the judge was honest — that is the parent map's subject, evaluation integrity, and it asks whether a number is a valid measurement of the agent. This map asks the separable question underneath it:

Does the number describe the agent, or the box you ran it in?

A benchmark can be uncontaminated, correctly labelled and honestly judged, and still report a figure that is eight to twenty-one points of somebody else's infrastructure — or, at the extreme now on record, thirty-seven.

Three weeks of arrivals have changed this piece in ways that are worth stating before the evidence. The core claim held everywhere it was tested and, on two occasions, in a form more consequential than anything here in early August: a published post-training gain reverses sign under a different harness, and two models one generation apart from the same vendor swap leaderboard places when the review pipeline changes. But two of the recommendations below have been overturned or sharpened by measurement rather than by argument. The term turns out not to be distributed across scaffold details — it is concentrated at one architectural boundary, and gridding four similar harnesses will report a small term and be read as evidence the term is small. And the term is now known to be designable and portable, which makes the comparison hazard worse rather than better, because a portable offset does not average out over a leaderboard.

The status is consolidating, not consensus — held there for reasons stated plainly at the end, the sharpest of which is that the field's published practice has not moved at all.

Part 1 — Ten layers, one result

Read these as a stack. Each is a place where two teams running "the same evaluation" can get different numbers with nothing about the agent differing.

The loop

The control surface above the harness — the thing that carries objectives, progress criteria and residual work across an extended run. LoopsBench [T1] names it and sweeps it in both directions on 112 dependency-graph tasks. Hold the model fixed and vary only the loop: resolve rate runs 18.75% down to 7.14%, a 2.6× ratio. Hold the loop fixed and vary the model across eight backends: 25.00% down to 4.46%. The two spreads are the same order.

A single boolean in the runner — whether an outer continuation loop restarts the agent on residual work — is worth +3.6 to +8.0 points in every cell of both sweeps, which is larger than most published model-to-model gaps in this vault.

The agent scaffold

Tencent WorkBuddy Bench [T1]: seven models × 260 tasks × three runs under two harnesses, mean absolute shift 8.6 points, one cell moving 13.5 with nothing varied but the harness. The authors say it themselves: "the harness is visibly not a neutral measurement instrument."

PolyWorkBench [T1] answered the two obvious objections — only two harnesses, one of them the publishing vendor's — with an unprompted four-harness matrix, none of them the authors'. Every model run under two or more harnesses spreads ≥0.08 Pass@1; one spans 0.209 on a single swap; the authors' stated range is 8–21 points.

The structural half kills the obvious remedy: the ordering of harnesses is not model-invariant. Harness A beats harness B for one model and loses for another. The Devil Is in the Interface [T1] makes that as clean as it gets — 11,700 trajectories over six tool architectures built to matched capability, where a natural-language search interface is Sonnet-4.5's best setup on pass^9 (+0.043) and among Kimi-K2.5's worst (−0.031 at every k), and explicit atomic tooling lowers cost for the weakest actor while raising it for the two strongest. That study also supplies the deflation this map keeps needing: resolve rate is broadly similar across all six architectures, so the metric the field uses to rank models is the one least sensitive to the harness dimension — which is a reason the confound stayed invisible, not a reason it is small.

The review pipeline, on short bounded tasks

The scaffold results were all on long-horizon issue resolution, where a scaffold has room to matter. OpenCodeReview [T1] closed that objection and found it worse on short ones. Six backends from four providers, 200 real pull requests, identical sampling: Claude-4.6-Opus is first of six under one review system and near-bottom under the other (25.10% against 11.57% SEM-F1), while Claude-4.8-Opus does the reverse. Two models from one vendor, one generation apart, swap places when the pipeline changes. The margin that settles it: the weakest configuration of the better system beats the strongest configuration of the worse one, so the system term dominates the model term across the whole model set. Precision regimes are system-owned and model-unstable (25–38% under one system for all six backends, 7–16% under another), as is cost: 352K–743K tokens per pull request against 1.2M–5.7M for the same models on the same diffs.

The plumbing between the runner and the provider

Adversarial Test-Hardening for AI-Written Code [T1] is the receipted incident, and it is the one to tell people about, because the proxy was fine. Every verdict was a rule-injected mutant and a failing test run — no model ever scored another model's output. The bias entered one level out, through an undetected 16,000-token output cap on one provider's wrapper that truncated replies and logged them under an unrelated-sounding rejection note. The cap applied to every call but only bit on verbose replies, and verbosity is a model property. Pooled p = 9.5 × 10⁻⁶⁶. Corrected: p = 0.0625, not supported.

The generalized hazard is any dimension on which the apparatus treats two models differently and which correlates with the outcome — a timeout, a token budget, a retry policy, a parser that tolerates one model's formatting quirks.

The transport between the reply and the shell

QuoteBench [T1] is the layer below the parser and, more importantly, the first design on this map that attributes rather than detects (see Part 2). Fix the model's reply and change one wrapper, and the 55.4–73.2-point loss belongs to the transport by construction. Two properties are new rather than restatements. The model's recovery is grammar-specific rather than defensive — 53/56 on the disclosed wrapper against 10/56 on the other, with adapted replies losing 28.6–64.3 points where the boundary is absent. And the failure is invisible to both instruments an operator would reach for: 23.4–47.0% of executions leaving the wrong final state exit zero, and ShellCheck flags 34.6% of the nested-only failures against 11.4% of the survivors, because each command is individually well-formed and the fault lives downstream of the text being linted.

The inference backend

What We Observe as LLM Behavior Can Be a Side-effect of Inference Backend [T1] goes below the agent entirely, to the software that renders the chat template and calls the kernel. Fully crossed: 3 models × 5 serving frameworks × 6 benchmarks × 4 generation modes, with weights, prompts, precision, hardware and evaluation protocol held constant.

The design earns its zero before it claims anything — a wrapper around the same in-process model object diverges 0.000 on all 14 metrics. Then, still under greedy decoding: LangChain diverges 0.049 on identical weights and engine, from prompt-template rendering alone; vLLM diverges 0.025 on one model of three, significant on 10 of 11 metrics, from nothing but a different attention kernel under non-associative floating-point arithmetic. Per-item disagreement reaches 48%, so a score stable in the mean can be composed of different correct answers. Roughly 39% of the term is structural and 61% is avoidable by decoding greedily and stating the generation configuration — three extra fields on a harness card, not a research programme.

The sandbox's security posture

Permission Denied [T1] adds a layer nobody had priced: twelve frozen model–harness bundles on 89 tasks under three nested policy levels. Every bundle worsens on both axes but in different currencies — −7.1 pp / +167.3% through −18.3 pp / +21.4% — so a permissive-sandbox leaderboard does not predict hardened ordering. The 2×2 crossing of two endpoint models with two harnesses is the useful part: the cost extreme follows the model while success follows the bundle. The layer that owns spend and the layer that owns capability are different ones.

The provider's health during the run

AgentChaos [T1] names the only layer here that is not under the evaluator's control at all. Injecting realistic provider faults — HTTP errors, timeouts, empty bodies, truncated replies, malformed tool-call payloads — moves pass@1 by 0.87 to 49.66 points depending on the system, with a persistent degradation regime worth 47.64–62.39 points on multi-agent systems and an intermittent one worth almost nothing. Every published pass@1 is implicitly conditioned on an undisclosed and unrecorded provider-health term, and no leaderboard entry in this vault states its error or truncation rate during the run. That makes provider health a live candidate explanation for irreproducible cells, alongside every layer above it.

The review protocol and the monitor's calibration

PRWeaver [T1] supplies the first instance on a security detection rate — the quantity teams use to decide whether a reviewer agent may gate a merge. One backbone fixed across three deployed auditing frameworks: detection spans 55 / 65 / 56%. The larger term is a configuration decision nobody prints: reviewing pull requests one at a time versus handing the auditor the whole release window moves detection 34–38 points on identical content — 50/60/54% against 16/22/22%. A resolve-rate confound wastes budget. A detection-rate confound means a gate that misses four-fifths of adversarial changes was bought on a number measured at 50–60%.

Real-Time Detection and Repair of LLM Agent Failures [T1] extends this to the instrument you would buy to watch the agent. A trajectory monitor calibrated on one model family and deployed on another sits at chance (AUROC 0.527) against 0.885 recalibrated on itself. Imported to another group's corpus, the ranking transfers zero-shot and the operating point does not — AUROC 0.745, detection 0.048 at a 5% false-alarm budget. The mechanism is disclosed rather than asserted: the detector's advantage is a monotone function of how many steps a failure has to develop after onset (+0.09 at ≤3 steps, +0.40 at ≥9), and the importing corpus has ≥9 post-onset steps on only 6.9% of its failures. The failure-shape distribution of the calibration corpus is a hidden term in every published detection rate.

The weights

DCAS [T1] is the layer that cannot be disclosed away. The open coding-agent ecosystem collects its fine-tuning trajectories almost entirely under one scaffold, and models trained on that data carry its conventions wherever they go: one reads 49.7 / 46.0 / 35.8 / 20.4 across four scaffolds; another reads 52.6 / 44.2 / 8.4 / 37.6, where the 8.4 is a tool-call format mismatch leaving most trajectories with zero parseable actions. The control makes it causal: the untrained base models those were fine-tuned from spread less across the same four scaffolds than anything fine-tuned from them. Mixing scaffolds in training has been tried — a model trained on diverse multi-format trajectories still falls from 50.5 to 26.2 and 15.6.

LEGO-RL [T1] then shows the term surviving post-training and inverting the sign of a published gain. One untrained checkpoint scores 64.0 / 62.4 / 57.2 across three harnesses; the same RL recipe gains +6.4 / +5.8 / +9.4 on it. A competitor's released post-trained model gains +3.4 under the harness its authors report and −0.4 under another, where the untuned newer base model it was built from scores higher. The authors decline to isolate a cause and state the pattern: "a gain obtained under one agent control flow need not survive another." With the model-generation term at +3.4 / +1.0 / +3.4 against a 6.8-point harness spread, this is a third independent setup where the apparatus outweighs the generation — and it is unusual in being a group measuring a competitor's artifact rather than its own. Two infrastructure observations ride along and are the same term one level down: the same sandbox stack produces a different termination mix under each harness (2.4% / 7.1% / 6.4% of trajectories excluded, timeouts dominating under one and setup failures under another).

Part 2 — Three things the measurement itself now says

Attribution has replaced detection, and it puts the map's own dominant method in question

Fourteen months of harness results here rest on swapping a scaffold and watching the ranking move. QuoteBench [T1] fixes the model's reply and changes one parser, which makes the loss belong to the transport by construction rather than by inference. It also supplies arithmetic the map had been missing: a matched gap decomposes into transport damage plus contract-conditioned compensation, and in the worked case those are −64.3 and +60.7 against a printed −3.6. A near-zero published difference was two large opposing terms.

The consequence is uncomfortable and worth stating: a whole-scaffold swap cannot attribute a reversal to any mechanism. So the method that established this map's claim is the method least able to explain it, and the claim's size is now much better evidenced than its cause.

The term is concentrated at one architectural boundary, not spread across scaffold details

This is the correction that changes the practical advice. Beyond Fault Localization [T1] reports the largest apparatus term this map holds — 37 points across six root-cause-analysis frameworks at a fixed model — and then localizes it. The three frameworks that run an open-ended adaptive investigation span only 2.0 points among themselves, while swapping the backbone model on one of them moves it 10.8.

So the term does not live in scaffold detail. It lives at the boundary between architectural classes, and once a design is on the adaptive side of that boundary a harness swap resolves almost nothing. Grid the design space, not the vendors. The failure mode this creates is specific and likely already happening: comparing four adaptive harnesses will report a small term, and that will be read as evidence the term is small. It converges with an independent finding that harness optimization separates models 1.8× more than scaffolds inside a similarly narrow band. Every framework number here is a single run the authors themselves label indicative, on one topology, with the two backbones differing 22× in cost per case.

The term is designable and portable, which makes comparison worse rather than better

StateM [T1] measures it from the constructive side: a runbook of durable states, phase-local context refresh and checked pre-commit transitions is worth +9.0 and +10.4 points on one benchmark against a +1.8-point model generation, and a frozen profile lifts a cheaper model tier above the expensive tier's reference row — at $15.20 of realized API charges against $574.68 for the matched frontier run.

AutoSaddler [T1] runs the direction this map had almost no evidence in — fix the apparatus, swap the model — and finds most of the term survives: a harness optimized with a frontier model as the task agent keeps +5.6 pp of its +9.0 pp when that model is replaced by a substantially weaker one from the same family. Read one way that is reassuring: a harness is a designable artifact, not a per-model fitting. Read as measurement, it is the harder version of the disclosure argument, because a systematic, transferable offset does not average out across a leaderboard. StarHarness [T1] states the same thing at its bluntest: on one benchmark a 27B open-weight model with an evolved harness scores 70.0% while a frontier model without it scores 50.8%.

And for the first time there is an intervention that shrinks the term rather than detecting it. Natural-Language Workflows Are Not Software Yet [T1] compiles a prose procedure into declared per-step read/write artifacts and halves the cross-executor spread, 32 points to 16 — while taking all-six-executors-agree from 48% to 80%, all-ten-runs-correct from 16% to 72%, and holding 100% of previously-passing cases under injected request errors and context compaction where prose drops to 80%. It comes with a stated mechanism (per-agent input tokens −63%), which is the first partial account here of why the term exists at all: prose hands every step every intermediate, and the executor has to infer which one was meant.

Part 3 — What to do

Reading a published number

Treat a single-harness leaderboard gap under about ten points as unresolved. Two independent grids put the harness term at 8.6 mean absolute and 8–21 points, against frontier separations of 2–14 on the same benchmarks. On a review pipeline it is larger than the whole model set's spread.

There is no correction table, at any layer. Both scaffold grids find the term to be an interaction rather than an offset — the ordering changes by model, and the tool-interface study shows it changing sign by model. So does the backend term. You cannot learn a per-harness adjustment once and subtract it.

A small measured term is not evidence of a small term — check whether the grid crossed an architectural boundary. Six frameworks span 37 points; the three adaptive ones span 2.0. A grid confined to one design class will under-report by an order of magnitude, and that is the most likely way a team gets this wrong now.

For an open model, ask what it was trained under before you ask what it scores — and for a post-trained model, ask which harness the gain was measured in. The expected drop under a non-training scaffold can be half the score, or a parser failure that zeroes it, and a reported improvement can be negative elsewhere while the untuned base outscores it.

Ask which layer the score was measured at, and assume the ones below it were not held. The scaffold grids say nothing about the serving stack; the serving-stack study has no agent in it; nobody reports provider health or sandbox policy level. The crossed experiment has still never been run.

Building a system

A candidate model evaluated on someone else's harness has not been evaluated for your system. The cheapest pre-deployment test is a run under your own scaffold, and it is not optional. Run it the other way too: re-run your harness comparison when your model changes, because two models one generation apart swapped review-pipeline places.

Prefer the intervention that shrinks the term to the one that measures it. Declaring per-step read/write artifacts halved cross-executor spread and took ten-run consistency from 16% to 72%. That is a design change available today, with a mechanism attached, and it makes the confound question smaller rather than better documented.

The review unit is a security parameter. Per-PR review retained 50–60% where whole-window review retained 16–22% on identical adversarial content. More context is not monotone: the narrower window is the safer default.

A monitor evaluated on someone else's deployment has not been evaluated for yours, and the specific thing you cannot import is the threshold. Ranking may transfer. The operating point will not.

Where a deterministic invariant exists, use it — the confound question does not arise. In the same study whose monitor sits at chance across model families, three recomputation checks transfer across families with nothing retuned, at 110 of 110 detections and 0 of 10 false positives.

And do not trust an exit code to tell you the apparatus behaved. Nearly a quarter to nearly half of executions leaving the wrong final state exited zero.

Publishing a number

Ship a harness card. The fields with measured justification are now: the loop implementation and its continuation policy; the agent scaffold, name and version; the container specification and sandbox policy level; the inference backend, its version and its generation configuration; the transport or wrapper between the model's reply and the shell; the provider error and truncation rate observed during the run; and — for anything that gates — the review unit. For an open model, add the collection scaffold of its training trajectories; for a post-trained one, report at least one arm outside the harness the gain was measured in.

Part 4 — What is not settled

The remedy is demonstrably incomplete, and provably so at one layer. Nothing establishes that any proposed field list is sufficient — the plumbing incident was a wrapper-level output cap no schema would have caught. And a scaffold baked into fine-tuning weights is not a reporting failure, so no disclosure practice repairs it.

Size is now much better evidenced than cause. The one design that attributes rather than detects reaches a single layer, the transport. At every other layer the map knows the term is large and cannot say what in the scaffold produces it — and the localization result says the answer is an architectural property rather than a set of tunable details, which is the harder thing to characterize.

The crossed experiment has never been run. Same benchmark, two scaffolds × two backends. Since both terms are model-dependent interactions rather than offsets, an interaction is the likelier prior — and would mean neither term can be honestly reported alone.

The transfer result runs in one direction only. A harness tuned on a strong model keeps most of its value on a weak one. The arm that would decide the matter — a harness tuned against a weak model, applied to a strong one — is unrun, and it is the arm that determines whether an evolved harness is a portable artifact or a mismatch compensator. The evidence leans toward compensation: gains shrink as the model strengthens on two of three benchmarks in one study, and skill banks that help a weak harness by +0.14 help a strong one by +0.01.

The backend result has never been measured on an agentic task. Its authors exclude code, multi-turn dialogue and agentic workflows — exactly where output length, stopping behaviour and tool-call formatting interact with the serving stack most. A long agent trajectory contains hundreds of generations, each a chance for a template difference to compound.

Nobody has tested whether partial adjustment recovers anything. Whether a rank-preserving within-harness comparison is usable, or whether cross-harness comparison is simply void, is unmeasured.

And the honest limit on this whole map. The two largest scaffold grids are two and four harnesses on one benchmark each. The backend study is at ~1B scale on non-agentic tasks. The weights study is four models on one benchmark at single runs per cell. The 37-point localization is single runs the authors label indicative. Several of the strongest entries here are single studies, two by single independent authors. What makes this consolidating rather than emerging is that a dozen groups working on a dozen different layers, with no shared method, all found the apparatus term to be of the order of the effect being reported. What keeps it below consensus is that not one of those results has been replicated by an independent group re-running another's harness on its own benchmark — which is the measurement that would settle it.

Meanwhile this vault is still ingesting single-harness leaderboard comparisons weekly.
