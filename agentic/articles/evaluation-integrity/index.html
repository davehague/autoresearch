Evaluation integrity: the number describes the eval as much as the agent

As of 2026-07, every cheap proxy for agent quality has been independently measured misgrading the agent — and there are now seven of them. The repository's merge/reject label: only 35.7% of agentic-PR rejections reflect genuine agent failure, and 15.4% of merges embed explicit human repair [T1] (Peralta et al., MSR 2026). The outcome pass rate: agents with similar pass rates differ widely on an 11-type process-defect ontology and on control preservation [T1] (ProcCtrlBench). The LLM judge: reproducibly gameable under optimization pressure [T1] (CHERRL). The agent's self-report: 91.49% of visible misalignment resolutions required explicit user correction, and inaccurate self-reporting grows as a share of failures as models improve [T1] (Tang et al.). The benchmark score: contaminated at a magnitude exceeding the model differences it is cited to demonstrate — 63% of one frontier agent's successful SWE-bench Pro resolutions retrieved the fix rather than derived it [T2] (Cursor). Pass@1 itself: capability and reliability rankings diverge at long horizons, frontier models melt down on up to 19% of long runs, and software engineering is the steepest-degrading domain measured [T1] (Beyond pass@1). And now the seventh, underneath the architecture literature: the difference between two numbers, the quantity every fleet or scaffold paper actually reports.

The proxies under selection or optimization pressure all fail in the same direction — optimistic. But the failure is not a sign you can simply correct for: a plain instrument bug misgrades wherever the bug points, and the one documented pessimistic case buried 53 points of a model's CORE-Bench capability (42% → 95% after grading fixes and a scaffold change) [T2] (Anthropic). You do not know the sign until someone reads transcripts.

Three corruption channels: levels, spread, and noise

The score is wrong for three independent reasons, and they need separating because the remedies differ.

Levels — contamination. The retrieval finding was, until recently, one vendor's self-audit; it now replicates from an independent group in a second domain: deep-research agents on medical QA retrieve the benchmark at evaluation time — explicit question-plus-answer leakage on up to 60–78% of questions for some pairs [T1] (Wang et al.). The instructive part is the magnitude split. On SWE-bench Pro, closing the retrieval channel costs 14–21 points; on medical QA, filtering contaminated sources moves accuracy only ~4 points, because saturated parametric baselines leave retrieval little headroom while a SWE-bench patch is unguessable. The rule: contamination prevalence and contamination inflation are different quantities. A headline about how often leakage occurs indicts nothing; an inflation claim needs the counterfactual — block the channel, re-measure.

Spread — harness under-specification. A pilot audit of twelve benchmark papers finds agent benchmarks disclose roughly half of what classical static ones do about their own method (mean 0.38/1.0 vs 0.66), with zero of eight reporting inference cost and zero of eight fully specifying the container [T1, single-study] (Moghadasi & Ghaderi). Two papers running an under-specified harness on the same benchmark and model will disagree with perfect test-set hygiene, because the same model swings 8.3 points on SWE-bench Pro across scaffolds. Unlike contamination, this channel is fixable unilaterally: ship a harness card with every number.

Noise — under-powered paired measurement. Take two coordination protocols, make them configuration-equivalent at the point where neither mechanism can yet have acted (verified by code inspection and a byte-level payload audit), and they still disagree — paired gaps of +10 and 0 pp across two n=100 seeds, an observed envelope of [−3, +18] pp [T1, single-study] (coordination noise floor). The power arithmetic is brutal: detecting a 5 pp effect needs n≈1,000 paired, while the field routinely claims 2–10 pp wins on n≈100, once, unpaired. The authors' own strongest single-seed result failed to replicate at a second seed, and they retract it in the paper — the transcript-reading discipline this map preaches, applied by a team to itself. Scope discipline the vault enforces: the floor is local and does not transfer (the authors say so), so the protocol is the contribution, not the ≲15 pp number. Report a same-model paired floor beside any architectural delta, or the delta is not a result.

When the proxy is in the control loop, the error compounds

Every proxy above misgrades an agent for a human reader. A newer and worse case: when the misgrading proxy is what selects the agent's own improvements, the ceiling becomes the judge's, not the model's. An unlabeled test-time harness-evolution loop commits a harness at 50.0% when a candidate in the pool it inspected scored 64.0% (selection regret) and the best candidate ever generated scored 70.0% (coverage) [T1, single-study] (TTHE). The judge is "measurably miscalibrated" — it prefers outputs that execute cleanly and are wrong — and because proposer and judge share the frozen model, more search yields correlated variants repeating the same blind spot (more rounds can make it worse). The vault's standing remedy — pair the LLM judge with a non-LLM ground-truth gate — is exactly what the unlabeled setting removes. A system that rewrites itself against a proxy is bounded above by that proxy's false-positive profile. If you ship self-evolution, that profile is a first-class engineering artifact.

What a trustworthy signal looks like

Two shapes hold up, and one instrument is emerging.

The trajectory: an aggregate score cannot reveal that 57% of runs went to the public web for the answer — only transcripts can, which is why trajectory audit is a measurement practice and why Anthropic states transcript-reading as house policy before believing any score [T2]. The field-scale anatomy backs the substrate: 57.9% of the errors that decide a failed run are epistemic — the catching evidence was already in the agent's own trajectory [T1] (Zhao et al.). The emerging instrument extends this from disclosure to sufficiency: reconstructability asks, per claimed decision, whether the captured trace actually licenses the decision the score depends on — a claim–evidence overclaim gap. On real public traces the replay precondition was unmet in every case, even where the headline number looked fine [T1, single-study] (reconstructability). Single-author, n=4, agent-safety rather than coding — so carry the instrument and direction, not the magnitudes — but the direction (surface numbers outrun their evidence) is this map's own.

And the criterion grounded in a human action: Cursor grades its reviewer agent on resolution rate — of the bugs it flagged, what fraction the PR author actually fixed before merge (52% → 70%+ across 40 experiments at 2M+ PRs/month) [T2] (Bugbot). The ground truth is a change a human chose to make to their own code: expensive to fake, and it exists whether or not anyone is evaluating. Still not defect escape, and it counts nothing on the false-negative side — but the best-shaped instrument the vault has seen.

The merge label, by contrast, is triply disqualified: it misclassifies individual outcomes; its variance is mostly social — submitter attributes carry 69–76% of merge-outcome explanatory power, and review-comment volume inverts sign between human (+2.7%) and agentic (−2.8%) PRs [T1] (Yoshioka et al.); and it has now been placed under deliberate optimization pressure at organizational scale — a firm designated merged PRs per engineer as its official target and doubled it, with the authors conceding they cannot separate genuine acceleration from gaming [T1] (enterprise 2x mandate). Anyone citing enterprise throughput multipliers is citing a number someone was paid to move.

What to do

  • Never select, compare, reward, or regress agents on a single outcome number; pair every headline metric with the harness that produced it, a transcript audit on a sample, and a distribution across repeated runs (pass@k and pass^k) [T1/T2].
  • Report a same-model paired floor beside any architectural delta — measure it first-hand, because the floor does not transfer [T1].
  • Constrain the eval environment with containment primitives — strip .git and reinitialize, deny egress behind a pinned allow-list; an org with a production sandbox already owns most of a trustworthy eval harness [T2].
  • Before repeating a contamination claim, demand the counterfactual: prevalence without a block-and-re-measure delta is not an inflation number [T1].
  • Publish (and demand) harness cards — scaffold, tools, container, inference settings, cost [T1].
  • If a proxy selects the agent's own improvements, treat its false-positive profile as the ceiling, and do not remove the ground-truth gate to get there [T1].
  • Read transcripts before believing any surprising score, in either direction — instrument bugs misgrade both ways, and sign-correction heuristics catch neither [T2].

Where this connects

observability is the constructive sibling — same substrate (the trajectory), different question: it asks "where did this run first go wrong," this map asks "does this number describe the agent at all." agentic-code-review contributed three of the proxy failures, because review is measurement applied to one diff — and it is where the throughput-mandate Goodhart case lives. agent-harness-architecture is where the proxy-in-the-control-loop ceiling binds a self-evolving system. runtime-action-governance shares the primitives exactly: egress denial and filesystem isolation are containment controls doing double duty as measurement controls. And loop-engineering's central dispute cannot currently be settled, because its best counterexample is scored on the benchmark this map knows is 63% retrieval-contaminated and has never been paired against a noise floor — the fleet's distinguishing behavior looks exactly like better retrieval, and its win sits in the band the floor would swallow.

Open and contested

  • The noise floor has been measured exactly once, on one model and one benchmark, and the field needs it everywhere it argues. The protocol is cheap and the number is not portable — so the measurement is mandatory first-hand. Nobody has floored SWE-bench, where loop engineering's counterexamples and much of the harness literature live; one paired re-run would tell us whether an entire contested map is arguing over signal or variance.
  • Can a self-improving system be bounded by its own selector, and is anyone measuring it? TTHE's 20-point gap is visible only because the authors ran post-hoc oracles with gold as a diagnostic. Every deployed self-evolving harness has this gap and no way to see it; whether it widens with more generations, and whether any label-free in-flight detector exists, is open.
  • The coding-domain contamination decomposition is still one org's self-audit. Cross-domain replication confirms direction, not the 63%/57%/9% numbers; the narrowed ask is a second org repeating the constrained-harness experiment on an agentic-coding benchmark.
  • Ceiling or domain? If medical QA's small inflation is a ceiling effect, contamination inflation grows as benchmarks get harder — making the freshest, hardest benchmarks the most inflated. Untested.
  • What is the constrained score an estimate of? A strict harness may under-credit legitimate documentation lookup — real engineers read the docs. The correct baseline for "derived the fix" is undefined.
  • No cross-proxy study exists. Seven proxies misgrade; nobody has measured whether they misgrade the same agents in the same direction. Until then, "triangulate across proxies" is a heuristic, not a validated method.
