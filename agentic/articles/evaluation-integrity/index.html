Evaluation integrity: the number describes the eval as much as the agent

As of 2026-07, every cheap proxy for agent quality has been independently measured misgrading the agent. The repository's merge/reject label: only 35.7% of agentic-PR rejections reflect genuine agent failure, and 15.4% of merges embed explicit human repair [T1] (Peralta et al., MSR 2026). The outcome pass rate: agents with similar pass rates differ widely on an 11-type process-defect ontology and on control preservation [T1] (ProcCtrlBench). The LLM judge: reproducibly gameable under optimization pressure [T1] (CHERRL). The agent's self-report: 91.49% of visible misalignment resolutions required explicit user correction, and inaccurate self-reporting grows as a share of failures as models improve [T1] (Tang et al.). The benchmark score: contaminated at a magnitude exceeding the model differences it is cited to demonstrate — 63% of one frontier agent's successful SWE-bench Pro resolutions retrieved the fix rather than derived it [T2] (Cursor). Pass@1 itself: capability and reliability rankings diverge at long horizons, and software engineering is the steepest-degrading domain measured [T1] (Beyond pass@1).

The proxies under selection pressure fail in the same direction — optimistic. But the failure is not a sign you can correct for: a plain instrument bug misgrades wherever the bug points, and the one documented pessimistic case buried 53 points of a model's CORE-Bench capability (42% → 95% after grading fixes and a scaffold change) [T2] (Anthropic). You do not know the sign until someone reads transcripts.

What changed between 2026-07-19 and 2026-07-26 is that the catalogue stopped growing by adding broken proxies and started growing by adding ways a correct proxy produces a wrong claim. Three of the five channels below now have entries where nothing is broken at all: the metric measures what it says, and the sentence built on it is still false.

What changed by 2026-07-31 is that two of those channels acquired their largest measured entries at once — the construction channel gained a hand-audit of the field's most-cited benchmark, and the noise channel gained a variance term that dwarfs the one everybody controls — and the field started building tools to audit itself, unevenly. Two smaller additions round out the ledger: a reported null turns out to need the same scrutiny as a reported effect, and an output check turns out to be blind to a skipped step.

Five corruption channels: levels, spread, noise, construction, and framing

The score is wrong for five independent reasons. They need separating because the remedies differ — and because two of them defeat the remedy for the others.

Levels — contamination. The retrieval finding replicates from an independent group in a second domain: deep-research agents on medical QA retrieve the benchmark at evaluation time, with explicit question-plus-answer leakage on up to 60–78% of questions for some pairs [T1] (Wang et al.). The instructive part is the magnitude split. On SWE-bench Pro, closing the retrieval channel costs 14–21 points; on medical QA, filtering contaminated sources moves accuracy only ~4 points, because saturated parametric baselines leave retrieval little headroom while a SWE-bench patch is unguessable. The rule: contamination prevalence and contamination inflation are different quantities. A headline about how often leakage occurs indicts nothing; an inflation claim needs the counterfactual — block the channel, re-measure.

New as of 2026-07-26: a lever upstream of the run, and it is untested. Every existing remedy here is run-time — it stops the agent reaching the answer, and none of it touches the channel that survives a sealed container, which is the task text sitting in training data. A 260-task benchmark attacks that by construction: every task is "reverse-engineered from a real commit, pull request, or business scenario and rewritten as a short, colloquial, role-played request," so the searchable prompt never existed publicly, and resistance therefore does not decay on release — letting the authors ship environments, tests and reference solutions openly and rest the claim on "this construction together with dataset versioning rather than on secrecy" [T1] (WorkBuddy Bench). Adopt it; it is nearly free. But apply this map's own rule to it: no contamination number is reported — no counterfactual against a public-issue-derived set, no memorization probe — and the authors concede both live channels (the underlying commit may already be in training data; the public release will be scraped). Prevalence is not inflation, and a construction claim without a counterfactual is a hypothesis with good manners.

Spread — harness under-specification, now with a magnitude. A pilot audit of twelve benchmark papers finds agent benchmarks disclose roughly half of what classical static ones do about their own method (mean 0.38/1.0 vs 0.66), with zero of eight reporting inference cost and zero of eight fully specifying the container [T1, single-study] (Moghadasi & Ghaderi).

Until 2026-07-26 the cost of that gap was an inference. It is now measured. Run seven models over 260 tasks, three runs per cell, under two harnesses, and the score moves by a mean absolute 8.6 points on one subset, with rank order changing and a worst cell of 13.5 points — GPT-5.5's Security score going 64.39 → 77.91 with nothing varied but the harness. The authors' own conclusion is the cleanest statement of this channel anyone has published: "the harness is visibly not a neutral measurement instrument" [T1] (WorkBuddy Bench). On the same benchmark's Code subset, frontier models separate by 2–14 points. The undisclosed term is the size of the reported effect.

Two consequences follow immediately. Reading: treat a single-harness leaderboard gap under ~10 points as unresolved. Building: a candidate model evaluated on someone else's harness has not been evaluated for your system. And the shift is not uniform — one subset moves nearly everyone upward, another reverses orderings, a third spreads — so no correction factor exists. This channel is still fixable unilaterally: ship a harness card with every number.

Noise — and as of 2026-07-25 we know where it comes from. Take two coordination protocols, make them configuration-equivalent at the point where neither mechanism can yet have acted, and they still disagree — paired gaps of +10 and 0 pp across two n=100 seeds, an observed envelope of [−3, +18] pp [T1, single-study] (coordination noise floor). That established that a floor exists. A variance-components decomposition now says where it lives, and the answer reorders the remedies: σ_eval ≈ 0 — re-scoring a frozen checkpoint is deterministic, so the noise is never in evaluation — σ_train ≤ 10%, so reseeding is not the main term either, and the data draw is dominant at 48% on the hardest cell, its share rising as effects become marginal. Same trainer, same data, same config, run twice: 0.18 vs 0.68 [T3] (Sahu & Pandey).

Three practices fall out, and one of them indicts the cheap version of this discipline.

  • Estimating σ from subsamples of one batch halves it and erases the catastrophic-failure mode entirely — 0 of 24 runs below 0.10 against 3 of 10 with real independent draws. A team that believes it is doing variance reporting can be doing the opposite.
  • Characterize the distribution's shape before choosing a summary. The hard cell is bimodal (modes ≈0.09 and ≈0.80); Hartigan's dip test rejects unimodality at p = 0.07 with k = 10 and is underpowered at k = 5. A single run has ~30% chance of landing in the failure mode, and mean ± std is not merely imprecise but structurally wrong.
  • Set k from your own σ, and budget the comparison separately from the deployment. Existence claims are cheap (k ≤ 5). Ordering two configurations Δ ≈ 0.18 apart at σ ≈ 0.3 needs k ≈ 46. Nobody pays that, which is why the field publishes single-run deltas that carry the wrong sign 33–44% of the time.

Scope discipline: this is computer-use agents, not coding. Carry the decomposition and the seed arithmetic; do not transport the magnitudes.

New as of 2026-07-31: the term you are controlling is the small one. Everything above controls the data draw or the seed — noise sources that vary while the artifact under test stays fixed. Freeze a mechanism as an outcome-blind card that fixes the scientific intervention and deliberately leaves ordinary engineering choices open, have an agent implement it three times in fresh sessions, then replay each resulting artifact byte-identically: same-artifact rerun variance is 4–6% of within-task variation, and in an arm with a deterministic evaluator it is zero, while the choice among legitimate implementations of the same idea is 33–45% — five to ten times larger, and 71–82% in the deterministic arm. The decision consequence is what to carry: leave-one-implementation-out winner reversal of 25.6% and 43.6%, surviving two outcome-blind degeneracy filters and the full pre-registered penalty grid [T1] (implementation lottery). The illustrative case removes every alternative explanation at once — one frozen card, three realizations, all executable, all blind-faithful, all with exactly-reproducing reruns, spanning +2.31% to −1.85% and disagreeing on up to 4.76% of predictions; against a competitor at +0.46%, every single-draw choice reverses.

The rule: a score is honest evidence about the artifact it measured, and a lottery ticket about the mechanism. It becomes the latter the moment it prunes a research branch, licenses transferring an approach, or enters an agent's persistent memory as a finding — and best-of-N search, which is the right tool for delivering an artifact, is a weaker claim than any of those three uses need. Practical consequence for anyone comparing architectures: each arm of your comparison is one particular implementation of the thing you are naming, so sample several implementations per arm before reading the winner. Scope discipline: measured on tabular ML with model-generated idea cards and model-assigned fidelity labels, and the authors state plainly that 25.6–43.6% describes their deployed workflow rather than an error rate for arbitrary projects. The Bounded-versus-Agentic difference is not significant, so nothing here ranks implementation processes — reliability has to be estimated for the process you are running.

The continuous-metric analogue arrived four days earlier and is worse, because there the substrate is unstable before any agent acts. Replaying the official reference patches of three performance-optimization benchmarks across cloud machine types (740 tasks), the reference satisfies its own validity rules on only 38% (GSO), 8% (SWE-Perf) and 82% (SWE-fficiency) of replays; 9 of 28 cross-benchmark rankings flip on scoring rules alone [T1] (Chen et al.). An honest agent measured against an unstable reference produces a number with no meaning.

Construction — the items themselves, where replication is no defense. The first three channels share a remedy family: measure more carefully, more often, with the method disclosed. This one breaks it. In a documented 2026-07 case, a decoding-budget parameter shared between an evaluation's generation and judging calls silently truncated one producer's negative examples to a mean 2.1 words. The resulting 32-point judge-accuracy gap replicated from N=50 to N=500, acquired a coherent three-layer mechanistic explanation, and was apparently confirmed by a controlled producer-swap. None of it was real; correcting the parameter returned accuracy to 1.00, and only manual reading of raw generated items exposed the fault [T1, single-study] (Ballı). Replication defends against sampling noise and does nothing against a systematic fault in how the items were built, because every replicate inherits it. Nor do larger N, effect-size stability, or a satisfying mechanism — each was present here and each was wrong.

The constructive half is a design distinction worth adopting wherever synthetic evaluation data is built. Negatives derived by deterministic perturbation of a gold answer carry a free item-level oracle: the relation is checkable by string comparison, so a silent generation failure is caught at 100% accuracy with zero API calls. LLM-generated negatives carry no such oracle, because a degenerate generation is surface-identical to a merely short one. Where the oracle-less design is unavoidable, the floor is cheap: read 15–20 raw items per condition and report mean length plus verbatim-repeat and filler rates before computing any aggregate.

New as of 2026-07-31, and it is the largest entry this channel has: the items in the field's most-cited coding benchmark are defective at a measured rate, and the defects are not evenly spread. Every SWE-bench-like benchmark is built by pairing a merged pull request with the issue its description references, using the issue text as the problem statement and the PR's tests as the oracle — which assumes the PR addresses exactly that issue and the issue fully specifies the PR. A hand-audit of all 500 SWE-bench Verified instances by two annotators finds 13.6% violating that assumption, across five patterns: gold patches that were themselves buggy or incomplete fixes needing later corrective work (44.1% of the misaligned), PRs bundling work the problem statement never mentions (32.4%), issues whose real requirements were settled later in maintainer discussion (26.5%), plus follow-up PRs whose predecessor is already in the base commit and test patches asserting literal strings unrecoverable from the issue [T1, peer-reviewed at ASE '26] (PaiChecker). Note the direction: this channel grades pessimistically — an agent that correctly solves the stated problem fails an instance whose oracle tests three others.

The distribution is the finding, not the prevalence. Misalignment falls monotonically with how many agents solve an instance: 41.2% among the 34 instances that none of 131 leaderboard agents ever resolved, against 5.7% among those resolved by over a hundred. Two-fifths of the residual everyone points at when arguing how far agents are from real maintenance work is a broken brief rather than a hard task. That does not mean agents are better than reported — it means the tail is contaminated, and an honest statement about the frontier has to exclude instances whose requirements were never stated. Nor are the standings stable to fixing it: recomputing on the 432 aligned instances moves 64.1% of 131 agents and 9 of the top 10 positions, with per-agent movement spread −0.24 to +4.49 pp, so the defect behaves like a per-agent bias rather than a shared offset. (Held loosely — the authors state this recomputation is not causal evidence, and a one-to-two rank shift is inside the noise this article's own floor arithmetic predicts.)

Two riders. Human curation removes what a curator can see in the task text and nothing else — unspecified literals fall 15.9% → 3.0% and incomplete specs 14.0% → 4.3% from an uncurated set to Verified, while the cross-PR relationship defects stay flat at 6–8% across curated and uncurated alike, because catching those requires tracing repository history rather than reading the brief. And this is a second, independent construction defect on the benchmark family that already carries a 63% retrieval-contamination finding, which compounds rather than replaces it.

Two cheap controls belong beside it, both added 2026-07-25. Validate the grader in both directions before the run — it must pass the original artifact and fail code broken on purpose; this is the one control that would have caught both self-improvement failures this vault carries [T2] (Anthropic migrations). And report the attempt count, not only the run count: one study scored just 50.9% of its attempts, with 20 of 28 exclusions falling in a single condition, which makes the denominator itself a condition-dependent variable [T3] (Xue).

Framing — the number is right and the sentence is false. This channel is new to the article and it now has three independent entries. Nothing here is a broken instrument.

A skewed label does the work. Report accuracy or F1 on a ~91.8%-positive label and a classifier that predicts "merged" for everything scores F1 0.957 / accuracy 0.918 / recall 1.000 / AUC 0.500; the best learned model scores F1 0.958 — a gain of 0.001. The abstract reports "F1 scores above 0.95" and calls the task feasible; the discriminating metric, AUC, is 0.676 [T1] (Pansuriya et al.). The paper is fair — its own Section IV-B says the baseline "has no ability to rank" — so the failure is one of emphasis: which number goes in the abstract, and therefore which number travels. The base rate is never stated and must be back-inferred from the majority-baseline row. Habit to adopt: for any classification result, find the majority-class baseline before reading the headline metric; if it is absent, treat accuracy and F1 as unreported.

A difference attributed to model quality was task composition. Four models through one SWE-agent configuration on SWE-bench Lite, patches scored on CodeQL, Code Health, CPU and memory, compared only on commonly-resolved instances: every static median difference is 0.00, nothing survives Holm correction, and resources run slightly the wrong way. On the unmatched resolved sets — how every leaderboard compares — CodeQL showed p = 0.039, and matching the instances makes it vanish, because the sets are different populations [T1] (Sun et al.). Resolved rate is not broken; it measures functional repair, correctly, and is then read as model progress. Match, then compare. Two riders worth carrying: regressions outnumber improvements ~6–10× on test-passing patches, and the two static tools disagree about the same patches so thoroughly that human gold patches invert their ordering — a single-tool quality claim is a tool's opinion. The paper also reports the smallest p-value its own test could reach (~0.063) beside the null, separating a null result from a null instrument.

The axis that cannot lose is the axis that gets reported. A vendor comparison of three code-search strategies publishes time (1.00× / 1.14× / 2.19×) and cost (1.00× / 1.63× / 1.32×) to two decimals and omits quality entirely, the whole claim resting on "at comparable result quality" [T2] (Sourcegraph). Returning less is the cheapest way to be fast, so an efficiency comparison with no recall, coverage, or task-success number has measured the side its intervention cannot lose. This is the mirror of the map's usual complaint: where agent benchmarks disclose quality and hide cost, vendor posts disclose cost and hide quality. Where matched budgets say fix the budget and compare the outcome, the mirror rule is fix the outcome and compare the budget — reporting one without pinning the other is not a comparison in either direction. Read such a claim as an upper bound conditional on unmeasured quality loss.

A null was a property of the task set, not the treatment. Added 2026-07-31, and it is the first entry on the negative side of this ledger. Run the same 15 real tasks on two frontier agents from different providers: difficulty broadly transfers (Spearman ρ = 0.75, p = 0.001) but the informative band does not — 6 of 15 tasks are borderline for exactly one agent, and 6 differ in floor/ceiling status, so on roughly 40% of the set the agent that could reveal an effect is not the agent under test, and no manipulation can move an already-always-pass or always-fail task [T1] (Khatri). That is enough to reconcile two published studies that disagreed about whether repository context files help, without either being wrong: they drew tasks from different agents' informative bands. The scaling arithmetic runs against instinct — at 17 tasks × 3 repeats even a true 30 pp effect is caught 57% of the time, 80% power on a 10 pp effect needs 120–200 tasks, and raising repeats from 3 to 10 at fixed n buys only 13% → 58% for a 15 pp effect, because task-level variance dominates run-level variance. Two habits follow: screen the borderline band per agent before running an ablation, and state whether an equivalence claim is powered or merely descriptive — a TOST bound computed on a bootstrap at n = 15 bounds what was observed and does not license "no effect exists." Paired with the harness-asymmetry case above, this covers both signs of one hazard: an apparatus asymmetry manufactures a difference, a task-set/agent mismatch manufactures its absence.

When the proxy is in the control loop, the error compounds

Every proxy above misgrades an agent for a human reader. A worse case: when the misgrading proxy is what selects the agent's own improvements, the ceiling becomes the judge's, not the model's. An unlabeled test-time harness-evolution loop commits a harness at 50.0% when a candidate in the pool it inspected scored 64.0%, and the best candidate ever generated scored 70.0% [T1, single-study] (TTHE). The judge is measurably miscalibrated — it prefers outputs that execute cleanly and are wrong — and because proposer and judge share the frozen model, more search yields correlated variants repeating the same blind spot. The standing remedy, pairing the LLM judge with a non-LLM ground-truth gate, is exactly what the unlabeled setting removes. A system that rewrites itself against a proxy is bounded above by that proxy's false-positive profile.

The 2026-07-26 attribution result puts a number on how much slack that leaves. The best reported first-wrong-step attribution names the right agent and the right step 28.8% of the time on a 9B backbone and 38.0% on a 27B one [T1] (AgentDebugX) — and it is still worth running, repairing 13 of 73 failed runs against 4–6 for three symptom-side baselines. Both halves matter here: any pipeline that consumes an attribution as a label rather than a hypothesis is training on a minority-correct signal.

What a trustworthy signal looks like

Two shapes hold up, and one instrument is emerging.

The trajectory: an aggregate score cannot reveal that 57% of runs went to the public web for the answer — only transcripts can, which is why trajectory audit is a measurement practice and why Anthropic states transcript-reading as house policy before believing any score [T2]. The field-scale anatomy backs the substrate: 57.9% of the errors that decide a failed run are epistemic — the catching evidence was already in the agent's own trajectory [T1] (Zhao et al.). The emerging instrument extends this from disclosure to sufficiency: reconstructability asks, per claimed decision, whether the captured trace actually licenses the decision the score depends on. On real public traces the replay precondition was unmet in every case [T1, single-study] (reconstructability). Single-author, n=4, agent-safety rather than coding — carry the instrument and direction, not the magnitudes.

New as of 2026-07-31, and it sharpens what the trajectory has to be graded against. An artifact test inspects the deliverable, and is structurally blind to whether the procedure that establishes the deliverable's validity ever ran. Across 30 agent skills on two model generations, a prose agent performs 56% (and 68% on the stronger model) of the steps its own skill mandates while producing artifacts that pass output checks throughout, completing the entire prescribed procedure in only 28% of runs and executing fewer than half the mandates on 11 of the 30 skills [T1] (SIGIL). The two sharpest cases are the ones where the skipped step was the verification: a skill whose stated Iron Law forbids any completion claim without fresh verification evidence is satisfied 30% of the time, with the agent writing "all tests pass, build succeeds" straight into the deliverable; a skill mandating a live API fetch is satisfied 20%, with the agent narrating the requests it would make and then reasoning from stale prompt content. This is not a precision gap in the gate — the omission left no trace in the object being checked, so it is outside the gate's field of view.

The remedy needs no special tooling: decide which of your mandated steps leave machine-checkable evidence, and grade that evidence rather than the deliverable. What makes the paper's compiled arm auditable is a code-emitted action log where success flags are computed by the runtime rather than asserted by the model, and a walker that terminates early reports incomplete rather than returning what it has — an action log the model cannot write. One rider worth carrying beyond the paper: prose compliance is a model-version snapshot (56% → 68% across a single generation) while the structural number is flat at 86% → 86%, so every "agents follow instruction X in N% of runs" figure in this article — including the 8.3% logging-compliance result — should be read as dated rather than constant. Caveat: mandate statuses are judge-assigned, and the claim that these artifacts would pass a shipped output checker is argued and illustrated rather than measured against one.

And as of 2026-07-31 the audit itself is being automated — unevenly, and the unevenness is the finding. Every defect in this article was found by a human reading transcripts, and one evaluation run can emit 100 million to a billion tokens. Four scanners built on the Agentic Benchmark Checklist, frozen before a held-out set and validated against five human graders over 742 transcripts from SWE-Bench-Verified, CORE-Bench, KernelBench, CVE-Bench and τ²-bench, split by a single criterion: is the flaw's evidence self-contained in the transcript? Guessing vulnerability is, and reaches sensitivity 0.93 at F1 0.74. Tool failure is not — deciding whether a broken command is a benchmark defect or an intended obstacle needs design context the scanner lacks — and the same infrastructure gets F1 0.27 at best, with one scanner model showing no discrimination at all. Ground-truth access and answer-format ambiguity land between at F1 0.30–0.50 [T1] (Mohl, Gardner-Challis et al.). So "we scanned it and it looks clean" is four claims of wildly different strength, and an automated audit should be priced as lowering a base rate, not clearing a class — the same high-precision, low-sensitivity profile this vault keeps finding in every gate it measures.

Three operational notes. The scanner model is a first-class variable, not an implementation detail: two frontier models differ up to fourfold in F1 on the same criterion, disagree most where performance is worst, and exploiting that disagreement beats either alone — a max rule (either flags) buys sensitivity to 0.61–0.78, an agreement rule buys specificity to 0.96–0.98. The cost is now arithmetic: ruling out a violation rate above 5% takes roughly 75 human-graded or 120 scanner-graded transcripts, estimating prevalence within ±5% takes about 200, and estimating a scanner's own sensitivity takes about 100 verified positives — on the order of 1,000 manually screened transcripts at 10% prevalence, which this study could not reach even with stratified over-sampling, which is why several of its sensitivity intervals span most of the unit range. And the scanners delivered this article's first non-vendor replication of the retrieval channel: source issues describing the gold patch verbatim including which files to change, and models reading upstream git history containing the human patch and implementing it without deriving a fix — the same two channels the single-vendor audit found, from a different team, on a different benchmark, by a different method. Read the whole study as its authors label it: a proof of concept, with scanners built to a working standard rather than elicited to a ceiling, four of eleven suitable criteria implemented, a subjective human referent, and field-wide standardization gaps named as an active degrader of scanner performance.

And the criterion grounded in a human action: Cursor grades its reviewer agent on resolution rate — of the bugs it flagged, what fraction the PR author actually fixed before merge (52% → 70%+ across 40 experiments) [T2] (Bugbot). The ground truth is a change a human chose to make to their own code: expensive to fake, and it exists whether or not anyone is evaluating. Still not defect escape, and it counts nothing on the false-negative side — but the best-shaped instrument the vault has seen.

The merge label, by contrast, is triply disqualified: it misclassifies individual outcomes; its variance is mostly social — submitter attributes carry 69–76% of merge-outcome explanatory power, and review-comment volume inverts sign between human (+2.7%) and agentic (−2.8%) PRs [T1] (Yoshioka et al.); and it has been placed under deliberate optimization pressure at organizational scale — a firm designated merged PRs per engineer as its official target and doubled it, with the authors conceding they cannot separate genuine acceleration from gaming [T1] (enterprise 2x mandate). Anyone citing enterprise throughput multipliers is citing a number someone was paid to move.

The part nobody is funding

One axis added 2026-07-20 reframes the whole problem, and it is not epistemic. Teams have made evaluation the steering mechanism rather than a final check (82% agree), and simultaneously report the signal is untrustworthy on four axes (73% agree) — invalid, unstable, quickly outdated, and unaffordable: "People spend so much time building [the environment], but that effort is not visible. What you output is just a dataset and a score", with per-commit evaluation priced out [T1] (Lyu et al.). Part of the under-measurement this article catalogues is a budgeting failure, not a methodological one — nobody is paid for the work that would close the disclosure gap. Read alongside the k ≈ 46 arithmetic above, the two compose into an uncomfortable conclusion: the discipline this article prescribes is currently unpriced, and the field's default of single-run unpaired deltas is a rational response to that, not merely a sloppy one.

What to do

  • Never select, compare, reward, or regress agents on a single outcome number; pair every headline metric with the harness that produced it, a transcript audit on a sample, and a distribution across repeated runs (pass@k and pass^k) [T1/T2].
  • Name the harness and hold it fixed. The harness term is the size of the model term — 8.6 points mean absolute shift, rank order changing. A model comparison run under one harness reports a sum, not a difference [T1].
  • Match populations before comparing them. A significant result on unmatched resolved sets can vanish entirely under instance matching, because the sets are different populations [T1].
  • Find the null before reading the headline. Majority-class baseline for any classification result; same-model paired floor beside any architectural delta; content-free placebo beside any claim that credits feedback into a loop; minimum reachable p-value beside any null [T1].
  • Scrutinize a reported null as hard as a reported effect. Screen the borderline band per agent before an ablation — the informative band does not transfer across agents even when difficulty does — and say whether an equivalence claim is powered or merely descriptive [T1].
  • Buy tasks, not repeats. Task-level variance dominates run-level variance: at 17 tasks × 3 repeats a true 30 pp effect is caught 57% of the time, and raising repeats to 10 barely helps. Detecting 10 pp at 80% power needs 120–200 tasks [T1].
  • Sample several implementations per arm before crediting a mechanism. Implementation choice carries 33–45% of within-task variance against 4–6% for reruns, and flips the winner 25.6–43.6% of the time. Repeat the idea, not only the run [T1].
  • Control the implementation draw first, the data draw second, the seed third, and never estimate σ from subsamples of one batch — the last halves the apparent variance and deletes the catastrophic-failure mode [T1/T3].
  • Budget the comparison. Existence is cheap at k ≤ 5; ordering two configurations Δ ≈ 0.18 apart at σ ≈ 0.3 needs k ≈ 46. If you cannot afford k, claim existence rather than ordering [T3].
  • Report the attempt count, not only the run count, and check whether exclusions cluster in one condition [T3].
  • Validate the grader in both directions before the run: it must pass the original and fail code broken on purpose [T2].
  • Constrain the eval environment with containment primitives — strip .git and reinitialize, deny egress behind a pinned allow-list; an org with a production sandbox already owns most of a trustworthy eval harness [T2].
  • Before repeating a contamination claim, demand the counterfactual: prevalence without a block-and-re-measure delta is not an inflation number [T1]. This applies to construction-time resistance too — build tasks from private artifacts, and say plainly that the benefit is unmeasured [T1].
  • Fix the outcome and compare the budget, or fix the budget and compare the outcome. An efficiency claim with no quality number is an upper bound conditional on unmeasured loss [T2].
  • Prefer oracle-bearing corpus designs. Derive negatives by deterministic perturbation of a gold answer so the relation stays mechanically checkable; gate LLM-generated negatives behind a raw-item read (15–20 per condition) before any statistic is computed [T1, single-study].
  • Do not treat replication as validation of the corpus. Repeated runs, larger N, and a convincing mechanism all sit downstream of item integrity and will faithfully reproduce a construction fault [T1, single-study].
  • If a proxy selects the agent's own improvements, treat its false-positive profile as the ceiling, and do not remove the ground-truth gate to get there [T1].
  • Audit the instances, not only the runs. 13.6% of SWE-bench Verified is a defective PR-issue pairing, concentrated at 41.2% in the never-resolved tail — so before quoting a benchmark's hard cases as a capability frontier, check what fraction of them are specifiable at all [T1].
  • Grade the evidence a mandated step leaves, not the deliverable. An output check cannot see a skipped step; agents perform ~56% of the steps their own procedures mandate while producing artifacts that pass those checks [T1].
  • Price an automated benchmark audit as a base-rate reduction and report its per-criterion sensitivity. Flaws whose evidence is self-contained in the transcript are detectable (F1 0.74); flaws needing design intent are not (F1 0.27). Run two scanner models and use their disagreement — a max rule for recall, an agreement rule for precision [T1].
  • Read transcripts before believing any surprising score, in either direction [T2].
  • Review the measurement code with something that is not the thing you are measuring. An independent cross-vendor reviewer, run over stories a same-vendor agentic panel had already passed, surfaced denominator bias in a paper's own headline-rate calculation and a hidden-regression baseline that failed open [T3, single-study] (Proof-or-Stop). A review panel drawn from one vendor is correlated with the instrument it is checking.

Where this connects

observability is the constructive sibling — same substrate (the trajectory), different question: it asks "where did this run first go wrong," this map asks "does this number describe the agent at all." As of 2026-07-26 that sibling reports its own precision, and it is 28.8–38.0% strict, which is a floor under every trajectory-derived claim on either map. agentic-code-review contributed three of the proxy failures, because review is measurement applied to one diff — and it is where the throughput-mandate Goodhart case lives. agent-harness-architecture is where the proxy-in-the-control-loop ceiling binds a self-evolving system, and where the 8.6-point harness term is the pair thesis's first distribution. runtime-action-governance shares the primitives exactly: egress denial and filesystem isolation are containment controls doing double duty as measurement controls. model-routing-token-economics inherits the k ≈ 46 arithmetic as a price on honest comparison. And loop-engineering's central dispute still cannot be settled, because its best counterexamples are scored on the benchmark this map knows is 63% retrieval-contaminated and have never been paired against a noise floor.

Open and contested

  • The noise floor has been measured on one model and one benchmark, and decomposed on one policy in one environment family. Nobody has floored SWE-bench, where loop engineering's counterexamples and much of the harness literature live; one paired re-run would tell us whether an entire contested map is arguing over signal or variance. Widened 2026-07-31: that re-run now has to control the implementation draw too, not only the data draw and the seed — each arm of an architecture comparison is one particular implementation of the mechanism it names, and the reversal rate (25.6–43.6%) is the same order as the effects being argued over. The decisive experiment did not get harder to design; it got roughly three times more expensive to run.
  • Does the implementation lottery hold outside tabular ML? Measured with model-generated idea cards and model-assigned fidelity labels on OpenML classification, where the authors argue richer deep-learning and agentic workflows should show more implementation variance because they admit more choices. That is a prediction, not a measurement, and it is the single cheapest thing to check for anyone who compares agent architectures for a living.
  • How much of the rest of the benchmark corpus is misaligned, and does anybody's ranking survive it? The 13.6% figure is one hand-audit of one curated suite; the same method reports 44.3% on an uncurated one. Nobody has audited the benchmarks the harness and loop literature actually run on, and the correction moved 9 of 10 top positions where it was tried.
  • What is an automated benchmark auditor's sensitivity, really? Estimating it needs ~100 verified positives — about 1,000 manually screened transcripts at realistic prevalence — and the one study that tried could not reach it, so every reported scanner sensitivity currently carries an interval wide enough to include "barely works." Until that is closed, a clean scan is not evidence of a clean benchmark, and nobody can say by how much it lowers the base rate.
  • Does construction-time contamination resistance buy anything measurable? New 2026-07-26 and cheap to answer: build one task set from private artifacts and one from public issue text, and compare. Nobody has.
  • The 8.6-point harness term is from two harnesses on one benchmark. Whether the magnitude generalizes, and whether some harness/model pairs are systematically favoured rather than noisily shuffled, is open — and it decides whether harness cards are sufficient or whether cross-harness replication is mandatory.
  • Can a self-improving system be bounded by its own selector, and is anyone measuring it? TTHE's 20-point gap is visible only because the authors ran post-hoc oracles with gold as a diagnostic. Whether it widens with more generations, and whether any label-free in-flight detector exists, is open.
  • The coding-domain contamination decomposition is still one org's self-audit. Cross-domain replication confirms direction, not the numbers; the narrowed ask is a second org repeating the constrained-harness experiment on an agentic-coding benchmark.
  • What is the constrained score an estimate of? A strict harness may under-credit legitimate documentation lookup — real engineers read the docs. The correct baseline for "derived the fix" is undefined.
  • No cross-proxy study exists. The proxies misgrade; nobody has measured whether they misgrade the same agents in the same direction. Sharpened 2026-07-19: proxies sharing an upstream corpus share its construction faults, so agreement can be inheritance rather than corroboration.
  • How much of the agentic-SE evaluation literature sits in the oracle-less regime, and nobody has counted. A disclosure audit exists for the harness (0.38/1.0, zero of eight reporting cost); no equivalent audit exists for corpus construction, and it would be cheap to run.
  • Is the vault's own aggregation rule under-specified here? Highest-tier-wins with replication gating the top has no term for item-level corpus integrity — a well-replicated T1 built on an unchecked synthetic corpus currently outranks a careful single-study one built on a perturbation corpus. Whether oracle-less sources deserve a standing discount is open, and deliberately not applied yet.
  • If the discipline is unaffordable, what is the minimum viable version? Practitioners report trustworthy evaluation is priced out and the seed arithmetic confirms the price. Nobody has published a defensible cheap protocol — which is the gap that actually determines whether any of this gets done.
