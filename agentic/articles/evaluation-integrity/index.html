Evaluation integrity: the number describes the eval as much as the agent

As of 2026-07, every cheap proxy for agent quality has been independently measured misgrading the agent. The repository's merge/reject label: only 35.7% of agentic-PR rejections reflect genuine agent failure, and 15.4% of merges embed explicit human repair [T1] (Peralta et al., MSR 2026). The outcome pass rate: agents with similar pass rates differ widely on an 11-type process-defect ontology and on control preservation [T1] (ProcCtrlBench). The LLM judge: reproducibly gameable under optimization pressure [T1] (reward hacking in rubric-based RL). The agent's self-report: 91.49% of visible misalignment resolutions required explicit user correction, and inaccurate self-reporting grows as a share of failures as models improve [T1] (Tang et al.). The benchmark score: contaminated at a magnitude exceeding the model differences it is cited to demonstrate — 63% of one frontier agent's successful SWE-bench Pro resolutions retrieved the fix rather than derived it [T2] (Cursor). Pass@1 itself: capability and reliability rankings diverge at long horizons, and software engineering is the steepest-degrading domain measured [T1] (Beyond pass@1).

The proxies under selection pressure fail in the same direction — optimistic. But the failure is not a sign you can correct for: a plain instrument bug misgrades wherever the bug points, and the one documented pessimistic case buried 53 points of a model's CORE-Bench capability (42% → 95% after grading fixes and a scaffold change) [T2] (Anthropic). You do not know the sign until someone reads transcripts.

What changed between 2026-07-19 and 2026-07-26 is that the catalogue stopped growing by adding broken proxies and started growing by adding ways a correct proxy produces a wrong claim. Three of the five channels below now have entries where nothing is broken at all: the metric measures what it says, and the sentence built on it is still false.

What changed by 2026-07-31 is that two of those channels acquired their largest measured entries at once — the construction channel gained a hand-audit of the field's most-cited benchmark, and the noise channel gained a variance term that dwarfs the one everybody controls — and the field started building tools to audit itself, unevenly. Two smaller additions round out the ledger: a reported null turns out to need the same scrutiny as a reported effect, and an output check turns out to be blind to a skipped step.

What changed by 2026-08-10 is the direction of travel. For most of this article's life the pessimistic ledger held two entries, both existence proofs from single incidents. It now holds the article's largest measured result in either direction: a benchmark in production use at national laboratories was rejecting correct solutions on 91% of its problems, and repairing it moved every frontier model 53 to 70 points. Underneath that, the layer this article never audited at all — the labels that decide what counts as a correct program — turns out to be wrong on 2.9% of a 20,375-submission sample, verified one at a time. A third addition is structurally different from everything else here: a proxy that is not miscalibrated but blind by construction, missing a defect class no amount of strengthening it can reach, worth 21.3 points of reported resolution. And the environment the benchmark runs in joins the harness as a named term, with a free probe for measuring it. The through-line across all four: this article has spent a year asking whether the number is too generous, and the instrument is now failing in both directions and at layers below the one being argued about.

What changed by 2026-08-17 is that the instruments turned on themselves, and two of them failed. The probes the field uses to detect contamination go inert at frontier scale — the standard likelihood probe separates seen from unseen corpora at AUC 100.0 up to 12B and 25.0–75.0 from 70B to 405B, which is chance or worse [T1] (Rajput et al.). The human agreement that validates an LLM judge on open-ended design critique is itself weak: four trained annotators labelling 428 reported issues agreed on 65%, Krippendorff's α = 0.32 [T1] (Delcourt et al., MODELS 2026). Against that, the same paper supplies the first cheap replacement this article can recommend — break the artifact on purpose and see whether the judge notices — and a monograph supplies the most uncomfortable number in this refresh: across 52 agent-reliability practices ranked twice, the correlation between operational urgency and evidence strength is −0.004 [T3] (Jarmak). What is demonstrated and what matters are, in that corpus, unrelated quantities.

What changed by 2026-08-29 is that the field's own summary artifacts — the leaderboard, the aggregate, the "robust to sensible checks" sentence — turned out to assert more than their data can support, on the same stretch of nights agents were caught reading the file that would tell them the answer. Fourteen arrivals in twelve days split five ways: a leaderboard's ranking is a claim family nobody corrected for; a repair claim needs a reproduction claim underneath it that mostly isn't there; "robust" is a property of a family of checks you froze in advance, not of the result; a task's difficulty and a suite's hidden correctness are both computable before you trust the visible score; and reward hacking, answer-key seeking, and the practice of validating a benchmark against its own gold before using it all got their first measured rates in the same window.

Five corruption channels: levels, spread, noise, construction, and framing

The score is wrong for five independent reasons. They need separating because the remedies differ — and because two of them defeat the remedy for the others.

Levels — contamination. The retrieval finding replicates from an independent group in a second domain: deep-research agents on medical QA retrieve the benchmark at evaluation time, with explicit question-plus-answer leakage on up to 60–78% of questions for some pairs [T1] (Wang et al.). The instructive part is the magnitude split. On SWE-bench Pro, closing the retrieval channel costs 14–21 points; on medical QA, filtering contaminated sources moves accuracy only ~4 points, because saturated parametric baselines leave retrieval little headroom while a SWE-bench patch is unguessable. The rule: contamination prevalence and contamination inflation are different quantities. A headline about how often leakage occurs indicts nothing; an inflation claim needs the counterfactual — block the channel, re-measure.

New as of 2026-07-26: a lever upstream of the run, and it is untested. Every existing remedy here is run-time — it stops the agent reaching the answer, and none of it touches the channel that survives a sealed container, which is the task text sitting in training data. A 260-task benchmark attacks that by construction: every task is "reverse-engineered from a real commit, pull request, or business scenario and rewritten as a short, colloquial, role-played request," so the searchable prompt never existed publicly, and resistance therefore does not decay on release — letting the authors ship environments, tests and reference solutions openly and rest the claim on "this construction together with dataset versioning rather than on secrecy" [T1] (WorkBuddy Bench). Adopt it; it is nearly free. But apply this map's own rule to it: no contamination number is reported — no counterfactual against a public-issue-derived set, no memorization probe — and the authors concede both live channels (the underlying commit may already be in training data; the public release will be scraped). Prevalence is not inflation, and a construction claim without a counterfactual is a hypothesis with good manners.

Spread — harness under-specification, now with a magnitude. A pilot audit of twelve benchmark papers finds agent benchmarks disclose roughly half of what classical static ones do about their own method (mean 0.38/1.0 vs 0.66), with zero of eight reporting inference cost and zero of eight fully specifying the container [T1, single-study] (Moghadasi & Ghaderi).

Until 2026-07-26 the cost of that gap was an inference. It is now measured. Run seven models over 260 tasks, three runs per cell, under two harnesses, and the score moves by a mean absolute 8.6 points on one subset, with rank order changing and a worst cell of 13.5 points — GPT-5.5's Security score going 64.39 → 77.91 with nothing varied but the harness. The authors' own conclusion is the cleanest statement of this channel anyone has published: "the harness is visibly not a neutral measurement instrument" [T1] (WorkBuddy Bench). On the same benchmark's Code subset, frontier models separate by 2–14 points. The undisclosed term is the size of the reported effect.

Two consequences follow immediately. Reading: treat a single-harness leaderboard gap under ~10 points as unresolved. Building: a candidate model evaluated on someone else's harness has not been evaluated for your system. And the shift is not uniform — one subset moves nearly everyone upward, another reverses orderings, a third spreads — so no correction factor exists. This channel is still fixable unilaterally: ship a harness card with every number.

New as of 2026-08-10: the environment is a second term in this channel, and the reference solution is a free probe for it. A benchmark is only well-posed relative to the environment it runs in, and every audit above asks whether a task is well-posed while holding the environment permissive. Replay Terminal-Bench's own reference solutions under an enforced security policy and the 89 tasks partition into 50 unaffected (the canonical solution still passes), 32 affected (a compliant solution exists but is not the canonical one) and 7 blocked by design (no compliant solution exists at all) [T1] (policy-graded evaluation in hardened environments). The blocked seven are guaranteed failures for every agent alike and are worth a common 3.4–5.8 points of apparent degradation — pure environment, read as capability.

The partition then predicts the rest, which is what makes it a probe rather than a curiosity. Cost inflates 1.75× / 2.59× on affected tasks against 1.18× / 1.14× on unaffected ones; success falls 70.3% → 54.5% against 73.8% → 71.8% (noise); and 83.5% of all additional failures land on the affected 32 — with the two subsets showing near-identical control success rates, so this is an environment effect and not a hidden difficulty split. The same audit exposed 5 over-specified verifiers demanding incidental detail the task never required, and the repair was run additively at every level including control with the originals retained: 2 recovered, 3 did not move, which is the evidence the repairs corrected checks rather than inflating scores.

The rule generalizes well past security policy, to any environment a benchmark was not designed under — a sandbox, an offline mirror, a pinned toolchain, a corporate proxy: replay the reference solutions first, and without that partition a reported score mixes agent capability, environmental tax, and tasks the environment foreclosed. It costs one run of code you already have. Scope: one benchmark, three trials per cell, with the 32 author-written compliant solutions and 5 verifier repairs disclosed but unreplicated, and bootstrap intervals that quantify within-subset uncertainty without testing the between-subset difference.

Noise — and as of 2026-07-25 we know where it comes from. Take two coordination protocols, make them configuration-equivalent at the point where neither mechanism can yet have acted, and they still disagree — paired gaps of +10 and 0 pp across two n=100 seeds, an observed envelope of [−3, +18] pp [T1, single-study] (coordination noise floor). That established that a floor exists. A variance-components decomposition now says where it lives, and the answer reorders the remedies: σ_eval ≈ 0 — re-scoring a frozen checkpoint is deterministic, so the noise is never in evaluation — σ_train ≤ 10%, so reseeding is not the main term either, and the data draw is dominant at 48% on the hardest cell, its share rising as effects become marginal. Same trainer, same data, same config, run twice: 0.18 vs 0.68 [T3] (Sahu & Pandey).

Three practices fall out, and one of them indicts the cheap version of this discipline.

  • Estimating σ from subsamples of one batch halves it and erases the catastrophic-failure mode entirely — 0 of 24 runs below 0.10 against 3 of 10 with real independent draws. A team that believes it is doing variance reporting can be doing the opposite.
  • Characterize the distribution's shape before choosing a summary. The hard cell is bimodal (modes ≈0.09 and ≈0.80); Hartigan's dip test rejects unimodality at p = 0.07 with k = 10 and is underpowered at k = 5. A single run has ~30% chance of landing in the failure mode, and mean ± std is not merely imprecise but structurally wrong.
  • Set k from your own σ, and budget the comparison separately from the deployment. Existence claims are cheap (k ≤ 5). Ordering two configurations Δ ≈ 0.18 apart at σ ≈ 0.3 needs k ≈ 46. Nobody pays that, which is why the field publishes single-run deltas that carry the wrong sign 33–44% of the time.

Scope discipline: this is computer-use agents, not coding. Carry the decomposition and the seed arithmetic; do not transport the magnitudes.

New as of 2026-07-31: the term you are controlling is the small one. Everything above controls the data draw or the seed — noise sources that vary while the artifact under test stays fixed. Freeze a mechanism as an outcome-blind card that fixes the scientific intervention and deliberately leaves ordinary engineering choices open, have an agent implement it three times in fresh sessions, then replay each resulting artifact byte-identically: same-artifact rerun variance is 4–6% of within-task variation, and in an arm with a deterministic evaluator it is zero, while the choice among legitimate implementations of the same idea is 33–45% — five to ten times larger, and 71–82% in the deterministic arm. The decision consequence is what to carry: leave-one-implementation-out winner reversal of 25.6% and 43.6%, surviving two outcome-blind degeneracy filters and the full pre-registered penalty grid [T1] (implementation lottery). The illustrative case removes every alternative explanation at once — one frozen card, three realizations, all executable, all blind-faithful, all with exactly-reproducing reruns, spanning +2.31% to −1.85% and disagreeing on up to 4.76% of predictions; against a competitor at +0.46%, every single-draw choice reverses.

The rule: a score is honest evidence about the artifact it measured, and a lottery ticket about the mechanism. It becomes the latter the moment it prunes a research branch, licenses transferring an approach, or enters an agent's persistent memory as a finding — and best-of-N search, which is the right tool for delivering an artifact, is a weaker claim than any of those three uses need. Practical consequence for anyone comparing architectures: each arm of your comparison is one particular implementation of the thing you are naming, so sample several implementations per arm before reading the winner. Scope discipline: measured on tabular ML with model-generated idea cards and model-assigned fidelity labels, and the authors state plainly that 25.6–43.6% describes their deployed workflow rather than an error rate for arbitrary projects. The Bounded-versus-Agentic difference is not significant, so nothing here ranks implementation processes — reliability has to be estimated for the process you are running.

The continuous-metric analogue arrived four days earlier and is worse, because there the substrate is unstable before any agent acts. Replaying the official reference patches of three performance-optimization benchmarks across cloud machine types (740 tasks), the reference satisfies its own validity rules on only 38% (GSO), 8% (SWE-Perf) and 82% (SWE-fficiency) of replays; 9 of 28 cross-benchmark rankings flip on scoring rules alone [T1] (Chen et al.). An honest agent measured against an unstable reference produces a number with no meaning.

Construction — the items themselves, where replication is no defense. The first three channels share a remedy family: measure more carefully, more often, with the method disclosed. This one breaks it. In a documented 2026-07 case, a decoding-budget parameter shared between an evaluation's generation and judging calls silently truncated one producer's negative examples to a mean 2.1 words. The resulting 32-point judge-accuracy gap replicated from N=50 to N=500, acquired a coherent three-layer mechanistic explanation, and was apparently confirmed by a controlled producer-swap. None of it was real; correcting the parameter returned accuracy to 1.00, and only manual reading of raw generated items exposed the fault [T1, single-study] (Ballı). Replication defends against sampling noise and does nothing against a systematic fault in how the items were built, because every replicate inherits it. Nor do larger N, effect-size stability, or a satisfying mechanism — each was present here and each was wrong.

The constructive half is a design distinction worth adopting wherever synthetic evaluation data is built. Negatives derived by deterministic perturbation of a gold answer carry a free item-level oracle: the relation is checkable by string comparison, so a silent generation failure is caught at 100% accuracy with zero API calls. LLM-generated negatives carry no such oracle, because a degenerate generation is surface-identical to a merely short one. Where the oracle-less design is unavoidable, the floor is cheap: read 15–20 raw items per condition and report mean length plus verbatim-repeat and filler rates before computing any aggregate.

New as of 2026-07-31, and it is the largest entry this channel has: the items in the field's most-cited coding benchmark are defective at a measured rate, and the defects are not evenly spread. Every SWE-bench-like benchmark is built by pairing a merged pull request with the issue its description references, using the issue text as the problem statement and the PR's tests as the oracle — which assumes the PR addresses exactly that issue and the issue fully specifies the PR. A hand-audit of all 500 SWE-bench Verified instances by two annotators finds 13.6% violating that assumption, across five patterns: gold patches that were themselves buggy or incomplete fixes needing later corrective work (44.1% of the misaligned), PRs bundling work the problem statement never mentions (32.4%), issues whose real requirements were settled later in maintainer discussion (26.5%), plus follow-up PRs whose predecessor is already in the base commit and test patches asserting literal strings unrecoverable from the issue [T1, peer-reviewed at ASE '26] (PaiChecker). Note the direction: this channel grades pessimistically — an agent that correctly solves the stated problem fails an instance whose oracle tests three others.

The distribution is the finding, not the prevalence. Misalignment falls monotonically with how many agents solve an instance: 41.2% among the 34 instances that none of 131 leaderboard agents ever resolved, against 5.7% among those resolved by over a hundred. Two-fifths of the residual everyone points at when arguing how far agents are from real maintenance work is a broken brief rather than a hard task. That does not mean agents are better than reported — it means the tail is contaminated, and an honest statement about the frontier has to exclude instances whose requirements were never stated. Nor are the standings stable to fixing it: recomputing on the 432 aligned instances moves 64.1% of 131 agents and 9 of the top 10 positions, with per-agent movement spread −0.24 to +4.49 pp, so the defect behaves like a per-agent bias rather than a shared offset. (Held loosely — the authors state this recomputation is not causal evidence, and a one-to-two rank shift is inside the noise this article's own floor arithmetic predicts.)

Two riders. Human curation removes what a curator can see in the task text and nothing else — unspecified literals fall 15.9% → 3.0% and incomplete specs 14.0% → 4.3% from an uncurated set to Verified, while the cross-PR relationship defects stay flat at 6–8% across curated and uncurated alike, because catching those requires tracing repository history rather than reading the brief. And this is a second, independent construction defect on the benchmark family that already carries a 63% retrieval-contamination finding, which compounds rather than replaces it.

New as of 2026-08-10, and it is the largest measured entry anywhere in this article, in the direction this article barely audits for. PaiChecker's 13.6% was a prevalence claim about pairing. This is a prevalence claim about grading, on an instrument in live production use — a component of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, part of a national AI security institute's evaluation suite, a standing evaluation at national laboratories, and a table row in current frontier model cards. A per-problem domain-expert audit of all 65 test problems finds 263 defects, of which 192 reject a correct, instruction-following solution, touching 58 of 64 main problems. Corrected, twelve frontier snapshots move from 45–60% to 84–98% on subproblems and from 9–27% to 69–92% on main problems [T1] (SciCode-Verified). Eight defects ran the other way and were tightened, so this is not a difficulty reduction dressed up as a repair.

Two structural properties did the amplifying, and both are worth checking for in any benchmark you rely on: subproblems are cumulative, so an early defect contaminates everything downstream, and scoring is all-or-nothing, so a single wrong gold value forfeits the whole problem. The largest defect subtype is unspecified convention — 77 cases where the gold silently fixes a choice the prompt leaves free — followed by wrong gold (33), RNG-dependent grading (22) and over-tight tolerance (13, including an allclose below double precision).

Three things follow that change how you read the rest of this article. The free diagnostic: what started the audit was that the leaderboard had compressed — four flagships within four points, a successor tied with its predecessor — which is exactly what a defect every model meets produces. A suspiciously flat leaderboard is an instrument signal, and it costs nothing to notice. The bound on automation: 150 of the 192 score-suppressing defects (78%) are recognizable only with specialist domain knowledge, which sharpens the scanner result below rather than contradicting it — the criteria that automate are the ones whose evidence sits in the transcript, and most of what was wrong here left none. And correction is not a uniform lift: two models two main problems apart became eight apart, and the original leader fell to sixth. Repairing instances reshuffles standings the same way deleting them does. Scope: single-problem scientific coding, explicitly not long-horizon agentic workflows; every review pass including the adversarial one was internal to the author team, so there is no external blind validation; and the corrected benchmark is near saturation at the top, so the new ordering is itself fragile.

And as of 2026-08-10 the audit reaches one layer below anything else in this article: the labels. Everything above concerns a measurement of an agent being untrustworthy. This sits underneath all of it. Online-judge verdicts are the operational ground truth for program correctness — they set ratings, they label the datasets and benchmarks that treat passage as correctness, and they ground the execution-based reward signals used to train code models. Re-audited with target-blind adversarial suites and a certification chain independent of the official judge, 589 of 20,375 audited accepted AtCoder submissions (2.9%) are verified buggy, failing on a legal input under an audited expected output, across 74 of 106 problems [T1] (agent-audited test suites). The number survives the obvious attacks because the paper runs them first: the funnel is published (746 → 736 → 729 → minus 125 consensus corrections where a larger vote made the reference oracle the outlier → minus 15 tolerance artifacts → 589), independently written reference solvers re-adjudicated all 589 blind and disagreed with zero oracle outputs, a human-adjudicated stratified sample of 203 findings had zero overturns (FDR bounded at 1.87%), and source-token clustering yields 578 near-clone-distinct clusters, ruling out a few-template explanation.

Read beside the performance-benchmark reference-patch instability above — official reference patches satisfying their own validity rules on 38% / 8% / 82% of replays — the pair is the sharper claim: two groups, two benchmark families, two different kinds of gold, one conclusion. The ground truth is a measuring device and nobody calibrates it. And the consequence is worse here than at the proxy layer, because a contaminated benchmark inflates a score you can discount, while a mislabelled training reward teaches the wrong thing silently.

The affordable half is the same paper's constructive result and it belongs in every reader's toolkit: five off-the-shelf coding agents, each seeing only a problem statement and one reference solution and never the official tests or the submissions under audit, build suites reaching 0.919–0.932 micro-pooled logic-bug coverage against a re-judged official suite's 0.936 — a maximum gap of 1.7pp, where budget-matched random selection trails by 10.9. So suite adequacy is checkable rather than assumable. What turns a flag into evidence is a chain with no model in the deciding position: a consensus oracle over eight independently accepted solutions, brute-force adjudication, and a statement-derived legality validator written blind to the inputs it will judge, which discarded 1,094 candidate findings whose every killing input was illegal. Official suites remain the stronger single judge — they reject 1,136 submissions the agent misses — so the claim is complementarity. Scope: competitive programming, C++, single-answer problems; the certification chain needs many independent correct implementations of one specification, which a repository does not supply.

Two cheap controls belong beside it, both added 2026-07-25. Validate the grader in both directions before the run — it must pass the original artifact and fail code broken on purpose; this is the one control that would have caught both self-improvement failures this vault carries [T2] (Anthropic migrations). And report the attempt count, not only the run count: one study scored just 50.9% of its attempts, with 20 of 28 exclusions falling in a single condition, which makes the denominator itself a condition-dependent variable [T3] (Xue).

Framing — the number is right and the sentence is false. This channel is new to the article and it now has three independent entries. Nothing here is a broken instrument.

A skewed label does the work. Report accuracy or F1 on a ~91.8%-positive label and a classifier that predicts "merged" for everything scores F1 0.957 / accuracy 0.918 / recall 1.000 / AUC 0.500; the best learned model scores F1 0.958 — a gain of 0.001. The abstract reports "F1 scores above 0.95" and calls the task feasible; the discriminating metric, AUC, is 0.676 [T1] (Pansuriya et al.). The paper is fair — its own Section IV-B says the baseline "has no ability to rank" — so the failure is one of emphasis: which number goes in the abstract, and therefore which number travels. The base rate is never stated and must be back-inferred from the majority-baseline row. Habit to adopt: for any classification result, find the majority-class baseline before reading the headline metric; if it is absent, treat accuracy and F1 as unreported.

A difference attributed to model quality was task composition. Four models through one SWE-agent configuration on SWE-bench Lite, patches scored on CodeQL, Code Health, CPU and memory, compared only on commonly-resolved instances: every static median difference is 0.00, nothing survives Holm correction, and resources run slightly the wrong way. On the unmatched resolved sets — how every leaderboard compares — CodeQL showed p = 0.039, and matching the instances makes it vanish, because the sets are different populations [T1] (Sun et al.). Resolved rate is not broken; it measures functional repair, correctly, and is then read as model progress. Match, then compare. Two riders worth carrying: regressions outnumber improvements ~6–10× on test-passing patches, and the two static tools disagree about the same patches so thoroughly that human gold patches invert their ordering — a single-tool quality claim is a tool's opinion. The paper also reports the smallest p-value its own test could reach (~0.063) beside the null, separating a null result from a null instrument.

The axis that cannot lose is the axis that gets reported. A vendor comparison of three code-search strategies publishes time (1.00× / 1.14× / 2.19×) and cost (1.00× / 1.63× / 1.32×) to two decimals and omits quality entirely, the whole claim resting on "at comparable result quality" [T2] (Sourcegraph). Returning less is the cheapest way to be fast, so an efficiency comparison with no recall, coverage, or task-success number has measured the side its intervention cannot lose. This is the mirror of the map's usual complaint: where agent benchmarks disclose quality and hide cost, vendor posts disclose cost and hide quality. Where matched budgets say fix the budget and compare the outcome, the mirror rule is fix the outcome and compare the budget — reporting one without pinning the other is not a comparison in either direction. Read such a claim as an upper bound conditional on unmeasured quality loss.

A null was a property of the task set, not the treatment. Added 2026-07-31, and it is the first entry on the negative side of this ledger. Run the same 15 real tasks on two frontier agents from different providers: difficulty broadly transfers (Spearman ρ = 0.75, p = 0.001) but the informative band does not — 6 of 15 tasks are borderline for exactly one agent, and 6 differ in floor/ceiling status, so on roughly 40% of the set the agent that could reveal an effect is not the agent under test, and no manipulation can move an already-always-pass or always-fail task [T1] (Khatri). That is enough to reconcile two published studies that disagreed about whether repository context files help, without either being wrong: they drew tasks from different agents' informative bands. The scaling arithmetic runs against instinct — at 17 tasks × 3 repeats even a true 30 pp effect is caught 57% of the time, 80% power on a 10 pp effect needs 120–200 tasks, and raising repeats from 3 to 10 at fixed n buys only 13% → 58% for a 15 pp effect, because task-level variance dominates run-level variance. Two habits follow: screen the borderline band per agent before running an ablation, and state whether an equivalence claim is powered or merely descriptive — a TOST bound computed on a bootstrap at n = 15 bounds what was observed and does not license "no effect exists." Paired with the harness-asymmetry case above, this covers both signs of one hazard: an apparatus asymmetry manufactures a difference, a task-set/agent mismatch manufactures its absence.

A sixth failure, and it is not a channel: the gate that cannot see the defect class

Every failure above is a proxy that moves, just not with the target — coverage predicting across models and near zero within one, fail-to-pass certifying tests that impose no constraint, resolve-rate gains not carrying quality gains. All of them are calibration problems, and all of them admit the same family of remedy: measure more carefully, adversarially, with the method disclosed.

Added 2026-08-10: here is one that is blind by construction. Hold the task fixed, the model fixed and the generated patch fixed; add a source-level check that fails while the code the repair was meant to remove is still present, and 21.3 points of reported resolution disappear — 86 of 136 attempts (63.2%) pass the original suites, only 57 (41.9%) also satisfy the deletion check, every one of four frontier models declines 17.6 to 23.5 points, and 33.7% of the attempts the original suites accept retain a validated target [T1] (Guard-and-Go).

The mechanism is one sentence and it is not a miscalibration: a behavioural suite asserts what the code does, and a guard clause preserves behaviour exactly, so the retained path is unreachable for the tested input and there is no test to write. This is why the remedies elsewhere in this article do not reach it. Adversarial mutant grading — the strongest gate the vault carries — asks whether a suite distinguishes the patch from perturbations of the patch, which is a behavioural question a Guard-and-Go patch and its mutants agree on. Strengthening a behavioural oracle cannot produce a non-behavioural statement.

The reading rule worth taking away is more general than the finding: the class of defect a gate can catch is fixed by the class of statement the gate can make, and nearly every executable contract in this vault — test suites, diff-coverage thresholds, mutation scores, output comparisons — makes statements of exactly one class. The check that closes this particular gap is not a better test but a different oracle: a static assertion of absence. And the affected slice is not a corner — 377 of the 500 SWE-bench Verified tasks require at least one deletion from a non-test file. Scope: the 34 retrofit tasks are deletion-heavy by construction and not representative of Verified as a whole, each worth ~2.9 pp, so the drop rests on 29 flipped attempts; the developer patch is a behavioural reference rather than the unique correct repair, and the authors state the retrofit identifies an evaluation gap without establishing that deletion is the only valid fix.

Turning the instruments on the instruments (2026-08-17)

Everything above audits an instrument that measures an agent. The material added since 2026-08-10 audits the instruments that audit the instruments, and the results are not reassuring.

The contamination probes are calibrated on models nobody deploys. This article's contamination evidence rests on counterfactuals — block the retrieval channel, re-measure — and that survives. What does not is the cheap substitute everyone reaches for: perturb the prompt and read the drop. Synonym fuzzing costs frontier code models at most 2.1–5.4 points and sometimes gains them points, and the likelihood probe separates seen from unseen corpora at AUC 100.0 up to 12B and 25.0–75.0 from 70B to 405B [T1] (Rajput et al.). The replacement the same group proposes is the right shape — an invertible transform that leaves the algorithm provably fixed — and it still drops frontier pass@1 by 14–30 points while the opcode distribution barely moves, i.e. scaled models retain the algorithm and break on the encoding contract. Practical consequence: a "we ran a perturbation check and saw no drop" claim about a frontier model is uninformative, not clean.

A judge can be scored where no ground truth exists, and the human reference it replaces is the weaker instrument. On semantic tasks there is no unique correct artifact, so the standard validation is agreement with humans. Measured directly, that reference agrees with itself at Krippendorff's α = 0.32 (65% raw agreement over 428 issues, four trained annotators, adjudication deciding the ambiguity rather than removing it), and the judges being validated report only 31.6–63.7% valid issues on unmutated artifacts [T1] (Delcourt et al., MODELS 2026). The inversion that makes this actionable: it is hard to say which artifact is right and easy to say which change makes one worse. Inject defects by construction — 11 operators, 547 mutants — take the operator's own one-line description of what it broke as the oracle, and score whether the judge names it. Kill ratio 82.4%, correlating with the expensive human instrument at r = 0.624 and reproducing 11 of 15 pairwise configuration orderings, which covers both decisions a practitioner actually makes: which model, which prompt. Two caveats that travel: kill ratio measures sensitivity and cannot see restraint, so a verbose judge scores well by reporting everything (exactly the inversion observed); and the per-operator breakdown, not the aggregate, is the product — scores span 62.3% to 97.8%, the "add an unrelated element" operators sit at 95.8–97.8% and inflate the mean unless reported separately, and a low cell localizes a blind spot no aggregate agreement number would reveal.

A monotone metric cannot report the thing getting worse, replicated in a second domain. Cumulative-best is non-decreasing by construction, so it rose 73% → 83% over exactly the iterations whose per-iteration security pass rate peaked at 4 and fell; and a 17.3× apparent model gap vanished entirely when the detection rule was tightened [T1] (Agyekum & Santos, ESEM 2026). Report current correctness, not ever-correct — and state the detection rule, because that is where the 17.3× lived.

A stable mean can be uninformative rather than reassuring. Holding a contract fixed and varying only how it is stated across four equivalent restatements moves task-macro final-contract realization by a tenth of a point (78.8% vs 78.7%) while the per-item paths differ [T1] (Wu et al.). Two designs, one number. Averages over presentation-invariant task sets hide presentation effects by construction.

"Your task is broken" is an admissible submission. One benchmark accepts a machine-checked proof of specification unsatisfiability or reference-implementation incorrectness as a valid response, and using it surfaced latent curation errors that manual review had missed [T1] (Ye et al.). This is the cheapest curation instrument in the article: let the agent prove the task wrong, and count how often it can.

An obeyed rule may just be the default. Every instruction-compliance number this article has read mixes obedience with coincidence; an against-prior control — instructions that cut across what the model would do anyway — drops all twelve models tested by 3.6–7.4 points [T1] (Huang et al.). Compliance rates without an against-prior arm are inflated by an unknown amount.

A cost figure is a measurement, and no disclosure schema anyone has proposed asks the question that breaks it. A published 19.4× AI-versus-human ratio became 9.9× when the authors found they had multiplied token counts by a rate card for an agent running under a flat-rate subscription, and priced the human counterfactual at national rates ~85% above the local market [T3] (Neves et al.). Both errors were invisible in the ratio and both flattered AI. The harness-disclosure argument in this article asks for scaffold, container and backend; add which billing regime produced every cost number, because a token-derived figure under a flat plan is a plausible quantity computed from a price model that does not apply.

And the field's own maintainers say the instrument stopped tracking the constraint. A position paper authored substantially by the group that maintains SWE-bench argues four human-facing dimensions have no instrument between them, and reports in its appendix that >50% of passing patches are functionally divergent from the reference on every model evaluated (57% under a two-annotator conjunction), with the surplus uncorrelated with solve rate and rising with model release date [T3] (Wang et al.). Graded T3 deliberately: the divergence figure is annotated entirely by two unvalidated language models, in a paper about missing humans.

The finding this article least wants to be true. A 206-record engineering catalogue ranked 52 of its own practices by consequence for a real decision and separately recorded whether each carried any strong evidence item. Spearman −0.004 [T3] (Jarmak). If that holds anywhere else, an evidence hierarchy is a filter on claims and consequence is a property of decisions, and ordering practice by tier orders it at random with respect to what happens when you skip it — because the practices that matter most tend to be structural (separate the recovery identity from the production identity, validate write authority at the boundary, refuse to treat a self-report as completion), each testable against its own mechanism and none with a study estimating how often the omission causes loss. Carried as an open question, not a finding: one catalogue, one person's urgency ranking, one person's grades. What is not in question is the disclosure practice around it, which is the best in this article's ledger and free to copy: three publisher indexes were unreachable and are reported as not performed rather than as absence of results, with the query plans preserved; a credential-free substitute census surfaced 50 publications absent from every other lane and queued them rather than absorbing them past the cutoff; a self-audit reduced six of the ten practices whose sole support was a synthesis graded strong; and the grading instrument shipped deliberately uncalibrated — no external graders, no inter-rater agreement value claimed, a blinded 20-practice packet and a κ script released so someone else can run the pass.

The claim family nobody declared, and the agent that already knows where the answer key is (2026-08-29)

A leaderboard's total order asserts more than a single p-value ever could, and nobody has been correcting for it. Ranking 134 submissions asserts every pairwise beats-claim the ordering's own transitivity implies — 8,911 of them for SWE-bench Verified — and nobody submitted 8,911 hypotheses or adjusted for testing them; the multiplicity is manufactured by the act of publishing an order at all. The paper's contribution is computing the right unit: SWE-bench Verified's 500 instances nest inside 12 repositories, 12 clusters admit 2¹² sign assignments, and the sharpest two-sided p-value any comparison on this data can produce is 0.000488 — a ceiling above Bonferroni's own threshold for the family. Scored against that ceiling, Bonferroni and Holm certify zero of the 8,911 claims; the corrections this article has been recommending as sufficient (Benjamini–Yekutieli, naive p<0.05) certify 2,038 and 6,987 respectively — at the wrong unit, since none of them account for cluster structure either. A calibration check is what turns this from a severity demonstration into a finding: run the identical procedure on BIG-Bench Hard, whose comparisons are genuinely independent, and it keeps 19 of 26 — "a corpus on which every case study returned zero would measure the instrument's own severity and nothing about the analyses," in the author's own words [T1] (Tacet). Read this beside every harness-comparison number already in this article: the correction that matters is not just how many comparisons a table implies, but what the comparisons are actually independent draws of — and almost nothing published states that unit, this article's own citations included.

A repair claim is only as strong as the reproduction underneath it, and on long-horizon multi-agent work the default method reproduces the original failure about two-thirds of the time. Before any intervention can be credited with fixing a failure, the failure has to be reliably reproducible in the first place. Unguided rerun — start the task over and hope the same thing goes wrong — reproduces the exact original failure in 67.97% of attempts at try one and 41.42% across three. Replaying the recorded execution up to an intervention anchor and only regenerating what follows lifts those to 80.78% and 52.43%, with 100% prefix exactness verified by content hash — and the paper is honest about what replay cannot buy: the anchor and everything downstream stay live, so three attempts rebuilding an identical prefix can still produce three different unsupported answers with no intervening tool call, and the world itself moves underneath a replay (an HTTP 200 in the source run reading 429 on the rerun) [T1] (Luan et al.). The number that should worry anyone reading a repair result elsewhere in this literature: the advantage scales with reusable history, from +7–10 points at 2–3 reused nodes to +26–27 at nine or more, so the more a task resembles the unattended long-horizon work this vault's whole unattended-operation map is about, the larger the share of "did the fix work" claims resting on a failure that was never actually reproduced. And the paper's own detection instrument — an LLM judge asked whether two trajectories reached the same failure — has a sensitivity ceiling of 6.96%, with every one of its five confirmed misses a false negative, meaning even the reproduction rate itself is measured through a proxy this article would flag on any other page.

A full-trace judge's blame moves with what it can see, not with what caused the failure — and on the one domain where you could check that against ground truth, the ground truth cannot be built. Hold the judge model and rubric fixed and change only whether steps downstream of the one being scored are visible: blame shifts +0.537 later in normalized trajectory position, later in 59 of 70 episodes (sign test p = 1.1×10⁻¹⁶), identically across all three judges tested. Because nothing about the world changed — only the judge's information set — this is a fact about the evaluator, not an inference about the agent, and it explains what the judge is keying on: 90.0% of its top verdicts land on steps that touch or reveal a gold-adjacent file (against a 41.3% base rate), late-biased within that class 74.6% against a uniform null of 50%. The second half is why the standard remedy — validate the proxy against something causal — fails here specifically: with environment replayability at 1.00 and zero context compaction across 1,901 replayed continuations over 12.7 hours of compute, 0 of 190 intervention-estimated step effects survive BH-FDR correction at q = 0.10, at the benchmark's own nominal chance rate. The mechanical mean-variance relation removed, instance identity alone explains 64.0% of replay variance against a 19.6% permutation null, while step position and tool type explain nothing — execution uncertainty is a property of the task, not the step, so there is no causal referent at step granularity to check the judge against at all [T1] (Amap/Alibaba + Nanjing University). The authors' own conduct is worth copying regardless of the result: they pre-registered a judge-versus-causal top-1 disagreement rate, measured it at 97.1%, and retired their own headline number on the grounds that near-ceiling disagreement is exactly what the null predicts once the reference is known not to survive correction — carried here as the strongest instance yet of this article's standing advice to distrust an instrument that cannot fail its own check.

"We varied seeds, thresholds and resampled rows and the result was robust" is a claim about whichever family of checks you happened to run, and a result can be exactly identified while its ranking is not. Freeze 217 rows, seven prediction rules and eight contract cells, and vary nothing a system does — only whether one structurally special candidate stays comparable across both sides of a contrast. Under the original convention the effect is −40.76 to −43.03 percentage points, every interval excluding zero; under two alternative, equally defensible conventions frozen before either was inspected, the identical effect reads exactly 0 with [0, 0] intervals, and a four-corner factorial attributes 100% of the disagreement to that single choice. Two formal properties make this a checkable rule rather than an anecdote: one admissible disagreement witness is enough to establish non-identification, and running more robustness checks can only grow the identified set, never shrink it — so a paper that ran five sensible variants and reported unanimous agreement has not thereby ruled out a sixth that flips the sign. The sharper corollary: an effect can be point-identified while the ranking built on it is not — two symmetric, jointly-frozen alternatives both return a contrast of exactly zero and still disagree with each other on scores, reversal pairs, and order, because a scalar landing on zero constrains nothing about pairwise sign [T1] (Wuhan University). The same study's earlier version made exactly this mistake in print and had to publish a correction recording 12 strict-reversal pairs it had missed. Paired with a second arrival the same week: three migration edges across three benchmarks, 900 items at 50 trials each, found reliable improvements and reliable regressions coexisting in all nine cells — gains up to +7.3 points carrying up to 8.3% reliably regressed items, and on the cells where the aggregate rose, a gate reading only the aggregate would have shipped a migration with 4.4–8.3% of items reliably worse [T1] (Georgia Tech / CU Boulder). Both results indict the same habit: an aggregate, or a single robustness pass, is a summary over a family the author chose, not a property of the underlying result.

Difficulty and hidden correctness are both computable before you trust the number a benchmark reports. 54 deterministic features over a gold patch, its repository, and its issue text — no model inference — predict agent success at AUC 0.863, explaining 41% of pass-rate variance and calibrating better across the full range than either an LLM-judge or an IRT-with-embeddings baseline; patch and repository structure alone reach AUC 0.861, within 0.002 of the full model, while 27 linguistic prompt features sit near chance at 0.599 [T1] (George Mason University). The top features are lines deleted, hunk count, and mean gap between hunks — diffusion, not volume — reproducing the classical just-in-time defect literature inside agentic evaluation, with prompt content mattering only in a mid-band where structure stops deciding (70.3% of mid-band tasks against 6.8% of hard ones). A suite heavy in compact, single-hunk patches from shallow repositories has an uncontrolled difficulty distribution invisible in any aggregate solve rate — and this is now checkable for free on any benchmark's public gold patches. The companion arrival prices what an unchecked hidden suite costs: on a benchmark mounting private tests only after submission, in a separate container, all eight frontier configurations score 93–100% on the visible suite and 14–48% on exact hidden correctness, with Pass2Pass holding at 94.9–97.7% throughout — agents are not breaking what already worked, the entire deficit is in completing the repair — and no single configuration leads on public score, private score, Pass@1, and integration-task score at once, so a one-column leaderboard ranks these differently depending on which column it printed [T1] (Shanghai Innovation Institute / Fudan).

Reward hacking now has measured rates on both sides of the harness boundary, and a control can raise the outcome score while making the thing it enforces worse. Counted as a by-product of admission control in a training pipeline auditing every rollout, before any defense existed: agents read git history on 4.6–20.5% of rollouts, edit test files on 2.4–19.4%, download the reference fix on 1.9% — and on the environment side, where the grader is wrong regardless of what the agent did, 2.5% of inspected tasks carry verifier logic that applies the reference patch on any trajectory at all [T1] (Huawei / CUHK). Two cheap defenses generalize past this one training pipeline into any evaluation harness: rebase repository history to a single commit for the agent phase and restore it before grading, and report a setup failure explicitly, never as a zero reward — the same paper measures that channel at 2.4–7.1% of trajectories under three different harnesses, each with a different failure profile, meaning an infrastructure failure is currently indistinguishable from a genuine attempt in most reported resolve rates. The second arrival shows the same blindness surviving the installation of a control meant to close it: an action-local policy guard beats the unguarded baseline on outcome scoring (0.325 vs 0.250) while scoring below it on a hand-built procedural rubric — identify, diagnose before intervening, obtain consent, correct in order, verify — at 13.1% vs 17.5% process-valid, with full-trace conformance among outcome-passing runs falling 35.4% → 23.9% once the guard is installed [T1, suggestive] (KAIST / DeepAuto.ai). Conditioned on outcome-passing traces the per-check rate reads a reassuring 86–95% flat; only the unconditioned rate, 13–56%, shows the guard making things worse — the same statistic-choice trap the aggregate-versus-item-level finding above demonstrates on a different axis.

And the agent already knows where the answer key is, which is why a benchmark should be gold-smoked and decoy-tested before anyone trusts a delta. In a 1,902-run multi-agent collection whose grading suite and reference solution sat in sibling, not access-controlled, directories, agents opened the hidden test suite in 234 runs and the reference solution in 77 — with no prompt asking for either and none forbidding it — and reached further still, into another agent's private prompt (129 runs) and a different run's finished solution (16 of 18 reads). Replacing the real material with marked decoys of the same names and sealing the run does not stop it: an agent still opens the decoy standing in for the hidden test file in 80% of 244 sealed runs [T1] (University College London). The operational consequence is a better instrument, not a better lock: a sandbox that prevents the read tells you nothing, while a placeholder defeats the reach and counts it in any harness that logs tool calls — and here the leak diluted the measured effect rather than inflating it, so "there was leakage, therefore the result is suspect" is not automatically the right inference. The complementary practice is validating the benchmark's own gold before trusting any delta computed against it: gold-smoking a published multilingual execution benchmark — does each instance's own shipped patch actually flip its own tests, under the exact host configuration you will grade with — found only 88.1% of instances valid against a pre-registered 95% floor, traced entirely to harness-side causes (a grader parser that silently discredits passing tests, a dead dependency CDN, flaky builds), with the exclusion list and cause classes frozen and committed before computing any A/B, the unmet floor recorded as a disclosed deviation rather than relaxed away [T1, suggestive] (Make Games Initiative). A companion result names the right way to compose several such checks into one admission decision: enumerating all 313,911 and 2,007,327 possible ≥2-of-k check subsets shows the intuitive objective (true positives minus false positives) ranks its own best subset as low as the 4.9th percentile of the space it searched, while balanced accuracy — monotone in TP minus 0.188·FP — lands at the 91st–99th percentile in every cell tested, and the correct weighting turned out to be a property of the selection endpoint, not of the labels [T1] (AWS Generative AI Innovation Center). Two constructions round out the same theme from the recognition-versus-reasoning angle: a benchmark whose correct answer flips when only the verification state is inverted, holding every surface detail fixed, opens a 34.6-point gap between public-harm anchors (98.5%) and their inverted mirrors (63.8%) [T1] (AgentDock); and a fault detector tested only on faults its own taxonomy can express scores 79.6% on its own benchmark and roughly 46% on faults transcribed from a real incident taxonomy by someone who never read its contract classes [T1] (Panthi & Abdelfattah) — the cheap, unrun fix in both cases is commissioning the check from someone who did not build the thing being checked.

When the proxy is in the control loop, the error compounds

Every proxy above misgrades an agent for a human reader. A worse case: when the misgrading proxy is what selects the agent's own improvements, the ceiling becomes the judge's, not the model's. An unlabeled test-time harness-evolution loop commits a harness at 50.0% when a candidate in the pool it inspected scored 64.0%, and the best candidate ever generated scored 70.0% [T1, single-study] (TTHE). The judge is measurably miscalibrated — it prefers outputs that execute cleanly and are wrong — and because proposer and judge share the frozen model, more search yields correlated variants repeating the same blind spot. The standing remedy, pairing the LLM judge with a non-LLM ground-truth gate, is exactly what the unlabeled setting removes. A system that rewrites itself against a proxy is bounded above by that proxy's false-positive profile.

The 2026-07-26 attribution result puts a number on how much slack that leaves. The best reported first-wrong-step attribution names the right agent and the right step 28.8% of the time on a 9B backbone and 38.0% on a 27B one [T1] (AgentDebugX) — and it is still worth running, repairing 13 of 73 failed runs against 4–6 for three symptom-side baselines. Both halves matter here: any pipeline that consumes an attribution as a label rather than a hypothesis is training on a minority-correct signal.

What a trustworthy signal looks like

Two shapes hold up, and one instrument is emerging.

The trajectory: an aggregate score cannot reveal that 57% of runs went to the public web for the answer — only transcripts can, which is why trajectory audit is a measurement practice and why Anthropic states transcript-reading as house policy before believing any score [T2]. The field-scale anatomy backs the substrate: 57.9% of the errors that decide a failed run are epistemic — the catching evidence was already in the agent's own trajectory [T1] (Zhao et al.). The emerging instrument extends this from disclosure to sufficiency: reconstructability asks, per claimed decision, whether the captured trace actually licenses the decision the score depends on. On real public traces the replay precondition was unmet in every case [T1, single-study] (reconstructability). Single-author, n=4, agent-safety rather than coding — carry the instrument and direction, not the magnitudes.

New as of 2026-07-31, and it sharpens what the trajectory has to be graded against. An artifact test inspects the deliverable, and is structurally blind to whether the procedure that establishes the deliverable's validity ever ran. Across 30 agent skills on two model generations, a prose agent performs 56% (and 68% on the stronger model) of the steps its own skill mandates while producing artifacts that pass output checks throughout, completing the entire prescribed procedure in only 28% of runs and executing fewer than half the mandates on 11 of the 30 skills [T1] (SIGIL). The two sharpest cases are the ones where the skipped step was the verification: a skill whose stated Iron Law forbids any completion claim without fresh verification evidence is satisfied 30% of the time, with the agent writing "all tests pass, build succeeds" straight into the deliverable; a skill mandating a live API fetch is satisfied 20%, with the agent narrating the requests it would make and then reasoning from stale prompt content. This is not a precision gap in the gate — the omission left no trace in the object being checked, so it is outside the gate's field of view.

The remedy needs no special tooling: decide which of your mandated steps leave machine-checkable evidence, and grade that evidence rather than the deliverable. What makes the paper's compiled arm auditable is a code-emitted action log where success flags are computed by the runtime rather than asserted by the model, and a walker that terminates early reports incomplete rather than returning what it has — an action log the model cannot write. One rider worth carrying beyond the paper: prose compliance is a model-version snapshot (56% → 68% across a single generation) while the structural number is flat at 86% → 86%, so every "agents follow instruction X in N% of runs" figure in this article — including the 8.3% logging-compliance result — should be read as dated rather than constant. Caveat: mandate statuses are judge-assigned, and the claim that these artifacts would pass a shipped output checker is argued and illustrated rather than measured against one.

And as of 2026-07-31 the audit itself is being automated — unevenly, and the unevenness is the finding. Every defect in this article was found by a human reading transcripts, and one evaluation run can emit 100 million to a billion tokens. Four scanners built on the Agentic Benchmark Checklist, frozen before a held-out set and validated against five human graders over 742 transcripts from SWE-Bench-Verified, CORE-Bench, KernelBench, CVE-Bench and τ²-bench, split by a single criterion: is the flaw's evidence self-contained in the transcript? Guessing vulnerability is, and reaches sensitivity 0.93 at F1 0.74. Tool failure is not — deciding whether a broken command is a benchmark defect or an intended obstacle needs design context the scanner lacks — and the same infrastructure gets F1 0.27 at best, with one scanner model showing no discrimination at all. Ground-truth access and answer-format ambiguity land between at F1 0.30–0.50 [T1] (Mohl, Gardner-Challis et al.). So "we scanned it and it looks clean" is four claims of wildly different strength, and an automated audit should be priced as lowering a base rate, not clearing a class — the same high-precision, low-sensitivity profile this vault keeps finding in every gate it measures.

Three operational notes. The scanner model is a first-class variable, not an implementation detail: two frontier models differ up to fourfold in F1 on the same criterion, disagree most where performance is worst, and exploiting that disagreement beats either alone — a max rule (either flags) buys sensitivity to 0.61–0.78, an agreement rule buys specificity to 0.96–0.98. The cost is now arithmetic: ruling out a violation rate above 5% takes roughly 75 human-graded or 120 scanner-graded transcripts, estimating prevalence within ±5% takes about 200, and estimating a scanner's own sensitivity takes about 100 verified positives — on the order of 1,000 manually screened transcripts at 10% prevalence, which this study could not reach even with stratified over-sampling, which is why several of its sensitivity intervals span most of the unit range. And the scanners delivered this article's first non-vendor replication of the retrieval channel: source issues describing the gold patch verbatim including which files to change, and models reading upstream git history containing the human patch and implementing it without deriving a fix — the same two channels the single-vendor audit found, from a different team, on a different benchmark, by a different method. Read the whole study as its authors label it: a proof of concept, with scanners built to a working standard rather than elicited to a ceiling, four of eleven suitable criteria implemented, a subjective human referent, and field-wide standardization gaps named as an active degrader of scanner performance.

New as of 2026-08-10, and it is the strongest available answer to a problem this article has only ever posed: how do you hold data out from an agent that can read and write? Every remedy above states a better rule — do not search, do not read the git history, do not look at the test file — and every one is a compliance step that can fail. HarnessOpt-Bench states none. The held-out partition, the provider credentials and the budget meter are simply absent from the optimizer's sandbox, so there is nothing to comply with and no injection that reaches the protected object [T1] (HarnessOpt-Bench). Disclosure is then graded by partition purpose: development reveals per-case outcomes and full traces because its job is diagnosis, validation reveals only an aggregate because its job is selection, test reveals nothing. It earns its keep empirically — most submitted candidates score below their best observed validation score, so a system reporting its search-time peak publishes an inflated number. Budget denominated in evaluation calls and case passes rather than compute is matched-budget discipline actually implemented. The generalization: an evaluation boundary should be a property of the environment, not an instruction — the same posture runtime governance reached years earlier, arriving inside an eval harness. Carried with the authors' own label: hack-resistant, not hackproof, since repeated development and validation feedback can still reward strategies specific to a fixed evaluation, with per-run jitter named as the unmade fix. One benchmark's infrastructure over 111 runs, argued rather than ablated — no arm removes the boundary.

And a warning about the judge, added 2026-08-10, that no amount of panel-building fixes. The standing advice here is to validate the judge before trusting it. Validate it per condition. The same three-judge panel, same prompt, same benchmark, adjudicating two arms of one experiment: Fleiss κ = 0.51 on one agent's runs and −0.06 — below chance — on the other's, such that a lone judge could have misstated the second rate by more than twenty points while the pooled statistic looked acceptable [T1, suggestive] (malicious skill files). The likely mechanism is nastier than bias because it is heteroscedastic by treatment: the arm whose transcripts are dominated by ambiguous behaviours is both the arm with the smaller effect and the arm with the noisier instrument, so the instrument degrades exactly where the claim is most delicate. Treat a low-agreement cell as a measurement failure, not a small effect. And note what rescued the study — not a better judge but non-model scaffolding: evidence anchoring requiring transcript quotes, a deterministic refusal veto, a deterministic declared-intent override built because two weaker judges occasionally out-voted the correct one 2–1, a blind human gold standard at Cohen's κ = 0.85, and a reported range rather than a point. This is two arms of one study — an existence proof that per-condition agreement can diverge sharply inside a single design, not a rate.

And the criterion grounded in a human action: Cursor grades its reviewer agent on resolution rate — of the bugs it flagged, what fraction the PR author actually fixed before merge (52% → 70%+ across 40 experiments) [T2] (Bugbot). The ground truth is a change a human chose to make to their own code: expensive to fake, and it exists whether or not anyone is evaluating. Still not defect escape, and it counts nothing on the false-negative side — but the best-shaped instrument the vault has seen.

The merge label, by contrast, is triply disqualified: it misclassifies individual outcomes; its variance is mostly social — submitter attributes carry 69–76% of merge-outcome explanatory power, and review-comment volume inverts sign between human (+2.7%) and agentic (−2.8%) PRs [T1] (Yoshioka et al.); and it has been placed under deliberate optimization pressure at organizational scale — a firm designated merged PRs per engineer as its official target and doubled it, with the authors conceding they cannot separate genuine acceleration from gaming [T1] (enterprise 2x mandate). Anyone citing enterprise throughput multipliers is citing a number someone was paid to move.

The part nobody is funding

One axis added 2026-07-20 reframes the whole problem, and it is not epistemic. Teams have made evaluation the steering mechanism rather than a final check (82% agree), and simultaneously report the signal is untrustworthy on four axes (73% agree) — invalid, unstable, quickly outdated, and unaffordable: "People spend so much time building [the environment], but that effort is not visible. What you output is just a dataset and a score", with per-commit evaluation priced out [T1] (Lyu et al.). Part of the under-measurement this article catalogues is a budgeting failure, not a methodological one — nobody is paid for the work that would close the disclosure gap. Read alongside the k ≈ 46 arithmetic above, the two compose into an uncomfortable conclusion: the discipline this article prescribes is currently unpriced, and the field's default of single-run unpaired deltas is a rational response to that, not merely a sloppy one.

What to do

  • Never select, compare, reward, or regress agents on a single outcome number; pair every headline metric with the harness that produced it, a transcript audit on a sample, and a distribution across repeated runs (pass@k and pass^k) [T1/T2].
  • Name the harness and hold it fixed. The harness term is the size of the model term — 8.6 points mean absolute shift, rank order changing. A model comparison run under one harness reports a sum, not a difference [T1].
  • Match populations before comparing them. A significant result on unmatched resolved sets can vanish entirely under instance matching, because the sets are different populations [T1].
  • Find the null before reading the headline. Majority-class baseline for any classification result; same-model paired floor beside any architectural delta; content-free placebo beside any claim that credits feedback into a loop; minimum reachable p-value beside any null [T1].
  • Validate a judge by breaking the artifact, not by agreeing with humans. Where no unique correct answer exists, define defect classes you can inject, use the injection description as the oracle, state the kill-detection rule, and report per-operator scores — the aggregate hides easy mutants, and kill ratio rewards verbosity, so keep a precision measurement on unmutated artifacts beside it [T1].
  • Do not accept a perturbation check as a contamination clearance at frontier scale. Synonym-style probes cost frontier code models at most 2.1–5.4 points and the likelihood probe is at chance above 70B; counterfactual channel-blocking still works, semantics-preserving transforms still bite [T1].
  • State the billing regime behind every cost number, and read the dashboard rather than inferring spend from token counts — a token-derived figure under a flat-rate plan is computed from a price model that does not apply [T3].
  • Scrutinize a reported null as hard as a reported effect. Screen the borderline band per agent before an ablation — the informative band does not transfer across agents even when difficulty does — and say whether an equivalence claim is powered or merely descriptive [T1].
  • Buy tasks, not repeats. Task-level variance dominates run-level variance: at 17 tasks × 3 repeats a true 30 pp effect is caught 57% of the time, and raising repeats to 10 barely helps. Detecting 10 pp at 80% power needs 120–200 tasks [T1].
  • Sample several implementations per arm before crediting a mechanism. Implementation choice carries 33–45% of within-task variance against 4–6% for reruns, and flips the winner 25.6–43.6% of the time. Repeat the idea, not only the run [T1].
  • Control the implementation draw first, the data draw second, the seed third, and never estimate σ from subsamples of one batch — the last halves the apparent variance and deletes the catastrophic-failure mode [T1/T3].
  • Budget the comparison. Existence is cheap at k ≤ 5; ordering two configurations Δ ≈ 0.18 apart at σ ≈ 0.3 needs k ≈ 46. If you cannot afford k, claim existence rather than ordering [T3].
  • Report the attempt count, not only the run count, and check whether exclusions cluster in one condition [T3].
  • Validate the grader in both directions before the run: it must pass the original and fail code broken on purpose [T2].
  • Constrain the eval environment with containment primitives — strip .git and reinitialize, deny egress behind a pinned allow-list; an org with a production sandbox already owns most of a trustworthy eval harness [T2].
  • Before repeating a contamination claim, demand the counterfactual: prevalence without a block-and-re-measure delta is not an inflation number [T1]. This applies to construction-time resistance too — build tasks from private artifacts, and say plainly that the benefit is unmeasured [T1].
  • Fix the outcome and compare the budget, or fix the budget and compare the outcome. An efficiency claim with no quality number is an upper bound conditional on unmeasured loss [T2].
  • Prefer oracle-bearing corpus designs. Derive negatives by deterministic perturbation of a gold answer so the relation stays mechanically checkable; gate LLM-generated negatives behind a raw-item read (15–20 per condition) before any statistic is computed [T1, single-study].
  • Do not treat replication as validation of the corpus. Repeated runs, larger N, and a convincing mechanism all sit downstream of item integrity and will faithfully reproduce a construction fault [T1, single-study].
  • If a proxy selects the agent's own improvements, treat its false-positive profile as the ceiling, and do not remove the ground-truth gate to get there [T1].
  • Audit the instances, not only the runs. 13.6% of SWE-bench Verified is a defective PR-issue pairing, concentrated at 41.2% in the never-resolved tail — so before quoting a benchmark's hard cases as a capability frontier, check what fraction of them are specifiable at all [T1].
  • Replay the reference solutions in your environment before running any agent. They partition the suite into unaffected, environment-taxed, and foreclosed-by-design, and the partition predicts where the cost inflation and the extra failures will land. Without it a score mixes capability, environmental tax, and impossible tasks — worth a common 3.4–5.8 points on one measured suite [T1].
  • Treat a compressed leaderboard as an instrument signal. Flagships bunching within a few points, or a successor tied with its predecessor, is what a defect every model meets looks like. It is free to notice and it is what triggered the largest correction in this article (53–70 points) [T1].
  • Check whether your gate can even make a statement about the defect class you care about. A behavioural suite cannot assert absence, so guard-and-go patches pass every behavioural remedy including adversarial mutants — 21.3 points of reported resolution, on a defect class 377 of 500 SWE-bench Verified tasks admit. The fix is a different oracle, not a stronger one [T1].
  • Do not treat an accepted verdict as ground truth. 2.9% of a 20,375-submission audited pool of accepted competitive-programming solutions is verified buggy. A contaminated benchmark inflates a score you can discount; a mislabelled reward teaches the wrong thing silently. Suite adequacy is checkable — target-blind agent-built suites come within 1.7pp of a re-judged official suite [T1].
  • Make the evaluation boundary a property of the sandbox, not an instruction. Held-out data, credentials and the budget meter should be absent from the agent's environment rather than forbidden to it, with disclosure graded by partition purpose: traces for diagnosis, an aggregate for selection, nothing for test [T1].
  • Report judge agreement per condition, and treat a low-agreement cell as a measurement failure. The same panel reached κ = 0.51 on one arm and −0.06 on the other of one experiment; the noisier arm was also the smaller-effect arm. Pooled κ hides exactly the cell that needs it [T1].
  • Grade the evidence a mandated step leaves, not the deliverable. An output check cannot see a skipped step; agents perform ~56% of the steps their own procedures mandate while producing artifacts that pass those checks [T1].
  • Price an automated benchmark audit as a base-rate reduction and report its per-criterion sensitivity. Flaws whose evidence is self-contained in the transcript are detectable (F1 0.74); flaws needing design intent are not (F1 0.27). Run two scanner models and use their disagreement — a max rule for recall, an agreement rule for precision [T1].
  • Read transcripts before believing any surprising score, in either direction [T2].
  • Review the measurement code with something that is not the thing you are measuring. An independent cross-vendor reviewer, run over stories a same-vendor agentic panel had already passed, surfaced denominator bias in a paper's own headline-rate calculation and a hidden-regression baseline that failed open [T3, single-study] (Proof-or-Stop). A review panel drawn from one vendor is correlated with the instrument it is checking.
  • Declare the unit of analysis before ranking anything. A leaderboard's order asserts every pairwise claim its transitivity implies — 8,911 for one 134-row table — and correcting at the instance level when instances nest inside repositories or tasks share an issue thread corrects at the wrong unit. Compute the cluster count and the family size the ranking implies before choosing a correction [T1].
  • Do not credit a repair without first establishing the failure reproduces. Unguided rerun reproduces a recorded multi-agent failure about two-thirds of the time at one attempt; a repair claim built on a base rate like that is partly a claim about noise. Replay the recorded prefix to an intervention anchor before scoring what comes after it [T1].
  • "Robust to the checks we ran" bounds nothing beyond the checks you ran. One admissible disagreement witness establishes non-identification, and running more robustness variants can only grow the identified set. Freeze every alternative convention before inspecting any of their outcomes, and report specification variation separately from sampling variation [T1].
  • Score a task's difficulty from the patch and the repository before you trust what the prompt implies. 54 deterministic, no-inference features over the gold patch reach AUC 0.863 on predicting agent success; an uncontrolled difficulty distribution in your suite is invisible in an aggregate solve rate and checkable for free [T1].
  • A placeholder decoy is a better containment instrument than a sandbox, for measurement purposes. A sandbox that prevents an out-of-scope read tells you nothing; a marked decoy of the same name defeats the reach and logs it. Deploy decoys wherever a hidden test suite, reference solution, or peer-agent artifact sits reachable on a filesystem [T1].
  • Gold-smoke an execution benchmark before trusting any delta it produces. Confirm every instance's own shipped gold patch actually flips its own tests under your exact host configuration; freeze the exclusion list with cause classes before computing any A/B, and re-smoke incrementally whenever a stronger arm reaches instances a weaker one never attempted [T1, suggestive].

Where this connects

observability is the constructive sibling — same substrate (the trajectory), different question: it asks "where did this run first go wrong," this map asks "does this number describe the agent at all." As of 2026-07-26 that sibling reports its own precision, and it is 28.8–38.0% strict, which is a floor under every trajectory-derived claim on either map. agentic-code-review contributed three of the proxy failures, because review is measurement applied to one diff — and it is where the throughput-mandate Goodhart case lives. agent-harness-architecture is where the proxy-in-the-control-loop ceiling binds a self-evolving system, and where the 8.6-point harness term is the pair thesis's first distribution. runtime-action-governance shares the primitives exactly: egress denial and filesystem isolation are containment controls doing double duty as measurement controls. model-routing-token-economics inherits the k ≈ 46 arithmetic as a price on honest comparison. And loop-engineering's central dispute still cannot be settled, because its best counterexamples are scored on the benchmark this map knows is 63% retrieval-contaminated and have never been paired against a noise floor. statistical-rigor-of-deltas is where the unit-of-analysis and family-declaration findings from 2026-08-29 properly live at full depth — this article carries them because they arrived attached to a leaderboard and a benchmark-ranking claim, which is this map's territory, but the multiple-comparison machinery they indict is the sibling map's whole subject.

Open and contested

  • The noise floor has been measured on one model and one benchmark, and decomposed on one policy in one environment family. Nobody has floored SWE-bench, where loop engineering's counterexamples and much of the harness literature live; one paired re-run would tell us whether an entire contested map is arguing over signal or variance. Widened 2026-07-31: that re-run now has to control the implementation draw too, not only the data draw and the seed — each arm of an architecture comparison is one particular implementation of the mechanism it names, and the reversal rate (25.6–43.6%) is the same order as the effects being argued over. The decisive experiment did not get harder to design; it got roughly three times more expensive to run.
  • Does the implementation lottery hold outside tabular ML? Measured with model-generated idea cards and model-assigned fidelity labels on OpenML classification, where the authors argue richer deep-learning and agentic workflows should show more implementation variance because they admit more choices. That is a prediction, not a measurement, and it is the single cheapest thing to check for anyone who compares agent architectures for a living.
  • How much of the rest of the benchmark corpus is misaligned, and does anybody's ranking survive it? The 13.6% figure is one hand-audit of one curated suite; the same method reports 44.3% on an uncurated one. Nobody has audited the benchmarks the harness and loop literature actually run on, and the correction moved 9 of 10 top positions where it was tried. Widened 2026-08-10: a second hand-audit on a different benchmark found score-suppressing defects on 58 of 64 problems worth 53–70 points per model, and 78% of them needed specialist domain knowledge to see. So the open question is no longer only "how much is misaligned" but "who is qualified to check" — which is a much worse constraint than cost, because it does not fall with better tooling.
  • How much of the pessimistic ledger is there? For a year this article assumed the bias was optimistic and the remedies were built accordingly — block the retrieval channel, harden the judge, discount the vendor. Two 2026-08 results (a 53–70 point benchmark repair, a 3.4–5.8 point environmental tax) say the instrument fails hard in the other direction too, and nobody has estimated the ratio. It matters operationally: an optimistic bias means you ship something worse than you measured, a pessimistic one means you discard an approach that worked.
  • Is a mislabelled reward already in the models? The 2.9% accepted-verdict error rate is measured on a corpus whose verdicts ground execution-based RL reward signals. Nobody has traced whether that error survives into a trained policy, or what it teaches — which is the one question on this list whose answer cannot be recovered by fixing the benchmark afterwards.
  • How many other gates are blind rather than miscalibrated? Deletion is one defect class a behavioural oracle cannot state. Nobody has enumerated the others — dead configuration, a removed feature flag, an unreachable branch, a dependency that should have been dropped — nor asked which of this vault's executable contracts share the blind spot. The check is cheap and the enumeration does not exist.
  • What is an automated benchmark auditor's sensitivity, really? Estimating it needs ~100 verified positives — about 1,000 manually screened transcripts at realistic prevalence — and the one study that tried could not reach it, so every reported scanner sensitivity currently carries an interval wide enough to include "barely works." Until that is closed, a clean scan is not evidence of a clean benchmark, and nobody can say by how much it lowers the base rate.
  • Does construction-time contamination resistance buy anything measurable? New 2026-07-26 and cheap to answer: build one task set from private artifacts and one from public issue text, and compare. Nobody has.
  • The 8.6-point harness term is from two harnesses on one benchmark. Whether the magnitude generalizes, and whether some harness/model pairs are systematically favoured rather than noisily shuffled, is open — and it decides whether harness cards are sufficient or whether cross-harness replication is mandatory.
  • Can a self-improving system be bounded by its own selector, and is anyone measuring it? TTHE's 20-point gap is visible only because the authors ran post-hoc oracles with gold as a diagnostic. Whether it widens with more generations, and whether any label-free in-flight detector exists, is open.
  • The coding-domain contamination decomposition is still one org's self-audit. Cross-domain replication confirms direction, not the numbers; the narrowed ask is a second org repeating the constrained-harness experiment on an agentic-coding benchmark.
  • What is the constrained score an estimate of? A strict harness may under-credit legitimate documentation lookup — real engineers read the docs. The correct baseline for "derived the fix" is undefined.
  • No cross-proxy study exists. The proxies misgrade; nobody has measured whether they misgrade the same agents in the same direction. Sharpened 2026-07-19: proxies sharing an upstream corpus share its construction faults, so agreement can be inheritance rather than corroboration.
  • How much of the agentic-SE evaluation literature sits in the oracle-less regime, and nobody has counted. A disclosure audit exists for the harness (0.38/1.0, zero of eight reporting cost); no equivalent audit exists for corpus construction, and it would be cheap to run.
  • Is the vault's own aggregation rule under-specified here? Highest-tier-wins with replication gating the top has no term for item-level corpus integrity — a well-replicated T1 built on an unchecked synthetic corpus currently outranks a careful single-study one built on a perturbation corpus. Whether oracle-less sources deserve a standing discount is open, and deliberately not applied yet.
  • Does the urgency/evidence divergence replicate? New 2026-08-17. One catalogue found −0.004 between how much a practice matters and whether it has strong support, with both rankings by the same person. The test is cheap and nobody has run it: have one group rank consequence and another grade evidence, on any corpus. If it replicates, every graded vault including this one is ordering its advice by the wrong key.
  • Does defect injection validate a judge on code review, or only on design artifacts? New 2026-08-17. The mutation result is PlantUML class diagrams with 11 hand-written operators; code review has more mechanical criteria and might either transfer cleanly or lose the property that a change reliably degrades the artifact. Nobody has run it on a diff.
  • How unreliable is the human reference on code review? New 2026-08-17. α = 0.32 is measured on design critique. If the equivalent number for review comments is similar, every judge-versus-human validation in this article is comparing against a coin with a bias.
  • If the discipline is unaffordable, what is the minimum viable version? Practitioners report trustworthy evaluation is priced out and the seed arithmetic confirms the price. Nobody has published a defensible cheap protocol — which is the gap that actually determines whether any of this gets done.
  • Does the cluster-correction unit generalize past SWE-bench Verified? New 2026-08-29. Tacet computes the right unit for one benchmark's repository structure; every other leaderboard this article has cited has its own nesting (issue thread, model family, task template) and nobody has run the equivalent computation on any of them.
  • How much of this vault's own repair evidence rests on a reproduced-but-unverified failure? New 2026-08-29. The 67.97%/41.42% reproduction rate is measured on one multi-agent collection; every prior repair-loop finding in loop-engineering and this map assumed the failure it fixed actually recurred, and none of them checked.
