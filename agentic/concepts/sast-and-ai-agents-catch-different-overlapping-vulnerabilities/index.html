Deterministic static analysis and AI security-review agents are complementary, not substitutable — and a harness's own built-in scope policy, not the underlying model, is what gates an AI reviewer's coverage

This is a blind, two-line-localization benchmark result at a scale (108 curated vulnerabilities, 20 apps, four languages, nine productized configurations) that gives this map's verifier-efficacy sub-thread a concrete coverage comparison between deterministic and agentic review for the first time. The headline numbers (82.4% vs. 79.3% recall, disagreeing on 28 findings, a pooled ceiling of 101/108) argue against treating either approach as a replacement for the other — each catches real vulnerabilities the other systematically misses, and the authors frame coverage and cost as only loosely related rather than tracking together.

The sharper, more actionable finding for this map is the harness-policy result: the same underlying model, run with its product harness's built-in review-scope restrictions removed, nearly doubled its coverage of the specific vulnerability classes that policy excludes or restricts (16/20 vs. 9/20 apps). This is a clean, controlled instance of the harness-as-score-confound pattern — model capability and harness policy are separable variables, and the harness's own configuration can be the dominant term — now demonstrated specifically inside a security-code-review task rather than general benchmark scoring.

Interaction edge: harness-as-score-confound — a same-model, harness-policy-toggle comparison showing an 80% relative coverage swing from a harness setting alone is exactly the kind of apparatus-vs-object confound that map's core claim tracks, now inside a code-review-specific task.

Bounds: 108 curated vulnerabilities across 20 apps is a bounded reference set — even pooling every tested configuration reached only 101/108, suggesting some ceiling may reflect the benchmark's own construction rather than tool capability limits; the nine evaluated configurations are specific products at a specific point in time (Opus 5, GPT-5.6 variants) that will drift as vendors ship updates; the "at least 7" unreported-real-findings figure is a stated lower bound without full enumeration in the abstract.
