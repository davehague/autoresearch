Localize the first wrong step, not the final answer

Outcome-only evaluation of an agent run tells you whether it succeeded, not where it went wrong or whether the success was earned. Two agents can reach the same answer with very different trustworthiness, and a trajectory that ends correct may have been carried by a lucky recovery over an earlier broken step. Wang et al. build TELBench — 1,000 instances, 2,790 human-annotated trajectories marking harmful error spans across search failures and unsupported claims — to make the first wrong step (the one that made the final answer unreliable) the unit of evaluation. Their DRIFT framework raises error-localization accuracy by up to 30 percentage points over baselines.

For observability this is the load-bearing move: instrument and grade the trajectory to find the fault's origin, not just its downstream symptom. It's the diagnostic counterpart to the harness practice of an external evaluator (separate-the-generator-from-the-evaluator) — both reject the final self-reported answer as sufficient evidence. Domain is deep-research (search-and-synthesize) agents, so the 30-point figure is that setting's; the principle generalizes. See audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence for how DRIFT localizes.
