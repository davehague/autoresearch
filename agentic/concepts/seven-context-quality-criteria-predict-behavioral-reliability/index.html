A different instrument for a question this map has mostly asked through decay and absence: is context quality itself a scoreable, predictive property?

This map's live instrument dispute is about presence — how much of what looks like positional decay is actually plain absence, unresolved since the 2026-08-18 downgrade. This source does not touch that dispute; it manipulates something orthogonal — not whether a fact is present, but how well-formed the context around present facts is (role clarity, schema quality, guardrail coverage, grounding, hardening) — and asks whether that is itself predictive of behavioral reliability. It is a genuinely different lens on "what the agent holds" (this map's sub-thread 1 object), scoring the quality of delivery rather than tracking survival or eviction of specific facts over a session.

The correlations (r = 0.40–0.63) are moderate and consensus-scored rather than derived from a clean causal manipulation of each criterion independently — weaker evidentiary standing than this map's strongest arrivals, which tend to isolate one variable with fixed weights and report exact effect sizes. The one genuinely controlled piece is the C1→C2→C3 ladder, and its finding is worth carrying forward on its own: structure buys most of the safety win, hardening buys the rest at a cost to other metrics. That is a specific, actionable claim this map did not previously have in this form — it says do the cheap, structural context-engineering work (typed schemas, clear roles, grounding) before reaching for hardening measures, because the former is where most of the critical-failure reduction lives, and the latter is not uniformly free.

Interaction note, not a tension: runtime-action-governance holds that a bolted-on agent-level guardrail framework layer contributes "approximately nothing" to refusal behavior — refusals come from the model's own reasoning (framework-guardrails-add-little-over-model-refusal). This source's "guardrail coverage" is a different construct: explicit refusal conditions and escalation thresholds written into the context the model reads, not a separate enforcement layer outside the model. Read together, the two are compatible rather than contradictory — both locate the effective control surface inside what the model itself processes, one by ruling out an external layer, the other by showing that ruling in richer in-context guardrail material correlates with the outcome that layer failed to buy.

Bounds

Security boundaries remain text-level — trusted and untrusted tokens share one inference mechanism, so injection hardening reduces rather than eliminates influence from malicious content. Multi-agent cascading error and compression/summarization risk are named as concerns but not evaluated in this design. The context-quality score is explicitly scoped by the authors as a preflight signal, not a substitute for adversarial evaluation, red-teaming, trace auditing or human review — so this result extends the map's practice-facing half (what to build into context) without bearing on the live positional-decay-vs-absence dispute at all.
