The famous case is the answer key the model already has

This map keeps running into the same problem from different directions: a score that is high because the model solved the task is indistinguishable, at the aggregate, from a score that is high because the model has seen the task. benchmark-scores-inflate-when-agents-can-retrieve-the-fix catches it at the retrieval layer; the-capability-ranking-survives-the-rewrite-that-degrades-the-agent catches it by rewriting the surface and watching absolute scores collapse while the ranking holds.

The evidence-reversed mirror is a third construction and it is cheaper than either. Hold the surface constant — a benefits notice that looks like Robodebt income-averaging, an applicant decision that looks like Workday Mobley, a production change that looks like a known outage — and invert only the verification state: signed cross-service authorization, eligibility met, audit trail present. The label flips from hold to proceed. Nothing else moves.

98.5% on the anchors, 63.8% on the mirrors. The worked pair is worth carrying because it is unambiguous: the anchor (12,400 debt notices, legal review returning "unlawful under current precedent," a three-year-old sign-off memo) is answered 30/30, while its mirror — one recipient, AUD 2,840, a confirmed dual-income period, and three independent services signing statutory basis, eligibility and due process — is wrongly held by 24 of 30 conditions.

What makes this a better instrument than a paraphrase attack is that the two members of the pair have different correct answers. A semantics-preserving rewrite tests whether the score survives a surface change; a mirror tests whether the model is reading the evidence at all, because a model that answers from the resemblance gets the anchor right and the mirror wrong by the same mechanism. The pair is self-controlling.

Two disciplines the authors apply to their own headline, and both should travel with the construction. First, they state that the gap does not by itself identify the cause and report that under an unadjudicated three-rater relabelling it narrows to about 4 points — the mirror labels are the ones humans disagree about, which is itself informative and is not smoothed away. Second, they disclose an instrumentation defect against themselves: in 17 of 106 scenarios a fixture adapter inferred a risk flag by keyword-matching a risk-term list, and in two mirrors the terms eval and score matched inside signed audit-tool references — so the instrument was partly flagging the evidence that resolves the risk. Excluding all 17 as a diagnostic slice, mirrors clean to 74.8%. That is the discipline benchmark-defects-suppress-scores-not-only-inflate-them asks for, applied to a benchmark's own metadata pipeline rather than to its task set.

The construction generalises past this domain. Any benchmark anchored on documented incidents can build one, and any that does not is reporting a number whose contamination-resistant component is unmeasured — including, on the authors' own account, this one after publication: the released set begins a contamination clock the moment it goes public, with graded rankings moving to fresh sealed versions as contamination accrues.
