"Use a different vendor's model as the reviewer" is not a measurement

The familiar argument for a second opinion is a product of errors: if checker i falsely accepts a wrong candidate with probability f_i and checker j with f_j, an ensemble requiring both accepts a wrong candidate at f_i·f_j. That identity holds only when the excess joint false-acceptance term D_ij is zero, and the paper's contribution is to measure D_ij directly — conditioned on incorrect candidates only, so shared prevalence cannot masquerade as shared error.

Two results follow, and they point in opposite directions from the usual advice.

Cross-family diversity did not buy quality. At matched 70% coverage the cross-family verifier carries 0.240 risk against 0.041 and 0.050 for the two same-family ones — a 19.9-point deficit that holds from 60% through 90% coverage with every bootstrap interval below zero. Whatever independence it supplies, it supplies it while being roughly five times more likely to accept a wrong patch. Low dependence is not useful when either marginal risk is poor.

Dependence is an operating point, not a label. The same-family pair sits at φ = 0.641 among incorrect candidates, with excess joint false acceptance of 0.060 — an additive reliability penalty on top of two individually strong marginals. The cross-family pair looks clean at φ = 0.021 at 70% coverage, and then all three pairs have positive excess-joint intervals at 75%. Loosen the acceptance threshold and you change which errors are accepted, which changes whether the checkers agree on them. So "are these two reviewers independent?" is not answerable about a pair of models. It is answerable about a pair of models at a coverage.

This extends rather than reverses the map's independence thread, which has been converging on the same place from three other directions in nine days. an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself found a cheap cross-model re-derivation recovering 79% of injected errors against self-checking's 71%. reviewer-value-tracks-capability-headroom then found independence not to be the active ingredient — the most independent condition available was the only significantly harmful cell — and split the rule by whether an external anchor exists to re-derive from. a-verifier-sharing-the-interpretation-shares-the-error named the residual as common-mode failure: shared interpretation of the source, not shared narration. And cogenerated-tests-and-fixes-fail-together measured the same coupling as a factor of 1.87 when the test and the fix come from one trajectory. This adds the missing quantity — a number for the coupling, with a confidence interval, computed the right way — and the composite claim becomes: independence is a measurable, operating-point-conditional property of an error distribution, and the practical test is φ among incorrect candidates at your intended coverage, not a model-family diff.

The stopping replay supplies the accompanying design rule, and it is blunt: the executable gate is doing the work. Self-report-only accepts 99.8% of revisions at 29.5% risk. Adding a visible test gate prevents 248 false completions and introduces none. A model verifier alone (Qwen-14B) reaches 4.6% risk; the same verifier plus the challenge suite reaches zero observed. Independence bought at the model layer is a fraction of what independence bought at the execution layer costs (verifier-catch-and-false-alarm-rates-are-measurable, gate-agent-commits-on-the-same-ci-as-humans). And a gate without independent correctness evidence propagates its own errors into the artifact it certifies — 7 of 237 incorrect proposal commits false-passed, all three incorrect explicit submissions false-passed, and three of four terminal checkpoint certifications were wrong.

One number to keep for evaluation-integrity: the paper refuses to read its own zeros. The conservative one-sided 95% bound on the zero-error challenge gate, computed over 30 task clusters, is 9.5% — treating the 1,877 challenge-passing revisions as independent would have given 0.16% and been anti-conservative for cross-task generalization (scoreable-runs-are-a-separate-denominator).

Caveats: verifier family and capacity are confounded — DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B is both the cross-family arm and the smallest, so "cross-family is worse" is not identified; matched-coverage risks are retrospective over a frozen revision distribution, so deployment needs held-out calibration and drift monitoring; collapsing 2,700 revisions to 617 unique task-code hashes raises all three risks (0.400/0.387/0.472) while preserving the ordering, so the 19.9-point figure is a revision-distribution estimate, not an intrinsic model constant. Function-level Python repair, one scaffold. Carried as suggestive.
