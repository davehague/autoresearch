The gain is not distributed, and a negative cell is a diagnosis rather than a failure

The headline held-out number here is nearly nothing — +0.55 on the equal-family macro average — and reporting it as the result would be the wrong reading in both directions. Underneath it, two families move +12.21 and +9.21, and two move −2.78 and −3.70. The aggregate is a mixture, and the interesting object is what sorts a family into which bucket.

The authors' answer is mechanism match, not task diversity. Budget Approval and Machine Operating both require the workflow to preserve explicit constraints, intermediate state and completion conditions across standardized tools, which is the thing the runbook's checked transitions are built to enforce; their held-out macro moves 71.91% → 81.94%. RefactorBench needs code-contract and stale-reference closure. WebArena needs query and page-state control and is dominated by retrieval, so a mutation-oriented workflow is dead weight. WooCommerce needs cross-system invariants over inventory entities, destination-specific deduplication, irreversible email actions and receipts.

Where it went negative, the repair was subtraction. RefactorBench's first profile over-emphasized minimality and backward compatibility and failed to close explicit code-migration obligations; a later, thinner profile that tracks visible obligations, verifies required signature and call-shape changes and performs one repository-wide stale-reference closure moves the matched rerun 76.39% → 79.17%. WooCommerce's first profile contained substantial procedure and simply missed the invariants that connect its systems; replacing generic control with those invariants moves 86.42% → 90.12% and 85.37% → 90.24% on the reused held-out split. The authors state the rule directly: a strong profile is not a maximal universal workflow — it binds the minimum invariants likely to drift and verifies them where a violation becomes consequential. That is the vault's standing finding that the most valuable harness edits are subtractions (strip-the-prompt-when-the-model-improves, restrict-the-tool-surface-to-cut-cost-not-capability) arriving as a transfer result rather than a design one, and it puts a second mechanism under a-skill-set-helps-the-weaker-harness-and-hurts-the-stronger: displacement is one way added structure hurts, and mis-aimed structure is another.

Abstention is the seventh answer. The protocol declined to apply any profile to attendance-payroll, and all 72 treatment-arm runs in that family are excluded from the efficacy aggregates rather than reported as a null. Read beside price-the-checkers-abstention-as-lost-coverage, which established that a checker allowed to say no verdict is a product feature rather than a gap, this says the same for an optimizer: the correct output of a harness-evolution loop on some task families is do not install a control layer here. Almost nothing on this map reports that option being exercised.

Bounds, and they matter for how far this travels. Family sizes are unequal and each instance carries one stochastic trajectory per arm, so the family-level deltas carry no intervals. Only the first frozen held-out evaluation is untouched — every recovery figure above is explicitly labelled post-evaluation diagnostic validation, which means the "thinner profile fixes it" claim is a diagnosis supported by matched reruns using aggregate evaluation feedback, not a second clean held-out test. On RefactorBench the reused held-out split does not move at all (77.78% before and after), so the recovery is visible on development and overall and not on the held-out partition. WebArena's frozen evaluation contains three non-tied A/B outcomes across 25 instances and WebTest is saturated, so two of the seven families carry essentially no information. And this arrival is the sixth consecutive one on this map with no compute-matched control.
