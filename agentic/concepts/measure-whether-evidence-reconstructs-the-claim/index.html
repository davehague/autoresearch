Measure whether the evidence reconstructs the claim

Two agent evaluations can report the same surface number — task success, attack blocked, monitor score — while one rests on a traceable decision and the other on nothing you can inspect. This paper turns that into a graded property: reconstructability, asking per claimed decision whether the captured evidence is sufficient to reconstruct the decision the claim depends on. It ships Evidence Sufficiency Cards, a counterfactual-replay protocol with an explicit replayability precondition, and a claim–evidence overclaim gap. The empirical kicker on real public traces: the replay precondition — can you even re-run the decision point? — was unmet in every scored trace, even where the headline number looked fine.

This extends evaluation-integrity's founding thesis (the number describes the eval as much as the agent) with an instrument on the evidence side. The map already holds that the trustworthy signal is the trajectory, not the endpoint (audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence), and that an undisclosed harness makes a score unfalsifiable (agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness). Reconstructability is the next construct beyond disclosure: not "was the harness reported" but "does the trace actually license the decision the score claims." It is observability's first-wrong-step localization seen from the evaluative side — both ask whether a run's own evidence supports the conclusion drawn from it — and a replayable decision point is exactly what a contained, instrumented harness produces.

Carried as a new limb, not a status move: the construct and its direction (surface numbers routinely outrun their evidence) fit the map, but the magnitudes are demonstration-grade and the domain is agent-safety evals, adjacent to the coding-benchmark evidence the map is built on. The vault holds the instrument and the direction, not the specific sufficiency scores.

Caveats: single author, no venue, arXiv v1; n=4 public traces plus one synthetic pair; the eight property classes and sufficiency scoring are the author's own schema, unvalidated by a second rater. "Replay unmet in every trace" rests on artifacts never built for replay — a selection effect, not a census. Single-study strength; the numbers illustrate, the construct is the contribution.
