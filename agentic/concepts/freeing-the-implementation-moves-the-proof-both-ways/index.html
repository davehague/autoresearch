Hold the contract, free the code, and the agent optimises for the checker's convenience

This map's organising claim is that the contract lives outside the agent, and its accumulated evidence is almost entirely about acquiring a good external contract — elicit it in its own phase, converge it against the source, freeze it (converge-the-spec-against-the-code-before-freezing-it, elicit-the-spec-in-its-own-phase-before-synthesis). The obvious next question is what the agent does underneath a contract it cannot change, and there has been no clean measurement of it, because in every prior source the specification and the implementation moved together.

Vero separates them by construction. Same 43 repositories, same 2,705 specifications, two modes: prove the reference implementation, or write your own and prove that. The aggregate barely moves (27/43 against 25/43 for the strongest configuration; 87.3% against 85.8% of individual specifications), and the aggregate is hiding two large opposite effects. Five instance-agent pairs across three repositories close all 250 of their specifications when free to write the code, against 201 when handed the reference — the agent picked a simpler algorithm satisfying the same contract and dramatically easier to reason about. Seventeen pairs go the other way, full solves in proof-only that fail once they must also produce the implementation.

The first half is the interesting one, and it is a mirror of an established result read from the other side. benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives complains that grading against a single reference solution is a grader false-negative — an equally valid alternative is marked wrong. Vero grades against a contract instead, so alternatives are admissible, and the immediate consequence is that the agent starts choosing them for reasons that have nothing to do with the user: it picks whatever is cheapest to verify. That is not misbehaviour under a contract that captures what matters. It is enforce-invariants-not-implementations working exactly as advertised — and it is also a warning, because "satisfies the specification and is easiest to prove" and "is the implementation you wanted" come apart precisely on the axes a formal specification tends not to cover: performance, memory, idiom, the shape a maintainer expects. This vault has the corresponding measurement in the non-formal setting (resolve-rate-gains-do-not-carry-quality-gains) and the corresponding failure mode (agents-build-to-the-oracle-you-expose). A contract is an oracle; the substitution behaviour is the same behaviour, in its legitimate form.

Two further observations transfer beyond the formal setting. Difficulty attaches to the shape of the obligation, not to the domain: existence-and-coverage specifications — "there is some x such that…, and every case is handled" — fail at 47.1%, the worst band; obligations built on supplied helper definitions fail 14.9 pp more; obligations that call one API repeatedly fail 11.7 pp more. If you are writing acceptance criteria for an agent, those are three known-hard grammatical forms, and two of them (existential, repeated call) are trivially rephrasable. And the residual is shared, not random: on verdict all eight configurations failed the same seven specifications; on piggybank every configuration passed 17 of 23 and failed the same six, split by whether the obligation quantified over a state or over a trace. Instances have walls. That is what makes specification shape a design variable rather than noise, and it is the same lesson as failures-concentrate-before-business-logic in a different register.

Bounds. One run per instance-configuration cell with no variance estimate, so a 27-vs-25 difference in repository solves is not itself a result — the mode comparison rests on the 5-vs-17 instance-level split, not on the totals. Lean 4 only, corpus biased toward code that ports cleanly, concurrent and temporal protocols absent. Three of the four configurations are near the floor and contribute little to the contrast. And "the agent substituted a simpler algorithm" is the paper's reading of five pairs; no independent assessment of whether the substituted implementations were acceptable code is reported, which is exactly the axis a proof cannot see.
