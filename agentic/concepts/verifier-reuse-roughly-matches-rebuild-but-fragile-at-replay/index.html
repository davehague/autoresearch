A verifier-quality metric can say reuse is free while a stricter check on the same data says it isn't -- the gap between the two readings is the finding

This map's standing argument is that independence is a property of the arrangement around a checker (what it is denied, who authors what it checks, where its expected value comes from, what it is permitted to do) rather than of the checker's identity. AgentProofBench adds a case this map's provenance vocabulary has not separately priced: the checker's own lifecycle as task requirements change. When a verifier program written for one task state is reused rather than rebuilt after the state changes, the headline balanced-accuracy metric reports no reliable gap (both 95% CIs span zero) -- but a stricter program-level check (is the program itself fully correct on the changed state) and a replay-level check (does the program correctly re-judge an earlier, now-invalid decision) both show reuse measurably underperforming rebuild, most sharply on the stronger model (DeepSeek Pro: 79/96 vs 86/96 joint-correct).

That divergence between the average-case metric and the stricter check is itself a provenance-style lesson in this map's register: a verifier's balanced accuracy looking stable under reuse does not mean the verifier's underlying logic is stable under reuse -- the metric that would catch the difference has to be stricter than the one that gets reported as the headline number. This sits adjacent to, rather than squarely inside, this map's core independence argument (nothing here is about who authors the checker or whether it shares the generator's substrate) -- filed here because no other map in this vault yet carries verifier-reuse-reliability findings, and this is the closest existing home for "can a checker be trusted as its job changes."

Bounds: single model family (DeepSeek Flash/Pro only), no cross-provider replication; 97 tasks / 79 families is a modest benchmark scale; "balanced accuracy" and the panel/witness-replay machinery are this paper's own constructs, not independently used elsewhere in this vault's evidence base; no ablations beyond the reuse-vs-rebuild and panel-error-vs-clean splits. Graded T2 rather than T1: a focused academic benchmark on one model family without the broader evaluation apparatus (multi-provider, held-out validation) this map's T1 sources typically carry.
