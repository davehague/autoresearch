A production network-ops system reports >90% autonomous incident resolution and zero critical incidents, gated by a progressive-autonomy promotion ladder — but discloses none of the raw numbers behind any of its headline figures

unattended-operation's standing claim is that externalized, durable run state — not the process — is the unit that survives restarts, now attested across several orgs and layers (Anthropic's replayable event log, Symphony's daemon, Cursor's Temporal-backed loop at 90%->99% reliability on 50M+ daily actions). This source adds a structurally different attestation: a promotion ladder gating how much autonomy an agent earns, rather than a mechanism for surviving a crash. Agents move between autonomy tiers only after sustained performance above undisclosed thresholds on five metrics — success rate, mean time to resolution, false-positive rate, rollback frequency, human override rate — evaluated over an undisclosed minimum period, with a dedicated verification agent running post-action health checks and "bake-in" monitoring before an incident is considered closed. That is a concrete, if thinly specified, instance of "the orchestrator is the first responder" that adds a qualification mechanism this map's existing attestations don't name explicitly.

Taken at face value, the headline numbers are large: resolution rates above 90% for supported incident categories, resolution time cut by roughly two orders of magnitude, a sub-5% false-positive rate with no attributed customer impact, and zero critical incidents from autonomous action over a multi-month production deployment "serving millions of customers."

Why this lands as single-study, not as consolidating evidence: none of those headline numbers come with an incident count, a denominator, a confidence interval, a comparison-condition number, a named company, or a code/data release. The paper states what its five promotion metrics are but not their threshold values or evaluation window length, and does not describe how "correct resolution" was verified per incident or how the human-driven baseline itself was measured. This reads as an unverified industry self-report dressed in a formally-formatted arXiv preprint rather than a reproducible evaluation — every figure above should be treated as a claim pending independent disclosure or replication, not as a measured effect this map can weight heavily.

Interaction edge: runtime-action-governance — "progressive autonomy with safety boundaries" and the promotion ladder's undisclosed thresholds are a governance-layer design this map's authorization/blast-radius thread would want to interrogate directly: the paper claims no action exceeded its "predicted blast radius," but gives no definition of how blast radius was predicted or bounded.

Bounds the author states: coverage is limited to a subset of incident types, long-tail/novel failures still need humans; incidents spanning network and compute domains require coordination across separate autonomous systems, called "an area of active development"; accountability/ auditability for autonomous action in critical infrastructure is explicitly not fully addressed by the paper's technical mechanisms; LLM output stochasticity is mitigated (schema enforcement, multi-model consensus, deterministic checks) but not claimed to be resolved; generalization beyond network operations is asserted, not evidenced.
