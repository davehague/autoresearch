The agent did not need to be told what went wrong; it needed to be told what else it could call

Most feedback design in this map argues about the content of the diagnosis — how specific the error, how localized the witness, whether to name the failing assertion. This is the first study that varied those knobs one at a time against the same paired workflows with the interpretations registered first, and the ranking is not the one the design literature assumes.

The recovery-tool list is the whole detectable effect. A receipt stripped of it sits at baseline (+0.9 pp, not clearing zero). Restore the list and +11.4 points come back. Meanwhile the localized expected-versus-observed witness beats a generic caution by −2.6 points — that is, not at all — and delivering the message at the moment of failure rather than deferring it is worth +0.9. Both nulls are bounded rather than asserted (≈18-point minimum detectable effect), which is what report-a-null-as-a-bounded-interval-not-a-failed-test asks for, and the authors flag their own always-warn arm as confounded because its envelope leaked affordances at every step — which is why the stripped-envelope rerun exists.

The behavioural trace says why. After a receipt the agent switches to a listed substitute 48.6% of the time and calls some other tool 33.5% — it is not re-reasoning about the failure, it is re-selecting from an enumerated set. And 443 of 484 events leave at least two admissible recovery actions, so the interface is not collapsing the choice to one forced move. Compare recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder: what an agent does after a failure is governed by what it can see to do, and the expensive part of a loop is not the recognition.

There is a second, quieter result inside the ablation that changes how you would buy a detector. A schema-only detector recovers most of the completion gain (+10.0 vs +15.0, head-to-head difference +5.0, p = .42) — so on the headline number the cheap detector is nearly as good. On the two numbers that govern what it costs to run, it is not: it fires 166 receipts to the learned detector's 111, and only 12.0% of its receipts can name more than one recovery action against 91.9%. Precision and affordance quality are what the mined contracts buy, and neither shows up in the completion delta. That is the same pattern as skill-use-precision-collapses-while-success-does-not — a component's real operating cost hiding behind a matched outcome score.

The scope condition is stated by the authors and is a deployment rule, not a caveat: gains concentrate where the fault blocks completion. When faulted-baseline completion is at most 16%, net gains run +15 to +28 points; where most runs complete despite the fault, net effects are +6.3 points or less with rescues offset by harms. Every positive study here uses a fault-enriched sample, so the net benefit is bounded by fault prevalence π — at the unfavourable interval endpoints a positive effect needs π ≳ 0.32. Audit your clean receipt rate and your actual fault rate before assuming the number transfers.
