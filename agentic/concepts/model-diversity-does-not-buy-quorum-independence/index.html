"Use three different models to vote" fails exactly the way "use a different vendor's model as reviewer" already failed — now with a proof

verifier-diversity-is-not-verifier-independence measured this on a two-checker code-review pair: cross-family model diversity bought no quality and no reliable independence, because dependence is a property of the error distribution at an operating point, not of which model authors sit at the table. This paper generalizes the same result to N-of-M authorization quorums, gives it a name — Epistemic Fault Domains, a shared upstream dependency (telemetry feed, document store, tool backend) that several nominally-independent reviewers all draw on — and a metric, the Structural Epistemic Cut (κ_E): the minimum number of modeled shared root faults whose exposure covers an authorizing coalition. Two negative theorems follow directly from the definition: arbitrarily large quorums can retain κ_E = 1, and adding voters at a fixed threshold cannot increase the cut once they share compatible exposure — voting more copies of the same exposed dependency buys nothing, no matter how the copies are labeled.

The number that makes this concrete: model diversity alone moves the unsafe-commit rate from 97.3% to 96.8% (Q1 → Q2, same shared evidence) — statistically indistinguishable, matching verifier-diversity-is-not-verifier-independence's finding that cross-family diversity bought no quality. Separating the evidence path each reviewer sees — regardless of whether the models also differ — is what moves the number, to 6.72% under majority vote (14.5x) and to 0.01%–0.11% under unanimity (~670x). This is the arrangement-not-identity thesis verifier-independence has been converging on from code review, evaluation, and now formalized and demonstrated at the authorization layer: independence is bought by what a checker is exposed to, never by swapping which model or how many sit on the panel.

For runtime-action-governance: this is the formal argument for why an authorization gate built as "N agents must agree" is not a safety property unless the N agents are also given independently sourced evidence — a quorum count alone is exactly the kind of nominal-diversity assurance the map's containment and classifier work has been substituting for.

Bounds

The authors are explicit that κ_E is a structural, basis-relative guarantee, not a calibrated probability: "κ_E(Q) ≥ k does not imply P(action is safe) ≥ 1 − ε." It degrades if dependency lineage metadata is incomplete or fabricated, and the 120-task benchmark uses stylized Bernoulli reviewers in a controlled simulation, not real LLM reviewer agents — latent pretraining-level dependencies between two different models (trained on overlapping corpora) sit entirely outside the formal model. This is a theoretical/simulated generalization of an empirically-measured phenomenon, not itself a real-LLM replication.
