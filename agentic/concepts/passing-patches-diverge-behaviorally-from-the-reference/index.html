Resolved means the tests went green, and on more than half of them the patch is doing something else

resolve-rate-gains-do-not-carry-quality-gains held the instances fixed across two model generations and found the later model's passing patches no better on any static-analysis or maintainability measure, and slightly worse on resource use. That is a null on quality. This is the sharper adjacent claim, from the group that maintains the benchmark: on the instances a model resolves, the accepted patch behaves differently from the reference solution more than half the time, and the rate is rising with model release date.

Two measurements, worth separating because their evidential weight differs enormously.

Surplus is measured mechanically and is solid. Diff size against the gold patch, cleaned of added files, non-Python files and test changes; the bloated fraction is the share of resolved instances above a 1.5× ratio. It is high, it does not correlate with solve rate — so it is not the price of capability — and it trends up with release date. Categorised, the surplus is mostly verbose implementation (~60%) and scope creep (50–65%), with defensive code and documentation behind. This is codeslop-is-the-search-surviving-into-the-patch measured on the resolved set rather than the trajectory, and it composes with patch-minimization-is-search-not-generation: the surplus is not incidental, it is the exploration surviving into the deliverable, and newer models explore more.

Divergence is the headline and it is annotated by language models. Two annotators (Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5 mini) read (problem statement, submitted patch, gold patch) and judged whether the submitted patch behaves as the reference does. Individually: 66% and 62% flagged. Conjunction — the figure the paper reports — 57%, and above 50% for every model evaluated. Roughly a third of the divergences are standard-behaviour differences, a third edge-case, ~20% missing functionality, >10% unrelated change.

The right reading is not "the benchmark is broken." Two competing explanations sit under that 57%, and the paper cannot separate them. One is the grader false-negative that benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives names: an equally valid alternative implementation should diverge from the reference, and marking that as a defect is the mistake. The other is genuine under-specification — the test suite admits behaviours the issue never sanctioned, which is fail-to-pass-does-not-constrain-the-fix and visible-tests-perturb-more-than-they-specify arriving on the resolved set. The categories tilt toward the second (missing functionality and unrelated changes are not "valid alternatives"), and the trend with release date tilts further: a model that solves more and diverges more is not finding more legitimate alternatives, it is finding more room. But the study does not adjudicate it, and anyone citing 57% should say which of the two they mean.

And there is an irony in the instrument that this vault is obliged to record. The paper's thesis is that humans are missing from coding-agent research. Its strongest empirical claim was annotated entirely by two language models, with no human validation set, no agreement measured against human labels, and no estimate of the annotators' own error rate. The conjunction rule is sensible conservatism and it is not validation — validate-the-judge-before-you-trust-the-migration and llm-judges-have-exploitable-biases both say why, and manually-read-raw-generations-before-trusting-bias-stats says what the missing step was. Two models agreeing narrows independent error, not shared bias, and the two annotators here were both asked to compare against a reference — the exact framing that would make a valid alternative look like a defect. The authors half-concede the general point (human evaluation is "prohibitively out of reach" without open interaction data), which makes the appendix a demonstration of its own thesis rather than an oversight, and it is why this note is graded a suggestive T3 rather than the T1 the sample size would otherwise buy.

Bounds. A position paper's appendix, not a study: no method detail on annotation prompts, no inter-annotator statistics against humans, no per-model table reproduced here because the paper reports trends and ranges rather than a full grid. The 1.5× bloat threshold is arbitrary and diff size is a coarse stand-in for surplus. mini-swe-agent only, SWE-bench Verified only, Python only — and the population is the resolved set, so nothing here says anything about the unresolved tail, where benchmark-misalignment-concentrates-in-the-unresolved-tail says the interesting failures live.
