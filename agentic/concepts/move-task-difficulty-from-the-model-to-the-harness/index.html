Move task difficulty from the model to the harness

The reflex when an agent fails is to reach for a bigger model. This paper argues the failure is often in the harness: a failure-mode → adaptation-strategy map plus an optimizer that reads actual failure trajectories discovers the custom tools, instructions, and orchestration-loop changes that recover a small model's latent capability. Across 21 task-SLM pairs it improved 16 and closed the gap to a frontier LLM on 7, with a best case recovering 89.7% of the LLM's performance at 4% of the cost — the paper's headline framing is small-model agents matching frontier performance at ~90% lower cost.

This extends model-routing-token-economics by changing what routing is over. That map treats the routing unit as the next increment of effort (which model, how many iterations) under a budget; this says the cheap tier isn't a fixed menu item — you can re-engineer its harness until it clears the bar, and then the routing decision changes. It converts the ~1000× cost dispersion among comparable-quality options (agent-cost-spans-orders-of-magnitude) from a selection problem into an engineering target: instead of picking the cheapest model that happens to pass, make a cheap model pass. It is also agent-harness-architecture's structure-over-prompts and observability-driven-evolution claims in one — the optimizer is a falsifiable control loop reading trajectories, the sibling of evolve-the-harness-at-test-time-without-labels aimed at cost rather than ceiling.

Caveats: seven business-oriented tasks (not code-repair benchmarks), 3 SLM families, one lab, one optimizer design, unreplicated. The cost/recovery numbers are best-case points — 5 of 21 pairs did not improve, only 7 fully closed the gap — so the distribution is "helps two-thirds, closes the gap a third, with a spectacular best case." The optimizer's own search compute is a one-time cost to weigh against the recurring inference savings. Carried at suggestive strength.

The ladder this note sits on (added 2026-07-29, connect). Three results in two maps state the same transfer at three intensities, and nobody had drawn the sequence. Rung 1 — the harness compensates: structure-outweighs-the-verification-step attributes +9.5 pp of a +11.0 pp production uplift to scaffolding and routing. Rung 2 — the harness substitutes: this note, where an optimizer's discovered adaptations let small models recover up to 89.7% of frontier performance at ~4% of cost, turning the cheap tier from a selection option into an engineering target. Rung 3 — the harness replaces: declarative-structure-outperforms-the-model-it-replaces carries it to the limit, where the heavy-lifting layer makes zero model calls (+24.1pp win rate at 0% invocation) and non-reasoning models driving the same loop end-to-end score below the no-LLM baseline up to 109B parameters.

Read together they give a routing question this map should be asking and isn't: for each stage of your loop, is the cheapest adequate tier a frontier model, a small specialist, or no model at all? Rung 3's scope condition is the important caveat on the ladder — it holds where the stage's decision is declaratively expressible (a posterior, a scored lookahead, a guarded predicate), which is why it was demonstrated in a probabilistic-search domain the authors concede favours it. Two adjacent economics notes belong in the same frame: prompt-caching-inverts-cloud-versus-local-economics (whether a model is expensive is substantially a property of how well the harness reuses context) and restrict-the-tool-surface-to-cut-cost-not-capability (tool surface is a spend lever independent of model choice, decided on cache-adjusted cost). In all four, the harness is the cost variable and the model is downstream of it.
