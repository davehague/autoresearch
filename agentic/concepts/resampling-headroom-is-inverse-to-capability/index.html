Resampling headroom is inverse to capability — and it is a variance measurement, not a capability one

Best-of-N headroom is usually reported as a capability number: how good can this agent get if you let it try three times. Read across a leaderboard it is something more useful — an estimate of how much of a model's single-run failure rate is variance rather than inability.

Across the 15 entries run more than once, Pass@3 − Pass@1 runs from +0.007 at the top (Opus 4.8/ClaudeCode, already saturated per-task) to +0.206 at the bottom (Qwen3.6-27B/Hermes), and the paper's finding is that it grows monotonically as base capability drops: mid-tier entries recover roughly 14–21 Grade points from three samples, the DeepSeek-v4-Flash entries sit at +0.10 to +0.11 regardless of harness.

Two readings follow, and they point opposite ways for practice.

Deployment. Mid-tier models can be run at materially higher effective quality behind multi-sample verification, and top-tier models cannot be moved by the same technique — the headroom is already spent. This is the same inverse-to-strength shape review-revise-loop-lifts-resolve-most-on-weak-generators measured for a review-revise loop (weak generator +29.4 pp, strong generator +3.2 pp), arrived at by a different mechanism: there the lift came from information fed back in, here from nothing but a second and third draw. Read together they suggest the loop literature's inverse-to-strength pattern may be substantially a resampling effect, which is precisely the confound a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm isolates — the null for a self-repair result is another attempt at the same budget, and that null is measured here at 10–21 points for mid-tier agents.

Measurement. A single-run comparison between two mid-tier entries is being drawn from distributions with 10–20 points of per-task recoverable variance in them, while the same comparison at the top of the leaderboard is nearly deterministic. The noise floor is not constant down the leaderboard, so a fixed "gaps under N points are unresolved" rule is wrong in both directions — too strict at the top, far too lenient in the middle. That is a refinement of the reading rule the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order set (treat a single-harness gap under ~10 points as unresolved) and a companion to coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor: the floor is a property of the cell, not of the benchmark.

Bounds. Pass@3 here is best-of-k Grade averaged per task, computed over at most three runs — it is headroom, not a variance characterization, and the paper says a stability metric is future work. nruns varies 1–3 across the leaderboard, so entries with one run contribute nothing to this and remain in the same tables. No intervals are reported. And best-of-N is only realizable in deployment where something can pick the good sample — the benchmark's oracle can, a production system generally cannot, which is the gap report-current-correctness-not-only-ever-correct names.
