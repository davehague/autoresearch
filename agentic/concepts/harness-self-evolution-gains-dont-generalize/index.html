A harness that gets better on the games it was tuned against mostly doesn't get better on the games it wasn't

This map's sub-thread (1) — does the search over harness configurations pay — already carries a domain-decomposition result on a different substrate (harness-evolution-wins-general-tasks-not-office, Evo-Bench): holding a policy model fixed, autonomous evolution beats a human-engineered baseline on general tasks but loses on office tasks. That result names which task class self-evolution helps. This source asks a different, equally load-bearing question on a different benchmark family (ARC-AGI-3): when a lineage improves on the games its own revisions were tuned against, how much of that improvement survives on games it never saw? The answer is "not much, on average, and with wide variance" — a near-null headline (+2.80 points, confidence interval straddling zero) from a benchmark purpose-built with a genuine held-out split, a stronger generalization design than most of this map's existing self-evolution evidence.

The two results compose rather than compete: Evo-Bench says self-evolution's comparative value depends on task domain; this source says that even where a lineage appears to be improving by its own in-process measure, that improvement is an unreliable predictor of what actually transfers. Taken together they sharpen this map's standing skepticism that "the search pays" is a reliable claim at all, as opposed to a claim that is sometimes true, hard to detect from inside the loop, and easy to overstate from development-set numbers alone.

The human-steered-ceiling-vs-autonomous-variance contrast is a second, independent instance of this map's anytime-best-checkpoint efficiency argument (also present in the Evo-Bench entry): if autonomous lineages range from zero to near-ceiling with no visible internal signal for which revision is actually best, a validation-held-out selection rule is doing real work that "run to budget exhaustion" is not.

Bounds: 27 lineages gives a wide confidence interval that already crosses zero; the "zero to near-ceiling" range is reported qualitatively, not broken out per model or lineage in the material reviewed. Single benchmark family, two revision rounds only — untested whether more rounds change the generalization picture in either direction. No compute-matched comparison against simply running the frozen initial harness for the same budget.
