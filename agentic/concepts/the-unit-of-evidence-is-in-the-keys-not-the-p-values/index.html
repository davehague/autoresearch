The design is in the schema, and the schema is the one thing a results table throws away

This map's demand is that a delta is not a result until its null is measured, and the nulls it has collected differ by subject: a paired same-model floor (measure-the-arm-delta-spread-not-the-arm-spread), a content-free placebo, a density-matched placebo, an independent data draw. Every one of those is a question about variation. This is a question about units, and it is prior to all of them — you cannot measure the spread of a delta until you have decided what an independent observation is, and that decision is not visible in any number this literature reports.

The claim is narrow and mechanical, which is why it holds. Every comparison on a leaderboard is paired: both systems attempt the same instances. Examples nest within tasks, instances nest within repositories, patches nest within submissions. All of that is legible in the artifact keys — a (submission, repo, instance) triple states its own nesting — and absent from the results table, where a reader could act on it. The author's aside is the whole argument compressed: a one-sentence prose disclaimer acknowledging the clustering, the common substitute for running the correct test, costs the author about as many words to write as the correct test costs to run.

The two numbers to carry. On BIG-Bench Hard the pooled chain-of-thought claim is significant either way — the paper's conclusion is not in doubt and the author says so explicitly — but the size of the evidence changes by 187 orders of magnitude, from p = 1.76×10⁻¹⁹² at the example level to p = 8.37×10⁻⁶ at the task level. As the paper puts it: a p-value of 10⁻¹⁹² is not something an experiment with 26 tasks can produce. The evidence amounts to 26 units, however many examples underlie each one. And on the SWE-bench Verified leaderboard, respecting the 12-repository nesting means the sharpest two-sided p-value the data can produce is 2/4,096 = 0.000488 — a discreteness floor above Bonferroni's threshold for the 8,911-claim family. The leaderboard is unanswerable at its own declared resolution.

The finding that changes what a reader should do with a corrected result. Table 3 prices both claim families under five fixed corrections. Read the Bonferroni row across the leaderboard's two unit levels: 6,467 of 8,911 endorsed at the instance level, 0 at the cluster level. The most conservative multiplicity correction available cannot see a design error, because it is not a design procedure. So "we applied Bonferroni" says nothing about whether the units were right, and a corpus can fail the design question while passing every correction. That is a distinct failure from the ones this map already holds — robust-within-a-family-is-not-identified-across-families found that robustness checks are claims about whichever family the analyst happened to try, and this finds the same shape one level down: a correction is a claim about whichever unit the analyst happened to assume.

Why it is a program fact rather than a judgment call. Two declarations per analysis — the artifact key and the cluster level — and the paired-or-clustered determination follows from declared functional dependencies between key fields, statically, before data is read. Theorem 7.3 proves the computation sound in Lean 4, given that the declared dependencies and identifying key are genuine facts about the run. A mechanism that assumes the structure away is refused rather than priced. The related protection: folding every observation onto its artifact on the way in means that where a benchmark instance carries several test assertions behind one generated patch, counting those assertions as separate trials has no expression in the language at all — which is a mistake this vault should expect to find in agent evaluations specifically, where one patch is graded by a whole test file.

Bounds. Both case studies are retrospective reconstructions made with the published outcomes in hand, so each prices a family chosen after the fact. The soundness theorem is conditional on the declarations being true — a wrong cluster declaration is priced as though it were right, and nothing checks it. The folding that blocks assertion-counting changes the estimand, and the author states this is not always in the conservative direction. And the honest comparison: the paper's own sequential budget is not the more powerful procedure on either corpus — it ties plain Benjamini–Hochberg on BIG-Bench Hard and loses 2,038 to 0 against Benjamini–Yekutieli on the cluster-level leaderboard. What it adds is the accounting and a design read off the keys, not more support.
