It never asks whether you meant to cherry-pick; it prices what the program looked at

Every mechanism this map has collected for keeping a delta honest is a discipline — measure the arm-delta spread, run the placebo arm, report the specification interval beside the sampling one, declare the family. All of them are things an analyst does or does not do, and none of them is checkable from the artifact after the fact. This is the first result in the map that makes one of them a property of the code.

The example that makes it obvious. Two clinical trials report the same subgroup finding. One reports on patients over 65 — a subgroup read off the enrollment records and fixed before any outcome was observed. The other reports on the patients who responded to treatment — a subgroup that only became available after every patient was measured. They can report identical p-values, and a multiple-comparison procedure handed those numbers has no way to tell them apart. But the difference survives in the program: selecting on a property of the key records a footprint of the observations it keeps, while selecting on a property of the outcome records a footprint of every observation it read. The record is permanent, and it is not a judgment about the analyst. The system never asks about intent; it prices what was looked at.

Translated to this vault's subject, the outcome-selected subgroup is "the instances this agent failed", "the trajectories where the judge disagreed", "the tasks where the harness change mattered." Those are the exact slices agent papers report, they are read off outcomes by construction, and the price they are currently charged is zero.

Pre-registration becomes a typing rule, not a norm. Because the wealth transformer is antitone in the realized p-value, affordability can be checked before the analysis runs — the checker inspects a program's declared bounds and schema before a single observation is read, and Theorem 7.5 shows checker acceptance implies the guarantee without conditioning on the honesty of the declared bounds. That last clause is the interesting one: a declared bound that is dishonest makes the analysis unaffordable, not falsely licensed. This is the same instinct as robust-within-a-family-is-not-identified-across-families with an enforcement mechanism attached rather than a promise.

The finding with the shortest path to practice. A sequential budget's power depends on the order claims arrive in, and that order is usually an accident. On the leaderboard family, reading order supports 0 claims, a random draw supports a median of 28 with a maximum of 302, and best-first — an oracle bound, not a valid ordering — reaches 6,545. Every ordering fixed independently of the evidence is covered equally by the guarantee, so a study whose claim order does not otherwise matter has two orders of magnitude to gain by shuffling the family with a seed fixed before any claim is priced. The library ships this and deliberately does not make it the default, because reading and chronological order are what an analyst writes without thinking, and reporting them shows what an unconsidered sequence costs. On BIG-Bench Hard, where effects are large and plentiful, ordering is nearly irrelevant (19 / 19 / median 17) — so the ordering tax is a symptom of a dense family of small separations, which is what agent leaderboards are.

Bounds, and they are not small. The guarantee is mFDR_η, a ratio of expectations that implies neither the false discovery rate nor the family-wise error rate — in a 4,000-trial simulation where every discovery is false by construction, the raw false share reached 1.00 while mFDR_η read 0.0031 and passed. The author states this plainly rather than letting a reader assume otherwise, and names an e-value foundation as the migration that would close it. Only fixed_fraction is covered by the proved theorem; the five shipped QUDE investment rules are measured without proof. The soundness results are conditional on declarations being genuine facts about the run — a wrong cluster declaration is priced as if it were right. And under synthetic validation at the instance level, every procedure tested including this one exceeds its own α under a complete null, which is the theorem operating under a hypothesis that was declared away rather than met.
