The deliverable is plausible, and that is the whole problem

This map catalogues proxies that misgrade optimistically. Most of them fail because the measurement is weak — a merge label carries social variance, a judge has biases, resolved rate is not quality. This one fails because the measurement is aimed at the wrong object. An output check inspects the artifact. In compliance work, incident review, financial reporting and any audit-shaped task, the procedure is the product: a report claiming figures that were never independently recalculated is defective even when every number in it is right, and no inspection of the report can tell you which happened.

The paper measures the gap rather than asserting it. Across 30 skills the prose agent performs 56% of its own mandated steps while producing artifacts that pass output checks throughout — a report with reasonable figures, a summary of review comments, a design document. The two sharpest cases are the ones where the skipped step was the verification: a skill whose stated Iron Law is that no completion claim may be made without fresh verification evidence is satisfied 30% of the time, with the agent writing "all tests pass, build succeeds" straight into the deliverable; a skill mandating a live API fetch before acting on state is satisfied 20%, with the agent describing the requests it would make and then reasoning from stale prompt content. In both cases the output is fluent and the check that would have caught it is the one that did not run.

That closes a loop this vault has been drawing from several directions. the-agents-final-report-is-its-least-reliable-artifact and audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence established that an agent's narration of its own work is weak evidence; cogenerated-tests-and-fixes-fail-together and fail-to-pass-does-not-constrain-the-fix established that an artifact-side check drawn from the same reading as the artifact does not constrain it. This adds the case where the check is fine and simply cannot see the omission, because the omission left no trace in the thing being checked. A passing output test is evidence about the output and no evidence at all about the process — and where the process carries the validity, that is the wrong half.

The constructive half matters as much. What makes the compiled arm auditable is not that it performs better but that its evidence is different in kind: a code-emitted node-path trace, one record per step, naming the outcome of any code-owned action, where success flags are computed by the runtime rather than asserted by the model and a walker that terminates early reports incomplete rather than returning whatever it has. That is the substrate this map keeps asking for (grade-process-defects-not-just-outcomes, measure-whether-evidence-reconstructs-the-claim) — an action log the model cannot write. The transferable practice for anyone gating agent work does not require a compiler: decide which of your mandated steps leave machine-checkable evidence, and grade that evidence rather than the deliverable. A skipped step that leaves no trace is not a gap in your gate's precision; it is outside your gate's field of view.

Caveats. The mandate statuses are assigned by a model judge against a fixed reference procedure — constrained to a four-way typed decision with unreached mandates excluded, but a judge, so the 56% is a judged quantity (llm-judges-have-exploitable-biases). The strongest form of the claim, that the artifacts would have passed a real output check, is argued and illustrated rather than measured against a shipped checker: the paper deliberately reports procedure execution instead of artifact correctness, on the position that a run skipping a mandated step is wrong regardless of what it produced. Thirty skills weighted toward document production, software process and compliance work — domains chosen because the procedure carries the validity, which is also the population where this claim is strongest. Nine runs per skill per arm, two model generations from one provider.
