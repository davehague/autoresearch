Stopping is the correction that installs; everything open-ended does not

This map's within-agent limb has been about when to keep repairing — repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum (validity peaks at round 2, then collapses under a noisy verifier), stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain (+60.6 pp at 0.72 rounds instead of five), recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder (reflect is the worst fixed policy at 27.5%). This adds what kind of correction is installable at all, and the axis is not the one you would guess.

Repairability tracks the constraint on the corrective action. A single fixed token installs at 0.97 ± 0.06 with a tight CI. Spatial-coordinate corrections land at 0.53–0.71 with ±0.26–0.35 — one seed at exactly 0.00 — and an open-ended generative field-fill at 0.14 ± 0.04. It is emphatically not about the base model's prior: the most reliably-installed correction has by far the lowest base log-probability (−13.6 against −3.2 and −3.4), because at the moment of over-execution the policy is committed to acting and assigns stopping almost no mass. Discreteness beats willingness. The ordering replicates on an architecturally distinct second policy, so the structure is not a tokenizer artifact.

And the transfer boundary is the sharper result for anyone building a loop. The frame-level repair reaches oracle-graded task success only where the corrected action is the task's sole remaining blocker (8/20 vs base 0/15, p = 0.006). Where it is one of several required steps: 0/5 vs 0/5. Where it was never the bottleneck: 2/5 vs 2/5. Fixing a real, verified, correctly-identified defect bought nothing in two of three cells. Read with repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum, the licence for a repair loop narrows twice over: the verifier must discriminate and the defect it finds must be the binding constraint. Every harness's default retry policy assumes neither.

One mechanism note worth keeping. Isolating the two terms of the update in the same trainer, the reward term alone installs the stop at 0.00 — an importance-ratio objective can only up-weight actions the policy already samples, and done() appeared in 0 of 15 base frames — while the imitation term alone installs it at 1.00, and adding reward back does not improve on it. Reward can only suppress what the agent already does; only a teacher can add what it never tries. That is a clean statement of why an unvalidated self-improvement loop cannot bootstrap a missing behaviour, and it pairs with gate-irreversible-actions-on-independent-confirmation: the weights make the stop available, the gate supplies its timing.

The paper's title is the practitioner form — teach it to stop, not just to click — and it explains why stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain is the reachable intervention: stopping is a single discrete token, the one correction that installs reliably.

Caveats: T3, computer-use agents rather than coding agents, carried for its structure rather than its magnitudes. One policy for the main numbers; the second-policy check is small-n and imitation-only. Held-out sets are 9–34 decision frames from 6–10 trajectories; end-to-end is 5 rollouts per arm on three cells, with the effect cell deliberately extended to 20 vs 15 after it showed a non-null result — the authors disclose this. The frame-level metric is verifier-circular by construction. Environments and backend proprietary and unreleased.
