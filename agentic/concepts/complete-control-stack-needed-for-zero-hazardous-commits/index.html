Partial governance fails gracefully downward, not safely -- and only the complete stack converts missing evidence into friction rather than risk

This map's accumulated evidence keeps finding that a layered, environment-first, fail-closed design beats a single checkpoint, and this source supplies the cleanest demonstration yet of why partial is not a discount version of complete: on the same 29-case suite, a baseline with no controls commits every hazardous action, and configurations with some but not all mechanisms still commit the large majority of them (26/29, 22/29) — there is no gentle slope from "mostly safe" to "fully safe," and the ablation shows most control families need more than one surviving mechanism to hold at all. The evidence-loss result is the sharper finding for this vault's unattended-operation concerns: the complete framework's response to missing evidence is not to guess and risk a false ALLOW, it defers (HOLD), at a cost that rises steeply with evidence loss (5.0%->55.5%) but never converts to permission (0/600) — exactly the fail-closed property this map has asked self-graded and SMT-style gates to demonstrate rather than assert, now shown under actual evidence degradation instead of a synthetic adversary. Interaction edge: evaluation-integrity — the OpenFOAM case (complete evidence coverage, still a FAIL judgment) is a clean instance of evidence coverage and correctness judgment being different questions, the same separation this vault's self-reported-evidence findings keep rediscovering elsewhere.

[T1, six-author academic study, no industry affiliation disclosed in the fetched text; the headline 0/29 is a closed-set conformance result against a suite the framework was built to catch, which the authors themselves say is not a safety probability; prototypes test selected mechanisms rather than the full eight-domain design end-to-end; real-world validation is one OpenFOAM toolchain (10 state-separation cases); multi-agent handoffs are checked only by deterministic interface tests, not under adversarial or model-authored conditions.]
