The average hides a domain where partial credit does not exist

This map already holds that a proxy can be honest at one granularity and uninformative at the next (proxy-metrics-rank-models-not-suites: coverage predicts real-bug detection at r = 0.861 across models and below 0.2 within one). This is the same failure along a different cut, and the cut is the one a buyer actually cares about.

Hold the agent fixed and move across task categories: strong all-around entries keep 0.85–0.90 on Knowledge, Legal and Manufacturing and drop to 0.63 and 0.50 on Commerce. Nothing about the models changed. What changed is the failure economics of the deliverable. Commerce tasks mix numerical reconciliation, cross-currency arithmetic, structured spreadsheet output and strict schema constraints, so a single arithmetic or schema mistake voids the entire artifact; Knowledge and Legal tasks award structural credit for partially correct work. The overall Pass@1 that ranks these entries is an average over both regimes.

The reshuffling makes the point independently of the dip. No row of the per-domain heatmap is uniformly bright. The Legal leader (Opus 4.8/ClaudeCode, 0.951) is not the Localization leader (Opus 4.7/ClaudeCode 0.836, DeepSeek-v4-Flash/Codex 0.855), and Manufacturing has a third (GPT-5.5/OpenClaw, 0.912). Ranking rearranges column to column across the middle of the leaderboard, which is the same instability no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models finds along the harness axis — an interaction, not an offset.

The operational rule is narrower and more usable than "report the distribution": find the subset of your workload where partial credit is not available, and score that separately. An agent at 0.79 overall may be at 0.50 on the all-or-nothing half, and the all-or-nothing half is usually the half someone wanted to automate. This is the task-category sibling of reliability-and-capability-rankings-diverge, which found the same divergence along task duration and named software engineering the steepest-degrading domain — two cuts, one lesson: the aggregate is averaging over the axis that decides whether the agent is deployable.

Bounds. Five domains and 67 tasks from one team's construction; the Commerce dip is a property of how these particular Commerce tasks were graded as much as of the domain, and the paper's own appendix attributes it partly to tasks that break at an arithmetic verification step. Whether the same collapse appears in repository maintenance work — where a failing test is likewise all-or-nothing — is untested here.
