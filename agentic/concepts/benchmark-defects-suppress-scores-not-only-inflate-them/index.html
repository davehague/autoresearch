The instrument was wrong by 30 to 70 points, in the direction nobody audits for

This map's catalogue is overwhelmingly a catalogue of numbers that are too generous — contamination inflating a resolve rate, a judge gameable under optimization pressure, a merge label crediting work a human finished. The 2026-07-12 refinement conceded that a plain instrument bug misgrades in whichever direction the bug points, and cited a 53-point buried capability as the case. This is that case at benchmark scale, on an instrument in production use: a component of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a standing evaluation in a national AI security institute's suite and at national laboratories, and a table row in current frontier model cards.

The diagnosis started from the shape of the scores, not from the code. The strongest 2026 models sat within four points of each other and a successor tied its predecessor. That leaves two readings — scientific coding is genuinely beyond current models, or the instrument cannot separate them — and the second is testable by audit. Two design choices make a defect maximally expensive here and are worth recognizing in any benchmark: subproblems are cumulative, so a defective early step contaminates everything downstream, and scoring is all-or-nothing, so one bad gold value forfeits the whole problem. A defect every model meets becomes a ceiling every model shares, which is exactly the compression observed.

The audit is the finding as much as the numbers are. 263 defects across 65 problems; 192 reject a correct, instruction-following solution; they touch 58 of 64 main problems. The largest single scientific subtype is unspecified convention (77) — the gold fixes a choice the prompt leaves free, such as whether a final 1×1 Householder reflector is applied, where both answers are valid and only one is accepted. Then wrong gold (33, including a sign error making a transfer matrix non-unitary), RNG-dependent grading (22, a test keyed to one random sequence rather than to the statistic), over-tight tolerance (13, including an allclose at rtol = 10⁻¹⁵, below double precision, and one target that is a rounding fingerprint), and spec↔gold contradiction (9, a prompt requiring a strict boundary against a gold using an inclusive one).

The expertise gate is the part that generalizes. 150 of the 192 score-suppressing defects (78%) are scientific — recognizable only by someone who can redo the physics or mathematics. A wrong gold value fails no surface check; it looks exactly like a hard problem. That is the boundary condition on automated-benchmark-flaw-scanners's result that transcript-visible flaws can be scanned for cheaply, and it sharpens benchmark-flaw-detection-is-uneven-by-criterion: the criteria that automate are the ones whose evidence is in the transcript, and the majority of what was wrong here left no trace there.

Correction is not a uniform lift, which is the operational consequence. Every model gains, but not equally: gaps widen (2 → 8 main problems between two models) and rank order changes (Seed 2.1 Pro first becomes sixth). That is removing-defective-instances-reshuffles-the-leaderboard arriving by repair rather than by removal, and it means a decision taken on the uncorrected leaderboard — which model to route the premium role to, which to build on — was taken on an ordering the instrument did not support.

Bounds. Single-problem scientific coding, not long-horizon agentic workflows with tool use and iterative development — the authors say so explicitly. Every review was conducted by the authors, so there is no external blind validation; the second adversarial pass is internal. Reported original-benchmark accuracies differ somewhat from the public leaderboard's. Grading counts a solution correct if it passes in either of two pinned NumPy/SciPy environments, which rescues 7–9 of 287 subproblems per model, and the corrected benchmark approaches saturation at the top (98.3% subproblem for the leader), so it will not discriminate for long. Pass@1 with single runs for most cells, so small differences between models should not be over-read — the paper says this too.
