Two modes, and it takes the tenth seed to see the second

The hardest cell's outcome distribution is not a noisy unimodal one. It has two modes — ≈0.09 taking 40% of runs and ≈0.80 taking 60% — with a dip test rejecting unimodality at p = 0.07 (ΔBIC = 13 for a two-component mixture). Three consequences follow, and they compound.

Mean ± std is the wrong summary. Not imprecise: wrong. The authors report an interquartile mean of 0.56 with CI [0.19, 0.81] instead, because the average of a bimodal distribution names a value the system rarely produces. A single run has ~30% chance of the failure mode — which is where the paper's own earlier "0/14, grounding cannot be bought" boundary claim came from, retracted here after five seeds spanned 0.00–0.86, wider than the entire effect the draft had claimed.

The second mode is invisible at conventional seed counts. At k = 5 the dip test gives p = 0.38; it takes the tenth seed to make the mode visible. So the standard "we ran it a few times" defence does not merely widen the error bar, it can miss the structure entirely. The power arithmetic splits claims in two: existence (this beats base) is cheap at k ≤ 5, but resolving two high-variance cells apart (Δ ≈ 0.18, σ ≈ 0.3) needs k ≈ 46. Validated out of sample — splitting seeds into halves, a k-seed mean predicts the held-out half 1.3–1.7× better than a single run, which misestimates by up to 0.34–0.42 at the 90th percentile. Reporting k seeds changes the answer, not just the error bars.

And the stress test lands on the field's practice rather than on one paper. Combine the improvement magnitudes agentic papers publish (+7.7 points on a named benchmark; +0.2 task success) with the variance measured here, and a single-run report has the wrong sign 33–44% (and 25–34%) of the time. "Close to a coin flip on its sign."

This is the third member of a family this map now holds explicitly. skewed-label-bases-inflate-classifier-headlines says find the null before reading the headline metric. coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor says measure a same-model paired floor beside any architectural delta. This one says characterize the distribution's shape before choosing a summary statistic, and set k from the cell's σ and the effect you mean to claim. All three are cases of the instrument, not the agent, producing the number — and this one adds that the honest version has a price (k ≈ 46) which is why nobody pays it (evaluation-steers-development-rather-than-checking-it).

Caveats: T3, computer-use agents rather than coding, one policy, one environment family — the shape and the seed-budget method transfer; the σ values, the 30% and the 33–44% do not, and the authors are explicit that the sign-flip figure is a bootstrap stress test on their measured distribution, not a claim about the cited papers' actual results. The dip test itself sits at p = 0.07 on k = 10, in one cell. Proprietary, unreleased environments and backend. Carried for the reporting discipline.
