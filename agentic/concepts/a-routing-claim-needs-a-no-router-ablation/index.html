The system worked and the component it is named after did not

This map's null family each name what a number would be if the claimed mechanism did not exist — a paired same-model floor (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor), a content-free placebo (a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm), a majority-class baseline (skewed-label-bases-inflate-classifier-headlines), a composed stage product (a-long-horizon-claim-needs-a-composed-stage-baseline), an always-serial arm (a-coordination-arm-needs-an-always-serial-reference). Routing has an equally cheap missing arm and it is almost never run: send everything to one model and keep the rest of the pipeline.

The study that ran it found the arm ties. The routed system solves 159 of 266 at $0.230 per solve; always dispatching to the cheapest fixer with the same verified handoff and no routing decision at all solves 159 of 266 at $0.227. Both sit +3.60 points above the blind-mixing line, so even the margin over random traffic splitting is the handoff's. The calibrated router in fact concentrated 263 of 266 tasks on the cheapest model, and its three diversions were insurance worth $0.003 per solve. The authors state the conclusion against their own headline: the handoff carries the result and routing collapses to cost allocation.

The paired calibration explains why, and the shape is worth carrying separately. On 99 fresh tasks, each attempted by all four fixers cold and with the handoff injected, the handoff redistributes rather than adds: +5.1, +4.0 and +2.0 points for the three cheaper fixers and −4.0 for the strongest, pooling to +1.8 with a 95% interval of [−1.0, +4.5]. Nothing is significant at n = 99 and the authors say so — the intervals carry the information, not the point estimates. But that shape is precisely what makes a cost router look successful while contributing nothing: the intervention lifts the floor toward the ceiling, so after it the cheap model suffices everywhere and there is no decision left to make well.

A third arm in the same paper is the one to steal for feature design. Holding solve rate matched, the router saves 30.5% of cost from task text alone and 34.3% when the searcher's hidden states are added — but 8.0–9.4% when the handoff's text is fed to the router. The searcher's internal state helps the router and the searcher's memo helps the fixers, and crossing them hurts. The authors also flag, against themselves, that part of the hidden-state effect at the gate is mechanical — that head's predictions are near-constant and lie entirely above the threshold, so blending shrinks the text head toward it and admits more tasks to the cheap model regardless of information content; they did not run a matched shrinkage control, so the informational contribution is not cleanly separated.

Bounds. One benchmark, one pool of four fixers, three repositories. The authors are explicit that the tie is a property of this pool — in deployment nobody knows in advance that one cheap model reaches parity, and the router is what converts that gamble into a calibrated decision made from free public data. So this is not "routers do not work"; it is that a routing paper without this arm has not shown that its router did anything, which is the same claim the always-serial arm makes about coordination papers. The calibration n is 99 and no handoff contrast in the paper reaches significance on either the calibration labels or the benchmark subset; the mechanism rests on the pattern recurring across two settings rather than on any test. The benchmark-side handoff comparisons are router-selected subsets rather than random assignment, and the paper does not run a pass-through arm in which fixers receive unverified reproduction claims, so the verification guard's contribution is inferred from a claim census rather than measured.
