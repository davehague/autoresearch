The null catalogue gets an entry about resolution rather than about a missing arm

Everything else in this map's null catalogue names a comparison you forgot to run — a placebo arm (a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm), an always-serial reference (a-coordination-arm-needs-an-always-serial-reference), a no-router ablation (a-routing-claim-needs-a-no-router-ablation), a withheld-hint arm (a-containment-claim-needs-a-withheld-hint-arm). This one names a resolution: the arm is right, the design is right, and the sample size per item makes the question unanswerable.

The demonstration is unusually complete because the same archive supports both readings. Under K=50 with Fisher + BH + a 20-point effect floor, the nine cells contain 457 reliable item-level changes. Draw one trial per item per model from that same archive under a fixed seed and rerun the identical classification: every item on every edge is inconclusive, and none of the 457 is recovered. Not attenuated — structurally impossible, since a comparison of two single binary observations cannot reach significance under the test.

The cross-tabulation is the part to carry into practice, because "just look at the flips" is what people actually do. Of 221 single-draw incorrect→correct flips, 120 correspond to reliable improvements, 25 to practically-equivalent items and 66 to inconclusive ones; of 175 correct→incorrect flips, 87 are reliable regressions and 80 are not. So a raw flip is about a coin toss for being real. Worse in the other direction: 232 genuinely changed items produced no flip at all, which is the half that never appears in an eyeball comparison. This is the same shape as one-run-scores-an-implementation-not-an-idea and a-multi-agent-configuration-is-a-distribution-not-a-run, stated for the case where the thing varying is the vendor's model rather than the seed or the session.

The permutation null is what makes the positive side believable. Rather than assuming the criteria are conservative, the study reruns the complete classification procedure — Fisher, BH and the effect floor together — on label-permuted data at matched sample size, 1,000 times, and reports that the 95th percentile of the reliable-change share is 0.0% in every cell. So every reliable change sits strictly above a measured floor rather than above an assumed one. That is report-a-null-as-a-bounded-interval-not-a-failed-test applied to a classification pipeline rather than to a single estimate, and it is the design detail most transferable to anyone building an acceptance gate: permute your own labels and see what your rule finds in noise before you trust what it finds in data.

Two design choices worth stealing. The practical-significance floor (ε = 0.2) sits alongside significance rather than replacing it, and the sensitivity sweep over {0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25} moves shares monotonically without changing any cell's qualitative reading — a cheap robustness check almost nobody runs on an effect-size threshold. And the four-way classification keeps practically equivalent and inconclusive as distinct outcomes, so "we could not tell" never gets reported as "nothing changed" — which is precisely the conflation this map exists to complain about.

Bounds. ε = 0.2 and K = 50 jointly determine what is detectable, and smaller genuine changes stay in the inconclusive bucket by construction. The benchmark screening deliberately selects suites where the models retain headroom, so these are estimates for informative item sets rather than population rates. One vendor's product line, three public benchmarks, non-agentic single-turn tasks. And K=50 is expensive: 135,000 responses to compare three model versions, which sets a real floor on what an engineering team can afford to run per migration — though the released response-level archive means the analysis can be redone without re-querying an API that will not exist in the same form later.
