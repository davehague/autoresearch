A 13.6% correction moves two-thirds of the standings

The prevalence number is the headline; this is the number that decides whether the prevalence matters. A construction defect affecting one instance in seven would be tolerable if it taxed every agent equally — a constant subtracted from every score leaves the ranking intact, and rankings are what leaderboards are read for. It does not. Removing the 68 defective instances moves 64.1% of 131 agents, in both directions, and changes 9 of the top 10 positions. The mean gain is small (2.77 pp) and the spread is what matters (−0.24 to +4.49 pp): agents lean on the misaligned instances unevenly, so the defect behaves like a per-agent bias rather than a shared offset.

Why the dependence would be uneven is not measured here, and the honest position is that it is unexplained. Two plausible mechanisms are both in this vault already and both would produce this signature. An agent that explores harder before patching may incidentally satisfy requirements the problem statement never stated — which is also what a better retriever looks like (benchmark-scores-inflate-when-agents-can-retrieve-the-fix). And an agent that guesses at unstated requirements rather than asking is rewarded on exactly the instances where the brief is incomplete (underspecified-targets-drive-unsafe-actions). Either way, a slice of what the leaderboard is measuring is tolerance for a broken brief, which nobody intends to rank agents on.

The transferable rule is the one this map keeps arriving at from different directions (proxy-metrics-rank-models-not-suites, harness-asymmetry-manufactures-model-differences, difficulty-bands-are-agent-specific): a benchmark ranking is a claim about the whole apparatus, and the apparatus includes how the instances were built. Before quoting a rank ordering — including one that decides which of two agents you deploy — ask what happens to it under a plausible correction to the instance set. Here the correction was 13.6% of instances and it moved most of the board.

Held at suggestive deliberately. The recomputation reuses official per-instance resolution data and simply drops the flagged instances; the authors state plainly that this is not causal evidence, and it assumes agent performance on the surviving 432 is unchanged by the exclusion, which is not verifiable from leaderboard data. The 68 exclusions are the paper's own hand-audited labels, several resting on judgment calls a different annotator team might make differently, and a rank movement of one or two positions is within what re-running the same agent might produce anyway — this map holds that same-configuration variance is routinely larger than published deltas (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor, one-run-scores-an-implementation-not-an-idea), and no such floor was measured here. The top-10 movement and the sign spread survive that objection better than the 64.1% headline does.
