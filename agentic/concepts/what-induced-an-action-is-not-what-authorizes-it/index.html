The gate should not be reading the transcript

This map's architecture is enforce below the prompt in layers, and its live problem is what the classifier rung should actually reason over. a-permission-reviewer-cannot-verify-the-authorization-it-reads establishes the negative version tonight: a model-based reviewer that infers user authorization from the working agent's transcript is defeated on 13 of 13 objectives on every harness that ships one, because the transcript's role labels are written by the harness and can be made to say anything. This is the constructive half, arrived at independently and from the ordinary-injection direction rather than the escalation one — and it is the same correction: authorization must come from something that was not reconstructed.

The framing does the work. The reason defenses in this family keep buying security with utility is that they answer one question where there are two. Did this observation cause the agent to propose this action is a provenance fact. Is this action within what the user granted is an authority fact. Open-ended work makes the first unavoidable — "find the quarterly report and send it to Alice" needs a file identifier that does not exist until after the search — so a defense that treats observation-influence as an attack signal conflates legitimate runtime adaptation with attack-induced behaviour, and the measurements show it: Tool Filter and IPIGuard narrow the execution space early and reach ASR 1.83% and 0.71% on the dynamic benchmark while their utility under attack falls to 45.29% and 43.62%.

The distinction offered instead is runtime instantiation versus authority expansion. External content may determine a local data value or a runtime object; it may not determine the operation type, target, recipient, permission scope or external effect. That is a line an operator can hold, and it is enforced at the point where it matters — a violation is defined as a call that actually reaches the executor and produces an unauthorized external effect, not as the agent reading or discussing attacker content.

Three properties, and the third is the one nobody else has. Authority Non-Escalation is least privilege plus complete mediation. Persistent Action Origin is the classical taint-tracking rule: a security attribute is not implicitly cleared because the value passed through normal processing. And Origin Non-Override — later legitimate execution may add evidence, but historical reappearance alone cannot promote an argument into authority. The paper's name for the failure this prevents is exact: provenance laundering, which is the same move the-harness-relabels-content-and-both-defenses-believe-it performs at the message-role layer. Two independent groups this month, on different objects, both finding that the thing that breaks is provenance surviving a re-presentation.

The ablation is why this is a design result and not a system paper. Removing cross-step origin tracking costs almost nothing in utility and raises ASR by 20× and 18× — persistence is nearly free security. Removing the argument-level veto raises ASR to 2.49% and 2.73% and increases both utility measures, which localizes an entire attack class: a call can preserve the correct task direction and the correct execution chain while expanding authority only in its argument bindings — the recipient, the target object. Any gate that authorizes at the level of "is this the right kind of action" and not "are these the right arguments" is open on exactly that seam, and this map's action-boundary results have generally been scored at the action level.

Read against the map's other bound. containment-at-the-endpoint-leaves-the-artifact-broken found that every executor control drives forbidden action to near zero and moves artifact preservation not at all — the gate fixes the act and not the state upstream of it. This is the complementary result: the gate can be made to fire correctly on adversarial input without collapsing legitimate dynamic execution, but only by holding two records rather than one. Neither result says a gate is sufficient.

Bounds. Explicitly not a formal guarantee — semantic judgments produce false positives and negatives, and recovery from a blocked call depends on the host agent's replanning, so utility is a function of model capability. The threat model trusts the user request, tool schema, the authorizer runtime and the executor, and the paper excludes direct attacks bypassing the runtime, pure data-dependency vulnerabilities, and unconditional delegation of control to external content. Two benchmarks, two main backbones plus four open-weight ones; benign utility is a mean over three runs while the attack metrics are weighted over instances. CaMeL beats it on ASR in three of four settings. And the cost is real — see a-runtime-authorizer-costs-about-two-x-in-tokens.
