Grade the grader first

Anthropic's eval practice post carries the vault's first vendor-documented case of an eval misgrading pessimistically: Opus 4.5 initially scored 42% on CORE-Bench, and after fixing the eval — "rigid grading that penalized '96.12' when expecting '96.124991…', ambiguous task specs, and stochastic tasks that were impossible to reproduce exactly" — plus a less constrained scaffold, the same model measured 95%. Every proxy failure in this map so far pointed optimistic; this one points the other way, which upgrades the map's thesis from "cheap proxies flatter the agent" to "the number describes the eval as much as the agent, and you don't know the sign until you look." Selection and optimization pressure push proxies optimistic; instrument bugs push wherever the bug points. The stated house rule generalizes both: "we do not take eval scores at face value until someone digs into the details of the eval and reads some transcripts" — the trajectory-audit practice this map already holds as T1 (audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence), confirmed as a vendor's operating procedure.

Two reporting conventions worth adopting directly. pass@k vs pass^k: probability that ≥1 of k attempts succeeds vs that all k succeed — at k=10 the two diverge toward 100% and 0% on the same agent, which is reliability-and-capability-rankings-diverge compressed into a metric pair anyone can publish. And two-sided task sets: "One-sided evals create one-sided optimization" — test where the behavior should occur and where it shouldn't, the false-negative discipline agentic-code-review keeps noting nobody applies to reviewer agents.

The scaffold half of the 42→95 swing also feeds agent-harness-architecture: an eval score is a property of the model-plus-harness pair (scaffold-topology-should-adapt-to-task-quality measured 8.3 points of scaffold effect; this anecdote implies larger), so a benchmark comparing models under one fixed scaffold is silently comparing scaffold fit.

Caveats: T2 vendor guidance with illustrative anecdotes, not methodology; the 53-point swing conflates grader fixes with scaffold changes and the decomposition isn't given; the post's "outcomes over trajectories" grading advice must be read next to ProcCtrlBench's T1 result that outcome-equal agents differ widely on process defects — grade outcomes, but validate the grader and diagnose failures on trajectories.
