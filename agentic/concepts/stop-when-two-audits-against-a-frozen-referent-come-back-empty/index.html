Seventeen repair rounds without damage, because the referent never moved

repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum is one of this map's sharper results: when verifier and repairer are both noisy, true validity peaks around round 2 and then falls below the no-repair baseline while acceptance keeps climbing — 55% of correct plans repaired into incorrect ones, 24% of those damaging repairs winning majority acceptance. Read literally, a seventeen-round repair loop should be deep in the destructive regime. This case ran seventeen and did not degrade.

The difference is not the round count and not the model. It is what the verifier compares against. In the interior-optimum setting the verifier judges a candidate on its own merits, so verifier noise and repairer noise compound freely and the loop wanders. Here the verifier compares code against a specification frozen before the code existed and never touched again — the same fixed document at round 1 and round 17. A loop with a stationary external referent has something the self-judged loop lacks: a fixed point it can actually reach, and no way for the referent to drift toward the artifact.

The finding curve is what a converging loop looks like: 10, 12, 9, 21, 2, 7, 8, 13, 6, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 10, 0, 0. Not monotone — two spikes, one late — which is itself worth noting, because a stopping rule of one empty pass would have terminated at neither and a rule of "findings are thinning" would have terminated three cycles early. Two consecutive empty passes is a cheap, harness-implementable rule, and it is the same shape as stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain — stop on what the loop is producing, not on a preset budget or a vote.

The scope condition this puts on repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum is a real one and it is testable: the interior optimum may be a property of self-referential verification rather than of iteration. The vault already holds the ingredients for the same claim from the other side — an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself and the-checker-should-see-less-than-the-generator both find that what the checker is denied sets its value, and a frozen prior document is a strong form of denial: it cannot have been contaminated by the code, because it predates it. See the-referent-not-the-reviewer-carries-the-separation.

Bounds, and they are heavy against a controlled T1. This is n = 1, self-reported, with no control arm — nobody ran the same task with a five-round cap, or with an unfrozen specification, so the comparison to the interior-optimum result is inference from design difference, not a measured contrast. There is no false-rejection accounting: 116 corrections were applied on the verifier's say-so and nobody checked how many were unnecessary or damaging, which is precisely the quantity repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum measures and this case cannot. Correctness after the fact rests on one manual execution, a pre-existing unit suite and ~30 sessions of use, not on an oracle for the new behaviour — there wasn't one. And the specification itself was produced by fourteen prior audit cycles (converge-the-spec-against-the-code-before-freezing-it), so "the referent was good" is doing unmeasured work.
