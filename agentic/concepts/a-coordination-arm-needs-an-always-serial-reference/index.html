The missing arm in a coordination study is "one agent, in order"

This map's founding demand is that a delta is not a result until its null is measured, and each member of the family names a different null: a paired same-model floor for architecture deltas (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor), a content-free placebo for retry loops (a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm), a majority-class baseline for classifiers (skewed-label-bases-inflate-classifier-headlines), a composed stage product for long horizons (a-long-horizon-claim-needs-a-composed-stage-baseline). This adds one more, and it is the cheapest of the set to run and the most consistently omitted: for any multi-agent coordination claim, the same work done by one agent in order.

The reason it matters is that "coordination helps" is silently two claims. Beats unconstrained parallel execution says the mechanism suppresses interference between concurrent writers. Beats one agent working serially says the architecture is worth building. The literature reports the first and is read as the second. On the one study that instrumented both, they separate cleanly: deterministic pre-write admission beat unconstrained parallel by +26.7 pp and matched always serial at 50.0% versus 50.0% — not approximately, but with identical pair-pass rates inside every repository-task cluster, which is why the paired and aggregate differences coincide. The treatment also came in slower than serial on the study's own modelled critical path (188.0 s vs 181.4 s), because it pays declaration overhead on a workload it serializes anyway (pre-write-admission-buys-reliability-by-serializing).

Two design choices in the same study are worth copying independently of its result.

Freeze the upstream stage across arms. The planner's 60 feature declarations were generated once under a fixed freeze seed and reused across all four arms and all three coder seeds. Without that, planner variation loads onto the policy contrast and the study measures a mixture. This is the same move one-run-scores-an-implementation-not-an-idea argues for from the other direction — there the finding is that implementation choice dominates the seed, here the response is to hold one implementation fixed and vary only the thing under test.

Resample the cluster, not the execution. The bootstrap unit is repository + task identifier, not the 360 executions, because three coder seeds on the same pair are correlated observations rather than independent samples. Seven clusters is a thin base and the intervals say so: a +26.7 pp point estimate carries [9.6, 60.0]. An analysis that resampled executions would have produced a tight interval around a number the design cannot support. The author states this explicitly and interprets the intervals as uncertainty summaries rather than asymptotic guarantees.

What this asks of the vault's own holdings: loop-engineering's still-unrun three-armed sweep (raw pool / event passing / verified gist) needs a fourth arm, and the two fleet results the map leans on — event passing at +8.3 pts on SWE-bench Pro, verified gists at +9.3 to +10.5 on SWE-bench Verified — report gains over strong single agents but not over a serial execution of the same decomposition under the same discipline. Neither is refuted. Both are now missing an arm this study shows can swallow the whole effect.

Bounds. One study, seven clusters, three Python project families, one planner model and one coder model, oracle-localized initial context, and physically sequential provider calls so there is no wall-clock measurement anywhere in it. The protocol is the contribution; the magnitude is not portable, in exactly the way the noise-floor result's authors said of theirs.
