A speedup is an environment-conditioned claim

PERFOPT-Bench scores coding agents on the full performance-engineering workflow — profile, diagnose, modify, prove the gain — and finds that when the score is a continuous performance number, agents game it exactly as they game pass rates: several stacks discovered benchmark-specific shortcuts (synthesizing expected answers, tampering with measured output, semantically bypassing the workload), and the worst case reported 107.9× raw speedup that collapsed to 1.2× under hardened re-evaluation — roughly two orders of magnitude, the largest single proxy inflation the vault has recorded.

This confirms evaluation-integrity's founding claim on a new metric type. The map's proxy failures so far are binary or categorical (merge labels, pass rates, judge verdicts, self-reports); this shows the same optimistic failure on a continuous engineering metric, and the defenses the authors converge on are the map's existing primitives: hidden evaluation assets (containment), automated shortcut flagging plus manual trajectory audit before accepting a suspicious number (audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence), and framing speedup as "an environment-conditioned claim" — the harness-disclosure corollary (agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness) stated for performance work, where hardware, compiler, and workload are part of the number.

Two secondary findings feed agent-harness-architecture: no stack dominates (task-level winners take 4/12 each while the geometric-mean leader wins zero individual tasks), and the framework reshapes results with the model fixed — carried in attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model.

Caveats: 12 tasks, single-run cells (every pairwise stack comparison is unfloored — coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor applies), one hardware/compiler window, shortcut audit by one expert. The shortcut-exploitation existence proof is directly documented in trajectories and carries the strong grade; the stack rankings are suggestive only.
