What does "resume" mean, and can you check it

This map's headline is that the durable unit should be externalized run state rather than the process, and it is attested at five layers: a replayable event log, repo artifacts plus git, a daemon, a durable-execution engine, and a work queue derived from disk state. Every one of those attestations is an architecture claim from an operator who ships it. None of them is a conformance claim, and this is the first source to notice the difference.

The paper's framing is the useful part. A persistence plane makes one promise — a run can stop, for a human approval or a crash or a preemption, and then continue — and that promise is only meaningful once you say what happens to effects that already fired. Network protocols and file systems have had explicit contracts, machine-checked models and conformance suites for decades. The agent frameworks that now gate payments, messages and file writes have none of the three.

The gap is incoherence, not absence. Three frameworks state three incompatible answers to "does completed work re-execute on resume": CrewAI's checkpointing claims exactly-once, LlamaIndex Workflows documents at-least-once and tells users to make preceding work safe to re-execute, LangGraph memoizes completed task results. A developer porting a side-effecting workflow across them crosses regimes with no type error and no property to consult. And two of the three do not satisfy the semantics they state.

The finding that reaches furthest is EO's split by path. LangGraph is exactly-once across interrupts and at-least-once across crashes on one API. Not a documented weakening — the framework's own pending-writes documentation says completed nodes' writes are stored precisely so that on resume "you don't re-run the successful nodes," and the measured behaviour re-runs the successful task whose write is durable. The exhaustive kill-point sweep removes the objection that the authors chose a lucky barrier: replace chosen barriers with every barrier, and all four incomplete durable prefixes of a five-operation protocol license re-execution of completed effect-bearing work. This vault has been treating crash-resumption as the property the durable-state pattern buys and pause-safety as the property it does not (durable-execution-engines-do-not-supply-barrier-semantics, and the disk-state work queue in build-the-work-queue-from-disk-state, which buys crash-resumption and none of pause-safety). The partition was too generous. On the framework most teams reach for, crash-resumption is the arm that fails.

The CrewAI cell is the one to internalize if you only keep one. A completed effect-bearing method re-executes, the external ledger reads 2 — and the framework's own state counter reads 11, numerically the exactly-once answer, because state was rebuilt from initial values rather than resumed. If the method charges a card, the card is charged twice while the flow's state looks correct. That is why the harness's oracle is an on-disk external ledger and not the framework's state, and it is the operational rule: an unattended fleet cannot audit its own duplication from inside the framework's state. You need a side-channel record of effects, or you will not know.

pydantic-graph supplies the liveness argument's deployed witness. Every probed safety property passes and the point of the plane fails: after a crash inside a node, its own resume entry point refuses to restore the file it wrote. Crash between nodes restores fine. So the safe cells are safe by unrecoverability, and the unsurvivable location is precisely where a crash in a long-running agent step will land. Safety vacuous, progress dead — and 2.x deletes the persistence machinery outright.

The version-drift datum is the argument for the contract, not just for the fixes. LangGraph's fork and silent-persistence violations reproduce at every release across a year (1.0.5 → 1.2.9), while two other violations shipped as 1.1.x regressions and were repaired in 1.2.9. Absent a stated contract, semantics drift inside one framework between minor releases — so a team that audited its persistence layer once has audited a version, not a plane.

What to do with it. Three things, in order of cost. Keep an external effect ledger for anything non-idempotent, because two of the five frameworks hide duplicates in their own state. Know which regime your framework is in per path, not per framework, since exactly-once across interrupts says nothing about crashes on the same API. And treat the six properties as a checklist you can run: the harness is pure-Python protocol sequences with no LLM in the loop, which means this audit is reproducible against your own pinned versions rather than being a result you have to take on trust.

Bounds. Single independent author; artifact private pending publication. The paper explicitly disclaims prevalence rates, ecosystem representativeness, and any claim about properties it did not probe. Verdicts are conformance on the probed paths at pinned versions. What is proved is the impossibility and independence results; what is machine-checked is the reference model; what is measured is five frameworks on named paths with an external ledger as oracle — the paper is unusually careful about which label attaches to which claim, and the strength here rides on the measurement being deterministic and mechanically reproducible rather than on sample size.
