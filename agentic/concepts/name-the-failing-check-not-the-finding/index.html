Less information, more repair

This map's 2026-07-30 arrival put a placebo arm under the retry loop and found the entire informational payload of execution feedback statistically indistinguishable from a content-free failure notice, while seeing your own failed attempt cost 6–7 points (a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm, showing-a-failed-attempt-anchors-the-retry). The natural next question — is there any feedback content worth supplying — has not had a controlled answer. Here is one, on a different substrate, and it points the same way.

Five repair rungs are run against one resampling control, paired on the identical prefix and task, with the rollback real (a committed trace plus its seed rebuilds the conversation at step k; every step after is a fresh model call) and success graded by an oracle the repair prompt never sees. The ordering is the finding:

  • resample (rollback, fresh sample, nothing said) — 16%
  • located (name the failing check, supply no values) — 45%
  • generic (a bare re-check instruction) — 36%
  • specific (the finding, with values) — 36%
  • recompute (route it to the calculator it is already holding) — 28%, not significant

Naming which check failed beats naming what it found by nine points, and beats routing the step to the tool that would fix the arithmetic by seventeen. The paper flags this as the result it did not expect, and keeps the two negative rungs rather than dropping them.

The mechanism this vault would propose is its own. specific puts the finding — with values — back into the context, which is exactly the condition showing-a-failed-attempt-anchors-the-retry measured as harmful: with its own output in view a model regenerates a near-identical program in 33–68% of retries against 2–14% blind. located names the obligation and leaves the derivation to be redone. That is bind-evidence-to-the-state-it-describes and re-derive-file-state-instead-of-storing-snapshots arriving at the repair prompt: the pointer is re-derivable, the finding is a pinned reading, and the pinned reading is what anchors.

It also sharpens what this map's recovery-policy result was missing. recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder found the default post-failure action every harness ships — repair from the execution feedback — the worst of three fixed policies. specific is that default, measured against a cheaper sibling, and it loses. So the rule that has been forming across three sources gains its constructive half: do not show the agent its failure, show it its obligation.

Two engineering details make this immediately usable. The economics run the same direction as the quality: located costs 6.4 calls per recovery against the control's 14.7, so the better rung is also the cheaper one. And the net accounting is unusually clean — over 120 episodes, charging every policy for any correct run it broke, located lifts task success 52% → 73% with zero correct runs broken, because the deterministic checks that flag the runs never flagged an already-correct episode (a-deterministic-recheck-beats-a-learned-monitor-on-precision). That is the precondition the whole result rests on and it is worth stating as a dependency: a repair loop is safe to run automatically only where its trigger has a false-positive rate near zero. Trigger this policy off a 17%-false-alarm monitor instead and the net accounting inverts.

Bounds. n=55 wrong episodes, three repeats per cell, one substrate (tool-using agents with a recomputable stated total), 3B–8B local models plus one commercial API, single independent author. Five rungs against one control and the paper applies Bonferroni honestly: only located survives at α = 0.01, with generic and specific explicitly downgraded to suggestive. So the ordering among the middle rungs is not established — what is established is that the withheld-values pointer beats a blank retry, and that the two rungs supplying the most information (the finding with values, and the tool routing) are the two that do not survive.
