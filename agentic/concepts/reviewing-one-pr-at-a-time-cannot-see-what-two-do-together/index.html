The gate above the diff

Read this map's gate inventory as a list and the shared assumption becomes visible. Hold agent commits to the same CI as humans, or run minimal blocking gates and correct after. Compute diff coverage outside the agent. Grade the reproduction test against adversarial mutants of the current patch. Tier the codebase by risk and set an automation level per tier. Replay the agent's own mid-trajectory checks on the unfixed base. Every one of them evaluates one change against the trunk, and every one is sound only if safety composes — if each individually safe PR stays safe once its neighbours land.

BulkPR-Bench builds the case where it does not and measures what agents do with it. The task is queue-level: recover which pending PRs consequentially interact, return the largest safe subset, and order it executably under rolling release with hidden safety checks. Three of six models beat the strongest sequential baseline on the relation-group metric (66.6% / 62.0% / 57.9% against 53.1%), which is the encouraging half. The other half is that 226 of 324 runs (69.8%) ended with an unsafe merge, strict whole-queue delivery tops out at 13.0% with all three sequential baselines at 0.0%, and only 8 of 324 runs completed a queue exactly, with no model managing all three repeats of any single repository.

The diagnosis is unusually clean for a benchmark paper. Agents respect 98.1–100.0% of the relations they explicitly recorded and that were evaluable — told that two PRs interact, they behave. They recover only 35.2–57.7% of the consequential relations. Handing them the gold relation graph moves RDS to 77.5–99.1%, a gap of 22.3 to 50.2 points. The failure is enumeration, not obedience. (The authors flag that the gold-fed arm also changes the information horizon and the decision maker, so the gap is diagnostic rather than a causal recovery estimate — and that incomplete recall does not separate cleanly from subset selection, ordering or revision.)

That is the same shape as transfer-coverage-beats-agent-count-in-orchestration, where a deterministic simulator with worker capability held constant found orchestration quality predicted by whether the plan declared the required cross-agent handoff and almost not at all by agent count. Undeclared edges are where multi-change work breaks, whether the edges run between agents at planning time or between pull requests at merge time. It is also why this composes badly with the map's existing findings rather than sitting beside them: a suite that executes no changed line at all in 64.8% of Python agentic PRs (agent-written-tests-miss-the-lines-they-add) is not going to catch an interaction between changed lines in two different PRs.

One operational number is directly usable: visibility pays. Raising the batch the agent can see from K=1 to K=32 improved every model, four of six by at least 11.7 points. A merge queue that shows the agent one candidate at a time is withholding the input the decision needs. Deferral was the opposite — removing it helped three models and hurt three, from +3.7 to −16.6 — so that knob is model-specific and should be measured rather than assumed.

Bounds. The 581 candidates are one designed pool per snapshot and the authors state the relation mix does not represent natural queue frequencies, so the absolute rates describe the benchmark. The oracle maximizes cardinality under equal weights, omitting business value, urgency, review cost and harm severity — the largest safe subset is not the subset a team wants. Passing means satisfying pinned tests, hidden verifiers and the registered gold, not production correctness. All six backends share one scaffold, one prompt, one ledger, one arrival order and one turn budget, so by this vault's standard the ranking is over model–harness pairs (the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order).
