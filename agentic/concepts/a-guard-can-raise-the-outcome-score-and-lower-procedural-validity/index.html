Two instruments, same runs, opposite verdicts

The vault already holds an-output-check-cannot-see-a-skipped-step — an artifact test grades what was produced and is structurally blind to whether the procedure that establishes its validity ran. That note measured agents skipping their own mandated steps while producing passing artifacts. This is the same blindness with a deliberately installed control in the loop, and the control makes it worse.

Run three systems on the same telecom tasks. Score them the way the benchmark does — final database state plus natural-language assertions — and the action-local guard beats no guard, 0.325 against 0.250. Score the same runs against a hand-built rubric of what the policy actually orders (identify, diagnose before intervening, obtain consent, correct in order, verify at the end) and the guard is below no guard: 13.1% process-valid against 17.5%, with full-trace conformance dropping 35.4% → 23.9% among runs that passed.

The two instruments disagree in sign, on the same trajectories, about the same intervention.

A plausible mechanism, and the paper does not claim it. A gate that blocks a proposed mutation returns the agent to a state it must recover from, and the recovery path is re-derived rather than resumed — so a late-firing check can push an agent through more improvised procedure than it would otherwise have executed. The paper offers the enforcement-point account rather than this one; what it establishes is that the sign flip exists, not why.

What this map should take from it.

  • A final-state metric cannot audit a control whose purpose is procedural. Reporting Pass/resolve/success on a system with governance installed measures the governance's effect on outcomes and says nothing about whether the governance worked. These are not the same question and the vault has been letting one stand for the other.
  • The rubric had to be author-designed, which is the state of the field. The benchmark's own evaluators score no temporal conformance, so measuring it required hand-writing a task-conditioned checklist — and being author-designed is a limitation the authors name, since the party proposing the method also defined the instrument that favours it. Carried at suggestive for exactly that reason: one domain, one rubric, one team. The sign flip is nonetheless against two of the three systems the same team is comparing, and the loser on the rubric is the incumbent it is arguing against, not the unguarded control.
  • Condition-on-passing statistics hide it. Step-TCR — the fraction of applicable checks satisfied among outcome-passing traces — reads 86.4 / 85.7 / 94.5 and looks almost flat. The unconditioned process-valid rate reads 17.5 / 13.1 / 56.2. The same data, and only the second one is decision-relevant.

Sits with the-approval-artifact-never-recorded-that-anyone-judged (the record has no field for what was understood) and an-audit-line-must-resolve-to-the-write-it-used (the field was populated and useless): three independent findings that the artifacts governance leaves behind do not carry the information governance is assumed to have.
