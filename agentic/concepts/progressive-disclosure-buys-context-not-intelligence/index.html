Progressive disclosure buys context, not intelligence

The map's founding instruction is retrieve just-in-time (just-in-time-context-retrieval, dynamic-context-beats-static-preloading), refined twice since — a ~100-line table of contents beats a monolithic manual (agents-md-is-a-map-not-a-manual), and a graph-of-definitions index over live source beats agentic-grep at lower dollar-per-solve (structural-index-beats-grep-at-lower-cost). This is the first controlled test of the packaged-context pattern itself, and it supplies the scale condition the map never had.

The finding is conditional in both directions. On a single book, a flat Agent Skills pack lifts harnesses that navigate raw text poorly and adds nothing to one that already greps for the entities each question names — for that harness the pack buys controllability of the retrieval path, not accuracy. At twenty books the same bare agent that tied on one book cannot reliably find the target book at all, and the pack pulls 1.7× ahead on English open QA, the most robust effect in the study. The slogan is the authors' and it is worth keeping: progressive disclosure buys context, not intelligence.

Two corollaries. First, the harness is inside the result: the same intervention is worth double digits under one harness and zero under another with the model held constant, which is this map's independent arrival at the pairing thesis carried elsewhere by install-time-security-is-a-harness-model-pair-property, agent-reactive-bugs-need-both-components-to-explain, and attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model. A context-engineering result reported without naming the harness is not portable. Second, the economics point the same way as the accuracy: on the cost–accuracy plane the frontier recedes down and to the right as the corpus grows — accuracy falls and the cost of buying any fixed accuracy rises — so a larger corpus is strictly less efficient to read, and the frontier's top point shifts from raw to packaged only at K ≥ 10.

Caveats: the authors' own sharpest threat is a pre-training confound on the multiple-choice subset — canonical novels the models have likely memorized, with the raw agent answering correctly in too few tool calls to have read anything — so the open-QA subsets are the clean test, fortunately where the effect is largest. Gains are task- and language-specific (absent or negative on Chinese, where base-model capability sets the ceiling); the scale axis is sampled at K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20} so the crossover is bracketed, not located; one chunking recipe; several cells sit within one standard error; and the domain is narrative prose, not code, with no transfer claimed.
