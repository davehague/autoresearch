Persistence is not validation, and removing variance is not removing error

The map's within-agent limb opened with a repair loop destroying correct work under a noisy verifier (repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum). This is the same lesson applied to a mechanism nobody suspected, because it looks like the conservative choice. Multi-step agents lose track of which entity they are acting on; the obvious defense is to pin the first resolved binding and reuse it. It works — drift goes to zero. And workflow success also goes to zero, because the mechanism has no opinion about whether the thing it is faithfully preserving was ever right.

What makes this measurement worth its tier is the disjoint scoring. Drift (correct at step 1, wrong later) and propagation (wrong at step 1, carried forward) are scored on non-overlapping workflow sets, so a defense cannot buy one with the other and the trade is unambiguous rather than an artifact of aggregation. Most agent evaluations report one task-success number in which these two cancel; this one separates them and finds a defense that is excellent on one metric and catastrophic on the other.

Two structural details transfer better than the ratio. The amplification rises with model capability — 1.8× on the smallest backend, 8.5× on the largest — with the plausible mechanism being that stronger models treat carried workflow state as authoritative, exactly the property that becomes a liability when the state is wrong. Anyone who has been reassuring themselves that a frontier model will notice should read that column. And the dependent-chain pattern amplifies 16.6× because the failure becomes self-consistent: mis-resolve the account at step 1, and the step-4 lookup of "that account's owner" returns a perfectly coherent wrong person. There is no local signal. This is the reference-level twin of silent-errors-dominate-agent-bug-reports — no exception, no contradiction, just a correct operation on the wrong object.

The design conclusion is that persistence and re-derivation are answers to different questions and are not interchangeable. Carrying state forward addresses a retrieval failure; it cannot address a validity failure, and it converts one instance of the latter into as many wrong irreversible actions as the workflow has steps. The corollary the vault should carry into any harness that maintains session state: a state-preserving mechanism needs a re-derivation path before anything irreversible, and the price is known — one cheap independent call (an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself).

The dialogue-systems inversion is worth stating because it explains why so many agent frameworks shipped this bug. In dialogue state tracking, slot persistence across turns is correct behaviour, and a wrong fill is recoverable by re-prompting. In agentic tool use each resolution triggers an irreversible write, so the identical mechanism flips sign. Patterns inherited from conversational systems do not survive contact with side effects.

Caveats: entirely synthetic entity stores, 3–8 step workflows (real enterprise flows run 15–50 steps with concurrent record changes), all eight models served through one provider. The direction of the injection result is partly mechanistic — a mechanism built to carry bindings forward will carry a seeded wrong one — so the contributions are the magnitude, the 4.7× spread across models, and the per-pattern structure. One fixed injection phrasing at one fixed position; the system prompt tells agents not to "silently switch," which may inflate lock compliance; bootstrap CIs but no formal hypothesis tests; workflows within a pattern share templates.
