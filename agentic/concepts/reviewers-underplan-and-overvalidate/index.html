The phase that predicts success is the one nobody spends on

Three sources in eight days now report the same shape from different corners of the vault, and it is worth naming before it gets buried in the individual results. A verify-repair loop past round two destroys validity while acceptance keeps rising (repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum). Feeding a failure back for repair is the worst of three fixed recovery actions, at 27.5% (recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder). And here, validation effort — an agent re-opening the diff, re-reading the files, checking its own findings again — is characteristic of the reviews that fail. In all three, effort spent after the substantive work is weak or negative, and the lever sits earlier: verifier discrimination, action choice, planning.

The mechanism the paper proposes for its own case is that excessive validation signals weak initial grounding. The agent that did not establish where it was cannot re-check its way there, so heavy validation is a symptom being measured, not a treatment being applied. That reading is consistent with repository-grounding-buys-localization-not-judgment from the same study — if localization is the binding constraint, effort spent confirming a poorly-localized finding is spent on the wrong problem.

The practical asymmetry is stark: planning is under 10% of effort in every agent measured, and it is the only phase with a significant association with success in two of the three. The reviewer agents are over-exploring and under-planning. Note the honest limit — this is an association across trajectories, not a manipulation. Nobody has forced a reviewer agent to plan more and measured the outcome, and the causal story could run the other way (tasks the agent can plan well are the tasks it was going to succeed at). That experiment is cheap and unrun, and it is the one this finding argues for.

The cost column carries a second finding that belongs to agent-harness-architecture as much as here. Claude Code and Rovo Dev run the same backend model, land within 0.2 points of each other on overall performance, and differ roughly 7× in cost with opposite behavioural profiles — one exploration-centric, one validation-driven. Across the whole study the spread is ~100×. Same model, different harness, different price and different failure signature: the harness-model pair thesis arriving inside this map's own domain. It also means a reviewer-agent comparison that reports the model is naming the wrong component, and that "cost-normalized review metrics" — the paper's own call — is not a refinement but a prerequisite.

Caveats: phase labels come from keyword matching over message text, reasoning content and tool call descriptions; they are manually validated but the same words can serve different intents. CodeRabbit is excluded entirely because it exposes no trajectory. Significance holds for two of three agents, on 362 tasks, with no reported correction across the phase comparisons. Costs are list pricing at one moment for products that ship weekly.
