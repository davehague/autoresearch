A rule everyone ignores in unison scores exactly like a rule everyone follows

That sentence is the whole finding, and it names a defect in a metric this vault relies on in several places: inter-implementer agreement, inter-annotator agreement, panel consensus, self-consistency. All of them read a rate of concurrence and none of them can see why the concurrence happened.

The construction that fixes it is cheap. For every decision on the surface, label whether the artifact under test pins it, offers options, or is silent. Then cross that label with observed agreement in both the treated and the untreated arm. The full-minus-control delta separates "the artifact caused this agreement" from "they would have agreed anyway." It is a coverage denominator: agreement over the decisions the artifact actually speaks to, priced against a counterfactual.

Run it and the aggregate falls apart. On this surface, no pinned decision shows a measurable agreement gain over the no-specification baseline. Every one is either a shared default the artifact got for free — error codes at 100% agreement with or without it — or an outright compliance failure at 47% and 53%. And the one rule that offered options rather than deciding halved agreement, 89% → 52%. The high aggregate was real and meant nothing about the artifact.

The companion measurement turns this from an observation into a reporting requirement, and it is the part worth carrying furthest, because it caught a verdict already in the authors' own record. Compliance with one pinned rule across four versions is non-monotone — 13/26%, 33/2%, 57/20%, 50/3% — with both implementers complying best under the version logged as weaker. The revision that had been recorded internally as an improvement lowered divergence, and it lowered divergence because compliance fell too: the models agreed more by ignoring the rule in unison. Every agreement claim needs the compliance number beside it.

This generalises past specifications, and it is the missing control in several instruments this map already distrusts. It is the demand a-null-measured-once-is-a-point-estimate makes, aimed at concurrence rather than at effect size. It is the reason verifier-diversity-is-not-verifier-independence is true stated as a measurement rather than as a warning — a panel's agreement rate cannot distinguish shared reasoning from shared priors without an arm where the reasoning is absent. And it is the same shape as an-instrument-fed-from-the-same-source-as-the-expectation-cannot-contradict-it one level up: the referent and the thing being measured have a shared cause that the metric cannot see.

The authors are explicit that this is what converted a threat into a result. Knight–Leveson says independently developed versions can still fail together; LLM implementers sharing training distributions are a worse case of it. The mitigation was a non-neural baseline, an execution judge, and the coverage denominator — "which converts the threat into a measurement and is precisely what revealed the null." Not eliminated, and the residual is stated: independence was verified only as pairwise disagreement, with the panel's least-agreeing pair at 78%.
