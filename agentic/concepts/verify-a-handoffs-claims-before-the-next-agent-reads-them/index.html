Seven in ten "I reproduced the bug" claims did not reproduce the bug

This map has held since 2026-07-10 that an agent's self-assessment is not a completion criterion — the-agents-final-report-is-its-least-reliable-artifact, users-correct-agents-in-nine-of-ten-resolutions, audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence. Those concern a report a human reads at the end. This is the same failure inside a pipeline, where nobody reads it: one agent's claim becomes another agent's premise at machine speed, and the receiving model has no reason to doubt a document handed to it as established fact.

The census is unambiguous. Of 249 handoffs claiming a verified reproduction, replaying the claimed command against the unpatched repository found 50 genuine (20%) and 174 demonstrably false (70%). The remaining fraction is unresolvable rather than confirmed. And overclaiming is correlated with difficulty, which is the property that makes it dangerous: handoffs produced after the searcher exhausted its turn budget were genuine 9% of the time against 22% for spontaneous ones, so the misinformation is densest on the tasks where the receiving model most needs help. The same asymmetry appeared between settings — 32% genuine during calibration on easier fresh tasks, 20% on the harder benchmark.

The remedy is deterministic and belongs upstream of the model. A sandbox replays the claimed reproduction; a claim that does not genuinely fail is stripped, along with its test file; a claim that does reproduce is materialized so the fixer can use it directly. No model sits in that decision. That is the same primitive gate-the-edit-on-evidence-observed-not-claimed applies to an agent's own trajectory, and the same shape as recheck-the-action-at-the-dispatch-boundary — a check evaluated at the boundary where one component's output becomes another's input, decided from recorded execution rather than from narration. It also sharpens what a-verifier-sharing-the-interpretation-shares-the-error leaves open: this gate does not adjudicate whether the searcher's judgment was good, only whether its factual claim replays, and that much is decidable without a model and without the shared-interpretation failure.

The design decision worth copying is when it was built. The false-claim rate was measured during calibration on fresh tasks, before any benchmark contact, and the gate was a direct consequence. Nobody had to suspect the searcher in advance; they had to instrument the handoff and look.

Bounds. One system, one benchmark, one searcher — a 7B model deliberately tuned for willingness to commit (sampling at temperature 0.9 raises its emission rate 3.4× at an 18% cost in per-handoff recall), which is a configuration chosen to trade precision for coverage and will overclaim more than a conservative one. The 70%-false figure is a property of that searcher, not a general rate for agent handoffs. Critically, no pass-through arm was run: nobody measured what the fixers would have scored on unverified handoffs, so the guard's contribution to the headline is an inference from the census rather than a measured effect, and the authors list that ablation as future work. The claim class here is narrow and unusually checkable — "this command fails on this repository" — and nothing establishes that the rest of a handoff (implicated files, diagnostic notes, dead ends) is verifiable the same way.
