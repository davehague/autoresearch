Cheaper inference relocates cost into repair labor

Every optimization in this map so far saves tokens while holding quality roughly constant, measured inside the task — SeqRoute's model allocation (route-the-session-not-the-query), SCATE's stopping rule (learn-when-to-stop-not-just-which-model), tool-surface reduction (restrict-the-tool-surface-to-cut-cost-not-capability). None of them looks at what happens downstream. This case does, and finds the savings partly re-spent on repair: the share of commits that were defect repairs rose 45.9% → 74.9%, and the share representing net-new logic fell 54.1% → 25.1%.

The statistics are handled better than the design deserves. Because the two periods differed in task difficulty (and the harder period was the one with the lower repair rate — a confound running against the conclusion), the authors report a Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio stratified by difficulty: 3.61 [2.46, 5.30], with per-tier ratios of 4.88 / 2.63 / 3.81, so the premium holds inside every tier rather than emerging from task mix. Behavioural indicators mined from commit timestamps all degrade together: debugging-spiral share 35.0% → 69.8%, longest uninterrupted repair run 18 → 58 commits, median inter-commit interval 5.9 → 12.7 min.

Priced with symmetric labor assumptions ($35/hr, 15 min per fix, applied identically to both arms), the cheap configuration still wins on total cost of ownership — 40.1% saved under shared GPU allocation, though 43.8% more expensive under dedicated reservation, and a four-axis sensitivity sweep keeps the shared-allocation saving positive across a 9%–56% range. So the claim is not "cheap models don't pay." It is that the ledger has to extend past the inference bill to close honestly, and this map has had no source that did.

The replay result is the most transferable part. Repricing the pooled 613 commits under four routing policies yields a monotonic frontier — Pure local $7,388 at 73.4% FCR → high-difficulty-to-API $8,412 at 64.6% → high+medium-to-API $9,890 at 57.9% → pure API $11,419 at 49.1% — with pairwise disjoint bootstrap intervals and the ordering stable in all 10,000 resamples. No policy sending any tier to the local model matches the pure-API defect profile, because the local model's repair rate exceeds the frontier model's even on the easiest tier. That answers, in the negative and for one setting, this map's open question about whether routing buys a free lunch.

Caveats — heavy. n=1 developer, non-randomized, sequential periods, and a self-study (the developer analyzed is the first author). Fix Commit Ratio is a regex over commit messages, reviewed by a rater explicitly not blind to the hypothesis, with no kappa; the authors call it an upper bound and note Period A's fixes were 41.6% test/CI maintenance. Days and commits from one developer are not independent replicates. No quantization ablation, so quantization damage cannot be separated from base-model capability — and the authors state the comparison should be read at the level of deployable harness+model bundles, not base models. No data release. Graded T3; cite the structure and the frontier's shape, not the magnitudes.
