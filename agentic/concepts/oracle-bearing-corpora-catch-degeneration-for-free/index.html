Oracle-bearing corpora catch their own degeneration for free

Two designs are used to build the "hallucinated" half of an LLM-as-judge bias corpus, and they sit on opposite sides of software engineering's test oracle problem. A mechanical-perturbation design derives each negative from a gold answer by a deterministic transform — an entity swap, a number change, a negation — so every item ships with its own oracle: the gold-to-negative relation is a metamorphic invariant, checkable in O(1) with no semantic judgment. An LLM-generated-negative design samples the negative from a model prompted to "produce a fluent but incorrect response," so there is no transform to invert and no relation to check — a generation that failed silently (too short, truncated mid-sentence) is surface-identical to one that is a genuinely short but well-formed hallucination, and telling them apart is exactly the expensive semantic act an oracle is meant to spare an analyst.

The paper makes this a demonstration rather than an assertion: it injects the closest mechanical analogue of its own generation fault (a silent no-op — the perturbation script skips its substitution and emits a negative identical to its gold) into a minimal perturbation-based corpus, at a 30% target rate via independent per-item Bernoulli draws (20 of 50 items realized as no-ops). A gold-to-negative string comparison — zero LLM calls, zero human reading — catches all 20 no-ops and confirms all 30 genuine perturbations, 100% accuracy, O(N) cost. The same fault class that survived four rounds of standard aggregate-robustness checking in the paper's own oracle-less pipeline (aggregate-robustness-checks-miss-stimulus-level-faults) is caught instantly here, because the design, not the diligence of the analyst, is what determines whether item integrity is checkable at all.

This names, for evaluation-integrity, an asymmetry the map has not carried before: not every misgrading proxy is equally auditable, and whether a synthetic corpus can be audited cheaply is a property fixed at construction time, before any bias measurement runs. The claim is narrow and the paper is explicit about its limits: it is not that mechanical-perturbation designs are better quality overall — a rule-built negative can be distributionally unnatural, cover only the perturbation types its authors implemented, and encode its own annotator biases into what counts as "wrong." The claim is only that their faults are findable while an LLM-generated negative's faults are not, absent the manual check in manually-read-raw-generations-before-trusting-bias-stats.

Caveats: the positive control exercises exactly one fault type (a silent no-op) in one small (N=50), purpose-built corpus on one source stream (Turkish wiki40b); the paper explicitly does not claim the asymmetry holds across other fault types, domains, or oracle-bearing corpus families, and names that sweep as future work. Single author, arXiv v1, no venue. Single-study strength.
