The third measurement of the same interior optimum

This map already carries two results saying that a repair loop has a peak and that running past it destroys work. True validity peaks at round 2 and falls below the no-repair baseline in six settings of eight, with 55% of correct plans repaired into incorrect ones (repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum); stopping on the sign of the marginal gain rather than on votes is worth +60.6 pp at 0.72 rounds instead of 5 (stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain). Both were measured on verify-and-repair over a produced artifact.

This is the same shape on a different object — corrective steering inside a live trajectory — and it is a cleaner experiment, because the two arms share the advisor, the executor, the benchmark and the advice format, and differ only in when the advisor is allowed to speak. Schedule-driven: 97.7–99.6% of instances, 3.25–7.38 interventions each. Detector-driven: 83.3–93.9%, 1.47–2.99. The detector-driven arm wins on every model by 2.27–5.30 points and costs less.

The reading that generalizes: in a loop with an advisor, the detector's job is not to find opportunities to intervene — the naive arm finds more of those — it is to suppress the interventions that would be issued into a healthy trajectory. Advice given to an agent that was not drifting is not neutral; it is a redirection with no error to correct, and it is where the periodic arm loses its points. That is showing-a-failed-attempt-anchors-the-retry pointed at guidance rather than at examples.

The re-planning baseline makes the same argument at a larger grain and loses harder: SAGE rewrites the plan and falls below the unmodified agent on two of three models, with 12–26 resolved-to-unresolved regressions against the triggered arm's 2–7. The severity of the intervention and its cost both track the damage.

The other half of the ablation is what keeps this from being an argument for doing nothing. Removing the advisor and keeping only the monitor's canned drift messages still beats vanilla substantially (25.00 / 18.94 / 54.17 against 21.76 / 13.17 / 52.50) while losing 3.4–9.5 points to the full system. So both halves pay, and the ordering is: a well-triggered model advisor > a well-triggered canned message > no intervention > an untriggered model advisor > a re-planner.

Caveats: three executors, two benchmarks, one harness, single runs per instance with no intervals; the cooldown (5 steps), blocking cap (5 consecutive) and stagnation threshold (7 steps) were all fitted to this harness's step distribution, so "how often is too often" is calibrated rather than derived.
