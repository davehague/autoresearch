The leaderboard model is not the production model

Run agents repeatedly on long tasks and the ranking scrambles: capability and reliability come apart. Frontier models suffer meltdowns on up to 19% of long runs — and the mechanism is unflattering to capability itself, since stronger models attempt more ambitious multi-step strategies, and ambition is a variance source. Software engineering degrades worst of the three domains measured (GDS 0.90 → 0.44).

This is a fifth independent proxy failure for evaluation-integrity, and it sits underneath the other four. rejection-labels-overstate-agent-error indicts the merge label. llm-judges-have-exploitable-biases indicts the judge. benchmark-scores-inflate-when-agents-can-retrieve-the-fix indicts the benchmark environment. constraint-violations-grow-as-agents-improve indicts the self-report. This one indicts pass@1, the quantity all four are ultimately trying to estimate. The map's thesis — every cheap proxy misgrades, and always optimistically — now reaches the base measurement, and the optimism has a named mechanism: a single attempt cannot see variance, and variance is what kills a long run.

Two agents at "70%" can be entirely different production assets — one decaying smoothly, one detonating on a fifth of long runs — and pass@1 assigns them the same number. That is the same distribution-blindness serial-workflows-collapse-in-production found when a benchmark-strong pipeline collapsed 100% → 20% under serial composition, and it is why long-horizon-failure-is-state-not-steps keeps being rediscovered: the failures live in the tail.

Operationally the Meltdown Onset Point is the useful artifact — it is a kill signal, not just a diagnostic. An unattended run past its MOP should be stopped and restarted rather than left to burn tokens (keep-the-agent-interruptible-and-reversible, unattended-operation), which turns this from an evaluation metric into a control-loop input, the same move localize-first-wrong-step-not-final-answer makes for trajectories.

Caveat: the four metrics (RDC, VAF, GDS, MOP) are proposed here and adopted nowhere else, so their construct validity rests on one paper; "meltdown" is the authors' own definition. The SWE figure is a domain slice of a general benchmark, not a SWE-bench result.
