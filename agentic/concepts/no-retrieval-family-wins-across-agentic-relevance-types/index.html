Different workflow signals need different inductive biases, and one aggregate number hides all of it

The four tasks are four relations between a query and the file an agent needs next, and the leaderboard splits along them. Structure wins where the connection is causal — a reproduced failure to the root-cause implementation file, where the visible stack frame is not the answer — and RepoMap takes trace2code on MRR while also taking the best budgeted context yield at 8K tokens overall, which is the metric that matters when the context window is the constraint. Semantics wins where the query exposes symbol or path cues. Small models win specific cells: jina-code-embeddings-0.5B, at a fraction of the parameters, takes comment2context MRR. And no single method leads MRR, Recall@20 and budgeted yield simultaneously.

Two sensitivity results matter more than the ranking.

Sample weighting is doing work. Four repositories carry 58.8% of the positive samples, Gin alone carries 88 of 345. Weight the 25 repositories equally and Qwen3-8B's Recall@20 lead disappears — its advantage was concentrated where the samples are. RepoMap falls hardest of all (Recall@20 0.6333 → 0.4619), which is the useful warning: a structural method's strength is a property of the repositories it was tuned against, and a corpus-imbalanced benchmark will hide that in one number. Reporting both views, as the authors do, is the discipline.

The complementarity is free. A post-hoc reciprocal-rank fusion over stored top-20 lists — no training, no new model, k = 60 — lifts overall MRR from 0.2296 to 0.2713 and pushes trace2code Recall@20 to 0.8795, above both of its inputs. Semantic and structural signals are not competing approximations of the same ordering; they are different orderings that happen to be right about different files. This is the retrieval-layer version of a pattern this vault keeps finding at the review layer, where running several narrowly-scoped reviewers beats one because they do not share blindspots (run-several-scoped-reviewers-not-one-mega-agent).

Two further notes. Abstention has a calibration gap: a threshold tuned with counterfactual wrong-repository controls in the mix looks useful and then fails to improve selective success on the 50 natural cases where a maintainer attributed the resolution to an upstream dependency or user error. Recognizing "this query does not match this repository" and recognizing "the answer is not in any repository" are different problems, and only the first is easy. And the map's existing structural-index finding needs its scope condition attached: structural-index-beats-grep-at-lower-cost holds here on budgeted yield and on causal-indirect retrieval, and does not hold on the repository-macro view.

Caveats: embeddings are run prompt-free and zero-shot with no query/passage prefixes, so these are not model-card-optimized configurations and the absolute numbers understate tuned deployments; hosted paid retrieval models appear only in a separate logged track, so the comparison is among open models plus one structural baseline. One provisional row (pplx-4B) carries a tokenizer caveat. The MRR margins at the top are narrow — 0.2379 / 0.2336 / 0.2267 — so the ordering is not resolved even where it is reported. Carried as suggestive for the leaderboard, strong only for the claim that the ranking is task- and weighting-dependent.
