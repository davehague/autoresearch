English got the interpreter before it got the comment syntax

If deletion-hazard-falls-because-the-rationale-decayed is right that the missing input is why, then supplying why at write time should stop the growth. It does, and the size of the effect is the reason to act on it: excess prompt size falls from +211.3% to +1.4% over 51 maintenance steps, at parity constraint satisfaction. The prompt settles at its minimum cover instead of diverging.

What a comment has to contain. This is where the result stops being obvious. The schema is: the failure that prompted the instruction, the hypothesis behind it, and how it has fared since — e.g. "task 0 failed with 'certain wording is not used the right number of times'; response uses 'wilderness' 2 times but requires exactly 5; d12 was passing so keeping that approach". Three ablations bound it. Comment-shaped noise lands inside the no-comment arm's confidence band — the gain is not from tokens, formatting or the ceremony of annotating. Narrative of attempts without their outcomes is the worst arm of all (+70.0%), worse than writing nothing, because it hands the next maintainer an unvalidated premise to extend. And within the schema, the two fields recording what actually happened carry the reduction; dropping the recurrence count alone costs 37% of it. The load-bearing content is outcome evidence, not intent.

The counter-intuitive one. Sweeping maintainer capability across three tiers, the uncommented arm's excess rises +67.7% → +571.9%. A more capable maintainer ratchets harder — it generates more plausible instructions per round and still cannot license a deletion — and at the top tier comments become a strict Pareto improvement on both size and satisfaction. This inverts the natural expectation that better agents need less scaffolding, and it is the reading that matters for anyone whose context files are maintained by agents rather than people, which is now most of them.

Enforced separation is what makes it a comment and not more prompt. The harness strips comments before the prompt reaches the executor, and rejects any instruction that cites a past failure. So the executor's input is strictly smaller in the treatment arm — this is not "add more context", it is persist-the-contract-not-the-conversation with a second channel for the audit trail. That distinction is why the result belongs on this map rather than being another point on the more-context curve, and why it composes with prevent-context-growth-upstream-not-downstream instead of competing with it.

Real prompts, real cost. Inverting WildIFEval to price the other side: 16 noisy instructions seeded into a prompt cost 24.1 pp of correctness on the true instructions already in it — this is context-is-a-finite-decaying-resource measured as interference rather than as window pressure. Comments recover +11.6 pp of that (50.4% → 62.0%, a 23.1% relative gain).

Bounds, and one operational warning. The controlled arm runs where retention is nearly free — covers of two or three instructions against a real median of 39, a 15-step horizon, one model as both maintainer and executor, mechanically verifiable constraints. It prices what excess costs and what a comment recovers; it does not show the ratchet persisting at real-file scale under this intervention. WildIFEval constraints are prose scored by an LLM judge, so every rate there is a rate under that judge; the contrasts reproduce under a second judge whose effect size is indistinguishable, but neither judge is ground truth. And the author's own ethics note is the operational rule: writing a comment removes nothing and is safe; acting on one is not. The protocol empties a non-trivial share of prompts and the uncommented arm scores higher on exactly those worlds — an operator who automates "delete any instruction whose rationale you can recover" will delete instructions whose rationale was real. Keep a human in the deletion path and hold safety-relevant instructions out of scope.
