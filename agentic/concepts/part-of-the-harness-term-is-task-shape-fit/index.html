Part of the harness term is task-shape fit, not harness quality

The third independent harness grid the vault has ingested, and the smallest — four models, two harnesses, one run per cell. Taken as a magnitude it adds little to the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order and no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models, which are better powered. Taken as a mechanism it supplies something neither of those has: an explanation for one swap.

Every model scores lower under Aider than under OpenCode, by up to 0.34 (GLM-4.6, 0.78 → 0.44), and the author's account is that Aider is a code-editing harness applied to an answer-writing task. The benchmark asks an agent to read a task file and write a structured solution file; a harness whose native loop is propose-a-diff-to-existing-source is being used outside the interaction shape it was built for. The failures are not format errors — they are overwhelmingly genuine wrong answers, so this is not a parsing artifact of the sort benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives warns about.

That decomposes the harness term usefully. Some of it is capability the scaffold adds or destroys, which is what this map's design bullets are about. Some of it is fit between the harness's native interaction shape and the task's — and that part is not a property of the harness at all, it is a property of the pairing with the workload. Which explains the otherwise puzzling result in no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models: harness orderings do not transfer across models there, and this says they will not transfer across benchmarks either, because a benchmark fixes a task shape and the shape is half the term. There is no leaderboard of harnesses to be had, in either direction.

The practical form is a sharpening of the map's standing rule. "Evaluate a candidate model on your own harness" (re-examine-the-harness-when-the-model-changes) is incomplete; the operative version is evaluate the pair on your own task shape, because a harness that wins on someone else's benchmark may be losing on theirs for a reason that does not apply to you — or winning for one that does not either.

A second, smaller observation from the same grid: under one harness the four accessible models span 0.66–0.93 against a reference frontier at 0.95–0.97, and the ordering tracks more than parameter count (two 32B-active models differ by 0.15). The author's framing is that evaluation is a deployment filter for the cheap, local or open models a practitioner tries first, not only a ranking of frontier agents — the tier where the spread is wide enough to matter and where nobody publishes.

Bounds. Single-study and weakly powered: one run per cell, four models, two harnesses, a single unaffiliated author, and the task-shape explanation is the author's reading rather than a manipulation — nobody ran the same harnesses on a code-editing task to complete the 2×2. Two OpenCode runs had infrastructure no-submission misses. The domain is electric power engineering, so the numbers are not portable; the decomposition is.
