Decompose the harness into separately liftable layers

The harness literature this vault carries all moves along one axis: search — evolve or discover whole harnesses and rank them by end-to-end pass rate (search-the-harness-with-raw-traces-not-summaries, evolve-the-harness-at-test-time-without-labels, evolve-the-harness-from-trajectory-observability). This is the orthogonal axis. Hold one harness fixed and ask what each internal layer contributes. The two are complementary and the framing is worth keeping: the unit of measurement, not the unit of optimization, is what changes.

The identifiability argument is the contribution. Production harnesses bundle belief tracking, action selection, reflection and revision into a single LLM-orchestrated prompt loop. In that regime per-layer marginals are not recoverable, because deleting any one role from the prompt also perturbs the others through the shared generation distribution — you cannot ablate one component of a joint sample. Attribution is well-posed only when each component is separately liftable. Their runtime enforces that structurally: typed state updated by patches, computed properties as total side-effect-free functions, actions declaring preconditions and effects, and a non-Turing-complete DSL so that toggling one layer's flag cannot reach into another's logic. That is enforce-invariants-not-implementations applied to the measuring apparatus rather than to the repo — and it is the constructive version of what benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives asks for when it demands per-component evaluation instead of one collapsed score.

Three things the decomposition buys that an end-to-end pass rate destroys:

An aggregate null resolves into two opposed regimes. Symbolic reflection scores Δ = −0.001 F1, which in any pass-rate table reads as "no effect." Lifted out, it is +0.140 to −0.148 F1 per board, sharply bimodal — revision rescues four boards and over-fires on three — and the authors' line is the useful one: "a mechanism that did nothing would produce a flat band at zero." The diagnosis follows immediately and is actionable: the preset library is calibrated for sustained ambiguity and misfires on transient confidence dips, so this is a trigger-calibration failure, not a structural failure of reflection. Carry this as a reading protocol for every null in the vault (aggregate-robustness-checks-miss-stimulus-level-faults, repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum, agent-improvement-distributions-can-be-bimodal).

LLM invocation rate becomes a measured dependent variable rather than a configured quota — 4.3% of turns, emerging from where the confidence gate opened. Most harness reporting states how often the model may be called; this states how often the structure actually needed it, which is the number that answers "what is the model still for."

Confounds surface instead of being absorbed. Win rate drops 74.1% → 57.4% when the reflection layer is installed with its gate off — a −16.7pp hole that an end-to-end number would have silently attributed to reflection. Because the layers are inspectable, the authors trace it to a simultaneous change in the question-budget policy and explicitly decline to read it as reflection's cost, publishing it as an open question. That is the disposition fix-the-eval-before-trusting-the-score and measure-whether-evidence-reconstructs-the-claim keep asking for.

Caveats: single domain (Battleship), n=54, and the runtime's declarative constraints are what make the ablation clean — which means the method transfers to harnesses someone is willing to rebuild this way, and not obviously to a prompt-orchestrated production loop. The authors name replication in a second domain with different action and observation geometry as the first item of future work. The approach is descriptive, not prescriptive: it tells you what your layers buy, not what to build.
