The judge decouples exactly where you would use it

This map's judge complaints have been about pressure: optimize against a judge and you find its blind spots (llm-judges-have-exploitable-biases); let a judge select a system's own improvements and the ceiling becomes the judge's (harness-self-evolution-is-capped-by-its-proxy-judge); validate a judge in both directions before you trust it (validate-the-judge-before-you-trust-the-migration). All of those describe a judge being worked against.

This is the passive case, and it is cheaper to observe. Nobody optimized anything. Three scoring components ran side by side on the same 1,206 (entry, task) pairs, and the two deterministic ones agree (Grade↔Pytest r = 0.85) while the judge sits at r = 0.18 against Grade and r = 0.13 against Pytest.

The conditional number is the finding. Restrict to tasks where the deterministic evaluators say the agent got at least partway — Grade ≥ 0.5, which is 81.6% of the population — and the Grade↔Judge correlation goes to r = −0.04. Inside the regime where a ranking metric has to discriminate, the judge is behaving independently of how well the task was done.

Why, mechanically

The judge is not producing a quality score. Its distribution is bimodal and saturated: 60.1% of verdicts land ≥ 0.8, 22.5% ≤ 0.5, and only 6.0% anywhere in the (0.5, 0.7) middle band, with 15.4% exactly zero. It is emitting pass / near-pass / hard-reject, and a three-valued signal read as a continuous one compresses the range any correlation could occupy before the content of the judgment matters at all. Anyone about to average judge scores across a suite should check this shape first — it is the control-the-data-draw-before-the-seed discipline of characterizing the distribution before computing a statistic, applied to a grader rather than a result.

The disagreement is also asymmetric and interpretable: 132 pairs score Grade ≥ 0.8 with Judge ≤ 0.4 against 97 in the other direction, and the over-penalized cluster is long-form generative output — cross-lingual fact-checks, contract-conflict analyses, prior-art briefs — where the judge overrides structural correctness on fluency, coherence or presentation grounds. Even conditional on Grade = 1.0, mean Judge is 0.70. The judge is not noisy about completion; it is scoring a different thing and being read as scoring completion.

The half that is not a criticism

The authors' own conclusion is the one to carry: the judge is not a suitable ranking metric on its own, and it is the only component sensitive to semantic degradations that pass every deterministic check. That is the constructive reading of the same decoupling — two axes, not one axis measured badly — and it is the empirical version of this map's standing corollary to pair every headline metric with a proxy that fails in a different direction. It also sharpens a-deterministic-prefilter-makes-a-model-gate-affordable: the deterministic layer is not merely the cheap filter in front of the judge, it is measuring a quantity the judge does not track.

Bounds. One benchmark, 67 tasks, and — the caveat with teeth — the judge model is never named, nor its decoding settings, so this is the behaviour of a judge under standardized prompts, not a calibrated statement about judges as a class. The r = 0.85 Grade↔Pytest figure is partly definitional, since Grade awards partial credit on the same structural elements Pytest verifies deterministically.
