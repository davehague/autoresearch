How to publish a zero

This map keeps demanding that a delta be measured against its null. The mirror-image problem is what to do when the null is what you found. The map already carries one half of it — a reported null needs the same scrutiny as a reported effect, because a task set may have no informative band (difficulty-bands-are-agent-specific) and an output check cannot see a skipped step (an-output-check-cannot-see-a-skipped-step). This is the other half: the reporting form.

The move is to decide, before collecting, what precision the planned sample can buy, and to make that the claim. Here the primary endpoint was anticipated to be zero-event, so the analysis plan fixed the equivalence margin at ±2U where U = 3.50% is the exact one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson limit at n = 84 — ±7.01 pp, "the resolution available at zero events given the planned sample size." The observed estimand is 0.000 with a simultaneous exact interval of ±4.34 pp, and the conclusion is equivalence within a pre-declared margin, not "no significant difference." The bounds are closed-form functions of the arm counts, derived by exploiting the estimand's algebraic structure so each one-sided bound depends on two arms rather than four, with Bonferroni across the two sides. None of that requires a fitted model — and the plan says so, recording that its prespecified mixed-effects logistic could not be fitted because with no outcome variation the coefficients are not identifiable. Declaring a planned analysis unfittable, in print, is the behaviour this map wants.

Three further practices ride along, all cheap.

An adversarial missingness table. Attrition is where a zero goes to die. The supplement recomputes every arm's rate under the worst case permitted by the attrition record — counting every invalid or superseded attempt as if it had been a violation — pushing the worst arm to 25.88% and showing that even then nothing in the retained logs contains a prohibited call. That is the same concern as scoreable-runs-are-a-separate-denominator, answered with a bound instead of a caveat.

Deterministic judges, human-audited. The primary outcome is read from the tool log and terminal state by a program judge, with final-answer prose contributing only to validity checks, so claims in text neither earn credit nor establish violations — audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence as a scoring rule. Two audited rounds of 40 trajectories agreed 40/40 on every field, and synthetic violation fixtures serve as positive controls, so the instrument is shown to be able to fire before a zero is reported from it.

Amendments frozen before the data are seen. Three deviations are logged, each frozen before the corresponding outcomes were viewed, including two whole-scenario reruns under a larger interaction budget that carried a pre-committed acceptance criterion and a pre-committed fallback to exclusion with recomputed margins.

And the reason to carry this as a model rather than a trophy: the same discipline surfaces the confound the design could not remove. Collection order was fixed — all low-effort trajectories of a scenario before its max-effort ones — so every effort contrast, including the secondary probing result, is entangled with collection time. The authors print it. A study that reports its own uncontrolled variable alongside a bounded null is telling you exactly how much of its claim to spend.

Bounds. One model family, one scenario suite, and an estimand explicitly defined within that fixed scenario set — the exact binomial bounds treat trajectories as independent Bernoulli trials conditional on the suite and do not generalize to an arbitrary population of scenarios. The protocol is the transferable part; the ±7.01 pp is a property of n = 84.
