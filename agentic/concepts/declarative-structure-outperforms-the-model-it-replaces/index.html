Declarative structure outperforms the model it replaces

agent-harness-architecture has been converging on a rule — where a well-defined mechanical operation exists, taking the model out of the path is the reliability win (enforce-invariants-not-implementations, patch-minimization-is-search-not-generation, prevent-context-growth-upstream-not-downstream). This is the cleanest measurement of it yet, because the heavy-lifting layer makes no model calls at all.

Four harness layers over one shared 500-particle MCMC belief backend, differing only in what sits on top. Adding declarative planning — hypothetical transition evaluation through a sim.next preview that ranks candidate actions by expected one-step posterior collapse, plus question timing under a declared budget — moves win rate from 50.0% to 74.1% with zero LLM calls. Under a pre-registered criterion (the single largest positive marginal to the primary metric), it is the unique heavy-lifting layer, and the only contrast in the whole decomposition whose Wilson 95% intervals do not overlap.

The comparison that gives it teeth is the LM-only arm. Captains that call the model on every turn with no posterior, no expected-information-gain scoring and no decision rule fall below the no-LLM baseline when they lack inference-time reasoning — and this holds up to 109B parameters (Llama-4-Scout: 0.353 F1, zero wins, against belief-only's 0.522 F1 and 50% win rate). The authors' conclusion is the quotable one: "model size is not the missing ingredient — inference-time reasoning is." A mid-reasoning model (gpt-5-mini) does reach the declarative planner's neighbourhood (77.8% vs 74.1%, overlapping intervals) — but buys at 100% LLM invocation what structure supplies at 0%, which is move-task-difficulty-from-the-model-to-the-harness with a price tag attached and a routing consequence (model-routing-token-economics, expose-a-cost-quality-dial-not-a-fixed-policy).

Where the model does enter, it enters thinly and ambiguously. L4 fires on 4.3% of turns, behind a gate whose four preconditions — confidence threshold, sustained low-confidence streak, cooldown counter, and a counterfactual preview clearing a minimum delta — are all runtime-computable without a model (give-agents-objectives-not-transitions, gate-actions-with-a-contextual-classifier-dial). It buys +0.005 F1 and the lowest win rate in the reflective family. That divergence is a real budget tension, not noise: LLM revisions sharpen local targeting while consuming turns from a finite per-game question budget, so they improve the metric they optimize and lose the game (stop-on-the-sign-of-the-marginal-gain, learn-when-to-stop-not-just-which-model).

This is an independent, code-released replication of the shape structure-outweighs-the-verification-step found in a vendor self-evaluation (+9.5pp of a +11.0pp total from scaffolding, +1.5pp from the verification loop) — same ordering, cleaner instrumentation, pre-specified metric.

The domain caveat is load-bearing and must travel with the number. Battleship is a probabilistic search problem where MCMC posteriors and one-step lookahead are an unusually natural fit; the authors say plainly that +24.1pp overstates what declarative planning recovers in less search-friendly domains. This is not software engineering and should not be carried as though it were. Further bounds: n=54 with wide intervals, and the authors' own 18-game versus 54-game divergence offered as a warning about small-n instability; the residual was measured against one locally served 9B model, so the bounded non-monotonic L4 effect may shift under a larger model — the sparse residual is a claim about this harness-model pair (agent-reactive-bugs-need-both-components-to-explain); and a substantial F1 gap remains against the strongest published Bayesian configuration on this suite (0.764 vs 0.557), plausibly from language-informed belief construction, an axis this decomposition deliberately does not cover.
