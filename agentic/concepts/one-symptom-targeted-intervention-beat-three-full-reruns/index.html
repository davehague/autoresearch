The repair signal is a location, not a message

This map spent 2026-08-23 on the content of a repair signal and found the content mostly does not matter: varying one part of a failure message at a time, the diagnosis is worth nothing and the list of other tools to call carries the whole effect (execution-feedback-beat-a-specification-patch-at-a-fourteenth-the-cost priced the two sources against each other), while showing-a-failed-attempt-anchors-the-retry found that putting the failed attempt back in context costs accuracy independently of any diagnostic attached to it. Both results point at the same conclusion from opposite ends: the model does not need to be told what went wrong.

This adds the variable that does the work. Hold the information roughly constant and vary where the run resumes from, and the effect is large: 20.15% against 6.90%, achieved with one intervention against three full attempts. The comparison is deliberately stacked against the winner — the task-level baselines get three chances at a lucky sample and stop at first success, the node-level method gets one shot — and it wins on every framework anyway.

The two control arms are what make this a finding about localization rather than about replay. Random-node and last-node interventions receive the identical single selective-replay budget and the identical machinery, and land at 3.73% and 1.31%. Selective replay is therefore not the mechanism; choosing the right node is. Last-node performing worst of everything tested is the sharpest version of the point: intervening at the visible symptom, which is what every self-correction loop does by construction, is the single worst place to intervene.

This is the loop-engineering complement to close-the-loop-from-attribution-to-rerun, which found attribution-plus-rerun repairing two to three times what symptom-level self-correction manages. Two independent groups, different benchmarks, different frameworks, same ratio. For a practitioner the design rule falls out directly: a repair loop needs an anchor policy, and the anchor should be the earliest node exhibiting a failure symptom, not the point where the failure became visible. That requires the harness to have recorded enough to resume from an arbitrary earlier node (replay-the-recorded-prefix-instead-of-resampling-the-run) — the capability, not the prompt, is the prerequisite.

The bound the authors put first, and it is real. Target selection and repair guidance are evaluated jointly, so the improvement cannot be attributed to either component alone — the symptom classification both picks the node and conditions the instruction. The random-node and last-node arms rule out plain replay, but they do not separate "resumed at the right place" from "resumed with a symptom-appropriate instruction". Anyone building on this should run the arm that holds the anchor fixed and strips the conditioning.

Further bounds. Long-horizon web and assistant tasks, three frameworks, one model at temperature zero. And the authors' own closing caution is the right one to carry: localized symptoms can guide an effective intervention without uniquely identifying a root cause. A 20% repair rate is not a claim that the cause was found — it is a claim that the intervention landed somewhere useful.
