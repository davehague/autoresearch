The largest source-code reading of harnesses yet says the convergence this map already found at N=3 is a field-wide property, not a coincidence of which three got picked

This map already holds a source-level convergence result at N=3 (three-harnesses-from-opposite-philosophies-landed-on-five-shapes): three harnesses built from opposing philosophies arrived at the same five architectural elements, with no element recurring as the same code — read as convergence of idea, not implementation. This source is a different design reading a larger sample: fixed taxonomy of seven subsystems applied a priori across eleven systems (not five shapes induced from two cases and checked against a third), roughly 4× the harnesses, and ~4M lines instead of three pinned commits. It does not re-run the N=3 study's specific five-element model, so it cannot directly confirm or refute that model's construct validity — the primary construct risk the N=3 paper flagged itself. What it does supply is the same direction of finding at a scale the smaller study could not reach: a structural absence (no general framework, no vector retrieval) that recurs across all eleven, independent of the specific architectural taxonomy used to read them.

The Twin Absences observation is the sharpest single fact here for this map's running argument that harness architecture is a property of the problem rather than of any one team's taste: eleven teams, evidently not coordinating, independently declined to import the general-purpose agentic frameworks the wider LLM-application industry treats as a default starting point, and independently declined vector-embedding retrieval in favor of deterministic search. That is a stronger convergence signal than the N=3 study's five shapes, because "nobody does X" across eleven independent implementations is harder to explain by diffusion or shared lineage than "these three converged on five shared elements," where the N=3 paper itself flagged partial non-independence (one case literally reuses another's provider catalogue).

The tool-to-platform periodization is new ground for this map: a dated claim (H1 2026) about where harness architecture is heading, evidenced by extensibility-surface convergence (skills ahead of MCP, 9/11 vs 8/11) and cross-pollination (a Claude Code plugin pattern reappearing verbatim in Codex; Omnigent orchestrating six-plus vendor harnesses). This is a claim about trajectory, which this map has tracked mostly through individual design-lever results; it has not previously carried a field-level periodization claim with a date attached.

Bounds

Excludes all benchmark-score comparison by design (self-reported scores judged inconsistent across vendors), so this is architectural taxonomy, not a performance claim — it says nothing about which of the eleven systems is better, only how they are built. Longitudinal claims (the tool-to-platform transition specifically) rest on 8 of 11 systems carrying both April and July snapshots; the other three enter cross-sectionally only. Source access is uneven across the eleven — Claude Code via a circulated snapshot rather than a public repository — and closed-source successors are excluded entirely, so the sample is not a clean random draw from "production coding harnesses" but the subset with source available to independent readers. The authors' own admission that their April landscape reading had shifted substantially by July is itself evidence that any single-snapshot structural study, this one included, has a shelf life shorter than the phenomenon it describes.
