A new dependent variable for the pair thesis, and it is the one operations cares about

This map's best-replicated claim is that a score belongs to the harness–model pair rather than to the model (attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model, no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models, the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order). Every measurement behind it so far has been of capability — resolve rate, speedup, detection rate — under a working provider. This is the first measurement of robustness, and it is the variable an operator running agents unattended actually loses sleep over: the API returned a 500, or a truncated reply, or a JSON body the tool-call parser could not read, and the run kept going.

The answer is that architecture decides. Across a 5 × 7 × 4 × 65 grid, task success falls anywhere from 0.87 to 49.66 points, and the ranking of who survives is stable: "MapCoder shows the highest Δpass@1 under every model on every dataset." Swapping the backbone across four providers does not reorder the systems. That invariance is what makes this an architecture claim rather than a model claim — and it is the cleanest form the pair thesis has taken, because here the model term is not merely smaller than the harness term, it is absent from the ordering.

The mechanism is legible enough to design against. A pipeline has no downstream stage that can repair its first stage, so MapCoder loses 83.87% to a fault on the first call and essentially nothing to the same fault on the third. An evolutionary loop that regenerates its own work absorbs faults wherever they land (EvoMAC: −1.61% to 6.45% across positions). A conversation pattern with a long tail of turns re-asks. A single-agent loop over 21.73 calls barely notices one bad response. The design lever is not "add retries" — it is where in the topology a single bad response is load-bearing.

Two second-order findings worth carrying. Persistent degradation is the regime that hurts (47.64–62.39% on the multi-agent systems) while intermittent faults at p = 0.3 are nearly free (−3.57% to 8.7%) — the systems recover from a bad response and do not recover from a bad provider. And the faults that cost most are the quiet ones: timeout-on-content 22.33–64.42%, empty-content 22.5–56.73%, against corrupt-content at −2.38% to 5.88%, because agents read past garbled characters without difficulty. What breaks an agent is an absence, not a corruption.

Caveats the authors state and this note inherits: one system per architectural pattern, so the pattern and the specific implementation are confounded — they read the ranking as "a pattern that holds consistently across the systems and models we test, not as a proven property of the architectural patterns themselves." All five were reimplemented on Google ADK, so absolute pass@1 is not comparable to the originals (Δpass@1 is, since both arms share the reimplementation). 65 configurations over 300 tasks leaves ≈4.6 triggered tasks per configuration, which is why some cells go slightly negative.
