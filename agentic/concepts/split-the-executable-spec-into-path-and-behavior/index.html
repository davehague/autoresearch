Split the executable spec into path and behavior

This map's newest clause is hold the spec outside the agent and re-present it at each decision it governs (track-the-spec-in-external-state, restate-the-checks-externally-at-completion), and it noted almost in passing that the spec in the study that produced it was executable — which is what made the failure visible at all (a-loaded-skill-is-not-a-retained-requirement). This is the first source to take executability as the design target and decompose it.

The decomposition is the contribution. Every piece of external-contract evidence the vault carries is behavioural — a fail-to-pass suite, twenty-four observable checks, a migration rulebook's output comparison — and all of it answers did the result behave correctly. The architecture spec asks a different question, does the intended path exist at all: CheckUnit (each required unit present), CheckRelation (adjacent units hold the intended relation), CheckDataFlow (required data states present between units), reported as localized violations naming the unit, relation or edge that broke. It is checkable before anything runs, on a repository where the units already exist to be found. That the architecture half alone is worth 4.1 points, and the behaviour half 6.7, says the two questions fail separately — and that their sum exceeds the joint 8.1 says they are partially redundant, not additive.

Two connections. The localized architecture violation is exactly the external anchor to re-derive from that an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself and reviewer-value-tracks-capability-headroom identify as the condition under which a cheap independent check wins — and here the check is mechanical, so its discrimination does not depend on a model's judgement at all. And it is a gate constructed from the requirement rather than inherited from the repository, which is a genuinely different position in ci-cd-for-agents's strictness argument, where the incumbent suite a-passing-suite-does-not-mean-a-tested-change executes no changed line at all in 64.8% of Python agentic PRs.

Caveats, and they are why this is carried as a design rather than a result: a system paper evaluated by its own authors against baselines they configured, with no preregistration, no seed variance and no confidence intervals on any contrast — and this vault's own finding is that single-run deltas of this size are wrong-signed 33–44% of the time (control-the-data-draw-before-the-seed). Resolved counts are 9/17/28 and 8. One primary model, one mini-model robustness split. Gains concentrate under ~4K LOC and thin out on Hard, so the long-horizon claim is weakest where the horizon is longest. And the checks steer the agent here; nobody has used them to admit or reject its work.
