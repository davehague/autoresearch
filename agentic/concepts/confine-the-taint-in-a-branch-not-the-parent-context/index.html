A fifth containment shape: let the agent look at something without keeping it

This map's containment vocabulary has been sandbox, egress allowlist, contextual classifier, human escalation (contain-at-the-environment-layer-first, gate-actions-with-a-contextual-classifier-dial) — four mechanisms that all act on a single trajectory, and all of which pay for safety in utility. Information-flow control has always been the principled version of the first two, and always had the same objection: label creep. Read one restricted record and dynamic taint tracking permanently elevates the whole context, revoking downstream tools for the rest of the task. The security is real; the agent is now useless.

Branching is the fix, and it is the mechanism worth carrying regardless of this particular engine. Inspect the restricted or unvetted data inside a disposable child trajectory seeded with a descended label. The descent folds into the child's label; the parent's is untouched by construction. Only a sanitized derivative — or an explicitly accepted return that is re-evaluated against the acquisition policy — merges back. The agent gets to look at the sensitive thing without the trajectory it still needs having to carry it.

The ablation is unusually clean for a governance result: same binary, same policy, same harness, --max-forks 0 as the only difference. Branching is worth 26, 16 and 18 points of utility on three models. The fourth is a null, and the authors report it as one — GPT-4o converts four branch-dependent episodes and loses four elsewhere to mediation overhead, netting zero. So the primitive requires a model capable of driving it; it is not a free harness upgrade.

Two properties distinguish this from the classifier layer the map already carries. First, no model is in the decision path. The check is a lattice operation over declared labels evaluated before dispatch, with the LLM explicitly policy-untrusted and the parent-preservation and merge-confinement results proved without any assumption about its behaviour. That is a strictly stronger version of the "dial, not a switch" claim: the dial exists — remedy plans, authorize and accept rulings, and models used them 32–50 times per arm — without anything judging content, so it cannot be paraphrased around the way an output-hygiene classifier can. Second, enforcement is prospective: the label descent is evaluated before the read executes, so instead of a silent failure the agent receives a structured, actionable remedy. That matters for a failure mode this map has measured directly — a gate that only ever says no gets approved through (permission-prompts-fail-open-under-fatigue).

Two boundaries to keep attached. Branching enforces trajectory isolation, not transactional rollback: an external network egress a child commits before being discarded cannot be reverted, so this buys confidentiality and buys nothing for pause-safety or effect-undo (keep-the-agent-interruptible-and-reversible). And enforcement is still not free — utility falls on 5, 10 and 8 of 39 episodes against the unguarded baseline for three of four models. The claim is that branching recovers most of what taint tracking forfeits, not that governance became costless.

Caveats: vendor-authored on the vendor's own engine, and bench-corp is synthetic and purpose-built to probe these primitives — three scenarios are structurally unwinnable without branching and six are designed to isolate distinctions the comparison baseline cannot express, so the Fides arm is explicitly not feature-equivalent (its ASR is a constant 12/42 on all four models, a deterministic policy mismatch rather than model-dependent susceptibility). 14 scenarios, 3 repetitions, per-arm results varying by up to three episodes; one model excluded post hoc for retry-dominated traces. Sanitizer correctness and registered transformations sit inside the trusted computing base. No latency, token-overhead, policy-authoring-ergonomics or approval-fatigue measurement.
