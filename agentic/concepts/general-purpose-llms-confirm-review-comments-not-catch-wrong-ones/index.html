The Verdict-Diagnosis gap has a comment-level twin: a review comment can be fluent, specific, and wrong, and every tested model is calibrated to believe it

This map's sub-thread (1) already carries the Verdict-Diagnosis gap at the level of a whole PR review — a verifier can correctly block a malicious PR while never identifying the actual vulnerability (verdict-diagnosis-gap-security-review). This source measures the same asymmetry one layer down, at the level of a single generated review comment, and with a cleaner instrument: rather than scoring whether a verdict (block/approve) was right, CRJudgeBench scores whether the comment's underlying technical claim holds up against the repository's actual context. The result generalizes the Verdict-Diagnosis gap's shape rather than merely echoing it — every one of seven models tested, spanning five different vendors/families plus one specialized agentic judge, shows the identical direction and a comparable magnitude of asymmetry: comments are believed roughly 9 times out of 10 regardless of whether they are actually right, because the confirming case is easy (recall 87.77-98.69% across the board, barely varying by model) and the disconfirming case is hard (recall 2.31-20.77%, a much wider spread that tracks something closer to real capability differences). That consistency across models is the more decision- relevant finding than any single model's number: this isn't one weak model's blind spot, it's what "judge a plausible-sounding technical claim" currently costs any general-purpose LLM.

Sentinel's result is the map's clearest evidence yet on a standing question this map's reviewer-capability thread has been circling: is untrustworthy-comment detection a capability gap (more RL, better model, closes it) or a structural one (something about the task resists closing this way)? Sentinel starts from the same 30B base model as the weakest baseline, is trained specifically and only for repository-grounded evidence-gathering before judgment (two rounds of iterative action-level distillation from a privileged teacher), and moves untrustworthy recall from 2.31% to 37.69% — a real, substantial, specialized-training gain — and still misses more than three of every five untrustworthy comments. That is evidence for "structural, but not immovable": specialized training buys real ground without closing the gap, which is a sharper reading than either "capability alone fixes this" or "nothing helps."

Interaction edge: verifier-independence — Sentinel is architecturally a repository-grounded agentic judge evaluating another model's (or human's) output, the same object-class that map's independence thesis concerns. This source doesn't test independence directly (Sentinel's teacher, GPT-5.6-Sol, and some compared baselines may share model lineage with systems whose comments it would be asked to judge in a real deployment, a relationship the paper does not address), but it supplies that map with a fresh, concrete instance of a trained judge's calibration profile — heavily asymmetric, confirmation-biased by default, partially correctable by training — worth reading against that map's arrangement-not-identity thesis.

Scope: the paper credits a model as correct if it either matches the gold label or explicitly catches and repairs an error in the comment itself mid-trajectory — a scoring choice that is not separately ablated, so part of the reported gap between models could reflect general code-repair capability bleeding into the trustworthiness-judgment score rather than judgment capability in isolation. Seven repositories only, and the authors themselves flag generalization to more repositories/languages as future work.
