The rendering is a lossy, non-injective function of the thing you are being graded on

The argument is stated formally and it is the reason the result should transfer beyond desktops. A screenshot is a render map from program state to pixels, and that map is not injective: distinct states produce identical images. A cell displaying 1,240 may hold a literal or a formula; rows may be hidden or scrolled off; precision may be rounded away. So there is no inverse — no perceptual model, however good, can in general recover the state from the image. Meanwhile the deliverable is the state: whether the task succeeded is a predicate over files, application backends and saved settings, not over what the screen showed. Acting on the render is acting on a lossy proxy for the thing being graded, and per-step lossiness compounds across hundreds of dependent operations (long-horizon-failure-is-state-not-steps).

The authors bound the claim rather than universalizing it, which is what makes it usable. State-grounding helps on state-addressable subgoals, partly on hybrid ones, and not at all on render-only ones — image editing, layout, WYSIWYG appearance, dragging a canvas selection, dismissing a non-scriptable modal. Their per-capability results match: largest margins on multi-item state and conflict disambiguation, weakest on human-in-the-loop and multimodal editing. The visual channel is retained as a delegated specialist rather than removed, and the ablation shows why removing it entirely is worse than the baseline.

Two results here are about the harness rather than the domain. The pair is the unit. Same backbone, +6.3 binary points and roughly 9× cheaper from the scaffold alone; and a weaker backbone gains too, so it is not compensating for one model's quirk — a fifth dependent variable for attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model and the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order. And the scope statement is unusually honest: on the short-horizon benchmark the harness and the reference are within a point. A harness that does nothing until the horizon is long is exactly the distinction separate-durable-scaffolding-from-model-compensation asks authors to make, and almost nobody reports the null half.

The delegation results are worth keeping separately. Flat delegation beat both recursive variants (61.6 vs 57.6 / 57.9 mean partial), with the recursive branch firing on only 7 of 108 tasks — offered as a design choice, not a claim about hierarchy. And once the main agent carries the task on state, the rare visual fallback does not need a frontier model: substituting a compact 31B specialist barely moves four of five benchmarks despite that model being far weaker standalone. That is a routing shape this vault has seen from the other side (route-the-premium-model-to-the-governing-role) — premium where the work governs, cheap where it is peripheral.

Caveats: computer-use, not software engineering — the mechanism transfers, the magnitudes do not. One backbone for the headline plus one weaker-backbone check; vendor research on its own harness and its own compact GUI model; per-task trajectory data unavailable for two compared systems.
