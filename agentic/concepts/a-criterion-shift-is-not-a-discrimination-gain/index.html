The null that was never claimed, and the four disciplines that make the claim readable

This map's founding demand is report the null beside the effect. report-a-null-as-a-bounded-interval-not-a-failed-test sharpened it: a failed significance test is not a measurement of absence. Here is the cleanest instance the vault has of a study following that rule against its own interest, plus three procedural details worth stealing wholesale.

The decomposition. A verifier's false-alarm rate falling is ambiguous between two very different worlds — the checker got better at telling correct work from faulty work, or it simply became reluctant to flag. Distinguishing them requires the second arm: run every condition on labelled-incorrect traces too, get a detection rate, and combine into the signal-detection pair (d′ for discrimination, c for the criterion). The answer is unambiguous on one side: c moves toward flagging less in 15 of 15 model × wording cells and survives correction in 13. On the other side ∆d′ survives in 0 of 15.

And the paper refuses to convert that zero into a claim. Two reasons, both structural rather than rhetorical. The ∆d′ estimates are positive in 13 of 15, leaning the same way as the criterion. And because the two arms have no per-item pairing — 465 targets on one side, 929 on the other — each is resampled independently, so SE(∆d′) is exactly twice SE(∆c). A sensitivity effect must therefore be double the size of a criterion effect to clear the same bar; the observed median ratio of the two magnitudes is 1.85, i.e. just under the threshold where the design could have seen it. The stated conclusion is "no discrimination gain we can detect," which is the honest form and is a different sentence from "no discrimination gain."

That is a general lesson this map should carry past this paper: an unpaired second arm silently halves your power on the term that arm exists to estimate. Any two-arm design where one measurement cannot be matched item-for-item to the other inherits this, and the fix is to report the ratio of standard errors rather than the two p-values side by side, so a reader can see which null was reachable.

Three further disciplines. (1) Cluster on the thing that is reused. Each prior-context cell draws 465 targets from a pool of 50 frozen episodes, so two targets sharing an episode are not independent; every interval is a cluster bootstrap over episodes, putting the effective sample nearer 50 than 465 and widening the headline by a median 14% — with calibration verified by simulation at the study's own geometry (0.94 coverage against a nominal 0.95), and per-item intervals persisted alongside the clustered ones so the difference is auditable rather than asserted. (2) One significance criterion. Every reported p is the achieved significance level of the interval printed beside it, so the two cannot disagree — which removes a whole class of "significant but the interval crosses zero" reading. (3) Declare the families in code. Holm-Bonferroni membership is fixed in the analysis program before any p-value is visible, corrected per wording over the same declared membership so cross-wording summaries are counts and never p-values, and the analysis prints a warning if a declared family is underpopulated — because a family missing members tests its survivors at α/k with a smaller k, which is more lenient than declared. That warning is the sharpest single implementation detail here: it catches the failure mode where multiplicity correction silently becomes weaker than advertised, which is the exact hazard correct-a-transfer-grid-for-multiplicity-before-reading-it documents from the other end.

The selection disclosure belongs in this note too. Models entered the study through a pre-specified instrument-sensitivity screen asking whether their dependent variable responds to explicit framing at all, in either direction — on the stated ground that a dependent variable that does not move cannot inform a null. Two of five candidates failed it, and the paper prints their headline contrasts anyway so the selection can be judged rather than taken on trust; on one, the contrast is significant and reversed. The reported direction is therefore explicitly a property of framing-responsive verifiers rather than of every model tried. Note also the asymmetry the authors flag against themselves: on one retained model a downward prime does not resolve, so a downward null on that model is weak evidence, and they report measured effects instead of claiming a detectability bound.

What it costs. Nothing here is expensive — the second arm is the same conditions on a second labelled set, the cluster bootstrap is a resampling choice, the family declaration is a few lines. What it buys is a result whose limits are legible, which is the property most of this map's ingested deltas do not have.
