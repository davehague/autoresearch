The accuracy gain from LLM cascades comes entirely from going from one model to two — a third or fourth escalation tier is, on average, decorative

This sole-author decision-theoretic study supplies model-routing-token-economics's cascade/ escalation sub-thread with a clean boundary condition that complements, rather than restates, its existing recovery-routing evidence (28% of recoverable failures cheap-only, 45% escalation-only, 27% both; a learned recovery router beating always-escalate at a fraction of its cost). That evidence established escalation has real value in failure-recovery; this source establishes a depth limit on escalation's value in the base case: two-model cascades beat the best single model by 2.1-8.2 points of mean accuracy across 8 models x 5 benchmarks x 9 deferral-scoring methods, but adding a third or fourth model improves nothing in 118 of 135 depth comparisons, with the single largest observed depth gain anywhere at +0.43 points.

The paper's own diagnosis, backed by counterfactual experiments with synthetic confidence scores, locates the cause in what a deferral score actually carries: depth helps only when a model's confidence signal separates query difficulty in a way that transfers to the next model in the chain, not merely within the current model's own correct/incorrect split. Most production deferral scores (self-reported confidence, output-probability thresholds) carry the within-model signal and not the cross-model one — which is exactly why "add another escalation tier" is the default production instinct this result argues against on average, though the paper is explicit that a practitioner optimizing for one fixed budget point, rather than budget-averaged accuracy, might still find a deeper cascade wins at that specific point.

Correction to secondary coverage: some aggregator summaries of this paper claim it shows "a pre-generation router beats the best cascade policy on four of five datasets." The paper's own text contains no such comparison — every reported result compares cascades against each other or against single-model selection, never against a pre-generation router. That claim does not belong to this source and should not be attributed to it.

Bounds the author states: results are specific to deterministic threshold cascades deferring on each model's own output score; adaptive routing and router-cascade hybrids are untested; one pool of 8 models and 5 binary-correctness benchmarks may not generalize to other pools or continuous-quality tasks; the synthetic confidence-score construction used in the counterfactual experiments is one specific construction among several that could be tried.
