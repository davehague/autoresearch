A pre-execution quality signal for skills exists and beats the obvious cheap proxies -- but it is an LLM judging LLM-interpretable text, and this map has repeatedly found exactly that proxy shape capped or miscalibrated elsewhere

EngramBench (skill-library-raises-efficiency-not-correctness-ceiling) established that skill-bank curation quality matters more than skill-bank size or history volume, hand-curating 368 raw packages down to 20 and finding that more aggressive curation (stripping 70% of source history) produced the highest-correctness configuration tested. What EngramBench didn't supply is a way to do that curation without an expensive execution-based ablation sweep for every candidate skill — exactly the gap MCRI-Eval targets: a pre-execution, text-only signal that could in principle flag which skills in a 63,812-skill hub are worth including before paying for the trajectories to find out. The downstream validation is the stronger of this paper's two legs and lands squarely on this map's sub-thread (1) (does the search pay): against five cheap surface signals specifically chosen as the hardest comparison — including the one signal (text length) that does beat MCRI-Eval on one of three benchmarks (BigCodeBench) before vanishing on the other two — MCRI-Eval is the only method with a significant positive downstream association everywhere tested, and community popularity (the signal every informal "pick the top-starred skill" heuristic relies on) predicts almost nothing (rho as low as -0.1557).

The caveat this map needs to carry forward is in the instrument itself, not just in effect sizes. MCRI-Eval is a two-stage LLM judge scoring a skill's own text against a rubric, with no execution anywhere in the loop for either stage — the same proxy-judge shape this map has repeatedly found capped (harness-self-evolution-is-capped-by-its-proxy-judge) or outright miscalibrated (TTHE's judge: 50% committed vs. 64% oracle-over-seen vs. 70% oracle-over-all). Nothing here measures whether MCRI-Eval shares that failure mode — there is no comparison against an execution-grounded oracle score, only against popularity signals and other text-only baselines, which is a weaker check than this map's existing judge- calibration results would ask for before trusting the instrument to gate a self-evolving skill library unsupervised.

The ecosystem-correlation leg is honest about its own limits in a way worth preserving verbatim for this map: effect sizes are weak-to-moderate (rho mostly 0.13-0.33) even restricting to the most popular 1% of skills to reduce tail noise, and the authors state plainly that popularity and downstream performance are "distinct criteria" — a reader should not treat the ecosystem-correlation number as corroborating the downstream-prediction number; they are two separate, only loosely related validations, and the weaker one (popularity) is the one most informal skill-quality heuristics already implicitly chase.

Interaction edge: evaluation-integrity — a judge scoring artifacts it did not execute, validated partly against a social-proof signal (stars/downloads) that the authors themselves show is a weak predictor of the thing that actually matters (downstream performance), is a specific, nameable instance of that map's standing contamination/identifiability question: does the score describe the skill's actual usefulness, or something merely correlated with visibility.

Scope: full reproduction of all reported experiments is estimated by the authors at ~0.4 billion tokens, and the datasets/code/logs are not included in the preprint's source package, so independent replication is not currently possible from the paper alone; this is a new instrument from this paper's own authors with no independent replication yet, on this map's own repeated pattern for freshly-introduced self-evolution/skill- evaluation benchmarks (skill-library-raises-efficiency-not-correctness-ceiling carries the identical caveat about EngramBench).
