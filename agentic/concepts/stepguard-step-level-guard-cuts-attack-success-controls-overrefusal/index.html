This map's enforce-below-the-prompt argument gets a concrete, benchmarked instance of the classifier-layer rung — sized, trained and cost-priced, with its own over-refusal measured rather than assumed

This map's single standing argument is that runtime permission must be enforced below the prompt in layers (environment, classifier, human-last). StepGuard is a fully specified, empirically measured instance of the classifier rung specifically, distinct from the model-mediated permission reviewers this map has already found to invert under adversarial pressure (a-permission-reviewer-cannot-verify-the-authorization-it-reads, 13/13 attack objectives allowed because the reviewer read authorization off a harness-written label) and from the pre-commit gates this map has found spend nearly their whole error budget on over-refusal (the-gate-fails-by-refusing-authorized-work, 28.1% over-refusal against 1.0% under-refusal). StepGuard's own utility costs (1.0-18.1 points depending on benchmark) are a directly comparable over-refusal measurement, on the more favorable end of that spread for two of three benchmarks and worst exactly where this map's prior sources also found governance hardest — AgentHarm's fully malicious, harder-to-triage multi-step tasks.

Two design details extend rather than merely confirm the map's standing enumeration. First, StepGuard targets a granularity between the trajectory-level judges this map has mostly seen (too coarse to gate one call) and no runtime check — a per-step guard cheap enough (4B, 599.9ms/call, 7.24% of task time) to sit in the hot path. Second, the training method (StepGen's prefix-aligned contrastive branching plus Balance-GRPO's class-imbalance correction) is itself a transferable recipe: the ablation shows vanilla GRPO trains a worse-calibrated guard than one explicitly reweighted for the safe/unsafe class gap, narrowing the safe-unsafe accuracy gap 13.0->8.0 points while simultaneously raising deployed utility (85.3->90.7 on AgentDojo) — a rare case in this map's evidence where correcting a training-time bias improves both the safety and utility axes together rather than trading one for the other.

Interaction edge: this map's mcp-annotations-cannot-express-transactional-guarantees finding (primary map agent-harness-architecture) notes that current tool-interface annotations cannot reliably signal which actions are consequential — a limit on how precisely any guard, learned or specified, can target its checks. StepGuard's own guard model reads the full proposed action and trajectory context rather than relying on interface annotations, which sidesteps that specific limit but does not resolve it for guards that must operate at lower cost.

Scope: single underlying agent model for all dynamic evaluations (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B), so generalization of the attack-success reductions to other agent models under attack is untested; StepGen's synthetic data generation may inherit coverage gaps from its teacher model and risk taxonomy, per the authors; no independent replication yet.
