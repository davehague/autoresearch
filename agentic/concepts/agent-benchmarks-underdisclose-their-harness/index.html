Agent benchmarks underdisclose their harness

A pilot audit scored 12 canonical benchmark papers against a 5-field disclosure schema (benchmark identity, harness specification, inference settings, cost reporting, failure breakdown): agent benchmarks average 0.38/1.0 vs 0.66 for classical static benchmarks; 0 of 8 agent papers report inference cost; 0 of 8 fully specify the execution container. The framing worth keeping: even with perfect test-set hygiene, two papers running an under-specified harness on the same benchmark and model will disagree — contamination explains levels, under-specification explains spread. That names a second corruption channel beside benchmark-scores-inflate-when-agents-can-retrieve-the-fix, and unlike contamination, authors can fix it unilaterally by shipping a harness card (schema + codebook released).

This is the first measurement of how far the field sits from evaluation-integrity's "report the harness with the number" corollary — and the answer is that the median published agent score currently cannot be checked on the variable known to move results more than model identity: agent-harness-architecture carries an 8.3-point same-model scaffold swing (scaffold-topology-should-adapt-to-task-quality) and a 53-point grader/scaffold anecdote (fix-the-eval-before-trusting-the-score). An undisclosed harness is an undisclosed variable larger than most claimed model gaps. The 0/8 cost line feeds model-routing-token-economics: cost spans ~1000× at comparable quality (agent-cost-spans-orders-of-magnitude), and the published record gives a router nothing to route on.

Caveats: single auditor, single pass, no inter-rater reliability; 12 hand-picked papers; measures disclosure, not correctness (a fully-disclosed bad harness scores 1.0); the agent/classical gap may partly reflect field age. Weak T1 carried at single-study strength.
