Benchmarks conflate model and harness and penalize alternatives

A stance marker that names, from the benchmark-design side, a problem the vault has been assembling from data. Three misalignments: model-harness conflation (an end-to-end score collapses model, scaffold, tools, retrieval, and review, so no one can attribute a gain), single-reference-solution bias (grading against one gold patch penalizes correct alternative implementations — a grader false-negative), and no component-level signal (end-to-end scoring cannot tell you which component to fix). The proposed direction is per-component evaluation — verify one component while holding the rest of the harness constant.

The synthesis value is that it connects three vault threads that arrived separately as the positive form of their shared complaint: harness under-disclosure makes a score unfalsifiable (agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness), unmatched search budgets credit design with compute (charge-every-search-method-the-same-budget), and a result does not transfer across a component swap (agent-reactive-bugs-need-both-components-to-explain). All three say the end-to-end number is the wrong unit; this says so as a design principle and points at component-level evaluation as the fix.

The single-reference-solution point is the one genuinely new limb: it is a grader false-negative (a good solution scored as bad), the opposite sign from the map's dominant "cheap proxies misgrade optimistically" claim, and the same one-gold-answer brittleness the merge label imposes when a human grades against the implementation they expected (rejection-labels-overstate-agent-error).

Caveats: position paper, T3 — argument from premises, no benchmark, no numbers; the constructive half (named component verifiers) is directional in the available text; industry authorship, affiliations not stated; v2 2026-07-18. Moves no status.
