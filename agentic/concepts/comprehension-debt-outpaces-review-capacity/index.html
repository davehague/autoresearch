Comprehension debt outpaces review capacity

The authors' definition: "code is accepted before developers can explain, modify, and maintain it safely." Agreement is near the top of the study (82%, mean 4.09), and the quotes are unambiguous about mechanism. On volume: "Previously, a week's commits might contain a few hundred lines. Now… it is 10K or 20K lines. There is really no way to read it. People have started to give up" (P15). On where the cost moves: "This review burden should have been borne by the person upstream. Now it is all pushed onto the person downstream" (P16). On knowing deferral: "I know there is a landmine in the AI-generated code. It may eventually explode, but I have no choice — I have to keep moving forward" (P20).

This is the interior view of a ceiling this map already measured from outside. The enterprise 2× field study found human review coverage falling 89%→68%, substantive review 39%→21%, per-reviewer load doubling and being absorbed as silent approvals (automated-review-displaces-human-review-at-scale). "People have started to give up" is what a 21% substantive-review rate feels like to the people producing it, reported independently by practitioners who were not being measured. Method triangulation on a claim this map leans on heavily.

What the practitioner data adds is that the exits are not believed. The two proposed responses — return maintenance to AI (53.9%) and preserve regenerability rather than the artifact (67.1%, the "regenerative software" framing: "it becomes critical to have reliable AI infrastructure available to regenerate the code") — are the two lowest-rated practices in a study where most clear 75%. Both also sit badly with what this vault knows: returning maintenance to the AI routes repair through the agent whose self-assessment is the least reliable signal available (users-correct-agents-in-nine-of-ten-resolutions), and regenerability assumes the spec is complete enough to regenerate from, which emergent-specs-corrupt-the-artifact says it usually is not. Carry this as an open tension, not as guidance.

Caveats: self-reported, no inter-rater reliability (three authors clustered jointly, no kappa), 15 of 20 interviewees at big tech, Feb–May 2026 window; 55 of 80 survey respondents recruited via a paid panel; Likert items carry no confidence intervals.
