A metric that measures search, reported as if it measured reliability

This map's running catalogue of proxy failures now has a well-populated family: a proxy that grades something real, honestly, and is then read as a claim it never made. Resolved rate measures functional repair and gets reported as model progress (resolve-rate-gains-do-not-carry-quality-gains). Coverage predicts bug detection across models at r ≈ 0.86 and near zero within one, and the field applies it within (proxy-metrics-rank-models-not-suites). Resolution rate measures usefulness and is read as precision (resolution-rate-is-not-precision).

Add pass@k. It measures proposal search — did any revision in this trajectory land — and it is routinely read as loop quality. The two come apart cleanly and in the direction that flatters: over three forced revisions ever-correct climbs 3.3 points while current correctness drops 14.7 and recovers 2.0. A team reading only the discovery curve sees a loop that improves with depth. The same run, read at the state the orchestrator would actually submit, is destroying 16% of the correct work it found.

The paper's four-part reporting prescription is the transferable artefact:

  • Current versus ever-correct, so retention is visible beside discovery.
  • Common-state transition rates. Free-running trajectories carry post-treatment risk-set bias: evidence affects whether a revision reaches a correct state, so comparing later regressions conditions on an evidence-dependent event. Freezing one wrong and one correct program per task before assigning conditions, then branching identical states, is the fix — and it is the same discipline this map adopted on 2026-07-28 when a critic-loop study froze and shared its starting suite before comparing arms (adversarial-critic-rounds-add-kills-over-a-frozen-suite, coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor).
  • Coverage with risk, not risk alone. Risk alone rewards a policy that obtains zero observed error by rejecting nearly everything: the strict fresh-evidence gate reaches zero false completions and covers 38.4%, discarding 311 sound completions to get there. Abstention is part of the evaluation, not something silently dropped from the denominator (scoreable-runs-are-a-separate-denominator).
  • Sound completion — accepted and correct — as the quantity that prices that trade.

And one arithmetic discipline this map should adopt verbatim. When a gate produces zero observed errors, the bound depends entirely on your independent unit. Over 30 task clusters the conservative one-sided 95% bound is 9.5%. Treating 1,877 challenge-passing revisions as independent gives 0.16% — a sixty-fold overstatement of confidence, arrived at by counting repeated draws from the same task as separate evidence. This is the same error as reading a within-model coverage number as a suite ranking, and the same remedy: name the unit or the number is unfalsifiable. The study also demonstrates the partner discipline of reporting what its design could have detected — six pre-registered contrasts, Holm-corrected, of which exactly one survives, stated as such rather than buried (fix-the-eval-before-trusting-the-score, skewed-label-bases-inflate-classifier-headlines).

Caveats: the reporting prescription is derived from one study's design rather than validated against alternatives; the study's own absolute rates come from 30 HumanEval tasks and 27 tasks selected for observed repairability, so nothing here estimates population prevalence of correctness loss. The sound-completion figures come from replaying fixed trajectories against stopping rules, which shows what a policy would have intercepted in recorded output, not how enforcing it changes subsequent model behaviour — the paper runs a separate prospective arm precisely because replay cannot answer that.
