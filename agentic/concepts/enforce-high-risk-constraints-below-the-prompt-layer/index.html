Enforce high-risk constraints below the prompt layer

This map's central instruction — govern what an agent may do with deterministic layers rather than with instructions or approval prompts (contain-at-the-environment-layer-first, permission-prompts-fail-open-under-fatigue) — has been built almost entirely from vendor engineering posts and benchmarks. This is the first practitioner-side measurement, and it is the strongest-rated practice in the whole study at 80.8% effective, from people who ship agents and carry the consequences.

It arrives attached to an uncomfortable admission that makes it more credible rather than less. The challenge it answers is "safety lags behind performance" (74% agree): teams knowingly accept risks when safeguards constrain agent performance or efficiency. So the endorsement is not aspirational safety talk — it is a report from people who have already traded safety for speed and found that the constraints which survive that trade are the ones enforced below the layer the agent can talk its way past.

That is the same conclusion the vault reached from three other directions: instructions do not govern what nobody scores (8.3% compliance, instructions-fail-to-govern-non-functional-requirements); agent-facing rules must be enforceable rather than conventional (make-agent-facing-rules-enforceable-not-conventional); and approval gates fail structurally, not just under fatigue (sibling-leak-defeats-human-in-the-loop-approval-gates). Four methods — controlled benchmark, repository mining, framework probing, practitioner survey — one instruction.

It also sharpens a cost this map should carry: the same run's field data shows users proposing exactly these deterministic guardrails to agent maintainers and being declined 62% of the time (the-model-harness-boundary-is-an-accountability-boundary). The practice is rated most effective by the people building agents and is hardest to contribute to the agents you did not build.

Caveats: self-report of perceived effectiveness, not a measured catch rate — nobody here measured what the constraint layer actually blocked; no inter-rater reliability; big-tech-skewed sample; Feb–May 2026; 55 of 80 survey respondents via a paid panel.
