Decisive errors strike early and surface late

The largest manual anatomy of coding-agent failure (1,794 trajectories, 63k+ annotated steps, 7 frontier models × OpenHands/MiniSWE/Terminus2): the decisive error lands at a median of 7 steps into runs averaging 27, while the first observable failure signal arrives ~10 steps later — a lag window in which the run looks healthy and is already lost. 57.9% of decisive errors are epistemic — the agent misused information it already had (false premises alone: 30.7% of all failures) — vs 32.8% competence and 9.4% environment blockers.

This quantifies the exact gap observability's methods exist to close. localize-first-wrong-step-not-final-answer is worth precisely the 10-step signal lag; and the epistemic share means audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence bets on the right substrate — in most failures, the evidence needed to catch the error was already in the trajectory, so an in-loop claims-vs-evidence audit has both headroom (10 steps) and material (the agent's own context) to intervene before the thrash phase. It also flips the optimization target: 71% of successful runs also hit errors — recovery, not error avoidance, separates outcomes — so a harness should optimize the error→detection loop, not the error rate (retry-cost-is-an-architectural-property is the same conclusion from the cost side).

The false-premise dominance connects to underspecified-targets-drive-unsafe-actions: there the agent guesses because the instruction under-specifies; here it fails because it asserts a premise its own trajectory contradicts. Both are ask-vs-assert failures, and both argue for validating assumptions as a first-class harness step rather than trusting fluent forward motion.

Caveats: Terminal-Bench CLI tasks are short-horizon; phase timings may scale differently on repo-scale work. One team's taxonomy (κ 0.78–0.94 mitigates). v1, days old, unreviewed.
