Evaluation steers development rather than checking it

Two findings from the same study that have to be read together, because apart they are reassuring and together they are not. Teams have made evaluation the organizing mechanism of development — stage 2 of a seven-stage loop, before the system is built, with iteration steered by it ("Without evaluation, you are completely blind" — P17). And the signal doing the steering fails on four axes practitioners named unprompted:

  • Invalid or undefined. "The agent may actually produce a better solution, or the previous evaluation standard may already be outdated" (P16). "Defining objectives for software qualities that existing evaluations cannot capture, particularly non-functional properties" (P17).
  • Unstable. "With the same model, scaffold, and version, I can run it twice and obtain different scores. How do I know whether a failure was caused by randomness or a flaw in the scaffold design? The regression test itself is broken" (P17).
  • Quickly outdated. One team found public benchmarks optimized against to uselessness "within about a quarter" (P2). "The model scored highly … but produced terrible results because it had learned the scoring rule rather than the intended capability" (P14).
  • Prohibitively expensive. "People spend so much time building [the environment], but that effort is not visible. What you output is just a dataset and a score" (P5).

The first three are this map's existing content — proxy invalidity, run-to-run noise, and Goodharting — arriving from practitioners rather than benchmarks, which is convergent validity the map has not had. P17's instability complaint is the same quantity coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor formalizes: without a paired same-configuration floor, a delta is uninterpretable, and practitioners are hitting that wall in production regression suites.

The fourth is new to this map and changes the remedy. The map has treated bad measurement as an epistemic failure — wrong proxy, undisclosed harness, contaminated set. P5 and P17 add that trustworthy evaluation is expensive and organizationally invisible: the environment build is the work, a dataset and a score are the only artifacts, and per-commit evaluation is priced out. Some under-measurement is therefore a budgeting outcome, which means part of the fix is organizational (fund and credit the environment) rather than methodological. That reframes why the disclosure gap persists (agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness): nobody is paid for the part that would close it.

Caveats: self-report from builders (18 of 20 ship what they described); no inter-rater reliability; big-tech-skewed sample; Feb–May 2026; Likert items without CIs; paid-panel majority in the survey arm. Directional corroboration, not an effect size.
