Zero effect, unstable leaderboard — and the paper that made that mistake was this paper's own earlier version

The useful half of robust-within-a-family-is-not-identified-across-families is the general one about declared families. This is the specific, load-bearing corollary, and it is the one most likely to be quietly wrong in a paper you are reading right now.

The two symmetric terminal treatments agree perfectly on the contrast under audit: T(b) = 0 for every declared background, policy and stratum, intervals [0, 0]. If your robustness argument is "the effect is the same under both conventions," you are done. But the same two treatments disagree on absolute scores, on which method pairs reverse, and on the method order — and the reason is structural rather than a quirk of this dataset. A ranking is identified over F only when sign(μ̂_m(s) − μ̂_m′(s)), tie state included, is constant over every s ∈ F for every method pair. A single scalar contrast equalling zero imposes exactly none of those constraints.

The demonstration that this is not pedantry is in the study's own record. The original report of the same substrate carried a precise, correct, interval-excluding-zero effect and a claim of "global stability" — and had to publish a version-preserving correction addendum recording eight weak orders, 12 strict-reversal pairs and Kendall τ_b down to −0.0501 across 24 analyses. The stable effect was real. The stability of the ranking it was quoted as supporting was not. That is a retraction produced by asking one question rather than by finding an error, which is why the paper keeps the evidential role of each stage separate: premise, prediction-free diagnostic event, conditional result, correction, falsification, non-recovery.

The practical form is a sentence you can check in any evaluation you read. If the paper reports a treatment effect and then states or implies a leaderboard conclusion, the leaderboard conclusion needs its own identification argument over the same declared family. They are different endpoints and the paper says so explicitly — "we keep T and R separate: a contrast can vanish while score levels and rankings still change."

This lands directly on this map's live worry about compressed rank reporting (a-compressed-leaderboard-is-an-instrument-signal) and on removing-defective-instances-reshuffles-the-leaderboard, which is this failure observed from the eligibility side without the formalism. It also sharpens what a-null-measured-once-is-a-point-estimate asks for: a null reported over a family needs the family declared, and a null on the contrast does not license any statement about who is ahead.

Scope. One substrate, one diagnostic benchmark, one localized witness. The result is a non-implication — effect identification does not imply ranking identification — established by construction and demonstrated once, not a prevalence claim about how often published rankings are family-dependent.
