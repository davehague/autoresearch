Admission-time validation recovered the serial number, not a parallel one

This map's coordination limb has converged since 2026-07-12 on a mechanism rather than a topology: the tax is raw, unvalidated state routed through a serializing controller, and the arms that beat strong single agents — icat-agent's event passing, DeLM's verified-gist blackboard (ablation: −4.9 pts without admission-time verification), Anthropic's filesystem substrate gated on the compiler and the suite — all validate at admission. On 2026-07-25 the map wrote down the counterfactual none of them had run: the same discipline, one strong agent, serially. This study runs it as a fourth arm, and the treatment lands on top of it.

Static admission raised pair pass from 23.3% to 50.0%. Always serial scores 50.0%. Not approximately — the two arms have identical pair-pass rates within every repository-task cluster, which is why the paired difference and the aggregate difference are the same +26.7 pp. Integration success 96.7% against 95.6%. On the conflict-labelled half the gain is large (6.7% → 60.0%) and again exactly serial's. On the clean half the treatment is indistinguishable from doing nothing (40.0% both).

The mechanism claim survives; the value claim does not. The plane demonstrably prevented two independently plausible trajectories from coexisting where their declared mutation authority overlapped — that is the map's hypothesis working. What it did not do is generate better code, and the way it bought reliability was by serializing 87 of 90 executions, including 93.3% of the clean ones. Its conflict sensitivity is 100% and its clean specificity is 6.7%. Mean modelled critical path is 188.0 s, above always serial's 181.4 s, because it pays declaration overhead on a workload it then runs in order anyway. An arm that serializes almost everything and costs more than serial is not a coordination result; it is a correctly-implemented safety interlock.

The selective arm is where the open engineering problem now sits, and it is a scope-coverage problem, not a policy-design one. Dynamic admission reserved committed scope only and promoted contingent scope on demand. Its classification was good — 66.7% of conflict executions serialized against 13.3% of clean ones — and it still lost, because 46 of 90 executions failed closed on undeclared scope, taking pair pass to 22.2%, below unconstrained parallel. The anatomy rules out the obvious explanation: 45 of those 46 blocked mutations targeted a file that was already in the frozen declaration, merely outside the admitted line regions (15 of them within ten lines). One block was on a wholly undeclared file. Zero promotions were formally rejected. So the planner's file-level recall was high and its region-level coverage was not, and the runtime had no bounded amendment state between "already contingent" and "refuse." That is prospective-enforcement-is-only-as-complete-as-its-contracts arriving in the quality domain — a fail-closed plane is only as usable as the completeness of what was declared to it.

What this changes for reading the map's existing evidence: neither icat-agent nor DeLM reports an always-serial arm on its own benchmark, and this study shows that "beats unconstrained parallel" and "beats one agent working in order" come apart on the same 360 executions. The still-unrun three-armed sweep (raw pool / event passing / verified gist) now needs a fourth arm before its output means anything, and coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor tells it to run all four paired.

Bounds. Seven repository-task clusters over three Python project families is the entire statistical base; the three coder seeds are correlated observations and the author says so, which is why a +26.7 pp effect carries a [9.6, 60.0] interval. One planner model, one coder model, single unfunded author. CooperBench supplies oracle-localized initial context from gold-patch locations, suppressing exactly the retrieval variance that context-retrieval-and-acquisition says dominates real work. Provider calls were physically sequential, so there is no wall-clock measurement here at all — the latency numbers are a model, and the paper declines the speedup claim itself. Nothing here says a well-built selective plane cannot win; it says this one did not, and that the arm which did win won by not being parallel.
