The within-arm noise looked survivable; the delta's noise was twice as large and retired four of the paper's own results

This map's catalogue of missing nulls gains its most self-inflicted entry. The study ran one generation per experimental cell, carrying an unmeasured ±5 pp assumption, then measured the assumption and found it was wrong by a factor of three — on the quantity that actually matters.

The distinction is arithmetic and it is the whole finding. Within-arm spread across identical re-runs has a median of 8.3 pp in the treated arm and 8.0 pp in the control. Either number, quoted alone, makes a 12-point effect look comfortable. But the claim is treated − control, and the arm-delta median is 14.4 pp with a maximum of 32.7 pp. One decision axis changes sign between identical re-runs of the same configuration (+23.3, then −9.3). "The delta spread is what matters, because claims are differences and the difference of two noisy quantities is noisier than either."

The second half is the part that generalises furthest: the floor is per axis, not global. Applying each axis's own measured spread:

A larger effect dies and a smaller one lives, because they sit on axes with different noise. "Reporting a single global threshold would have gotten both of these backwards." That is the sharpest available argument for what report-the-delta-inside-a-difficulty-band and coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor have been circling: the noise floor is a property of the measured quantity, not of the study, and one number for the whole paper is a fifth of a measurement.

Two methodological details are worth stealing wholesale. Hold the unit set fixed across repeats. An earlier pass inflated the estimate to 35 pp by comparing one repeat on 25 units against another on the 12 that happened to finish — measuring the unit sets rather than the churn, an error the authors caught and reported rather than quietly fixing. And look for corroboration inside your own other arms: an unrelated experiment in the same study independently showed executability varying 12.9 pp across identical re-runs of the same arm, which is a free replication of the floor nobody had to pay for.

The reporting rule the authors derive is stated as a bound on the instrument, not on the object: under a three-repeat design, effects below ~8 pp at n=75 or ~14 pp at n=25 should not be read as robust without more repetitions. And the justification is the sentence this map should carry into every future arrival: "a benchmark that cannot bound its own noise cannot adjudicate anyone else's claims — including ours."

This is the same demand a-null-measured-once-is-a-point-estimate makes, arriving from the positive side — there, a null that was never re-measured; here, six positive effects re-measured against a floor that had been assumed. Four of them did not survive. And it is the sibling of report-specification-variation-separately-from-sampling-variation, which adds the axis this one does not touch: repetition measures stochastic variability and cannot measure definitional variability at all.

Scope, stated by the authors. Three repeats give an initial estimate of run-to-run variability without characterizing its distribution; effects in the ~8–15 pp range are exploratory. The subsample is 25 units, and the 1/√n heuristic used to project the floor to n=75 is flagged as a heuristic.
