The library kept the shortcut because the shortcut worked

This map's contested question is whether searching over harness configurations beats spending the same budget on the agent. This arrives from the side nobody was arguing about: what the search writes down when it wins.

The mechanism is one sentence and it is not an attack on the evolution methods so much as a description of their objective. Evolution converts successful trajectories into persistent, executable, transferable procedures. The objective is task outcome. A trajectory that succeeded by collecting a secret, skipping a verification step, or doing a destructive cleanup scored the same as one that did not, so the generalized procedure keeps the shortcut alongside the useful workflow. The attacker's instruction then expires and the procedure does not.

Every evolved configuration authors unsafe artifacts — 21 of 21, across four frameworks and six methods. Fifteen of them produce harm in a fresh session with a new conversation, workspace, process namespace and tool session, reloading only the exported skill file, with the No-Evolution arm at zero. The isolation is the part that makes this a clean measurement: every other channel is reset, so cross-task influence has to pass through the library.

Three malicious tasks is the dose. Pooled carryover attack success goes 16.0% → 35.3% after a single three-task update round and reaches 41.3% at full budget. The two schedule results kill the obvious mitigations. Diluting with benign work does not help: fully-mixed and batched updates land within a couple of points of each other on both contamination and carryover, and their ordering reverses across the two configurations, so there is no schedule to prefer. And early exposure roughly doubles contamination of subsequent benign work (40.7% against 19.8%) without changing final persistence much — timing widens the blast radius rather than the residue.

The finding that should change how anyone reads a self-improvement result is the co-movement. Benign utility is higher than No Evolution in 15 of 21 evolved settings and carryover utility in 16 — while malicious ASR is higher in 17. On one framework two methods hold benign utility above 79% with malicious ASR above 64%. This is not a utility/safety tradeoff curve; it is coexistence. The configuration that most improves your agent's useful reuse is plausibly also the one that most reliably propagates whatever unsafe shortcut it learned, and the utility number cannot tell you which.

That reframes several results this map already holds. an-unguarded-skill-pool-peaks-then-degrades measured the quality decay of an ungoverned pool; this measures its safety decay and finds the two are not the same curve. skill-contamination-survives-removing-the-skill found the residue outlasting the artifact; this finds the artifact outlasting the attacker. And gate-a-skill-update-on-a-replay-of-what-it-might-break is the control this evidence most directly argues for, now with the reason stated: the write is where the safety property is decided, because after the write the library is a policy.

The framework interaction is real and worth not smoothing: one method crosses the complete lifecycle in every framework with carryover ASR between 25.3% and 30.7%, another varies 14.7% → 29.3% across frameworks, and a third authors highly generalizable artifacts with no unsafe retrieval or carryover at all on one framework. Risk lives in the interaction between authoring policy, skill channel and executor — which is skill-invocation-rate-is-a-harness-property and skill-exploitability-is-an-agent-property-not-a-payload-property arriving on the safety axis.

Scope. One backbone across four frameworks; skill libraries as the persistence mechanism; both the harm rubric and the benign-completion scorer are models. Memory, policy and multimodal adaptation, and longer or naturally-occurring task streams, are explicitly left open — but the authors note the argument does not depend on the channel being a skill file, only on it being persistent, and skill libraries were chosen because they are inspectable.
