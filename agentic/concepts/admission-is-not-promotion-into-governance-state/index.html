The guardrail's memory is a write-once trust decision that nobody labelled as one

A pre-action control that repairs rather than blocks needs somewhere to get the repair from. Here it is a governed buffer that supplies a clean value when the evidence gate quarantines a corrupted one, and it trusts its own most recent admitted write. Against the defect classes the gate covers — stale, superseded, contradictory, malformed — that is sound: the predicates catch those before they can reach the buffer. Against a class the gate declares it does not cover, it is not sound at all, and the reason is structural rather than accidental: a corrupted value the predicates never flag is admitted, and admitted values are exactly what the buffer trusts.

The distinction the paper draws is the one to carry out of it. Admission determines whether a record may participate in the action in front of you. Promotion into persistent state determines whether it may influence actions you have not seen yet. Promotion is the stronger decision, and therefore current admissibility does not imply future reference trustworthiness. The vulnerability is created by the interaction of incomplete coverage with stateful remediation — neither alone produces it — and the mechanism generalises conceptually to caches, agent memories, feature stores, retrieval memories, policy state and evidence registries, which the paper names while explicitly declining to claim prevalence in any of them.

It is measured, and the mitigations are honest about being partial. Under the daily workflow, plain buffering produces at least one poisoned substitution on every seed (mean 14.70). A quarantine window — a write becomes trusted only after three consecutive consistent admits — drops that to 0.20 with a mean absolute value error of exactly zero; a rolling median-of-3 drops it to 1.83 but leaves the value error essentially intact at 2.019, which says something useful: the two mitigations do different jobs, and only one of them protects the magnitude rather than the count. Both beat plain on every seed of that workflow.

And the registered claim fails on the second workflow, and is printed as failing. The weekly workflow's much smaller substitution population produces seeds with zero baseline poisoning, so "strictly lower than plain on every seed" is impossible to satisfy on those seeds. The hypothesis is marked False in the robustness table, the paper states that this is a sample-size property of that workflow rather than evidence against the mechanism, and it is neither re-weighted nor resolved by dropping seeds. In a map whose evidence base is largely vendor claims and adversarial benchmarks, a control-plane paper that publishes its own unmet decision rule is worth more than the result it publishes.

The coverage property is the other half, and it is the one to quote at anyone selling a guardrail stack. Composing controls manufactures no detection coverage: the composed plane's detected class set equals the union of its members', a structural tautology that is nonetheless machine-verified across every seed and then measured by injecting two classes declared uncovered in advance — detected at 0.000 at every gate and in the composition, on 30/30 seeds. The corollary is stated as an obligation on the readout rather than as a theorem: declared uncovered classes remain uncovered, and an honest composed readout must say so. Stacking gates buys you enforcement of what the members already detect, and nothing else.

Bounds. A mechanism demonstration on open payload data with a declared synthetic metadata layer and declared injection rates; no prevalence claim for production systems, and explicitly none for the caches and agent memories the mechanism is generalised to conceptually. Zero model calls — the object under test is the control plane, not an agent. Some symmetric intervals on low non-negative counts extend slightly below zero and are printed as computed rather than truncated.
