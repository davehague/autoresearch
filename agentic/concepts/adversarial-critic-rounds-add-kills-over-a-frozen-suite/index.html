Hand the test-writer its own survivors, and freeze the draw before you believe the delta

This map has held that an agent writing its own backstop quits early and self-certifies (lazy-generation-caps-agent-test-coverage), that the existing suite frequently never reaches the changed line (a-passing-suite-does-not-mean-a-tested-change), and — as of 2026-07-27 — that the field's certification criterion for a machine-written reproduction test does not measure constraint at all (fail-to-pass-does-not-constrain-the-fix). The remedy in all three cases is the same shape: grade the test against something the generator did not see. This puts a causal number on the cheapest version of that remedy.

The loop is mechanical end to end. A Tester writes a suite; rule-based mutation (mutmut, not a model, so the injected defect is independent of every model under test) names the surviving mutants; a Critic receives those named survivors with their diffs and writes tests to kill exactly those; the round is scored by whether the test kills the mutant under pytest. No model judges anything (separate-the-generator-from-the-evaluator). It stops after two consecutive dry rounds or four rounds.

The methodological half is the more valuable one for this vault. The study's first experiment let each arm draw its own initial suite, and the resulting comparison could not isolate the loop, because the initial draw is enormously variable — five independent draws on one subject spanning 29 to 45 kills, and one Tester producing 69/168/50/62/65 across repeated attempts on another. That is a spread larger than most published architectural deltas. The fix is the paired protocol this vault has been asking for (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor, control-the-data-draw-before-the-seed), executed: one round-0 suite per replicate, its bytes and its pristine and post-round-0 survivor sets committed to the repository before any continuation may run, with all continuations starting from that identical state. Only then is the loop's contribution a within-replicate quantity — and it is 78% of the remaining survivors.

The heterogeneity is informative rather than noise. On idna the loop swept nearly everything (ρ = 0.99); on graph-guard it killed exactly 5 of 10 remaining survivors in every replicate, a hard stop at that subject's numeric-tolerance mutants. So the loop's value is bounded by the mutant classes the model can reason about, not by the number of rounds you grant it.

Bounds worth carrying: four scorable subjects, three of them the author's own repositories; one language; one fixed Tester model; mutant kills are a proxy for real-fault detection and, with no equivalent-mutant detection performed, every reported kill rate is a floor. Three of four subjects saturate at 98.6–100% in both loop arms, which is why the study's cross-provider question is reported as an underpowered pilot rather than a result.
