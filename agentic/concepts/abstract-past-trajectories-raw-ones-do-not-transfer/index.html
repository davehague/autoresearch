Not the conversation, not a summary of it — a lattice

This map settled a dispute on 2026-07-15 by holding content constant and varying what gets persisted: 96% completion when only the contract survives, 79% with no memory at all, 71% with full history. Full history landing below the no-memory baseline was the drift mechanism caught in the act (persist-the-contract-not-the-conversation), and it explained the earlier result where generic memory scaffolds degraded all ten models tested (memory-scaffolds-can-degrade-long-horizon-agents).

Here is the same variable, isolated by a different group on a different content class, with the same sign and a bigger number. Give an agent the raw trajectories of past successful repairs — the reasoning, the failed explorations, the redundant tool calls, the issue-specific artifacts — and it resolves 57.6%. Give it a structured abstraction built from those same trajectories and it resolves 80.0%. Twenty-two points, and raw is the worst variant in the study.

So far this confirms. The part that extends the rule is the shape of what survives.

"Persist the contract, discard the conversation" reads as binary — keep the structured obligations, drop the narrative. Neither half describes this. What is persisted is not a contract: nobody stated these strategies in advance, they were mined from trajectories that happened to work. And it is not one thing. The hierarchy runs from concrete sub-goals grounded in project artifacts, through project-agnostic investigation strategies, to cross-project principles, and every single level loses to the mixture — low-level 64.0%, medium 60.8%, high 58.4%, full 80.0%. The ordering among the singletons is the tell: concrete beats abstract, because a repository-specific edit needs executable detail and a general principle has nothing to act on. But concrete alone is still 16 points short, because detail from another issue does not tell you why it applied.

The operative variable, then, is not contract-versus-conversation. It is structured-and-multi-scale versus raw, and the thing worth carrying forward from experience is a lattice: the specific detail that makes a fix executable sitting alongside the general strategy that makes it transferable, indexed so the agent can draw from both at once. This map's rule gains a sixth clause.

Two results make this hard to explain away. First, the plans transfer across scaffolds. Distilled inside one agent and prepended verbatim to a structurally different one — different tools, different prompts, different verification logic, none of it modified — they lift Pass@1 75.8% → 81.0%. Whatever was learned is not that agent's idiosyncrasies. Second, tokens fall while doing it (1,245K → 1,180K, steps 60.45 → 58.18). Delivered context that removes exploratory search rather than adding deliberation is this map's fifth-clause economics again (selected-context-cuts-trajectory-tokens-at-equal-localization) — the rare intervention that is not paying for accuracy with budget.

One asymmetry is worth carrying into any deployment. The same plan repairs different deficiencies in different agents: for one scaffold the dominant gain category is Partial Fix (49%) — right root cause, incomplete coverage — and for the other it is Wrong Location (52%). An identical intervention doing different work in different harnesses is this vault's harness-conditioning finding pointed at a technique rather than a benchmark (the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order), and it means a reported plan-injection delta does not tell you which of your own problems it will fix. Relatedly, 9 of 17 regressions are execution failures under a plan the authors judged reasonable: procedural memory improves what the agent aims at and does not make it follow through.

Bounds. One benchmark, with the standing contamination caveat (benchmark-scores-inflate-when-agents-can-retrieve-the-fix). The retrieval pool is the same repository as the target — leakage is controlled temporally but not topically — so the cross-scaffold arm demonstrates agent-independence and says nothing about whether this transfers across codebases, which is the question a team adopting it actually has. No seed variance or intervals anywhere; Pass@1 is one run per configuration, so the backbone comparison is a single draw each (one-run-scores-an-implementation-not-an-idea). The ablation runs on 125 hard-weighted instances rather than 500, so its percentages are not directly comparable with the headline. Both the grouping and abstraction operators are GPT-5 calls, so the hierarchy encodes one model's sense of what a coherent procedural segment is. Comparison against other agents on different backbones is a confound this vault already names (benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives) — the arms to trust are the same-backbone baseline and the transfer.
