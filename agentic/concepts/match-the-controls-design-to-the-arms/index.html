A control that had fewer chances to move is not a floor

Having a no-op control is the demand this map already makes. This is the next question, and it is the one that decides whether the control means anything: under what design was it run?

Checkpoints. A frozen model re-evaluated twice and a treatment arm evaluated at four checkpoints are not comparable objects. The arm has more opportunities for a problem to cross the decision band, so more transitions accrue to it for purely combinatorial reasons. Measured: the two-checkpoint control reports 4 learned and 5 corrupted; the design-matched four-checkpoint no-op, assembled from baseline evaluations the study already owned, reports a median of 10 and 8 over 120 orderings. The treatment arms did not change. Their apparent margin over the floor fell from 18–21× to 11–13× — still large, and no longer the number that was going to be printed.

Budgets that one arm binds against and another does not. A fixed token cap is not a neutral setting once arms diverge in style. The distillation arm's student adopts its teacher's verbosity and its truncation rate goes 16.5% → 65.3%; every truncated completion scores as a failure, so the arm with the largest real gain is scored as the most destructive. The remedy is cheap and mechanical — log generation length per checkpoint — and the honest downstream move is to exclude that arm's corruption counts from the capability analysis rather than report a number known to be confounded with verbosity. Note the shape: this is not a missing control, it is a shared parameter that stopped being shared the moment the arms' output distributions separated. Any budget-matched comparison in this vault has the same exposure — cf. scoreable-runs-are-a-separate-denominator, where equal slots did not deliver equal usable candidates.

Seeds. Three, not one, and scored on the same problems. In the source's own run the third seed is the one that changes the finding: a majority-vote method reads corruption-to-learning 0.55, 1.53, 1.33 across seeds — same method, same data, same budget, landing on either side of the ratio's decision point. The discipline the paper models is refusing to pool them as a fixed effect afterwards to recover significance, even though pooling would have given it p = 0.0076.

Together with a-null-measured-once-is-a-point-estimate, this is the pair of questions to ask of any reported floor in this vault: how many times was it measured, and was it measured the way the arm was? Both corrections here run against the study's own headline, which is the direction that tells you the control is doing work.
