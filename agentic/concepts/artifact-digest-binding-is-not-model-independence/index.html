The author who built the artifact-binding mechanism is also the one who names, unprompted, exactly why it isn't the independence guarantee it looks like

This map's whole arrangement thesis is that independence is a property of the arrangement — what the checker is denied, who authors what it checks, where its expected value comes from — rather than of the checker's identity alone. This source is a clean test case for that thesis from a domain (formal Lean proving) this map hasn't drawn on before, and it comes out mixed in exactly the way the thesis predicts. The information boundary is real and specific: the reviewer cannot read the actor's transcript, prior reviews, or orchestration state, and acceptance requires the verifier's pass and the reviewer's approval to agree on one artifact digest — a mismatch invalidates it outright, which is a stronger binding than most agentic-review pipelines in this vault provide (most bind on content similarity or a judge's say-so, not on a cryptographic match forcing the verifier, the reviewer, and the stored file to all be talking about the literal same bytes). The model-identity boundary, which this map's open questions have repeatedly asked every artifact-binding design to report, is explicitly absent: "the reviewer shares the actor's model family, so context separation does not establish independent errors" is the paper's own sentence, offered without hedging in its Limitations section.

That is a rare and useful thing for this map to have: a source that builds exactly the mechanism the open question is asking about, gets real measured gains from it (93->100, a cost reduction, 672/672 on PutnamBench), and then names the missing half itself rather than leaving a reader to infer it. It directly answers this map's open question "Is the model boundary worth anything once the information boundary is in place?" — not with an experiment (nobody here ran the 2x2 of same/different model x full/restricted evidence), but by supplying an uncontrolled real-world instance exactly on one side of that 2x2, with the author aware enough of the gap to flag it. The measured gains therefore most cleanly support "information restriction alone produces a benefit" rather than "this is an independent check" — which is the same conclusion an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself reached from the opposite direction (there, the model boundary was tested and mattered; here, it is absent and named as a limitation rather than measured).

Interaction edge: verifiable-execution-records — the digest-matching acceptance protocol (verifier pass + reviewer approval + on-disk artifact all resolving to the same hash) is a working instance of exactly the "approval binds to a validated artifact identity" property that map's Guarded Commits source measures at infrastructure scale (guarded-commits-ledger-checks-realized-artifact-not-just-dispatch-fields); this is the same mechanism shape in a formal-verification domain rather than a software-commit one, and neither paper cites the other.

Marked open-question rather than confirms/extends because the paper does not resolve this map's standing question, it sharpens it: the author's own disclosure is evidence the map's model-boundary concern is recognized as live by practitioners building exactly this kind of system, not evidence about what the model boundary's absence actually costs in error detection. Nobody has run that experiment here either.

Scope: single author, no institutional co-authors, published to a NeurIPS workshop track rather than a refereed main conference; the evaluation is explicitly "descriptive, with no repeated configurations, confidence intervals, or component ablations" by the author's own words, so the respective contributions of retrieval restriction, fresh review, and session persistence to the 93->100 result are each unresolved; the harness is unreleased, limiting external reproduction.
