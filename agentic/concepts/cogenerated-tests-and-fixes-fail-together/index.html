The self-graded gate is worse than self-graded — it is correlated

"The agent grades its own homework" understates the problem by assuming the grader is merely lenient. It is not independent: the test and the patch are generated from one reading of the bug, so when the reading is wrong the test is wrong in a way that matches the patch. Measured, that shows up as a coupling factor of 1.87 — nearly twice the joint-failure rate independence would predict.

This is the same structural claim the vault keeps arriving at from unrelated directions, and this is its cleanest quantification. structure-outweighs-the-verification-step found rescues collapsing 6 → 2 when observe/compare moved back onto the model that produced the artifact. an-independent-checker-beats-the-same-model-checking-itself found a cheap cross-model re-derivation recovering 79% of injected errors against self-checking's 71%. restate-the-checks-externally-at-completion found a self-generated validator inheriting exactly the omission that produced the artifact. All four say: a check drawn from the same state as the work is not a check. Here the correlation is measured directly rather than inferred from an effect size.

The mitigation is ordering, and it is nearly free. Emit the reproduction test first, before any fix attempt, with source files off-limits and the prompt steered toward the most observable witness of the failure — the way a human starts debugging. The test then has no fix to anchor against, and that sequencing alone is worth +7.0 Resolved. This is a scheduling change to the harness, not a model or prompt improvement, which puts it beside separate-the-generator-from-the-evaluator as structural rather than instructional.

The generalization worth watching: any agentic workflow that produces an artifact and its acceptance criterion in one trajectory has this failure mode, not just test-and-fix. Self-evolving harnesses selecting their own improvements (harness-self-evolution-is-capped-by-its-proxy-judge), agents writing the AGENTS.md they then follow (generated-context-files-cost-more-than-they-help), and any loop where the same context produces both the plan and its completion check are the same shape. The transferable rule is to separate the two in time or in context, and to measure the coupling factor rather than assuming independence.

Caveats: measured on one backbone (GPT-5-mini) over a 433-instance benchmark intersection, one trial per instance; the joint-failure baseline assumes the two marginal rates are the right independence model, which is a modelling choice; benchmark repair rather than production CI.
