The contract was complete, external, and visible before a line was written — and the route still decided the program

This map's practice has been assembled from two failures of possession: the agent cannot critique a spec (agents-are-weak-at-spec-critique) and cannot retain one that arrives in pieces (emergent-specs-corrupt-the-artifact). Both are answered by holding the contract outside the agent and delivering it whole. SpecPath grants that answer in full and finds a third failure underneath it.

The design is deliberately unfavourable to its own hypothesis. Execution is deferred: the complete history is visible before implementation begins, from a fresh copy of the base repository. There is no intermediate code to create inertia, no changing workspace, no changing target — the final contract is identical by construction and independently re-derived from the visible text by two blind reviewers, who matched gold on all 25 histories and all 50 encoded operations. Only the route differs. Under those conditions, a third of the runs that got it right when told directly get it wrong when told by a route that supersedes, retracts, splits or merely repeats.

The operator that does the most damage is the one that does nothing. Duplicate — restate the same atoms a second time, changing no obligation — produces the largest observed CPV (19 positive blocks, 18.3%), ahead of explicit override (8, 10.8%). The ordering churns across repeats and the authors mark it exploratory, but the direction of the surprise is the finding: this is not a story about non-monotonic revision semantics being hard. It is a story about presentation being load-bearing at all, which is the more uncomfortable claim, and the two direct controls keep it honest — wording (−3.1 pp) and neutral added turns (−4.0 pp) both have intervals spanning zero on average FCR, so the effect is not "longer prompts are worse."

What this does to the map's practice. restate-the-checks-externally-at-completion says re-present the contract at the decision it governs; this says the history of how it became the contract keeps acting even when the contract is fully re-presented up front. The two are compatible and the combination is the actionable form: an agent asked to work from a conversation should be made to write down the currently binding obligations before it writes code — the paper's active-contract resolution step — because the vault now has evidence that no amount of completeness in the conversation substitutes for that normalization. This is the same instruction persist-the-contract-not-the-conversation reached from the memory side, arriving here from the specification side.

And it is a scaffold property, not only a model property. mini-swe-agent shows 43.0% any-CPV against OpenHands' 30.7% on heavily overlapping intervals — too weak to rank, strong enough to say the harness renders the transcript and the rendering is part of the estimand. Deployment estimates range 0.0% to 71.3% on 5–28 blocks each, which is the honest version of "we cannot tell you which model to pick."

Bounds. Five curated families are the inferential unit, so N = 5 clusters and no bootstrap can add more; correctness is bounded by the frozen probes, meaning tested contract realization rather than program equivalence; the history operators are controlled interventions, not a sample of how real review conversations actually revise; CPV is undefined when direct fails, so this conditions on demonstrated competence and does not estimate how often this bites in the wild.
