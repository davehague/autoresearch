The gain went where the training went

This map has the apparatus term measured many ways. This is the version that is hardest to argue with, because every number was produced by one group under one protocol with the harness as the only thing varying, and because the finding lands on a competitor's released model rather than the authors' own.

Three claims, separable.

(1) The bare confound, on an untrained checkpoint. One model, three harnesses: 64.0 / 62.4 / 57.2. A 6.8-point spread with nothing else different. The authors' own methodological response is the one this map recommends: because step-0 differs by scaffold, improvements are measured relative to each scaffold's own baseline and never compared across scaffolds. That is attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model adopted as a reporting rule inside a paper rather than urged at other papers.

(2) The training gain is harness-specific too. +6.4 / +5.8 / +9.4 for the same recipe on the same checkpoint. So the apparatus term is not a constant offset that cancels in a difference — it interacts with the treatment. A paired design does not save you here.

(3) The transfer failure, which is the new thing. A released post-trained model gains +3.4 under the harness its authors report and −0.4 under another, where the untuned newer base model beats it. The authors decline to isolate a cause and state the pattern: a gain obtained under one agent control flow need not survive another.

Read against fine-tuning-installs-the-training-scaffolds-conventions, this is a confirmation with a different shape. That note measured base-vs-fine-tuned spread across four scaffolds and found collapses up to 29 points, establishing that training installs scaffold conventions into weights. This one measures the gain rather than the level, on a model whose training scaffold is inferable from where it wins, and finds the gain sign-flipping. The two together make the practical rule sharper than either alone: a post-training claim is scoped to the harness the training data was collected in, and the sign of the effect — not just its size — is inside that scope. Neither study can attribute the mechanism, and both say so.

What this costs a practitioner. Choosing between a post-trained coding model and its own base is not a model-selection decision that can be delegated to a benchmark row. If the harness in production is not the one the model was tuned in, the published delta is not evidence about the deployment — it may be evidence against it. The same reasoning applies in reverse to anyone switching harness after settling on a model: cf. re-examine-the-harness-when-the-model-changes, which this extends to re-examine the model when the harness changes, and no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models.

A fourth number belongs here for calibration and is easy to miss. On these three harnesses the model generation term — one full base-model release — is worth +3.4 / +1.0 / +3.4, while the harness term on a fixed checkpoint is 6.8. The apparatus outweighs the generation, again, on a third independent setup.
