A fourth thing the checker's information set contains: its own recent work

This map's mechanism has been subtraction — withhold the issue text (reconstruct-the-problem-from-the-patch-to-verify-it), withhold the exploration trace (the-checker-should-see-less-than-the-generator), freeze the referent before the artifact existed (the-referent-not-the-reviewer-carries-the-separation). Every design here decides what the checker gets to see about the thing it is judging. This result is about something else in the context entirely, and it moves the verdict anyway.

The manipulation is a completed audit→repair exchange on a held-out item — the model audited some other trace, was asked to produce the corrected solution, and did. Then it is asked to judge a fresh trace, and the request is enforced byte-identical across conditions by tests that diff the rendered prompts. False alarms fall in 15 of 15 model × wording combinations, 2.8 to 11.5 pp.

The control that makes it a claim about audits rather than about context is the length-matched filler, and it is the load-bearing one: a prior exchange restating an unrelated problem at a word count computed per episode moves the rate by +1.58, −0.18, −0.30 — null on two models, and on the third it moves opposite to the episode, which makes that model's headline conservative rather than inflated. Prior context of any kind does not do this. Prior context that was an audit does.

The scale comparison is the one to remember. On one model, the episode moves the false-alarm rate −3.59 pp while a maximal explicit leniency prime — "reviewers are criticised far more for reporting an error that turns out to be benign than for missing one" — moves it only −1.76 [−3.72, +0.07], an interval touching zero and therefore not an established effect at all. Whatever a prior audit does to that model, an explicit instruction does not reach. So the incidental wiring of the pipeline is doing something a deliberate prompt cannot, which is the worst possible combination for anyone trying to control a checker's strictness from the prompt layer.

The direction is wrong for the obvious explanation, and that is what makes it interesting. The nearest published account — judgements drifting toward the polarity of the preceding conversation, with negative histories inducing 1.52× more drift — predicts that an episode whose audit reported an error should raise the false-alarm rate. It lowers it further: −5.62 pp [−8.27, −2.87] against an ordinary episode, negative at all five wordings on the model where the manipulation lands cleanly, plus an observational within-pool split converging (−10.30 pp where the frozen episode reported an error against +0.77 where it did not). The other two models cannot carry that test for a stated and measurable reason — on one, 37 of 50 of its "repairs" still end on a "verdict": "correct" assertion, so the cell is degenerate rather than contradictory.

No mechanism is claimed, and the refusal is the credible part. Three components separate — the repair content, the error verdict once repair is removed from both sides, and the repair request — and each fails to survive on precisely the model the other explains. Repair content carries the two Qwen models at 4/5 wordings each and is null on Ministral at all five; the error-verdict episode is 5/5 on Ministral and 1/5 and 0/5 elsewhere. What the evidence supports is that different components account for the effect across models, in the same direction, with no common route identified.

What this does to the map's design advice. Every prescription here so far is of the form deny the checker input X. This adds a second axis and it is procedural rather than informational: declare, and control, what job the checker has just finished and what job it has been told comes next. The two obvious pipeline shapes — one model audits and a second repairs, versus one model audits and then repairs its own findings — are not equivalent instruments, and nobody reporting a verifier's precision says which one produced it. That is a harness-card field for checkers, in the same sense agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness asks for one for benchmarks.

Bounds. Three open-weight models, one benchmark, one task family (stepwise mathematical verification, not patches), no frontier or closed judge. The models were selected by a pre-specified instrument-sensitivity screen and two candidates failed it — and on one of the screened-out pair the contrast is significant and reversed (+1.58 pp [+0.45, +2.82]). The authors print those rows so the selection can be judged, and state the resulting scope honestly: the direction describes framing-responsive verifiers rather than every verifier they tried. A ladder of merely stated future repair responsibility behaves differently and is reported as an appendix rather than claimed — with one thing carried over, that every prospective rung which moves d′ on the Qwen models moves it down (13 of 13 surviving contrasts), so a stated repair role is not a free improvement either.
