"Resolved" decays, and the cheap way to check for decay is wrong three times out of four

Closing a review thread or editing the flagged line is treated as evidence a concern was fixed — but a later, unrelated revision can silently reintroduce the problem without touching the resolved marker at all. REVAL reframes each review concern as a persistent verification obligation with named dependencies, and re-checks it exactly when one of those dependencies changes. The naive alternative everyone would reach for first — "the flagged line isn't the latest edit anymore, so it must be fine" — is wrong on 72.9% of the cases that are actually still broken.

This extends agentic-code-review's existing finding, from agent-review-comment-resolution-field, that GitHub's raw isResolved flag is a usefulness signal rather than a correctness signal (resolution-rate-is-not-precision). That study showed the label is noisy at the moment it's set; this one shows a concern marked correctly resolved at that moment can still go stale as the codebase moves — a second, independent way the same metric can mislead, with a concrete fix borrowed from regression-test selection and build-system dependency tracking rather than from anything code-review-specific.

The mechanism also hands ci-cd-for-agents a reusable primitive: dependency-gated revalidation of non-test obligations, with a named break-even cost (0.42ms) below which bothering to track dependencies isn't worth it. And it gives unattended-operation's unfaithful-work-reports thread a structural reading of one way a "fixed" claim goes stale without anyone lying: the claim was true when made, and the evidence under it moved.

Caveats: evaluated entirely against a self-constructed synthetic oracle, not real review histories (the paper frames a protocol for that as future work, not a completed result); single author, no stated institution; witness adequacy — whether the dependencies named for a concern are the right ones — is explicitly flagged as a separate, unresolved question from revalidation correctness itself.
