The number describes the deployment that calibrated it

This map has established the apparatus-as-term claim on resolve rates, on Pass@1, on enterprise workflow scores, on the inference backend, and — as of tonight — inside fine-tuned weights. This is the same claim arriving in the monitoring layer, and it is worth carrying separately because the artefact whose number moves is not the agent at all. It is the instrument you would buy to watch the agent.

Two measurements make it.

Cross-family deployment. A monitor calibrated on qwen2.5:7b and deployed on llama3.1:8b, over a matched task, tool and injector plan, sits at chance — AUROC 0.527 with a healthy false-alarm rate of 0.75. The same target recalibrated on itself reaches 0.885. The paper is explicit that this is a property of the calibration and not of a hard corpus, which is exactly the shape of every entry on this map: not that the method fails, but that the published number was measuring a pairing.

Cross-corpus import. Taken to AFTraj-2K — 1,882 trajectories from another group, another set of agents and frameworks, imported with no change to the monitors — the monitor reaches AUROC 0.745 and detects 0.048 at the 5% false-alarm budget. The ranking transfers zero-shot and the operating point does not. That distinction is the sharpest thing here, and it does not appear anywhere else in this vault. A detector can be genuinely, transferably better at ordering trajectories by risk while the threshold that turns the ordering into an alarm is a local artefact, and the number a vendor prints is the thresholded one.

The mechanism is measured rather than asserted, which is what elevates this above a portability complaint. The horizon diagnosis predicts the collapse: the temporal detector's advantage over a memoryless baseline is +0.09 at post-onset horizon ≤3 steps, +0.14 at 4–8 and +0.40 at ≥9, and on AFTraj-2K only 53 of 771 failures (6.9%) have ≥9 post-onset steps while 61% end within three steps of onset. So the corpus's shape — how much room a failure has to develop before the episode ends — is a hidden variable in every reported detection rate, and it is not a property anyone discloses. The cross-provider arm confirms it constructively: a gemini-2.5-flash corpus matched on task, tools and injector plan detects 0.38 against qwen's 0.57, and at matched horizon the two agree (0.53 vs 0.56). The provider difference was a horizon difference.

Two more disclosures the same study makes involuntarily. Temperature is part of the calibration: a temperature-matched null still false-alarms on 36% of healthy episodes once the deployment runs at 0.9 rather than 0.2, and the paper reports detections at that rate as not deployable evidence. And the per-dataset spread swallows most orderings — the author says so directly, presenting the monitor comparison as "deliberately unflattering" because several per-dataset orderings that look decisive are ties once power is accounted for, so only the pooled comparison is defensible. That is one-run-scores-an-implementation-not-an-idea volunteered by an author about his own leaderboard.

The reading rule this map already carries transfers with one substitution. A model evaluated on someone else's harness has not been evaluated for your system; a monitor evaluated on someone else's deployment has not been evaluated for yours, and the specific thing you cannot import is the threshold. The disclosable fields are the calibration corpus, the model family and serving temperature it was fit on, and the post-onset horizon distribution of the failures it was scored against.

And there is a design consequence the same study demonstrates: the layer that carries neither burden is the deterministic one. Recomputation-based checks need no healthy null, no threshold and no calibration, and transfer across model families with nothing retuned — 110 of 110 at 0 of 10 (a-deterministic-recheck-beats-a-learned-monitor-on-precision). Where an invariant exists, the apparatus-confound question does not arise.

Bounds. Single independent author; 3B–8B local models plus one commercial API. The cross-family recalibration contrast is one pair of models on one matched plan. The external-corpus results are two benchmarks the author did not build, which is the strongest part of the evidence, but the operating-point collapse on AFTraj-2K is explained by a corpus-shape difference the author diagnoses rather than independently controls.
