The scorer is most certain exactly where the run has stopped going anywhere

The null in step-level-credit-does-not-track-causal-contribution would be much less useful without this: the audit does not merely fail to find signal, it identifies what the signal is.

Implicit credit — the outcome-conditioned token log-probability ratio that a growing family of agentic-RL methods trains on — rises with the policy's own probability of the action it is scoring. Median rank correlation +0.752, registered as a directional prediction before the data and surviving multiplicity correction, replicating at +0.70 in a second model family under a repaired instrument. Regress credit jointly on fluency and on the replay-measured causal increment and fluency carries 2.37× the weight. Then the number that closes it: conditioning fluency out leaves a partial correlation of −0.004 (p = 0.87) between credit and causal contribution. Nothing survives. The increment's apparent weight in the joint model is the shadow fluency casts on it. And the outcome conditioning — the whole point of the construction, the part that is supposed to inject hindsight — is uncorrelated with contribution: knowing how the trajectory ended changes the score without moving it toward what any step did.

Why this is worse than noise. A signal that is random is merely uninformative. A signal that tracks predictability is anti-informative in the failure mode that matters most to anyone running long agents. The paper's worked trajectory: the policy issues the same action four times in a row after the environment has already answered Nothing happens., and the scorer's confidence on those repetitions exceeds 0.9999 — because the next token is maximally predictable — while replay judges those same turns pivotal. A stuck loop is the highest-confidence region of the trajectory. Any instrument built on the model's own certainty will allocate its attention away from the steps where an agent has stopped making progress.

Two direct operational consequences, both measured in the same study:

  • A confidence threshold is a cost knob, not a detector. Routing the lowest-confidence turns to a judge recovers 11.9% [9.4, 14.9] of pivotal turns while routing 13.1% of all turns — chance — for the reason above. The same threshold does cut judge calls by 13.1% per turn and 14.0% per trajectory, and the authors release the rule with its negative result attached precisely so it is not repurposed as triage. Compare a-tiny-monitor-prices-a-doomed-trajectory, where a cheap monitor does earn a detection claim — the difference is that its signal is not derived from the policy's own certainty.
  • Self-derived confidence is disqualified as a health signal generally. This is the same lesson as a-self-supervision-signal-can-read-healthy-while-the-model-dies, arriving from the attribution side rather than the training-signal side: quantities the model authors about itself move the wrong way under degeneration.

For the atlas's standing question, this is a clean instance: the measurement was not merely unvalidated, it was strongly structured by something other than the thing it claimed to measure — which is the hardest kind of instrument failure to notice, because the output looks informative.
