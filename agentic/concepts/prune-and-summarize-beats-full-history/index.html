Prune to recent interactions + summarize, don't retain full history

A controlled four-way sweep on a 50-task enterprise expense-itemization workflow: no-user-model baseline 8.0%; full conversation history 71.0% completion at 1,480,996 tokens / 14.56 h; prune to the last 5 tool call/response pairs 79.0% at 535,274 tokens / 5.39 h; prune + automated summarization 91.6% completion, 99.64% itemization accuracy, at 553,374 tokens / 5.79 h. So pruning + summarization beats full-history retention by +20.6 completion points while spending ~2.7× fewer tokens and ~2.5× less wall-clock — reliability and efficiency move together, not as a trade-off. Verbose enterprise tool responses are the culprit that full-history retention accumulates into context overflow and stale-state errors.

Vault significance: this is the quantified, cross-stack confirmation the context-engineering map was missing. Anthropic's guidance argued compaction + just-in-time retrieval qualitatively; LongDS- Bench measured unmanaged long context failing on Claude. This measures the fix on GPT-5 and reproduces the direction on Sonnet 4.5 — same direction, different stack, with effect sizes — directly weakening the map's "single-stack" caveat. It's still one enterprise task, so not a general constant.

Rebutted by (added 2026-07-29, connect): this note is the anchor of the vault's largest live dispute and, until now, pointed at none of it. Four results push back, and they do not agree with each other either. chainswe-summarization-compounds-maintenance-errors finds that in multi-bug maintenance the history-processing configs — summarization, sub-agent delegation — degrade more than simply retaining full history, the exact reverse of the ordering here. compaction-selection-is-all-or-nothing finds summarization does not degrade gracefully: it selects whole blocks and drops planted operational facts entirely at the first boundary. compaction-can-launder-killed-process-output finds it worse than lossy — summarization has no epistemics, so it promotes a killed process's partial output into a durable "confirmed result." persist-the-contract-not-the-conversation finds full history landing below no memory at all, which agrees with this note's direction while disagreeing about the mechanism. And search-the-harness-with-raw-traces-not-summaries inverts it outright for a different consumer: scores-plus-summary scored no better than raw scores, and worse on best candidate.

The discriminators that would resolve this are cheap to state and unrun: what is being summarized (verbose tool output vs accumulated repository state vs prior solution attempts), who consumes it (the actor continuing a task vs a diagnostician reconstructing why an attempt failed), and whether the summary is the only surviving copy (recoverable compression vs destructive). This note's win is measured on the first cell of each — tool output, actor, recoverable. Nobody has crossed the axes.
