Monitor agents with trusted supervisor models

"We use other, trusted AI systems as 'supervisors' to constantly review a working agent's reasoning, actions and plans to see if it is going off track." Detection precedes prevention: a supervisor that catches a harmful action blocks it before execution. Architecturally this is the same pre-execution classifier Cursor and Anthropic ship (gate-actions-with-a-contextual-classifier-dial) — a third org, applied to internal engineering traffic rather than a consumer product.

The contribution this vault was missing is the measurement frame: "coverage (the fraction of traffic monitored), recall (the fraction of misaligned behaviors caught), and time-to-response."

coverage is the metric neither of the other two vendors reports, and it is the one that makes their headline numbers interpretable. A classifier that blocks ~4% of actions has said nothing about safety until you know what fraction of actions it saw. Recall names the false-negative side directly — the false-allow rate the runtime-action-governance map has been asking for is a function of coverage and recall, so this post specifies the quantity without publishing it. Time-to-response concedes that some misaligned actions land and matters only if the run is reversible, which is why this couples to keep-the-agent-interruptible-and-reversible.

Practical read: instrument the gate, not just the agent. A governance stack without coverage and recall figures is unfalsifiable, however sophisticated its layering.

Caveat: DeepMind names the metrics but publishes no values for them, so this is a proposed discipline with deployment scale behind it, not a measured result.
