The number nobody had a reason to publish

This map's instruments have been validated by showing they beat the alternative: first-wrong-step localization beats outcome grading (localize-first-wrong-step-not-final-answer), span-level error localization beats span-blind analysis, claims-versus-evidence auditing beats the transcript (audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence). None of that establishes that the localization is right, and until now nothing in the map reported an absolute accuracy.

It is 28.8% strict on a 9B backbone and 38.0% on a 27B one. Naming the right agent alone reaches 56.0%; landing within one step of the truth reaches 44.0%. So the honest statement of the map's core practice is: a majority of attributions are wrong, and it is still the best move available — the recovery arm repairs 2–3× more failures than three self-correction baselines (close-the-loop-from-attribution-to-rerun). Both halves are load-bearing, and the second one is why this is mixed rather than a downgrade.

Three consequences.

Attribution output is a hypothesis, not a fact. Anything downstream that treats a localized step as ground truth — a training signal, a regression label, a report to a human, an automated rollback — is consuming a minority-correct estimate. That is the same failure shape the vault has recorded when a misgrading proxy is used to select rather than merely to report: an unlabeled self-evolving harness committing a 50% program with a 64% one in the pool (harness-self-evolution-is-capped-by-its-proxy-judge). Attribution accuracy is now a measurable input to any system that acts on trajectories, and nobody measures theirs.

The gain is a weak-backbone property. The advantage over the single-pass baseline is large at 9B (28.8 vs 21.7), small at 27B (38.0 vs 36.4), and the paper reports it disappearing on hosted models, where one global read is already stronger and cross-examination adds nothing at 1.6× the tokens. So the elaborate attribution machinery is compensation for a weak diagnostician, which is the vault's decision rule for harness components stated exactly (separate-durable-scaffolding-from-model-compensation, re-examine-the-harness-when-the-model-changes) — and it is why the authors route per model rather than invoking the expensive path unconditionally.

It sets a floor under the evidence side. measure-whether-evidence-reconstructs-the-claim asks whether a captured trace can reconstruct the decision a claim depends on. If the best available reconstruction of which step went wrong is right under four times in ten, then a trace's reconstructability is bounded by more than its capture completeness — the reader is also the bottleneck.

Caveats: Who&When is general agentic traces, not coding agents, and the ceiling may differ on execution-grounded software tasks where a test suite supplies an oracle the general setting lacks — which would be a cheap and worthwhile thing to measure. Open-weight backbones for the headline; the hosted-model result is in an appendix. Suggestive.
