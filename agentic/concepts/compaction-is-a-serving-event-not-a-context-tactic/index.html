Compaction is priced, and the bill lands on the sessions that can least afford it

This map has argued about compaction as an information problem: what survives the rewrite, what silently does not, and whether the surviving summary is a faithful stand-in for the trajectory it replaced (compaction-selection-is-all-or-nothing, compaction-can-launder-killed-process-output, context-renewal-does-not-discharge-a-completed-obligation, prune-and-summarize-beats-full-history). Every one of those is about fidelity. This adds the missing axis — compaction has a price, and it is large — measured on 13.5M production sessions rather than a benchmark.

It concentrates where the work is. Sessions containing at least one compaction are 7.8% of sessions and 44.2% of all tokens, 37.1% of LLM calls, 38.9% of tool calls. So compaction is not spread evenly over the workload; it is a near-certainty in the long, expensive sessions — the ones running the multi-hour, high-stakes tasks where a fidelity failure costs the most. The two axes of concern coincide.

It is aggressive. The median event discards 72.8% of prompt tokens, with the middle half discarding 58–81% and 6.1% of events discarding 90% or more. Whatever "compaction selection is all-or-nothing" means qualitatively, this is its magnitude: roughly three-quarters of the window gone at the median.

It costs the cache. Median hit-rate drop 66.1%, with 34.3% of events erasing ≥90% and 21% erasing ≥99%. The rewritten prompt shares little prefix with what preceded it, so the next call cold-starts. The authors put compaction "in the same league as a model switch" — but with one distinction worth carrying: a model switch is imposed externally (usually by rate limiting), while compaction is a cost the harness inflicts on itself as a direct consequence of managing the window (the-agent-token-bill-is-a-cache-lifecycle-problem).

It sits on the critical path. Compaction is implemented as a separate LLM call — a long prefill over the history being summarized, then a decode to produce the summary — consuming a median ~22% of the turn's total execution time and ~34% at P90.

And the trigger is not one threshold. Pre-compaction context utilization is multi-modal, with peaks near 50%, 65–66% (the overall median), 80% and near-100% — and remains multi-modal within a single model, so it is not merely an artifact of aggregating heterogeneous context limits. Whatever policy governs compaction in a production agent is a set of rules, not a line.

The practice this suggests is prevention over cure, which is the direction this map already leans (prevent-context-growth-upstream-not-downstream, selected-context-cuts-trajectory-tokens-at-equal-localization, re-derive-file-state-instead-of-storing-snapshots). Those results were argued on token efficiency and localization fidelity; this supplies the third reason. Every deterministic byte kept out of the window is a compaction event not triggered, and therefore ~22% of a turn and two-thirds of a cache not spent. It also sharpens the failure-loop finding (failure-quadruples-the-compute-of-a-turn): the 36-call retry archetype accumulating 80K+ tokens of error output per call is a compaction generator.

Bounds. One vendor, one product, one sampled week, telemetry only. The compaction policy, its multi-modal triggers and the summarizer's design are Copilot's; the structural claims (concentration in long sessions, aggressive drop, cache destruction, critical-path latency) are what transfer, not the exact percentages. No correctness data anywhere, so this prices compaction without saying anything about whether the compacted sessions produced worse work — the fidelity question this map's other notes cover remains separately open.
