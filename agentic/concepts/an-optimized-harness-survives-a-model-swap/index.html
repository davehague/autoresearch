If the apparatus term were noise, it would not survive the swap

This map's claim is that a published agent score is jointly produced by the model and the apparatus, and that the apparatus term is the size of the effect being reported (the-loop-implementation-is-a-larger-term-than-the-model-tier, no-harness-ordering-holds-across-models). Almost every instance so far detects the term by swapping the apparatus at a fixed model. This is the crossed direction, and it is rarer: fix the apparatus, swap the model.

A harness discovered by automatic search under Opus 4.6 keeps +5.6 pp over the base harness when the task agent's model is replaced by Haiku 4.5 — a substantially weaker model — against +9.0 pp in the cell it was optimized in. Roughly 60% of the gain travels across a model boundary the optimizer never saw.

Two readings, and they point opposite ways, which is why the note is worth keeping on its own.

The first is reassuring for practice: a harness is a real engineering artifact and not a per-model overfitting, so the effort of building one is not stranded when you change models. That is the constructive counterpart to everything in this map — the apparatus term is portable enough to be designed, not just measured.

The second is the uncomfortable one for measurement. If the apparatus contribution were model-specific noise, a reader could at least argue it averages out across a leaderboard. It does not. A harness carries a systematic, transferable offset into every cell it is used in, which means a leaderboard whose rows were each produced under a different, undisclosed harness is not comparing models at a fixed handicap — it is comparing models plus a persistent per-row constant of several points. That is the harder version of this map's disclosure argument, not the softer one.

And the direction of the transfer is what is measured, which bounds it. Stronger optimizer → weaker executor is the arm that was run. The reverse — a harness tuned against a weak model, then applied to a strong one — is where an-evolved-harness-compensates-defects-it-does-not-raise-the-ceiling predicts the transfer should fail, because a harness that compensates for defects a stronger policy no longer commits has nothing to do. Nobody has run that arm here, and it is the one that would decide whether a harness is a portable artifact or a model-shaped patch.

Bounds. One benchmark, one model family, one direction, single-study. The +5.6 figure is reported in an appendix without an interval. The retained fraction (~62% of the in-cell gain) is a single point estimate on a single pair of models and should not be read as a general transfer rate.
