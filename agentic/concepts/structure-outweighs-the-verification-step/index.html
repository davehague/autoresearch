Structure carries the uplift; the verification checkpoint is small and last

The map's instruction to tune structure over prompts (re-examine-the-harness-when-the-model-changes, enforce-invariants-not-implementations) has never had an interior. This supplies one, from a production system measured against three benchmarks stressing unrelated failure modes: scaffolding, planning, and routing account for the large majority of the architectural uplift, and the verification loop that the system is built around contributes +1.5 pp of +11.0.

The nuance that keeps this from being a case against verification: the loop's contribution is positionally concentrated. +1.5 pp is the difference between mid-pack and near-top precisely because everyone at the top has already exhausted scaffolding — the checkpoint converts the residual tasks nothing else reaches. The authors' ranked guidance is therefore "invest in that order, but do not skip the third."

What makes the layer ordering affordable is the model mix: each stage runs on the lightest model that performs it reliably, with 0.5–4B post-trained specialists at 0.02–0.1× frontier per-call cost. That is what turns "verify every step" from a luxury into a default — the compare stage ran on all 397 loop-triggering tasks. It is the same move as move-task-difficulty-from-the-model-to-the-harness, applied to stage staffing rather than harness adaptation, and it composes with restrict-the-tool-surface-to-cut-cost-not-capability: the harness edits that pay are structural, not prompt-level.

Caveats: T3 vendor self-evaluation of the authors' own product, proprietary weights and prompts, headline runs unrepeated (the authors bound run-to-run sensitivity at ±3 pp and disown differences below it), the GAIA layer tiers are internal estimates whose selection rules were not recorded, and there is no controlled same-model scaffold ablation at matched token budget — the same objection that downgraded harness-evolution-underperforms-matched-budget-test-time-scaling. Take the decomposition shape, not the magnitudes.
