The harness term is huge between architectural classes and nearly gone inside the best one

This map's central claim is that the apparatus is a term of the order of the effect being reported — that a published model comparison carries an unreported harness term roughly as large as the differences it is cited to show. the-loop-implementation-is-a-larger-term-than-the-model-tier is the strongest version: six loops at a fixed model span 2.6× and 11.6 absolute points, comparable to the spread across four generations of frontier model under a fixed loop.

This arrival reproduces the first half of that on a different task and then splits it. Across six frameworks at a fixed model the spread is 37 points — larger than anything on this map. But the three frameworks at the top span 2.0 points among themselves, while swapping the backbone model on one of them moves it 10.8. Inside the top band, the model is over five times the framework term.

The boundary is nameable, which is what makes this a scope condition rather than a contradiction. The frameworks sort cleanly by one property: whether the agent runs an open-ended, adaptive investigation, requerying telemetry as evidence emerges. The three that do hold 77.6–79.6%. The three that do not trail in exactly the order of their constraint — TaskWeaver follows a largely fixed plan (65.2%), OpenRCA commits as soon as it can emit an answer (46.6%), MABC does not iterate over evidence at all (42.6%). So the apparatus term here is not distributed across scaffold details; it is almost entirely one binary architectural choice, and once you are on the right side of it the remaining scaffold differences stop resolving.

Two details argue this is a real saturation rather than a ceiling artifact. First, none of the top three was built for RCA — they are general-purpose agents repurposed for it, so the band is not the product of task-specific tuning that happened to converge. Second, the model advantage widens exactly where the task gets harder: from causal-chain depth 2 to depth 5 every framework on the weaker backbone decays by 28–44 points while the stronger backbone holds flat. If the top band were saturated by task difficulty, difficulty would compress the model gap, not open it.

And the attribution is behavioural rather than architectural, which is the part this map should keep. The stronger backbone wins by having a broader diagnostic intent repertoire — resource_probe in 82.8% of its trajectories against 16.6% — and by acting on what it retrieved on 54.3% of rounds against 24.9%. Neither of those is something the framework supplies. That is a mechanism for why a framework swap inside the adaptive band cannot move much: the frameworks are all granting the same affordance, and what differs is whether the model uses it.

What this does to the map. It does not touch the core claim that the apparatus is an unreported term of the order of the effect — 37 points is that claim in its strongest form. What it puts a condition on is the comparison design the map has been recommending. Gridding two or four scaffolds is only informative if the scaffolds straddle an architectural boundary; four adaptive harnesses will report a small term and be read as evidence the term is small, when the same study across the boundary would have reported 37 points. The prescription that follows is a sampling rule: grid the design space, not the vendors.

Bounds. Every number in the framework comparison is a single run with no significance testing — the authors explicitly label those differences indicative and reserve replication (mean±sd over five runs, pass@k) for the intervention validation only. So the 2.0-point top-band spread is one run each and could easily be run-to-run noise; the honest statement is that the top-band spread is not resolved by this design, not that it is 2.0. The 10.8-point model gap is also single-run, though it is large enough to survive plausible variance and is corroborated by the depth-stratified pattern. One task domain, one topology, one benchmark. The two backbones differ by 22× in cost per case ($0.21 vs $4.64), so "the model is the dominant lever" is a statement about capability, not about what a budget-matched comparison would show. And residual implementation differences across frameworks may confound the comparison, which the authors flag as their own internal-validity threat.
