Aggregate robustness checks cannot catch a stimulus-level fault

A shared max_tokens=5 decoding budget, set for a judge's one-token A/B output and silently inherited by a generator's paragraph-length call, truncated one producer's Turkish hallucinated answers to a mean of 2.1 words. Every standard check the field treats as rigorous evidence made the resulting artifact more convincing, not less. A larger sample tightened the confidence interval around a fabricated 32-point cross-lingual judge-accuracy gap rather than revealing it. Replication from N=50 to N=500 reproduced the same wrong number in the same direction, and was read — correctly, in the narrow sense that the number really was stable, incorrectly in every sense that mattered — as evidence against measurement noise. A three-layer mechanistic account (tokenization-fragmentation comparison across judges, the judges' own pretraining-corpus technical reports, a lexical-overlap and length analysis reinterpreted through an item-response-theory lens) converged on a causal story that fit the data at every stage and was entirely wrong. A controlled producer-swap experiment, built specifically to rule out a confound, instead manufactured a second false positive: GLM's selection accuracy read 0.61 on the truncated producer's items versus 1.00 on the unaffected producer's items, read as a genuine producer-judge interaction. Only a manual reading of twenty raw generated items — the one step none of the four prior checks performed — found that 81.0% were truncated filler and 12.2% were verbatim copies of the factual field, unanswerable by construction. Separating the decoding budgets made every affected quantity recover to ceiling with zero residual effect.

The mechanism is structural, not a matter of care: none of the four checks is designed to inspect the items themselves. They all measure aggregate judge behavior, which is downstream of, and therefore blind to, item-level degeneration. A bigger N, a second seed, a converging causal story, and a controlled manipulation are four different ways of asking "is this effect real," and all four assume the inputs to the effect are well-formed — an assumption none of them tests. This sharpens evaluation-integrity's standing thesis that a proxy misgrades and, under selection pressure, misgrades optimistic (fix-the-eval-before-trusting-the-score shows the opposite-sign case, a pessimistic instrument bug) by adding a new failure mode: a corpus-construction bug that survives every downstream statistical safeguard because none of them looks upstream of the statistic. It is the same family as agent-benchmarks-underdisclose-their-harness's "under-specification explains spread," one level more severe — here under-specification doesn't add spread, it fabricates a headline effect wholesale while leaving a real, adjacent effect merely distorted beside it (see the paper's Section 4.2 style-bias finding), and no aggregate statistic can tell the two apart.

Caveats: single pipeline, single fault (a shared decoding-budget parameter) — an existence proof, not a census of how often this occurs. Judge set is three models from three families; the producer comparison is one pair (DeepSeek vs Gemini) on one language (Turkish); the measurement is a binary faithfulness classification only. Fleiss' κ across the three judges was 0.041 even setting the fault aside — very low inter-judge agreement the paper does not further interrogate. Single author, arXiv v1, no venue. Single-study strength.
