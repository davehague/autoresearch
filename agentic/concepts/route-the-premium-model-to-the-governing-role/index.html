The bill is set by which role holds the premium model, not by which frontier model you chose

Six days after the map first recorded role-based allocation from a 1M-LOC migration — small models implementing in parallel, the largest models reserved for review and for writing the rulebook — a second organization reaches the same allocation independently, and prices it on a single fixed task under four model mixes, which is the comparison the map has wanted and nobody runs.

The structure of the number matters more than its size. Workers do 69–90%+ of the token volume and a small share of the spend; planners do the reverse. So the cost curve is dominated by the role that produces the most tokens, and the quality curve is dominated by the role whose output governs the others. Those are different roles, which is why the cheap-worker configuration lands at $1,339 against $10,565 and still finishes the suite.

This is the map's headline — the unit of routing is the next increment of effort — with a role dimension attached: the increments that justify a premium are the ones whose output is reused. A plan is consumed by every worker under it. A rulebook rewrite changes a whole batch (fix-the-loop-not-the-artifact). A review catches a class rather than an instance. An implementation of one subtask is consumed once. Rank the roles by reuse and the allocation falls out, and it falls out inverted relative to instinct: the frontier model does the least typing.

Two discounts on the headline. First, this is a fan-out over a shared plan, so prefix reuse is high and prompt-caching-inverts-cloud-versus-local-economics applies — the absolute figures are an upper bound on a well-cached deployment and are not portable to a low-reuse workload. Second, the terminal quality criterion is "passed 100% of the test suite" on a suite the vendor selected, with no manual reading of the output; equal-score is doing heavy lifting in "equal terminal quality" (resolve-rate-gains-do-not-carry-quality-gains). The line-count gap between configurations suggests the outputs were not equivalent even where the scores were.

Caveats: T2 vendor post on the vendor's own product; single task; single run per configuration; no full N×N matrix over planner/worker pairs; one model (GPT-5.6 Sol) abandoned mid-study. Suggestive for the rule, single-study for the magnitudes.
