Silent errors dominate agent bug reports

42.4% of hand-labeled agent-reactive bugs produce no observable failure signal at all: no exception, no warning, no retry loop, no non-zero exit — just a fluent answer, an action trace, or a modified workspace that is wrong. This is the field-scale case for why this map's central instruction is to grade the trajectory rather than the outcome (localize-first-wrong-step-not-final-answer).

The cross-tabulation says something sharper than the headline. The two triggers most reliably silent are the two that are claims-versus-evidence mismatches: task-instruction non-compliance goes silent in 81% of cases, and statement fabrication in 90%. That is precisely the signal audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence proposes auditing — the agent asserts it did something, the trajectory shows otherwise, and nothing in the runtime compares the two. It also converts a vivid single case report into a recurring category: compaction laundering a killed process's partial output into a confirmed result (compaction-can-launder-killed-process-output) is one instance of statement fabrication, and there are 20 here from user-filed issues rather than one anecdote.

The consequence for gates is direct and belongs to ci-cd-for-agents as much as here: a silent error passes every check that looks for exceptions or exit codes. Detection requires an oracle that knows what should have happened, which for this bug class frequently does not exist — the authors name absent test oracles as the reason these are hard to catch and hard to reproduce.

Caveats: no inter-rater reliability statistic; reproduction difficulty is asserted qualitatively with no unreproducible-rate, attempt counts, or flakiness figure reported; the symptom distribution is over reported and engaged issues, which plausibly under-counts silent errors relative to crashes (a crash is noticed and filed; a quiet wrong answer may never be attributed to the agent at all), so 42.4% should be read as a lower bound on the silent share.
