Relocating one sentence was worth 19 points; adding 1,900 tokens of guidance was worth −2.3

This map has held for a while that the-specs-format-is-not-what-pays. This refines it into something sharper and more usable: the format does not pay, but the position within the format does — and it pays more than any amount of additional prose about how to read the document.

The manipulation is as clean as this vault gets. The rule text is byte-identical across arms. Only its location moves.

Two things fall out, and the second was unregistered by anyone. Moving the rule into the document's dominant decision structure — the matrix a reader is already consulting to make the decision — takes compliance 23% → 42%. And stating it in both places is worse than stating it once well. The pre-registration allowed for "≈ max, not the sum"; the only anticipated surprise was compounding. Instead, "a second, weaker statement does not reinforce the dominant signal; it competes with it." For anyone who has ever answered a compliance problem by adding a reminder somewhere else in the document, that is the result to internalise.

The negative control on the other side of the same study makes the contrast unmissable. The specification's own consumption protocol — the constraint-propagation and ambiguity-stop principles from its formal model, which an audit revealed had never actually been supplied to implementers — was then supplied with everything else frozen. ≈1,900 tokens of meta-level guidance moved compliance by −2.3 pp, left ambiguity-log precision flat to three decimals, and left executability unchanged. Relocating one sentence: +19. Explaining how to consume the document: nothing. That is progressive-disclosure-buys-context-not-intelligence and instructions-fail-to-govern-non-functional-requirements arriving together, and it bounds what the study may claim: "these documents determine decision X" holds; "the SDD method determines X" does not — now measured rather than untested.

And then the finding that makes compliance unsafe as a headline. Crossing compliance against behavioural outcome decomposes one collection rule into three claims with different verdicts. Its prose is a would-agree-anyway default — implementers write native arrays without it (15/24 against 14/24). Its matrix example is what acts, and it acts destructively: it flips the element type and the declaration carrier, moving both implementers off a valid CREATE DOMAIN … AS TEXT[] they produce unprompted onto an invalid CREATE TYPE … AS VARCHAR(100)[]. Zero invalid declarations in control; ten under the rule. On the compliance axis this reads as a specification success. It is the concrete reason the study refuses to collapse its nine axes into a weighted score, and the reason rule efficacy — does obeying it improve the outcome — has to be measured separately from rule compliance. Compare a-guard-can-raise-the-outcome-score-and-lower-procedural-validity: the same instrument-choice failure, in the other direction.

Carried with the study's own scope condition, which is severe. The placement effect is produced almost entirely by one implementer (38 → 78 → 47 → 64%) while the other floors at 3–6% regardless of where the rule sits. It is implementer-dependent and should not be generalized beyond the tested panel. What travels is the comparison — placement beat restatement, and both beat meta-guidance — not the 19-point number.
