The best gate on the board is the cheapest model on it, thinking as little as possible

This map's routing results almost all run one way: harder task, stronger model, more thinking. The exceptions have been about cost (route-the-premium-model-to-the-governing-role, route-the-session-not-the-query) rather than about a task where the strong setting is worse. Here is one where it is, measured across 30 conditions and six providers on a single pinned prompt.

Capability does not order the leaderboard. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite at minimal reasoning tops the grid at 92.8% mean trial accuracy with the best consistency on the board (91.5% pass^5, the all-five-trials-succeed estimator). The same model at high reasoning falls to 83.4% and its over-refusal rate more than doubles, 14.3% → 30.4%. gpt-5.5 is worse with reasoning on than off. Gemini 3.5 Flash drops nearly ten points. And gpt-oss-20b beats gpt-oss-120b in both error directions, which is the cleanest statement of the finding: model size does not choose the action-boundary reviewer.

The direction is not uniform, and the paper is careful about that — raising reasoning effort repairs a badly miscalibrated gate (gpt-5.4-nano goes 58.5% → 76.4%, shedding an 80.4% over-refusal rate) while leaving a well-calibrated one flat or slightly worse. So the operating rule is not "turn reasoning off." It is that reasoning effort at a gate is an axis you have to measure per model on your own boundary, because the within-model floor-vs-high contrast is the only design that isolates it and the sign is model-dependent.

Two economic consequences follow directly and both cut in the operator's favour.

The gate is a place where the cheap model is genuinely the right model, not a fallback you accept for cost. That is unusual in this map — cheaper-inference-relocates-cost-into-repair-labor is the more common shape — and it pairs with a-deterministic-prefilter-makes-a-model-gate-affordable: if the gate does not want the frontier model and can be prefiltered, the whole control is close to free.

The leaderboard headline and the deployment choice come apart. DeepSeek V4 Pro with reasoning off leads the modal-of-5 vote at 93.4% while sitting at 79.2% pass^5 — it wins the majority verdict and flips between answers across trials. An operator whose gate fires once per action is buying single-call behaviour and should read mean trial accuracy and pass^5; a reader scanning the top line is reading something else. The same distinction a-compressed-leaderboard-is-an-instrument-signal draws for benchmark aggregation shows up here as a concrete routing mistake you can make by reading the wrong column.

Carried with the paper's own restraint: individual model differences are reported descriptively and not tested for significance, and every score is a snapshot of a dated provider endpoint with the roster frozen 2026-06-08. What generalises is the shape — capability does not predict calibration, reasoning effort is model-dependent, size does not choose the gate — not any particular row.
