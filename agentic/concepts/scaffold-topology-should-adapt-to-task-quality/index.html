Grade the task, then pick the topology

icat-agent runs a quality rubric over the incoming issue before committing to a workflow shape. Well-defined issues go straight to parallel patching plus validation; under-specified issues get an exploration phase first. One scaffold, two topologies, chosen per task.

This extends harness-is-tuned-per-model-and-version along an orthogonal axis. That claim says the harness must be re-tuned as the model changes; this one says it should re-shape as the task changes, at runtime, per issue. It is also the harness-layer echo of multi-agent-helps-only-decomposable-tasks: if coordination's sign depends on task shape, then a scaffold that measures task shape and switches accordingly is the natural engineering response to that finding — the scaling study's own architecture-selector predicted the best topology for 87% of held-out configurations, which is the same idea offline. icat-agent does it online.

The load-bearing framing claim is that the scaffold "unlocks latent model capability": the weights already could do it, and the structure was in the way. That sits with evolve-the-harness-from-trajectory-observability (structure over prompts) and with separate-the-generator-from-the-evaluator — the parallel patch-and-validate branch is a generator/evaluator split, spawned conditionally.

Practical read: an under-specified ticket is not just harder, it wants a different pipeline. Most production fleets run one fixed shape per ticket regardless of how well the ticket is written, and emergent-specs-corrupt-the-artifact says under-specification is the expensive case. A cheap triage rubric in front of the fleet is the intervention both results point at.

Caveat: no ablation isolates the rubric/pivot from the event-passing substrate (event-passing-beats-shared-context-in-agent-fleets), so the effect size is the pair's, not this mechanism's alone. Benchmark contamination caveats apply as they do to the parent source.
