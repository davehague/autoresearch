The miner and the injector were the same imagination

This map has repeatedly found that a benchmark's score is a joint property of the system and the instrument (benchmarks-conflate-model-harness-and-penalize-alternatives and the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order state it for the harness layer). Here is the version specific to detectors, and it is unusual because the authors ran it against themselves.

The setup that produces the good number is honest and normal: contracts mined by cross-fitting on clean task-disjoint traces, evaluated against the benchmark's own perturbation taxonomy plus generic invariant classes. 79.6% recall at a 1.39% false-positive rate. Every published detector number you will read is produced this way.

The setup that produces the real number is the one nobody runs. Transcribe eight fault types from a published taxonomy of production silent failures, have the fault author work from the taxonomy and fixture shapes only — never the contract classes — and inject them into the same workflows. Recall falls to ~46%. And the split inside that number is the actionable part: 83% for violations a structured value can express (an out-of-set status, a negative quantity, a broken conservation relation) against 22% for corruption inside a plausible string. The contract vocabulary is the ceiling, and the modes it cannot see are exactly the ones that arrive in the expected format.

The authors name the mechanism as a threat to their own positive studies: for two of the three environments they designed the faults so the contract sources could express them, so those studies test whether the signal is actionable, not whether the detector finds arbitrary faults. Detector–injector alignment is the detection-side sibling of the-capability-ranking-survives-the-rewrite-that-degrades-the-agent — the evaluation and the thing evaluated share an author's model of what can go wrong.

Two further numbers make this more than a caveat. On the incident suite, completion is null in both tiers while corrupted values propagate in roughly one episode in five, and about two thirds of those episodes still pass. Task completion is not a proxy for absence of downstream corruption, so a detector evaluated only on completion is being graded on a metric that cannot see its residual failure mode (cf. an-aggregate-gain-and-a-reliable-regression-coexist-in-every-cell). And on 53,078 organic responses from 427 public tools — no agent, no counterfactual — the unchanged detector fires on 0.80%, concentrated in 54 tools, against 1.39% on synthetic clean workflows. The base rate a detector meets in the wild is not the base rate its benchmark gave it.

The transferable practice is cheap. Before quoting a detector's recall, commission one fault set from somebody who has not read the detector, out of a source the detector's authors did not write, and report both numbers side by side. This study did, and the gap is the finding.
