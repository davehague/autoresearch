The transform cannot be its own receipt

This map's founding argument is that independence is a property of the arrangement, not of the checker's identity — what it is denied, who authored what it checks, where its expected value comes from. Here is the same argument in a place nobody was looking for it: the context-management layer, where the "checker" is a step that reports on the fidelity of its own output.

The measurement is a clean natural ablation. On configurations whose safety-critical content nearly fills the token budget, the pipeline with a deterministic post-compaction verifier escalates to an explicit Unsafe verdict on 28% of them — refusing to certify a compaction it could not perform correctly. The identical pipeline without it reports apparent 1.00 recall and has dropped a mean 57% of the constraints. Not silently degraded output with a hedged label: full-confidence success.

Why this belongs to this map rather than to context engineering alone. The failing arrangement is not a weak checker — there is no checker. The transforming step is reporting on itself, so its expected value comes from the same process that produced the artifact, which is the exact cancellation an-oracle-anchored-on-the-code-it-judges-cannot-fail proves for a numeric assertion, instantiated here as a preservation metric computed by the thing doing the dropping. The fix has the map's usual shape and costs almost nothing: a separate deterministic pass over the compacted output that checks each pinned item is present verbatim, with zero model calls, plus a defined refusal state when the budget cannot hold what must be held.

The refusal state is the underrated half. A verifier that can only say pass/fail on the artifact still leaves the system with an impossible job — it must emit something at the budget. Adding Unsafe as a first-class outcome lets the arrangement decline rather than silently choose, which is the same design move as price-the-checkers-abstention-as-lost-coverage: an honest abstention is information, and a forced verdict at capacity is not.

And the composition result matters. Removing any one of the three components — indexing-time labels, the verifier, the escalation — drops the system below 0.50. They are not redundant safety margins. They fail on different inputs: labels fail on declarative phrasing, the verifier fails to catch what was never labeled, and the escalation covers the case where correct labeling still cannot fit. Which is an-executable-check-and-a-model-judge-fail-on-different-inputs's complementarity argument holding for three deterministic components rather than for a rule and a judge.

Bounds. One system, one corpus, and the verifier's job here is narrow and mechanical — checking presence of pinned strings, not judging semantic preservation. That narrowness is why it is deterministic and free, and it is also why nothing here says a verifier can catch a weakened rule; the human study on the same runs found a Weakened class where key tokens survive but a qualifier is dropped, which a presence check passes. The 57% figure comes from the 1.4% of configurations at the density boundary, not from the typical case.
