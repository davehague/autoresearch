The gate on a metric is a different question from the gate on a model, and getting it wrong costs everything

This map's catalogue is mostly about numbers that are too generous. This is a result about how a checking rule earns its place, and it lands on a mistake that looks like rigour: asking a newly authored check to cover a decent fraction of the faults you already know about.

That floor is exactly backwards for a selection endpoint. A sparse, precise operator fails a recall floor by construction and is precisely what the endpoint wants — the metric's job is to change what remains to draw from, so one high-confidence flag on a bad candidate beats broad coverage that also removes good ones. Run inside the same loop, on the same candidates, with the same training labels and the same number of authoring calls, the recall-gated arm admits nothing on any of three seeds while the deployed-decision arm admits on all three. Not "helps less" — stops admission outright. And the operator the study eventually ships was explicitly rejected by the recall arm for covering none of the 140 fault classes the pool still missed.

The complementary half is the composition step, and it is unusual in this vault because it contains no inference at all: every one of 313,911 and 2,007,327 candidate subsets was scored, so "which objective should I select on" becomes arithmetic. The natural-looking objective — TP − FP, count the good flags, subtract the bad ones — ranks its own argmax as low as the 4.9th percentile of the space it is searching, and its correlation with the true endpoint is positive only for pairs, going negative (−0.338) by six operators. The cause is measured and it is bias rather than variance: refitting on more labelled data converges below the frozen rule, so more data does not rescue it. The repair is one number. Balanced accuracy is monotone in TP − 0.188·FP, weighting a false alarm 5.33× less than the naive objective, and it lands in the top decile of the enumerated landscape in all four cells. The weight is read off the training split's own class ratio and is shown to be a property of selection endpoints rather than of these labels: a twelve-point λ sweep puts HumanEval+'s optimal plateau around the same ratio in 4 of 4 cells while λ=1 falls strictly outside every time.

The safety property is the one to carry. Once checks are admitted on the deployed decision, composition is safe over its entire combinatorial closure — of 2,007,292 non-vacuous subsets, zero harm the endpoint, established by enumeration rather than by a power argument. The converse holds too, and it is the cautionary half: admission accepts none of the 15 hand-written operators, and those 15 applied together as one filter lose accuracy. Hand-authored checks that each look sensible do not compose into a metric; admitted ones do.

What this asks of anyone reporting a checker. State the endpoint the check was admitted against, and state the class weight used to combine checks. Neither is currently reported anywhere, and the enumeration shows both are worth more than the choice of authoring model. Bounds. One domain (Python from MBPP+ / HumanEval+), chosen precisely because it has an exact oracle that can be withheld; both benchmarks come from one distribution family. Only 58 of 428 held-out tasks are decidable at this sampling depth, so absolute deltas are small by construction. Every ∆ is measured against flagging nothing and never against another rule — the shuffle null licenses "this beats doing nothing" and explicitly not "A beats B" — and no two rules separated in nine paired tests.
