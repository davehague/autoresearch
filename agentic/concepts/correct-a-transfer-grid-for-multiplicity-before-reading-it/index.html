One hundred and thirty-five hypotheses, one survivor

A transfer study produces a grid, and a grid is seductive: nine sources by nine targets, colour-coded, with visible regions of green. This one has cells running from −9.5 to +11 points and, read cell by cell, would support almost any narrative you brought to it — that bigger sources transfer better, that same-family transfer is safer, that retail is the forgiving domain. Under global Holm correction across all 135 route hypotheses, exactly one route survives.

That is the discipline this map keeps asking for, applied to a study design the vault had not yet covered. The existing family says a delta is not a result until its null is measured — a paired same-model floor (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor), a content-free placebo (a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm), a no-router arm (a-routing-claim-needs-a-no-router-ablation), a withheld hint (a-containment-claim-needs-a-withheld-hint-arm). Multiplicity is the null you get for free by looking at enough cells, and it is the one an agent-transfer grid manufactures by construction, because the grid is the experiment.

What the source does instead is the transferable part. Rather than mining the grid and reporting the survivors, it defines a route-agnostic aggregate contrast — the same artifact on its matched domain against two mismatched domains, controlling prompt length and format within source, across all 81 eligible configurations — and reports that as the primary inference: +1.72 points, CI95 [+0.25, +2.97], p = .035 by exact blocked permutation. Then it says what that supports and what it does not: a modest average domain-alignment advantage, explicitly not improvement on most routes. The grid is retained as descriptive evidence that transfer is heterogeneous rather than smooth, which is itself a finding, just not a route-level one.

Three details worth stealing:

  • The correction level is stated and both are reported. Family-specific Holm retains two negative cross-family routes that global Holm does not, and a Benjamini–Hochberg FDR analysis is reported as a sensitivity check rather than swapped in as the headline. Naming which family you corrected within is the difference between a correction and a choice.
  • Failure to survive is not read as zero. The paper says so explicitly. That is the correct reading of a non-significant cell in an underpowered grid, and it is the same demand as report-a-null-as-a-bounded-interval-not-a-failed-test — the interval, not the verdict.
  • The confirmatory family is fenced. Pass@2–4, cost, and the exploratory frozen-versus-redistillation comparison are declared descriptive and kept out of the 135, so secondary analyses cannot be promoted after the fact.

Why this matters beyond one paper. Almost every artifact-reuse result an engineering team will read is a grid: this context file across these repositories, this skill bank across these agents, this prompt across these models. The vault already carries results whose headline is a small number of impressive cells (a-skill-set-helps-the-weaker-harness-and-hurts-the-stronger is one, and the sign flip there is real), and the standing question of this atlas — whether any measurement of an agent can be trusted — has an uncomfortable answer here: on a grid this size, a handful of double-digit cells is what noise looks like. The operational form is blunt. If someone shows you a transfer heatmap, ask how many cells were computed and what the prespecified contrast was. If the answer is that the heatmap is the analysis, the heatmap is a hypothesis generator.

Bounds. One benchmark for the corrected grid (TAU2-Bench), Qwen2.5-Instruct / Qwen3 / Llama3.1 targets, and the aggregate contrast is itself a single number resting on a blocked permutation over configurations that share sources — the study's own strongest inference is modest and it says so. The correction discipline demonstrated here is a protocol claim, and protocol claims travel further than the effect they were used to report.
