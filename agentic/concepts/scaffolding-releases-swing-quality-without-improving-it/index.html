Scaffolding releases move quality 16 points in both directions and cost 70% more

Fix the model, vary only the middleware, and walk 35 sequential releases of one scaffolding against 50 stratified SWE-bench Verified tasks: resolve rate ranges 23.0%–39.0% (mean 30.5%) with no significant improving trend (ρ=0.208, p=0.231), while average tokens climb 391K→668K (+70%) with a strong monotonic trend (ρ=0.743, p<0.0001). Users of that scaffolding paid 70% more over the window for capability no better than where it started, and had no way to know which release they were on was a good one. Regression risk localizes: the LLM Provider layer and Context Management are the hot spots; Extensibility and Security changes are comparatively safe. The ecosystem context makes it worse — release velocities up to 18/week and issue backlogs in the thousands.

Half of this confirms the map and half of it bites. The confirming half is attribute-performance-to-the-stack-not-the-model on a third task family with a stronger design — within one scaffolding over time rather than between scaffoldings at a point — and a 16-point swing inside the middleware means any benchmark comparison naming the model but not the scaffolding version carries an uncontrolled term larger than most claimed model gaps. The biting half is that this is harness improvement, run by humans, in public, at high velocity, for 35 releases, and it does not climb. Arriving alongside harness-evolution-underperforms-matched-budget-test-time-scaling — the automated version losing to plain sampling under matched budgets — the joint statement is that neither the human nor the automated version of harness improvement has shown a durable, generalizing gain under controlled measurement. That is what moves agent-harness-architecture to contested on its self-evolution limb.

Two ripples. The token trend is a cost lever nobody watches (model-routing-token-economics): spend drifts up as a side effect of dependency upgrades, and move-task-difficulty-from-the-model-to-the-harness's optimism about tuning the cheap tier assumes a stable substrate this says you don't have — pin your scaffolding version the way you pin your model. And the Context Management hot spot is pointed at context-engineering: the surface this vault treats as highest-leverage is also the most regression-prone.

Caveats: one scaffolding studied deeply (the other four characterized only on release/issue velocity), so the no-trend result is n=1 at the scaffolding level — not "scaffolding evolution doesn't work" in general. 50-task slice, single run per release, so part of the 23–39% band is unmeasured sampling noise; the paper does not floor it, which is exactly coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor's complaint. Component risk is association from commit tracing, not intervention.
