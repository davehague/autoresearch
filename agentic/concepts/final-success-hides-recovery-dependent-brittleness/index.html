A new, benchmark-portable instrument for this map's standing question — final-success rate alone cannot distinguish an agent design that executes cleanly from one that stumbles and recovers

This map's standing concern is whether a number describes the agent at all, as opposed to the apparatus or a flawed proxy. Prefix executability gives that concern a trajectory-level instrument rather than another outcome judge to audit: instead of scoring only the final state, it scores every prefix of the tool-call sequence against schema, precondition, and data-dependency constraints, and reports a family of metrics (TER, AUC-PEC, First-Error Depth, Recovery Rate) alongside final success.

The ranking-reversal result is the sharp part: Plan-Conditioned ReAct is the best-scoring design by final success on the BFCL-v3/Llama-4-Maverick cell, while being the worst by executability — nearly every one of its "successes" passed through at least one invalid prefix along the way and recovered. That means an evaluator relying on final success alone would recommend the most recovery-dependent, least-clean design as the best one. Dual-Loop's result — adding pre-execution plan verification closes both the executability and the final-success gap together — is the constructive half: process reliability and outcome correctness are not necessarily in tension; a cheap verification step can buy both at once, at least on these two benchmarks.

Interaction edge: harness-as-score-confound — this is a metric-validity instance of that map's apparatus-vs-object distinction, applied within a single harness/model rather than across harnesses: the same model and task can be ranked oppositely by two equally legitimate measurements of "did it work."

Bounds: results are model- and benchmark-dependent point estimates without paired significance testing, a limitation the authors themselves note explicitly for tau3-bench; several Recovery-Rate- eligible subsets are small (7-13 episodes for Dual-Loop in multiple cells), making those specific RR estimates imprecise; three LLMs and three benchmarks, not a sweep across production coding-agent harnesses.
