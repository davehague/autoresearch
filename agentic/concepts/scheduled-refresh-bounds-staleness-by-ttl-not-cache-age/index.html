The variable a drift benchmark sweeps has to actually reach the component under test — cache age doesn't, once the system maintains its own indexes, and a lapse-count metric can silently mean two opposite things

This map's single pipeline argument runs from acquisition through selection to serving and disclosure. ChurnBench prices the serving stage specifically: once material has been selected and staged (a cache, a materialized aggregate, a vector index sitting over a live source), how does its age relate to whether it's still true when a query hits it? The paper's central move is architectural rather than statistical — it builds ground truth as a bi-temporal ledger so a "correct when retrieved, wrong when evaluated" answer can be mechanically distinguished from an answer that was never correct at all — and the finding that follows is a clean negative result stated as a benchmark-design lesson: drift-window length, the variable every existing RAG/agent freshness benchmark would naturally sweep, is the wrong variable for any system that refreshes on a schedule, because a bounded TTL means the variable never actually reaches the retrieval layer. The controlled ablation is what elevates this from a plausible mechanism to a demonstrated one: turning off the exact same refresh code, with nothing else changed, moves the same measurement 11x at the long window and 0x at the short one — the signature a TTL-bounded mechanism predicts and a cache-age-driven one does not.

The attribution result sharpens what "serving the right thing" means for this map: staleness exposure is not a property of a tier's width alone, nor of an entity's mutation rate alone, but of their product — the widest tier here is technically the most stale (contract terms, never refreshed within the tested windows) but produces zero errors because those entities barely change, while the tightest tier (1-day TTL, the fastest-changing entities) also produces zero errors under tiering specifically because its short TTL keeps it perpetually fresh. Only the middle tier, where TTL width and change rate actually overlap, shows any exposure. This gives the map a concrete design rule for its serving stage: TTL assignment should be driven by measured per-entity mutation rate, not by intuition about which data "feels important," and a benchmark's own instrumentation needs to record per-entity refresh timestamps rather than lapse-event counts, since a lapse-count metric reads identically as zero for "this entity never goes stale" and for "the staleness check never ran at all" — a measurement trap the paper catches and names directly.

Interaction edges: unattended-operation — a durable, unattended agent fleet reading from cached or staged context over long horizons is exactly the deployment shape this finding targets: the paper's practical guidance (tier TTL to measured entity change rate, not to a static schedule) is an operational rule for exactly the "what does an unattended fleet need when nobody is watching" facet this map tracks, distinct from durable state or credential governance but adjacent to both. observability — the freshness-error/reasoning-error split, made possible only by resolving ground truth at two timestamps from an authoritative ledger, is a diagnostic technique for telling a data-freshness fault apart from a model-reasoning fault, complementary to this map's run-localization sources.

Bounds: single model (a NIM-served Nemotron variant), single agent framework (LangGraph), single domain (software asset management), and entirely synthetic data — calibration of two of the five mutation-rate streams rests on practitioner judgment rather than published statistics, and prevalence claims are explicitly stated not to transfer to real estates. No baseline architecture comparison was run under the final design (naive tool-calling and single-index retrieval arms exist in the released code but were not evaluated on the reported 180-task set), so the paper makes no claim that its staged/federated grounding architecture beats alternatives. Statistical power on the raw freshness- error sweep is limited (counts of 4-7 per 180-task cell, differences of at most 3) — the paper does not rest the negative cache-age finding on the sweep alone, backing it with the direct per-entity refresh-timestamp mechanism evidence and the much larger ablation gap (4 vs 45) instead.
