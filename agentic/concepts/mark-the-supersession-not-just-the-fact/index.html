The cheap intervention is a written value chain committed before the answer — and the expensive machinery is the part that over-fires

Given that state-drift-survives-perfect-retrieval, the question is what to build. This source answers it with an ablation rather than an architecture pitch, and the ordering is the useful part.

Extraction is not the lever. Parsing turns into structured units on its own recovers 0.174 — barely above the dense-retrieval baseline. What moves the number is marking that a unit was superseded: 0.174 → 0.298, the largest single step in the ablation. Then telling the answerer to recompute rather than quote is worth another 6 points. Both are bookkeeping about validity, not about content, which is the point.

And the aggressive component loses money. Deterministic dependency propagation — walk the graph, flag everything downstream of a changed unit as needing recheck — helps its target modes and costs 12.5 points on anti-traps, the scenarios where the anchored answer is still correct and only an over-eager invalidation policy diverges. Removing it leaves the stronger backbone marginally better overall. That anti-trap arm is the design lesson: a state tracker built without one is optimising for the failure it was named after and will happily invent the opposite failure. Compare compaction-selection-is-all-or-nothing and goal-hinted-pruning-beats-generic-compression — the same asymmetry, that an eviction or invalidation policy needs a control for what it wrongly discards.

The wrapper is the finding a practitioner can use tomorrow. Strip the persistent store entirely. Take the transcript, the backend's retrieved chunks and the question, and replace the answer call the backend was going to make anyway — so no extra LLM calls, overhead of a fixed 155-token instruction and a ≤250-word trace. The trace writes, for every slot the question touches, the initial value, each revision and the current operative value in chronological order with turn numbers, plus the latest stated inputs of every standing rule. It is committed before the answer, which blocks post-hoc justification — the same mechanism as anchor-a-verification-claim-to-the-content-it-was-checked-against and stop-when-two-audits-against-a-frozen-referent-come-back-empty: fix the referent before the conclusion can move it. Then four precedence rules resolve: later supersedes earlier, standing rules outrank instances, derived quantities are recomputed rather than quoted, a fact is retired only by explicit supersession or expiry.

It improves every backend on both benchmarks, +31.7 to +66.6 points on the state-tracking benchmark. And the control is what makes that number worth anything: a call-count-, word-budget-, format- and context-matched generic extraction prompt captures a large share of it, leaving +15 to +32 attributable to the state structure rather than to writing more words before answering. Very little in this vault's context-engineering literature ships a matched-volume control, and it is the difference between a technique and a token-count artefact.

The result that should reframe the build-versus-prompt decision. The wrapper beats the full persistent-store system on the same scenarios. The authors' reading is the honest one: when the transcript fits in context, resolving state at answer time is enough, and the store earns its keep only when it does not. That is a direct extension of memory-delivery-not-storage — and a warning against reaching for a memory framework when the actual defect is that nobody told the answerer which value is in force. It also composes with restate-the-checks-externally-at-completion: re-presenting the operative state at the decision it governs is the same move as re-presenting the contract at the completion decision, and both beat expecting the model to hold it.

Bounds. StateMem mirrors the lazy-reader policy family that generated the benchmark's traps, so the authors read the ablation margins as an upper bound and point at the external benchmarks as the generalisation test — where the system leads all memory systems but the margins are single-digit and concentrated on drift-shaped question types. Synthetic scenarios, well short of a modern context window. Single run per configuration at temperature 0, judge sharing a model family with one substrate, scenarios rendered by Claude-family models. The store form costs ~165–600 encoder calls per scenario — substantially more than a long-context model on its own — and it is the wrapper, not the store, that carries the matched control.
