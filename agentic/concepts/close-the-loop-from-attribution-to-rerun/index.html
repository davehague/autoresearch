The map's signal, finally wired to an action

localize-first-wrong-step-not-final-answer has been this map's founding instruction, and decisive-errors-strike-early-and-surface-late gave it its sharpest justification — the decisive error lands at median step 7 of ~27 and surfaces ~10 steps later, with 57.9% of decisive errors epistemic, meaning the catching evidence was already in the trajectory. Everything the map held was diagnostic. This closes the loop: Detect → Attribute → Recover → Rerun, with the rerun starting from the attributed checkpoint.

The comparison is the point. Three named self-correction baselines all repair from the symptom — the visible failure at the end — and land at 4–6 of 73. Attributing first and rerunning from step k lands at 13. That gap is the operational value of the median-step-7-surfaces-at-step-17 finding, priced: a retry that begins where the error surfaced is starting ten steps downstream of the thing it needs to change.

It composes cleanly with the two recovery findings loop-engineering carries. The default post-failure action every harness ships — repair from execution feedback — is the worst of three fixed policies at 27.5%, with ~30% of failures reached by none of them (recovery-actions-are-not-a-capability-ladder); this is the constructive complement, and the reason it works is that it is not a fixed policy at all but a diagnosis selecting the retry point. And it stays on the safe side of repair-loops-have-an-interior-optimum: one rerun from an attributed checkpoint, not an open-ended verify-repair loop whose true validity peaks at round 2 and then falls below baseline.

Two implementation details are transferable independently of the results. Detection runs deterministic rule packs first and escalates to LLM judgment only for what they cannot decide, which is enforce-invariants-not-implementations applied to observability. And attribution is an explicit cost ladder — heuristics, one whole-trace pass, binary search, per-step inspection, budgeted ensembles — rather than a single strategy, which is expose-a-cost-quality-dial-not-a-fixed-policy inside a diagnostic tool.

Caveats, and one is load-bearing. The evaluation is GAIA and Who&When — general agentic and assistant traces, not coding agents; the method travels, the magnitudes should not. The authors state the GAIA experiment "neither isolates attribution's effect alone nor is a blind single-shot score," so +7.8 points bundles attribution with the rerun and no arm separates them. Recovery execution is gated on human approval, so this is not evidence for an unattended repair loop (keep-the-agent-interruptible-and-reversible). Strong for the ordering, single-benchmark for the magnitude.
