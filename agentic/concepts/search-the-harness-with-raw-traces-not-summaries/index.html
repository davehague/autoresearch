Search the harness with raw traces, not summaries

Every prior text optimizer compresses its feedback — a window of (solution, score) pairs, a scalar reward, a short reflective summary — spending 0.002–0.026 MTok per iteration. Meta-Harness spends 10.0 MTok, giving its proposer unrestricted filesystem access to the source code, scores and execution traces of every candidate ever evaluated, retrieved by grep and cat rather than packed into a prompt. The ablation isolating that choice is the finding: holding proposer, search procedure and evaluation budget fixed and varying only what the proposer may read, scores-only reaches 34.6 median accuracy, scores-plus-LLM-summary reaches 34.9, and full trace access reaches 50.0. On best-candidate accuracy the summarized condition is worse than raw scores (38.7 vs 41.3) — the summary spent tokens and removed signal.

The mechanism the authors give is horizon. A harness decision — what to store, when to retrieve it, how to present it — surfaces as a failure many reasoning steps later, so compressed feedback removes precisely the evidence needed to trace a downstream failure back to the design choice that caused it. The proposer's observed behaviour matches: it reads a median of 82 files per iteration, and in the qualitative search trace it notices that two candidates regressed together, hypothesizes the regressions were confounded by a shared prompt edit, isolates that edit, and pivots to a safer additive change. None of that is reachable from a scalar.

The scope boundary matters. This does not contradict prune-and-summarize-beats-full-history or goal-hinted-pruning-beats-generic-compression, which measure an agent executing a task and are right that verbose tool output should not accumulate in its context. The consumer here is different: an optimizer diagnosing why prior attempts failed, for which the discarded detail is the whole point. The two together give a rule with an edge — compress what the actor carries forward, preserve what the diagnostician can reach back for — and the retrieval pattern that makes the second affordable is the vault's usual one, queried at point of use rather than pre-packed (just-in-time-context-retrieval, dynamic-context-beats-static-preloading, one-level-of-disclosure-is-enough).

Two adjacent readings. It is an observability claim before it is an optimization one: trace fidelity is load-bearing for improvement, which is audit-agent-claims-against-trajectory-evidence and evolve-the-harness-from-trajectory-observability pointed at the harness itself, and it rhymes with patch-minimization-is-search-not-generation — the trajectory is a durable artifact worth computing over, not exhaust. And the discovered harness beats ACE (Agentic Context Engineering) by 7.7 points while using 4.5× fewer context tokens (11.4K vs 50.8K), so accuracy and context cost moved together rather than trading, with the full Pareto frontier reported instead of one operating point (expose-a-cost-quality-dial-not-a-fixed-policy, restrict-the-tool-surface-to-cut-cost-not-capability).

Caveats: the ablation is measured in one domain (online text classification over three datasets), which the authors note is their smallest-context setting — they argue the gap should widen in harder regimes, which is a prediction, not a result. One proposer agent throughout (Claude Code on Opus 4.6), so the finding is a harness-model-pair property until someone varies it (the-harness-shifts-benchmark-rank-order). Small populations (40 candidates) and no seeds or intervals on the ablation cells.
