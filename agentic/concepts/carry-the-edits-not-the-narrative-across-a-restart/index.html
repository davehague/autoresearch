What crosses the restart boundary

On 2026-07-30 this map took a placebo-controlled result that looked like an instruction to discard everything: the entire informational payload of execution feedback is worth +0.000 / +0.026 / +0.008, while putting the failed attempt back in context costs −0.061 and −0.069, and blind resampling wins outright below 7B (showing-a-failed-attempt-anchors-the-retry, a-retry-study-needs-a-placebo-arm). This study runs the blind arm on a coding-agent harness — cold restart is exactly blind resampling, with a monitor choosing the moment — and it is worth +0.2 points. Then it changes one thing and gets +5.2.

The thing it changes is the channel. The retry inherits no prompt history at all. What it inherits is the dead run's repository diff, mounted as a removable git apply-backed overlay that starts off and is described to the agent as an unverified lead from a stopped attempt, which it may inspect, apply or discard. The authors report having tried the intuitive alternative — summarize the aborted trajectory into prompt context — and abandoned it because "verbose text summaries induce severe LLM anchoring effects, prematurely locking the restarting agent into the prior attempt's faulty reasoning paths." They arrived at the vault's anchoring finding from the design side and routed around it.

So the map's rule sharpens rather than reverses: the penalty is on the narrative, and the value is in the artifact. A retry that inherits the reasoning inherits the error that produced it. A retry that inherits the edits, with no history and an explicit licence to throw them away, inherits the work. The evidence is on both sides of the ledger: RestartSmart recovers more genuine failures (28.9% vs 20.2%) and destroys far fewer runs that would have passed anyway (8.8% FP lost vs 27.5%) — a cold restart discards a false alarm's progress entirely, which is why its recovery gains are cancelled by its collateral damage.

Two second-order details are the practical content.

Where you cut decides what you carry. An abort frequently lands mid-edit, and an overlay assembled from a fragment is worse than none. The policy locates the first edit at or after the alarm and extends until five consecutive edit-free steps have passed, so the diff is a settled change. Cutting at the alarm instead takes FP lost from 8.8% to 18.8% and the resolve gain from +5.2 to +2.2 — over half the effect is in the patience window.

The larger policy uses the overlay better. The 27B model critically evaluates the prior edits and restores false-alarm runs to completion, where the weaker policies gain less. That is the opposite direction from the placebo study's finding that the anchoring penalty shrinks as first attempts improve, and it is consistent with it: what scales is the ability to judge an offered artifact, which is a capability the blind-resample regime never exercises.

The same paper supplies a third data point for the capability-asymmetry rule the vault keeps meeting. Its prompt-intervention baseline (a process reward model course-correcting the live trajectory) adds +4.2 points on the weaker policy and subtracts 3.2 on the stronger one — matching a-weak-reviewer-rewrites-where-a-strong-one-edits and review-revise-loop-lifts-resolve-most-on-weak-generators. Injecting guidance into a capable agent's reasoning is the harmful cell. Handing it an artifact it is free to refuse is not.

Bounds. One benchmark (SWE-bench Verified), one scaffold (mini-swe-agent), 100 test instances, five seeds — better than this literature's norm and still thin at these magnitudes. The +5.2 is bought with +43.8% net tokens after early-stop savings, so it is a compute-for-accuracy trade rather than a free gain, and it requires a 25% false-positive budget, i.e. aborting a quarter of runs that would have succeeded. The anchoring mechanism is asserted from a design decision rather than measured in an arm: no summary-overlay condition is reported, so "the narrative is the poison" is inference from two studies pointing the same way, not a controlled contrast inside this one.
