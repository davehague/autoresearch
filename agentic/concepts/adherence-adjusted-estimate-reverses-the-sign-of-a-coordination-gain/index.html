The headline "team beats single agent" number can be measuring the wrong tasks -- and nobody who only reports the aggregate delta would ever know

This map has spent months accumulating reasons a reported multi-agent coordination gain might not mean what it claims: unfloored deltas that a measured noise floor could swallow whole, a placebo arm that would separate "the information helped" from "another sample helped," run-to-run variance exceeding the gap being attributed to architecture. This source adds a mechanism none of those name: the aggregate delta can be positive because the agents didn't coordinate, not despite it. Under the unconstrained reference configuration, the +5.6-point headline gain decomposes into +15.8 on the 19 tasks where the declared organization never actually happened (the lead agent monopolized the budget, worked alone, or bypassed its teammates) and -5.9 on the 17 tasks where it did. A reader who only sees the +5.6 or even the naive Adherence-corrected +11.8 would conclude coordination helped; the paired per-task decomposition says the opposite is closer to true in this configuration, and the "team" framing is doing essentially nothing for the cases where it was real.

The instrument that makes this visible — Adherence as a binary per-run compliance verdict, feeding a Wald-ratio CACE estimator borrowed wholesale from causal inference's complier-average-treatment-effect literature — is itself the more durable contribution. It gives this map, for the first time, a general answer to "was the team result measuring the organization or measuring something else" that doesn't require a bespoke placebo design per paper: audit whether the declared topology ran, stratify by that audit, and report both strata rather than only the pooled one. The ten-framework audit (none of AutoGen, AG2, LangGraph, or the other mainstream multi-agent frameworks can compute this for every run they execute) says this measurement has been available to no one building on current infrastructure — every past multi-agent coordination number this map holds was produced by frameworks structurally unable to tell the reader whether collaboration happened.

The constrained-configuration result (97.2%/91.7% Adherence, CACE and the adherent-paired-estimate agreeing at +3.0) is the positive control this decomposition needed: when the organization reliably runs as declared, the naive and adherence-corrected estimates converge, which is what should happen if the instrument is measuring something real rather than manufacturing a correction out of noise.

Interaction edge: evaluation-integrity — a general-purpose compliance-auditing instrument for "did the configured system actually run as configured" is exactly the kind of measurement-validity tooling that map's standing contamination/identifiability concern has been asking every benchmark claim for; this is a candidate template other multi-agent and tool-use benchmarks could adopt, not just a result confined to coding.

Standing caution for this map: every prior "team beats single agent" delta this map carries was produced by frameworks this audit shows cannot compute an Adherence-equivalent stratification, so none of them can currently be checked against the specific failure mode demonstrated here — a positive pooled delta driven entirely by the non-compliant subset. That is a gap in the map's existing evidence, not a refutation of any one of its notes.

Scope: the 17-vs-19 adherent/non-adherent split that overturns the Open-card headline result is a post-hoc subgroup decomposition on a 36-task sample, not an independently replicated or pre-registered design; each configuration cell in the deployment-contrast table runs its task set once rather than across seeds, so the -5.9-vs-+15.8 split itself carries unquantified sampling noise this map's own noise-floor concern (coordination-deltas-need-a-measured-noise-floor) would ask to be measured before leaning on the sign.
