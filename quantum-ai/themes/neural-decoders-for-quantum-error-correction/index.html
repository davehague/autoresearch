Neural decoders for quantum error correction

Status: contested

Sub-threads: none — opened 2026-11-04 with 6 bullets; too early to split [reviewed 2026-11-11 @ 8 bullets]

Scope

Can a learned (neural-network) decoder beat the best classical decoders on accuracy at the latency a real quantum processor demands, and on more than small codes? Covers neural decoders for surface, color and qLDPC codes, real-time and FPGA/GPU deployment, and the classical decoders (matching, belief propagation, search) that they must beat. Decoders are the classical half of error correction, so this is where AI most directly touches the hardware milestones in below-threshold-error-correction and the timelines in fault-tolerance-timelines-roadmaps-and-resource-estimates.

Through-line

  • 2026-11-11 — a third classical BP variant beats Relay-BP, and a neural decoder for a third hardware modality reaches distance 8 on an FPGA. PriorTilt, a non-learned belief-propagation decoder, beats both Relay-BP and beam search at every tested point on three qLDPC codes, cutting logical error on the [144,12,12] Gross code by about 4.3x versus Relay-BP at p = 1e-3 (ye-priortilt-decoding-quantum-ldpc-codes-with-sparse-perturbations-of-belief-propagation-priors) — Relay-BP's claim atom now carries a second rebutted-by link, alongside Cascade's. Separately, PRISM, a transformer decoder built for PsiQuantum's fusion-based (photonic) architecture, matches integer-programming decoder accuracy on surface, color and qLDPC codes up to distance 24 using known erasure locations, and an FPGA implementation decodes distance-8 logical blocks at 1.56 microsecond amortized latency (barter-machine-learning-decoders-for-fusion-based-quantum-computing-in-real-time) — a real latency number beyond the vault's prior distance-3 FPGA ceiling, but measured on erasure data rather than in a live loop on an existing photonic processor, since fusion-based hardware at this scale does not yet exist. Status stays contested: the classical side keeps winning the accuracy race (now three non-learned contenders ahead of or rivaling Relay-BP), while the neural side's hardware-latency evidence inches forward on a new error model rather than closing the gap on the original one.
  • 2026-11-07 — the Mamba decoder is now peer-reviewed; the evidence itself is unchanged. lee-mamba-scalable-neural-decoders-for-practical-real-time-qec published in npj Quantum Information on 2026-10-28, upgrading it from preprint to peer-reviewed. The finding stays simulation-only and read at abstract level here, so this does not move the theme's contested status, only one source's evidence tier.
  • 2026-11-06 — a second non-learned decoder rivals Relay-BP, widening the classical side. A dithered belief-propagation decoder beats standard BP and BP+OSD+CS-10 under circuit-level noise on a distance-11 surface code and a distance-12 bivariate-bicycle code, and matches or beats Relay-BP at higher error rates while cutting multiplications by about 98% and dropping the need for per-node random-number generators (cai-dithered-belief-propagation-for-real-time-quantum-memory-decoding). Simulation-only with no FPGA or hardware result yet, unlike Relay-BP's reported 24 ns/iteration prototype, and not compared against any neural decoder directly. The classical-versus-neural contest stays contested, now with two credible non-learned contenders.
  • 2026-11-05 — a fifth neural decoder claim, same shape as the rest. Neural triage decoding beats BP-OSD and a Tiered BP-relay-MLE baseline on two ultra-high-rate qLDPC codes (LP20, Kasai) with 28x to 343x lower amortized GPU latency (hu-fast-and-accurate-neural-triage-decoding-of-ultra-high-rate-quantum-ldpc-codes), but it is simulation-only with phenomenological noise, no hardware loop, and no released supplementary information at ingestion — the accuracy-versus-latency contest stays contested.
  • 2026-11-04 — a stack-wide review lists decoding as an established AI use case but does not settle the speed question. The 28-author survey has a decoding section alongside code discovery (alexeev-artificial-intelligence-for-quantum-computing); as a survey of reported results it adds no new accuracy, latency or hardware evidence, so the status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-04 — the live disagreement is accuracy versus latency, not whether neural decoders can be accurate. Independent deployability work finds accuracy is driven by data scale, and that microsecond FPGA latency needs INT4 quantization with one architecture fitting at distance 9 (yan-rethink-the-role-of-neural-decoders-in-quantum-error-correction); Cascade's 40 microsecond GPU latency misses the superconducting budget (gu-cascade-scalable-neural-decoders-for-practical-fault-tolerant-quantum-computation). Cascade's disputed real-time claim carries a rebutted-by link to that study.
  • 2026-11-04 — classical push-back and the answer to it. IBM's Relay-BP, a non-learned decoder, reaches orders of magnitude lower logical error than BP+OSD on the Gross code and matches matching on surface codes in simulation, with an FPGA prototype at 24 ns per BP iteration (ibm-relay-bp-improved-belief-propagation-for-real-time-decoding-of-quantum-memory); Google's Tesseract is another classical baseline (google-tesseract-search-based-decoder-for-quantum-error-correction). Cascade reports about 17x lower logical error than Relay-BP in simulation, so Relay-BP's atom is rebutted-by Cascade's, but that rebuttal is simulation-only and unreproduced. This unresolved neural-versus-classical contest is why the theme opens at contested.
  • 2026-11-04 — hardware evidence is thin. The only neural decoder in a live hardware loop is a distance-3 FPGA demo, 550 ns total latency (yang-real-time-surface-code-qec-with-fpga-neural-network-decoder); AlphaQubit 2's sub-microsecond result is TPU timing on simulated data plus offline decoding of recorded Willow data, not a closed loop (google-alphaqubit-2-scalable-real-time-neural-decoder-for-topological-codes). NVIDIA's pre-decoder is a vendor benchmark with a 1.1x to 1.5x accuracy gain (nvidia-cuda-q-qec-0-6-real-time-qec-with-nvqlink-and-ising-pre-decoder).
  • 2026-11-04 — the anchor result. AlphaQubit beat a tensor-network decoder by roughly 4% to 6% on Sycamore data at distance 3 and 5, with a published Nature version; data needs grew about 100x from distance 3 to 11 and throughput was well short of real time (google-alphaqubit-learning-high-accuracy-error-decoding). A Mamba variant argues the transformer's O(d^4) cost is the latency obstacle (lee-mamba-scalable-neural-decoders-for-practical-real-time-qec).
  • 2026-11-04 — opened at contested. Opened from the AI-for-QEC baseline batch; no neural decoder had previously been ingested.

Open questions

  • A neural decoder running in a closed loop on hardware at distance 5 or more, at superconducting cycle times (about 1 microsecond); PRISM's distance-8 FPGA result is a latency measurement on erasure data, not a live loop on an existing photonic processor.
  • A like-for-like comparison of a neural decoder against Relay-BP, dithered BP, PriorTilt and Tesseract at equal latency and equal noise, run by someone other than the neural decoder's authors.
  • Whether PriorTilt's speed and accuracy advantage over Relay-BP survives an FPGA or hardware implementation; none is reported yet.
  • An FPGA or hardware demonstration of the dithered BP decoder, to see whether its arithmetic-cost advantage (fewer multiplications, no RNG) translates into real latency.
  • Whether the data requirement (about 100x from distance 3 to 11 for AlphaQubit) is tolerable at the distances useful algorithms need.
  • Whether Cascade-style gains survive realistic non-depolarizing noise and hardware drift.
