Quantum machine learning: advantage versus dequantization

Status: contested

Sub-threads: none — opened 2026-11-04 with 4 bullets; too early to split [reviewed 2026-11-27 @ 25 bullets]

Scope

Can quantum machine learning (QML) beat the best classical method, and where do classical simulation and dequantization results say it cannot? Covers trainability of variational models (barren plateaus) and their classical simulability, dequantization via random Fourier features, benchmarks of QML models against classical baselines, and the advantage claims that survive that push-back: a space advantage on streamed classical data and a learning advantage on quantum data. Optimisation advantage claims are in quantum-optimisation-claims-versus-classical-baselines; simulation-based advantage claims are in quantum-advantage-vs-classical-simulation.

Through-line

  • 2026-11-27 — a wall-clock-timing push-back on the "fewer parameters means efficient" framing, and a resource-theoretic bound on what quantum models can represent. Timing a hybrid quantum-classical physics-informed neural network (QCPINN) stage by stage on a 1D PDE benchmark, with the quantum circuit simulated on CPU, the authors find hybrids run 9-97x slower than a classical PINN at every stage despite having 38-44% fewer parameters — because the quantum circuit itself supplies at most 2.5% of each hybrid's parameters while dominating runtime, which grows roughly exponentially with qubit count (lyu-fewer-parameters-longer-runtimes-the-wall-clock-cost-of-hybrid-quantum-physics-informed-neural-networks). This is a wall-clock-timing critique distinct from wolf-a-realistic-end-to-end-runtime-crossover-estimation-for-quantum-enhanced-training-of-large-scale-machine-learning-models's asymptotic resource accounting already here; it is CPU-simulator evidence on one small benchmark and does not test whether the overhead persists on real quantum hardware. Separately, a purely theoretical resource-theory result shows that for a fixed encoding and readout, coherence, imaginarity and entanglement in a quantum model's trainable gates bound its representable functions' oscillation, odd part, and distance from product functions respectively, and that on domains bounded away from zero, Clifford trainable gates alone suffice for uniform function approximation at only a polynomial increase in encoding depth (shaya-quantum-resources-constrain-function-approximation) — giving this theme's trainability/simulability tension a resource-theoretic vocabulary, since non-Clifford structure (ordinarily what separates classically-simulable models from the rest) turns out replaceable by Clifford gates at modest cost for a broad function class. No numerical experiment or hardware demonstration; proved only for one- and two-qubit models. Both single-/two-author preprints; status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-26 — a resource-count study widens the fine print behind a specific asymptotic QML speedup claim, rather than challenging it on paper. For training a linearized neural network with a quantum linear solver — a use case with a suggested provably favorable asymptotic quantum scaling — a realistic end-to-end resource estimate, counting data access, computation and readout together rather than query complexity alone, finds that runtime crossover versus classical methods emerges only at very large problem sizes, long computation times, and highly restrictive output requirements (wolf-a-realistic-end-to-end-runtime-crossover-estimation-for-quantum-enhanced-training-of-large-scale-machine-learning-models). This is the same "advantage survives only in impractical regimes" pattern this theme keeps finding, now applied via engineering-cost accounting instead of a trainability or dequantization argument; it is a theoretical estimate, not a hardware run or a classical-simulability proof, and the abstract does not name the specific prior claim it stress-tests. Five-author preprint; status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-25 — a third push-back study names the mechanisms that manufacture a quantum-kernel advantage, on real breast-cancer data. Running three active benchmarks — all exact classical statevector simulations of 8-qubit fidelity kernels with no quantum component in the inference path — the authors show an apparent +0.1736 edge over a tuned RBF-SVM on M-SYNTH mammogram data falls to +0.0019 (statistically indistinguishable) against a random forest at the same input, the quantum kernel loses to the selected classical arm at every WDBC training-set size, and best-of-30-seed selection alone adds +0.0210 on BreastMNIST (saad-quantum-kernels-on-breast-data-concentration-surrogates-and-two-ways-an-advantage-gets-manufactured). This names comparator choice and selection-history inflation as the two concrete mechanisms behind the kind of single-number advantage claims the matched-tuning-budget benchmark and the Xanadu benchmark already here found fragile — but one of the paper's own matched dequantization surrogates failed to reproduce a tested kernel, an unresolved loose end. Single-group preprint; status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-23 — a comparative empirical study across three QML architectures sharpens this theme's trainability-versus-simulability tension with a concrete tunable knob. Studying exponential concentration (where observables become insensitive to input variation as system size grows) across fidelity quantum kernels, projected quantum kernels, and quantum extreme learning under a unified variance-based framework, the authors find global fidelity kernels concentrate most sharply (driven by Hilbert-space dimensionality), local-observable methods reduce but do not eliminate it, and scaling the data encoding's bandwidth mitigates concentration in moderate regimes — but that protection weakens in exactly the highly expressive settings where a quantum model might otherwise be worth claiming an edge in (mondragon-sampedro-controlling-exponential-concentration-in-quantum-machine-learning). This confirms the Nature Communications perspective already here (structure that avoids barren plateaus tends to confine models to a classically-tractable regime) with an architecture-spanning empirical result rather than only a theoretical argument. Two-author preprint, simulation-based, no new hardware run or explicit dequantization proof; status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-21 — a "quantum-inspired" structure turns out to be an efficient classical algorithm that already beats classical baselines on its own terms. A fine-tuning layer modelled on photonic beam-splitter meshes — structured orthogonal transforms with only O(n log n) trainable parameters, implemented entirely classically — roughly doubles certified-robust accuracy over conventional fine-tuning and LoRA on a real dermatology (alopecia) image classifier, with no quantum hardware used or needed (tsurkan-quantum-inspired-orthogonal-adapters-for-certifiably-robust-medical-image-classification). The paper gestures at a future quantum circuit implementation but never builds or tests one. Status stays contested: another data point for how often a "quantum circuit" motif resolves into an efficient classical algorithm once written down that way — the dequantization side of this theme's pattern, not an advantage claim.
  • 2026-11-19 — a real industrial dataset joins the comparable-not-better pile. Testing quantum support vector regression against classical SVR on real Panamax bulk-carrier sensor data for imputing missing fuel-consumption readings, a model using the ZZFeatureMap_1 feature map reaches the highest average R² (up to 0.991 at 90% missing data) and lowest aggregate error, but the authors describe the classical RBF-kernel baseline as "highly competitive" with it (maroto-sanchez-a-quantum-support-vector-regression-framework-for-missing-data-imputation-in-ship-performance-data). The abstract does not say whether the quantum models ran on hardware or a simulator, nor the dataset's record count — the same narrow-margin shape this theme keeps finding, now on a genuine industrial rather than synthetic or small academic dataset. Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-17 — a second quantum-recurrent capacity benchmark, same shape as the field's other comparisons. A task-driven information-processing-capacity benchmark finds variational quantum recurrent neural networks (QRNNs) comparable overall to classical recurrent baselines, with one QRNN variant showing higher average capacity on a task jointly probing memory and nonlinearity — but classical models scale better with memory size within the studied range, and the quantum models' edge appears only with a good parameter initialization, a search that gets harder as model size grows (viqueira-benchmarking-memory-and-nonlinear-processing-capacity-of-quantum-recurrent-neural-networks). Extends the theme's quantum-recurrent/reservoir sub-pattern alongside yesterday's data-re-uploading and the sleep-apnea QRNN results, all landing on the same "comparable, sometimes better small, worse at scale" shape. Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-16 — three more single-study entries: a theory advance on the trainability question, a reservoir-computing engineering knob, and a classical-optimizer-engineering contribution — none bearing directly on advantage. An analytical framework decomposes a variational circuit's compact-Lie-group representation to give an exact gradient-variance formula under 2-design conditions and a general sufficient condition for trainability, plus a sampling algorithm that decides trainability from circuit runs — polynomial in 1/tolerance — without building the representation-theoretic projectors (xie-structure-makes-quantum-circuits-trainable); this is a new analytical tool alongside cerezo-does-provable-absence-of-barren-plateaus-imply-classical-simulability and koutahi-barren-plateaus-in-quantum-linear-combinations-interference-and-lie-algebraic-control, deciding whether a circuit is trainable without resolving the separate question of whether a trainable circuit is also classically simulable. Separately, bringing data re-uploading (standard in circuit-based QML) into physical quantum reservoir computing significantly improves learnability of higher-order nonlinear polynomials and chaotic time-series prediction, with an information-processing-capacity trade-off between memory and nonlinearity — an internal ablation (re-uploading versus a dissipative protocol) with no classical reservoir or recurrent-network baseline reported (cindrak-data-re-uploading-in-quantum-reservoir-computing). And a Krotov-inspired optimizer adapted from quantum optimal control provably matches the exact empirical-loss gradient for fixed-generator gates and dominates tuned PennyLane first-order baselines on a 4-bit parity classifier, approaching L-BFGS-B's accuracy at first-order cost on a data-reuploading "crown" task — tested only on two small toy classifiers, with no hardware run and no advantage claim (frohlich-hybrid-krotov-gradient-descent-optimization-for-quantum-machine-learning). None of the three moves the theme off contested: the first sharpens a theory question, the other two are training/engineering contributions that make no quantum-advantage claim either way.
  • 2026-11-14 — two more single-study entries, one a real parameter-efficiency win that fades with scale, one a quantum model beating strong classical baselines on an expressivity task its own authors say isn't about quantum advantage. A hybrid quantum recurrent network (QRNN) with 94 parameters beats a 15,930-parameter classical RNN at sleep-apnea classification on the smallest, easiest dataset (83.0% vs. 69.0% accuracy), draws roughly even at medium size, and loses on the largest, most complex split (73.7% vs. 80.3%) — real numbers on the familiar "wins small, fades at scale" shape, run on a noiseless emulator with no hardware or shot noise, needing about 10x more training epochs than the classical model, and compared against a classical baseline deliberately left unregularized (mayorga-redondo-sleep-disordered-breathing-detection-with-quantum-recurrent-neural-networks). Separately, SQMM, a 642-to-666-parameter quantum spectral-memory model for implicit neural representations, beats not just a quantum baseline (QIREN, by 3x to 14x on MSE) but genuine classical ones too — FINER on audio, NeRF on full-size image reconstruction — while using comparable or fewer parameters; but every result is simulation-only, per-instance curve fitting rather than generalization, with the authors stating plainly that "quantum computational or hardware advantage is explicitly out of scope" and the paper's own measurement cost scaling exponentially (roughly 4^m) in the memory-qubit count, tested only at m=2 (moroz-broitman-spectral-quantum-memory-for-implicit-neural-representations). Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-13 — the largest-scale quantum-in-an-LLM test yet undercuts its own headline, a new uncertainty-quantification framework opens without an advantage claim, and a fully-quantum RL framework ships with no experiments at all. A trained quantum module replacing eight transformer blocks inside a real 30-billion-parameter model (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507) holds near-baseline perplexity while removing 16.33% of the model's parameters, and its quantum heads run on IBM's 156-qubit ibm_basquecountry Heron processor without retraining — but every control the authors run themselves (no entangler, a frozen circuit, a parameter-matched classical MLP) matches or beats the trained module, and the authors state outright that they "claim no quantum advantage" and that the hardware run "demonstrates executability, not advantage" (amoussou-multi-head-quantum-neural-networks-replacing-transformer-blocks-in-a-30b-llm). Separately, a Dunjko-group paper trains quantum circuits to output Dempster-Shafer mass functions — belief over label subsets rather than single labels — reaching 0.83 AUROC for out-of-distribution detection on small MNIST/CIFAR-10 subsets in simulation, with no classical Dempster-Shafer baseline run and the authors stating plainly that "the quantum fusion advantage is not provable, since no classical hardness result is known" (kempkes-uncertainty-aware-quantum-machine-learning-based-on-dempster-shafer-theory). A third entry is purely theoretical: a "fully quantum" reinforcement-learning framework with no mid-circuit measurement proves convergence of a new policy-gradient estimator to stationary points, accepted at NeurIPS 2026, but contains no simulation, benchmark, or hardware result of any kind (tavakoli-a-new-framework-for-quantum-reinforcement-learning). None of the three moves the theme off contested; the first adds pushback, the other two are capability/framework contributions that make no advantage claim either way.
  • 2026-11-12 — three more single-study entries, two pushing back and one extending an interpretability thread. A parameter-matched controlled study of an 8-qubit quantum channel-attention gate on DenseNet121 for ASL recognition finds no statistically significant accuracy difference from a matched classical MLP gate or from no attention at all, despite the quantum gate being far more compact — the authors' own conclusion is no evidence of quantum advantage (bhalgami-compact-quantum-channel-attention-for-static-asl-recognition). A 6-qubit quantum reservoir forecasting insurance claims is statistically indistinguishable from ridge regression in simulation for four of five tested designs, though a separate hardware-aware qubit-layout result in the same paper beats Qiskit's default optimizer on noise robustness in all 21 paired rounds on real IBM ibm_kingston hardware — a compilation-flavored result riding inside an application paper (rusnakova-hardware-aware-design-and-layout-selection-in-quantum-reservoir-computing-for-insurance-claims-forecasting). Separately, the "Reading Why" authors extend their exact Born-evidence-map decomposition from attention weights to amplitude-encoded image classifiers, validate it against occlusion and gradient-times-input attribution, and report the first hardware number in this pair's work: 72.3% simulated accuracy on a 10-class Caltech subset falls to 37.7% on ibm_kingston, with the headline numbers themselves flagged exploratory by the authors over a test-set-informed data trim (tongloy-seeing-why-exact-evidence-maps-for-quantum-and-optical-image-classifiers). Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-10 — two more single-study entries push back, one from finance and one from RL theory. A six-author, methodologically strong ablation (four walk-forward folds, five seeds, bootstrap intervals, a locked holdout) on a hybrid quantum temporal network ranking 2,000 Tokyo Stock Exchange equities finds the circuit statistically indistinguishable whether trained, frozen, or absent, with a best Deflated Sharpe Ratio of 0.17 across all six tested conditions, and reproduces the null result on a separate 2,000-US-equity set (chen-trained-frozen-or-absent-identifying-what-a-quantum-layer-contributes-to-cross-sectional-equity-ranking). Separately, a decoder-design analysis of IQP-circuit reinforcement-learning policies shows the same Fourier-Pauli aggregation that prevents exponential concentration (a barren-plateau-like training failure) also permits efficient classical estimation of action probabilities, and its best-performing 21-qubit policy is explicitly, self-disclosedly classically simulable — a trainability/simulability trade-off that mirrors the Cerezo et al. link already in this theme, now shown for RL rather than supervised QML (sequeira-trainability-simulability-tradeoff-in-parameterized-iqp-based-reinforcement-learning-policies). Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-09 — five more single-study entries, none landing a verified advantage. A quantum-Hamiltonian-embedding classifier trains one parameterized Hamiltonian whose ground state defines the decision rule and argues, under cryptographic hardness assumptions, for a hypothesis class beyond classical reach; its IBM Heron run shows inference feasibility at scale but no reported head-to-head win over a classical baseline (molteni-supervised-learning-with-quantum-hamiltonian-embeddings). A theory paper shows single-pass linear-combination-of-unitaries circuits have a gradient-variance floor immune to interference-based cancellation, while the repeated, fully ancilla-controlled version can still concentrate exponentially under joint Haar-random sampling — a standard barren plateau — sharpening rather than resolving the trainability question already active via cerezo-does-provable-absence-of-barren-plateaus-imply-classical-simulability (koutahi-barren-plateaus-in-quantum-linear-combinations-interference-and-lie-algebraic-control). A different-direction paper uses quantum measurement's own sampling noise to replace part of a diffusion sampler's injected randomness, cutting shots per sample about 5,300x on an 8-dimensional benchmark, with hardware use unstated (guo-leveraging-quantum-measurement-randomness-for-shot-efficient-diffusion-models). A training-paradigm paper runs optimization entirely on a quantum computer via Quantum Hamiltonian Descent, giving a grid-resolution-independent error bound and a higher global-minimum-finding rate at equal cost, in simulation only — filling out the spectrum between classically-trainable (ballo-gimbernat-classical-training-of-conditional-iqp-born-machines) and noise-assisted (ngo-noise-regularized-training-of-quantum-circuit-born-machines-under-a-finite-shot-budget) training already in this theme (burato-banchi-fully-quantum-training-of-parametric-circuits-with-quantum-hamiltonian-descent). And a simulated 4- to 8-qubit layer inserted into a vision-language model reached 80.7% accuracy, 2.28 points above classical fine-tuning, on one dataset — narrow and placement-dependent, with the paper's own title security claim untested despite running six attack settings (syfullah-khalil-can-quantum-layers-offer-performance-and-security-advantages-in-vision-language-models). Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-08 — two independent structural results say popular QML model classes are weaker than they look, from different angles. Amplitude encoding is proved to be invariant to nonzero real rescaling of the input, an architecture-independent restriction on the hypothesis class that adding nonlinear gates alone does not fix; a parameter-free nonlinear feature map that removes the blind spot improves accuracy 21 to 44 percent over conventional amplitude and angle encoding on four small benchmarks (gurgul-overcoming-the-representational-limitations-of-amplitude-encoding-for-quantum-neural-networks). Separately, two checkable circuit-symmetry rules (time-reversal, reflection) predict which Fourier frequencies a Pauli-encoded circuit cannot reach before training — reducing to a GF(2) linear system, holding in 17.6% of 6,120 surveyed templates — and the predicted rule was confirmed to hold and break on cue on a 65-qubit IBM chain as relaxation noise was switched on and off (sulimov-hidden-selection-rules-and-amplitude-ceilings-in-quantum-fourier-models). Neither paper's proofs were checked here, and neither bears on classical simulability directly — they are expressivity limits internal to the quantum model class, not dequantization results. Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-08 — two further single-study contributions to trainability, neither moving the advantage question. Residual hardware noise is shown (in simulation) to regularize quantum circuit Born machine training, with an optimal intermediate noise strength; a proposed method (GENIE) that selects noise level during training within a fixed shot budget stays close to the best of four fixed strategies while each fixed strategy performs poorly in some settings (ngo-noise-regularized-training-of-quantum-circuit-born-machines-under-a-finite-shot-budget). A Born-measured-attention transformer reaches about 77% accuracy on e-SNLI, matching a same-size softmax transformer, and the same trained weights run on IBM's ibm_kingston and on optical hardware, demonstrating portability and exact per-word evidence rather than any accuracy or efficiency edge (tongloy-reading-why-born-measured-attention-with-exact-word-evidence-on-light-and-qubits). Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-07 — classically trainable is not the same claim as classically simulable, and this source is careful about the difference. Conditional IQP Born machines with input-state conditioning (rather than diagonal-layer conditioning) can be trained entirely classically, via classical estimates of a conditional MMD objective and its gradients, even though IQP sampling itself stays only conjectured hard; on a 16-qubit Curie-Weiss benchmark the design cut mean total-variation distance about 6x versus diagonal conditioning, and classical training scaled to 2048 qubits for one observable away from the critical crossover (ballo-gimbernat-classical-training-of-conditional-iqp-born-machines). The authors make no advantage claim and do not test the hard, near-crossover regime at scale. This extends the theme's roster of classically-trainable quantum-inspired models without adding a new dequantization result. Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-06 — a second independent benchmark erases the quantum-kernel edge under fair tuning. A 324-cell factorial benchmark that matches the classical RBF kernel's tuning budget to the quantum feature map's finds tuned quantum kernels statistically indistinguishable from a tuned RBF on all four real datasets tested; any edge survives only on synthetic data generated by the feature map itself and shrinks with dimensionality, while a fidelity kernel needs about 5×10⁷ circuit evaluations at 10,000 samples (bhalgami-empirical-evaluation-of-quantum-kernel-methods-for-classification). This is single-author and not yet reproduced, but it reaches the same conclusion as Bowles et al. through a kernel-specific, matched-budget protocol, adding a second independent line to the push-back side. Stays contested.
  • 2026-11-04 — the surviving advantage claims are theorems and simulations, not hardware. Google Quantum AI and Caltech prove an exponential machine-size advantage for streamed classical data (4 to 6 orders of magnitude on IMDb and single-cell RNA data with fewer than 60 logical qubits), but the real-data numbers are classical predictions, the advantage is in space not runtime, and a non-peer-reviewed critique of the resource accounting exists only as an unverified lead (zhao-exponential-quantum-advantage-in-processing-massive-classical-data). A separate simulation study forecasts a learning advantage on quantum data at 30 to 40 noisy qubits, extrapolated from simulations of at most 22 qubits (danaci-evidence-of-quantum-machine-learning-advantage-with-tens-of-noisy-qubits). Neither is aimed by the variational push-back, and neither has a hardware run.
  • 2026-11-04 — the push-back targets trainable variational models and comes mostly from one network. A Nature Communications perspective argues that barren-plateau-free structure usually implies classical simulability (cerezo-does-provable-absence-of-barren-plateaus-imply-classical-simulability); a PRX Quantum paper reproduces QCNN classification classically on up to 1,024 qubits, so the popular QCNN premise carries a rebutted-by link (bermejo-quantum-convolutional-neural-networks-are-effectively-classically-simulable); random-Fourier-feature dequantization covers quantum neural networks and kernels under stated conditions (sahebi-dequantization-of-supervised-quantum-machine-learning-via-random-fourier-features). Xanadu's independent benchmark found classical models ahead of 12 popular QML models on small simulated tasks, against a literature in which about 40% of sampled papers claim quantum wins (bowles-better-than-classical-the-subtle-art-of-benchmarking-quantum-machine-learning-models). The Cerezo, Holmes and Cincio group is behind three of these four, and every result is conditional (random initialisation, sufficient conditions, small noise-free tasks).
  • 2026-11-04 — opened at contested. Credible pushback on the old hope (trainable variational QML) sits beside new advantage claims on different ground (classical-data space, quantum-data learning); the dispute is which regime, if any, has an advantage that survives dequantization. No hardware demonstration of a QML advantage appeared in this search.

Open questions

  • Whether the breast-cancer kernel study's own matched dequantization surrogate failure (at bandwidth 2π on M-SYNTH raw) is a one-off or signals a broader gap in the surrogate-replaces-quantum-kernel argument.
  • Whether the exponential-concentration/bandwidth-scaling trade-off has been tested on real hardware, and whether any regime found trainable by bandwidth-restricted encoding retains a task where a classical model cannot match it.
  • Whether a quantum circuit implementation of the butterfly fine-tuning layers is ever built and compared head-to-head against the classical version's certified-robustness numbers.
  • Whether the quantum ship-performance SVR result was run on real hardware or a simulator, the underlying dataset's record count, and whether the margin over the RBF baseline survives a matched-tuning-budget comparison like the kernel benchmark already in this theme.
  • Whether the Lie-group trainability framework's sampling algorithm has been demonstrated on any numeric example, and whether it changes how barren plateaus are diagnosed in practice versus existing empirical checks.
  • Whether the data re-uploading QRC gain holds against a classical reservoir computer or recurrent network on the same chaotic time-series task, and whether "physical" QRC here means fabricated hardware or a physical-model simulation.
  • Whether hybrid Krotov's convergence and accuracy gains hold on larger variational circuits, circuits that exhibit barren plateaus, or real hardware with shot noise.
  • Whether the QRNN sleep-apnea parameter-efficiency edge survives on real hardware with shot noise, with a regularized classical baseline, and on datasets larger than 1,200 instances.
  • Whether SQMM's win over classical INR baselines (NeRF, FINER) extends to generalization (unseen inputs) rather than per-instance fitting, and whether its measurement cost stays practical past a 2-qubit memory register.
  • Whether a quantum layer in any financial-ML pipeline has shown a distinguishable signal under a matched-budget ablation; the one rigorous test so far (equity ranking) is a clean null.
  • Whether the IQP-RL trainability/simulability trade-off (sparse aggregation preventing concentration but permitting classical estimation) holds for policy structures denser than the tested block-chain design, and whether any IQP-RL policy keeps a performance edge once simulability is ruled out.
  • Whether the quantum-Hamiltonian-embedding classifier's IBM Heron run ever beats a classical baseline empirically, separate from its cryptographic-hardness theorem, and whether an independent group checks the assumptions behind that theorem.
  • Whether the single-pass LCU variance-floor result and the repeated-interleaving concentration result hold up to independent scrutiny beyond the four-qubit matchgate example.
  • Whether quantum-measurement-noise-assisted diffusion sampling ran on real hardware, and whether the roughly 5,300x shot reduction holds beyond one small Gaussian-mixture benchmark.
  • Whether fully-quantum (Quantum Hamiltonian Descent) training ever outperforms a classical optimizer's success rate head-to-head, rather than only its own schedule-design baseline, and whether it has been tried on real hardware.
  • Whether the vision-language-model quantum-layer accuracy gain generalizes beyond Visual Spatial Reasoning and LLaVA-OneVision, and whether the paper's own security-advantage claim is ever actually tested with reported robustness numbers.
  • Whether the amplitude-encoding rescaling limitation and the Fourier selection-rule proofs hold up to independent scrutiny, and whether either constraint is specific to small benchmark regimes or persists at the scales where a quantum model might otherwise claim an edge.
  • Whether the conditional-IQP classical-training result holds in the near-crossover regime at scale, and whether classical trainability is ever shown to undercut IQP's conjectured sampling hardness directly.
  • A hardware or end-to-end circuit-level test of quantum oracle sketching, and an independent check of its resource accounting (the Zenodo critique, May 2026, is an unverified lead).
  • Whether the quantum-data advantage holds on a device at 30 to 40 qubits, where the forecast is extrapolated from 22-qubit simulations.
  • Whether the simulability and dequantization results transfer to the fault-tolerant-era constructions, and whether a group outside the Cerezo, Holmes and Cincio network reaches the same conclusions.
  • Whether the space advantage extends to problems earlier thought dequantized, as the Zhao authors suggest.
  • An independent reproduction of the matched-tuning-budget kernel benchmark, and whether its conclusion holds on larger or higher-dimensional real datasets.
