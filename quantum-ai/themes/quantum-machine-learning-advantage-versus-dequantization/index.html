Quantum machine learning: advantage versus dequantization

Status: contested

Sub-threads: none — opened 2026-11-04 with 4 bullets; too early to split [reviewed 2026-11-07 @ 4 bullets]

Scope

Can quantum machine learning (QML) beat the best classical method, and where do classical simulation and dequantization results say it cannot? Covers trainability of variational models (barren plateaus) and their classical simulability, dequantization via random Fourier features, benchmarks of QML models against classical baselines, and the advantage claims that survive that push-back: a space advantage on streamed classical data and a learning advantage on quantum data. Optimisation advantage claims are in quantum-optimisation-claims-versus-classical-baselines; simulation-based advantage claims are in quantum-advantage-vs-classical-simulation.

Through-line

  • 2026-11-07 — classically trainable is not the same claim as classically simulable, and this source is careful about the difference. Conditional IQP Born machines with input-state conditioning (rather than diagonal-layer conditioning) can be trained entirely classically, via classical estimates of a conditional MMD objective and its gradients, even though IQP sampling itself stays only conjectured hard; on a 16-qubit Curie-Weiss benchmark the design cut mean total-variation distance about 6x versus diagonal conditioning, and classical training scaled to 2048 qubits for one observable away from the critical crossover (ballo-gimbernat-classical-training-of-conditional-iqp-born-machines). The authors make no advantage claim and do not test the hard, near-crossover regime at scale. This extends the theme's roster of classically-trainable quantum-inspired models without adding a new dequantization result. Status stays contested.
  • 2026-11-06 — a second independent benchmark erases the quantum-kernel edge under fair tuning. A 324-cell factorial benchmark that matches the classical RBF kernel's tuning budget to the quantum feature map's finds tuned quantum kernels statistically indistinguishable from a tuned RBF on all four real datasets tested; any edge survives only on synthetic data generated by the feature map itself and shrinks with dimensionality, while a fidelity kernel needs about 5×10⁷ circuit evaluations at 10,000 samples (bhalgami-empirical-evaluation-of-quantum-kernel-methods-for-classification). This is single-author and not yet reproduced, but it reaches the same conclusion as Bowles et al. through a kernel-specific, matched-budget protocol, adding a second independent line to the push-back side. Stays contested.
  • 2026-11-04 — the surviving advantage claims are theorems and simulations, not hardware. Google Quantum AI and Caltech prove an exponential machine-size advantage for streamed classical data (4 to 6 orders of magnitude on IMDb and single-cell RNA data with fewer than 60 logical qubits), but the real-data numbers are classical predictions, the advantage is in space not runtime, and a non-peer-reviewed critique of the resource accounting exists only as an unverified lead (zhao-exponential-quantum-advantage-in-processing-massive-classical-data). A separate simulation study forecasts a learning advantage on quantum data at 30 to 40 noisy qubits, extrapolated from simulations of at most 22 qubits (danaci-evidence-of-quantum-machine-learning-advantage-with-tens-of-noisy-qubits). Neither is aimed by the variational push-back, and neither has a hardware run.
  • 2026-11-04 — the push-back targets trainable variational models and comes mostly from one network. A Nature Communications perspective argues that barren-plateau-free structure usually implies classical simulability (cerezo-does-provable-absence-of-barren-plateaus-imply-classical-simulability); a PRX Quantum paper reproduces QCNN classification classically on up to 1,024 qubits, so the popular QCNN premise carries a rebutted-by link (bermejo-quantum-convolutional-neural-networks-are-effectively-classically-simulable); random-Fourier-feature dequantization covers quantum neural networks and kernels under stated conditions (sahebi-dequantization-of-supervised-quantum-machine-learning-via-random-fourier-features). Xanadu's independent benchmark found classical models ahead of 12 popular QML models on small simulated tasks, against a literature in which about 40% of sampled papers claim quantum wins (bowles-better-than-classical-the-subtle-art-of-benchmarking-quantum-machine-learning-models). The Cerezo, Holmes and Cincio group is behind three of these four, and every result is conditional (random initialisation, sufficient conditions, small noise-free tasks).
  • 2026-11-04 — opened at contested. Credible pushback on the old hope (trainable variational QML) sits beside new advantage claims on different ground (classical-data space, quantum-data learning); the dispute is which regime, if any, has an advantage that survives dequantization. No hardware demonstration of a QML advantage appeared in this search.

Open questions

  • Whether the conditional-IQP classical-training result holds in the near-crossover regime at scale, and whether classical trainability is ever shown to undercut IQP's conjectured sampling hardness directly.
  • A hardware or end-to-end circuit-level test of quantum oracle sketching, and an independent check of its resource accounting (the Zenodo critique, May 2026, is an unverified lead).
  • Whether the quantum-data advantage holds on a device at 30 to 40 qubits, where the forecast is extrapolated from 22-qubit simulations.
  • Whether the simulability and dequantization results transfer to the fault-tolerant-era constructions, and whether a group outside the Cerezo, Holmes and Cincio network reaches the same conclusions.
  • Whether the space advantage extends to problems earlier thought dequantized, as the Zhao authors suggest.
  • An independent reproduction of the matched-tuning-budget kernel benchmark, and whether its conclusion holds on larger or higher-dimensional real datasets.
