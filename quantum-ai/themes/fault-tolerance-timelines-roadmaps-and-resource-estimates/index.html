Fault-tolerance timelines: roadmaps and resource estimates

Status: emerging

Sub-threads: none — 4 bullets; too early to split [reviewed 2026-11-04 @ 4 bullets]

Scope

When could a fault-tolerant machine with enough logical qubits to matter exist? Vendor roadmaps (dated targets) and published resource estimates (how many qubits an algorithm needs) are the two instruments, and neither is an experiment. Hardware evidence that would make the targets credible lives in below-threshold-error-correction and logical-qubits-beyond-break-even.

Through-line

  • 2026-11-04 — AI-found circuit and compilation gains point the same way as the algorithmic cost reductions, but they are estimates on the authors' own metrics; status stays emerging. An LLM evolutionary search cut Toffoli and qubit counts in seven of eight tasks by 4.1x to 39x on the Toffoli-qubit product (gu-discovering-state-of-the-art-quantum-circuits-with-large-language-models), and AlphaTensor-Quantum matched, without beating, the best human T-count for a Shor adder and a chemistry operator (ruiz-quantum-circuit-optimization-with-alphatensor). Both extend the RSA-2048 pattern that algorithms and compilation, not hardware, supply most of the reduction, and neither moves a vendor date. A Google-affiliated theorem also puts a machine-size advantage for streamed classical data at fewer than 60 logical qubits, a resource estimate rather than a run (zhao-exponential-quantum-advantage-in-processing-massive-classical-data). See ai-for-quantum-circuit-compilation-and-algorithm-discovery and quantum-machine-learning-advantage-versus-dequantization.
  • 2026-11-04 — chemistry resource estimates keep falling, from algorithms, and the classical target is moving too; status stays emerging. Google's spectrum amplification cuts fault-tolerant ground-state energy estimation cost by 4x to 195x on iron-sulfur and CO2-fixation catalyst models in a Physical Review X paper, with no logical-qubit or Toffoli counts in the abstract (low-fast-quantum-simulation-of-electronic-structure-by-spectrum-amplification). That matches the pattern in the RSA-2048 estimate: algorithms, not hardware, supply most of the reduction. A modelling paper expects quantum phase estimation to pass full CI for tens to hundreds of atoms within a decade but classical methods to stay ahead in many cases for decades (gundlach-quantum-advantage-in-computational-chemistry), and a classical FeMoco calculation to chemical accuracy undermines the flagship target (zhai-classical-simulation-of-the-femoco-model-to-chemical-accuracy). All of this is estimates and projections, no experiment, so it stays emerging. See classical-and-ai-methods-as-the-rival-to-quantum-for-chemistry.
  • 2026-11-04 — decoder evidence bears on the dates but does not move them. Whether qLDPC codes deliver their promised savings depends on the decoder as much as the hardware: with Tesseract's most-likely-error decoding the Gross code is 14x to 19x more efficient than surface codes, against about 10x with correlated matching or BP+OSD, and about 4x if long-range couplers are 10x noisier (google-tesseract-search-based-decoder-for-quantum-error-correction). Relay-BP, an IBM classical decoder, has an FPGA path at 24 ns per BP iteration in a follow-up prototype (ibm-relay-bp-improved-belief-propagation-for-real-time-decoding-of-quantum-memory). A neural decoder, Cascade, claims much lower logical error on the Gross code and suggests costs 'significantly lower than anticipated' (about 40% fewer qubits, a projection), but in simulation only and with GPU latency that misses the superconducting budget (gu-cascade-scalable-neural-decoders-for-practical-fault-tolerant-quantum-computation). The open question about the AI-for-quantum angle now has a partial answer: decoders are a gating item, and no source moves a vendor date. Status stays emerging; see neural-decoders-for-quantum-error-correction.
  • 2026-11-04 — opened at emerging. IBM's bicycle-architecture paper and roadmap target 200 logical qubits and 100 million gates in 2029 (ibm-bicycle-architecture-tour-de-gross-starling-roadmap); Microsoft's Majorana 2 blog also claims 2029, resting on the disputed topological route (microsoft-majorana-2-announcement-blog); Gidney's RSA-2048 estimate dropped about 20x in qubits from algorithmic changes alone (gidney-rsa-2048-under-a-million-noisy-qubits). Vendor dates converge on 2028 to 2029 for a first 100-to-200-logical-qubit machine. All suggestive: roadmaps are intent, estimates are model-dependent, and the IBM Kookaburra 2026 milestone could not be confirmed.

Open questions

  • Did IBM hit Kookaburra in 2026?
  • Roadmaps from Google, Quantinuum and QuEra, and resource estimates for chemistry and materials workloads (not only cryptanalysis).
  • Whether AI-assisted design (Microsoft's claim) or calibration changes any of these dates; decoders are now covered but move none of them yet.
  • Decoding latency at the Gross-code scale on real hardware, which gates the qLDPC timelines.
  • Logical-qubit and Toffoli counts for the FeMoco 76-orbital active space from the original papers; secondary figures (about 1,459 logical qubits, roughly 1.0e9 Toffolis) are unverified.
  • Further chemistry resource estimates (arXiv 2511.09124, 2605.30455) were seen in search snippets only and not read.
