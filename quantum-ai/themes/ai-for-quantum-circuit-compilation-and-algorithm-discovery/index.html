AI for quantum circuit compilation and algorithm discovery

Status: emerging

Sub-threads: (1) reinforcement-learning circuit synthesis and compilation (AlphaTensor-Quantum, IBM's transpiler passes); (2) LLM-driven circuit and algorithm discovery; (3) LLM coding agents and benchmarks for quantum code; (4) analytical, non-learned alternatives to search-based ansatz design; (5) AI-assisted theorem proving in quantum complexity theory [reviewed 2026-11-07 @ 5 bullets]

Scope

Does AI (reinforcement learning, evolutionary LLM search, LLM coding agents, AI-assisted theorem proving) produce quantum circuits, compilations, algorithms, and proofs that beat the best known human or heuristic ones, and are the gains independent of the group reporting them? Covers T-count and Toffoli-count optimisation, circuit synthesis and routing, LLM-found ansatzes and reversible circuits, benchmarks for LLM-written quantum code, and AI-generated proofs bearing on quantum complexity theory. Calibration and control are in ml-for-qubit-calibration-control-and-code-design and decoding is in neural-decoders-for-quantum-error-correction. Most gains here are fault-tolerant resource counts, which only pay off on hardware that does not yet exist; the bar is independent reproduction and hardware runs, not benchmark tables.

Through-line

  • 2026-11-07 — AI's role here now includes proving theorems, not just finding circuits. An AI research system built by Physical Superintelligence, running instances of an unreleased Anthropic model, produced a complete proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis (2014) polynomial-influence conjecture, formalized in Lean 4 with no sorry or added axiom in its four core theorems and replayed by two independent Lean kernels; human involvement was limited to problem selection and editing the draft (turturean-ai-assisted-proof-of-the-aaronson-ambainis-conjecture). The theorem itself bounds how much quantum query algorithms can outpace classical ones on problems without special structure (classical approximation to within (ε/d)^7, on most random inputs) — a limit on query reads, not total time, so it does not touch structured quantum advantages. One system, one paper, read-through but not independent re-formalization from the Stanford group that proved a narrower version in August 2026. Opens a fifth sub-thread; status stays emerging.
  • 2026-11-06 — a non-learned challenger to search-based ansatz design. Three data-informed rules (a Fourier-coefficient SVD, quantum-Fisher-information rank growth, and Pauli-readout coverage) set a variational circuit's entanglement structure and depth directly from the dataset and gate set, without training or a grid search; on the Madelon dataset the prescribed 239-parameter circuit reached 78.5% test accuracy and ablations matched all three predicted effects (seksaria-data-informed-ansatz-selection-for-variational-quantum-circuits). This is single-dataset, single-group, and not compared head-to-head against a trained or searched ansatz on the same task, but it opens a fourth sub-thread: whether analytical rules can replace the learned search this theme otherwise tracks. Held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-05 — the first agent-level evidence on quantum research is a reliability warning, not an accelerant claim. OmniQ turns 59 published computational-quantum papers into reproduction tasks; across five LLM agent systems and six harnesses, success ranged from about 19% to 93% depending on the system, and a self-improving "Residual Agents" variant lifted a cheap model from 68% to 85% by reusing expert-verified repairs, without reaching the strongest system's 93% (du-reimagining-computational-quantum-research-with-ai-scientists). This is not a circuit or compilation result, but it directly answers this theme's own open question about agent-level evidence on quantum code: even frontier agents fail to reproduce a meaningful share of published results, which argues for treating every AI-discovered circuit above as unverified until an independent run confirms it. Held at emerging; this adds caution rather than evidence of capability.
  • 2026-11-04 — LLM coding agents have one weak data point. Qiskit HumanEval, IBM's 101-task benchmark, gave a best pass@1 of 46.53% for IBM's own Qiskit-tuned 8B model and 0 of 2 difficult tasks passed by any model (vishwakarma-qiskit-humaneval-an-evaluation-benchmark-for-quantum-code-generative-models). It is a 2024 benchmark of open models, so it says nothing about current frontier models or agents; no agent-level evidence on quantum code was found in this pass.
  • 2026-11-04 — LLM-driven discovery is the newest and thinnest strand. A Harvard evolutionary LLM search cut Toffoli and qubit counts versus the cited state of the art in seven of eight tasks, from 4.1x to 39x on the Toffoli-qubit product, and a 12x gain on the decoded-quantum-interferometry (DQI) Reed-Solomon decoder, but these are resource counts verified by the authors' own sampled evaluator, in a preprint with two Anthropic-affiliated authors using an Anthropic model (gu-discovering-state-of-the-art-quantum-circuits-with-large-language-models). The only hardware run of an AI-discovered algorithm, one 12-qubit LiH circuit on Quantinuum H2-1, deviated by 3.1 mHa with a 3.4 mHa uncertainty, consistent with chemical precision only within the error bar (finger-automated-near-term-quantum-algorithm-discovery-for-molecular-ground-states). A mathematics case study keeps the decisive judgments human (wang-human-ai-co-discovery-of-sign-embedding-quantum-algorithms). All single-study, so the strand stays emerging.
  • 2026-11-04 — opened at emerging; RL compilation is the best-supported strand, because it has a second group. AlphaTensor-Quantum matches or beats published T-count optimisers on a standard benchmark but only matches the best human constructions for Shor adders and a chemistry operator (ruiz-quantum-circuit-optimization-with-alphatensor), and an independent reusability report reproduces some key results and generalises to random 5- to 8-qubit circuits (zen-reusability-report-optimizing-t-count-in-general-quantum-circuits-with-alphatensor-quantum). IBM's RL transpiler passes cut depth about 40% on 133-qubit routing against its own default heuristic and are near-optimal only on small synthesis problems (kremer-practical-and-efficient-quantum-circuit-synthesis-and-transpiling-with-reinforcement-learning). The 28-author review frames all of this as AI already advancing the stack but does not adjudicate reproduction (alexeev-artificial-intelligence-for-quantum-computing). Held at emerging rather than consolidating: the RL gains are against heuristics, the independent check is a short abstract-level preprint, and no compile gain has been shown to change a hardware result.

Open questions

  • Whether the PSI/Turturean Aaronson-Ambainis proof gets an independent, non-PSI formal re-verification, and whether the AI-theorem-proving pattern recurs on other quantum-complexity open problems.
  • Whether any AI-found circuit or compilation has been re-verified or re-run by a group unconnected to its authors, beyond the single AlphaTensor-Quantum reusability report.
  • Whether the Toffoli and T-count reductions survive a hardware-appropriate objective (depth, routing, magic-state throughput, decoder load) instead of the authors' chosen metric.
  • Agent-level evidence on quantum code: Qiskit HumanEval is a 2024 benchmark of open models. OmniQ gives frontier-agent reproduction rates (19% to 93%) but for reproducing published results, not for discovering new circuits; whether agent-discovery success tracks agent-reproduction success is untested.
  • Hardware evidence that an AI-discovered algorithm helps: one 12-qubit LiH point with an error bar as large as the error.
  • Whether the data-informed (non-learned) ansatz-selection rules match or beat a trained/searched ansatz on the same task and compute budget, and whether they generalize past one dataset.
