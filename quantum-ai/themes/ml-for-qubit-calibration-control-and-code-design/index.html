Machine learning for qubit calibration, control and code design

Status: emerging

Sub-threads: none — opened 2026-11-04 with 4 bullets; too early to split [reviewed 2026-11-28 @ 18 bullets]

Scope

Does machine learning (reinforcement learning, language-model agents, vision-language models, bandit controllers) measurably improve how a quantum processor is tuned, controlled while running, or designed? Covers calibration and bring-up, control-parameter drift tracking during error correction, online error-mitigation selection during training, and automated discovery of error-correcting codes. Decoding has its own theme, neural-decoders-for-quantum-error-correction. The standard here is hardware data and independent baselines, not benchmark scores.

Through-line

  • 2026-11-28 — a new facet opens: a neural surrogate for a system's forward dynamics, not just a static design property or its underlying parameters. A physics-informed regional attention transformer, with translational invariance of the governing physics baked in as an inductive bias and conditioning on time-dependent global control parameters, learns to predict the spatiotemporal evolution of structured open quantum systems in place of integrating coupled master and field equations, reaching high predictive fidelity in- and out-of-distribution on a driven-dissipative qubit and an EIT quantum memory, with substantial wall-clock acceleration over repeated numerical simulation (du-regional-attention-transformers-for-spatially-structured-open-quantum-dynamics). Unlike QCap and the FNO quantum-dot model, which predict a static property (capacitance, charge density) from a design, and unlike the JEPA world model and GRU-QRL, which infer a system's fixed parameters, this predicts a full time-evolving trajectory under control — but on only two small simulated systems, with no quantitative fidelity/speedup figures given, no fabricated-device test, and no comparison to any of those methods. Two-author, peer-reviewed (npj Quantum Information) paper; held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-27 — a new facet opens: reusing one trained model's parameters across multiple fabricated chips of the same design, the photonic analogue of this theme's chip-by-chip tuning problem. A local analysis for a quantum photonic neural network shows that compensating for one chip's fabrication error provably raises expected loss on independent chips; Monte Carlo ensemble training (MC-ENS), which optimizes one shared parameter set by averaging simulated losses over a statistical fabrication-error model, then beats single-chip, sharpness-aware, and covariance-informed training on cross-chip generalization for CNOT synthesis, Ising dynamics, and classification, with the trained parameters reused on unseen chips with no device-specific retraining (bashir-cross-chip-transfer-in-quantum-photonic-neural-networks). Unlike this theme's superconducting and spin-qubit tuning results, which calibrate one physical device, this targets variability across instances of a design — but entirely against a statistical fabrication-error model in simulation, not measurements from physically fabricated chips, and not compared against this theme's other tuning results. Three-author preprint; held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-25 — the error-mitigation facet gets its first hardware-validated learned method, and the agentic-AI sub-pattern extends to a third experimental domain: quantum optics. A Fourier neural operator trained on theory-informed noise models for Shor order finding raises exact order recovery from 19% to 34% in simulation (median Jensen-Shannon divergence halved), and a version trained and calibrated only in simulation raises correct order estimates from 35 to 53 out of 101 across five real IBM quantum processors with no hardware fine-tuning (markidis-theory-informed-quantum-error-mitigation-for-shor-order-finding-with-fourier-neural-operators) — unlike APGEM, the boosting result, and the small-circuit mitigator already in this facet, this one is validated on real multi-device hardware, though it is built specifically for Shor's output-distribution structure and not compared against any of them. Separately, QuOptLab integrates an AI agent with a simulated quantum optics laboratory (optical instruments, robotic arm, cameras); the agent cuts thin-film thickness-estimation error from 7.13 to 0.066 nm² over six self-directed epochs and raises simulated edge-coupler coupling efficiency from 85.81% to 91.98% through iterative, feedback-guided redesign (li-quoptlab-advancing-quantum-optics-experiments-with-an-ai-agent) — a third domain for this theme's agentic-AI sub-pattern alongside qlaybot's device fabrication layout and QORA's NV-center pulse discovery, but entirely inside a simulated lab rather than on a fabricated optical bench or a real robotic arm. Both single-group preprints; held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-24 — a third device-design facet opens (fabrication layout, not property-prediction or charge-density surrogates), and a third mechanism joins the error-mitigation facet. qlaybot, a multimodal LLM-agent harness, connects microscope-image interpretation of exfoliated 2D-material flakes to the tools that draw and inspect a device's lithographic layout; an agent using qlaybot passes 81.5% of a new benchmark's layout-design tasks, against 24.1% for the same agent with only conventional tools, with the gap tracing to image interpretation specifically (miao-domain-advantage-in-two-dimensional-quantum-device-design-with-multi-modal-agentic-harness) — unlike QCap (capacitance prediction) and the FNO surrogate (charge-density simulation), this targets the fabrication-layout step itself, scored by automated layout-fit checks rather than a fabricated chip. Separately, circuit cutting lets a learned small-circuit error mitigator, trained once, be reused unmodified on circuits several times wider and deeper, recovering close to 90% or more of the noise-induced energy error on a holographic Ising-chain eigensolver and beating Clifford data regression and probabilistic error cancellation (kyaw-mitigating-large-circuits-with-a-learned-small-circuit-mitigator) — a third distinct mechanism in the error-mitigation facet alongside APGEM and the boosting result, not yet compared against either. Both single-group, simulation/benchmark-only preprints; held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-23 — the device-characterization facet gets a second Hamiltonian-identification method, and tuning hardware itself gets a new facet. A self-supervised "world model" (a joint-embedding predictive architecture) infers a device Hamiltonian's field and coupling maps from noisy measured dynamics by predicting future measurement representations in latent space, recovering a 100-site Heisenberg-chain disorder map and beating a masked-autoencoder baseline and direct time-averaged-observable regression by 4.4x to 30x (wang-kyriienko-world-models-for-learning-quantum-many-body-dynamics) — a second, architecturally distinct approach to the same Hamiltonian-identification task the GRU-QRL result opened 2026-11-20, with no comparison between the two methods. Separately, an FPGA-in-the-loop multiplexed-gradient-descent controller autonomously tunes an emulated electrostatically-defined quantum dot — tracking drift, recovering from charge jumps, stabilizing in low-SNR regimes — without needing the device's cross-capacitance matrix in advance, and runs the learning loop itself on cryo-compatible FPGA logic rather than an external classical control stack ("compute-in-place") (gaytan-fpga-in-the-loop-physical-learning-for-autonomous-spin-qubit-tuning). This opens a new facet: where this theme's existing tuning results compare algorithms, this is the first to target the physical location of the controller itself. Both single-group preprints, both simulation/emulation-only (no fabricated chip, no real measured dynamics); held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-20 — a second entry lands in the error-mitigation facet, and a drift-tracking variant extends the characterization facet. Boosting noisy parameterized quantum circuits (AdaBoostR, Real AdaBoost) for binary classification needs fewer circuit executions than probabilistic error cancellation to hit the same classification-accuracy target as circuit depth and noise increase, backed by a proven common complexity bound for the studied circuit architectures and Pauli noise model (lukianenko-beyond-error-mitigation-using-boosting-for-noisy-quantum-machine-learning) — a different mechanism from APGEM's bandit-based technique selection, but the same facet: a classical-ML trick substituting for standard error mitigation. Simulation-only (teacher-student, finite-shot), single-group, no hardware run. Separately, a recurrent GRU-based quantum reinforcement learning agent tracks a drifting transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian substantially better than a non-recurrent QRL agent or a QGRNN baseline (kaldari-adaptive-hamiltonian-identification-via-quantum-reinforcement-learning-under-non-stationary-dynamics) — Hamiltonian identification is the characterization step calibration depends on, so this extends the device-characterization facet Diff-QST opened, but it is simulation-only on a textbook model system with no quantitative tracking-error figure given. Both single preprints; held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-18 — a new control-discovery facet opens, a calibration-based fix for QNN trainability lands, and a second device-design surrogate joins the facet opened 2026-11-06. QORA, a multi-agent AI framework closing the loop between reasoning, calibration and control, discovered AID-ROD, a pulse sequence on a dense ensemble of spin-1 nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond that nearly doubles measured spin coherence time over the best human-designed sequence, DROID-C3PO (yan-ai-scientist-discovered-robust-quantum-control) — real closed-loop hardware discovery, but a single nine-author preprint with no stated measurement uncertainty and no comparison to non-AI automated pulse-design methods. Separately, reusable calibration of a reference circuit sharing a quantum neural network's gate architecture restores the training signal hardware noise otherwise suppresses, with simulations showing faster convergence and an IBM Quantum experiment confirming improved closed-loop variational optimization, including a 100-qubit-aggregate modular demonstration (shen-restoring-quantum-neural-network-trainability-through-reusable-calibration) — real hardware validation, but the improvement is stated qualitatively with no quantitative figure and no comparison to this theme's other error-mitigation entries (e.g. APGEM). And QCap, a physics-corrected graph neural network, predicts the capacitance matrix of any planar superconducting device layout in seconds rather than hours, matching Ansys Q3D to a median 0.177% error on 4,009 held-out designs and reaching a 100-qubit device too large to simulate directly (shanto-qcap-physics-corrected-graph-neural-network-for-capacitance-matrix-of-superconducting-device-layouts) — a second device-design surrogate alongside the FNO quantum-dot result, validated against the same classical simulator it was trained to approximate, not a fabricated chip. All three single-group preprints; held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-17 — a first entry for AI-accelerated state tomography, opening a device-characterization/readout facet. Diff-QST, a measurement-conditioned diffusion model, reconstructs a density operator from noisy, incomplete Pauli measurements in two or three denoising steps, beating maximum-likelihood estimation and semidefinite-programming least squares on fidelity in all 120 tested 2-to-5-qubit simulated conditions and reconstructing faster at every system size — with the fidelity edge able to reverse once states or noise drift from the training ensemble (choi-quantum-state-tomography-with-measurement-conditioned-diffusion-models). This theme's scope was calibration, control and code design; tomography/readout is close enough in spirit (it's the measurement step calibration and control both depend on) to open as a new facet here rather than a new theme, but it's simulation-only and single-group like everything else in it. Status stays emerging.
  • 2026-11-11 — a first entry for online, learned error-mitigation selection, opening a new facet of this theme. APGEM, a contextual-bandit controller, picks among five standard error-mitigation techniques at each training step of a quantum model, using only feedback the training algorithm already produces; it reaches 95.5% of an oracle's performance across 80 simulated algorithm-and-noise conditions and 99.9% of oracle utility under noise models fit to eight real IBM devices (majid-adaptive-policy-guided-error-mitigation-apgem). The oracle is defined within the study's own menu of techniques, and the "eight real IBM devices" are noise models, not device time — single-group, simulation-only, held at emerging alongside the rest of this theme.
  • 2026-11-06 — a third learned-search method for code discovery, and a first device-design surrogate. LiftDiff, a guided discrete-diffusion model, generates lifted-product codes beating the Gross code across a range of error rates, including a [247,19,14] code with the best kd²/n among its own baselines (peled-liftdiff-guided-discrete-diffusion-for-quantum-code-construction) — a third distinct learned-search approach after RL and GFlowNet, same simulation-only, single-group pattern. Separately, an FNO surrogate trained on a Thomas-Fermi simulator predicts quantum-dot charge-density fields at 0.6% error with a 17,000x GPU speed-up, and drives an inverse co-design loop to shape double-dot geometries (rychetsky-fno-based-inverse-co-design-of-semiconducting-quantum-dot-devices) — a new device-design facet, but against a simulator, not a fabricated chip. Held at emerging: all three code-discovery methods and this surrogate remain single-group and untested on hardware.
  • 2026-11-05 — a second learned-search approach to code discovery, still simulation-only. GFlowQEC, a generative flow network, finds more than twice as many new top-scoring 180-qubit lifted-product codes per paid evaluation call as tuned parallel tempering (batista-gflowqec-generative-flow-networks-that-learn-to-sample-quantum-ldpc-codes), echoing the RL-based code discovery already here. The lead reverses at larger search budgets, excludes training cost, and covers one code size with no decoder in the score — held at emerging.
  • 2026-11-04 — a stack-wide review frames this theme but adds no hardware evidence. The 28-author survey of AI for quantum computing lists device control, system characterization and code discovery among the places AI is already applied (alexeev-artificial-intelligence-for-quantum-computing), which matches the RL-control and calibration-agent results here. It surveys reported results without reproducing them, so the status stays emerging. Compilation and algorithm discovery have their own theme, ai-for-quantum-circuit-compilation-and-algorithm-discovery.
  • 2026-11-04 — opened at emerging. The best-evidenced item is Google's reinforcement-learning control of a running Willow surface code: 3.5x better logical stability against injected drift (2.4x from RL alone), and about 20% extra suppression over the standard calibration stack and expert tuning in fine-tuning experiments (google-reinforcement-learning-control-of-quantum-error-correction). It is real hardware but one lab, a 299-author preprint with unverified journal status, and a drift model that was largely injected. Scalability (speed independent of size to distance 15) is simulation only.
  • 2026-11-04 — language-model agents are at the characterization stage. An LLM-orchestrated system characterised 108 of 112 qubits in 4.7 hours, 4 to 5x faster than an estimated manual campaign, but its scope was mostly single-qubit characterization with no gate fidelities and shorter T2 Ramsey times than experts (xu-vibe-calibration-llm-agent-autonomous-bring-up-of-a-112-qubit-processor). NVIDIA's Ising Calibration 1.5 is a vendor benchmark score on an in-house test with no shown device run (nvidia-ising-calibration-1-5-and-qcaleval-automated-calibration). Held at emerging because the hardware demonstrations are single-group preprints and the vendor claim is unvalidated.
  • 2026-11-04 — code discovery by RL is still simulation. A noise-aware RL agent finds codes and encoders up to 20 qubits and distance 5; no RL-discovered code beating known ones or running on hardware was found (olle-rl-discovery-of-qec-codes-and-encoders-with-a-noise-aware-agent).

Open questions

  • Whether the open-quantum-dynamics transformer surrogate's fidelity and speedup gains hold with quantitative figures attached, at larger/noisier systems, and against a fabricated device's measured dynamics or this theme's other surrogates.
  • Whether the Shor-order-finding Fourier-neural-operator mitigator generalizes beyond its one algorithm, and how it compares against APGEM, boosting, or the small-circuit mitigator on a shared task.
  • Whether QuOptLab's agent-driven gains (thin-film measurement, edge-coupler design) survive on a fabricated optical bench with a real robotic arm and camera, rather than a simulated laboratory.
  • Whether qlaybot's layout-design advantage survives when a produced layout has to go through real lithography and measurement, rather than an automated geometric-fit check.
  • Whether the small-circuit mitigator's circuit-cutting reuse trick has been compared against APGEM's bandit-based technique selection on the same task, and whether either survives a real hardware run.
  • Whether the world-model and GRU-QRL approaches to Hamiltonian identification have been compared head-to-head, and whether either holds up against a fabricated device's real measured dynamics rather than simulated Ising/Heisenberg lattices.
  • Whether the FPGA-in-the-loop quantum-dot auto-tuner's compute-in-place advantage survives on a fabricated device, and how its tuning time and stability compare quantitatively to existing classical-computer-based auto-tuners.
  • Whether the boosting-vs-PEC resource advantage survives non-Pauli, correlated, or drifting noise, and at real-device scale rather than teacher-student simulation.
  • Whether GRU-QRL's Hamiltonian drift-tracking advantage holds with a quantitative figure attached, and against real hardware drift rather than a simulated transverse-field Ising model.
  • Whether QORA's AID-ROD pulse sequence gain reproduces independently, and whether it beats non-AI automated pulse-design methods (e.g. GRAPE) on the same system rather than only the named human-designed baseline.
  • Whether reusable-calibration QNN training's hardware gain holds with a quantitative figure attached, at circuit scales beyond the 4-qubit-channel building block, and against this theme's other error-mitigation methods (e.g. APGEM) head-to-head.
  • Whether QCap's capacitance predictions hold against a fabricated and measured device, not just against the Ansys Q3D simulator used to generate its training labels.
  • Whether APGEM's error-mitigation policy holds up on real device execution rather than fitted noise models, and against drift and correlated noise a model does not capture.
  • Whether RL control gains hold against natural drift over hours or days, and appear in an independent lab.
  • Whether LLM-agent calibration reaches two-qubit gate calibration and reports fidelities, not just characterization.
  • A peer-reviewed version of the RL-control result (journal status unverified).
  • Independent validation of vendor calibration models on real devices.
  • Whether any of the three learned-search code-discovery methods (RL, GFlowNet or diffusion) finds a code that beats the best known construction, and whether the comparison still holds when training/search cost is counted in full.
  • Whether the FNO quantum-dot design surrogate transfers from its Thomas-Fermi training simulator to a fabricated and measured device.
