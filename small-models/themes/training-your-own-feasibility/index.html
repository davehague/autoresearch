Is training our own on the cluster feasible and cheap?

Setting aside whether it's worth it, can we actually train specialised small models on our HPC cluster without it being a research project — and how cheap is it?

Through-line

  • 2026-08-08 (run 13) — Stays contested. Two entries, and between them they remove the two most expensive inputs the run-12 build order assumed we would have to buy. The training corpus. CalibForge argues the standard synthesis recipe — author a task, execute it, verify it is solvable — produces tasks that are feasible but not learnable, because feasibility says nothing about difficulty relative to the model being trained. It revises candidates against observed solver behaviour: multi-solver calibration steers generation into the disagreement band of a heterogeneous solver pool, contrastive calibration targets an explicit strong-pass/weak-fail relation. From that it builds 5,431 calibrated terminal tasks, with ablations showing both strategies beat authoring-plus-validation alone and beat single-solver feedback, and largest gains over base of +24.71 points on Terminal-Bench 2.0, +27.68 on SWE-bench Pro, +30.04 on Doc2Repo — the last two off the synthesis target (training-tasks-must-be-calibrated-to-the-solver-not-just-validated). The run-12 build order has an unstated dependency nobody named: a set of tasks our model fails on instructively. We do not have one, every method on this map assumes it, and this is a published procedure for manufacturing it at a corpus size that is a week of cluster time rather than a research programme. The mechanism is this map's recurring one on its fifth instance — the usable signal is a difference between two runs — and it is another thin-external-anchor case: solver disagreement is internal, but every candidate is first gated by real execution in a terminal. The teacher. U-OPSD trains with no labels, no teacher and no reward model — sample several completions, majority-vote a pseudo-solution, distil the shortest correct one into the incorrect ones — gaining 8.5% at Qwen3-4B and 10.7% at 8B over base, beating supervised OPSD by 3.2% and 2.3% and GRPO by 0.7–1.1% (label-free-self-distillation-beats-supervised-distillation-on-math). What it removes is what matters here. Every distillation result on this map assumes frontier traces to log, an API bill to pay, and the provenance defect run 7 found, where a run goes flat because the supervising model did not generate the SFT traces (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization). U-OPSD has no teacher, so it has no provenance problem by construction, and it costs sampling rather than procurement — a materially different proposition for a cluster with idle capacity and no purchasing path for frontier tokens. It sits before the run-12 build order rather than competing with it: extract the free gains from the student alone, then spend teacher budget on the residue. Both are tightly bounded. CalibForge does not state its base or trained model identity or size, so whether it is a small-model result at all is unknown, and its headline deltas are maxima over configurations rather than means — two trained configurations report 32.58% and 47.57% on the same benchmark with no stated cause. U-OPSD is competition mathematics only, the task class with the strongest self-consistency structure and the least resemblance to our work, on two sizes of one family, with no token-matched comparison — and cost matching is exactly the control that reversed the self-inspection result in run 7 (self-inspection-harnesses-lose-to-repeated-sampling-at-equal-cost). Its thinking-mode parity is the honest ceiling: it largely recovers what extended reasoning already supplies. The validation column is untouched by either — five probes now grade training signals, and still nothing validates a model against a task. [reviewed 2026-08-08 @ 9 bullets]
  • 2026-08-07 (run 12) — Stays contested. A fourth probe joins the pipeline instrumentation, and this one is the cheapest yet: one extra teacher forward pass per trajectory, no extra rollouts, no critic, no learned parameters. AgentOPSD aggregates token-level teacher–student log-probability gaps into turn-level evidence and recursively updates a Bayesian belief over which turns of a multi-turn agent trajectory earned its single sparse outcome reward. On Qwen2.5-3B and 7B across ALFWorld, WebShop and Search-QA it beats GRPO by +6.2 to +10.6 points, reaching 89.1% on ALFWorld at 7B (turn-level-credit-adds-more-to-the-3b-than-the-7b). The mechanism is this map's recurring one and the complaint is the familiar one: scalar aggregation over a trajectory destroys signal the run already paid to collect — the same objection rubric-rl-silently-drops-signal-recoverable-by-self-distillation made about rubric criteria and distillation-harms-are-invisible-in-aggregate-metrics made about condition-averaged accuracy. The remedy is shaped like SA-OPD's: run the teacher–student pair and read a difference. What is new is the grain — it grades turns of an agent trajectory rather than tokens of a completion, which is the level our own workflows fail at. The size direction is the accumulating result and it is now hard to write off. Every delta is larger on the 3B than the 7B — +9.4 vs +7.9 (ALFWorld), +10.3 vs +7.2 (Search-QA), +10.6 vs +9.3 (WebShop). That is three more direct measurements on the pile putting training- and scaffold-adjacent levers largest at small scale, against the one relayed study claiming a mid-tier peak (harness-benefit-peaks-at-middle-tier-models). Nobody has plotted the curve and the mid-size arm stays mandatory in our own design, but the pattern no longer rests on any single paper. Counterweight from the same run, and it belongs here rather than being filed away: EnvACE's scaling row shows 1.7B → 8B worth 14.23% on BFCL and 21.4% on τ²-Bench, several times its own method's effect. Method deltas being largest at the small end does not mean small models end up in front — base size is still doing more of the work than the technique on realistic tool-use benchmarks. One arrangement here answers part of run 11's open question directly. AgentOPSD's teacher is the student's own policy conditioned on skills retrieved from a SkillBank, and at inference the student uses no external skills at all (skill-text-can-supervise-training-and-be-dropped-at-inference). SKILL-KD's caveat was a standing context tax — a 3,010-word library riding on every inference, unpriced (textual-skill-patches-lift-a-frozen-4b-by-23-points). This arrangement removes that tax by construction, because the library is a training-time input rather than a serving-time one. Read together they suggest a sequencing rather than a choice: build the skill library the cheap way (log frontier traces on failures, diff, patch, validate by re-running, prune), use it to condition a teacher, distil into the student, ship weights with an empty prompt. Each half is independently measured; the composition is our inference and not a published result — different skill mechanisms, different model families — and it is small enough to run on our own cluster. Limits across the whole item: preprint, one group, unreplicated, and all three environments are bounded and programmatically verifiable, the same property that made this map's node-graph evidence resist generalisation. Absolute levels outside ALFWorld stay modest (49.2% on Search-QA), and on WebShop the 3B leads the 7B on score while trailing it badly on accuracy — a live instance of this map's own rule that the summary metric picks the winner.
  • 2026-08-06 (run 11) — Stays contested, and the theme's binding item — validation, not detection — gets its first instrument that grades the training signal itself rather than a proxy for it. SA-OPD's move is an ablation, not a heuristic: run the teacher and student a second time with the prompt deleted, and compare the two teacher–student log-ratios. Supervision that barely changes when the question disappears was never about the question. Filter a token only where low input-groundedness meets high optimisation impact — neither criterion works alone (random 67.6/67.8, divergence-only 69.8/69.6, groundedness-only 69.0/70.3, both 72.2/72.1 on Geo3K/MathVista) — and recover up to +7.2 points on CountQA, +5.0 on Geo3K, +1.9 average on math distillation, for 2.64–7.53% extra training time (input-ablation-detects-spurious-distillation-supervision). Two extra forward passes, no labels, no teacher retraining. Three reasons this is the most useful thing this map has ingested. It is diagnostic before it is corrective — the input-grounding-gap distribution is a readout of how contaminated a given teacher/student/corpus combination is, computable on our own pipeline before we commit a run, which is exactly the shape of instrument the validation item has been missing. It separates a defect class run 7 had merged: provenance failure (wrong supervisor, flat run against clean data) and spurious supervision are indistinguishable in a loss curve but have different fixes, and this one needs no teacher swap. And it kills the cheap substitutes — 69.9% of filtered tokens are content words, not formatting, and they sit at both high and low entropy, so entropy-based token selection systematically misses them. The uncomfortable half is the familiar one. Vanilla OPD was leaving 5–7 points on the table on individual tasks while the math average moved +1.9, which would read as noise. That is the sixth pipeline stage in this vault showing the same invisible-in-the-average pattern, after curation, the distillation objective, the RL objective, deployment precision, and now skill drift (smaller-validated-skill-libraries-beat-larger-unvalidated-ones, where dropping consolidation costs 27.4 points on one task and nothing on two others). Every distillation number this map has ingested was measured with an unknown amount of this defect in it. Scope it honestly: students are 1.7B–2.6B, teachers 4B–35B, so there is no frontier-teacher arm — the configuration we would actually run — and nothing tests whether a wider capability gap changes spurious-signal density. Three hyperparameters with no sensitivity analysis visible. No stated limitations, and the obvious failure case goes untreated: a genuinely input-independent-but-correct behaviour (safety refusal, format compliance) is by this definition indistinguishable from a spurious one. One preprint, four days old, no code link. A second item widens the feasibility question rather than the instrumentation one. SKILL-KD lifts a frozen Qwen3.5-4B by 23.4 average points using textual skill patches distilled from teacher/student trajectory contrasts, cross-family, with no weight updates at all (textual-skill-patches-lift-a-frozen-4b-by-23-points). This map exists to ask whether training our own is feasible; that result asks whether, for some workflows, we need to train at all. It does not answer it — the baselines are other skill-induction methods, never a fine-tuned student — but a +23-point, no-cluster, one-week option belongs on the ledger next to the training runs it might displace.
  • 2026-08-05 (run 10) — Stays contested; two items, and they sit on the theme's two opposite halves. On the compute/tooling half (settled at "yes, easily"), a claim that would extend the settlement one stage further if it holds: Syzygy reports compressing a 35B to 1.7 bits per weight in under 15 GPU hours of retraining, contrasted with BitNet-style methods that require training from scratch, and says it scales to 3T parameters (retrofit-compression-claims-15-gpu-hours). Fifteen GPU hours is a lunch break on hardware we already staff. Filed as an open question rather than a confirmation because it is a vendor number with no recipe, no hardware named, and no accounting of what "retraining" covers — but it is cheap to falsify and it changes what we build if true, which puts it near the top of the list of things to verify ourselves. Its value is not the compression, it is the sweepability: precision stops being a one-way commitment and becomes a knob we can produce several settings of and evaluate properly. On the instrumentation half (the theme's real constraint), run 9's deployment finding gets an itemized bill and it lands worse than expected. Mach-1's own per-benchmark table shows compression retaining 99.5% of AIME26 and 83.2% of IFBench, with agentic tool use (τ²-bench 90.0%, BFCL-v3 92.0%) clustered at the bottom alongside instruction-following (compression-degrades-agentic-skills-more-than-reasoning). So the invisible-defect property that runs from curation through the distillation and RL objectives to deployment precision does not merely cost knowledge at the last stage — it costs the ability to follow instructions and call tools, which is the capability a trained specialist exists to provide. The theme's standing conclusion holds and sharpens: feasibility is gated on instrumentation, not compute, and the validation gap named in run 5 is still open, since the two probes we have (prefix loss on inputs, KL divergence on outputs) both grade proxies. What run 10 supplies is not a probe but a rule that requires no new instrument at all: evaluate every stage on the capability you are shipping, split by condition, never on an average. Discount the source as a vendor model card with no independent evaluation, no error bars and no ablation separating bit width from retraining recipe.
  • 2026-08-04 (run 9) — Stays contested; the theme's core finding extends to the last stage of the pipeline, and for the first time picks up a working detector. Run 8 generalised the defects that cap a run are invisible in the summary number from curation into the distillation and RL objectives. Quantization is the same shape at deployment: across 58 quantizations of one 27B model, factual knowledge degrades non-linearly with compression while the same author's same quant ladder showed no cliff at all on AIME-120 and Terminal-Bench 2.1 (quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly). Compress the model, run the reasoning eval, see nothing, ship, lose the long tail. The chain is now complete end to end — curation, distillation objective, RL objective, deployment precision — and every link fails the same way, so this is a property of aggregating metrics rather than a property of any one stage. The new thing is the instrument. Knowledge loss tracks KL divergence from the full-precision original at r = −0.981, which means a candidate compression can be screened on a sample of our own traffic without running any knowledge benchmark. Note what kind of detector that is and where the theme's gap remains: the run 5 prefix-loss filter grades inputs (is this trace worth training on), and this grades output distribution shift against a known-good reference. Both are cheap, both work, and neither validates a model against a task — the validation gap named in run 5 is still open, and the honest read is that we now have two narrow probes rather than a pipeline. Doesn't move status: this is a deployment finding reaching backward into a training theme, on a single self-published study of a single model.
  • 2026-08-03 (run 8) — Stays contested; the theme's core finding — the defects that cap a run are invisible in the summary number — extends from the data pipeline into two more stages, the distillation objective and the RL objective. First, distilling a 9B teacher into a 1.7B student cut context-override error 44%→24% on unambiguous items while silently flipping 15% of correct abstentions into stereotype answers on ambiguous ones; the two harms correlate at only ρ=0.19 and a single averaged accuracy hides both, detectable only by splitting the evaluation by condition (distillation-harms-are-invisible-in-aggregate-metrics). Second, rubric-based RL loses the learning signal for criteria some rollouts already satisfy — over 57% of samples affected, ~1.8 per sample — because scalar reward aggregation washes it out, recoverable by on-policy self-distillation (no frontier teacher) in ~2× fewer steps (rubric-rl-silently-drops-signal-recoverable-by-self-distillation). Neither moves status, and both are single-source/thin (one BBQ study with one author; a method paper that never reports model sizes, so transfer to our scales is unknown). What they add is generality to the sub-thread: the validation gap is not specific to data curation — it recurs wherever a summary metric aggregates over conditions or criteria, which is every stage of the pipeline. The self-distillation half also adds a second point to the "you don't always need a frontier teacher" column.
  • 2026-08-02 (run 7) — Stays contested, and the data-pipeline constraint gains a failure mode the theme had not catalogued — one that fires on a clean corpus. On-policy distillation carries an unstated precondition, teacher consistency: the model supplying token-level supervision should also have generated the demonstrations behind the SFT reference. Assembling a corpus the practical way violates it constantly (mixed or unknown provenance; different models preferred for generation and supervision), and in those cross-teacher settings even a stronger teacher can yield little improvement over the SFT reference (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization). The diagnosis is that raw teacher–reference disagreement mixes genuine teacher evidence with a recurring style component — wording, formatting, reasoning cadence — so part of the update budget goes to imitating another model's prose rhythm; residualising it out recovers the gain, reaching 82.4% AIME 2024 and 63.0% LiveCodeBench v5 from an 8B SFT checkpoint. This does not move status, and the positive result should be discounted hard — one paper, one checkpoint, code unreleased, and the headline numbers arrive without baseline figures beside them, so the magnitude of the improvement is unstated. What is cheap and robust is the negative half, and it belongs in our build plan: a flat distillation run against clean data is an expected outcome of provenance mismatch, not evidence the student cannot learn the task. Note where this sits relative to the theme's existing instrument — the run 5 prefix-loss filter grades whether a trace is worth training on and is silent on whether the supervisor is compatible with it. Two distinct pipeline defects, both invisible in a loss curve, and now only one of them has a detector.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 5) — Stays contested, but the data-side constraint got its first real instrument. Training value of a long reasoning trace turns out to be readable from its first 100 tokens — loss at a randomly perturbed checkpoint separates difficult examples, and prefix loss similarity provably tracks gradient similarity — beating existing curation baselines by 1.7% at 91% greater token efficiency, with no strong external teacher in the filtering loop (reasoning-data-quality-readable-from-first-tokens). The operationally decisive number is the 91%, not the 1.7%: curation becomes a prefix scan on hardware we own rather than a full-corpus frontier-API bill, and the circularity of needing a good model to build the data that makes a good model is broken. Two supporting datapoints on the compute side: sub-2B fine-tuning runs on a single L4 overnight (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators), and a full recompile cycle — synthetic data generation, fine-tune, eval — takes 30–50 minutes on 8×H200, which makes a tuned model a CI/CD artifact rather than a research project (compiled-small-model-nears-frontier-beats-orchestrator). Still contested because the instrument is narrow: it grades long reasoning traces for reasoning-model SFT and addresses neither the syntactic nor the semantic corruption modes that produced the fragility finding in the first place. One usable component with a known cost is not a validation pipeline. The theme's live question is unchanged — can we curate and validate the corpus — but it is now a question about coverage rather than about whether any tooling exists.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 4) — Moved emerging → contested. The "yes, easily" read survives on compute and fails on data. Fine-tuning small models is acutely fragile to training-data quality: across 23 models (270M–4B), syntactic corruption caused near-total failure, semantic corruption showed threshold effects rather than gradual decay, more-capable students absorbed corruption more readily (a "capability curse"), and alignment training sometimes reduced resilience (finetuning-is-fragile-to-data-quality, which rebuts the unqualified form of finetuning-is-cheap-enough-to-be-routine). Distillation carries its own preconditions (distillation-does-not-universally-close-the-gap). The revised read: the cluster is not the constraint and never was — the data pipeline is, and its failure mode is quiet, sudden, and invisible in a loss curve. Feasibility questions should move from "can we run the job" to "can we curate and validate the corpus."
  • 2026-08-01 — Opened, and the early read is "yes, easily." Multi-node post-training is first-class in PyTorch-native tooling (pytorch-native-multinode-posttraining-is-ready, torchtune), and fine-tuning is fast/low-VRAM enough to be routine, now on AMD too (finetuning-is-cheap-enough-to-be-routine, Unsloth). emerging and deliberately scoped to feasibility — "we can" is not "we should"; the worth-it question lives in the parity and serving-economics themes.

Sub-threads: the data-curation risk thread remains the live edge and has now split three ways — detection (one cheap instrument, scoped to reasoning traces), validation (still nothing: no story for catching corruption before it silently caps a run), and, new in run 7, provenance (traces and supervisor must be compatible, or a clean corpus still yields a flat run). All three share one property that is really the theme's core finding: the corpus defects that matter are invisible in a loss curve, so feasibility depends on instrumentation we do not yet have rather than on compute we do. The compute/tooling thread is settled ("yes, easily") and a retrain-cadence sub-thread is forming: if a recompile is 30–50 minutes, the relevant feasibility unit is the deployment cycle, not the training run. Run 9 forces one revision to the thread's scope: the invisible-defect property does not stop at training. It runs through deployment precision too, so the instrumentation debt covers the whole path from corpus to served checkpoint. Two cheap probes now exist — prefix loss on inputs, KL divergence on output distribution — and both grade proxies rather than task performance, which is why validation stays the open item. Run 10 adds the one thing that closes part of the gap without an instrument: the failure is only invisible if you look at an average, and the capability that degrades most at the last stage is instruction-following and tool use, so a condition-split agentic eval on our own tasks is a validation step we can build today. The retrain-cadence sub-thread also gains a second unit — if retrofit compression really costs under 15 GPU hours, precision joins fine-tuning as something swept per deployment rather than chosen once. Run 11 finally puts something in the validation column: a detector that grades the supervision rather than the corpus or the served checkpoint, built on the same move that works everywhere else in this vault — give the check an external referent, here the input itself. Three probes now exist across the pipeline (prefix loss on inputs, input-grounding gap on supervision, KL divergence on output distribution), and the shape of what is still missing is clearer for it: nothing yet validates a model against a task, which remains the only thing that would let us decide feasibility rather than infer it. Run 11 also opens a sub-thread that cuts across this map's premise — (e) do we need to train at all? A frozen 4B gaining 23.4 points from validated prompt state is not an answer, but it is a cheap alternative that belongs on the same ledger as the training runs, and it shares this map's core defect: the failure that kills it is invisible in the mean. Run 12 partly closes (e) by showing the two are not rivals: skill text can live in the teacher and be dropped at inference, so the frozen-weights route is a cheap way to build supervision rather than a substitute for training. That yields the concrete build order this map has lacked — library first, teacher second, distil third, ship weights with an empty prompt — and each step is independently measured even though the chain is not. The instrumentation column now holds four probes (prefix loss on inputs, input-grounding gap on supervision, turn-level credit on trajectories, KL divergence on outputs), and the residue is unchanged and increasingly conspicuous: nothing validates a model against a task. One new caution belongs on the ledger — every probe added so far grades a training signal, and EnvACE's scaling row is a reminder that base size still outweighs technique on realistic tool use, so better instrumentation does not by itself make a smaller model sufficient.
