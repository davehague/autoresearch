Is training our own on the cluster feasible and cheap?

Setting aside whether it's worth it, can we actually train specialised small models on our HPC cluster without it being a research project — and how cheap is it?

Through-line

  • 2026-08-22 (run 26) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, both audits rather than methods, and together they say that the cheapest way to train our own does not work and that we would not have been able to tell. Seven standard measurement practices, each of which inverts a self-improvement finding when its control is absent. Phantom Gains pushes a frozen, untrained Qwen3-8B through the identical pipeline as three rounds of rank-32 LoRA self-training. A single greedy decode manufactures a corruption-to-learning ratio of 1.5 on the untrained model, largely inference-batching nondeterminism. The expansion statistic that separates acquisition from sharpening assigns that same model 0.280, against a null measured over 110 frozen comparisons of 0.058 [0.038, 0.078] — non-zero, so the obvious threshold repair does not survive. A fixed token cap pushes the distillation student's truncation 16.5% → 65.3% on style alone. One seed gives majority-vote CLR anywhere in 0.55–1.53. A 10-prompt safety probe shows a phantom 10-point drop that 50 prompts dissolve (seven-standard-measurement-practices-each-invert-a-self-improvement-finding). The repair costs no new experiments: a per-problem exact test against a pooled baseline under FDR control, whose defining property is that it detects nothing on all 11 held-out replicates. Under that null, external distillation acquires and self-training does not — and self-training breaks what worked. Low-base pool detections: distillation 8–11, three self-training arms 0–2, with a regression rejecting "distillation just gained more overall" at β = 1.91 [1.25, 2.56], p < 10⁻⁸. Corruptions in the difficulty band: STaR 106, majority-vote 88, design-matched frozen control 8 (self-training-expands-nothing-and-corrupts-at-ten-times-the-floor). The arm that survives the audit is the one needing a stronger external teacher, which reinstates the dependency run 16 flagged as a supply we do not control (frontier-teacher-traces-are-a-supply-we-do-not-control). This does not overturn run 13's label-free self-distillation result or run 15's warm-up finding — different statistics, different setups — but any of them reporting a transition ledger without a frozen control should be re-read. And it reprices this map's standing experiment. Transition metrics — which tasks a change won and which it lost, as against a mean — need roughly 10× the data, and a 112-problem pilot is explicitly insufficient (a-transition-level-ledger-needs-ten-times-the-data-an-accuracy-number-does). The paper's own working design is k = 128 samples per problem per checkpoint, and its null needed 110 frozen comparisons. Run 26's credit audit independently confirms the shape from the other side: a 128-task training design with a minimum detectable effect of ~11.8 points, which is larger than any effect worth having. The plan of record here — run the candidate checkpoint on our tasks and record where it fails, with repeats — is a transition ledger, and sized honestly it is an order of magnitude more evaluation than anyone drafting it would have costed. The consolation is that the extra cost is inference, not GPU-hours, and the nulls come free from replicates a multi-arm design already owns. The second audit adds a confound that will hit every A/B we run. In a seven-arm credit-assignment experiment — including an arm built from causal ground truth — no arm reliably beat the untrained policy, and the checkpoints' apparent instrument signature was fully explained by training dose: sparser credit rules dropped more turns from batches, moving optimizer steps 112 → 88 per round (credit-rule-comparisons-measure-training-dose-not-credit). Any harness or data component that filters, gates, prunes or routes changes the effective sample as a side effect, and any downstream difference is confounded with it. Match effective sample size, or the comparison measures dose. Discounts: Phantom Gains' empirical finding is bounded by its own authors — three rounds at ~270 optimizer steps is far short of the schedules it positions against, the expansion claim rests on 22 low-base problems (10 base-unreached, called inconclusive), corruption is measured on MATH problems almost certainly in pretraining, and one backbone family carries every claim. The credit audit is one environment, one scale, offline LoRA, 128 tasks, no validated positive control. In both cases the methodological conclusions travel considerably further than the empirical ones, which is the right way round for planning.

  • 2026-08-21 (run 25) — Stays contested. One arrival, and it lands exactly on the resource run 24 said this map had been mis-provisioning against. Reshape the environment; do not generate a new one. EnvHarness wraps a static environment in plug-in components over the standard reset/step interface — Stage, Contract, Chain — leaving the underlying logic alone, which means every reshaped task inherits the original human-built verifier. An automated loop reads the policy's own trajectories, writes components against diagnosed weaknesses, and commits only what fresh rollouts validate. Skills extracted from reshaped environments beat skills from the originals on all five benchmarks: ALFWorld held-out 61.4 → 70.4 (+9.0), ALFWorld average +5.9, WebArena average +3.1, SWE-bench Verified 49.88 → 52.58, OfficeQA EM +1.80, SpreadsheetBench Pass@1 +3.27 (reshaping-an-environment-beats-generating-new-ones). It also beats the generation pipelines it was compared against — GenEnv, VeriEnv and SWE-smith. Two details carry more than the deltas. Skills from unmodified environments can be worse than none: on SpreadsheetBench they score 45.88 against a 46.44 no-skill baseline, and on SWE-bench they lengthen episodes 53.58 → 55.01 for their 2.21 points. And the token cost is grounding, not method: on WebArena, EnvHarness totals 137.3M against VeriEnv's 137.8M, the same footprint at the same grounding, with design tokens 1.46M against 38K and a small share of either budget. The argument for wrapping is the verifier. Run 19 identified RL environments and infrastructure as what does not distil (rl-environments-and-infrastructure-are-what-does-not-distil); run 21 priced a verified environment at under thirty thousand tokens (a-verified-rl-environment-costs-under-thirty-thousand-tokens). If the scarce good is the trusted verifier, wrapping keeps it for free and synthesis pays to rebuild it. That is a cheaper answer to the environment dependency than anything this map has held. And it is a training signal, not only an inference-time prop. Qwen3-8B-base, GRPO, one node of 8× H100, model and algorithm and task set fixed, varying only which environment the rollouts ran against: ALFWorld in-distribution 81.4 → 87.9, WebShop score 75.6 → 79.2, WebShop success 66.0 → 67.4, with ALFWorld held-out the one regression at 89.6 → 88.8 (an-8b-trained-in-reshaped-environments-beats-one-trained-in-the-originals). Set that beside run 24's measurement that agent execution is 91.3% of a harnessed-RL run's wall-clock (agent-execution-is-ninety-one-percent-of-the-training-bill) and the two compose into a provisioning statement this map can act on: if nine tenths of the bill is rollouts, the leverage is in what the rollouts run against, and this is the first source to vary that term with everything else pinned. Reshaping is a way to spend the 91% better rather than a way to shrink it. It sits directly in front of the sandbox-concurrency measurement run 24 queued — how many concurrent sandboxes can we run, and what does our image registry do at 100 cold pulls — because both are about the same 91%. The disqualifier is stated by the authors and it matters for us. The method requires a resettable gym-style interface: a Stage must set an initial state, a Chain must restore a known state between subtasks. That excludes any environment backed by a live service, a real user account, or a physical system — a sent email or a placed order cannot be undone. Several of the workflows this vault would actually target are in that excluded class. Discounts: the headline gains are skill transfer, not trained policies, and several deltas sit inside their reported standard deviations (SWE-bench ±2.72 against a 2.70-point gain); the RL arm is one model, one algorithm, one run per arm, and loses one of four metrics; the designer agent is a frontier model whose cost is acknowledged and never priced in dollars or GPU-hours, the fifth consecutive framework paper here to skip that number; and run 21's finding that synthesis uplift rises with base model size (environment-synthesis-uplift-rises-with-base-model-size) sorts the opposite way from this run's headroom results, with nothing adjudicating.

  • 2026-08-20 (run 24) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, and the first one says this map has been provisioning for the wrong resource. The GPU is not the bottleneck in harnessed agentic RL — the coding agent is. Across 3,699 OpenHands SDK trials, LEGO-RL measures agent execution at 91.3% of mean wall-clock: 840.5 s of 920.4 per trial, against 2.3% for sandbox setup and 3.9% for verification (agent-execution-is-ninety-one-percent-of-the-training-bill). Every cost argument on this map has been about accelerators — memory ceilings, EFLOPs, A100-hours, an 87% GPU price increase (the-hardware-side-of-buy-vs-build-took-an-87-percent-price-increase), DRAM up 4–9× (system-memory-repriced-four-to-nine-fold-in-twelve-months). That framing is right for supervised tuning and classical RL, where the environment is cheap and the backward pass is not. It is wrong for the paradigm this vault has now ingested three runs running (harnessed-agentic-rl-has-a-name-and-five-implementations), because the environment is a full coding agent editing a repository in a container for fourteen minutes. The scarce resource is sandboxed CPU concurrency, container image throughput and disk — things our cluster has and prices badly — not the accelerator hours it rations. Both of the paper's engineering wins are attacks on the 91.3% rather than the 8.7%, which is why they are large. Async scheduling completes 7 steps against synchronous's 3 in the same 7.5 hours; correcting for the two runs' different optimizer throughput leaves 1.9 h per step synchronous against 1.0 h asynchronous. Lazy image delivery improves median cold-start 1.7× and maximum latency 23× across 100 SWE-bench Verified images, cutting network traffic 21.6 → 1.59 GB and disk writes 65.6 → 5.29 GB. It also softens the standing complaint that none of these papers publishes a dollar figure: the dominant term is not accelerator-hours. And run 23's enumeration of failure surfaces gets two of them measured. History alignment is bought by discarding data — trajectories whose history the harness genuinely rewrote or truncated are excluded from optimization rather than approximated. The one not on run 23's list is specific to the model class we would host: for sparse MoE, reproducing the token sequence is not enough when rollout and training route to different experts. Replaying rollout-time routing moves the rollout–training log-probability correlation 0.9946 → 0.9993 and |Δlog p| 0.0062 → 0.0025 on the same workload (routing-replay-is-what-makes-moe-harness-training-faithful). A run that trains on the right tokens with the wrong experts will not crash — it will train slightly off-policy and report plausible curves. The instrument that catches it costs one correlation statistic. The second arrival closes an escape hatch that has been doing quiet work in every cost estimate here. The unwritten assumption has been an agent will drive the training runs. Across PostTrainBench — 7 benchmarks, 4 base models, 20 agent configurations — the training strategy is locked in before the agent writes any code or runs any experiment, and the whole remaining budget goes to local adjustments inside it. The lock-in does not respond to the task: 80.7% of Claude Code trajectories converge on full-parameter SFT and 89.6% of Codex CLI trajectories converge on parameter-efficient tuning (an-agent-post-training-a-model-picks-its-strategy-before-it-runs-anything). Those two choices are separated by 8.41 GB against 58.88 GB on this map's own memory measurement (full-parameter-tuning-at-inference-memory-eight-gigabytes) and four orders of magnitude in its cost ledger. The most consequential decision in an automated pipeline is inherited from the harness vendor at ~85% consistency. Add it to run 12's grader finding as a variable that has to be pinned (grader-model-is-a-fourth-variable-that-has-to-be-pinned). Three escalating interventions fail to move it. An experience scaffold — persistent journal, distilled skill library, dedicated evaluator agent — lifts execution +12.6 on GSM8K and +40.8 on HumanEval and leaves the strategy static; a human reviewer redirects the initial strategy successfully and the agent falls back into local-adjustment loops once training starts; 2–8× more inference tokens pays off on easier tasks and yields almost nothing on AIME 2025 (the-scaffold-moved-execution-forty-points-and-strategy-zero). Read it as a split rather than a verdict: agents reliably get from a base model and a benchmark to a better checkpoint, and they do not notice three experiments in that the approach is wrong. Given this map's ledger — LoRA, full-parameter, GRPO, evolution strategies, on-policy distillation, environment synthesis and prompt-resident skills each measured right under some condition and wrong under another — strategy selection is where our value is, and it is the part that does not automate. A rare published price, worth keeping: 10 hours on 4× NVIDIA A800 per configuration, three runs each, for agent-driven post-training of a 1.7B. Small, affordable, well inside what our cluster grants — and the price of an attempt whose most important decision was made in the first minutes. Discounts: LEGO-RL still publishes no GPU count or dollar cost for its own production runs and no repeats on any arm; the post-training study runs its interventions on one agent-model pair with three runs and no intervals, on a 1.7B base and three well-trodden benchmarks, over a corpus of trajectories people chose to publish.

  • 2026-08-19 (run 23) — Stays contested, but the reason has flipped halves. The feasible side got its two strongest results yet; the cheap side got worse, and this map is named for the second one. Full-parameter tuning ran in 8.41 GB. Agentic ESOpt replaces RL with evolution strategies — perturb parameters, run inference, score whole trajectories, never run a backward pass — and measures peak GPU memory on Qwen3.5-4B at 8.41 GB against agentic GRPO's 58.88 GB and agentic PPO's 89.40 GB, a stated 85.7% reduction, at full-parameter optimization rather than adapters (full-parameter-tuning-at-inference-memory-eight-gigabytes). The constraint that has made agentic RL a cluster activity is a property of backpropagation, not of training. The wall-clock is the quieter half: 9.4 hours against 19.0 at 9.4 EFLOPs against 10.9 on Sudoku at H*=15 — not memory bought with compute, but both won at once, because RL is spending its compute on a credit-assignment problem that grows with horizon and ES has none (the-gradient-free-advantage-grows-with-horizon-length). That mechanism lands on exactly this vault's workloads, and it composes with run 22's lever in a way nobody has tried: ES through an unmodified shipped harness, where the harness's opacity stops mattering because the trainer was never going to look inside the trajectory. And a trained 9B, on a public benchmark, from data we could assemble. Agent Lightning v1.0 moves Qwen3.5-9B on SWE-bench Verified from 41.8% to 56.4% — +14.6 — on 6,000 examples (~5,000 post-filter) through the deliberately minimal mini-SWE-agent harness, with two smaller arms at +16.6 (Llama-3.2-3B search) and +18.3 (Qwen3-4B instruction-following) (a-9b-gains-14-6-points-on-swe-bench-through-a-minimal-harness). Three properties matter: 9B is inside every hardware bound here, 6K examples is a corpus rather than a commission (compare ~29,000 synthesis tokens per verified environment, a-verified-rl-environment-costs-under-thirty-thousand-tokens), and SWE-bench Verified has an external verifier instead of the authors' own. Note what the harness choice does to run 22's mechanism: the stronger harness was the better training surface was read as harness quality becoming supervision quality (the-stronger-harness-was-the-better-training-surface); +14.6 through a minimal harness is the first datapoint for the cheaper hypothesis, that the harness's job is to produce in-distribution trajectories rather than to be good. Not a controlled comparison — different model, benchmark, task — but the two readings differ by whether we must build a sophisticated harness before training through it, which is a cost this map is trying to bound. What run 22 recorded as a fixed failure mode now has a name, and it is five names. ClawGym II's headline was stability over 200–400 steps; this run supplies what was unstable. When the trainer sees only request–response pairs, it must cope with retokenization, sample merging, advantage calculation, loss normalization, and backend scheduling (the-trainer-sees-only-request-response-pairs-and-that-is-five-failure-surfaces) — five places a run looks healthy while optimizing a sequence the model never emitted. This is the engineering bill for a lever run 22 described as deleting an engineering effort. That reading survives; it trades environment construction for a shorter, sharper list of silent failures, which is a failure class this vault has hit repeatedly (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization, rubric-rl-silently-drops-signal-recoverable-by-self-distillation). None of the five carries a published magnitude. The cheap first check on our own stack: re-tokenize a captured trajectory with the trainer's template and diff it against what the proxy served. The cost side moved the wrong way, and it is the half this map exists for. Still no dollar figure, no GPU count and no wall-clock on the SWE-bench run — "modest compute" is the only quantity given — and ESOpt's 8.41 GB is a footprint, not a bill, measured at 4B with nothing showing it scales to the 27B arm the same paper runs. The authors' own stated limitation is that ES needs more trajectory samples than RL, so wherever task evaluation is expensive — every real agentic environment — the sample count is the cost and it is unreported. Meanwhile the hardware ledger underneath all of it went stale: system DRAM repriced 4.3–9.3× on DDR5 and 2.2–2.8× on DDR4 in twelve months (system-memory-repriced-four-to-nine-fold-in-twelve-months), and the damage falls hardest on the cheap configurations — expert offload, CPU-assisted decoding, KV spill — because those are the ones that buy affordability by spending host memory (the-cheapest-self-hosting-configurations-are-priced-in-the-component-that-moved-most). The gradual-entry ramp this map assumed is the part that repriced. Nobody has costed our actual target configuration at current component prices, and every figure in this map's ledger predates the move — a one-afternoon exercise that has not been done.

  • 2026-08-18 (run 22) — Stays contested. One arrival, and it moves the feasibility question onto a target this map has never had: not can we train a model, but can we train a model through software we did not write and cannot see inside. RL through an opaque third-party harness is now a working rig. ClawGym II trains Qwen3-30A3B — open weights, ~3B active, squarely in the band our cluster handles — through OpenClaw and Claude Code executed unmodified in per-rollout sandboxes. The engineering is all in the plumbing: a serving proxy at the model boundary captures the calls the harness emits, those calls are reassembled into prefix trees to recover multi-turn structure and deduplicate shared context, and both critic-based PPO and critic-free GRPO are adapted to optimize over the tree with training-inference consistency maintained. +9.98 and +14.81 Pass@1, stable over 200–400 optimization steps, carried to JobBench and OfficeQA (black-box-rl-trains-an-open-30b-through-a-harness-it-never-reads). Mix-harness training optimizes one policy jointly under heterogeneous harnesses. Why this belongs on the feasibility map rather than only the harness map. Run 21 priced the binding input and it was environment construction — ~29,000 synthesis tokens per verified environment (a-verified-rl-environment-costs-under-thirty-thousand-tokens), tens of dollars for a hundred. A shipped agent harness plus its task environment is an environment, already built, already verified by whoever ships it, and now trainable-through without reimplementation. That is the largest single reduction in this map's dependency list since it was coined — provided the rig itself is cheap, which is exactly what the paper does not say. And it is not said at all. No cost figures, no wall-clock, no GPU count, no cluster description. A sandboxed rollout system running large-scale concurrent harness executions for 200–400 RL steps is not obviously cheap, and this map's whole function is to price the thing everyone else assumes. Run 21 established that environment synthesis uplift rises with base model size (environment-synthesis-uplift-rises-with-base-model-size); ClawGym II's single 30B tells us nothing about whether the same holds when the trainable target is a 4B. And the stability claim — the paper's own headline — is stated as no divergence over 200–400 steps, which is a statement about the failure mode it fixed, not about how many steps the result needed. One thing it does close, quietly. The instrumentation debt this map has been accumulating for eleven runs is entirely about grading signals we generate. Prefix-tree reconstruction is the first tool here that recovers the structure of a trajectory produced by software we do not control — a prerequisite for any of those probes ever being pointed at a real deployment rather than a benchmark harness we wrote.

  • 2026-08-17 (run 21) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, and between them the two biggest unpriced items on this map get numbers — one because it was finally measured, the other because someone argued it away. The dependency this map called unpriceable costs about 29,000 tokens. Run 19 named environment construction as the largest unpriced input in the build order and made it the strongest structural argument for building this vault holds — workflow-specific, non-transferable, unpurchasable from a model vendor at any price (rl-environments-and-infrastructure-are-what-does-not-distil). Envs-FORGE publishes the figure incidentally, in a table about something else: four synthesis policies each export exactly 100 gold-verified terminal environments, for 2.27M–2.88M tokens — 22,723 to 28,811 per accepted environment (a-verified-rl-environment-costs-under-thirty-thousand-tokens). At any current frontier rate that is tens of dollars for the corpus, our arithmetic on an unnamed synthesizer. It joins SKILLER's $8.95 and AutoDesign's $0.27 per completed task as the third dollar figure in this family, all pointing the same way: the front end of the build order is cheap and we have been budgeting it as a research programme. What it does not cover is most of the dependency — no RL run, no Docker infrastructure, no gold-verification labour, no seed corpus — and the export size is fixed at 100 by construction, so read it as an upper bound on the cheapest link. The transferable number is not tokens but acceptance rate: 100 accepted from 203 attempts, on terminal tasks with executable verifiers. Our workflows may verify far worse, and twenty synthesized environments against one real workflow would tell us. And the RL loop itself may be skippable for one class of task. ReasonMaxxer diffs RL-trained reasoning models against their bases and finds the beneficial footprint is 1–3% of token positions, always inside the base model's top-5, concentrated at high-entropy branch points that base entropy alone identifies (rl-reasoning-gains-live-in-one-to-three-percent-of-tokens). The sparsity is causal, not descriptive — targeted patches recover most of the gain, random patches recover none. Reproducing it takes a contrastive loss at those positions, a few hundred base rollouts, no online generation: tens of problems and minutes on one GPU, roughly three orders of magnitude below full RL (rl-level-reasoning-for-three-orders-of-magnitude-less-training-compute). That is below this map's cheap endpoint, and it converges with two results already here — label-free self-distillation beating its own supervised version, and warm-up needing teacher-compatible rather than correct traces. Three mechanisms, one structural claim: on reasoning, the expensive external signal buys less than assumed. The two do not agree, and the disagreement is the useful part. ReasonMaxxer says the loop is unnecessary; Envs-FORGE gets terminal-agent gains precisely by building harder executable environments. Both can hold if RL does two jobs — selecting among branches the base already contains (cheap, replicable, math-shaped) and installing procedures it does not (expensive, needs environments, agent-shaped). Hold that as a hypothesis, not a finding, and note which pile our work is in: every ReasonMaxxer benchmark is math, and this vault's best predicate says that is the pile least like our workflows. The size story inverts, and it is the fourth thing this run argues for a mid-size arm. Envs-FORGE's uplift on tb-core rises monotonically with base size — +6.8 at 4B, +7.2 at 9B, +8.1 at 27B, +9.2 at 35B (environment-synthesis-uplift-rises-with-base-model-size) — the exact inverse of the harness headroom law, which three studies have now shown falling as base score rises. Run 19's repair predicted it without naming the case: headroom is addressable only where the intervention supplies something the model can execute. A scaffold asks the model to do less and pays out at the failures; a curriculum of harder environments asks it to do more and needs a competence floor. Training and scaffolding are not two routes to one gain — they sort opposite ways on base capability, which retires the habit of pricing them against each other and replaces it with a question about our size. Discounts: both are single preprints with no repeats or intervals; Envs-FORGE is one model family and one task family, its transfer priors and 0.5-pass-rate target are unablated constants, and its reward is the verifier its own pipeline produced; ReasonMaxxer's "matches or exceeds full RL" is an abstract claim against the authors' own RL reproductions, math-only, with no agentic or tool-use arm anywhere, and it has been public since May without a replication we have checked for.

  • 2026-08-16 (run 20) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, both instrument rather than capability, and the more useful of the two findings is a negative one. A fifth probe, and the first that improves an existing one rather than covering a new stage. Run 11's SA-OPD detects spurious supervision by deleting the prompt and comparing teacher–student log-ratios (input-ablation-detects-spurious-distillation-supervision) — which conflates this token depends on the question being present with this token depends on what the question asks. CROP separates them with an original / paraphrase / counterfactual triplet: paraphrase so the answer is unchanged, alter so the answer changes, and score positions by the margin between the two (paraphrase-calibrated-counterfactuals-beat-input-deletion-as-a-supervision-probe). The referent moves from the input's presence to its content. Cost is three extra student rescoring passes, no labels, no reward model, no extra rollouts — the same shape as everything on this map that works. The negative result is the part to act on. Under a fixed supervised-token budget across six benchmarks, CROP beats the strongest competitor by 1.92 (Qwen3-4B → 1.7B, 47.98 vs TIP 46.06) and 2.96 (Qwen3-8B → 4B, 57.48 vs Pure OPD 55.25) — but Pure OPD, supervising every token, is the strongest non-CROP arm in the second setting. Entropy, TIP, TA-OPD and CREDIT all lose to not selecting at all. If we build this pipeline the default is supervise everything, and any selector we add has to beat that baseline explicitly rather than being assumed to help. The gains themselves are a fifth to a third of SA-OPD's best single-task result and sit inside the band three independent noise measurements have bracketed for this literature. The second arrival extends the build-identity rule to compression, and it belongs here because pruning is a route to a small model we might take. SNIPER introduces CRAFT and reports that existing structured pruners deviate from their requested compression ratio by up to 33% (pruners-miss-their-own-compression-target-by-up-to-33-percent) — so every retention-versus-compression curve in that literature is plotted against an x-axis with unmeasured error a third wide. Free for us to check, unlike the endpoint variance: read the realized parameter count off the file, and report realized compression, never requested. Two limits keep it from mattering much to our plan: it is the wrong shrink axis for our premise (our build order starts from an existing 1.5B–27B base, and run 19 supplied a suitable Apache one), and its 18 tasks are perplexity, multiple choice and NLI with no agentic, tool-use or instruction-following benchmark among them — an average retention measured entirely on the categories compression treats best. Recovery fine-tuning cost is unstated, which is the one number that would make prune-and-recover comparable to start smaller and post-train. The residue is unchanged and now five probes deep: prefix loss grades inputs, input-grounding and CROP grade supervision, turn-level credit grades trajectories, KL divergence grades outputs. Every one grades a training signal. Nothing here validates a model against a task.

  • 2026-08-15 (run 19) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, and together they name the largest unpriced dependency on this map — which turns out not to be compute, data curation, or teacher access. Post-training alone moved a 27B by 3.2×, at a size we could reproduce. Run 18 took Z.ai's word that GLM-5.3 gained Terminal-Bench 3.0 4.6 → 28.3 on an unchanged 743B base (post-training-alone-moved-an-open-model-six-fold-on-a-terminal-benchmark) and noted it was the lever we have at a scale we do not. Qwen3.8-27B is the same claim two orders of magnitude smaller: at identical parameter count to Qwen3.6-27B, DeepSWE 1.1 13.3 → 42.2, QwenSWEBench 49.3 → 79.0, Terminal-Bench 2.1 63.4 → 73.0, OSWorld-Verified 63.9 → 84.3, with a release-day community config diff reporting zero architectural changes (post-training-alone-tripled-a-fixed-27b-on-a-coding-benchmark). 27B on a single node is inside this map's premise; 743B is not. Between run 18's two compute endpoints — ~4,000 B200-hours to pretrain a 1B, one A100-hour for a LoRA on a 1.5B (a-tuned-1-5b-runs-real-work-on-a-laptop-cpu-for-one-a100-hour) — this says the lever is post-training, not pretraining, which is also the economically obvious answer: a lab amortizes one base across several releases. And the thing that makes post-training work is the thing nobody describes. Lambert dismisses distillation as the explanation for GLM-5.3 in one sentence — "one does not simply 'distill' RL environments, infrastructure to run them at scale, or algorithms to mix them together effectively" (rl-environments-and-infrastructure-are-what-does-not-distil). Three independent disclosures now converge on the same missing object and none prices it: Z.ai's entire account is "more environments, more diverse tasks, and more compute spent training on them"; Qwen's card says nothing at all; Lambert names it and adds that a Chinese RL-environment data market is growing, partly supplied by American vendors. So the binding input is environment construction, it sits upstream of every step in the run-12 build order, and this vault has no measurement of what it costs. The diagnosis is that raw teacher–reference disagreement mixes genuine teacher evidence with a recurring style component — wording, formatting, reasoning cadence — so part of the update budget goes to imitating another model's prose rhythm; residualising it out recovers the gain, reaching 82.4% AIME 2024 and 63.0% LiveCodeBench v5 from an 8B SFT checkpoint. This does not move status, and the positive result should be discounted hard — one paper, one checkpoint, code unreleased, and the headline numbers arrive without baseline figures beside them, so the magnitude of the improvement is unstated. What is cheap and robust is the negative half, and it belongs in our build plan: a flat distillation run against clean data is an expected outcome of provenance mismatch, not evidence the student cannot learn the task. Note where this sits relative to the theme's existing instrument — the run 5 prefix-loss filter grades whether a trace is worth training on and is silent on whether the supervisor is compatible with it. Two distinct pipeline defects, both invisible in a loss curve, and now only one of them has a detector.

  • 2026-08-01 (run 5) — Stays contested, but the data-side constraint got its first real instrument. Training value of a long reasoning trace turns out to be readable from its first 100 tokens — loss at a randomly perturbed checkpoint separates difficult examples, and prefix loss similarity provably tracks gradient similarity — beating existing curation baselines by 1.7% at 91% greater token efficiency, with no strong external teacher in the filtering loop (reasoning-data-quality-readable-from-first-tokens). The operationally decisive number is the 91%, not the 1.7%: curation becomes a prefix scan on hardware we own rather than a full-corpus frontier-API bill, and the circularity of needing a good model to build the data that makes a good model is broken. Two supporting datapoints on the compute side: sub-2B fine-tuning runs on a single L4 overnight (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators), and a full recompile cycle — synthetic data generation, fine-tune, eval — takes 30–50 minutes on 8×H200, which makes a tuned model a CI/CD artifact rather than a research project (compiled-small-model-nears-frontier-beats-orchestrator). Still contested because the instrument is narrow: it grades long reasoning traces for reasoning-model SFT and addresses neither the syntactic nor the semantic corruption modes that produced the fragility finding in the first place. One usable component with a known cost is not a validation pipeline. The theme's live question is unchanged — can we curate and validate the corpus — but it is now a question about coverage rather than about whether any tooling exists.

  • 2026-08-01 (run 4) — Moved emerging → contested. The "yes, easily" read survives on compute and fails on data. Fine-tuning small models is acutely fragile to training-data quality: across 23 models (270M–4B), syntactic corruption caused near-total failure, semantic corruption showed threshold effects rather than gradual decay, more-capable students absorbed corruption more readily (a "capability curse"), and alignment training sometimes reduced resilience (finetuning-is-fragile-to-data-quality, which rebuts the unqualified form of finetuning-is-cheap-enough-to-be-routine). Distillation carries its own preconditions (distillation-does-not-universally-close-the-gap). The revised read: the cluster is not the constraint and never was — the data pipeline is, and its failure mode is quiet, sudden, and invisible in a loss curve. Feasibility questions should move from "can we run the job" to "can we curate and validate the corpus."

  • 2026-08-01 — Opened, and the early read is "yes, easily." Multi-node post-training is first-class in PyTorch-native tooling (pytorch-native-multinode-posttraining-is-ready, torchtune), and fine-tuning is fast/low-VRAM enough to be routine, now on AMD too (finetuning-is-cheap-enough-to-be-routine, Unsloth). emerging and deliberately scoped to feasibility — "we can" is not "we should"; the worth-it question lives in the parity and serving-economics themes.

Sub-threads: the data-curation risk thread remains the live edge and has now split three ways — detection (one cheap instrument, scoped to reasoning traces), validation (still nothing: no story for catching corruption before it silently caps a run), and, new in run 7, provenance (traces and supervisor must be compatible, or a clean corpus still yields a flat run). All three share one property that is really the theme's core finding: the corpus defects that matter are invisible in a loss curve, so feasibility depends on instrumentation we do not yet have rather than on compute we do. The compute/tooling thread is settled ("yes, easily") and a retrain-cadence sub-thread is forming: if a recompile is 30–50 minutes, the relevant feasibility unit is the deployment cycle, not the training run. Run 9 forces one revision to the thread's scope: the invisible-defect property does not stop at training. It runs through deployment precision too, so the instrumentation debt covers the whole path from corpus to served checkpoint. Two cheap probes now exist — prefix loss on inputs, KL divergence on output distribution — and both grade proxies rather than task performance, which is why validation stays the open item. Run 10 adds the one thing that closes part of the gap without an instrument: the failure is only invisible if you look at an average, and the capability that degrades most at the last stage is instruction-following and tool use, so a condition-split agentic eval on our own tasks is a validation step we can build today. The retrain-cadence sub-thread also gains a second unit — if retrofit compression really costs under 15 GPU hours, precision joins fine-tuning as something swept per deployment rather than chosen once. Run 11 finally puts something in the validation column: a detector that grades the supervision rather than the corpus or the served checkpoint, built on the same move that works everywhere else in this vault — give the check an external referent, here the input itself. Three probes now exist across the pipeline (prefix loss on inputs, input-grounding gap on supervision, KL divergence on output distribution), and the shape of what is still missing is clearer for it: nothing yet validates a model against a task, which remains the only thing that would let us decide feasibility rather than infer it. Run 11 also opens a sub-thread that cuts across this map's premise — (e) do we need to train at all? A frozen 4B gaining 23.4 points from validated prompt state is not an answer, but it is a cheap alternative that belongs on the same ledger as the training runs, and it shares this map's core defect: the failure that kills it is invisible in the mean. Run 12 partly closes (e) by showing the two are not rivals: skill text can live in the teacher and be dropped at inference, so the frozen-weights route is a cheap way to build supervision rather than a substitute for training. That yields the concrete build order this map has lacked — library first, teacher second, distil third, ship weights with an empty prompt — and each step is independently measured even though the chain is not. The instrumentation column now holds four probes (prefix loss on inputs, input-grounding gap on supervision, turn-level credit on trajectories, KL divergence on outputs), and the residue is unchanged and increasingly conspicuous: nothing validates a model against a task. One new caution belongs on the ledger — every probe added so far grades a training signal, and EnvACE's scaling row is a reminder that base size still outweighs technique on realistic tool use, so better instrumentation does not by itself make a smaller model sufficient. Run 15 moves the build order's cost profile without changing its shape, and opens a sub-thread the map has been running blind on. (f) the out-of-domain column. Every training result this map holds is reported in-domain, and the one paper that looked found the default choice — full-parameter warm-up — buying in-domain convergence at persistent OOD damage while LoRA matched it and kept the generality. Since our premise is workflow-specific specialists deployed next to adjacent workflows, OOD retention is not a robustness nicety but the property that decides whether a specialist is deployable at all, and we have never measured it. It joins validation as a column with almost nothing in it — and unlike validation it needs no new instrument, only an eval battery fixed before training. The data-curation thread also relaxes: run 13 removed the teacher and this run removes the requirement that logged traces be correct, so the front of the pipeline is now cheap enough that the binding constraint is unambiguously instrumentation rather than either compute or procurement.
