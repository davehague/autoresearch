Is training our own on the cluster feasible and cheap?

Setting aside whether it's worth it, can we actually train specialised small models on our HPC cluster without it being a research project — and how cheap is it?

Through-line

  • 2026-08-03 (run 8) — Stays contested; the theme's core finding — the defects that cap a run are invisible in the summary number — extends from the data pipeline into two more stages, the distillation objective and the RL objective. First, distilling a 9B teacher into a 1.7B student cut context-override error 44%→24% on unambiguous items while silently flipping 15% of correct abstentions into stereotype answers on ambiguous ones; the two harms correlate at only ρ=0.19 and a single averaged accuracy hides both, detectable only by splitting the evaluation by condition (distillation-harms-are-invisible-in-aggregate-metrics). Second, rubric-based RL loses the learning signal for criteria some rollouts already satisfy — over 57% of samples affected, ~1.8 per sample — because scalar reward aggregation washes it out, recoverable by on-policy self-distillation (no frontier teacher) in ~2× fewer steps (rubric-rl-silently-drops-signal-recoverable-by-self-distillation). Neither moves status, and both are single-source/thin (one BBQ study with one author; a method paper that never reports model sizes, so transfer to our scales is unknown). What they add is generality to the sub-thread: the validation gap is not specific to data curation — it recurs wherever a summary metric aggregates over conditions or criteria, which is every stage of the pipeline. The self-distillation half also adds a second point to the "you don't always need a frontier teacher" column.
  • 2026-08-02 (run 7) — Stays contested, and the data-pipeline constraint gains a failure mode the theme had not catalogued — one that fires on a clean corpus. On-policy distillation carries an unstated precondition, teacher consistency: the model supplying token-level supervision should also have generated the demonstrations behind the SFT reference. Assembling a corpus the practical way violates it constantly (mixed or unknown provenance; different models preferred for generation and supervision), and in those cross-teacher settings even a stronger teacher can yield little improvement over the SFT reference (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization). The diagnosis is that raw teacher–reference disagreement mixes genuine teacher evidence with a recurring style component — wording, formatting, reasoning cadence — so part of the update budget goes to imitating another model's prose rhythm; residualising it out recovers the gain, reaching 82.4% AIME 2024 and 63.0% LiveCodeBench v5 from an 8B SFT checkpoint. This does not move status, and the positive result should be discounted hard — one paper, one checkpoint, code unreleased, and the headline numbers arrive without baseline figures beside them, so the magnitude of the improvement is unstated. What is cheap and robust is the negative half, and it belongs in our build plan: a flat distillation run against clean data is an expected outcome of provenance mismatch, not evidence the student cannot learn the task. Note where this sits relative to the theme's existing instrument — the run 5 prefix-loss filter grades whether a trace is worth training on and is silent on whether the supervisor is compatible with it. Two distinct pipeline defects, both invisible in a loss curve, and now only one of them has a detector.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 5) — Stays contested, but the data-side constraint got its first real instrument. Training value of a long reasoning trace turns out to be readable from its first 100 tokens — loss at a randomly perturbed checkpoint separates difficult examples, and prefix loss similarity provably tracks gradient similarity — beating existing curation baselines by 1.7% at 91% greater token efficiency, with no strong external teacher in the filtering loop (reasoning-data-quality-readable-from-first-tokens). The operationally decisive number is the 91%, not the 1.7%: curation becomes a prefix scan on hardware we own rather than a full-corpus frontier-API bill, and the circularity of needing a good model to build the data that makes a good model is broken. Two supporting datapoints on the compute side: sub-2B fine-tuning runs on a single L4 overnight (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators), and a full recompile cycle — synthetic data generation, fine-tune, eval — takes 30–50 minutes on 8×H200, which makes a tuned model a CI/CD artifact rather than a research project (compiled-small-model-nears-frontier-beats-orchestrator). Still contested because the instrument is narrow: it grades long reasoning traces for reasoning-model SFT and addresses neither the syntactic nor the semantic corruption modes that produced the fragility finding in the first place. One usable component with a known cost is not a validation pipeline. The theme's live question is unchanged — can we curate and validate the corpus — but it is now a question about coverage rather than about whether any tooling exists.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 4) — Moved emerging → contested. The "yes, easily" read survives on compute and fails on data. Fine-tuning small models is acutely fragile to training-data quality: across 23 models (270M–4B), syntactic corruption caused near-total failure, semantic corruption showed threshold effects rather than gradual decay, more-capable students absorbed corruption more readily (a "capability curse"), and alignment training sometimes reduced resilience (finetuning-is-fragile-to-data-quality, which rebuts the unqualified form of finetuning-is-cheap-enough-to-be-routine). Distillation carries its own preconditions (distillation-does-not-universally-close-the-gap). The revised read: the cluster is not the constraint and never was — the data pipeline is, and its failure mode is quiet, sudden, and invisible in a loss curve. Feasibility questions should move from "can we run the job" to "can we curate and validate the corpus."
  • 2026-08-01 — Opened, and the early read is "yes, easily." Multi-node post-training is first-class in PyTorch-native tooling (pytorch-native-multinode-posttraining-is-ready, torchtune), and fine-tuning is fast/low-VRAM enough to be routine, now on AMD too (finetuning-is-cheap-enough-to-be-routine, Unsloth). emerging and deliberately scoped to feasibility — "we can" is not "we should"; the worth-it question lives in the parity and serving-economics themes.

Sub-threads: the data-curation risk thread remains the live edge and has now split three ways — detection (one cheap instrument, scoped to reasoning traces), validation (still nothing: no story for catching corruption before it silently caps a run), and, new in run 7, provenance (traces and supervisor must be compatible, or a clean corpus still yields a flat run). All three share one property that is really the theme's core finding: the corpus defects that matter are invisible in a loss curve, so feasibility depends on instrumentation we do not yet have rather than on compute we do. The compute/tooling thread is settled ("yes, easily") and a retrain-cadence sub-thread is forming: if a recompile is 30–50 minutes, the relevant feasibility unit is the deployment cycle, not the training run.
