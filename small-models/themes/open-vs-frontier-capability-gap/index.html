Open vs frontier — is the capability gap closing?

Whether open-weight models are close enough to frontier proprietary models that the remaining gap is about economics and robustness rather than raw capability.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-20 (run 24) — Stays contested. One arrival, on the checkpoint this vault has been accumulating reasons to choose, and it is the cleanest natural experiment this map has had.   • 2026-08-17 (run 21) — Stays contested. Two arrivals: the first splits this map's central number into two numbers that behave differently, and the second is the first independent hands-on with the checkpoint run 19 named as our candidate. Most of the gap is repeatability, not ceiling. Every reading on this map is a single figure — 0.8 points on an agentic aggregate, 29 on abstract reasoning, 18.6 between two open 30Bs, 11.5 with the pairing handicapped in its own favour. Seven frontier and frontier-class models on 36 long-horizon AI R&D tasks, three runs each, decompose one: best is Claude-Opus-4.7 at 0.739 avg@3 / 0.790 best@3, worst is DeepSeek-V4-Pro at 0.502 / 0.668, and the field spread is 0.237 on avg@3 against 0.122 on best@3 (the-open-frontier-gap-is-mostly-a-consistency-gap). Average separates these models roughly twice as sharply as peak does. The weaker models are not much worse at their best; they are worse at getting there again. That is a different problem with different remedies — a ceiling deficit needs a better model, a consistency deficit yields to sampling, verification, retry and escalation, which are the cheapest and best-supported levers this vault owns. The optimism has one live counterweight and it arrived last run. Run-k-and-pick only pays if the k runs differ, and agents in identical situations make near-identical decisions (identical-agents-make-identical-choices-and-that-is-the-failure-mode). Whether a smaller or more heavily tuned model has a narrower behavioural distribution is unmeasured, and it is the pivot: a consistency gap is cheap to close if failures are independent and expensive if they are correlated. The two together define one free experiment — measure avg@k against best@k on our own tasks, and count distinct solution strategies across the k runs. It joins the exercises already queued and needs no training run. The candidate checkpoint survives contact with a practitioner. Qwen 3.8 27B was vendor-reported at unstated effort, harness and precision, and discounted accordingly (an-apache-27b-posts-frontier-band-agentic-coding-numbers). Run independently from a 17GB Q4_K_M build on a laptop and a DGX Spark, it draws accurate bounding boxes on real photographs, builds working HTML tools and drives coding-agent tool-calling loops (a-27b-runs-locally-at-a-fifth-of-hosted-throughput). Agentic tool use and instruction following are exactly what compression takes first, and they survived a four-bit build — the strongest evidence this map has that our named candidate is real rather than a benchmark artifact, and a concrete instance of run 19's observation that 89× the parameters bought six points. What does not survive is any implicit serving assumption: 15–30 tok/s locally against 74–184 hosted, with MTP recovering ~72% of the shortfall. This vault's economics have never carried a latency term, and for an interactive agent loop that is a product decision rather than an accounting one. Discounts: n=3 per cell is thin for a claim about variance, no intervals are published, the open models in that study are all frontier-scale rather than small, and Opus-4.7 is a stale comparator by this map's own release-lag standard (the-open-frontier-gap-is-read-off-a-release-lag-not-a-capability-lag) — so the gap it measures flatters the open side by an unmeasurable amount. The hands-on is a blog post with informal probes, no benchmark, no repeats, one quantized build tested against full-precision hosted endpoints, and build identity applies as ever: it says a 17GB build of this checkpoint works, not which 17GB build (the-quant-label-is-not-a-spec).

  • 2026-08-10 (run 15) — Stays contested. Nothing this run measures the gap; two things attack the instruments the gap is read from, and one of them attacks the model's own identity. The predicate this map leads with was two predicates. Run 11's cut — long-running work, where a harness supplies state and structure, versus long-context work, where the material must sit inside the model — gets a third term from a 1,547-paper census that disambiguates what the literature conflates: long-horizon is a task property (sequential decisions required), long-context is a model property (tokens attendable in one pass), long-term memory is a system property (what persists across steps or sessions), and the three are logically independent (long-horizon-long-context-and-long-term-memory-are-three-independent-properties). Our "long-running" bundled task horizon with system memory, and those are separately buildable — horizon by decomposition and external task state (external-task-state-lifts-long-horizon-agents), persistence by a memory subsystem where deterministic machinery already beat model-authored memory at zero LLM tokens (deterministic-memory-beats-llm-authored-memory-at-zero-token-cost). The consequence lands on the cheapest open item this map holds: sorting our own workflows was to be a two-pile sort and should be a three-column sort — steps required, tokens that must be resident, state that must survive a session. Only the middle column is a model property we would have to buy or train for. If our workflows sort mostly into columns one and three, the gap question may not need a training run. The most-cited coding benchmark has more error in it than the gap it measures. The same survey's SWE-bench case study reports 32.67% of "successful" patches involving solution leakage, 31.08% passing only on weak tests, and top systems dropping 66% of their solve rate after contamination filtering (a-third-of-swe-bench-successes-were-leakage). A 66% drop is larger than any open-vs-frontier separation this map holds — larger than the 29 points on ARC-AGI-2, an order of magnitude larger than the 0.8 on an agentic aggregate. It does not say which way decontamination moves the gap; nobody has measured that, and leakage could favour either side. It converges with the noise-floor tally from the other direction (12–14% of web-agent outcomes flipping run-to-run, ±0.035–0.066 on harness contrasts, a 9.4% trial error rate) on one conclusion: most published comparisons in this map's own literature sit inside their own error. Relayed by a survey rather than produced by it, so read the primary work before quoting 66% as ours. And a sixth confound, this one attacking what "the open model" even refers to. Artificial Analysis's new Endpoint Accuracy Index scores serverless providers against a self-hosted deployment of the official weights, and GLM-5.2 (max) reads 100% to 73% depending on the endpoint — with nominal precision failing to predict it (Nebius FP4 100%, DeepInfra FP4 73%) and two of its three components being tool calling and long-context recall (api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points). Since run 9 this map has refused a model verdict without harness, precision and grader named; run 13 added effort. The endpoint is the fifth, and it is the first that changes the weights being served rather than how they are run or scored. Practical form: any comparison we run against an API is a joint measurement of model and endpoint, so pin the endpoint and the date, or re-run the baseline. Retroactively it supplies a further uncontrolled variable for open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness, where practitioners running nominally identical open weights disagreed on basic rule-following — a dispute this map has now handed three candidate explanations (harness, build, endpoint) and settled with none.

One person produced that against a public API in one run. The cost of being rigorous about a comparison is evidently low, which removes the last excuse for not running ours. Discount for the reproducer disclosing they authored the harness being validated, and for a single benchmark, model, provider and effort setting; and note this tells us nothing about open-ended work, where the gap keeps reappearing.

When judge choice perturbs scores by several times the separation, the separation is unmeasured (grader-model-is-a-fourth-variable-that-has-to-be-pinned). The substitution itself is a defensible improvement — grading models with GPT-4o that are generations ahead of it was a real weakness, and that objection argues for the change. What it establishes is a fourth confound to pin, alongside harness (open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness) and precision (quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly): the grader. Unlike the other two it leaves no trace in the artifact — a leaderboard snapshot carries no record of which judge produced it, so two screenshots a day apart are not comparable and nothing on the page says so. Direct instruction for our own work: if we grade our workflows with an LLM judge, the judge is a version-pinned dependency, a judge upgrade invalidates every prior comparison, and any before/after has to be re-scored under one judge rather than compared across two. The compilation study founding combined-small-model-plus-custom-harness was LLM-judged, so this is retroactive as well as prospective. The closeness is real and it should be read against, not instead of, this map's task-level evidence. The same model trails frontier by 12.3 points on SWE-bench Pro and 15.3 on FrontierSWE while sitting at the frontier band on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe). Both measurements are correct about different things: an index built from nine evaluations averages the task classes where the gap lives into the ones where it doesn't. That makes the run-11 predicate — long-running versus long-context work — precisely the split an aggregate cannot express, and it means near-parity on the Agentic Index is not evidence for or against that predicate. Provenance is thin in a specific way: this is a forum reconstruction with screenshots, not a published changelog with a before/after table across all 24 models, so whether the grader swap moved every model uniformly or these two differentially is unknown — and that distinction decides whether anything about the ordering changed at all.

That predicts new cases rather than relabelling settled ones — and it is consistent with the verifiable/non-verifiable candidate rather than replacing it, since what a harness cannot supply is exactly what no reward model can cheaply verify. Where a harness can carry state, structure and verification, open weights close or win; where the material has to sit inside the model, they trail, and the best harness anyone has built does not fix it. Practically that retires the cheapest kind of update this map receives (a leaderboard moved) and leaves only the expensive kind (a task-split measurement with all four pinned), which is the right trade but means this map should expect to move less often. Run 15 splits the leading predicate and grows the entanglement list to five. Long-running was two things — task horizon and system memory — and separating them turns the queued workflow-sorting exercise into a three-column sort where only one column (resident context) is a model property we would have to buy or train for; the other two have working answers on this vault's ledger already, which is the cheapest route to shrinking this map's question rather than answering it. The fifth entanglement is the endpoint, and it is different in kind from the other four: harness, precision, grader and effort change how weights are run or scored, while a serverless endpoint changes which weights you are served — 100% to 73% of reference on one open checkpoint, undisclosed and unhashable. So the standing rule extends from "name the harness, precision, grader and effort" to "and name the endpoint and the date, or the model is not identified." Alongside that, the run's contamination finding gives this map its bluntest self-assessment: on the benchmark family most often used to argue about coding, filtering leakage costs top systems 66% of their score — several times any separation this map reports — which means the honest position on published gaps is not that they are wrong but that they are unresolved, and only our own uncontaminated task-split measurement will change that.
