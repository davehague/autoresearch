Open vs frontier — is the capability gap closing?

Whether open-weight models are close enough to frontier proprietary models that the remaining gap is about economics and robustness rather than raw capability.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-07 (run 12) — Stays contested. The closest leaderboard reading this map has ever held arrived, and the same event showed why the reading has no sign. On Artificial Analysis's Agentic Index, the open-weight Qwen3.8-Max scores 58.4 against Claude Opus 5's 59.2 — under a point apart on an independent aggregate specifically about agentic work (open-weight-model-within-a-point-of-frontier-on-an-agentic-aggregate). Under the previous grading regime the same two models read 55.4 and 55.3, with the open model marginally ahead. Artificial Analysis had replaced GPT-4o, Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507 and Gemini 3 Flash Preview as grader models on several constituent benchmarks with GPT-5.6 Luna, citing stronger agreement with human judgment; both scores moved 3–4 points and the ordering between them reversed. Take the closeness and discard the ordering, for a reason that is arithmetic rather than political. The instrument moved the readings by 3–4 points; the gap being read was 0.1, then 0.8. When judge choice perturbs scores by several times the separation, the separation is unmeasured (grader-model-is-a-fourth-variable-that-has-to-be-pinned). The substitution itself is a defensible improvement — grading models with GPT-4o that are generations ahead of it was a real weakness, and that objection argues for the change. What it establishes is a fourth confound to pin, alongside harness (open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness) and precision (quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly): the grader. Unlike the other two it leaves no trace in the artifact — a leaderboard snapshot carries no record of which judge produced it, so two screenshots a day apart are not comparable and nothing on the page says so. Direct instruction for our own work: if we grade our workflows with an LLM judge, the judge is a version-pinned dependency, a judge upgrade invalidates every prior comparison, and any before/after has to be re-scored under one judge rather than compared across two. The compilation study founding combined-small-model-plus-custom-harness was LLM-judged, so this is retroactive as well as prospective. The closeness is real and it should be read against, not instead of, this map's task-level evidence. The same model trails frontier by 12.3 points on SWE-bench Pro and 15.3 on FrontierSWE while sitting at the frontier band on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe). Both measurements are correct about different things: an index built from nine evaluations averages the task classes where the gap lives into the ones where it doesn't. That makes the run-11 predicate — long-running versus long-context work — precisely the split an aggregate cannot express, and it means near-parity on the Agentic Index is not evidence for or against that predicate. Provenance is thin in a specific way: this is a forum reconstruction with screenshots, not a published changelog with a before/after table across all 24 models, so whether the grader swap moved every model uniformly or these two differentially is unknown — and that distinction decides whether anything about the ordering changed at all.
  • 2026-08-06 (run 11) — Stays contested, and the run-10 hit on the residual-gap hypothesis is partly walked back by two better-controlled measurements pointing opposite ways on the same night. Against the gap: OneDayAgent pins one harness across five backends from three families with no per-backend tuning — the portability control this map has never had — and the open GLM-5.2 tops it at 0.821 on 104 open-ended day-long tasks, above every shipped commercial agent measured (AutoClaw 0.799, Codex on GPT-5.5 0.664, Manus 0.645, Genspark 0.635, ChatGPT-Agent 0.626), with a 9B open backend at 0.613 landing within 0.05 of Codex (one-harness-generalizes-across-five-backends-without-tuning). For the gap: Prime Agent — the strongest designed harness published — drove GLM-5.2 against Opus 5 in a stock, unmodified Claude Code across nine long-context and long-horizon benchmarks and won 3 of 9, losing OOLONG 0.700 vs 0.900 and OBLIQ 0.669 vs 0.795 (open-weights-in-the-best-harness-still-trail-frontier-in-a-stock-sdk). And GDPevo, contamination-resistant and externally graded like Databricks, finds GLM-5.2 6.98 points below Opus-4.8 on enterprise workflows at 39% of the cost per task (open-model-trades-seven-points-for-61-percent-of-the-bill). So run 10's reframed question — which reported gaps survive removing benchmark contamination — gets its first answer, and the answer is some do. Databricks and GDPevo both decontaminate, both grade externally, and they disagree: tie on pull requests, seven points on business workflows. The residual-gap candidate is alive and needs a sharper predicate than "open-ended," because it is now failing to sort the cases. The Prime Agent result offers one, and it is the most useful thing this map got tonight. The open pairing's three wins were ManyIH instruction-following, LongCot-Mini, and building an emulator from scratch; its six losses cluster on long-context comprehension. Long-running work, where a harness supplies state and structure, versus long-context work, where the material has to sit inside the model. That predicts new cases rather than relabelling settled ones — and it is consistent with the verifiable/non-verifiable candidate rather than replacing it, since what a harness cannot supply is exactly what no reward model can cheaply verify. It also fits this map's own entanglement rule: when the harness carries the load, open closes; when the model carries it, open trails. Every arm here has precision unstated, so the three-way confound stands, and GLM-5.2 at 744B is a reminder that none of this is a size measurement.
  • 2026-08-05 (run 10) — Stays contested, but the theme's most testable residual-gap hypothesis takes its first direct hit, and it takes it on the task class where the hypothesis said the gap would be widest. Run 8 instanced the "closed on verifiable, trails on open-ended" shape with Qwen3.8-Max: frontier-band on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 12–15 points down on SWE-bench Pro and FrontierSWE. Databricks ran the same comparison on work that is neither bounded nor public — tasks harvested from its own merged pull requests on a multi-million-line codebase written by 3,000+ engineers, spanning Scala services, Rust systems code, a React/TypeScript frontend, protobuf/gRPC and Bazel, filtered to human-authored test-covered changes, graded by held-out tests rather than an LLM judge, with git history sealed after agents were caught recovering the true implementation from it. GLM 5.2, open weights, was statistically tied with Claude Opus 4.8 on quality, at $1.28 per task against $1.94 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-private-uncontaminated-codebase). The authors' summary is "open models are here for coding." Three things make this the strongest gap measurement the map holds, and one thing keeps it from moving status. It is uncontaminated — private tasks, which is the objection the authors themselves raise against SWE-bench and which no other source here escapes. It is objectively graded. And it is priced per task, the unit that appears on a bill, rather than per token. Against that: the post publishes no pass-rate table, no task count and no interval behind "statistically tied," so the tie's strength is unverifiable; and Databricks commercially serves open models, so the incentive points at this conclusion — though it points away from the frontier vendors who supply most of this map's contrary evidence, which is not nothing. Also unchanged: this is one harness-controlled study and says nothing about open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness, which is a claim about variance across scaffolds, not about a mean. Read alongside run 9's three-way confound warning, the design here is notable for pinning two of the three legs — one harness family, one task set — while leaving precision unstated, which is now the standing gap in every comparison this map collects.
  • 2026-08-04 (run 9) — Stays contested; no new gap measurement, but the theme's own methodological rule gets an instrument, and a note on how much of any gap here is now unmeasured. Since run 7 this map has held that no model verdict is interpretable without naming its harness, because every live-use verdict turned out to be a joint measurement. Scale AI's taxonomy operationalises that: 41 failure modes, each assigned to an edge between model, harness, environment and evaluation rather than to a blamed component, κ=0.76 against human labels (agent-failures-belong-to-edges-not-components). Edge attribution is the honest output for this map's founding dispute — identical DeepSeek V4 Flash weights at full precision, opposite verdicts, the split tracking the scaffold. It settles nothing (no census of which edge failures land on, and κ measures rater agreement rather than correctness, so a taxonomy can be reliably wrong), but if we ever run our own comparison the failure-coding scheme has to be fixed before the experiment, not after. One thing to carry into every future comparison on this map: run 9's serving evidence establishes that quantization silently costs long-tail factual recall while leaving reasoning benchmarks flat (quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly). Practically nobody runs a frontier-class open checkpoint at full precision, and the local-use verdicts this theme collects are overwhelmingly from quantized weights — so a third confound now sits alongside model and harness in every open-vs-frontier anecdote here: which quantization, and on which capability. The theme's entanglement problem is not two-way, it is three-way.
  • 2026-08-03 (run 8) — Stays contested, and the theme's most testable residual-gap hypothesis gets a concrete coding-task instance. Alibaba's open-weight Qwen3.8-Max (2.4T MoE, ~95B active; open weights shipping, plus an open 27B running in ~17 GB VRAM) reaches the frontier band on a bounded terminal benchmark — Terminal-Bench 2.1 86.6, ahead of Opus 4.8 and Fable 5 at 84.6, just behind GPT-5.6 Sol at 88.8 — while trailing 12–15 points on the harder, open-ended SWE benchmarks: SWE-bench Pro 67.7 vs Fable 5's 80.0, FrontierSWE 73.5 vs 88.8 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe). That is exactly the shape the sub-thread predicted — parity where the task is verifiable/bounded, a persistent gap where it resembles the multi-step engineering our workflows are. It reinforces the contest rather than resolving it: the trajectory toward parity is real on one axis and visibly incomplete on the other. Two discounts, both familiar to this theme. The numbers are vendor-reported with no independent replication and the weights aren't out yet, so nothing is verified; and every score is a model-plus-harness measurement in Qwen's own scaffold, so per this theme's entanglement rule none of these gaps is a pure model gap. The open 27B is the object most relevant to us, and its scores weren't published — the thing to chase next.
  • 2026-08-02 (run 7) — Moved emerging → contested. Run 6 said a fourth converging datapoint with real measurement would justify consolidating; what arrived instead is the first evidence running the other way, and it lands exactly on the counter-claim this theme had flagged as untouched — robustness in messy live use. Practitioners running DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 locally at full precision reached opposite verdicts on whether it can follow rules at all, in a 198-comment dispute that never resolved (open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness). That is the axis a blended intelligence index cannot see, and it is contested in the same week the index put this model mid-pack at 50 (open-weight-model-mid-pack-on-intelligence-index) — so it qualifies the forecast in open-frontier-gap-closing-fast-risk-is-regulatory rather than refuting it. Two things sharpen the contest rather than muddying it. (a) A specific, testable shape for the residual gap surfaced in that thread: open weights have largely closed on verifiable tasks and still trail on non-verifiable ones, because non-verifiable work needs reward models the open labs cannot afford to build. (b) The dispute itself sorted by harness, not by model — same weights, same precision, opposite verdicts, the difference being OpenCode vs Pi vs a bare chat window, plus an unowned chat template. So "is open robust in live use" may not be answerable at the model level at all. Arriving alongside, and cutting the other way: on production-fidelity oncall root-cause analysis, the best of five frontier agents managed 25.3% on realistic inputs and 10.0% on hard ones, with the weakest hallucinating a root cause in 40% of reports (frontier-agents-top-out-at-25-percent-on-realistic-oncall-rca). No open-weight arm, so it does not measure the gap — but it lowers what the gap is a gap to, and it is the strongest-method reminder yet that "frontier plus a default SDK" is a weak incumbent on messy multi-source work. Third datapoint, smaller: a 2.8T open-weight model now serves from a single node of rentable commodity hardware at 952 tok/s (accelerator-memory-capacity-swings-self-hosting-economics) — practical reach, independent of leaderboard position. Net: the capability trajectory still points toward parity; what is now genuinely contested is whether measured parity survives contact with a real workload, and by how much of that is the harness.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 6) — Stays emerging; a second practitioner voice sharpens the direction without adding measurement. Lambert forecasts open weights reaching the current frontier tier (GPT-5.5 / Opus 4.8 / GLM-5.2) in ~6 months, with DeepSeek leading the open field (open-frontier-gap-closing-fast-risk-is-regulatory) — consistent with the theme's economic-not-capability read, and echoed the same night by the DeepSeek V4 Flash datapoint already here (open-weight-model-mid-pack-on-intelligence-index). What's new is a counterweight the theme wasn't tracking: the binding threat may be regulatory, a policy ceiling that freezes the open frontier below the closed one for reasons unrelated to capability. Held at emerging deliberately — this is a forecast plus one index number, and the robustness-in-live-use counter is untouched. But note the accumulation now runs three-for-three in the "gap closing" direction, and a fourth converging datapoint with real measurement would justify consolidating.
  • 2026-08-01 — Opened. Two early signals point the same way: a practitioner's read that the race is "economic staying power and fast-following, not a widening capability gap" (open-frontier-gap-is-economic-not-capability), and an open-weight model landing on the same blended intelligence axis as frontier (open-weight-model-mid-pack-on-intelligence-index). Both thin — one opinion, one datapoint — hence emerging. The live counter-claim to watch: closed models stay "more robust in messy live use."

Sub-threads: what actually bounds open is now the theme's centre of gravity, and it has three live candidates rather than two: robustness in messy live use (contested as of run 7, and possibly a harness property rather than a model one), a regulatory cap above a capability line, and the newest and most testable — non-verifiable tasks, where the constraint is reward-model quality and therefore compute, not weights. That third candidate is the one worth splitting off first, because it predicts where to look for the gap instead of merely asserting one. A second thread is now unavoidable: this theme's measurements and harness-engineering-vs-default-sdk's are entangled — every live-use verdict here turned out to be a joint measurement of model and scaffold, which means "is the gap closing" cannot be answered without fixing a harness first. Run 9 makes that thread three-way rather than two-way: precision joins model and harness as an uncontrolled variable in every local-use verdict this map collects, since a quantized checkpoint loses long-tail recall while its reasoning scores stay flat. Any future comparison here has to pin all three. Run 10 puts the non-verifiable-tasks candidate under real strain: on uncontaminated, objectively-graded, multi-language production engineering work — the most open-ended task this map has ever measured — an open model tied the best frontier model on quality and beat it on cost per task. The candidate is not dead (one study, no table, self-published), but the next thing to look for is no longer "where is the gap on hard tasks" so much as which reported gaps survive removing benchmark contamination. That reframing is cheap to test and would explain the run 8 / run 10 disagreement without either being wrong. Run 11 tests it and gets a mixed answer — two decontaminated, externally-graded studies disagree (tie on pull requests, seven points on enterprise workflows) — so the reframing survives as a method but stops being an explanation. What replaces it is a sharper predicate for what actually bounds open, and it should now lead the thread: long-running vs long-context. Where a harness can carry state, structure and verification, open weights close or win; where the material has to sit inside the model, they trail, and the best harness anyone has built does not fix it. That is testable by holding the harness fixed and varying only how much material the task forces into context — cheap, and directly answerable on our own workflows. It also subsumes the entanglement thread rather than competing with it: the gap appears precisely where the scaffold has nothing to contribute. Run 12 leaves that predicate untouched and disqualifies the instrument most often used to argue against it. Leaderboard aggregates cannot express a long-running/long-context split by construction, and this one moved 3–4 points on a grader change while the open-vs-frontier separation it reported was under a point — so no aggregate reading, in either direction, should be treated as evidence on this map again without its grading version stated. The entanglement list grows to four: model, harness, precision, grader. Practically that retires the cheapest kind of update this map receives (a leaderboard moved) and leaves only the expensive kind (a task-split measurement with all four pinned), which is the right trade but means this map should expect to move less often.
