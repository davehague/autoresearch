Open vs frontier — is the capability gap closing?

Whether open-weight models are close enough to frontier proprietary models that the remaining gap is about economics and robustness rather than raw capability.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-14 (run 18) — Stays contested, and the gap is not measured this run either — the qualifier list grows instead, which is becoming this map's characteristic mode. Open-weights status is a date, not a property. Z.ai released GLM-5.3 with weights withheld, promised roughly two weeks later pending safety hardening, and available meanwhile only through its own API (open-weights-status-is-a-date-not-a-property). Every published number about it — the coding claim over Claude Opus 4.8, the CyberGym lead over Mythos 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol — was produced on the vendor's hosted deployment. For that window it is epistemically a closed frontier model, and that window is exactly when release-day numbers propagate. This is the third qualifier on the word open in four runs: run 16 established that "open" names a class, not an artifact (the-open-field-is-stratified-enough-that-open-is-not-a-capability-statement, 18.6 points between two open 30Bs at identical active parameters), run 15 that the endpoint changes which weights you are served (api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points, a 27-point spread), and now that the date decides whether there is an artifact at all. Pin-list instruction: name the checkpoint and the date it became public, not the model family. The competitive numbers themselves are unusable and say so on their face. Z.ai's flagship coding comparison — GLM-5.3 31.4% at ~50k tokens against Opus 4.8 29.5% at 120k, with Claude Fable 5 at 39.5% at maximum effort — runs on an internal benchmark, states an effort setting for one row and not the others, and claims a 1.9-point lead over Opus. Run 13 measured 15.4 points from the effort dial alone on one open checkpoint with everything else fixed (reasoning-effort-moves-one-model-15-points). The lead sits inside a band roughly eight times its width, on a benchmark nobody can re-run, with no way to apply run 12's grader check (grader-model-is-a-fourth-variable-that-has-to-be-pinned). The token-efficiency half is the more interesting claim — higher score on 60% fewer tokens is the shape that breaks the quality/cost frontier (designed-harness-lifts-score-and-cuts-tokens-together) — but token counts are not costs, and run 14's four-way split means a 2.4× token reduction can be a smaller or larger bill depending entirely on where it falls (cache-reads-dominate-an-agentic-token-bill). The row to keep is the one the vendor loses in its own table. On ExploitGym in a 6-hour window, GLM-5.3 completes 130 tasks against Mythos 5's 247 — nearly 2× behind — on the longest-running, most open-ended task in the set, while leading the aggregate cyber benchmark printed beside it (post-training-alone-moved-an-open-model-six-fold-on-a-terminal-benchmark). Same boundary this map keeps drawing: at or near frontier on scored procedures, far from it on producing a solution to something novel (open-model-trails-frontier-29-points-on-abstract-reasoning). Note that run 11's sharper predicate — long-running work where a harness supplies state versus long-context work where material must sit in the model — does not cleanly explain this one, since ExploitGym is long-running by construction. Worth watching rather than resolving; it is one vendor row.

  • 2026-08-12 (run 16) — Stays contested. The gap is not measured this run either; what moves is the question's grammar, from one comparison to two. "Open" is not a capability statement, and neither is a size class. NVIDIA's model card for its new open-weights Nemotron 3.5 Lightning (30B total / 3B active, Mamba-2 + MoE + attention hybrid, >20T tokens, SFT + GRPO) publishes a comparison in which its own model loses every benchmark shown to two peer open models: MMLU-Pro 81.94 against 85.63 (Qwen 3.6 35B A3B) and 85.20 (Gemma 4 26B A4B); GPQA Diamond 75.44 against 83.40 and 79.61; SWE-bench Verified 51.56 against 70.12 and 57.40 (the-open-field-is-stratified-enough-that-open-is-not-a-capability-statement). 18.6 points on a coding benchmark, at identical 3B active parameters. The spread among open 30B-class models here is larger than several open-vs-frontier gaps this map holds. That is the same lesson as the-quant-label-is-not-a-spec one level up the stack — the label names a class, not an artifact — and it compounds with run 15's endpoint finding (api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points): pick the wrong open model and lose 18.6 points, serve it on the wrong endpoint and lose a quarter of what remains. The practical consequence for our own experiment is that "we tested an open model" is not a described condition, and that every open-vs-frontier number on this map inherits a model-selection variance nobody has bounded. Two smaller notes. The release sells speed and routing (4× output speed, 30% faster agentic completion, both against unnamed comparators) rather than capability, and a vendor shipping a small open model and a cross-model router in one announcement is telling you the small model does not carry the workload alone (cross-model-escalation-routes-7-percent-of-calls-and-cuts-74-percent-of-cost). That is an argument against reading the architecture as a small-model win, not against the architecture. And the card states no reasoning-effort setting, so it inherits reasoning-effort-moves-one-model-15-points — a band roughly as wide as one of the gaps it reports. From a different direction, the map's procedure-versus-novel-artifact predicate got its first prospective confirmation this run, on the parity map rather than this one: eight open SLMs tuned on emergency-department tasks beat commercial baselines on triage and referral — both assignment into defined rubrics — and failed on open-ended diagnosis (lora-tuned-open-slms-beat-commercial-baselines-on-two-clinical-tasks-of-three). First time the boundary predicted rather than described, and first time outside coding, extraction and agentic tool use.

  • 2026-08-10 (run 15) — Stays contested. Nothing this run measures the gap; two things attack the instruments the gap is read from, and one of them attacks the model's own identity. The predicate this map leads with was two predicates. Run 11's cut — long-running work, where a harness supplies state and structure, versus long-context work, where the material must sit inside the model — gets a third term from a 1,547-paper census that disambiguates what the literature conflates: long-horizon is a task property (sequential decisions required), long-context is a model property (tokens attendable in one pass), long-term memory is a system property (what persists across steps or sessions), and the three are logically independent (long-horizon-long-context-and-long-term-memory-are-three-independent-properties). Our "long-running" bundled task horizon with system memory, and those are separately buildable — horizon by decomposition and external task state (external-task-state-lifts-long-horizon-agents), persistence by a memory subsystem where deterministic machinery already beat model-authored memory at zero LLM tokens (deterministic-memory-beats-llm-authored-memory-at-zero-token-cost). The consequence lands on the cheapest open item this map holds: sorting our own workflows was to be a two-pile sort and should be a three-column sort — steps required, tokens that must be resident, state that must survive a session. Only the middle column is a model property we would have to buy or train for. If our workflows sort mostly into columns one and three, the gap question may not need a training run. The most-cited coding benchmark has more error in it than the gap it measures. The same survey's SWE-bench case study reports 32.67% of "successful" patches involving solution leakage, 31.08% passing only on weak tests, and top systems dropping 66% of their solve rate after contamination filtering (a-third-of-swe-bench-successes-were-leakage). A 66% drop is larger than any open-vs-frontier separation this map holds — larger than the 29 points on ARC-AGI-2, an order of magnitude larger than the 0.8 on an agentic aggregate. It does not say which way decontamination moves the gap; nobody has measured that, and leakage could favour either side. It converges with the noise-floor tally from the other direction (12–14% of web-agent outcomes flipping run-to-run, ±0.035–0.066 on harness contrasts, a 9.4% trial error rate) on one conclusion: most published comparisons in this map's own literature sit inside their own error. Relayed by a survey rather than produced by it, so read the primary work before quoting 66% as ours. And a sixth confound, this one attacking what "the open model" even refers to. Artificial Analysis's new Endpoint Accuracy Index scores serverless providers against a self-hosted deployment of the official weights, and GLM-5.2 (max) reads 100% to 73% depending on the endpoint — with nominal precision failing to predict it (Nebius FP4 100%, DeepInfra FP4 73%) and two of its three components being tool calling and long-context recall (api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points). Since run 9 this map has refused a model verdict without harness, precision and grader named; run 13 added effort. The endpoint is the fifth, and it is the first that changes the weights being served rather than how they are run or scored. Practical form: any comparison we run against an API is a joint measurement of model and endpoint, so pin the endpoint and the date, or re-run the baseline. Retroactively it supplies a further uncontrolled variable for open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness, where practitioners running nominally identical open weights disagreed on basic rule-following — a dispute this map has now handed three candidate explanations (harness, build, endpoint) and settled with none.

  • 2026-08-09 (run 14) — Stays contested. One entry, and it is a method result rather than a gap measurement. DeepSeek's self-reported 82.7% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 for the open-weight V4 Flash 0731 — measured in an unreleased in-house harness at max effort — was reproduced by an outside party at 82.7% ±1.79 over 445 trials in a public, downloadable harness with the entire configuration and per-trial record published (vendor-benchmark-score-reproduced-in-an-independent-public-harness). This map has been discounting open-model vendor cards heavily, and on this occasion the discount was unnecessary. Read it narrowly: a bounded terminal benchmark at max effort with skills off is the setting where a scaffold has least room to act, and it is the same task class where open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe already placed open weights at frontier level. So it does not move the gap; it says one open vendor's number on the procedure-execution side of this map's boundary is honest, which is the side the boundary already predicted would be honest. What it does supply is the template for the experiment this vault keeps deferring. Run 13 left us with four variables that have to be pinned or the number has no sign — harness, precision, grader, reasoning effort. This run publishes model, provider, harness version, effort, skill state, trial count and a four-way token split, plus an error bar (±1.79) and an operational error rate (42 of 445 trials failed, 9.4%). One person produced that against a public API in one run. The cost of being rigorous about a comparison is evidently low, which removes the last excuse for not running ours. Discount for the reproducer disclosing they authored the harness being validated, and for a single benchmark, model, provider and effort setting; and note this tells us nothing about open-ended work, where the gap keeps reappearing.

  • 2026-08-08 (run 13) — Stays contested, and the run after this map's narrowest reading it gets its widest. ARC Prize published third-party-verified semi-private results for the open-weight DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731: ARC-AGI-1 89.0% at $0.02 per task, ARC-AGI-2 61.4% at $0.04 per task, both at maximum reasoning effort. Frontier on the same benchmark reads GPT-5.6 Sol 92.5% and Claude Opus 5 90.4%, the latter at $2.06 per task. That is a 29.0-point deficit bought back at roughly 1/50th the price (open-model-trails-frontier-29-points-on-abstract-reasoning). Set beside run 12's Agentic Index reading — Qwen3.8-Max 0.8 points from Opus 5 — the two are not in conflict and should not be averaged. They are two points on the boundary this map has been trying to locate, and together they draw it more sharply than either does alone: open is at or near frontier on executing a procedure and far from it on producing a solution to something novel. That is the same cut parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators extracted from the literature's comparator bias and open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe found on coding, arriving here from a held-out set where no comparator games are available. It is also compatible with run 11's long-running versus long-context predicate without confirming it — ARC is neither, which is precisely why it isolates the third thing. The price relation deserves stating in full because it is steeper than anything else on the vault's ledger and steeper on both axes: GDPevo gave up 7 points for 61% of the bill; this gives up a third of the score for 98% of it. Not a better bargain — a different one, and which one is acceptable is a property of the task rather than of the models. It is the strongest argument yet for a threshold test on our own workflows over a head-to-head race. The unplanned finding may outlast the headline. The same checkpoint reads 46.0 / 56.0 / 61.4 at low / high / max reasoning effort with weights, harness, precision and grader all fixed — a 15.4-point swing from a runtime dial (reasoning-effort-moves-one-model-15-points). This map already refuses a model verdict without harness, precision and grader named; effort is the fourth such variable, the first that is a pure runtime setting, and the one most leaderboard rows omit. Every row in this vault lacking an effort setting hides a band of about that width, and a max-effort open model compared against an unspecified-effort frontier model produces a number with no sign. Cost is published only at the top rung, so the model's own cost/quality curve is unmeasured — which makes an effort sweep on one model the cheapest cost/quality curve available to us, needing no training run at all. Discount the whole entry on scope: one benchmark family that resembles none of our work, and the frontier comparison arm is secondary-sourced from a leaderboard aggregation rather than ARC Prize's own result pages.   • 2026-08-07 (run 12) — Stays contested. The closest leaderboard reading this map has ever held arrived, and the same event showed why the reading has no sign. On Artificial Analysis's Agentic Index, the open-weight Qwen3.8-Max scores 58.4 against Claude Opus 5's 59.2 — under a point apart on an independent aggregate specifically about agentic work (open-weight-model-within-a-point-of-frontier-on-an-agentic-aggregate). Under the previous grading regime the same two models read 55.4 and 55.3, with the open model marginally ahead. Artificial Analysis had replaced GPT-4o, Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507 and Gemini 3 Flash Preview as grader models on several constituent benchmarks with GPT-5.6 Luna, citing stronger agreement with human judgment; both scores moved 3–4 points and the ordering between them reversed. Take the closeness and discard the ordering, for a reason that is arithmetic rather than political. The instrument moved the readings by 3–4 points; the gap being read was 0.1, then 0.8. When judge choice perturbs scores by several times the separation, the separation is unmeasured (grader-model-is-a-fourth-variable-that-has-to-be-pinned). The substitution itself is a defensible improvement — grading models with GPT-4o that are generations ahead of it was a real weakness, and that objection argues for the change. What it establishes is a fourth confound to pin, alongside harness (open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness) and precision (quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly): the grader. Unlike the other two it leaves no trace in the artifact — a leaderboard snapshot carries no record of which judge produced it, so two screenshots a day apart are not comparable and nothing on the page says so. Direct instruction for our own work: if we grade our workflows with an LLM judge, the judge is a version-pinned dependency, a judge upgrade invalidates every prior comparison, and any before/after has to be re-scored under one judge rather than compared across two. The compilation study founding combined-small-model-plus-custom-harness was LLM-judged, so this is retroactive as well as prospective. The closeness is real and it should be read against, not instead of, this map's task-level evidence. The same model trails frontier by 12.3 points on SWE-bench Pro and 15.3 on FrontierSWE while sitting at the frontier band on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe). Both measurements are correct about different things: an index built from nine evaluations averages the task classes where the gap lives into the ones where it doesn't. That makes the run-11 predicate — long-running versus long-context work — precisely the split an aggregate cannot express, and it means near-parity on the Agentic Index is not evidence for or against that predicate. Provenance is thin in a specific way: this is a forum reconstruction with screenshots, not a published changelog with a before/after table across all 24 models, so whether the grader swap moved every model uniformly or these two differentially is unknown — and that distinction decides whether anything about the ordering changed at all.

  • 2026-08-06 (run 11) — Stays contested, and the run-10 hit on the residual-gap hypothesis is partly walked back by two better-controlled measurements pointing opposite ways on the same night. Against the gap: OneDayAgent pins one harness across five backends from three families with no per-backend tuning — the portability control this map has never had — and the open GLM-5.2 tops it at 0.821 on 104 open-ended day-long tasks, above every shipped commercial agent measured (AutoClaw 0.799, Codex on GPT-5.5 0.664, Manus 0.645, Genspark 0.635, ChatGPT-Agent 0.626), with a 9B open backend at 0.613 landing within 0.05 of Codex (one-harness-generalizes-across-five-backends-without-tuning). For the gap: Prime Agent — the strongest designed harness published — drove GLM-5.2 against Opus 5 in a stock, unmodified Claude Code across nine long-context and long-horizon benchmarks and won 3 of 9, losing OOLONG 0.700 vs 0.900 and OBLIQ 0.669 vs 0.795 (open-weights-in-the-best-harness-still-trail-frontier-in-a-stock-sdk). And GDPevo, contamination-resistant and externally graded like Databricks, finds GLM-5.2 6.98 points below Opus-4.8 on enterprise workflows at 39% of the cost per task (open-model-trades-seven-points-for-61-percent-of-the-bill). So run 10's reframed question — which reported gaps survive removing benchmark contamination — gets its first answer, and the answer is some do. Databricks and GDPevo both decontaminate, both grade externally, and they disagree: tie on pull requests, seven points on business workflows. The residual-gap candidate is alive and needs a sharper predicate than "open-ended," because it is now failing to sort the cases. The Prime Agent result offers one, and it is the most useful thing this map got tonight. The open pairing's three wins were ManyIH instruction-following, LongCot-Mini, and building an emulator from scratch; its six losses cluster on long-context comprehension. Long-running work, where a harness supplies state and structure, versus long-context work, where the material has to sit inside the model. That predicts new cases rather than relabelling settled ones — and it is consistent with the verifiable/non-verifiable candidate rather than replacing it, since what a harness cannot supply is exactly what no reward model can cheaply verify. It also fits this map's own entanglement rule: when the harness carries the load, open closes; when the model carries it, open trails. Every arm here has precision unstated, so the three-way confound stands, and GLM-5.2 at 744B is a reminder that none of this is a size measurement.

  • 2026-08-05 (run 10) — Stays contested, but the theme's most testable residual-gap hypothesis takes its first direct hit, and it takes it on the task class where the hypothesis said the gap would be widest. Run 8 instanced the "closed on verifiable, trails on open-ended" shape with Qwen3.8-Max: frontier-band on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 12–15 points down on SWE-bench Pro and FrontierSWE. Databricks ran the same comparison on work that is neither bounded nor public — tasks harvested from its own merged pull requests on a multi-million-line codebase written by 3,000+ engineers, spanning Scala services, Rust systems code, a React/TypeScript frontend, protobuf/gRPC and Bazel, filtered to human-authored test-covered changes, graded by held-out tests rather than an LLM judge, with git history sealed after agents were caught recovering the true implementation from it. GLM 5.2, open weights, was statistically tied with Claude Opus 4.8 on quality, at $1.28 per task against $1.94 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-private-uncontaminated-codebase). The authors' summary is "open models are here for coding." Three things make this the strongest gap measurement the map holds, and one thing keeps it from moving status. It is uncontaminated — private tasks, which is the objection the authors themselves raise against SWE-bench and which no other source here escapes. It is objectively graded. And it is priced per task, the unit that appears on a bill, rather than per token. Against that: the post publishes no pass-rate table, no task count and no interval behind "statistically tied," so the tie's strength is unverifiable; and Databricks commercially serves open models, so the incentive points at this conclusion — though it points away from the frontier vendors who supply most of this map's contrary evidence, which is not nothing. Also unchanged: this is one harness-controlled study and says nothing about open-model-live-use-robustness-is-disputed-and-tracks-the-harness, which is a claim about variance across scaffolds, not about a mean. Read alongside run 9's three-way confound warning, the design here is notable for pinning two of the three legs — one harness family, one task set — while leaving precision unstated, which is now the standing gap in every comparison this map collects.

  • 2026-08-04 (run 9) — Stays contested; no new gap measurement, but the theme's own methodological rule gets an instrument, and a note on how much of any gap here is now unmeasured. Since run 7 this map has held that no model verdict is interpretable without naming its harness, because every live-use verdict turned out to be a joint measurement. Scale AI's taxonomy operationalises that: 41 failure modes, each assigned to an edge between model, harness, environment and evaluation rather than to a blamed component, κ=0.76 against human labels (agent-failures-belong-to-edges-not-components). Edge attribution is the honest output for this map's founding dispute — identical DeepSeek V4 Flash weights at full precision, opposite verdicts, the split tracking the scaffold. It settles nothing (no census of which edge failures land on, and κ measures rater agreement rather than correctness, so a taxonomy can be reliably wrong), but if we ever run our own comparison the failure-coding scheme has to be fixed before the experiment, not after. One thing to carry into every future comparison on this map: run 9's serving evidence establishes that quantization silently costs long-tail factual recall while leaving reasoning benchmarks flat (quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly). Practically nobody runs a frontier-class open checkpoint at full precision, and the local-use verdicts this theme collects are overwhelmingly from quantized weights — so a third confound now sits alongside model and harness in every open-vs-frontier anecdote here: which quantization, and on which capability. The theme's entanglement problem is not two-way, it is three-way.

  • 2026-08-03 (run 8) — Stays contested, and the theme's most testable residual-gap hypothesis gets a concrete coding-task instance. The numbers are vendor-reported with no independent replication and the weights aren't out yet, so nothing is verified; and every score is a model-plus-harness measurement in Qwen's own scaffold, so per this theme's entanglement rule none of these gaps is a pure model gap.   • 2026-08-01 — Opened. Two early signals point the same way: a practitioner's read that the race is "economic staying power and fast-following, not a widening capability gap" (open-frontier-gap-is-economic-not-capability), and an open-weight model landing on the same blended intelligence axis as frontier (open-weight-model-mid-pack-on-intelligence-index). Both thin — one opinion, one datapoint — hence emerging. The live counter-claim to watch: closed models stay "more robust in messy live use."

Sub-threads: what actually bounds open is now the theme's centre of gravity, and it has three live candidates rather than two: robustness in messy live use (contested as of run 7, and possibly a harness property rather than a model one), a regulatory cap above a capability line, and the newest and most testable — non-verifiable tasks, where the constraint is reward-model quality and therefore compute, not weights. That third candidate is the one worth splitting off first, because it predicts where to look for the gap instead of merely asserting one. A second thread is now unavoidable: this theme's measurements and harness-engineering-vs-default-sdk's are entangled — every live-use verdict here turned out to be a joint measurement of model and scaffold, which means "is the gap closing" cannot be answered without fixing a harness first. Run 9 makes that thread three-way rather than two-way: precision joins model and harness as an uncontrolled variable in every local-use verdict this map collects, since a quantized checkpoint loses long-tail recall while its reasoning scores stay flat. Any future comparison here has to pin all three. Run 10 puts the non-verifiable-tasks candidate under real strain: on uncontaminated, objectively-graded, multi-language production engineering work — the most open-ended task this map has ever measured — an open model tied the best frontier model on quality and beat it on cost per task. The candidate is not dead (one study, no table, self-published), but the next thing to look for is no longer "where is the gap on hard tasks" so much as which reported gaps survive removing benchmark contamination. That reframing is cheap to test and would explain the run 8 / run 10 disagreement without either being wrong. Run 11 tests it and gets a mixed answer — two decontaminated, externally-graded studies disagree (tie on pull requests, seven points on enterprise workflows) — so the reframing survives as a method but stops being an explanation. What replaces it is a sharper predicate for what actually bounds open, and it should now lead the thread: long-running vs long-context. Where a harness can carry state, structure and verification, open weights close or win; where the material has to sit inside the model, they trail, and the best harness anyone has built does not fix it. That is testable by holding the harness fixed and varying only how much material the task forces into context — cheap, and directly answerable on our own workflows. It also subsumes the entanglement thread rather than competing with it: the gap appears precisely where the scaffold has nothing to contribute. Run 12 leaves that predicate untouched and disqualifies the instrument most often used to argue against it. Leaderboard aggregates cannot express a long-running/long-context split by construction, and this one moved 3–4 points on a grader change while the open-vs-frontier separation it reported was under a point — so no aggregate reading, in either direction, should be treated as evidence on this map again without its grading version stated. The entanglement list grows to four: model, harness, precision, grader. Practically that retires the cheapest kind of update this map receives (a leaderboard moved) and leaves only the expensive kind (a task-split measurement with all four pinned), which is the right trade but means this map should expect to move less often. Run 15 splits the leading predicate and grows the entanglement list to five. Long-running was two things — task horizon and system memory — and separating them turns the queued workflow-sorting exercise into a three-column sort where only one column (resident context) is a model property we would have to buy or train for; the other two have working answers on this vault's ledger already, which is the cheapest route to shrinking this map's question rather than answering it. The fifth entanglement is the endpoint, and it is different in kind from the other four: harness, precision, grader and effort change how weights are run or scored, while a serverless endpoint changes which weights you are served — 100% to 73% of reference on one open checkpoint, undisclosed and unhashable. So the standing rule extends from "name the harness, precision, grader and effort" to "and name the endpoint and the date, or the model is not identified." Alongside that, the run's contamination finding gives this map its bluntest self-assessment: on the benchmark family most often used to argue about coding, filtering leakage costs top systems 66% of their score — several times any separation this map reports — which means the honest position on published gaps is not that they are wrong but that they are unresolved, and only our own uncontaminated task-split measurement will change that.
