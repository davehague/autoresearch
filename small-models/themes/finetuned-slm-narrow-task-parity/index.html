Do fine-tuned small models match bigger models on narrow tasks?

The central bet of the whole vault: can a fine-tuned small model own a specific workflow as well as a much larger (or frontier) model? This is where the train-vs-buy decision lives.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-28 (run 32) — Stays contested. Two arrivals on the core bet, and they clear parity in two different ways — one against much larger models, one against the next size up. An open 35B-A3B tuned on 10K real examples beat five larger models on the workflow it trained for. TaoLive's Live-Stream QA: HAT 94.8 against GLM-5.2's 93.0, Qwen3.7-max's 92.5, DeepSeek-V4-flash 91.4, DeepSeek-V4-pro 91.2, Qwen3.6-plus 88.7, from a base of 80.3 (a-tuned-open-35b-beat-five-larger-models-on-its-own-task-and-none-of-them-nearby). Set that beside run 30's counterexample — 35,207 B200 GPU-hours and 200B tokens of proprietary legal data for 2.9 points, then losing two of three legal exams to a general 31B (a-domain-specialist-cost-thirtyfive-thousand-b200-hours-and-moved-its-base-two-point-nine-points) — and the discriminating variable is not scale. It is whether the target is a workflow you already operate at volume with logs. 10K trajectories of the actual task beat 200B tokens of adjacent text by an order of magnitude on both cost and result. And the boundary is exactly where this map keeps finding it. Harness-variant QA, the same task under scaffolds the model never trained on, holds at 94.6 — the task generalises. Broad synthetic scenarios do not: 84.0 tool robustness against GLM-5.2's 86.7, 77.6 prompt robustness against 84.0, IFEval recovering only to 83.5 against Qwen3.7-max's 90.4. That is run 28's 450M browser specialist (a-450m-specialist-beat-two-larger-generalists-and-lost-to-the-third) at 78× the parameters, and it makes the routing requirement explicit rather than optional: parity holds inside the trained task distribution, not inside the capability, so anything off-distribution has to leave. The second arrival clears a smaller gap for free, and the mechanism is new here. TTPO post-trains with no labels at all — majority vote over the model's own rollouts, distilling agreeing rollouts and RL-penalising disagreeing ones — and puts a trained Qwen3-4B at 61.1 against an untrained Qwen3-8B's 60.7 in the label-free test-time setting, with the same crossing at 58.6 against 58.6 in the labeled-data setting (a-label-free-4b-passed-an-untrained-8b). For this map the useful form of the question is not "can a 4B match a frontier model" but "can a 4B we post-trained replace the 8B we would otherwise serve" — half the weights, half the memory, which run 31 established is the binding constraint on ownership. The 1.7B gains most (+7.2 over base), so the crossing is a property of the gap between adjacent sizes rather than a law. Discounts: TaoLive's evaluation sets are unreleased and rubric-graded by a model, its frontier rows are zero-shot, and three of five comparators are the same vendor's; TTPO is competition mathematics, the friendliest possible domain for majority voting, on year-old Qwen3 backbones, and the 0.4-point crossing is inside noise on its own — it is the pattern across two settings that carries.

  • 2026-08-27 (run 31) — Stays contested, and the arrival is the first parity comparison on this map run against a baseline that could actually win. Most claims here compare a tuned specialist against an untuned comparator; this one compares against an oracle-routed ensemble of the same specialists. Open-MOPD releases an end-to-end multi-teacher on-policy distillation recipe — post-training procedure, training trajectories and evaluation suites — run on an 8×A100-80GB academic setup, one outer step at 1298s with reward refresh included at 2.10% of computation time (a-full-multi-teacher-distillation-recipe-fits-on-eight-a100s). The comparator is the hardest available one, and it answers the question this map actually asks: if we tune per workflow, what does merging cost? At 3B across three domains, 0.31 points — 31.24 for the merged student against 31.55 for the perfect router. That is the strongest evidence held here for the one-model-many-workflows plan, and it lands against a run of results pointing the other way — run 30's general 31B beating a purpose-built legal specialist on two of three benchmarks (a-general-31b-beat-the-domain-specialist-on-two-of-three-legal-benchmarks), run 28's 450M browser specialist beating two larger generalists and losing to a 4B (a-450m-specialist-beat-two-larger-generalists-and-lost-to-the-third). Read together the shape is consistent rather than contradictory: specialisation is worth something, and merging specialists costs almost nothing — what fails is specialising against a stronger general model than the one you started from. The reproducibility is the second half of the value: run 30's most informative training result shipped as a 35,207-GPU-hour invoice and a noncommercial artifact; this one ships as a recipe anyone can re-run. Status holds. 3B student, three domains, same-origin RL teachers, and an absolute ceiling of 31.55 — the headroom being divided is small enough that a 0.31-point merge cost may not survive at a scale where the specialists are actually good. Nothing here says the result transfers to a 27B student or to frontier-API teachers, which is the configuration we would buy, and run 28's same-origin constraint (a-same-origin-teacher-beats-a-stronger-cross-origin-one) predicts it would not transfer cleanly. No seeds or intervals for the headline table in the material available.

  • 2026-08-26 (run 30) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, both parity claims with the base model in the table, and both of them shrink when you read the rows rather than the average. Run 29's open attribution got answered on a different axis, and the answer is 0.2 points. A community benchmarker ran 13 GGUF files, 5 seeds each, 65 runs, >300 GPU-hours through tool-eval-bench 2.6.0 in hardmode at 50% context pressure, publishing means with 95% intervals and the raw CSV: Ornith-1.5 144.2 [141.7, 146.7] against Tiel-Coder 144.0 [141.8, 146.2] (the-quant-and-template-that-carried-the-whole-margin-are-worth-nothing-here). Tiel is Ornith plus the code-weighted imatrix quant plus the Qwen-Sharp chat template — the two layers that carried run 29's entire +4 SWE-bench Live margin, measured here at zero. A maintainer also supplies the correction this map's blurb needed: "Tiel is an Ornith imatrix quant with different chat template instructions. There is no finetune involved with Tiel." That makes run 29's headline a packaging result, not a training one. Not a refutation — 88 tool-call tests are not 25 repository issues, and TielCoder's author responded arguing the difference only shows on hard out-of-distribution problems — but the vault's position moves from unattributed to unreplicated, and a claim whose whole margin lives in two layers that measure zero elsewhere no longer gets the benefit of the doubt. The same table says the generation of the base outranks everything the community applies on top of the last one. Three independent tunes, four quant providers, a code-weighted imatrix and a hand-written chat template on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B span 131.5 to 144.2; the current-generation dense Qwen3.8-27B scores 152.6 with none of it, intervals non-overlapping (a-generation-of-the-base-outranks-every-tune-applied-to-the-last-one). That is the second benchmark this week with that shape, after run 29's SWE-bench Live table. It also puts a floor under this map's tenth parity claim, below. And a new invisible confound arrives free: Ornith-1.5-Heretic — the same weights, abliterated — scores 132.2, twelve points down and below the base it was tuned from (abliteration-cost-twelve-points-of-tool-calling-and-leaves-no-trace), with nothing in the parameter count, quant label or file size to show for it. And the best-resourced instance of this map's own bet ever published moved its base 2.9 points. Thomson Reuters continual-pretrained Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on 200B tokens curated from a >19T-token proprietary legal and tax corpus, aligned it with Constitutional DPO and post-trained with DPO plus RL. Against its own base: LegalBench 80.3 → 79.9 (backwards), Tax Q&A 86.2 → 86.6, MMLU-Pro 85.2 → 85.7, MBE 80.1 → 83.4, Harvey Legal Agent 69.5 → 73.4, average 71.7 → 74.6 (a-domain-specialist-cost-thirtyfive-thousand-b200-hours-and-moved-its-base-two-point-nine-points). Three of six cells move less than a point; the field's standard legal benchmark moves the wrong way; and the domain where the data moat is widest moves 0.4. Worse for the parity story: a general Gemma 4-31B with no legal training beat the specialist on both legal knowledge benchmarks — 83.1 vs 79.9 on LegalBench, 88.8 vs 83.4 on the bar exam — then collapsed to 34.2 on the agentic one against the specialist's 73.4 (a-general-31b-beat-the-domain-specialist-on-two-of-three-legal-benchmarks). The generalisation this map should now carry: domain continual pre-training does not buy knowledge, because a strong open base already has it — it buys procedure. And procedure is the thing this vault's harness half claims you can buy with a prompt for nothing. Nobody ran that arm. Vendor-published, vendor-run comparators with no harness disclosed for any of them, no seeds, no intervals — the eleventh entry in this map's standing complaint about comparator quality (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators).

  • 2026-08-25 (run 29) — Stays contested. Two arrivals. One is a community parity claim with a published baseline and an unresolved three-way attribution; the other is a second independent sighting of the constraint that decides who we can distil from. A stacked fine-tune reached a frontier tier's solve count, and its own base model is in the table at the same score as Sonnet 5. TielCoder-35B-A3B — Ornith-1.5's fine-tune, plus a code-weighted imatrix quant, plus a custom chat template — solves 12/25 SWE-bench Live against Opus 4.6 medium 12, Sonnet 5 medium 8, and stock Ornith-1.5 8 (a-22gb-four-bit-local-model-tied-a-frontier-medium-on-twelve-of-twentyfive). Publishing the fine-tune's own baseline puts this above run 27's Gemma 4 12B claim of 2.7× with no benchmark and no baseline arm at all (five-thousand-examples-on-a-sixteen-gigabyte-card-claimed-two-point-seven-times), and below run 28's 450M specialist, which ran comparator arms and published its loss (a-450m-specialist-beat-two-larger-generalists-and-lost-to-the-third). But the entire +4 margin belongs jointly to the two layers that are not the fine-tune (three-stacked-changes-one-number-and-no-ablation), one of which is a chat template — i.e. plausibly a free harness fix wearing a fine-tuning result. This map's nine-deep complaint about comparator quality (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators) now needs a sibling: a published baseline is not an ablation, and a stacked intervention with one number cannot support a parity claim about any single layer. n=25, single run, self-benchmarked, unnamed harness. And the distillation route's family constraint replicated, in a method that has nothing to do with teacher choice. R2-OPD masks the 30% of teacher-reward spans where reward and actual reasoning progress disagree; it gains +2.51 avg@4 on a DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B student and +0.43 — a sixth — when moved to a Qwen3-1.7B/e3-1.7B pair (the-distillation-fix-transferred-across-families-at-one-sixth). Run 28 established the same asymmetry for teacher selection: same-origin pairs generalize, cross-origin pairs memorize, even when the cross-origin teacher is stronger (a-same-origin-teacher-beats-a-stronger-cross-origin-one). Two independent instances, one about who you distil from and one about a technique, both degrading across the same boundary. Alongside run 24's silent cross-teacher failure without style residualization (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization), the generalization to carry is: distillation techniques do not port across model families at anything near their published strength. Not fatal for us — our checkpoints are Qwen-family — but it sets a retroactive reading rule: every OPD number in this vault is a same-family number. The cross-family arm here is a single pair, both students are ~1.5–1.7B on math only, and there are no seeds, so a 0.43-point result is indistinguishable from zero.

  • 2026-08-24 (run 28) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, and for the first time this map gets a parity attempt with the comparator arms actually run — plus a constraint on the distillation route that we cannot buy our way out of. The first honest parity attempt on this map is a loss, reported as a loss. A practitioner fine-tuned LFM2.5-VL-450M on browser screenshots for a structured-perception task and published a 100-case held-out benchmark with larger generalists run on the same cases: Qwen 3.5 4B 55/100, fine-tuned 450M 44/100, Gemma 4 E4B 41/100, Qwen 3.5 2B 39/100, base model 0/100 (a-450m-specialist-beat-two-larger-generalists-and-lost-to-the-third). The specialist beats two larger generalists on the task it was purpose-built for and loses to the third by 11 points, with the largest model in the table winning. The model card then says it "did not pass" the unchanged production gate. This map's standing complaint has been comparator quality — nine parity claims resting on untuned or differently-interfaced baselines (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators), and an eighth whose margin sat inside the noise (the-eighth-parity-claim-and-its-margin-is-inside-the-noise). Run 27's Gemma 4 12B tool-calling fine-tune claimed 2.7× with no benchmark and no baseline arm (five-thousand-examples-on-a-sixteen-gigabyte-card-claimed-two-point-seven-times). This is what that class of project looks like once the arms are run, and the answer is: real specialization, no parity. Note the ranking is measure-dependent — strict pass gives the 450M a 5-point win over Qwen 3.5 2B; rubric mean gives it a 3-point loss. And this map finally has the shape of its cost curve, not just endpoints. 16,646 examples bought 0 → 30/100. The next 33,354, concentrated deliberately on the first version's failure classes — multilingual OCR, forms, auth screens, modals, tables, dashboards, calendars, occlusion — bought 30 → 36 (the-second-tranche-of-training-data-bought-a-fifth-of-the-first). Roughly a fifth the points per example, in the best case for marginal data. Anyone budgeting a specialization run off the first tranche's slope will overspend badly. The number to measure is where the curve bends, not where it starts. The distillation route has a compatibility constraint, and it outranks teacher strength. Across a matrix of reasoning teachers and students, same-origin teacher–student pairs generalize cross-lingually and cross-domain while cross-origin pairs primarily memorize — and the same-origin advantage holds even when the cross-origin teacher is the stronger standalone model (a-same-origin-teacher-beats-a-stronger-cross-origin-one). That is a procurement constraint: the open student we pick determines which teachers are usable, and the obvious move — distil from the strongest model we can call — produces a memorizer. It extends run 19's finding that RL environments and infrastructure are what does not distil (rl-environments-and-infrastructure-are-what-does-not-distil); the teacher's advantage does not distil across lineages either. One piece of cheap money in the same paper, and it inverts standard practice. Easy, hard and random 25,000-problem training pools converge to nearly identical final accuracy across every teacher–student pair, and problems the teacher itself cannot solve remain useful (on-policy-distillation-transfers-behaviour-not-answers). What transfers is reasoning behaviour, not answers. So curating a distillation pool by difficulty, and filtering to teacher-correct examples, is wasted effort — which removes an entire preparation stage from a first fine-tune, and fits run 23's procedural-anchoring mechanism exactly (skills-anchor-procedure-they-do-not-inject-knowledge): a groove does not care how hard the problem was that demonstrated it. Standing instruction, updated. Run 27's four free steps stand — no-fine-tune arm on the same prompts, count invalid tool calls not emitted ones, repeat it, check divergence from the base weighted by base confidence. Add two: match the teacher's lineage to the student's before matching its strength, and stop sorting the training pool by difficulty. The 450M item did the first of run 27's four and none of the rest; the OPD paper supplies the two new ones and runs no seeds. Discounts throughout: the 450M numbers are n=100, single run, no seeds, self-published and self-benchmarked by the party that trained the model, with comparators it also ran and may have under-elicited — and an in-thread objection that the 0/100 base is a formatting floor rather than a perception floor, which would deflate the whole trajectory; teacher labels came from a Qwen 3.6-35B with admitted OCR errors, so the ceiling is a 35B's perception, not ground truth; and the OPD paper is under review, reports its headline claims qualitatively rather than as effect sizes with intervals, runs no seeds, covers reasoning models only — the authors state findings may not extend to "multimodal, tool-using, or interactive agent settings", which is the workload class this vault actually cares about — and conflates architecture, tokenizer, corpus and post-training recipe inside the word "origin".

  • 2026-08-23 (run 27) — Stays contested. Two practitioner arrivals, pointing opposite ways at the same lever, and read together they hand this map a diagnostic it has never had. The floor of the cost curve is one consumer GPU and five thousand public examples. A r/LocalLLaMA poster fine-tuned Gemma 4 12B for tool calling and CLI use because it was the largest model that fit comfortably in 16 GB of VRAM — glaive-function-calling-v2 plus all splits of AgentInstruct, reformatted into Gemma 4's chat template with ReAct trajectories converted to tool calls, 5,211 examples, QLoRA rank 16, 3 epochs, batch 32, loss 1.695 → 0.120 — and claims 2.7× on tool calling and +15.7% tool calls emitted (five-thousand-examples-on-a-sixteen-gigabyte-card-claimed-two-point-seven-times). Most of the work was dataset reformatting, not training. Nothing about the training side of this map's central bet is out of reach. But there is no benchmark, no baseline arm and no repeat behind either number, so the headline cannot be separated from the model learning to emit the template it was trained on — which is the harness's problem rather than the model's. And the second number's sign is assumed: more emitted tool calls is presented as progress, while tonight's Thinkingbox finds 77.5% of agent failures are failures to recover from a tool error (three-quarters-of-agent-failures-are-failures-to-recover-from-a-tool-error). More calls into an environment the model cannot repair from is not obviously a gain. And fine-tuning can break the capability being fine-tuned. Four community derivatives of Qwen3.8-27B, measured against stock on the same prompts: heretic-ara 0/69 invalid tool calls at 0.717% top-1 flips, Blackfrost 1/275 at 3.844%, and AEON Ultimate 44/299 at 2.997% — a lower flip rate than Blackfrost and forty-four times the broken calls (a-community-finetune-overturned-a-point-nine-nine-nine-one-token). The discriminating variable is which decisions get overturned: the stable derivatives flip tokens the base model was already unsure about; AEON replaced 2 at p = 0.9991 with ql at 0.9158, producing 543ql where a port belonged. This map has priced fine-tuning as data, compute and cost, and has never priced regression in the target capability. The mechanism there is abliteration rather than task-specific SFT, so it is a cousin — but the diagnostic transfers and costs nothing. Standing instruction for our own first fine-tune, and all four steps are free: run the no-fine-tune arm on the same prompts; count invalid tool calls rather than emitted ones; repeat it; and check divergence from the base checkpoint weighted by the base model's confidence at each position. None were done in either item tonight. The Gemma weights also ship at Q4_K_M, the band where both 4-bit weight formats broke tool calls in the same practitioner report that supplied the diagnostic. Discounts: both are self-published, unrefereed, n=1, with no released evaluation data; the Gemma numbers have no denominator at all.

  • 2026-08-22 (run 26) — Stays contested. One arrival, not a parity comparison, aimed at the route this map has treated as the cheap way to get a small model onto a narrow task. Bootstrapping a small model off its own outputs did not acquire anything, and it corrupted what already worked. Phantom Gains audits three rounds of rank-32 LoRA self-training on Qwen3-8B against a frozen control pushed through the identical pipeline. On the low-base pool, external distillation from gpt-oss-120b registers 8–11 detections while majority-vote SFT, STaR and a policy-gradient arm register 0–2 — and a logistic regression rejects "distillation simply gained more overall" at β = 1.91 [1.25, 2.56], p < 10⁻⁸ (self-training-expands-nothing-and-corrupts-at-ten-times-the-floor). In the 1,163-problem difficulty band, problems solved at baseline and lost after training run STaR 106, majority-vote 88 against a design-matched frozen control median of 8. For this map's bet that means the label-free path to a task-owning small model is, in this regime, a net-negative trade: nothing new reached, an order of magnitude above the floor broken. And the audit lands on how nine of this map's parity claims were read. Seven standard measurement practices each invert a finding when the control is absent — most pointedly, one training seed gives the same method accuracy shifts of +3.3, −1.5 and +0.7 and a corruption-to-learning ratio anywhere in 0.55–1.53 (seven-standard-measurement-practices-each-invert-a-self-improvement-finding). This map's running complaint has been comparator quality — nine parity claims resting on untuned or differently-interfaced baselines (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators). Add seed variance to it. A margin inside the noise was already this map's standing discount (the-eighth-parity-claim-and-its-margin-is-inside-the-noise); the noise is now measured, and on a single-seed single-decode design it is several points wide. Discount the reach, not the direction: one backbone family (Qwen3-8B), one regime — three rounds at ~270 optimizer steps, far short of the schedules the paper positions against — and corruption measured on MATH training problems almost certainly inside pretraining. The authors are explicit that the claim is that this regime does not expand, not that self-training cannot.

  • 2026-08-20 (run 24) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, neither a parity comparison, and both aimed at what this map has been assuming a small model has to be good at. The ninth untuned-or-differently-interfaced comparator, and it loses to a 9B anyway. SkillGate trains a Qwen3.5-9B on which skill file to open mid-episode and reports it ahead of its own 27B at 3× the parameters and of frontier references at roughly 40× — DeepSeek-V4-Pro, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5, Kimi-K2.6, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, DeepSeek-V3.2 — on selection reliability, with trial success moving 40.8% → 53.2% against 47.0% for the same budget spent on outcome reward alone (skill-selection-is-a-trainable-capability-that-scale-does-not-supply). The authors label the frontier rows not controlled comparisons — served through their own providers' APIs with native function calling — which is the ninth instance in this vault's ledger of a parity claim resting on a comparator that was never adapted to the task (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators). Weight it accordingly. What survives the discount is narrower and more useful: scale does not supply a reliable selector. General capability compensates for a missed skill without preventing the miss, which is the first mechanism this map has for why a tuned small model can hold a narrow workflow against a much larger untuned one — the large model is not failing at the task, it is failing at a decision nobody trained. And the narrow-task bet's usual escape route just got measured. This map's standing argument is that a small model can own a workflow because the workflow is narrow, with anything it doesn't know supplied from outside. The candidate 27B has now moved a full generation in exactly that direction — +23.4 Agentic Index, +31.3 points of τ-banking, +19.1 of Terminal-Bench 2.1, −4.0 of factual recall (the-candidate-checkpoint-traded-recall-for-agentic-capability) — so the vendors are optimising for the same split this map bet on, and the parametric knowledge our workflows were never going to use is the thing being spent. That is confirmation of the bet's premise, not of the bet. It also narrows where the bet is safe. Run 19's GAIA result split this map's workload sort into three piles by noting that information-seeking fails on missing evidence rather than misapplied procedure. A checkpoint that trades recall for tool-reaching is straightforwardly better on the procedure piles and straightforwardly worse on any workflow where the answer has to come from the weights. Run 24's coherence-debt work supplies the harness-side condition that makes the trade safe: context and parametric memory substitute, and a supplied fact works as reliably at 128,000 characters' distance as beside the edit — but only if the harness supplies it, because when it does not, the agent fabricates rather than stopping (a-missing-fact-produces-wrong-work-not-a-stopped-agent). Still nothing this run that races a tuned small model against a tuned comparator at matched cost. Discounts: SkillGate is one preprint, one backbone family, no repeats or intervals on 385 pooled trials, with three of its five benchmarks constructed to reward having skills; the checkpoint comparison is two aggregator rows with no intervals.

  • 2026-08-18 (run 22) — Stays contested. One arrival, and it is the eighth parity-adjacent claim measured against an untuned comparator — but it carries the cheapest quality lever this map has ever priced. One recording, +18.2 points of strict success. UI-Mate-27B, an open-weight GUI agent, is trained on an environment-grounded stack and then handed in-context demonstrations. On the self-demo subset of OSWorkerBench, a single demonstration moves strict success 17.2% → 35.4% and progress 67.9% → 81.1% (one-demonstration-doubles-strict-success-on-a-users-own-workflow). No training, no cluster, no generation loop. Set that against this map's cost ledger: $8.95 for a compact model's skill library (a-skill-library-for-a-compact-model-costs-nine-dollars-to-build), ~29,000 tokens per verified RL environment (a-verified-rl-environment-costs-under-thirty-thousand-tokens), one A100-hour for a tuned 1.5B (a-tuned-1-5b-runs-real-work-on-a-laptop-cpu-for-one-a100-hour). This is an order of magnitude below all of them. The framing is the part that generalizes past GUIs. The authors' stated problem is that routine workflows depend on user-specific tools and tacit conventions, so unstated instructions produce arbitrary variation across runs. That is the failure mode every parity claim on this map has to survive in deployment and never gets tested on — benchmark tasks are fully specified, real ones are not. A demonstration is an external referent for the unstated part, which is the same mechanism underneath everything that works in this vault, supplied by the environment rather than generated by a model. Read the split before budgeting for it. Strict success roughly doubles (+18.2) while progress moves +13.2 from an already-high 67.9%. The agent was mostly getting there; the demonstration converts near-misses into completions. That is a conventions problem being solved, not a capability one — so expect it to pay where our model already almost works and to do very little where it does not, which is the headroom law pointing the same direction it always does. On parity itself, nothing moves. UI-Mate's controlled contrast is +17.7 strict success over its own untuned Qwen3.6-27B base — a genuine like-for-like isolation at fixed size and family, and the right control for the authors' question. It is the wrong control for ours. Run 16 stopped treating this as a per-paper flaw at the seventh instance; this is the eighth, and the boundary predicate stands unchallenged because nothing here tests a tuned open model against a well-prompted frontier agent. No frontier arm, no intervals, no repeats, no cost figures, and one subset of one benchmark for the demonstration result.

  • 2026-08-15 (run 19) — Stays contested. One arrival, and it is the levels complaint crossing an architecture boundary for the first time. Run 12 named this map's most-repeated structural finding: the literature reports deltas and almost no levels, and the levels are low (tuned-small-agents-still-fail-most-realistic-tool-tasks). The tally since — a purpose-tuned Qwen3-8B at 46.0% BFCL-v4 / 36.7% τ²-Bench / 16.0% VitaBench, CalibForge's terminal agent at 47.57% after a 24.71-point gain (trained-terminal-agents-top-out-below-50-percent), the best of five frontier agents at 25.3% on realistic oncall RCA (frontier-agents-top-out-at-25-percent-on-realistic-oncall-rca), three agentic memory systems spanning 21–54% (memory-system-cost-is-set-by-internal-behaviour-not-conversation-length). BDH-CQ adds a sixth, and its own framing concedes the point: the stated contribution is "a new state of the art in benchmark cost efficiency" — 29.5% pass@2 at $0.0007 per task on a 150M recurrent latent-reasoning model (cost-efficiency-state-of-the-art-at-29-percent-is-the-levels-problem-again). What makes it worth a separate entry is what it rules out. The previous five instances were all transformers under varying training and scaffolding regimes, which left open the reading that low levels are a property of the current recipe. A non-transformer landing in the same band says the ceiling is a property of the task class and how it is measured. The consequence is unchanged and now six times confirmed: a race between two systems that both fail most of the work is the wrong experiment, and run 16's sharpening still stands as the replacement — not does the small model match, but which fraction of our calls needs frontier, and can we identify them cheaply (cross-model-escalation-routes-7-percent-of-calls-and-cuts-74-percent-of-cost). Weakest of the six taken alone: ARC-AGI-1 is a puzzle benchmark resembling none of our work, pass@1 is unreported so 29.5% is already the generous number, and the top community objection — that the architecture was trained on ARC-shaped data — is unanswered (benchmark-parity-claims-lack-construct-validity). No movement on the parity question itself this run; the map's own best predictor, the procedure-versus-novel-artifact cut, gained a seventh domain from the Qwen release but on a vendor table rather than a controlled comparison (an-apache-27b-posts-frontier-band-agentic-coding-numbers).

  • 2026-08-14 (run 18) — Stays contested. Two arrivals, and between them the map's best-supported claim and its oldest complaint each get their sharpest instance yet. A LoRA-tuned Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B scores 0.620 on InterCode-ALFA against an untuned Qwen2.5-Coder-7B at 0.613, its own base at 0.540, and GPT-4o at 0.730 (the-eighth-parity-claim-and-its-margin-is-inside-the-noise). What is genuinely new is that this author published the comparators, so the margin could be checked at all — more than most peer-reviewed instances on this ledger manage — and what is missing is one line: an interval. That lands this squarely in the noise-floor tally run 14 and run 15 assembled (12–14% of web-agent outcomes flipping run-to-run, ±0.035–0.066, a 9.4% trial error rate): most published comparisons in this literature sit inside their own error, and a bootstrap over 300 tasks would have shown it. One property raises this instance's weight despite its grade and should change how this map ranks evidence: the grading is execution-based — each command runs in a container, scored by diffing filesystem and stdout against a reference — so run 12's grader confound does not apply at all (grader-model-is-a-fourth-variable-that-has-to-be-pinned). Weight an execution-graded tie above an LLM-judged win. The ceiling is equally plain: single-turn, 64-token outputs, no tool loops, no multi-step state — not agentic work, and therefore silent on the capability class compression takes first (compression-degrades-agentic-skills-more-than-reasoning).

  • 2026-08-12 (run 16) — Stays contested, and for once the reason it stays is interesting: the new evidence confirms the bet and confirms the standing objection to how the bet keeps getting tested, in the same paper. The boundary predicted, for the first time. Two procedures won, the one novel-artifact task lost — which is the cut this vault drew over runs 10–13 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe, open-model-trails-frontier-29-points-on-abstract-reasoning), used prospectively rather than fitted retrospectively, and holding in a domain — clinical decision support on real records — unlike any of the coding, extraction and tool-use cases it was built from. Run 15 found full-parameter warm-up causes persistent out-of-domain degradation while LoRA at rank 16–64 matches it in-domain and keeps the generalization (full-parameter-warmup-buys-in-domain-speed-and-persistent-ood-damage). Here LoRA also wins in-domain, against a commercial baseline, in a different field. Two independent arrivals from opposite directions, and our premise — workflow-specific specialists deployed beside adjacent workflows — is exactly the case where both point the same way. If we run one training arm, run LoRA. The confusion-matrix result is the protocol lesson: the tuned models catch highest-severity patients the commercial baselines miss, a per-condition finding an accuracy mean would destroy (distillation-harms-are-invisible-in-aggregate-metrics) — and note the direction, since split reporting here made the small model look better. Grade per condition regardless of which way you expect it to cut. And it is the seventh. Which is why the map does not move: the confirming evidence keeps arriving in a form that cannot settle the question. Tuned small versus prompt-optimized frontier is still an experiment we would have to run.   • 2026-08-08 (run 13) — Stays contested. A small counterexample to an assumption this map has been carrying without testing it. U-OPSD's improvement over base is 8.5% at Qwen3-4B and 10.7% at Qwen3-8B — the larger model gains more (method-gains-favour-the-8b-over-the-4b). Nearly every training and harness result on this vault's ledger runs the other way: harness design swings a 4B by 22 points, self-inspection penalties run 8.0–11.3 at 1.5B against 2.0–1.3 at 7B, SKILL-KD lifts a 4B by 23.3 and a 35B-A3B by 16.7 (textual-skill-patches-lift-a-frozen-4b-by-23-points), AgentOPSD adds more to the 3B than the 7B (turn-level-credit-adds-more-to-the-3b-than-the-7b). The inference drawn from that — interventions have most headroom where the model is weakest — is intuitive and evidently not universal. The mechanism suggests a predicate worth carrying, because it resolves a tension run 12 left open. U-OPSD's supervision is the model's own majority vote, so its input quality scales with the model: a stronger model votes better. That gives a cut — interventions that supply signal from outside the model help the weak model most; interventions that harvest signal from inside it help the strong model most. Run 12 recorded two facts that sat awkwardly together: base size outweighs technique on multi-turn tool use (1.7B → 8B worth 14.23% on BFCL and 21.4% on τ²-Bench, several times the method's own effect, tuned-small-agents-still-fail-most-realistic-tool-tasks) while method deltas were consistently largest at the small end. Both stay true, and which way a given method scales now has a candidate explanation rather than a shrug. The practical conclusion is unchanged and reinforced: our experiment needs a mid-size arm, not the smallest checkpoint that fits. Weak evidence, and it should be held that way. One method, one model family, two sizes, competition mathematics only, and the effect inverts between thinking and non-thinking modes (+0.9% at 4B and parity at 8B against supervised OPSD once the model reasons at length). The 8.5-vs-10.7 difference is small enough that unreported seed variance could account for it. This is a counterexample to a generalisation, not a rule of its own. [reviewed 2026-08-08 @ 9 bullets]

  • 2026-08-07 (run 12) — Stays contested. A quiet but load-bearing correction to how this map reads its own evidence: the parity conversation is conducted almost entirely in deltas, and the levels are low. After agentic RL with an internalized world model, a purpose-tuned Qwen3-8B reaches 46.04% on BFCL-v4, 36.7% on τ²-Bench, 16.0% on VitaBench, and 46.78% TF1 on FinMCP-Bench (tuned-small-agents-still-fail-most-realistic-tool-tasks). Those are the best numbers that paper reports for its own method, on multi-turn tool-use benchmarks built to resemble real work — and the model is failing most of the tasks. Every claim on this map is a delta: a method adds 7 points, a harness swings 22, compression retains 95%. Deltas are the right unit for isolating a lever and the wrong unit for deciding to ship, and this map has been accumulating the first while implicitly reporting the second. It cuts both ways, which is why it belongs here rather than simply against the bet. frontier-agents-top-out-at-25-percent-on-realistic-oncall-rca found the best of five frontier agents at 25.3% on realistic oncall RCA. Nobody does well on messy multi-turn work. A 16% small model reads as damning next to a leaderboard and much less so next to what the frontier alternative actually delivers on the same class of task — which is the comparison our decision turns on, and the one almost nobody runs. The standing instruction stands and gets a companion: alongside "pin both protocol variables," report levels next to every delta, because a map made of improvements cannot tell us whether anything is good enough. One number sharpens the size question this map keeps deferring. Scaling the same trained system from 1.7B to 8B is worth 14.23% on BFCL and 21.4% on τ²-Bench — several times the training method's own effect. Read against run 12's other finding that method deltas are largest at the small end (turn-level-credit-adds-more-to-the-3b-than-the-7b), the two are compatible and jointly discouraging for the smallest arm: techniques help the small model most in relative terms and still leave it behind in absolute ones. That argues directly for a mid-size arm in our own experiment rather than the smallest checkpoint that fits. Limits: preprint, one group, unreplicated, no frontier arm run alongside under the same protocol — so the level comparison against frontier is assembled across studies rather than measured.

  • 2026-08-05 (run 10) — Stays contested; a sixth parity claim arrives, and for once it draws its own boundary rather than leaving us to.   • 2026-08-03 (run 8) — Stays contested; a new parity-at-lower-cost datapoint on a genuinely high-value narrow task, carrying the theme's standing comparator caveat rather than escaping it.   • 2026-08-02 (run 7) — Stays contested; no new parity result, but the theme's comparator problem gains a mirror image that changes how we should read every head-to-head in it. The standing critique has been that the literature compares a tuned specialist against a lazy, zero-shot generalist — a bias flattering the small model (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators). The new finding runs the other way: in a token-cost-matched experiment, letting a model judge its own output cost 8.0 and 11.3 points at 1.5B but only 2.0 and 1.3 at 7B (indistinguishable from zero), and self-rewriting stayed 3.6–10.1 points below baseline even at 7B (self-judgement-penalty-is-largest-on-the-smallest-models). So a parity test run through a default SDK's reflection and self-selection loops imposes a penalty that is largest on the small model and near-zero on the large one — exactly the setup a naive head-to-head would use, biasing against the small model. Taken with run 6's harness-swing datapoints, the pattern is consistent in both directions and the conclusion is the same: a small model's measured score is not a property of the model, so a fair fight has to fix two protocol variables, comparator tuning and scaffold, not one. Second, weaker touch on the training side: distillation acquired a fourth named precondition — the model supervising on-policy distillation must be the one that generated the SFT traces, or the run can go flat against a clean corpus (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization). Relevant here because "we distilled and it didn't reach parity" now has a boring explanation that has nothing to do with the small model's ceiling.

  • 2026-08-01 (run 6) — Stays contested; a secondary touch adds a confound the parity debate has to hold. Two harness datapoints (harness-design-is-a-large-quality-lever-on-small-models, graph-action-space-harness-lifts-small-model-retrieval) show a small model's measured accuracy on a fixed task swinging 22 points (4B classification) or 10.74 F1 (1.5B retrieval QA) with the weights unchanged and only the harness varied. The fair fight is not merely missing, it is being avoided, and no accumulation of results in this style will settle our decision.
