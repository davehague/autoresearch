Do fine-tuned small models match bigger models on narrow tasks?

The central bet of the whole vault: can a fine-tuned small model own a specific workflow as well as a much larger (or frontier) model? This is where the train-vs-buy decision lives.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-02 (run 7) — Stays contested; no new parity result, but the theme's comparator problem gains a mirror image that changes how we should read every head-to-head in it. The standing critique has been that the literature compares a tuned specialist against a lazy, zero-shot generalist — a bias flattering the small model (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators). The new finding runs the other way: in a token-cost-matched experiment, letting a model judge its own output cost 8.0 and 11.3 points at 1.5B but only 2.0 and 1.3 at 7B (indistinguishable from zero), and self-rewriting stayed 3.6–10.1 points below baseline even at 7B (self-judgement-penalty-is-largest-on-the-smallest-models). So a parity test run through a default SDK's reflection and self-selection loops imposes a penalty that is largest on the small model and near-zero on the large one — exactly the setup a naive head-to-head would use, biasing against the small model. Taken with run 6's harness-swing datapoints, the pattern is consistent in both directions and the conclusion is the same: a small model's measured score is not a property of the model, so a fair fight has to fix two protocol variables, comparator tuning and scaffold, not one. Second, weaker touch on the training side: distillation acquired a fourth named precondition — the model supervising on-policy distillation must be the one that generated the SFT traces, or the run can go flat against a clean corpus (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization). Relevant here because "we distilled and it didn't reach parity" now has a boring explanation that has nothing to do with the small model's ceiling.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 6) — Stays contested; a secondary touch adds a confound the parity debate has to hold. Two harness datapoints (harness-design-is-a-large-quality-lever-on-small-models, graph-action-space-harness-lifts-small-model-retrieval) show a small model's measured accuracy on a fixed task swinging 22 points (4B classification) or 10.74 F1 (1.5B retrieval QA) with the weights unchanged and only the harness varied. The parity question — "can a fine-tuned small model match a bigger one?" — assumes the small model's score is a property of the model, but a large slice of it is a property of the harness it was measured under. This does not move the theme, but it means any parity or non-parity result run through a default SDK is confounded by scaffold quality on the small-model side, and the author's framing is the sharp version: "this model is bad at X" is often "my harness is bad at X."
  • 2026-08-01 (run 5) — Stays contested, and one of the theme's standing caveats hardened into a finding. Two new parity datapoints arrived, both narrow. Sub-2B Qwen3 models with a discriminative classification head passed the published numbers for GPT-3, PaLM 540B and GPT-4 on five multiple-choice benchmarks, trained on a single L4 overnight (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators); and a tuned 4B matched frontier resolve rates on terminal execution, though only as a subagent inside a frontier-driven loop (small-execution-subagent-cuts-frontier-tokens). The hardened caveat: the multiple-choice comparators are zero-shot GPT-3/PaLM/GPT-4 — one to two generations stale, in the weakest configuration — and the authors state they never tried few-shot. That makes five sources in this vault claiming parity against an untuned, unoptimized generalist. What began as a caveat on individual results is now a property of the literature: the field systematically compares a tuned specialist to a lazy generalist. The fair fight is not merely missing, it is being avoided, and no accumulation of results in this style will settle our decision. Note also a genuine methodological gain buried in the weaker paper — reframing a verifiable task as discriminative classification rather than generation is worth 2–3pp for free at deployable sizes, which is a lever we control independent of model choice.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 4) — Both sides gained real weight, so contested holds — but the shape of the dispute changed. For: the single-benchmark objection is largely answered — fine-tuned 360M–3B models beat GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 across nine benchmarks in two domains on relation extraction (0.83 vs 0.69/0.66 general; 0.833 vs 0.578 literary) (subbillion-finetuned-beats-frontier-across-nine-benchmarks). Against: distillation is shown to close the gap only under conditions, with three named failure modes and an explicit "small models cannot universally replicate large teacher performance" (distillation-does-not-universally-close-the-gap). These are compatible, and together they sharpen the theme into something more useful than a yes/no: parity is real and reproducible inside a task class (narrow, structured, strong teacher, clean data) and does not generalise out of it. Two live caveats on the parity result: the frontier comparators were zero-shot (tuned specialist vs untuned generalist), and relation extraction is the task class most favourable to tuning.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 3) — The missing evidence class arrived: two published head-to-heads of a fine-tuned small model vs a proprietary frontier model, in different domains — an 8B specialist on par with GPT-4o in astronomy (finetuned-8b-matches-gpt4o-in-astronomy) and a fine-tuned Phi-3.5 Mini comparable to GPT-4o on enterprise search labeling (finetuned-slm-matches-frontier-enterprise-labeling). Parity side now clearly stronger. Still contested, deliberately: both are single-benchmark, single-domain, and the construct-validity critique applies to any one benchmark — no cross-domain or contamination-controlled comparison yet.
  • 2026-08-01 (run 2) — Added a workflow-level datapoint: open-weight models run locally as coding agents are a real alternative to subscription frontier services (open-models-viable-for-real-local-workflows). This is the kind of evidence the skeptic asked for — usefulness in a real loop, not a leaderboard — so it strengthens the parity side without resolving the contamination critique. Stays contested: still no controlled head-to-head vs a proprietary frontier model.
  • 2026-08-01 — Opened, and set contested on day one. Evidence for: a fine-tuned Qwen3-4B matched or beat a 30×-larger teacher on 8/9 narrow benchmarks (finetuned-4b-matches-30x-larger-model). Credible pushback against, landing the same night: benchmark parity lacks construct validity and is inflated by contamination, so leaderboard parity ≠ doing the job (benchmark-parity-claims-lack-construct-validity, which rebuts the parity claim). Note the parity result's teacher was a large open model, not a proprietary frontier one — so "matches frontier" is not yet shown.

Sub-threads: the "domain head-to-heads vs frontier" thread now spans six domains (astronomy, enterprise search, general + literary relation extraction, commonsense multiple choice, terminal execution) and has earned its own map — but splitting it would inherit the zero-shot-comparator flaw, so the more useful split may be by comparator quality rather than by domain. The conditions for parity thread remains the decision-useful one. A third thread now stands on its own and has grown a second half: the comparator-protocol critique is no longer a footnote on individual results but a systematic property of this literature — and as of run 7 it is two-sided. The published protocol flatters the small model on comparator tuning and penalises it on scaffold, and the two biases are not guaranteed to cancel. Any fair fight we design has to pin both.
