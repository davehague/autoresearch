The answer splits by axis

The question worth asking isn't "small model or frontier model." It's "a small model plus a purpose-built harness, versus a frontier model plus its default SDK, on the same work." For one class of work, that comparison now has a published answer — and it's a split decision: the pairing wins on cost by two orders of magnitude and loses on quality by single digits.

The head-to-head

Compiling Agentic Workflows into LLM Weights (arXiv 2605.22502) runs the comparison directly. It takes three task-oriented dialogue procedures of rising complexity — travel booking (14 nodes), Zoom support (14 nodes plus product knowledge), and insurance claims (55 nodes, 2,381 paths) — and races three configurations on each: a small model with the procedure fine-tuned into its weights, the same procedure driven by a LangGraph orchestrator over Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 handed the procedure in context. Two hundred conversations per condition, scored 1–5, replicated under a second judge. The compiled model was Qwen2.5-3B on the simplest procedure and Qwen3-8B on the other two.

Against the orchestrated frontier — the closest published stand-in for "frontier plus a default SDK" — the compiled small model won graceful handling 4.81 to 4.38 and naturalness 4.92 to 4.58 on the hardest procedure (both p<0.001), and cut failure rates from 24% to 5.5% on travel and 17% to 9% on insurance. Against frontier in-context, it reached 87–98% of quality on the two larger procedures. Per conversation it cost $0.0007 versus $0.327.

The detail that makes this trustworthy is the ablation: the compiled 3B was also raced against the same 3B base model under the same explicit orchestration, and beat it on all five criteria. That isolates the compilation from the model size — a control that vendor benchmarks varying model and harness together can't offer.

Read it one axis at a time

On cost, the pairing wins, and wins harder as work gets harder — 128× on the simplest procedure, 462× on the hardest. Two multipliers compound: roughly 65× per token from self-hosting an 8B instead of calling Claude, and 2–7× on token volume, because a compiled model's prompt is a fixed size while an orchestrated prompt grows with every node and branch it carries. Complexity, which is supposed to erode a small model's edge, widens it.

On quality, the frontier still wins — narrowly, but the in-context baseline takes every quality column on every procedure. "Near-frontier" means losing by a little. The residual gap sits in information accuracy, not procedure execution: compiling a procedure doesn't compile world knowledge, so any task needing broad recall keeps a frontier-shaped hole.

On flexibility, the usual objection fades. The standard complaint about tuned models is that they ossify — change the procedure and you're back in a training cycle. Here the full recompile (synthetic data, fine-tune, eval) runs 30–50 minutes on 8×H200. That's a CI build, not a research project.

The same shape from other angles

The pattern shows up from unrelated methods. Don't Blame the Large Language Model: How Agent Harness Evolution Shapes Coding Agent Quality (arXiv 2607.03691) tracked 35 harness releases and found resolve rate flat while token consumption rose 70% — the scaffold moving economics, not accuracy. And Terminus-4B (arXiv 2605.03195) put a 4B execution subagent underneath a frontier planner and cut the planner's token use 21–32% and its terminal calls 62–79% with resolve rate flat, by keeping verbose shell output out of the planner's context. Note the caveat: that paper was withdrawn from arXiv on 1 July for product-IP reasons — not retracted for error, but no longer distributed or independently checkable, so treat it as a single unverifiable datapoint.

Across a compilation study, a longitudinal harness study, and a subagent study, the finding rhymes: the small-model-plus-custom-scaffold move buys economics, and the frontier keeps a narrow quality edge.

Where it breaks down

Every result above sits on node-graph workflows — dialogue with enumerable paths, the most compilable work there is. A 55-node, 2,381-path procedure is complex, but it's finite and known in advance. Most real workflows aren't, and the one controlled study of open-ended coding is exactly the one that found harness work quality-neutral. You can't compile a procedure you can't enumerate. Whether procedure compilation survives contact with unbounded tasks is untested, and the mechanism gives real reason for doubt.

The cost side has its own counterweight. A four-term TCO analysis — Self-Hosting Frontier AI Models: 2026 TCO Analysis (digitalapplied.com) — puts the break-even for self-hosting frontier-class open weights at 600M tokens/month for code and 1.2B for chat, and only with a dedicated engineer; below that the API wins, sometimes by 30×. That looks like it contradicts the 462× figure until you notice they price different objects: a compiled 3B and a self-hosted 70B are not the same proposition. "Is self-hosting cheaper" is unanswerable until you say which model. Three numbers from the pessimistic side are worth carrying into any estimate: realistic utilization under P99 sizing is 35–45%, not the ~70% back-of-envelope models assume; the inference engineer is 25–30% of TCO and never amortises; and every frontier release forces one to two weeks of engineering every four to eight weeks — a recurring tax on riding the open ecosystem, not pure upside. (This is a vendor-adjacent blog analysis, not peer-reviewed; weight it accordingly.)

The honest current position

For work you can write down as a procedure, a compiled small model plus a custom harness beats frontier-plus-defaults on cost by ~100–460× and trails it on quality by single digits, and re-compiling to a changed procedure costs under an hour. What's unproven is whether any of that transfers to work you can't enumerate in advance — which is most real work. That's the experiment still left to run.

One caveat about the parity evidence generally: the strongest small-vs-frontier wins so far are measured against zero-shot, unoptimized frontier baselines. More results in that style won't move the decision — the informative comparison is against a frontier model that's also been given a serious harness.

Open questions

  • Procedure compilation on open-ended or agentic work — untested, with reasons for doubt.
  • The hybrid setup (frontier planner, tuned small specialists) needs a second datapoint that hasn't been withdrawn.
  • End-to-end cost including curation and training — unpriced by anyone, on either side.
