1f:["$","h2","h2-3",{"children":"Where this leaves a practical decision"}]
20:["$","p","p-16",{"children":"If you are deciding whether to wrap a cheaper model in better software or simply buy a more capable one, three things here are usable."}]
21:["$","p","p-17",{"children":"Scaffolding pays in proportion to how much the model is currently failing. AI4AI measured this directly — a weaker target gained 0.262 on average, a stronger one 0.110, with available headroom predicting uplift at r=0.75 — and DarwinX's three benchmarks line up the same way: 7.7 points of gain on a base at 75.5%, 49.5 on a base at 43.5%. 22:["$","p","p-18",{"children":"That rule has a limit, and SKILLER found it. Its skill libraries helped a 9B model by 4.3 to 20.4 points and a 4B model by only 1.8 to 13.3 — even though the 4B had more room to improve on all five benchmarks tested. A written procedure only helps a model that can already follow procedures. Scaffolding that removes work from the model pays out where failures are; scaffolding that hands the model more instructions needs a floor of competence first."}]
