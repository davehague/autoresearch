The optimizer was Opus 4.6, the transfer test was Haiku 4.5, and no open-weight model appears anywhere

AutoSaddler's +9.0 / +9.6 / +10.0 across GAIA2, SWE-Bench Pro and Terminal-Bench 2.0 is a frontier-on-frontier measurement. Claude Opus 4.6 ran the optimization; cross-model transferability was tested on Claude Haiku 4.5. Both closed, both from one vendor's family.

For this map — the pairing of a small or open model with a custom harness — that leaves the load-bearing question untouched, and the vault has two conflicting priors for it:

  • Run 27 measured structural harness search carrying to the open model at four-fifths of its frontier strength (structural-harness-search-carries-to-the-open-model-at-four-fifths).
  • Runs 25 and 26 measured instruction-shaped fixes carrying at about one third (the-prompt-level-fix-recovers-three-times-less-on-the-open-model, with the disclosure result at zero, the-open-weight-model-is-the-one-that-cannot-use-the-disclosure).

AutoSaddler patches the harness as code, which puts it on the structural side and argues for the four-fifths prior — roughly 7–8 points on an open target rather than 9–10. But that is a prior, not a reading, and this run's other harness paper lands on the opposite side: open-models-captured-half-the-harness-uplift-frontier-models-did finds an open 27B taking 8.3 points where a frontier model at similar headroom took 17.8 on the same benchmark.

The experiment that would settle it is small. Re-run AutoSaddler's loop with an open-weight model as the target — the optimizer can stay frontier, since the patches are code and the vault's four-fifths result was measured exactly that way. Until someone does, the method is a frontier-model capability with an assumed discount, and the assumed discount ranges from 20% to 67% depending on which of the vault's two mechanisms applies.

Discounts: one paper, one vendor's model family, no open arm to reason from at all; "cross-model transferability" tested only within that family, which is the weakest possible transfer test; and the vault's four-fifths and one-third figures come from different benchmarks and different intervention shapes, so neither is a clean prior for this one.
