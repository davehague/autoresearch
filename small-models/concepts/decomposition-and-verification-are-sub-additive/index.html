Task decomposition and deliverable verification each buy 3.3 points alone and 5.0 together — they are fixing the same failure

OneDayAgent's 2×2 ablation on AgentIF-OneDay, GLM-5.2 backend:

VariantOverall
DIRECT (neither)0.771
DECOMP only0.804
VERIFY only0.804
FULL (both)0.821

+3.3 each, +5.0 combined. Verification cost 2.2 minutes of added latency; 95 of 104 tasks passed it on first attempt; 35 of 104 triggered context compression. The full harness scored highest and had the lowest score-per-latency ratio of the four.

Two readings, and the second is the one that changes plans.

The straightforward one supports run 9's build rule. Verification here reads the final deliverable — an artifact outside the model — and repairs it. That is a loop with an external referent, and it earns its keep on open-ended day-long work rather than on verifiable short answers, which is where environment-verification-succeeds-where-self-inspection-fails needed a second instance. It also arrives with a cost figure, at least in latency, which is the axis external-task-state-lifts-long-horizon-agents left blank.

The sub-additivity is the more useful finding. If decomposition and verification addressed distinct failures the gains would stack near +6.6; they deliver +5.0, so roughly a quarter of each module's benefit overlaps the other's. The natural explanation is that both are compensating for one underlying failure — the model losing the plot over a long horizon — decomposition by preventing the drift and verification by catching it afterwards. That predicts diminishing returns from stacking further long-horizon machinery, and it argues for choosing the cheaper of the two rather than buying both: at 0.804 apiece, DECOMP-only or VERIFY-only captures two-thirds of the full gain, and VERIFY costs 2.2 minutes.

It also puts a ceiling on this whole class of intervention. The gap between no modules and both modules is 5.0 points on a 0–1 rubric, against the tens of points that designed-ablation results elsewhere in this map report — 22 points from harness layout on a 4B classifier (harness-design-is-a-large-quality-lever-on-small-models), 28.9 points from external task state (external-task-state-lifts-long-horizon-agents). The difference is what the baseline already contains: DIRECT is not a bare model, it is this harness's scaffolding minus two modules, and it already scores 0.771. So the large numbers in this map may be measuring scaffold vs no scaffold, and the small ones module vs scaffold — a distinction the map should hold before treating any of them as comparable.

Limits: one backend for the ablation, one benchmark, the authors' own rubric, no repeat runs or intervals, and no token cost — only latency, which does not price ~87 backend calls per task.
