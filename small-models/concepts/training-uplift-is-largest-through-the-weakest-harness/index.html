Train one open model through three shipped coding harnesses and the gain is largest through the weakest one — the headroom law arriving at the training stage

Run 22 varied the harness while training a model into it and found the stronger harness gained more: Qwen3-30A3B picked up +14.81 through Claude Code against +9.98 through OpenClaw (the-stronger-harness-was-the-better-training-surface). The reading recorded then was mechanistic and appealing — a harness that fails informatively produces trajectories with more learnable structure — and it went into the pairing map as the reason the two levers might compound after all.

LEGO-RL runs the same shape of experiment with three arms instead of two and gets the opposite ordering. Holding Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the 2,699-task training index, the 200K-token context budget and the 126 training steps fixed, and varying only the harness:

harnessbeforeafteruplift
OpenHands SDK64.0%70.4%+6.4
Claude Code62.4%68.2%+5.8
OpenCode57.2%66.6%+9.4

Uplift sorts inversely to starting score, monotonically. The final ordering survives — OpenHands still finishes ahead — but the spread between best and worst harness narrows from 6.8 points to 3.8. Training bought back nearly half of what choosing the weaker harness cost.

That is the headroom law, which three inference-time studies established across a fifty-point uplift range and seven agent configurations (harness-uplift-and-base-score-are-inversely-related-across-seven-agents, the-headroom-law-replicates-across-a-fifty-point-uplift-range, harness-uplift-tracks-headroom-not-model-size), showing up for the first time when the intervention is a gradient rather than a prompt. Run 23's Agent Lightning result pointed the same way without controlling anything — +14.6 points through mini-SWE-agent, a deliberately minimal harness (a-9b-gains-14-6-points-on-swe-bench-through-a-minimal-harness). This controls it.

The practical consequence reverses run 22's. If the training surface has to be good, harnessed agentic RL inherits the harness-building cost the paradigm was supposed to delete. If the gain is largest where the harness is weakest, you train into whatever you will deploy in, and the harness's job is to produce in-distribution trajectories rather than to be excellent.

Nothing is formally refuted: different benchmark (SWE-bench Verified against ClawGym-Bench), different algorithm (GSPO against PPO/GRPO over prefix trees), different model. But this vault reasons with the loose version, and the better-controlled arm points at headroom.

Grade: suggestive — a three-arm comparison with the model, task index, step count and context budget all pinned is the tightest control this question has had, and the ordering is monotonic. It is not strong because there are no repeats or intervals on any arm, the three harnesses differ in more than strength, and one paper's three points cannot outweigh another paper's two by itself.
