Which skill the agent opens is its own trainable capability — a 9B trained on the choice beats models with forty times the parameters that were not

Every skill result this map holds improves the library: compress it 346× (skill-libraries-compress-346x-and-score-higher), validate it so the smallest wins (textual-skill-patches-lift-a-frozen-4b-by-23-points), price it at $8.95 (a-skill-library-for-a-compact-model-costs-nine-dollars-to-build), move it upstream into training so the deployed student ships an empty prompt (skill-text-can-supervise-training-and-be-dropped-at-inference). None of them trains the decision of which entry to open mid-episode.

SkillGate does, and reports that the decision is where the value sits. On five agentic benchmarks under a fixed 16-candidate slate, a Qwen3.5-9B policy goes 40.8% → 53.2% trial success. The matched control is the same training budget spent on outcome reward alone — 47.0% — so 6.2 of the 12.4 points come from crediting the choice specifically rather than from training at all.

The scale comparison is the sharper half. The paper's own 27B backbone at 3× the parameters does not produce a reliable selector, and neither do frontier references at roughly forty times the parameters (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GLM-5, Kimi-K2.6, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B and others, served through their providers' APIs). General capability compensates for a missed skill; it does not stop the miss. On the ablation's own framing: choosing the right skill is a distinct capability that must be trained directly rather than expected to emerge from scale.

The credit-design ablation isolates why nothing else works. Five placements of the selector signal, same initialisation, same 100 steps: nowhere 42.1%, the prompt group 41.8%, the whole trajectory 41.8%, the first oracle read 45.0%, the only read if oracle 50.0%. Broadcasting the signal at trajectory level moves clean-oracle behaviour from 21.4% to 33.9% and moves success not at all. Only credit landing on the identity tokens changes the choice.

For the pairing this is the first intervention whose two halves cooperate by construction: the training exists only because the library exists. Run 23's Agentic ESOpt result — a training pass buying 36% as much when a skill library is already installed (training-uplift-falls-to-a-third-when-a-skill-library-is-already-installed) — is the case against stacking; this is the shape of intervention that would evade it, because it trains the interface rather than the task.
