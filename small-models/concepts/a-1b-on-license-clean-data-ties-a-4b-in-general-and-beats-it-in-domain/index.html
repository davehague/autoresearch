Three benchmark families, three different winners — the boundary predicate's cleanest single-table instance

DFM Mimir v1 is a 1B-parameter Hierarchical Reasoning Model trained on 161 permissibly-licensed datasets. Its results against a 4× larger general model and a 2.3B-effective one:

Family (n tasks)Mimir 1BHRM-Text 1BQwen 3.5 4BGemma 4 E2B
English (7)69.066.169.356.6
Math & Code (3)64.146.965.075.4
Danish (10)56.821.749.244.6

Read as one result rather than three: the 1B ties on general knowledge and comprehension at a quarter of the parameters, wins by 7.6 on the domain its data was curated for, and loses by 11.3 on math and code. The authors name the last one as a limitation themselves.

That is the cut this vault drew over runs 10–13 and first applied prospectively in run 16 (lora-tuned-open-slms-beat-commercial-baselines-on-two-clinical-tasks-of-three): assignment into a specified target distribution wins; producing a correct novel artifact loses. It has now held across coding (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe), extraction, tool use, clinical decision support, shell commands (the-eighth-parity-claim-and-its-margin-is-inside-the-noise) and Danish-language NLP — six domains, four size classes, and here for the first time with all three outcomes visible inside one table from one training run, which removes the between-study confounds that made earlier instances arguable.

The practical value is that the predicate is free to apply. Run 15 queued a three-column sort of our workflows into horizon / context / persistence (long-horizon-long-context-and-long-term-memory-are-three-independent-properties) and run 17 queued a headroom measurement (harness-uplift-tracks-headroom-not-model-size); this predicate runs over the same workflow list in the same sitting, needs no model experiment, and its track record is now the best on the board.

Note what this instance does not replicate: the comparator here is not zero-shot in the usual sense — Qwen 3.5 4B and Gemma 4 E2B are shipped instruct models on standard benchmarks, not a tuned specialist against an untuned generalist on the specialist's own task. The Danish win is a curated-data win, which is a weaker and more honest claim than a tuning win.

Grade: single-study — one preprint, no repeats or intervals, author-chosen comparators, only family averages visible (a 7-task mean can hide a 20-point per-task spread), Gemma 4 E2B evaluated non-thinking on the families where it loses, and Danish is the authors' home domain with a partly self-authored benchmark suite. HRM is also not a transformer, so the recipe does not transfer to a Qwen- or Llama-family plan without re-derivation.
