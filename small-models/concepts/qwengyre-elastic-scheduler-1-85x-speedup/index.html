QwenGyre's elastic GPU scheduler and trajectory deduplication add a scheduling-and-redundancy training-cost lever this thread hasn't logged in this shape, worth 1.85x wall-clock and a 6-point benchmark gain on a 2.4T/95B-active model

Every training-cost lever this thread has tracked so far changes what the model trains on or how the distillation/RL objective is shaped — pruning, layer dropout, evidence-graph grounding, a dozen-plus on-policy-distillation mechanisms. QwenGyre changes neither: its Elastic Scheduler reallocates GPUs between rollout and training live, without interrupting executions already running, fixing the specific pathology extreme-long-horizon (XLong) agent training creates — a single rollout can run hours and consume close to 1M tokens, leaving GPUs idle while they wait. Its Trajectory Processor turns branching rollouts (compaction, sub-agent spawns, retries) into prefix-shared trees with bounded, deduplicated samples, scoring partial progress even on a timed-out rollout rather than discarding it.

On Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B at ~700K tokens/rollout: 1.85x faster than colocated scheduling, 1.78x faster than async, and NL2RepoBench rising 52.5%→58.5% in 48 steps — roughly 44% less wall-clock time than async scheduling to reach that gain. The speedup figures describe infrastructure efficiency (GPUs kept busy), not a new accuracy mechanism; the accuracy gain is reached faster because the scheduler stops wasting compute on idle GPUs and redundant trajectory branches.
