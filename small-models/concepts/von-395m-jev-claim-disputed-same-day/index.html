A 395M open "Jev replacement" claiming to beat its proprietary target on the releaser's own benchmarks gets a same-day independent synthetic test putting it 5 points behind (92.3% vs. 97.4%) — the fastest-cycle instance yet of this map's vendor-claim-vs-independent-test split, with commenters separately questioning whether two years of funded R&D should be replicable in days

Von, a 395M-parameter, CPU-runnable open model released as a drop-in replacement for TypeSafe's proprietary Jev "System One" classifier, is claimed by its author to beat Jev on their own benchmarks. Within the same comment thread, one practitioner ran an independent synthetic test suite across Von, Jev, GLiNER2, and a fourth model (Laya), finding Von at 92.3% task accuracy against Jev's 97.4% — a 5-point gap in the opposite direction from the release's claim. Separately, commenters note that near-identical "drop-in Jev replacement" releases have surfaced repeatedly within days of each other despite Jev reportedly representing roughly two years of funded research, questioning the plausibility of the underlying narrow-task-parity claims on replication-speed grounds alone rather than benchmark grounds.

This is a faster and thinner instance of the vendor-claim-vs-independent-test pattern this map has logged on the quantization side (Bonsai 2's 98.2% vendor claim vs. 91.4-91.7% independent re-score, byteshape-bonsai2-independent-benchmark-below-unsloth-iq3xxs) and the narrow-task-parity side (a 35B beating five larger models on its own workflow, then losing every neighboring evaluation): here the independent counter-test landed the same day as the release, on an anonymous developer's model, with no public benchmark on either side.
