"First open-source on-device 145B model" is, per its own Hugging Face metadata, a derivative of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next — undisclosed in the launch post

Edge0 launches Edge0-145B as "the world's first open-source on-device AI model with 145 billion parameters," reporting 29GB peak memory and 80+ tok/s decode on a laptop, plus internal benchmarks edging out Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 on DeepSWE 1.1, Terminal-Bench 2.1, and IFBench. The launch post names no base model. A r/LocalLLaMA commenter (33 points) reports that the Hugging Face repo's own JSON config lists "base_model": "Qwen3.8-Flash-Next", labeled there as a quant rather than a fine-tune — visible only to someone who opens the config file, not to a reader of the announcement itself. Other top comments ("Another Qwen tune!", "finetune of fine-tunes, fork of forks") treat this as an open secret in the community rather than a surprise, which is itself informative: readers who know to check config files catch it, readers who don't get a capability claim ("145B," "first," "frontier capabilities") detached from its actual lineage.

This is a live instance of the provenance gap this vault flagged in run 30 (Ornith-1.5-Heretic's abliteration costing 12 points with no trace in parameter count, quant label, or file size): a model's marketing identity and its verifiable lineage are separable, and the gap between them is where a reader's assessment of "how much new capability is this" actually goes wrong. Edge0's benchmark numbers may be entirely real — they're unreproduced, not disputed — but whatever delta they show is a quant/fine-tune-of-known-base delta, not a from-scratch 145B model's delta, and nothing in the announcement would tell a reader that.
