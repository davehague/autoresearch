Checking a synthetic training task is solvable is not enough — calibrating its difficulty against a pool of solvers is worth up to 30 points

CalibForge's claim is that the standard synthesis recipe for agent training data — author, execute, verify solvable — produces tasks that are feasible but not learnable, because feasibility says nothing about difficulty relative to the model being trained. It revises candidates against observed solver behaviour two ways: multi-solver calibration steers generation into the disagreement band of a heterogeneous solver pool, and contrastive calibration targets an explicit strong-pass / weak-fail relation. From that it builds 5,431 calibrated terminal tasks. Ablations report both strategies beating authoring-and-validation alone and beating ordinary single-solver feedback; largest gains over the corresponding base model reach +24.71 points on Terminal-Bench 2.0, +27.68 on SWE-bench Pro and +30.04 on Doc2Repo, with the last two off the synthesis target.

This supplies the missing input to the build order run 12 assembled. That order — log frontier traces where our small model fails, diff against the failure, patch, re-run to validate, prune, condition a teacher, distil (skill-text-can-supervise-training-and-be-dropped-at-inference) — has an unstated dependency: a corpus of tasks our model fails on instructively. We do not have one, and every method on this map assumes it. Here is a published procedure for manufacturing it, at a corpus size (5,431) that is a week of cluster time rather than a research programme.

The mechanism is this vault's recurring one, now on its fifth instance: the usable signal is a difference between two runs. SA-OPD compares teacher and student with and without the prompt; AgentOPSD compares them turn by turn; SKILL-KD diffs a student failure against a teacher success; this diffs a strong solver against a weak one to locate difficulty (input-ablation-detects-spurious-distillation-supervision, turn-level-credit-adds-more-to-the-3b-than-the-7b, textual-skill-patches-lift-a-frozen-4b-by-23-points). It is also a clean instance of the corrected referent rule: solver disagreement is an internal referent, but every candidate is first gated by actual execution in a terminal, which is as external as this vault gets — the same thin-external-anchor-over-internal-generation structure as internal-referents-are-cheap-and-small-not-worthless, buying large numbers here rather than small ones.

Limits, and one of them caps how far this can travel. The base and trained model identities and sizes are not stated, so whether this is a small-model result at all is unknown — the exact ambiguity that has bitten this vault before. "Largest improvements" is a maximum over configurations rather than a mean, so the headline deltas are best-case; the two trained configurations report 32.58% and 47.57% on the same benchmark with no stated cause for the spread. No cost accounting for generating or calibrating 5,431 tasks, and no comparison against training on human-authored tasks at equal budget. Single preprint, unreplicated.
