The size story resolves into a headroom law — harness uplift tracks how much the target was failing, not how small it is

Run 9 left this open explicitly: three vault measurements said harness effects are largest at 1.5B–7B, one relayed study said benefit peaks at the middle tier, and nobody had plotted the curve (harness-benefit-peaks-at-middle-tier-models). AI4AI plots it and finds no peak. The weaker target (GPT-5.4-mini) gained +0.262 on average across scaffolding conditions; the stronger target (Gemini-3.5-flash) gained +0.110; and available headroom predicts uplift at Pearson r=0.75. For the stronger target, 96% of its macro uplift (0.42) came from a single benchmark — the one where it still had room.

That is a better-specified variable than "model tier," and it dissolves the disagreement rather than picking a side: a mid-tier peak is what you observe when task difficulty is fixed and the small models are failing for reasons a scaffold cannot fix while the large ones have nothing left to gain. Replace the axis with headroom and both observations fall on one line.

Two consequences, and they point in opposite directions for us.

The uncomfortable one: the more capable the small model we start from, the less the harness will buy. Every argument in this vault for pairing a strong scaffold with a small open model has implicitly assumed the harness contribution is a property of the pairing. It is a property of the residual. If we pick a stronger open checkpoint to close the model gap, we spend the harness gain doing it — which is not obviously a bad trade, but it is a trade, and nobody here has been treating it as one.

The useful one: headroom is measurable before committing to anything. Run 15 already queued a three-column sort of our workflows into horizon / context / persistence (long-horizon-long-context-and-long-term-memory-are-three-independent-properties). This adds a cheaper prior step that needs no model experiment at all — run our candidate small model on our tasks as shipped and record where it fails. The failure rate is the predicted harness uplift, at r=0.75. Tasks where it already succeeds are tasks where scaffolding is wasted effort, and tasks it fails completely may be the ones a scaffold can move most.

Limits: two target models is two points on a correlation reported across conditions, not a curve across model sizes; both targets are closed small frontier models, so the extrapolation to an open 4B–30B checkpoint is unmeasured; and the headroom being measured is headroom on Theory-of-Mind multiple choice, where a scaffold has unusually much to offer (eight-builders-chose-deterministic-code-over-verification-loops). A ceiling effect at the top of a bounded benchmark also produces r=0.75 without any interesting mechanism, and the paper does not rule that out.
