RouterDC ranks first or tenth of eleven depending on a quality/cost weight the paper chose — a sixth confound, and it swings harder than the grader or the effort dial

Sweeping the quality/cost weighting rather than reporting one operating point, LLMRouter finds router rankings fully invert: RouterDC is first on generic tasks under quality-only scoring and tenth of eleven under the most cost-sensitive setting. MLPRouter runs the other way on vision — weak when quality dominates, optimal at higher cost weights. Lightweight routers become more competitive as the budget tightens, so the cheap router is not merely cheaper, it is better ranked.

This is the same defect class as grader-model-is-a-fourth-variable-that-has-to-be-pinned (a judge swap moved scores 3–4 points on a 0.8-point gap) and reasoning-effort-moves-one-model-15-points (a runtime dial worth more than the separation between adjacent models), and the swing here is larger than either: first to tenth. Any "best router" claim — including this paper's own 14.6% (routing-across-models-is-learnable-routing-a-model-across-itself-is-not) — is a claim at an operating point that is usually unstated.

The operational consequence is small and unavoidable: the quality/cost weight is a pinned parameter of any routing experiment we run, and the router gets selected at our weight, not at a paper's. Since the vault's own position is that the unit is cost per completed task under a price we control, our weight is not the literature's default and the published ordering should be assumed not to transfer.

Second-order: this is the sixth variable on the pin-or-the-number-has-no-sign list — harness, precision, grader, reasoning effort, endpoint (api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points), and now the cost weight. Five of the six are runtime or reporting choices that leave no trace in the artifact. That is the accumulating reason this vault keeps concluding that the expensive kind of evidence — a task-split measurement with everything pinned — is the only kind worth commissioning.
