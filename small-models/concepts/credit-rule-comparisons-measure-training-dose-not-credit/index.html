Seven training arms, one of them the causal ground truth, and what separated the checkpoints was how many examples each rule happened to keep

Qwen2.5-7B, offline LoRA on 128 held-out tasks, seven pre-registered arms: outcome-only, implicit credit, judge credit, two shuffled controls, an inverted control, and a replay-truth arm built from the causal ground truth itself. No arm reliably outperforms the untrained policy at 0.422. All six confirmatory comparisons stay inconclusive inside a frozen ±3-point equivalence band under Holm correction — and the design's minimum detectable effect was ~11.8 points, so the experiment could not have seen a realistic effect if one existed.

What the checkpoints did differ by is measurable. Between-arm Jensen–Shannon divergence 0.0520 against within-arm 0.0198 (p = 0.0001) — a real instrument signature, fully explained by training dose. Sparser credit rules drop more turns from batches, moving optimizer steps from 112 to 88 per round. The author's rule: comparisons of credit rules must match effective sample size, or they measure dose, not credit.

This is the failure mode waiting inside every harness A/B this vault would run, because a harness component that filters, gates, prunes or routes changes the effective sample as a side effect. Context pruning cuts 73% of tokens (context-pruning-cuts-73-percent-of-tokens-with-cheap-heuristics); skill retrieval changes what reaches the model (skill-retrieval-precision-collapses-from-30-to-3-percent-as-a-library-grows); compaction thresholds differ by 23k tokens between two harnesses driving one endpoint (compaction-threshold-is-a-harness-setting-nobody-reports). Each of those alters how much the model sees, and any downstream difference is confounded with it.

The list of conditions a harness comparison has to name is now three deep: compaction policy, output ceiling, effective sample size. None of the harness ablations this vault has ingested reports any of them.
