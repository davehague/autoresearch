The frontier column in every comparison is months old by construction, which biases every parity reading toward open by an amount nobody outside those labs can measure

Nathan Lambert, on why GLM-5.3 reads as frontier-adjacent: "It is very, very likely that OpenAI and Anthropic have far better internal models than Z.ai... American companies tend to take months to release their models to the public, which massively flatters the Chinese labs." He calls release timing "likely the largest determining factor," ahead of compute efficiency, benchmark focus, and model narrowness.

This map has spent eight runs assembling a pin-list — model, harness, precision, grader, reasoning effort, endpoint, GPU, clock. Every entry describes how the experiment is run or scored. This one describes the comparator's provenance, and no discipline on our side removes it: we can pin everything about our own measurement and still be measuring against an artifact its owner shipped months after it was superseded internally.

What it does to the readings we hold. Run 12's Agentic Index put an open model 0.8 points from Opus 5 (open-weight-model-within-a-point-of-frontier-on-an-agentic-aggregate). Run 10's Databricks study found an open model statistically tied on quality on uncontaminated pull requests at $1.28 per task against $1.94 (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-private-uncontaminated-codebase). Both are readings against released frontier models, and both are load-bearing for this map's contested status. If the release lag is real, both are biased toward open — by an unknown quantity.

The correct conclusion is not that the gap is bigger. It is that the gap's size is not recoverable from public benchmarks, which is a worse epistemic position than a gap of known magnitude and one this vault has not been accounting for. Run 15's contamination finding did something similar to SWE-bench (32.67% solution leakage, a 66% solve-rate drop after filtering — a-third-of-swe-bench-successes-were-leakage); this generalizes the problem from one benchmark's data to the entire comparison class.

An instance landed in the same run. Qwen's release-day card benchmarks an August 2026 open 27B against Opus 4.6 Max, two releases behind what a buyer would actually call (an-apache-27b-posts-frontier-band-agentic-coding-numbers). That is vintage bias visible in a public artifact, and unlike the internal-model version it is free to correct: record the comparator's release date. It is absent from most of this vault's own ledger.

Limits, and they are large. This is an essay, not a measurement — the internal-model claim is unfalsifiable from outside by construction, which is both why it matters and why it cannot be graded higher. Lambert has a stated position in the open-model debate and a post-training textbook in market. And the argument cuts less far than it first appears against the results that matter most to us: Databricks' tie was measured on its own private codebase against models a buyer could actually call, so as a procurement comparison it stands regardless of what is sitting unreleased in a lab.
