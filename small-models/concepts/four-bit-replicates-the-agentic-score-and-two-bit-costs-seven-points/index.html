The cliff is between 2-bit and 1-bit, and only the agentic benchmark could see the 2-bit cost

Six Unsloth quantizations of Qwen3.8-27B, one engine (llama.cpp, 16 Aug 2026 build), F16 KV cache pinned across all arms, three benchmarks:

IFBench said 2-bit was free. GPQA Diamond said it was "a bit lower." Terminal-Bench 2.1 said it costs seven points. Only the 89-task agentic benchmark separated 2-bit from 4-bit — and that benchmark cost $2,308 of the run's ~$3,000 total.

That is the operational form of run 27's finding that quantization is an unnamed experimental condition rather than a free deployment step (a-fifth-to-a-third-of-correct-answers-flip-where-metrics-look-clean): the condition is invisible to cheap instruments. Any arm of ours that changes quantization and validates on a single-turn benchmark has validated nothing about agentic behaviour.

On the 4-bit question this partially rehabilitates a plan and only partially. Aggregate scores at Q4_K_M are indistinguishable from BF16 on all three instruments including the agentic one — 17 GB fits a 24 GB card with room for context, and in aggregate the fit is free. But run 27's result was never about aggregates: it was that a fifth to a third of individual correct answers flip while the metric stays clean, and a pass-rate comparison at n=89 cannot detect flips that cancel. Both are true — the average is safe, the individual answer is not. For throughput-graded work, take the 4-bit; for work where one specific answer must be right every time, this does not clear it.

Discounts: one practitioner, self-funded, no raw data released; per-arm scores are published as charts, so the Terminal-Bench figures above are read off a graph and should be treated as ±2 points; single run per arm, so the Wilson intervals capture binomial noise but not the run-to-run variance run 27 showed is the larger term (the-best-agent-succeeds-once-on-91-percent-and-every-time-on-25); Unsloth quants only, so quantizer is confounded with bit-width (the-quant-label-is-not-a-spec); KV cache held at F16, so the weight-precision × cache-precision interaction is untested; and no 3-bit arm, which is the band several commenters actually deploy.
