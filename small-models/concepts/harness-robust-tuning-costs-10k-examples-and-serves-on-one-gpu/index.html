The whole recipe is 10K SFT examples, 4K RL tasks, and it serves at P95 8.1s on one H20

The published cost of taking an open 35B-A3B from 80.3 to 94.8 on a production workflow, and keeping it robust to harness changes:

  • 10K SFT examples, collected from real live-streaming traffic
  • 4K RL interaction tasks
  • Three stages: HSA-SFT → General On-Policy Distillation → HSA-RL
  • Serving: BF16 on a single NVIDIA H20, TP=PP=1, with a NextN MTP draft head grafted from the base checkpoint
  • Latency in production: P50 3.4s, P95 8.1s

Four figures of examples, not nine. This is the cheapest data bill on this map by two orders of magnitude on either axis. Run 30's domain specialist consumed 200B tokens and 35,207 B200 GPU-hours to move 2.9 points (the-first-published-invoice-for-building-a-domain-specialist-is-35207-b200-hours). Run 31's multi-teacher distillation recipe fits on eight A100s (a-full-multi-teacher-distillation-recipe-fits-on-eight-a100s) and is still a distillation run over a corpus. This is 14,000 items total.

The reason it is cheap is the reason it is narrow, and the two cannot be separated: the data is real traffic from a workflow the trainer already operates. There is no corpus to license, no environment to synthesise (run 21 priced a verified RL environment at under 30K tokens each, a-verified-rl-environment-costs-under-thirty-thousand-tokens), and no labeling step, because the live-stream interaction is the trajectory. The precondition for this recipe is owning a workflow that already runs, at volume, with logs. That is a specific and checkable test to apply before costing any training here, and it is a different precondition from the ones this map has been tracking.

The GPU-hours are the conspicuous omission. Three training stages including an RL phase on a 35B-A3B is not free, and no figure is published — so this is a data cost, not a training cost, and it should be quoted that way.

The serving line is the more transferable one. A 35B-A3B in BF16 on one H20 — not a cluster, not eight cards — at P50 3.4s, meeting a latency budget the paper says "forces a compact policy model" and excludes the frontier APIs it beats on the task. Run 31 recorded the open model that is 3.1 points off the frontier needing 160–200 GB resident (an-mit-licensed-open-model-is-three-points-off-the-frontier-on-terminal-bench); this is the tier below, and it fits on one accelerator with the latency headroom to be the reason you chose it. Where latency is a hard constraint, the frontier API is not a baseline you beat, it is a baseline you cannot use — and that is the clearest statement on this map of when owning wins for a reason other than price.

Discounts: no GPU-hours, no wall clock, no dollar figure for any of the three training stages. The H20 is a China-market accelerator with lower memory bandwidth than an H100/H200, which makes the latency number more impressive and less directly comparable to what we would rent. Latency is reported with MTP speculative decoding on, and the draft head was itself post-trained. Single deployment, one workflow, and the production outcome is an A/B on GMV with no effect size.
