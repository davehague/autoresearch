Seven measurement practices, several of them standard, each of which flips a reported self-improvement result when its control is absent

Phantom Gains pushes a frozen, untrained Qwen3-8B through the identical self-training evaluation pipeline as the trained arms and asks what the pipeline says about a model that did not change. The answer is: quite a lot.

F1single greedy decode per problemcorruption-to-learning ratio 1.5 on an untrained model, largely inference-batching nondeterminism
F2one-success expansion thresholdfrozen model "expands" 0.280 of unreached AIME problems; measured null 0.058 [0.038, 0.078] over 110 frozen comparisons
F3fixed token capdistillation student's verbose style lifts truncation 16.5% → 65.3%, confounding capability loss with style
F4transition ledger at accuracy-sized Ntransition metrics need ~10× the data; a 112-problem pilot is insufficient
F5one training seedmajority-vote CLR spans 0.55–1.53 across three seeds; accuracy +3.3, −1.5, +0.7
F610-prompt safety probea phantom 10-point refusal drop that 50 prompts dissolve
F7null measured oncea point estimate treated as a parameter; the "corrected" threshold's null is 0.058, not zero

The repair costs no new experiments. A threshold-free per-problem exact test against a pooled 1,408-draw baseline under FDR 0.05 detects nothing on any of 11 held-out replicates, and is unchanged under the multiple-testing rule, the error rate and the pool size. The nulls are built from baseline replicates a multi-arm study already owns — "though not from as few as most possess."

This vault has discounted single-run, no-interval results for twenty-five runs on instinct. F5 is what the instinct was worth: one method, three seeds, and conclusions that contradict each other. F4 prices the experiment this vault keeps queuing — a per-task ledger of where the candidate checkpoint fails needs an order of magnitude more tasks than a mean does, plus frozen replicates to measure the null against.
