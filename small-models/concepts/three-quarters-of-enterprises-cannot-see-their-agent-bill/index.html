49% scaled back agent deployments on cost while only 26% can see what they are spending — the market-scale version of this vault's accounting problem

KPMG's Global AI Pulse (Q2 2026), 2,145 senior leaders across 20 countries at $50M+ organizations: 49% scaled back AI agent deployments because costs exceeded benefits, against 79% still naming AI a top investment priority and average spend flat at $188M. The diagnostic pair matters more than the headline — only 26% have real-time visibility into AI costs and 33% cite limited understanding of token pricing as a barrier. The named driver is metered pricing meeting multi-step agents that call tools repeatedly.

The headline is sentiment and should not be carried as a fact. The 74% with no cost visibility is the item worth keeping, because it is the market-scale counterpart of what this vault established by measurement. Run 10 found per-token pricing ranks things wrongly and moved the unit to cost per completed task. Run 14 found the unit still underspecified: a published per-trial artifact showed 3.28B of 3.34B tokens were cache reads — 98% of the volume (cache-reads-dominate-an-agentic-token-bill), which is the mechanical reason the same model costs 2–3.7× more through a different scaffold (harness-cost-spread-replicates-on-an-open-model).

So a third of these organizations are not confused about a simple thing. They are correctly reporting that an agentic bill is not legible from a headline per-token rate, because it is composed of cache-read, cache-write, prompt and output in a mix the harness sets, and nobody shows them the decomposition. That promotes run 14's action item: instrumenting the four-way token split per completed task is arithmetic, not research, and it is apparently the thing half the market is missing before it can decide anything.

Do not read the pullback as an argument for building. Every cancellation here is a cancellation of an API bill, and the ranked remedies — cheaper models 40–60%, routing 30%+, token overhead 20–40%, budgets 10–20% (switching-to-cheaper-models-is-the-largest-named-coding-cost-lever) — need no infrastructure. Where it does bear on our decision is price risk: a 49% pullback rate is the demand-side counterpart to a vendor moving prices on unilateral notice (open-model-api-prices-are-not-a-stable-planning-input).
