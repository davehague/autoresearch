Cognition's SWE-2, RL-post-trained from open Kimi K3, lands within a few points of GPT-6 Astra on FrontierCode at roughly a quarter of Astra's cost, and beats Claude Fable 5.1 outright on Terminal-Bench 2.1

FrontierCode 1.1 Main: SWE-2 50.0% vs Claude Fable 5.1's 50.9% and GPT-6 Astra's 53.3% — near-parity with Fable at 64% lower cost, within a few points of Astra at roughly a quarter of Astra's cost. DeepSWE 1.1: SWE-2 73.0% vs Fable's 67.4% and Astra's 74.1%. Terminal-Bench 2.1: SWE-2 92.8%, ahead of Fable's 91.4% — an outright win on a benchmark this map already tracks closely (runs 33, 41, 44). Behaviorally, RL post-training cut median steps-to-first-edit from 48 (predecessor SWE-1.7) to 18, with 58% fewer turns and 81% lower average cost per task.

This is a direct, controlled instance of the vault's central bet — an open base model, RL-post-trained on a specific workflow, closing most of the gap to two frontier proprietary models at a fraction of the cost — joining the running tally of finetuned-SLM-vs-frontier instances this map has kept since run 5 (TaoLive's 94.8-vs-93.0, T-Tech's 116M-request production win, and others). The "fewer turns, lower cost" shift is framed here as efficiency, but it is the identical behavioral axis run 42's τ²-bench and run 45's GPT-6 Astra GDPval-AA regression flagged as a failure mode (agents that under-explore relative to what a task needs) — nothing in this source checks whether SWE-2's shorter trajectories cost it accuracy on a benchmark built to reward exploration, the way GDPval-AA did for Astra.
