The most attractive number on the frontier price sheet expires on 18 September — and this vault has no rule for how to carry a price with a date on it

GPT-5.6 Sol's $2.50 / $15.00 per million is not a price. It is 50% off a $5.00 / $30.00 list, applied automatically to openai/gpt-5.6-sol by a reseller, running through 18 September 2026 — thirty-one days from ingest. The flex tier's $1.25 / $7.50 is the same discount on a lower-priority queue.

Every cost comparison this vault holds treats published prices as scalars. Run 14 decomposed an agentic token bill into cache-read, cache-write, prompt and output (cache-reads-dominate-an-agentic-token-bill). Run 18 tracked a 6–12× move on one of those columns and a pricing schedule that varies by time of day (api-pricing-now-varies-by-time-of-day). Run 20 costed a designed harness at ~$0.27 per completed poster. None of those numbers carries an expiry, and one of them now should.

The distinction that matters for a build decision is between a list change and a promotion, and today's sources contain one of each. OpenAI's 30 July cuts were list changes with a stated mechanism — roughly 20% lower end-to-end serving cost from optimization work — which is the kind of move that tends to persist because the underlying cost moved. A reseller's introductory 50% with a printed end date is a customer-acquisition expense, and the correct expectation is that it reverts. Averaging them into one "frontier price" produces a number that is wrong in a direction that flatters renting.

So the open question is procedural rather than empirical, and it is ours to close: what price do we carry forward? The defensible answer is list, with promotions recorded separately and never used in a break-even that amortizes past their expiry — but this vault has been carrying whatever number the source printed. Every serving-economics comparison here should be re-read for which kind of number it used, and that audit has not been done.
