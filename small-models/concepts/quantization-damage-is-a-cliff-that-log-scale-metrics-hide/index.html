Quantization damage is a cliff, and the two charts everyone publishes — log-scale KLD and Top-1% accuracy — are the two that turn it into a slope

An independent sweep of LFM2.5-2.6B across a full cross-product of model quantizations and KV-cache quantizations found mean KL divergence from the full-precision original moving 0.0037 → 0.0114 → 0.0755 → 0.1892 across four steps down the ladder (Q8_0|q8_0, Q6_K_L|q8_0, Q5_K_S|q3_0, IQ4_XS|q3_0) — a 51× spread, most of it in the last two steps. The author's operative warning is about presentation, not about the model: "Logarithmic KLD and Top-1% plots lie to you by telling you that quality degradation is smooth, while it's actually a cliff." Top-1% reads 100% at bf16 against 97.6% at Q8, implying early gradual decay while the real collapse happens far lower and far faster. The sharpest cliff appears on a "same sampled token" metric requiring a patched llama.cpp; linear-scale mean KLD is second best.

Run 9 gave this vault its cheapest instrument — knowledge loss tracking KL divergence from the full-precision original at r = −0.981 (quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly). This independently confirms KLD is the right measure and corrects how it must be read: on a log axis it converts a cliff into a slope, and a cliff is exactly what the screen exists to catch. That is the same failure this vault has now traced through curation, the distillation objective, the RL objective, deployment precision and skill-library drift — a summarising transform destroying the signal the measurement already paid for — appearing this time not in an average over conditions but in a chart axis. It is the cheapest defect on the entire ledger to avoid.

Two secondary results are directly actionable. Model precision degrades faster than KV-cache precision on this model, which inverts the ordering a production operator reached serving frontier-scale MoE checkpoints (operator-quantization-cuts-cost-at-benchmark-parity). Both can hold — weights dominate memory at 700B and do not at 2.6B — so the weights-versus-cache split is a knob to sweep per model, not a rule to inherit, and sweeping it costs an afternoon of perplexity runs. And abliteration carries a flat ~0.075 KLD at every quantization level, so an uncensored variant is roughly a free two-rung drop down the quality ladder, paid whether or not you also quantize.

Limits, and they bound the instrument rather than the finding. Perplexity and KLD grade distributional fidelity, not task performance — the third probe in this vault to grade a proxy, and none of them can see what compression-degrades-agentic-skills-more-than-reasoning showed compression actually takes, which is instruction-following and tool use. One model, one author, one wikitext corpus, no replication, no agentic evaluation, and the 128k cache figures are extrapolated from 32k runs.
