On the trained task the open 35B clears the best model in the table by 1.8 points, on 10K SFT examples and 4K RL tasks. One column to the right it is third; two columns right it is sixth.

This is the largest capability gap this map has seen closed by fine-tuning — the beaten models include two frontier-tier systems and the vendor's own flagship — and it is closed on a proprietary rubric eval built from the deployer's own traffic. Both halves of that sentence matter. Run 30's Thomson counterexample spent 35,207 B200 GPU-hours and 200B tokens of proprietary legal data to move its base 2.9 points, then lost two of three legal exams to a general 31B (a-domain-specialist-cost-thirtyfive-thousand-b200-hours-and-moved-its-base-two-point-nine-points). The difference between that failure and this success is not scale — it is that TaoLive's target is a workflow with an owner who has the traffic, and 10K real examples of it beat 200B tokens of adjacent text.

The generalisation profile is the familiar one, at 78× the parameters of the last instance. Run 28's 450M browser specialist beat two larger generalists on its own task and lost to a 4B by 11 points elsewhere (a-450m-specialist-beat-two-larger-generalists-and-lost-to-the-third). Here: T2, which is the same task under harness variants the model never saw, holds at 94.6 — so the task generalises. T3, which is broad synthetic scenarios, does not — 84.0 against GLM-5.2's 86.7, and 77.6 prompt robustness against 84.0. IFEval recovers only to 83.5 against 90.4.

The operating rule this supports: narrow-task parity is real, it is reachable with four figures of examples rather than nine, and the boundary it holds inside is the task distribution you trained on, not the capability. A router that sends anything off-distribution to a general model is not an optimisation here, it is a requirement — which is run 31's merge-versus-router finding read from the other side (merging-domain-experts-captures-a-third-of-the-headroom-by-default).

Discounts, and they are the usual fatal ones for a vendor technical report: T1, T2 and T3 are the authors' own unreleased evaluation sets, rubric-graded by a model; the only externally comparable column is IFEval, where HAT does not lead. The frontier rows are zero-shot on a task the open model trained 10K examples for — a comparison this vault normally rejects, stated openly here as the setup. Three of the five comparison models are the same vendor's. No seeds, no intervals, single runs.
