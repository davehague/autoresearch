A practitioner-reported 506,849-token context benchmark on an AMD 7900 XTX is the first source on this thread where the write-up's authorship, not just its methodology, is disputed — and the author confirms heavy AI co-authorship rather than denying it

This thread has logged many single-practitioner self-reports with disputed baselines, undisclosed quants, or unverified speed claims, but always on the assumption that a human wrote the report describing their own measurements. This source is different: the top comment on a 506,849-token-context, 81-94%-quality AMD benchmark reads "The write up reads exactly like it was written 100% by Claude," with a reply adding "all the tropey claude-isms are there." The author does not deny it, replying that the piece came from "qwen, ornith, and a gemma a3b distill along with my own findings and writing."

That confirmation matters more than the specific numbers in the post. Every other self-reported figure this thread has logged carries the usual discounts (single author, single rig, no third-party check), but still assumes a human transcribed real measurements into prose. Here the prose itself is partly AI-generated, which opens a different failure mode: a drafting model smoothing, rounding, or inventing specifics ("81-84%," "506,849 tokens") that read as precise but were generated rather than measured. This map has not previously needed to ask "was the report itself written by the thing being benchmarked" as a distinct question from "was the benchmark methodology sound" — this is the first source where that question is squarely live, confirmed by the author, and worth checking on future heavily-AI-assisted write-ups before taking their specific numbers at face value.
