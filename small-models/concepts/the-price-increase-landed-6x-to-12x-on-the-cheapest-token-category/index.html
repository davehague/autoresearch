The price increase landed at 1.5–12×, and its largest multiplier fell on the token category that is 98% of an agentic bill

Run 12 recorded DeepSeek's warning of a "significant" API price increase with no figure and no date, alongside the founder's framing that even a 2–10× rise would still undercut most Western APIs (open-model-api-prices-are-not-a-stable-planning-input). The rate card is now published, effective 16:00 UTC on 2026-08-16, and the increase is inside that envelope. Where it sits inside the envelope is the finding.

Multipliers against the old sheet, off-peak → peak:

ModelCache-hit inputCache-miss inputOutput
v4-flash2.50× → 5.00×1.57× → 3.14×2.36× → 4.71×
v4-pro6.07× → 12.14×1.52× → 3.03×2.28× → 4.55×

The spread within one model is wider than the spread between models. V4-Pro's cache-hit input rose 6.07× while its cache-miss input rose 1.52× — a 4× difference between two columns on the same card on the same day. The old cache-hit rate was roughly 1/240th of the cache-miss rate for the same model; it is now 1/30th.

That matters because of what run 14 measured: on a published per-trial agentic artifact, cache reads were 98% of the token volume — 3.28B cached against 27M prompt and 38.9M output (cache-reads-dominate-an-agentic-token-bill). Priced at V4-Pro rates, that exact mix costs $57.48 old → $167.00 off-peak → $334.00 peak: a 2.91× / 5.81× increase, against a headline output-column increase of 2.28×/4.55×. The cache-read share of the bill moves from 20.7% to 43.2%.

So a team forecasting this change from a blended or output-weighted per-token rate under-predicts its own agentic bill by roughly a factor of two, and finds out a billing cycle later. That is the concrete cost of not having done the arithmetic this vault has been deferring since run 10: cost per completed task, decomposed into cache-read, cache-write, prompt and output. Set beside three-quarters-of-enterprises-cannot-see-their-agent-bill — only 26% of surveyed enterprises have real-time cost visibility — most of the market cannot see the shape of the increase it just received.

Two things this does not establish. DeepSeek's old cache-hit rate was an outlier low even among resellers, several of whom already served V4 Flash below DeepSeek's non-cache rates, so this is partly one outlier normalising rather than the open-model API market repricing. And run 15's endpoint finding means routing around it by picking a cheaper reseller spends an unmeasured share of the capability being bought (api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points).
