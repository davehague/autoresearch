An automatically-optimized harness improves five open-weight models by 11.87 to 19.56 points — the first time this map's method has been run on anything we could host

Every automated-harness result this map holds has been measured on closed frontier bases. DarwinX ran its population search on GPT-5.5, GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 4.8 (evolved-harness-clears-the-harness-noise-floor-at-avg-55); HarnessOpt-Bench's five optimisers were all closed frontier releases (optimizer-model-outweighs-harness-when-an-llm-builds-the-harness); AI4AI's two targets were small frontier models behind APIs (builder-written-harness-nearly-doubles-a-weak-model-at-test-time). The standing complaint has closed eight consecutive runs: nothing says what a designed harness does for a model we could run.

AutoDesign's controlled table has seven code-agent-model configurations, five of them open weight, with a single learned DesignHarness attached to each:

Average 54.99 → 67.39. Every cell improves, and the open cells improve by two to four times what the frontier agents gain.

The harness itself is worth naming because its content is this map's design rule again: paper ingestion with provenance links back to source locations, rule-based validators for unsafe or missing assets and broken links, a VLM visual critic on the rendered artifact, and localized diagnostics that drive targeted revision rather than regeneration. External referents throughout — the same family DarwinX's search converged on unprompted (an-automated-search-converged-on-external-verification-unprompted) and eight independent builders chose by hand (eight-builders-chose-deterministic-code-over-verification-loops).

It also publishes a price, which almost nothing on this map does: ~$0.27 per completed poster on the weakest target and under $3 for a full autonomous loop of 253 tool calls and 11 editing turns over 40 minutes. Second dollar figure in this family after SKILLER's $8.95 (a-skill-library-for-a-compact-model-costs-nine-dollars-to-build), and pointing the same way: the interventions deferred as expensive are not. Unpriced, as with DarwinX: the optimization loop that produced the harness.

Four discounts. One task family, and paper-to-poster is a document-transformation task with rule-checkable output — the side of the procedure-versus-novel-artifact predicate where open models already do well. The evaluator is frozen and the optimizer optimizes against it, with no held-out or transfer arm, which is the proxy-fitting shape DarwinX's own ablation warned about. No trial counts, no intervals, no repeats — the two frontier gains (+5.59, +5.01) sit inside every noise floor this vault has bracketed, and only the open-model gains clearly clear them. And the optimizer required human intervention on premature convergence.
