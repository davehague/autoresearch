Agents that learn from gold answers gain up to 16.44 points; agents that learn from their own reflection or exploration gain less — and the best of them stops 24 points below a ceiling built from the same information

GDPevo compares four supervision types for updating an agent's persistent state: base (nothing), fewshot (training questions + gold answers — the SFT analogue), reflect (training questions + rule-based score feedback — the RL analogue), and self (training questions + environment access only — unsupervised). The reported lifts all come from the fewshot arm: +16.44 pp (Opus-4.8 / Claude Code, 50.63% → 67.07%), +15.14 (GPT-5.5 / Codex), +13.97 (GLM-5.2), +5.21 (DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Preview). The boring supervision won.

That is the same ordering this map has produced three times now, and the explanation is run 9's rule: a loop earns its cost when it closes against something outside the model. Gold answers are external; a self-authored reflection is not. It matches self-inspection losing to plain repeated sampling at matched token cost (self-inspection-harnesses-lose-to-repeated-sampling-at-equal-cost), environment audits beating self-checks (environment-verification-succeeds-where-self-inspection-fails), and SKILL-KD's patches being validated by re-running the student rather than by asking the teacher (smaller-validated-skill-libraries-beat-larger-unvalidated-ones). Four independent instances, one mechanism.

The ceiling is the sobering half. Handing the model all the hidden rules plus the training questions and their gold answers produces 91.6%. The best evolved agent reaches 67.07%. So roughly a quarter of the achievable range is lost not to missing information but to the agent failing to extract and reuse what it already saw — with the rules present in the training experience. Self-evolution is real and it is nowhere near the limit of its own premise.

Two secondary readings. Lift scales with the model, not against it: +16.44 for the strongest model against +5.21 for the weakest, so experience-reuse amplified the existing spread rather than compressing it. That cuts against any hope that cheap experience accumulation lets a weak model catch a strong one — on this benchmark it did the opposite. And the benchmark's construction is the transferable part: rule hybridization decomposes a workflow into atomic business rules, spreads subsets across training tasks and recombines them in held-out tests, with full automation regenerating the whole suite (120 → 240 tasks) in two days. That is a contamination defence we could copy for our own workflows, and cheaper than it sounds.

Limits: the accessible tables show base and fewshot for only two of four agents, so reflect and self are described but their numbers are not visible — the claim that fewshot won rests on the paper's framing more than on a table this vault can read. Harness is not pinned across all four agents (Codex for GPT-5.5, Claude Code for the rest). No intervals, no repeats, n=120 in V1.
