The default conversion moved the model 0.219 KLD from the original before any quantization — more damage than the publisher's own 3-bit quant

Converting DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 (284B total, 13B active, QAT, experts natively MXFP4, everything else FP8 or BF16) from the original safetensors, AtomicChat found llama.cpp's converter hard-codes a downconversion of FP8 tensors to Q8_0. That alone puts the output 0.219 mean KLD from the original weights, before quantization begins. Their 118 GB three-bit quant measures 0.2065. The default "lossless" 162 GB baseline is farther from the original model than a 3-bit compression of it. Replacing those tensors with BF16 made the base bit-exact. (A second, loud defect: without --no-lazy, token_embd.weight becomes NaN.)

This is a defect in the vault's own chosen instrument, not another axis of variation. quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly gives us a cheap screen — KL divergence from the full-precision original at r = −0.981 — and quantization-damage-is-a-cliff-that-log-scale-metrics-hide tells us to plot it linearly. Both presume the reference is the original. Run this screen on this checkpoint against a default-converted base and the numbers are not merely noisy, they are centred on the wrong point, and the worst quants come out looking best. Before the screen is trusted on any checkpoint, verify the reference bit-exact against the original weights — which here required source-level intervention.

Published rungs, against the corrected bit-exact base: AD-BF16 162.1 GB / 4.25 expert bits / PPL 4.5289 / KLD 0; AD-IQ2_M 104.0 GB / 2.79 bits / PPL 4.8822 / KLD 0.2567; AD-IQ1_M 70.2 GB / 1.81 bits / PPL 6.3813 / KLD 0.5641. The 154 GB → 104 GB step costs ~4% of top-1 for 50 GB.

One reframing worth carrying: on MI300X, decode throughput was flat at 36.92–37.75 tok/s across a 1.4× file-size range — compute-bound, not memory-bound. So choosing a smaller quant buys VRAM headroom, not speed, which makes precision selection a fit decision (does the checkpoint run on hardware we have) rather than a cost-per-token one.
