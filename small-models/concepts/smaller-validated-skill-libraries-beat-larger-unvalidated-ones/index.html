The winning variant produces the smallest library — 40% of the rules and 44% of the words of the largest — and the largest gain. Two mechanisms separate it from the others: the patch is derived from the contrast between a student failure and a teacher success on the same task (Student-only and Teacher-only each read one trajectory and both land ~7 points lower), and every patch is validated by re-running the student, with refinement while the student still fails.

This is the external-referent rule in its sharpest form yet. The referent is not the teacher's opinion of what went wrong, and not the model's self-assessment — it is a measured re-run. It joins self-inspection losing to repeated sampling at matched cost (self-inspection-harnesses-lose-to-repeated-sampling-at-equal-cost), environment audits beating self-checks (environment-verification-succeeds-where-self-inspection-fails), and gold answers beating self-generated experience (worked-examples-beat-self-generated-experience-for-agent-evolution). It also cuts against the accumulate-everything instinct behind every memory and skill-library design: on this evidence, more guidance text is worse guidance text, and the only reliable way to know which rules to keep is to test them.

The consolidation ablation is the part to be uneasy about. Drift-Aware Skill Consolidation decides per patch whether to add, modify, delete or skip a rule; removing it:

w/o consolidation79.214.687.427.485.1
w/ consolidation79.224.389.354.886.6

LiveMath collapses by 27.4 points and Spreadsheet by 9.7, while SearchQA is identical and ALFWorld moves 1.5. Skill drift is task-dependent, and it is invisible in the average — a five-task mean would show a moderate decline and hide the fact that two tasks failed entirely. That is now the fifth pipeline stage in this vault exhibiting the same pattern, after curation, the distillation objective, the RL objective and deployment precision. The named drift modes are worth keeping: boundary-shifting (a rule quietly narrowing to one case), skill-bloat (incidental detail accreting), and destructive-update (a new rule overwriting a good one).

For our own build this is the cheapest actionable item on the map: log frontier traces on tasks our small model fails, diff against the failure, patch, re-run to validate, prune — and evaluate per task, never on the mean, because the failure mode that kills this method is one the mean cannot see.

Limits: single student for the ablations, five benchmarks, no intervals or repeats, and no accounting for the cost of teacher trajectories, repeated re-runs, or the 3,010 words carried on every inference.
