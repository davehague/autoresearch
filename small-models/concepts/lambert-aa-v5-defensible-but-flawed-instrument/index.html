An independent analyst's read: the widened gap is partly real, partly the old number being wrong all along

Nathan Lambert's independent commentary on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index v5 takes a middle position this map hasn't logged before on its own widened-gap finding: he thinks the "open models are collapsing" alarm is overstated, but concedes the prior v4.3 index "was flattering the open weight models too strongly" — meaning at least part of the widened v5 gap (3 points → 12) is v4.3 having understated the gap, not v5 overstating it. His structural account for why open models trail specifically on the new harder categories (Terminal-Bench-Science, CritPt v2): closed labs train on RL environments that closely match evaluation distributions, requiring an RL stack most open labs haven't built yet — a capability-training-process gap, not a raw-capability ceiling, and one open labs could close with investment rather than a fundamental limit. He separately flags a disclosed conflict of interest in Artificial Analysis's own business model (paid lab verification work) and recommends treating no single index — including this one — as authoritative.

This doesn't supply the before/after same-model score comparison last run's open gap asked for, and it doesn't reproduce FrontierCode 2.0 or Terminal-Bench-Science independently. What it adds is a falsifiable structural hypothesis: if an open lab ships a model trained on eval-distribution-matched RL environments and its score on exactly those two new categories narrows the gap, that supports Lambert's "process gap, not ceiling" account over a pure capability-gap one.
