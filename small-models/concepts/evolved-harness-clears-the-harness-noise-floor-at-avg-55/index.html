A designed harness moves Terminal-Bench 7.7 points at avg@55 — the first harness effect stated at a resolution the noise floor cannot absorb

This map's shipped-harness half was effectively closed by run 12. HarnessOpt-Bench measured the harness contrast at 0.079 normalized gain against per-task noise floors of ±0.035 to ±0.066 over 111 scored runs, and that made "swapping harnesses is quality-neutral" a claim with an error bar rather than an absence of evidence (harness-effect-on-quality-is-modest). Two more independent noise measurements followed — 12–14% of web-agent outcomes flipping run to run, and a 9.4% trial error rate over 445 trials in run 14's public reproduction (vendor-benchmark-score-reproduced-in-an-independent-public-harness). The standing conclusion was that most single-run comparisons in this literature sit inside their own error.

DarwinX reports +7.7 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1 — 75.5% base to 83.2% on a matched frozen base — at avg@55, and 84.7% on a stronger base, level with or above Claude Code on Fable 5 (83.8%) and Codex on GPT-5.5 (83.1%). Fifty-five trials per cell, on the same benchmark family where the noise floor was measured. On WebArena-Infinity the same procedure moves audit-clean pass@1 from 43.5% to 93.0%, and on TerminalWorld's 41-task held-out split from 61.0% to 68.3%.

This does not overturn run 12; it separates the two halves the map has been trying to keep apart since run 10. Comparing shipped harnesses buys cost; designing a harness buys quality (harness-design-is-a-large-quality-lever-on-small-models, harness-swap-moves-cost-not-quality-on-real-engineering-tasks). HarnessOpt-Bench measured the first; this measures the second, and it is the first time the designed side has been reported at a trial count that makes the comparison legitimate rather than suggestive.

Three things keep it from being decisive. The 93.0% is in-domain against the search's own fitness verifier — a 49.5-point jump on the benchmark being optimized is the shape of proxy-fitting, and the paper's own TerminalWorld ablation shows the best proxy-fitting variant generalizing worst (four specialists solving 24–27 of 41, merged reaching 28). Every base is a closed frontier model — GPT-5.5, GPT-5.6 Sol, Opus 4.8 — so nothing here says what an evolved harness does for an open 27B we could host. And no cost is reported, for a loop that evaluated 62 variants on one benchmark and confirms at avg@55; run 12's instruction to budget the search on the optimiser model cannot be priced against this (optimizer-model-outweighs-harness-when-an-llm-builds-the-harness).

The held-out numbers are the ones to carry: 68.3% on TerminalWorld and 84.2% on SWE-bench Verified from a Terminal-Bench-evolved harness with no in-domain evolution. That transfer is the finding with the most consequence for us — it suggests harness gains are not as workflow-specific as this vault's own framing assumes.
