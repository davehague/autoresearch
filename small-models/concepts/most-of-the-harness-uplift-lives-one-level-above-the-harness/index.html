Rewriting the harness bought less than rewriting the strategy for rewriting the harness — on the two hardest environments, most of the gain was in the outer loop

HSI nests three scopes over one frozen model: a task harness, an evolver that rewrites the harness, and a meta-evolver that rewrites the evolver's own strategy code under a frozen outer anchor. Disabling only the meta-evolver:

environmentmeta-offmeta-oncost of removing the outer loop
TextWorld46.065.0−19.0
MiniHack5.815.8−10.0
Crafter36.444.6−8.2
BabyAI77.381.3−4.0

TextWorld loses 19 of its 25 points without it. The pattern is ordered: the harder the harness space, the larger the share of uplift that sits one level above the harness.

If we had built this, we would have written the evolver and stopped — the meta-level is the part that reads as over-engineering on a whiteboard and turns out to carry the result. It also lines up with the quantity that keeps surfacing in this vault whenever a model is put in charge of improving something: the optimiser model outweighs the harness at 1.8:1 when an LLM builds the harness (optimizer-model-outweighs-harness-when-an-llm-builds-the-harness), and builder capability outweighs the platform at 11:1 (builder-capability-outweighs-the-platform-eleven-to-one). Here the "builder" is a rewrite loop, and improving it pays more than improving its output.

The unresolved question is whether the meta-evolver is doing something structurally different or simply adding a second round of search. Nothing in the paper separates those, and a matched-budget control — the same total rewrite steps spent entirely at the harness level — is not reported.
