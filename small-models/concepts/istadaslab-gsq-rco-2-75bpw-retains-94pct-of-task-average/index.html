A gradient-searched per-tensor precision allocation (GSQ+RCO) keeps a looped MoE within 0.8-7.0 points of its full-precision AIME25/GPQA-Diamond/LiveCodeBench v6 scores all the way down to 2.75 bits per weight, with the 3.50 bpw setting losing under a point on two of the three benchmarks

Against Inkling 2 Mini's NVFP4/MXFP8 base scores (AIME25 99.33, GPQA-Diamond 86.87, LiveCodeBench v6 79.62, task average 88.61), four GSQ+RCO GGUF quants retain: IQ2_S at 2.75 bpw — 92.33/84.34/73.90 (83.52 avg, 94.3% retention); Q2_K_XL at 3.00 bpw — 97.00/86.53/76.95 (86.83 avg, 98.0%); IQ3_XXS at 3.25 bpw — 96.00/85.86/77.90 (86.59 avg, 97.7%); IQ3_S at 3.50 bpw — 98.33/86.20/79.05 (87.86 avg, 99.2%). RCO assigns precision per-tensor (169 tensors) by gradient descent against a size budget rather than uniform bit-width; GSQ is the scalar quantization method used within that budget. Every bit-width is within 1.0 point of every other on GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench v6; the AIME25 math benchmark shows the largest spread (92.33 to 99.33, a 7-point range).

Lands as a favorable same-model, same-architecture data point for this map's precision-tax thread, in the retention range nariq-3bit-qwen38-27b-matches-bf16-long-horizon reported for a different model on long-horizon agentic benchmarks — but this result is on static reasoning/coding evals, not agentic tasks, so it doesn't speak to whether a flat benchmark score here could still hide the kind of agentic-trajectory degradation quantization-flat-accuracy-hides-trajectory-collapse found elsewhere under a flat accuracy number.
