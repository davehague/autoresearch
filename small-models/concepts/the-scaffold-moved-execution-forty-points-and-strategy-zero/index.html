Experience, a human correction, and 8× the reasoning tokens each improve how well an agent runs its training plan, and none of them makes it change the plan

Three escalating interventions on the same setup — Qwen3-1.7B-Base, GSM8K / HumanEval / AIME 2025, Claude Code on Opus 4.6, three independent runs per configuration under a 10-hour budget on four NVIDIA A800 GPUs:

  • Experience. A scaffold with a persistent experiment journal, a skill library distilled from open-source projects and documentation, and a dedicated evaluator agent producing concrete diagnoses. Execution improves across the board — +12.6 points on GSM8K, +40.8 on HumanEval — and the strategy stays static.
  • Guidance. A human reviewer revises the chosen strategy before training begins. The redirect works. Once training starts the agent falls back into local-adjustment loops.
  • Compute. The scaffolded agent spends 2–8× more inference tokens than the autonomous baseline. It pays off on the easier tasks and yields almost no gain on AIME 2025.

The authors' summary is that the strategy is plastic only in a short window before training begins; inside the window both experience and guidance shape it, and once it closes the same mechanisms uniformly fail. What is missing is not capability but a mechanism for spontaneously initiating strategy re-evaluation during execution.

Read this against the map's other results rather than as pessimism. +40.8 points on HumanEval from a scaffold is a large execution gain and it is exactly the kind of uplift run 17's headroom law predicts for a weak target — the same shape as the builder-written harness that nearly doubled a weak model at test time (builder-written-harness-nearly-doubles-a-weak-model-at-test-time). Agents reliably get from "here is a base model and a benchmark" to a better checkpoint. What they do not do is notice three experiments in that the approach is wrong and switch.

For us that gap is expensive precisely because of this map's own ledger. LoRA, full-parameter, GRPO, evolution strategies, on-policy distillation, environment synthesis and a prompt-resident skill library have each been measured as the right answer under some condition and the wrong one under another. Strategy selection is where this vault's value is, and it is the part that does not automate.

The cost figure is worth keeping because so few of these papers give one. 10 hours on 4× A800 per configuration is a small, entirely affordable price for an agent-driven post-training attempt on a 1.7B — well inside what our cluster grants. It is also the price of an attempt whose most important decision was made in the first minutes.

Grade: suggestive — three escalating interventions on a fixed setup with hardware, budget and repeat count all stated is a real experiment, and the three failures are consistent with one another. It is not strong because it runs on one agent-model pair with three runs and no intervals, on a 1.7B base and three well-trodden benchmarks, and negative results establish that these interventions fail rather than that none would.
