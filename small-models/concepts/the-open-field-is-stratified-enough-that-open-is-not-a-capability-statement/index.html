A vendor's own card puts its new open 30B behind two peer open models on every benchmark shown — 18.6 points on SWE-bench Verified, at identical active parameters

NVIDIA's model card for Nemotron 3.5 Lightning (30B total / 3B active, Mamba-2 + MoE + attention hybrid, OpenMDW-1.1, 1M context, >20T pre-training tokens, post-trained through May 2026 with SFT and GRPO) publishes a comparison against Qwen 3.6 35B A3B and Gemma 4 26B A4B:

Nemotron 3.5 LightningQwen 3.6 35B A3BGemma 4 26B A4B
MMLU Pro81.9485.6385.20
GPQA Diamond75.4483.4079.61
SWE-bench Verified51.5670.1257.40

It loses all three, and loses SWE-bench Verified by 18.6 points to a model with the same 3B active parameters. What the release sells instead is speed — 4× output speed, 30% faster agentic task completion, both against unnamed comparators — and routing.

The spread among open 30B-class models on a coding benchmark here is larger than several open-vs-frontier gaps this vault holds. "An open model" is therefore not a capability statement, and neither is a size class. This compounds with api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points: pick the wrong open model and lose 18.6 points, then serve it on the wrong endpoint and lose a quarter of what remains. It is the same lesson as the-quant-label-is-not-a-spec one level up the stack — the label names a class, not an artifact.

Note the quieter concession. A vendor shipping a small open model and a router in one announcement is saying the small model does not carry the workload alone (cross-model-escalation-routes-7-percent-of-calls-and-cuts-74-percent-of-cost). That is not an argument against the architecture; it is an argument against reading the architecture as a small-model win.
