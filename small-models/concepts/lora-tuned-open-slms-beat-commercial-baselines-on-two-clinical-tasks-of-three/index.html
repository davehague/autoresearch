Two procedures won, the one open-ended task lost — the vault's parity boundary used prospectively for the first time

Zhang, Guo and Gong benchmark eight open-source SLMs under zero-shot prompting, prefix tuning, LoRA and full fine-tuning on three emergency-department tasks — triage level prediction, specialist referral recommendation, diagnosis prediction — over 2,083 MIMIC-IV-ED cases, against Claude Haiku 4.5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5. LoRA-tuned open SLMs beat the commercial baselines on triage and referral; diagnosis prediction "remains challenging." Confusion-matrix analysis shows the tuned open models detect highest-severity patients the commercial baselines miss.

The split lands exactly where this vault said it would. The boundary drawn over runs 10–13 is parity on executing a specified procedure, failure on producing a novel artifact (open-weight-model-ties-frontier-on-verifiable-trails-on-hard-swe, open-model-trails-frontier-29-points-on-abstract-reasoning). Triage is assignment into a five-level acuity scale defined by written criteria; referral is selection from an enumerated specialty list; diagnosis is open-ended generation over a very large label space with no rubric. This is the first time the boundary has been used prospectively rather than fitted retrospectively, and it survives a domain change — every prior instance was coding, extraction, or agentic tool use.

LoRA carrying the sweep matters beyond this domain. full-parameter-warmup-buys-in-domain-speed-and-persistent-ood-damage found full-parameter tuning causes persistent out-of-domain degradation while LoRA at rank 16–64 matches it in-domain and keeps the generalization. Here LoRA wins in-domain too, in a different field, on real clinical records. Two independent arrivals from opposite directions, and our premise — workflow-specific specialists deployed beside adjacent workflows — is the case where both point the same way. If we run one training arm, run LoRA.

The confusion-matrix result is the protocol lesson. Catching highest-severity cases the baselines miss is a per-condition finding, both the thing that would justify deployment and the thing an accuracy mean destroys (distillation-harms-are-invisible-in-aggregate-metrics). Note the direction: split reporting made the small model look better. Grade per condition regardless of which way you expect it to cut.
