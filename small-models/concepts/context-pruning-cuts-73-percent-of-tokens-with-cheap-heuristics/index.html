Lightweight heuristics cut 73% of an agent's tokens with little quality loss — and the sophisticated scorer bought nothing over them

Kolukuluru et al. study context pruning in deep-research agents, starting from the observation that "context grows rapidly while the marginal value of additional evidence often declines." Evaluating pruning across three pipeline stages and a range of scoring methods, they report up to 73% token reduction with little quality degradation from lightweight heuristics, with more expensive marginal-value estimators failing to earn their cost.

This fits the two rules the vault already holds rather than adding a third. Pruning is a pure removal intervention — no extra call, no critic, no reflection loop — and designed-harness-lifts-score-and-cuts-tokens-together predicts exactly this shape: removing model calls buys cost with quality flat or better, arranging more of them buys only cost.

The cheap scorer winning sharpens the second rule. internal-referents-are-cheap-and-small-not-worthless says internal referents are cheap and small; a pruning heuristic is about as internal as a referent gets, yet here it is sufficient. The corollary worth stating: internal referents are adequate for subtractive decisions and inadequate for generative ones. Self-critique fails because the model must judge whether its own answer is good (self-inspection-harnesses-lose-to-repeated-sampling-at-equal-cost); pruning succeeds because the model need only guess which of its own notes it will not need again, where being approximately right suffices and the downside is bounded.

One limit on transfer. A research agent accumulates retrieved evidence, most of it genuinely redundant. Our workflows accumulate task state and tool results, where dropping the wrong item breaks the run rather than costing marginal quality — and the vault's best long-horizon result works by moving that state out of context and auditing it against the environment, not by scoring it for deletion (external-task-state-lifts-long-horizon-agents). Complements, not alternatives: externalize what must persist, prune what merely accumulated, and do the externalization first because it makes the pruning safe.
