Local throughput on one checkpoint moved 4–10× in three days of community serving work — so the self-hosting side of every break-even here has an unmeasured decay rate

Qwen3.8-27B was released on 16 August 2026. Run 21 measured it locally at 15–30 tok/s and this vault built two maps' worth of reasoning on that. By 19 August the same checkpoint decoded at 114–124 tok/s on one RTX 3090 and 120–218 on two, via INT4 quantization, a DFlash2 drafter and vLLM tuning. One of the single-card reports states its own trajectory explicitly: 82 tok/s two days earlier, 99 the next day, 114–124 the day after — three improvements in three days, on frozen weights.

This vault has a well-developed account of instability on the hosted side. Prices are not a stable planning input (open-model-api-prices-are-not-a-stable-planning-input); a promotion is not a price basis (a-thirty-day-promotion-is-not-a-price-basis); pricing varies by time of day; the frontier flagship halved while the open vendor raised prices. The self-hosted side has been treated as the stable term — you buy hardware, you get throughput, you divide. It is the less stable term of the two, and it moves in one direction.

The open question is the shape of the curve, and nobody has published it. What we would need to make any break-even honest is the decay rate of a first-week throughput measurement: how much of the eventual steady-state number is available on day 3, day 30, day 180? That is answerable from public data — pick three past open checkpoints, plot community-reported tok/s against days-since-release on comparable hardware — and until it exists, the operating rule is defensive: do not run a self-hosting comparison in a checkpoint's first month, and if you must, state the release date next to the number.

There is a second-order version that matters more for a capex decision. Hardware is bought once and the serving stack keeps improving on it for the life of the card. That means owned hardware appreciates in effective capacity while a rented API's throughput is whatever the provider chooses to serve — which is the first argument for building that this vault has found that does not depend on per-token arithmetic, and it is currently unquantified. Set against it: the same instability makes any dated capex-versus-opex crossover unreliable, and this vault has just recorded the capex side moving too (system-memory-repriced-four-to-nine-fold-in-twelve-months).
