A paraphrase/counterfactual triplet separates "depends on the input" from "depends on what the input asks" — and most token selection is still worse than selecting nothing

Run 11 added the vault's third pipeline probe: SA-OPD detects spurious distillation supervision by deleting the prompt and comparing teacher–student log-ratios before and after (input-ablation-detects-spurious-distillation-supervision). Deletion conflates two different things — a token that depends on the question being present, and a token that depends on what the question actually asks.

CROP separates them with a triplet. Paraphrase the question so the answer is unchanged; alter it so the answer changes; score each response position by a paraphrase-calibrated counterfactual sensitivity margin. Positions that move under the counterfactual but not under the paraphrase are the task-relevant ones. The paraphrase arm is what subtracts surface sensitivity, and the authors identify it as the load-bearing component.

Results, under a fixed supervised-token budget so every arm pays equal training cost and only the selection differs, across AIME24/25, MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond, HumanEval and IFEval:

  • Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-1.7B: CROP 47.98 average against TIP at 46.06 (+1.92)
  • Qwen3-8B (GRPO) → Qwen3-4B: CROP-ent 57.48 against Pure OPD at 55.25 (+2.96)

The second row carries the more useful finding, and it is a negative one. Pure OPD — supervising every token — is the strongest non-CROP arm in that setting. Entropy, TIP, TA-OPD and CREDIT all lose to not selecting at all. For a pipeline we would build, that sets the default: supervise everything, and require any selector we add to beat that baseline explicitly rather than assuming selection helps.

Cost is three extra student rescoring passes per sample — original, paraphrase, counterfactual — with no labels, no reward model and no extra rollouts. Same shape as every probe on this map that works. It composes with run 15's finding that warm-up needs teacher-compatible chain-of-thought rather than correct answers (incorrect-teacher-rollouts-warm-up-a-student-as-well-as-correct-ones); both say the expensive input to a distillation run is not the one we assumed. The authors decline to price their own overhead, calling latency and energy out of scope.

Two limits on the size of the claim. The gains are 1.92 and 2.96 average points — a fifth to a third of SA-OPD's best single-task result (+7.2 on CountQA), and inside the band three independent noise measurements have bracketed for this literature. And the setting is Qwen-family, mathematics-derived triplets, with 1.7B and 4B students taught by 4B and 8B teachers — our end of the compute range, but not the frontier-teacher distillation the build order proposes.

The residue is unchanged and now five probes deep. Prefix loss grades inputs, the input-grounding gap and this grade supervision, turn-level credit grades trajectories, KL divergence grades outputs. Every one of them grades a training signal. Nothing yet validates a model against a task.
