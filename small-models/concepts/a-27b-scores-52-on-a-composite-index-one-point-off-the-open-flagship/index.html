Qwen3.8 27B scores 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — eleven points behind the frontier ceiling and one point behind DeepSeek V4 Pro

The gap this map has been tracking finally has a same-day, same-methodology reading that includes the checkpoint we would actually host. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index as of 2026-08-18: Qwen3.8 27B scores 52, against a median of 9 for open-weight models of comparable size. The band above it: Claude Opus 5 (max) 63 at $2.34 per task, Claude Fable 5 62 at $3.14, Claude Opus 5 (high) / GPT-5.6 Sol (max) / Grok 4.6 (high) 61 at $1.23, $1.23 and $0.84, Kimi K3 (max) 60, Qwen3.8 Max 58, Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) 56, DeepSeek V4 Pro (max) 53.

Three distances, and this map has been conflating them. Eleven points from the 27B to the closed ceiling — not parity, and it should not be reported as parity. Eight points from the 27B to the best open model (Kimi K3). And one point from the 27B to DeepSeek V4 Pro, the open flagship whose pricing anchored this vault's cost arithmetic three runs ago. The decision-relevant distance is the third: the gap that matters is not open-to-frontier, it is this checkpoint to the checkpoint we would otherwise rent, and on this index that gap is inside the measurement.

Read it as a screen, not a verdict, for a reason this vault has documented at every other stage of the pipeline. A blended index is an average over heterogeneous benchmarks, and averaging is the operation that has hidden a defect class at every link — curation, distillation objective, RL objective, deployment precision. Run 21 measured the specific version of it that applies here: the open-versus-frontier separation is 0.237 on avg@3 against 0.122 on best@3 (the-open-frontier-gap-is-mostly-a-consistency-gap), so most of what a single composite number calls a capability gap is repeatability, and a single composite number cannot show which. Two models one point apart on this index could differ substantially in how often they fail.
