The thing that separates labs is not in the traces — it is environments, the infrastructure to run them, and the recipe for mixing them, and this vault has never priced any of it

Lambert dismisses the distillation explanation for GLM-5.3 in one sentence: "one does not simply 'distill' RL environments, infrastructure to run them at scale, or algorithms to mix them together effectively."

That lands directly on the build order this vault has been assembling since run 11. Its first step is log frontier traces where our small model fails, and runs 7, 15 and 16 accumulated four separate cautions about that dependency — cross-teacher supervision failing silently (cross-teacher-distillation-fails-silently-without-style-residualization), warm-up needing teacher-compatible rather than correct traces (incorrect-teacher-rollouts-warm-up-a-student-as-well-as-correct-ones), an access variance on the teacher itself (frontier-teacher-traces-are-a-supply-we-do-not-control), and rollout corpora carrying payloads no reviewer can read (public-agent-rollouts-carry-payloads-no-reviewer-can-read). All four ask whether we can get the traces. This asks whether the traces were ever the valuable object.

Three independent arrivals now say the same thing and none of them prices it. Z.ai's account of GLM-5.3's entire gain — a six-fold move on a terminal benchmark from an unchanged 743B base — is "more environments, more diverse tasks, and more compute spent training on them" (post-training-alone-moved-an-open-model-six-fold-on-a-terminal-benchmark). Qwen's card for a 27B showing 3.2× on DeepSWE at fixed parameter count says nothing at all about how (post-training-alone-tripled-a-fixed-27b-on-a-coding-benchmark). Lambert names the missing object explicitly and adds that a Chinese RL-environment data market is growing, partly supplied by American vendors — which is the first hint in this vault that environments are a thing one can buy rather than only build.

So the largest unpriced dependency on this map is not compute, data curation, or teacher access. It is environment construction, and it sits upstream of every step in the build order. The closest thing to a price this vault holds is CalibForge, which generates and calibrates 5,431 terminal tasks against solver disagreement (training-tasks-must-be-calibrated-to-the-solver-not-just-validated) — tasks are an input to an environment, not the environment, and CalibForge does not state the size of the model it trains or what the pipeline cost.

Two things follow that are ours to do. Price it, or find who sells it. If an RL-environment market exists, a quote is a cheaper answer than a research programme, and it converts the vaguest line in our build order into a number. And note what it does to the buy-vs-build framing: an environment is the one asset in this whole argument that is workflow-specific, non-transferable, and therefore not purchasable from a model vendor at any price — which is the strongest structural argument for building that this vault has found, and it is unquantified.

Limits: an essay, no measurement, and an author with a stated position and a post-training textbook in market. The claim that environments do not distil is an assertion about tacit organizational capability, which is the hardest kind of claim to check and the easiest kind to be right about for the wrong reasons.
