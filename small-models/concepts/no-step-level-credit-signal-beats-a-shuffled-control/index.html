Audited against what actually changes the outcome, none of the three credit signals used to train LLM agents identifies the steps that mattered better than chance

Ground truth is built by executed replay: at each decision point, resample K=4 alternative actions from the policy itself, roll each forward to completion at least three times, and measure the shift. Every signal overlaps its marginal-matched shuffled control under frozen verdict order. Per-step sign agreement is chance: 50.7% (Qwen), 44.9% (Llama).

The ground truth is itself sparse and model-dependent. Only 30.5% of Qwen's decision points with defined ground truth carry measurable causal effect (38.3% for Llama) — so a harness change that improves the other two-thirds of steps is unmeasurable at the step level by construction. And the fraction of points with no policy-supported counterfactual differs by a factor of two between two similar-scale policies, 13.1% for Qwen against 26.8% for Llama, because Llama's tighter probability concentration makes four distinct alternatives harder to sample inside budget. The instrument's coverage is a property of the model being measured.

Run 23 concluded that we have no validated way to attribute a harness component's contribution short of ablating it end-to-end with repeats (invocation-counts-are-necessary-to-read-a-null-and-insufficient-to-attribute-a-win). This reaches the same place with a causal instrument in hand, and makes it worse: the step-level signals are not merely unvalidated, they are measuring something else (implicit-credit-weights-fluency-at-two-and-a-third-times-causal-effect).
