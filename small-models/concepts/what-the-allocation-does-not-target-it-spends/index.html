The 40 points of reasoning were paid for out of coding, math and structured output — the capabilities an agent is made of

A fixed-byte-budget precision reallocation on Gemma 4 E4B recovered 96.74% of the BF16 source's reasoning at 24% of its size (precision-is-an-allocation-not-a-scalar). The retention table shows where the bits came from:

Coding falls to 24.219% absolute against the source's 41.406%; math to 15.625% against 25.781%. The card says it outright: "coding is degraded and should not be relied on."

Run 10 established that compression takes instruction-following and tool use first, because loss sorts by distance from a closed-form answer (compression-degrades-agentic-skills-more-than-reasoning). This build takes them deliberately, to fund abstract reasoning — and the four capabilities at the bottom of that table are precisely what an agent executing a workflow consists of.

That makes the direction wrong for us, and instructively so. This vault's best-performing predictor is that small models win on executing a specified procedure and lose on producing a correct novel artifact — six domains, four size classes, used prospectively three times (a-1b-on-license-clean-data-ties-a-4b-in-general-and-beats-it-in-domain, the-eighth-parity-claim-and-its-margin-is-inside-the-noise). An allocation that spends instruction-following and structured output to buy reasoning optimises against the half of the predicate we would deploy on. The default calibration corpus in this technique is a reasoning corpus, and reasoning is not our workload.

The instruction is therefore specific rather than cautionary: if we compress anything, build the calibration corpus from our own agentic traces, allocate against tool-call correctness and schema adherence, and publish the columns that paid. And accept that the trade is real — this table is not evidence that allocation is free, it is evidence that allocation moves damage rather than removing it.

One item this reinforces about measurement. A single aggregate retention number for this build would read around 80% and tell us nothing; the decision lives entirely in the ranking. That is distillation-harms-are-invisible-in-aggregate-metrics arriving at deployment precision for the second time, and it is why precision-is-an-allocation-not-a-scalar argues the KLD screen needs a per-capability form before it can grade an allocated quant at all.
