At matched headroom on the same benchmark, a frontier model took 17.8 points from the harness layer and an open 27B took 8.3 — and the strongest open model took 0.3

Recuris evolves a memory layer on one mid-sized model and transfers it, unchanged, to nine others. On τ²-Retail (114 tasks):

Two pairs make the point. GPT-5.6 Sol starts at 58.3 and gains 17.8; Qwen3.6-27B starts at 62.8 and gains 8.3 — comparable headroom, less than half the capture. And Claude Opus 5 starts lower than Qwen3.6-35B (72.4 vs 78.2) yet gains 15.6 against 0.3, which is the opposite of what the headroom law predicts (harness-uplift-tracks-headroom-not-model-size).

This is the fourth independent measurement of the same asymmetry, and the pattern across them is specific: open models capture structural harness changes well and instruction-shaped ones badly.

  • Run 25: a prompt-level memory fix recovering three times less on the open model (the-prompt-level-fix-recovers-three-times-less-on-the-open-model).
  • Run 26: the open-weight model was the one that could not use a one-line disclosure at all, while six closed models recovered 30–61 points (the-open-weight-model-is-the-one-that-cannot-use-the-disclosure).
  • Run 29: the self-discovery cliff three to four times larger on open models (the-self-discovery-cliff-is-three-to-four-times-larger-on-open-models).
  • Against: run 27's structural harness search carrying at four-fifths (structural-harness-search-carries-to-the-open-model-at-four-fifths).

A state-grounded memory layer is instruction-shaped — it works by telling the model what to attend to and when to retrieve. That places it in the bad regime, and it is a direct hazard to the vault's central pairing: the harness half of "small open model plus custom harness" delivers a discounted return on exactly the model class we would deploy.

One competing explanation has to be named, and it is not weak. The memory was evolved on a single deployment model (Doubao-2.0-Pro) and transferred. Every number in this table is a transfer number, so a frontier model may simply be better at consuming somebody else's evolved memory. That is a different claim from benefiting more from its own. The experiment that separates them — evolve the loop natively on each target — was not run, and none of the frontier models had memory evolved on them either, so their gains are a lower bound.

Discounts: transfer-only design as above; single paper; 35 of 37 pairs improved, so the direction is uniform and only the magnitude splits; τ²-Retail is one benchmark and SkillFlow shows the open 27B gaining 16.6, its largest gain anywhere, which cuts against the reading; and Granite-4.1-3B's +13.4 from a 9.7 base is a floor effect, not a counterexample.
