Any multi-domain training run silently weights domains by response length, not by importance

The diagnosis in Open-MOPD is not gradient conflict between domains. It is arithmetic:

  • Math and code responses average ~10,500 tokens. Instruction-following responses average ~409.
  • Under a shared token-level objective, instruction following's 20.3% share of prompts becomes 0.99% of gradient tokens.
  • Result: naive multi-teacher distillation leaves IF 6.16 points below the oracle while math is 1.89 below — three times the damage, concentrated on the short-answer domain.

Three named drivers: structural sequence-length disparity across domains, dynamic convergence drift from non-uniform learning rates, and multi-step reward staleness from asynchronous policy updates.

This generalises well past distillation and it lands on our workflow sort. Every short-answer workflow — classification, extraction, routing, tool selection, structured output, anything graded on one line — is the IF column. Every long-CoT workflow is the math column. Pool them under one token-level objective and the short ones get trained at a hundredth of their nominal weight. That is a mechanism, not a correlation, and it is checkable before spending anything: compare mean response length across the domains you intend to train together and you have the ratio the optimizer will apply.

It also adds a boundary at a seam this vault already had. Run 19's three-pile sort — procedure, retrieval, generation — separated workflows by whether a scaffold can take them over. This adds a training boundary at roughly the same joint, and the two do not agree: a short-answer procedural task is highly scaffoldable and, in a mixed training run, nearly untrainable. Those two facts push toward the same build order for opposite reasons — scaffold the short tasks, train on the long ones.

The corresponding fix is cheap. Reward refresh, the largest single ablation term at +0.81, costs 2.10% of computation time; one outer step runs 1298s with it included.

Discounts: 3B student, three domains, one benchmark family per domain; the length ratio here (26:1) is extreme and a milder ratio may not produce a measurable effect; the mechanism is inferred from the fix working rather than from a direct instrumented measurement of gradient attribution; and the whole analysis assumes on-policy distillation with dense token-level rewards, so it does not automatically transfer to SFT or outcome-reward RL.
