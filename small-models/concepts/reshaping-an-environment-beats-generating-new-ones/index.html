Wrapping an existing environment in plug-in components beat generating new ones on all five benchmarks — at the same token budget, and while keeping the verifier that was already trusted

EnvHarness leaves a static environment's logic alone and wraps it over the standard reset/step interface with three component types — Stage (force an initial state), Contract (constrain legal or costly actions), Chain (concatenate two environments into one longer episode). Because nothing inside is rewritten, every reshaped task inherits the original human-built verifier. An automated loop, EnvRigger, reads the policy's own trajectories, writes components aimed at diagnosed weaknesses, and commits only what fresh rollouts validate.

Skills extracted from reshaped environments beat skills extracted from the originals on every benchmark tested (means over three runs):

It also beats the domain-specific generation pipelines it was compared against: GenEnv (62.6 on ALFWorld average), VeriEnv (39.6 WebArena average) and SWE-smith (50.12 on SWE-bench Verified).

Two details matter more than the deltas. First, skills from unmodified environments can be worse than no skills at all — on SpreadsheetBench they score 45.88 against a 46.44 no-skill baseline, and on SWE-bench Verified they lengthen episodes from 53.58 to 55.01 average steps for their 2.21 points. Practising what the agent already does reliably produces redundant skills. Second, the token cost is grounding, not method: on WebArena EnvHarness totals 137.3M tokens against VeriEnv's 137.8M. GenEnv's 64.2M is 3.5× lower on ALFWorld only because its rollouts are LLM-simulated rather than executed. Design tokens — the calls that propose and revise components — are 1.46M against 38K, and are a small share of either budget.

This extends rather than overturns run 21's finding that a verified RL environment can be synthesized for under thirty thousand tokens (a-verified-rl-environment-costs-under-thirty-thousand-tokens). The claim here is that synthesis is usually the wrong move: the expensive, non-transferable artifact in an RL environment is the verifier, which is exactly what run 19 identified as what does not distil (rl-environments-and-infrastructure-are-what-does-not-distil). Wrapping keeps it for free.

The binding limit is stated plainly by the authors: the method requires a resettable gym-style interface. A Stage must set an initial state and a Chain must restore a known state between subtasks, which excludes any environment backed by a live service, a real user account, or a physical system — precisely the workflows where sending an email or placing an order cannot be undone.

Grade: suggestive — five benchmarks in four domains, three runs each with standard deviations reported, and a token accounting against a matched-grounding baseline is better practice than most of what this vault ingests. It is not strong because the headline results are inference-time skill transfer rather than trained policies, several deltas sit inside their reported standard deviations, and the designer agent's cost is acknowledged but never priced.
