On its own purpose-built task, a fine-tuned 450M beat a 2B and a 4B-class generalist and lost to a 4B by 11 points — then failed its own release gate

A 100-case held-out browser-screenshot benchmark, strict pass, with comparator arms actually run:

modelstrict passmean rubric
Qwen 3.5 4B55/10083.4%
webbrain-vl-2-450M (fine-tuned)44/10076.24%
Gemma 4 E4B41/10081.3%
Qwen 3.5 2B39/10079.1%

Base LFM2.5-VL-450M scored 0/100. The model card states the model "did not pass" the unchanged production gate.

This map has nine parity claims resting on untuned or differently-interfaced baselines (parity-claims-keep-using-zeroshot-comparators) and a running complaint that the eighth one's margin was inside the noise (the-eighth-parity-claim-and-its-margin-is-inside-the-noise). This item has the arms, publishes the loss, and is still not parity. A 450M specialist against a 4B generalist on a task the small model was purpose-built for and the large one was not: 11 points short, with the largest model in the table winning. Run 27's Gemma 4 12B tool-calling fine-tune claimed 2.7× with no benchmark and no baseline (five-thousand-examples-on-a-sixteen-gigabyte-card-claimed-two-point-seven-times); this is what that class of project looks like once the arms are run.

Note also that strict pass and rubric mean do not rank the same way — the 450M's 5-point win over Qwen 3.5 2B on strict pass is a 3-point loss on rubric mean.

Discounts: n=100, single run, no seeds, self-published and self-benchmarked by the party that trained the model; comparators were run by the same party and may be under-elicited; a commenter argues the 0/100 base is a formatting floor rather than a perception floor ("rescore the base with a lenient parser first"), which would deflate the 0 → 44 trajectory substantially; and teacher labels came from Qwen 3.6-35B with admitted OCR errors and hallucinations, so the ceiling is a 35B's perception, not ground truth.
