Aligning rollout and trainer kernels to produce bit-identical logprobs lets async RL run with plain unclipped importance sampling, reaching 2.40x the throughput of aligned synchronous training at comparable quality — on small models only so far

REINFORCE Zero (NVIDIA Research and Inferact, on vLLM) fixes the usual async-RL problem — training on stale rollouts from a lagged policy — not by clipping around the mismatch but by aligning the rollout and trainer kernels so they produce bit-identical log-probabilities, then applying plain unclipped importance sampling with vanilla REINFORCE and group-mean advantages. On a 1.5B reasoning model it trains stably for 20,000 updates; without the alignment, rollout-to-learner KL rises 83.33x and AIME24 pass@1 collapses 34.27%->13.96%. At policy lag 64 on Qwen3-4B with Python tools, mean reward climbs 0.287->~0.78 over 5,000 updates. At 12,000 updates on 16 GPUs it reaches 2.40x the training-step throughput of aligned synchronous training (68.55 vs. 164.77 hours) at comparable final quality. The same zero-mismatch kernel alignment is separately validated at day-0/day-release scale on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and Kimi K3.1 (including a LoRA adapter on every module for Kimi K3.1, with AIME 24/25/26 mean@8 rising 0.451->0.664 over 100 LoRA steps), and on AMD MI355X for both — but only as a short-run correctness check, with no throughput comparison yet at that scale.

This vault's on-policy-distillation lineage has repeatedly flagged train/serve logprob mismatch as a stability hazard to avoid, not something with a measured cost. This is the first entry to put a concrete number (2.4x) on fixing it rather than merely avoiding it, which matters for cluster-training feasibility math: the "safe" aligned path and the "fast" async path no longer trade off against each other, at least at the scale tested. The catch is exactly that scale qualifier — the speed and quality results are both on small models (1.5B-30B-A3B); for the two flagship models in the post's title, only the zero-mismatch property is shown, not a sustained run or a throughput win at that size.
