Growing a skill library from 5 entries to 100 drops actual-use retrieval precision from 29.6% to 3.3% — the accumulation story has a ceiling that has nothing to do with skill quality

As the skill pool grows from 5 to 100 entries, actual-use retrieval precision falls from 29.6% to 3.3% — roughly a ninefold fall for a twentyfold growth in library size. The authors name retrieval as the critical bottleneck limiting practical skill effectiveness, and it is the one axis in the study where a manipulated variable produces a clean monotone effect.

Every skill-library result this vault holds implies a scaling story: accumulate skills, get better. This says the story inverts somewhere between 5 and 100 entries, and not because the skills got worse. At 3.3% precision, roughly thirty out of thirty-one retrieved skills are not the ones used — the library is mostly injecting distractors into a context window this vault has separately shown is expensive and quality-relevant. Run 18's context pruning cutting 73% of tokens with cheap heuristics (context-pruning-cuts-73-percent-of-tokens-with-cheap-heuristics) is the same accounting seen from the other end.

Two things we already hold need re-reading in this light. Editable libraries shrink the bill and append-only ones grow it (editable-skill-libraries-shrink-the-bill-append-only-ones-grow-it) was filed as a token-cost result; on this evidence editability is also what keeps a library inside its retrievable range, which makes it a quality lever and a considerably more important one. And smaller validated libraries beat larger unvalidated ones (smaller-validated-skill-libraries-beat-larger-unvalidated-ones) may not be about validation at all — a smaller library retrieves better whether or not anyone validated it, and the two effects are confounded in every published comparison including that one.

The build consequence is direct, because a skill library for a candidate checkpoint has been this vault's cheapest named plan at $8.95 (a-skill-library-for-a-compact-model-costs-nine-dollars-to-build). The number to design against is that somewhere between 5 and 100 entries retrieval stops working, and nobody has published where. Two implications worth carrying: a library should be scoped per workflow rather than accumulated globally, and any skill-library evaluation must report pool size, because a result at n=5 and a result at n=100 are not measurements of the same system. This vault has never recorded pool size for any of its skill results.

The pushback is against our own extrapolation rather than against any specific finding — no result here is contradicted, but the direction they were all pointing is.
