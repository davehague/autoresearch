RL's entire beneficial footprint on reasoning is 1–3% of token positions, every promoted token already in the base model's top-5

A token-level diff between RL-trained reasoning models and their bases, across multiple model families and multiple RL algorithms, finds the effect is sparse and predictable:

  • 1–3% of token positions differ in a way that matters.
  • The promoted token is always within the base model's top-5 alternatives — RL redistributes mass over branches the base already holds.
  • The affected positions are the high-entropy ones, and base-model entropy identifies them with no RL-trained model in hand.
  • The whole correction is low-dimensional, representable in a tiny fraction of parameters.

The claim is tested causally in both directions rather than asserted from the diff: patching only the identified positions recovers a large fraction of RL's accuracy gain, and patching random positions does not. That is a stronger internal control than most findings this vault carries, and it is what makes the result more than a curiosity about where gradients land.

It converges with two independent lines already on this map, and the convergence is the reason to weight it. label-free-self-distillation-beats-supervised-distillation-on-math found that majority-voting a pseudo-solution and distilling the shortest correct completion beats the supervised version of itself. incorrect-teacher-rollouts-warm-up-a-student-as-well-as-correct-ones found warm-up needs teacher-compatible chain-of-thought rather than correct answers. Three separate results, three separate mechanisms, one structural claim: on reasoning, the expensive external signal buys less than assumed, and the model's own distribution carries most of the gain.

The boundary is where our decision lives, and it is sharp. Every benchmark behind this is math reasoning — verifiable single answers, short horizons, no tool calls. The gains this vault actually wants sit in a different pile of its own best predicate: terminal agents, tool invocation, schema adherence. And the contemporary evidence on that pile points the other way — environment-synthesis-uplift-rises-with-base-model-size gets terminal-agent gains precisely by building harder executable environments, which is a claim that the RL loop contributes something a sparse correction cannot supply.

The reconciliation this vault should hold as a hypothesis rather than a finding: RL does two jobs. Selecting among branches the base already contains is cheap and replicable without the loop. Installing procedures the base does not contain is expensive and needs environments. If that split holds, it partitions our build order by task class rather than making it uniformly cheaper — and it says which half to try first, because one of them costs a GPU-hour.
