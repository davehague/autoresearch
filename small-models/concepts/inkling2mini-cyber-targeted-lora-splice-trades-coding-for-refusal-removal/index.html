A LoRA touching 0.0065% of Inkling 2 Mini's parameters, trained in under an hour on one GPU, removes 99 of 100 cybersecurity-research refusals and edges past an abliterated Qwen3.8-27B on CTIBench CVE→CWE (66.0% vs. 60.0%) — but drops HumanEval+ from 93.3% to 82.9%, naming an explicit capability cost for a cheap targeted edit

Trained a rank-16 LoRA (alpha=32) strictly on self_attn.o_proj in layers 4-23 (20 of 704 tensors, 1.72M params) on a 555-row eval-disjoint mix (382 on-policy Inkling-2-Mini + 173 off-policy abliterated-Qwen3.8-27B MITRE rows), then spliced the adapter directly into the quantized GGUF tensors so it runs unmodified in llama.cpp/Ollama/LM Studio. Same-harness, same-judge (Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B) results: CyberSecEval MITRE-120 refusals 100→1 of 104 scoreable prompts; CTIBench CVE→CWE-100 63.0%→66.0% (stock→edited), ahead of an abliterated Qwen3.8-27B's 60.0% on the identical benchmark; AdvBench harmful-prompt compliance (medium-thinking) 0.0%→95.2%. Disclosed cost: HumanEval+ full-164 93.3%→82.9%; CyberMetric-500 94.0%→92.8% (a small regression even within the targeted domain); non-thinking-mode compliance only 64.4% versus 95.2% for thinking mode.

This extends the vault's weight-editing thread (run 98's single-case Astra gain, run 105's 16-trajectory study finding the single-case result doesn't generalize) with a different edit target — behavioral refusal removal rather than a capability boost — and, unusually for this thread, an author-disclosed cost rather than a gain-only report. It is a cleaner instance of "cheap, targeted edit, real but bounded trade-off" than most entries here, because the trade (coding accuracy down, refusal rate down, one CTI benchmark up) is quantified on both sides rather than asserted only on the win side.
