"35% pruned" is not a described artifact — existing pruners deviate from their target compression ratio by up to a third

This vault's build-identity rule has four axes so far. Quantization: Q4_K_M non-monotonic across three of four independent GGUF builds of the same model (the-quant-label-is-not-a-spec). Expert count: a community build dropping 320 of 896 experts per layer, 711 GB → 478 GB at unchanged nominal quant (expert-pruning-is-a-second-unlabelled-shrink-axis). Precision allocation: a 40-point spread inside one nominal IQ2_XXS label (precision-is-an-allocation-not-a-scalar). Endpoint: a 27-point accuracy spread across providers serving the same weights (api-endpoint-changes-an-open-models-accuracy-by-27-points).

SNIPER adds a fifth, and unlike the others it comes with a metric. The authors introduce CRAFT — Compression Ratio Adherence Factor — and report that existing structured pruners deviate from their requested compression ratio by up to 33%, against SNIPER's 0.98. Their method replaces greedy per-component heuristics with a binary knapsack solve over coarse components at fixed importance estimates, followed by a fine-grained stage that meets the budget exactly, unifying depth and width pruning in one allocation. Across four architectures (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Phi-4-14B, GPT-OSS-20B), 18 tasks and five domains, it takes a mean rank of 1.25 against six state-of-the-art pruners — 81.58% average retention on Phi-4-14B at 35% against ShortGPT's 77.18% and 2SSP's 70.16%, and 86.99% on GPT-OSS-20B.

The consequence is the diagnostic, not the method. Every retention-versus-compression curve in this literature is plotted against an x-axis with unmeasured error a third wide. Two checkpoints both labelled "35% pruned" can differ in realized size by more than the quality gap the comparison is measuring — the same defect as the quant label, the endpoint, the GPU and the model tier, one axis further down the stack. Unlike the endpoint variance, this one is free to check: a pruned checkpoint's parameter count is a number we can read off the file. Report realized compression, never requested compression, and require the same of anything we adopt.

Two things limit its weight for our decision. This is the wrong shrink axis for our premise — our build order starts from an existing 1.5B–27B checkpoint and post-trains it, and run 19 supplied a suitable Apache-licensed base (an-apache-27b-posts-frontier-band-agentic-coding-numbers), so pruning a 14B or 20B down is a route we would take only if none existed. More limiting: the eighteen tasks are perplexity, multiple choice and NLI — not one agentic, tool-use or instruction-following benchmark among them. An "81.58% average retention" measured entirely on the categories compression treats best is the aggregate this vault has spent ten runs learning not to trust (compression-degrades-agentic-skills-more-than-reasoning). Retention on this battery is not evidence about an agent.

The unpriced item is recovery fine-tuning. Retention is reported with and without RFT on Slim Orca, and the RFT arm's training cost is never stated — which matters because our real comparison is not pruned versus not but prune-plus-recover versus start smaller and post-train, and only one side of that is costed.
