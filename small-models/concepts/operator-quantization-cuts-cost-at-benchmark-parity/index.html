An operator serving frontier-class open weights reports ~30% lower cost per token from quantizing weights and KV cache, at benchmark parity

Cloudflare reports two levers on production serving of Kimi K-series and GLM. KV cache BF16 → FP8 halves cache size: on Kimi K2.6 in-memory context rises from roughly 686,000 to about 1.37 million tokens, peak throughput +41% (2,192 vs 1,558 tok/s), accuracy variance under 1%. Weights FP8 → INT4 on GLM 5.2 shrinks the checkpoint 705 GB → 421 GB, per-GPU memory 88 GB → 52 GB, allows ~1.18M tokens of KV cache on the same hardware, and yields +55% throughput at low concurrency / +16% at 64 concurrent requests, with quality within 0.8 points across benchmarks. Net: roughly 30% lower cost per token. Integrity checking costs under 1% of throughput and latency.

The KV-cache lever is new to this vault and it is the one that fits our traffic. accelerator-memory-capacity-swings-self-hosting-economics found that fitting weights into one node's memory swung serving economics ~45%; this says the cache is the other half of the same memory budget, and halving it roughly doubles the context that can be held resident — the exact constraint long agentic loops hit. For a self-hosting plan, weight precision and cache precision are two independent knobs, both converting into batch size.

The quality claim needs the caveat stated in the same breath. "Within 0.8 points across benchmarks" is measured on the class of evaluation shown to be blind to quantization's actual defect: quantization-erases-long-tail-knowledge-invisibly found reasoning and agentic benchmarks essentially unmoved on the same compression ladder while long-tail factual recall degraded non-linearly, visible only under an obscurity-stratified probe. Nothing here is dishonest — the standard evals are reported and the numbers are likely right — but an aggregate benchmark delta is the summary statistic this vault has now watched conceal a defect class at four pipeline stages. If our workload leans on recall rather than on reasoning traces, 0.8 points is not our number.

Discount for source: a vendor engineering blog with a direct interest in "we serve open models cheaply and well," self-reported, eval suite unnamed, quantization method not reproducible from the post, cost given as a percentage rather than dollars against an API baseline, and no utilization stated — the term self-hosting-savings-erased-below-engineer-scale-thresholds found decisive. The ratios and mechanisms are checkable on our own hardware; the ~30% is theirs.
