"Similar safety profiles" is an underpowered null: high-intensity lifting's fall risk is unknown, not absent

The meta-analysis comparing high-intensity (≥70% 1RM) with low-to-moderate-intensity resistance training in adults ≥50 reported fall incidence RR 2.68 (95% CI 0.65–11.11) and adverse events RR 2.42 (0.66–8.88), neither statistically significant, and concluded this suggests "similar safety profiles."

The vault does not accept that conclusion. "No significant difference in falls (RR 2.68, 95% CI 0.65–11.11)" is true. "Similar safety profiles" does not follow from it. Both point estimates sit above 2 — pointing at harm — and both intervals reach past a near-elevenfold increase in falls. Falls are rare events; 1,283 people spread across 18 heterogeneous trials cannot resolve them. shared/study-appraisal.md lists this exact move — an underpowered null sold as "no effect" — as a fatal reading error (flag #3), whoever makes it. What this comparison delivers is no information about safety, and no information is not reassurance.

Why this matters for the vault, and for David specifically. GOAL.md names knee/joint-safe as a hard constraint, records a March 2026 patellar contusion, and gates return-to-pickleball on consistent stretching. The companion finding on the same page — high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength — recommends ≥70% 1RM lower-limb loading. A source that recommends heavy leg loading in adults over 50 while carrying an unexcluded fall-risk point estimate of 2.68 is precisely the situation shared/safety.md was written for: interaction and safety evidence is asymmetric — a missed signal is worse than an over-cautious flag; raise the flag on any credible signal and tier it in the note.

So the flag is raised and tiered honestly. direction: harm records where the point estimates lie. strength: single-study and stance: open-question record that nothing is demonstrated. Nobody should read this note as evidence that heavy lifting causes falls; they should read it as evidence that the question is open and the vault will not pretend otherwise on David's knee.

Bounded honestly. These were secondary outcomes in a review designed and powered around strength and BMD. Adverse-event ascertainment in exercise RCTs is notoriously inconsistent. The trials pooled healthy older adults alongside sarcopenic and osteoporotic cohorts, whose baseline fall risk differs enormously. And there is a real countervailing literature the vault already holds: daily-steps-broad-healthspan-outcomes found fewer falls with more walking, and stronger legs plausibly prevent falls rather than cause them. That tension is the point — it is unresolved.

The practical shape of the claim. Any regimen expression of high-intensity resistance training is capped at "proposed — review with your doctor / physio" (shared/decision-rule.md, hard overrides). Load selection during patellar-contusion recovery is a clinician and physiotherapist conversation. The vault records the evidence; it does not prescribe a load, and it does not invent one.
