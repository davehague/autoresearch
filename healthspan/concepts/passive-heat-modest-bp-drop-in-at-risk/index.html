Whole-body passive heating produces a modest systolic-BP drop, mostly in at-risk adults

The one signal to survive the 20-RCT meta-analysis: whole-body ("systemic") heating lowered systolic blood pressure by −4.11 mmHg (95% CI −7.36 to −0.86), and in people with coronary risk or established CVD by −2.52 mmHg (−4.26 to −0.79). Local heating did nothing (+0.96 mmHg, ns), and the effect did not extend to vascular-stiffness, glucose, or lipid markers.

Why this matters for the vault. A ~4 mmHg systolic reduction is clinically non-trivial at a population level and is the honest kernel of benefit under the deflating overall result (passive-heat-null-on-most-cardiometabolic-markers). But it is (a) surrogate — BP, not events; (b) concentrated in whole-body heating and higher-risk people, i.e. least applicable to a healthy adult; and (c) from small, biased, unblinded trials. It supports passive heat as a mild adjunct for someone with elevated BP under clinical guidance — not as a general longevity lever.

Bounded honestly: no invented dose or protocol — trials varied widely in temperature, duration, and modality, and the review did not establish an optimal regimen. Any use is a knee-friendly, low-impact comfort/recovery habit with a small BP bonus at best.
