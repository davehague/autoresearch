The best-powered result in the paper is a null, in the disease the cohort is named for

None of the three plant-based diet indices was associated with incident stroke across 700 adjudicated strokes in REGARDS — the REasons for Geographic And Racial Differences in Stroke cohort, built for this endpoint and powered for it.

⚑ This is the paper's cleanest analysis and it is the one its conclusion passes over. The abstract lands on "Healthy plant-based diets appear to support heart health," which is a statement about coronary outcomes; the stroke null gets one clause. This vault's standing rule, written on this same map for the MIND trial, is that when a paper's headline disagrees with its most credible analysis, the most credible analysis is the finding (null-diet-trial-recovered-by-adherence-subsetting).

⚠ It is a null with the usual observational limits, and they cut both ways. Exposure is a single food-frequency questionnaire with known validation error, which biases toward the null through misclassification. So this does not establish that plant-based eating is irrelevant to stroke — it establishes that in the largest, longest, best-adjudicated look at that question in an under-studied US population, the association did not appear.

⚑ A well-powered null in the outcome a cohort was designed for is worth more than a moderate hazard ratio in an outcome it was not, and shared/evidence-model.md is explicit that Status moves both ways. What it moves here is not mediterranean-diet's Status — that map rests on all-cause mortality across 1.8 million adults — but the breadth of what a dietary-pattern score can be claimed to protect against.

⚑ The second null in this paper is also worth carrying: associations did not differ by race, in a cohort recruited specifically to test that. A null on effect modification, from a study designed to find it, is a real contribution.
