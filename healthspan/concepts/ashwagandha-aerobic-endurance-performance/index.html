Ashwagandha's clearest ergogenic effect is on aerobic endurance — in young athletes

A three-level meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials (599 participants, 79 effect sizes) found ashwagandha improved overall exercise performance (Hedges' g = 0.47, 95% CI 0.25–0.69, I²=60%), with exercise type the clearest moderator (P_between = 0.006). The most consistent domain was aerobic endurance, g = 0.54 (0.22–0.85), p=0.002. Strength effects were positive but uncertain; power and muscular endurance were too sparsely studied to adjudicate. Dose analyses were explicitly hypothesis-generating; the source reports 500–600 mg/day of extract as its most evidence-supported range.

Why this matters for the vault. ashwagandha rests on a single meta-analysis (ashwagandha-lowers-cortisol, ashwagandha-subjective-stress-anxiety-soft) with two recorded weaknesses: subjective endpoints and an industry-adjacent trial base. This source repairs both — its outcomes are objective performance measures, and its authors declare no conflicts of interest — and it opens a second, independent thread on a different outcome family. That is exactly the independence the map's logged gap was asking for, arriving on an axis nobody was watching.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, and why population is the binding constraint). The source's reliability verdict is mixed, capping strength. But the harder limit is not evidence tier — it is population. These are young adults and athletes; one cohort averaged 17.4 years old. Ergogenic findings in trained young athletes are among the least transferable results in sports nutrition, and study_population records that as the neutral fact it is — population-match to David is computed at regimen time (shared/evidence-model.md). David is a middle-aged adult recovering from a patellar contusion whose aerobic goals are knee-safe steps and Zone-2 (daily-steps, zone-2-cardio), not competitive endurance. A g of 0.54 on a young athlete's time-to-exhaustion test is not a healthspan outcome, and this vault does not launder ergogenic surrogates into longevity claims. Add: 13 small trials, I²=60%, and unstandardized extracts whose withanolide content differs substantially between brands.

The safety_flags: [interaction] and a timing tension worth naming. Ashwagandha already carries the wind-down cluster's interaction flag — it is sedating, and stacking it with l-theanine, chamomile, magnesium-glycinate, and melatonin is redundant sedation, not additive benefit. This source adds a wrinkle: its better-evidenced effect is a daytime, pre-exercise one, dosed hours away from the evening use-case the cluster assumes. The flag holds; the map now records that ashwagandha's two threads point at different times of day.

No dose is recommended — the vault reports the source's 500–600 mg/day range as the source's finding, alongside Bachour's ~125–600 mg/day, and synthesizes neither (shared/safety.md).
