A supervised multidomain programme produced musculoskeletal symptoms in about one participant in five

LatAm-FINGERS reported 478 adverse events: 412 in the structured intervention arm against 66 in the health-advice arm. The largest single category was musculoskeletal symptoms — 113 (21%) in the intervention group versus 13 (2%) in the control group. Serious adverse events occurred in 50 (9%) of intervention participants versus 24 (5%) of controls. There were eight deaths (3 intervention, 5 control), none judged related to the intervention.

This is the cost side of the vault's largest multidomain benefit, measured in the same trial that produced it. A structured programme that adds supervised exercise to the lives of 60–77-year-olds generates roughly six times the adverse-event count of the advice-only arm, and puts a musculoskeletal complaint on one participant in five. None of it is surprising and none of it argues against the intervention — moving more means more sore joints — but the number exists, it is unflattering, and it is absent from every summary of this trial the vault could find.

The discipline has to survive contact with an intervention the vault likes. Here the signal is stronger than in Shen 2026, not weaker: it is a direct count, not an underpowered interval, and it is large and one-sided.

Read at its true strength. These are aggregate event counts by arm, not a per-component attribution: the trial cannot say which domain — exercise, diet, cognitive training, vascular monitoring — produced the musculoskeletal symptoms, though exercise is the obvious candidate. Ascertainment was also plausibly asymmetric: an arm with weekly supervised contact has far more opportunity to report a sore knee than an arm receiving advice, so some of the 6× gap is surveillance, not injury. The vault records the number without converting it into an injury rate.
