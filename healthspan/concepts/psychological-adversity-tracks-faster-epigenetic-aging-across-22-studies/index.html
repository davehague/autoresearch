A 22-study meta-analysis agrees with the vault's single genetic study: loneliness tracks faster biological aging

Loneliness, depression, and chronic stress each show a consistent, positive association with epigenetic age acceleration on second-generation clocks (PhenoAge, GrimAge) across a meta-analysis of 22 studies. The loneliness pool is the cleanest: β=0.07 (95% CI 0.06–0.08) with zero between-study heterogeneity (I²=0%) — depression and stress show the same direction at larger, noisier effect sizes (I²=55.2% and 68.4% respectively).

This confirms, with a different and larger design, what social-connection has held on a single study. loneliness-causally-associated-with-biological-age-acceleration-mendelian-randomization used genetic-variant instruments in one 309,746-person UK Biobank cohort to argue against simple reverse causation; this source pools 22 separate observational cohorts and lands the same direction. Neither design alone proves causality — MR carries its own instrument-validity assumptions, and this meta-analysis cannot rule out adversity-as-consequence-of-aging — but two structurally different vulnerabilities producing the same answer is the strongest form of triangulation this vault can currently offer on the question.

Why suggestive, not stronger. Observational on both sides for 22 of 22 studies, small effect sizes on a clock-model-unit scale with no stated years-of-aging translation, and meaningful heterogeneity for two of the three pooled adversities. Appraised mixed.

The clock-generation specificity is the sharper finding for biological-age-clocks. The positive association held specifically for second-generation clocks (PhenoAge, GrimAge) across all three adversities and was not reported for first-generation clocks (Horvath, Hannum) in the retrievable abstract — consistent with that map's standing rule that clock generation changes the answer (first-generation-clock-metabolic-signatures-failed-external-replication).
