The diet does not raise testosterone. The weight loss does.

Across the Mediterranean diet, ketogenic/very-low-carbohydrate diets and intermittent fasting, weight loss emerged as the critical mediator common to all three — approximately 3 nmol/L of total testosterone per 10 kg lost.

⚑ Put that beside the paper's own headline and the ranking dissolves. The reported VLCKD advantage is +1.5 to +3.0 nmol/L. The mediator predicts 3 nmol/L per 10 kg. The entire ketogenic effect is what 5–10 kg of weight loss buys, however the weight came off — and VLCKD is characterised by rapid early weight loss. The review's mechanistic account leads with "rapid weight loss, improved insulin sensitivity, reduced inflammation" and appends "potential direct HPG axis effects" without evidence isolating one. The ranking is a weight-loss-speed ranking wearing a macronutrient costume.

⚑ This is the fourth independent confirmation of the same pattern in this vault, on a fourth outcome. ketogenic-advantage-under-matched-energy-is-about-one-and-a-half-kilograms — match energy and keto's weight advantage shrinks to 1.49 kg at GRADE LOW. ketogenic-advantage-becomes-uninformative-when-protein-is-also-matched — match protein too and it says nothing. intermittent-fasting-matches-caloric-restriction-for-weight and intermittent-fasting-cardiometabolic-markers-match-caloric-restriction — fasting equals plain calorie restriction. Four literatures, four outcomes, one answer: the composition is not the lever, the deficit is.

⚠ The mechanism is unsurprising and that is a point in its favour. Adipose tissue expresses aromatase, which converts testosterone to oestradiol; less fat means less conversion, alongside recovering SHBG and improving insulin sensitivity. A finding that follows from established physiology needs less evidential support than a surprising one — which is fortunate, because the support here is weak.

⚠ Part of the measured gain may be a binding-protein artefact. Most included studies measured total testosterone without SHBG. SHBG rises as insulin resistance improves, mechanically raising total testosterone while the free, bioavailable fraction moves less. An unknown share of the 3 nmol/L is plausibly not usable androgen. The review flags this; no included study resolves it.

⚠ Grading. Tiered cohort rather than rct: the mediator is a cross-study observation from a narrative synthesis that ran no meta-analysis, not a randomised contrast of matched-deficit diets — which is the trial nobody has done. Capped suggestive by a mixed source that was not PROSPERO-registered, searched only SciSpace, Google Scholar and PubMed, and pooled 7 animal studies into a human clinical synthesis.

Scope. Men with BMI ≥ 30 and suppressed or low-normal baseline testosterone. The mediator is weight loss, so the effect exists only where there is substantial weight to lose and a depressed baseline to recover from. It does not generalise to men of normal weight with normal levels.
