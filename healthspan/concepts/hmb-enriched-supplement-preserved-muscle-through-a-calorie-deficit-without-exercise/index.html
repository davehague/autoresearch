A 24 g protein-and-HMB powder held muscle through a 12-week deficit that cost the control arm 0.6 kg of it

In a double-blind, energy-matched randomised trial (n = 102, ITT, 5.9% attrition), adults with obesity on a prescribed calorie deficit took either a 65 g/day supplement containing 3.0 g calcium-HMB and 24 g protein or 65 g/day of maltodextrin. Over 12 weeks the supplement arm held skeletal muscle mass (median +0.7 kg) while the control arm lost it (median −0.6 kg) — a between-group difference of 1.3 kg (95% CI 0.5–1.9, p < 0.001). 52.9% of the supplement arm gained at least 0.5 kg of muscle against 11.8% of controls (OR 8.44, 3.23–25.22), and basal metabolic rate was preserved rather than falling (+62.0 kcal/day, 35.0–89.0).

⚑ What makes this worth carrying: no exercise, by design. Participants were sedentary at baseline by inclusion criterion, no training was prescribed, and physical activity was measured by IPAQ-SF at baseline and weeks 3, 6, 9 and 12 with no between-group difference at any timepoint. This is the cleanest isolation of a nutritional lever from a training lever in the vault — the thing multidomain-lifestyle's standing unseparable-bundle complaint has been asking for and not getting.

⚑ And a confound that runs the useful direction. The supplement arm also lost far more fat (6.6 kg vs 1.5 kg), which almost certainly means it ate less — 24 g of protein and 9.5 g of fibre satiate; 65 g of maltodextrin does not (energy-matched-supplement-arms-diverged-fourfold-on-fat-loss). A steeper deficit normally costs MORE lean mass. This arm was on the steeper deficit and lost none, while the control on the shallower deficit lost 0.6 kg. The uncontrolled difference between arms therefore works against this finding rather than producing it, which is the one configuration in which a confound strengthens a result.

⚠ Why suggestive and not strong, and why the concept is not named after HMB. The intervention arm received at least seven things the control did not — HMB, 24 g protein, 9.5 g fibre, 9.3 g fat, L-glutamine, wheat oligopeptides, and vitamins D and E — against plain sugar. The trial is energy-matched and nothing-else-matched, so it cannot attribute the effect to any single ingredient, and the authors say so (hmb-trial-cannot-separate-hmb-from-the-24-grams-of-protein-beside-it). The source is appraised mixed and caps every concept drawn from it. The most parsimonious candidate is the one this vault already grades at consolidating: total daily protein (protein-target, plateauing ~1.6 g/kg/day), added to people who were not hitting it, in the exact condition where protein's muscle-sparing effect is best established.

The instrument is the weakest rung on this map's own gradient. Muscle mass was measured by multifrequency bioelectrical impedance (InBody 770), carefully standardised (fasted, empty bladder, 15 min seated, fixed morning window, one technician). fat-free-mass holds fat-free-mass-measurement-method-gradient — the finding that this literature's signal shrinks as the measurement improves, with BIA weakest. BIA infers lean mass from body water, and the two arms differed in protein, fibre and fat intake and therefore very likely in hydration and glycogen. A DXA replication is the single thing that would most change how this result reads.

What it does not say. Nothing about anyone on a GLP-1 — drug-driven and diet-driven weight loss are not established as the same exposure, and this vault refuses that transfer. Nothing about older or sarcopenic adults (mean age 36.8). Nothing beyond 12 weeks, so nothing about durability or regain. Nothing about people who train, which is the better-evidenced intervention this trial deliberately excluded. And nothing that establishes a case for buying HMB.
