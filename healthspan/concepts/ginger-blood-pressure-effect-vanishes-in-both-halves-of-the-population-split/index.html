Ginger's five-millimetre blood-pressure drop is not significant in people with diabetes, and not significant in people without it

The pooled estimate looks useful: systolic −5.03 mmHg (95% CI −8.49 to −1.57), p = 0.004; diastolic −2.03 mmHg (−3.90 to −0.15), p = 0.034, across 12 randomised trials.

⚑ Then the authors split the population by health status and the effect is gone on both sides. Verbatim: "Stratification by health status revealed non-significant reductions among both T2DM and non-T2DM participants." Not significant in people with diabetes. Not significant in people without diabetes. The shorter trials (≤ 8 weeks) reach only p = 0.053.

A pooled estimate that is significant while every partition of its population is not is the arithmetic signature of between-study variance, not of a treatment effect. I² = 96.6% for systolic and 94.0% for diastolic says the same thing in the other notation, and it is GRADE's own stated reason for rating both outcomes low certainty (⊕⊕◯◯) — "very serious inconsistency."

⚠ No healthy population is in the pool. Every included cohort is metabolically or immunologically ill — type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, NAFLD, pre-hypertension, hypertension, allergic rhinitis, elevated adiposity — in trials of 11 to 81 participants lasting 4 to 12 weeks. shared/evidence-model.md requires study_population record the actual cohort, and there is no basis here for any claim about a healthy adult.

⚠ What is genuinely good about this paper, recorded so the refusal is of the evidence and not of the authors. RoB 2 applied. GRADE applied and reported honestly as low. Publication bias tested four ways and absent (Egger SBP p = 0.187, DBP p = 0.378; Begg SBP p = 0.246, DBP p = 0.304). No funding and no conflicts, in a supplement literature where sponsorship is the norm. And the authors disclose in their own limitations that "the review protocol was not prospectively registered." They printed every one of the things that sink the estimate.

⚑ This lands in the vault's standing blood-pressure pattern rather than breaking it. stretching-blood-pressure-effect-is-larger-than-the-better-evidenced-levers records that the interventions reporting the biggest millimetre drops here are consistently the ones with the weakest evidence, while resistance-training-lowers-blood-pressure-older-adults — the best-evidenced lever — reports less. A −5.03 mmHg from twelve trials of eleven-to-eighty-one people across seven diseases at I² above 96% is that pattern arriving again.
