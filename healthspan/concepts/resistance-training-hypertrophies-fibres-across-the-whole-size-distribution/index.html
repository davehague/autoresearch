Resistance training grew the fast fibres across their entire size range — and the fibres outgrew their blood supply

Eight weeks of supervised whole-body resistance training in seventeen recreationally active young men, with muscle biopsied before and after and analysed fibre type by fibre type at three points across the size distribution rather than only at the average.

What the distributional analysis adds over a mean. A muscle sample contains fibres spanning a wide size range, and a handful of unusually large ones can move an average without anything typical having changed. Quantile mixed models ask separately whether the small fibres (10th percentile), the typical fibres (median) and the large fibres (90th percentile) each grew. Type IIA fibres — the fast, fatigue-resistant ones that carry most of the hypertrophy response — grew at all three. Median type IIA area went from 5,232 µm² to 5,925 µm². Type I fibres, the slow oxidative ones, grew only at the median. So the hypertrophic response was broad in the fast fibres and patchy in the slow ones, with the authors noting that median type I area rose in nine participants and fell in three.

A composition shift toward the oxidative end. Type IIX fibres — the fastest, most glycolytic, least fatigue-resistant type — went from 1.2% of fibres to effectively zero (median 0.0%, IQR 0.0–1.3), and IIA/IIX hybrid fibres roughly halved, from 8.9% to 4.4%. This is the well-described IIX→IIA transition that follows resistance training: the muscle does not gain new fibre types so much as convert its most explosive fibres into larger, more sustainable ones. VO₂max and fat oxidation capacity rose alongside it — in a group being fed carbohydrate during every session, which the authors flag as the more surprising part.

⚑ The finding nothing else in this vault holds: hypertrophy outran the blood supply. Capillaries relative to fibre area fell in type IIA fibres at the median and upper percentiles, while capillaries around each fibre did not change. In plain terms — the fibres got bigger and the number of vessels feeding them did not keep pace, so each unit of muscle had less capillary supply after eight weeks than before. Whether that matters functionally over a longer programme is untested here. It is recorded because it is a measured, directional cost inside the vault's foundation lever, and this ledger holds no other observation of it.

⚠ The gating limitation, and it is not small: there is no non-training control arm. Both randomised groups trained identically; the randomisation was of an intra-workout drink, which changed nothing (intra-workout-protein-adds-nothing-when-daily-total-is-already-met). Every finding above is a main effect of time in people who all trained — a before-and-after, not a randomised contrast. Eight weeks of supervised training producing hypertrophy and strength gains in recreationally trained 25-year-olds is also about as unsurprising as a result in this literature gets, and this vault already holds resistance-training at high-tier evidence including direct mortality data (resistance-training-lowers-mortality). This note adds mechanism and texture, not support.

Graded single-study for that reason, and because the source is appraised mixed — 17 completers of 29 randomised, no a priori power calculation, participants drawing their own allocation envelopes, and a cohort collected under 2009–2010 ethics approvals whose gene-expression results were published separately. What earns it a place is the measurement: biopsy-level fibre-type morphometry, cross-checked against DXA lean mass, with large-fibre outliers visually verified against the original sections rather than assumed to be artefacts.

A note on the training programme itself, recorded as description not prescription. 2–4 sets of 8–15 repetitions, 2–3 minutes rest, ~2-second concentric and eccentric tempo, 2–4 sessions per week, with the final set taken to concentric failure in alternate weeks only. That undulating failure/non-failure structure is interesting against rt-non-failure-beats-failure-dynamic-strength, where non-failure training outperformed training to failure for dynamic strength — this trial did both on alternating weeks and cannot separate them.
