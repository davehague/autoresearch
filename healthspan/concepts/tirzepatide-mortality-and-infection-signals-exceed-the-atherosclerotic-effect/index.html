Tirzepatide's mortality and infection numbers are bigger than atherosclerosis can explain

The same cohort that produced a 1.4 percentage-point reduction in major cardiovascular events also reported, at one year: all-cause mortality HR 0.55 (0.42–0.72), hospital admission for infection HR 0.64 (0.55–0.75), and infection-related mortality HR 0.40 (0.26–0.61). The authors read this as evidence that "broader non-atherosclerotic biological mechanisms may contribute to the observed benefit."

Why this is an open question and not a finding. A 45% reduction in death from any cause over twelve months is larger than any randomised trial of any GLP-1 or dual agonist has produced — larger, notably, than SELECT, which was randomised, blinded, and ran four times longer. When an observational study reports a bigger effect than the randomised trial of the same drug class, on a harder endpoint, over a shorter window, the base rate says the excess came from the design.

And the infection results point at the specific confounder. Frailty is what determines whether an older adult is hospitalised with an infection and whether that infection kills them. A cohort in which the tirzepatide starters were systematically less frail — in ways insurance claims do not record: gait speed, appetite, cognition, functional status, who is being cared for at home — would produce exactly this constellation: fewer infection admissions, far fewer infection deaths, and a large all-cause mortality gap.

The study's negative controls do not test that. Two outcomes the drug should not affect — lumbar radiculopathy and abdominal hernia — were checked and were null, which is the right instinct. But those are diagnoses that ambulatory, functioning people receive; they are insensitive to a frailty gradient. A negative control rules out only the confounding it can detect, and these two cannot detect the explanation that fits the secondary results.

Why this matters for the vault. It cuts both ways and that is the point. glp1-optic-neuropathy-signal-may-be-residual-confounding recorded an observational harm signal in this drug class that plausibly reflected who takes the drug rather than what the drug does. Here the same class of design produces an implausibly large benefit. The scepticism has to be symmetric or it is not scepticism. It also caps the primary result, because the composite cardiovascular endpoint contains all-cause mortality: if part of the mortality gap is confounding, part of the headline inherits it (tirzepatide-lowers-mace-against-a-cardiovascular-neutral-comparator).

Bounded honestly. The broader-mechanism reading is not refuted — GLP-1 receptor agonism has documented anti-inflammatory effects, and weight loss itself changes infection risk. It is simply not the parsimonious explanation for effects of this size in one year, and this design cannot separate the two. What would settle it: a randomised trial reporting infection outcomes, or an analysis with a frailty-sensitive negative control.
