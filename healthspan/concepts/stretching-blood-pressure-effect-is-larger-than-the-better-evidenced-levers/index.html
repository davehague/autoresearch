Stretching allegedly lowers systolic pressure by 10 mmHg, without moving the stiffness it would have to work through

In a network of 47 randomised trials, stretching lowered systolic blood pressure by 10.43 mmHg (95% CI −18.88 to −1.99) and took the top rank on that outcome at SUCRA 91.8%. In the same network, on the same participants, stretching is non-significant on brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity and non-significant on carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity — the two arterial-stiffness measures through which a mechanical intervention would plausibly have to act.

Why the number does not survive contact with the rest of the vault. resistance-training lowers systolic pressure by 6.11 mmHg across 51 randomised trials and 2,025 older adults (resistance-training-lowers-blood-pressure-older-adults) — an effect built from progressive loading, measured on twenty times the evidence, and already treated here as clinically meaningful. This paper reports stretching beating it by two-thirds, from a modality the same authors define as involving no external load and no sustained cardiopulmonary demand, with the number of contributing trials never published. A 10 mmHg systolic reduction is at the scale of a single antihypertensive drug.

This is the vault's implausible-magnitude signature, and it now has a fifth instance. The pattern: a large effect from a low-intensity intervention, on a small number of studies, with the supporting mechanism missing from the paper's own results. It fired twice on collagen, once on ashwagandha, on every SUCRA leaderboard the vault has declined, and two days before this on Logan 2026's stretching result of SMD −1.02 for pulse wave velocity (longitudinal-stretching-lowers-arterial-stiffness).

⚠ And the relationship between those two stretching results is the sharpest part. Logan found stretching moving pulse wave velocity by a full standard deviation. Wen finds stretching non-significant on pulse wave velocity — by both measures — while producing a large blood-pressure effect Logan's network did not feature. Each paper's large stretching effect lands on the outcome the other finds nothing on. Two independent syntheses in the same week, on the same intervention, disagreeing about which measurement moves, is not corroboration. It is what a small, noisy literature looks like when it is sampled twice.

Graded single-study deliberately, below the source's suggestive cap. The source is appraised mixed, which would permit suggestive. This specific estimate is held lower because it has no per-node trial count, no supporting mechanism inside its own network, and an actively disagreeing independent source. It is recorded so a future run does not rediscover it as new, and it is not adopted.

What must not be concluded. stretching earns its place from GOAL.md's pickleball gate and from a 79-randomised-trial range-of-motion result, not from cardiovascular claims. Reading this estimate as "stretching is blood-pressure treatment" would be exactly the laundering shared/safety.md blocks — and would put a mobility behaviour in the place of a clinician conversation.
