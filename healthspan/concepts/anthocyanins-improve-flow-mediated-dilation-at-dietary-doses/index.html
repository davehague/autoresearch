Sixty-five randomised trials, one durable finding: arteries widen slightly better

Flow-mediated dilation — how much an artery widens after blood flow is briefly occluded and released, read by ultrasound — rose 1.41 percentage points (0.90 to 1.91) with chronic anthocyanin intake and 1.50 (1.16 to 1.84) acutely, in healthy participants at a pre-specified dose of ≥50 mg/day. GRADE rated the acute result strong and the chronic one moderate. Insulin fell slightly (−2.24 pmol/L).

Both intervals clear zero with room to spare, which distinguishes this from the marginal findings elsewhere in this vault. It is a machine-measured outcome, not a questionnaire, replicated across a large trial base.

It is still a surrogate. FMD tracks with later cardiovascular events; it is not an event. A 1.41-point improvement in artery diameter is not a heart attack prevented, and the randomised evidence here reaches no further than this marker (anthocyanin-hard-endpoint-evidence-is-observational-only).

What the same 65 trials did not move matters as much: lipids, blood pressure, glucose and insulin resistance were all null (anthocyanin-randomised-evidence-is-null-on-lipids-blood-pressure-and-glucose).

The dose is food, not a capsule. 50 mg/day is reachable from ordinary portions of berries. No trial here tested an extract or supplement against food, and no supplement dose is stated or derivable.
