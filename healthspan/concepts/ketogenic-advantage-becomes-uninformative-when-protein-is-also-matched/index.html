The analysis that could have been quietly omitted, and dissolves the headline

Żurawski 2026's registered protocol defined a protein-matching criterion — arms no more than 0.3 g/kg/day or 5 percentage points of energy apart — and then reported how many included trials met it. Three of six did. In the other three, the ketogenic arm ate proportionally more protein than its comparator.

Restricted to the three matched trials, the pooled estimate is −1.79 kg (95% CI −5.69 to +2.11), p = 0.187.

Protein is the confound with the strongest prior here, and the authors name the mechanism themselves: higher intake "may increase satiety and diet-induced thermogenesis and may help preserve fat-free mass during energy restriction" — effects that "can influence spontaneous energy intake and the composition of weight loss even when the prescribed energy targets are comparable." A ketogenic diet with more protein than its comparator is not a test of carbohydrate restriction; it is a test of two things at once.

⚠ Read precisely, because this interval is uninformative in both directions. It runs from −5.69 to +2.11. That is compatible with no difference at all, with keto being nearly four times better than the headline, and with keto being worse. The authors' own sentence is the correct one: "this analysis neither confirms nor excludes an independent effect of carbohydrate restriction when protein intake is matched." This is an absence of a demonstrated effect, not a demonstration of absence — the distinction shared/study-appraisal.md insists on and this vault has applied to five underpowered nulls on resistance-training-programming and to the near-miss on magnesium-null-on-insulin-resistance.

So the cleanest available test of the actual proposition — does restricting carbohydrate itself do anything, holding calories and protein constant — is three trials wide and says nothing. ⚑ That is the single most important fact on the new map, and it is more informative than the significant headline, because it identifies exactly what is missing rather than estimating something that conflates two exposures.

⚑ What makes this source unusual is that it ran the analysis at all. The exploratory protein-matched subgroup could have been dropped without any reader noticing — it was registered, and registered analyses go missing routinely. Instead it is reported, discussed at length, and allowed to qualify the paper's own conclusion. This vault spent last night cataloguing three papers whose statistics could not support their claims; this is a paper that published the statistic ruling out its own headline, the same behaviour that earned Hu 2026's prediction interval its grading on sedentary-behavior.

The map's top open gap, stated at coining: a controlled-feeding trial matching both energy and protein, running longer than twelve weeks, measuring body composition and verifying ketosis biochemically. The authors ask for exactly that.
