A direct head-to-head finds 16:8 and 5:2 fasting cost the same 0.6 kg of muscle, whichever one you pick

Randomised directly against each other — not each against a non-fasting control — daily 16:8 time-restricted eating and weekly 5:2 fasting produced statistically indistinguishable muscle-mass loss (−0.6 kg, 95% CI −0.7 to −0.4, both arms) over 4 weeks in 68 women with overweight or obesity, while 5:2 ran numerically ahead on weight (pooled −2.7 kg) and fat-mass (pooled −1.7 kg) trajectories over time.

Why this is worth banking despite being small. intermittent-fasting's standing claim — that fasting variants are a delivery format for calorie restriction, not separate metabolic levers — has mostly rested on large network meta-analyses comparing protocols indirectly through a shared comparator. This is a small, short, single-sex trial, but it is a genuine direct randomisation between two active protocols, and the muscle-loss number landing identically in both arms is exactly what "format, not lever" predicts: the cost tracks the energy deficit, not which days you take it on.

Read the lean-mass number as a caution, not a reassurance. This map already carries, from other designs, that intake reduction costs lean mass unless protein intake is defended (shared thread with protein-target and fat-free-mass, e.g. early-time-restricted-eating-lowered-lean-body-mass). A fourth-week 0.6 kg loss that doesn't distinguish 16:8 from 5:2 extends that thread rather than complicating it.

Why suggestive, not strong. n=68, 4 weeks, women only, open-label (no blinding is feasible for a dietary schedule), no power calculation stated, and full text was not reached so the statistical significance of the between-arm weight/fat difference is UNVERIFIED — it may be a trend read off a trajectory rather than a tested contrast. The source is appraised mixed.

What it does not say. Nothing about men, nothing past 4 weeks, no hard outcome, and no claim that 5:2 is definitively better than 16:8 — only that neither protocol spares muscle more than the other over this short window. No schedule or window is recommended (shared/safety.md); the trial's own protocols are reported as evidence, not advice.
