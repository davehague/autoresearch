After 79 randomized trials, nobody can say how much stretching to do

Oba 2026 pooled 79 RCTs (n=3,287) and established that chronic static stretching improves lower-limb flexibility at g = 0.851. It did not establish how much stretching that takes. The authors state plainly that stretch volume, intensity and frequency were inconsistently reported across the included trials and were not examined as moderators.

This is the number a least-work framework most needs, and the largest synthesis of the literature declines to supply it. GOAL.md's organizing principle is least amount of work and least discomfort for the maximum benefit, and shared/safety.md forbids synthesizing a dose that no source states. So the vault's position is explicit: stretching works; the minimum effective amount of it is unknown; and no protocol will be manufactured from a pooled estimate that averaged over the question.

Why the absence is structural rather than an oversight. Reporting of stretching protocols in this literature is genuinely inconsistent — hold time, sets, sessions per week, weeks of intervention, and intensity ("point of discomfort" versus "maximal tolerable") are recorded differently or not at all across trials. A moderator analysis needs a common unit, and there isn't one. A meta-analysis cannot recover a dose its primary studies never reported.

The contrast with the vault's other behavioural levers is instructive. daily-steps has a dose-response curve with an inflection (~5,000–7,000 steps/day) and zone-2-cardio has a front-loaded volume curve with a ~75 min/week floor and ~150 min/week knee — both from observational data, both imperfect, but both specifiable. Stretching, with a randomized base of seventy-nine trials, is less prescribable than either. Design quality and prescriptive usefulness are different axes, and this is the clearest example of the gap in the vault.

Read at its true strength. mixed, capped suggestive. This is an absence recorded as a gap, not a finding: the trials that produced g = 0.851 obviously used some dose, and any of them individually would name one. What does not exist is a synthesised, defensible answer — and per shared/safety.md, picking one trial's protocol and presenting it as the dose would be exactly the invention the vault forbids.

What would resolve it. A dose-response meta-analysis or a randomized dose-comparison trial of static stretching — hold time × frequency × weeks against range of motion. It would be straightforward to run and, as of this synthesis, has not been.

For David: stretch consistently, because consistency is the part the evidence supports and GOAL.md names. Do not expect a prescription from this vault, and treat any confident "30 seconds × 3 sets × 5 days" advice as someone else's convention rather than a finding.
