Fifty-eight percent of the retrieved trials were dropped because their numbers were not extractable, and nobody wrote to ask

The retrieval was excellent and the inclusion was not. The search identified 17,195 records, screened 9,058 after de-duplication, and retrieved 66 full-text reports with none unavailable — a retrieval record most syntheses cannot match, and better than tai-chi's ingest on the same night, where 25 of 72 full texts could not be obtained at all.

⚑ Then 38 of those 66 reports — 58% — were dropped, not for design, population or outcome, but because "compatible arm-level post-intervention quantitative input for the prioritized eligible outcome was unavailable or could not be derived without unverifiable assumptions." The primary network that survived holds 12 studies and 491 participants.

And the paper states plainly what it did not do: "Study authors were not contacted for missing numerical data, and no unpublished arm-level numerical data were incorporated."

⚑ Selection into this network was therefore governed by how completely each paper reported its own numbers — and reporting completeness is not independent of what the numbers said. Trials with clean, quotable, arm-level post-intervention means and standard deviations are systematically more likely to be trials that found something worth tabulating. Trials that reported only a P value, only a within-group change, or only a figure are disproportionately the ones whose results were unremarkable. This is a soft form of the same mechanism as publication bias, operating one stage later — not "which trials got published" but "which published trials got into the pool."

⚠ Two things stop this from being a straightforward criticism. (1) The paper declares it. Most syntheses give a full-text exclusion count and a reason category and leave it there; this one states the decision rule and the deliberate non-contact policy in the results text, which is what made this note possible. (2) The alternative is not obviously better. Deriving arm-level inputs "without unverifiable assumptions" is exactly the imputation practice that produces the implausibly homogeneous pools this vault flagged elsewhere in the same run (near-zero-heterogeneity-across-wildly-heterogeneous-trials-is-a-signal-not-a-reassurance). A synthesis that refuses to invent standard deviations is making a defensible trade — it buys a cleaner pool at the cost of a smaller and non-randomly selected one.

⚑ What it means for reading the result. The paper's own headline estimates rest on the 42% of retrieved trials that reported their numbers well, and the paper does not assess how the excluded 58% might have differed. Combined with a statistically detectable direct-versus-indirect disagreement on the Pilates node (the-pilates-balance-effect-is-indirect-and-its-direct-evidence-is-null) and estimates that mostly collapse when force-plate outcomes are removed (balance-effects-shrink-when-laboratory-posturography-is-dropped), the network is best read as a demonstration of fragility rather than a source of effect sizes.

The transferable habit. ⚑ In a PRISMA flow diagram, read the gap between "full texts retrieved" and "studies included," and read the reason. A large gap for design or population reasons is normal screening. A large gap for data-availability reasons is a selection mechanism, and if authors were not contacted it is an avoidable one.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal.
