The claim that aspirin causes late cardiovascular harm after you stop it is the fragile part of this paper

ASPREE's extended follow-up reports that during the post-trial period — a median 4.3 years after the randomised phase ended — participants originally randomised to aspirin had more major adverse cardiovascular events: HR 1.17 (95% CI 1.01–1.36). This is the paper's most novel result. It is also the one the vault declines to believe on this evidence, and the reasons are all stated in the paper itself.

Four of them, and they compound.

It is not a randomised comparison, and the authors say so — the phrase in the paper is "a non-randomized comparison." After the trial stopped, post-trial aspirin use was similar between the two originally randomised groups. So this is not aspirin versus no aspirin; it is a legacy-effect contrast between two groups now taking comparable amounts of the drug.

The subset is consent-selected. Only participants who had no in-trial cardiovascular event and agreed to be followed further entered this analysis — 15,668 of 19,114. Inverse-probability weighting was applied, but differential willingness to be followed, correlated with health, is the selection mechanism weighting is least able to repair.

There is no multiplicity adjustment, explicitly. The paper states that "two-sided 5% significance levels were used… without adjustment for the multiple outcomes being tested" — and it tests two outcomes across three time windows. A result whose interval runs from 1.01 to 1.36 is exactly the result that choice decides.

And the signal sits in a post-randomisation subgroup. The companion post-trial bleeding analysis reached borderline significance only in participants who did not adhere to their randomised assignment during the trial — a group defined by their own later behaviour, which reintroduces precisely the confounding that randomisation removed.

Why record it at all. Because the alternative to recording it honestly is a future run either treating it as established or rediscovering it as news. shared/evidence-model.md has a status for this: open-question.

And because the discipline has to be symmetric. This vault spent three weeks refusing nulls that were too underpowered to mean anything — five times on resistance-training-programming, and again on a null that would have been convenient (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit). Two days ago it accepted two nulls at full strength because the power was genuinely there (aspirin-no-effect-on-healthspan-loss-older-adults, sit-vo2max-gain-independent-of-sprint-number). Refusing a barely-significant positive from a non-randomised window is the same rule pointed the other way. The distinguishing variable was always the strength of the design and the width of the interval, not whether the answer was welcome.
