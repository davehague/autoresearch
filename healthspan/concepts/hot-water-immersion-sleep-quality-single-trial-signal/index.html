One trial says a hot bath helps you sleep — the heat map's first sleep signal, and it stays unbought

In a single crossover trial, post-exercise hot-water immersion improved subjective sleep quality against control (p < 0.001) and against cold-water immersion (p = 0.017) on a 0–100 mm visual-analogue scale, and produced less fatigue than contrast water therapy (p = 0.050). No effect size is reported — the source gives direction and p-values only.

This is the first sleep thread on sauna-heat, which until now held one deflationary finding. That map has stood since 2026-07-07 on the result that passive heating is null on FMD, PWV, glucose and lipids with only a modest systolic-BP drop (passive-heat-null-on-most-cardiometabolic-markers, passive-heat-modest-bp-drop-in-at-risk), summarised as comfort and recovery, not a cardiometabolic lever. A sleep benefit would be a genuinely new axis.

Capped single-study, and the comparison it has to survive is unforgiving. The source is appraised unreliable — this p < 0.001 is one significant cell among dozens of uncorrected comparisons across eight small studies with a mean PEDro of 5 whose commonest failing is unblinded assessors, on a visual-analogue scale, in 14–40 young athletes, six of eight studies male-only. Meanwhile the vault knows what the sleep field's numbers look like: the entire supplement shelf pools to PSQI −0.70, an effect whose whole interval sits below the ~3-point minimal important difference (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold), while exercise + CBT reaches −2.80 at moderate certainty (exercise-plus-cbt-improves-sleep-in-insomnia) and the field's largest synthesis finds benefit concentrates in poor sleepers (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers). A single unblinded VAS result does not enter that comparison, and it moves no Status.

And it lands in a cluster the vault already calls redundant. Melatonin, magnesium, L-theanine and chamomile all compete for the same bedtime slot with small or unattributable effects. A hot bath's advantage over all of them is not efficacy — it is that there is nothing to buy. That is worth recording precisely because it is the shape of thing GOAL.md favours; it is not worth recording as a demonstrated benefit.

No temperature, duration or timing is prescribed (shared/safety.md). Note also the tension inside the same source: the trial that credits heat with better sleep sits beside water-immersion-does-not-improve-perceived-recovery-either-temperature, where no perceptual outcome responded to either temperature in any study — so the one subjective outcome that moved is the one measured in a single trial. Status held emerging on sauna-heat.

Rebutted by: water-immersion-sleep-benefit-is-absent-on-every-subjective-measure — a meta-analysis of ten randomised trials in healthy adults (2026-08-05) tested this same subjective construct and found nothing: PSQI total p = 0.1136 and all seven components null, including subjective sleep quality at p = 0.3721. That is a higher tier (pooled RCTs rather than one cell in a narrative review), a better population (healthy adults rather than 14–40 young male athletes), and a mixed rather than unreliable source. The subjective half of this claim is now the weaker of two accounts. What the newer source does not rebut is sleep quantity, which this trial never measured and which did move there (water-immersion-raises-total-sleep-time-and-sleep-efficiency) — so the tension resolves into a scope condition: the benefit that survives is in minutes counted, not in quality reported.
