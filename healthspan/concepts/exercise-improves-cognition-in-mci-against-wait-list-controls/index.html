Exercise raises cognitive test scores in mild cognitive impairment — measured against people told to wait

Across 74 randomised trials and 5,578 people with mild cognitive impairment, exercise interventions improved cognitive test scores in several domains. Mind–body exercise raised MoCA by 3.94 points (95% CI 2.28–5.59) and MMSE by 4.52 (2.34–6.70) — both screens are scored out of 30, so those are large moves. Resistance training lowered ADAS-Cog by 5.02 points (−7.30 to −2.74), on a battery where lower is better. Digital and virtual-reality exercise cut Trail Making Test B — connecting alternating numbers and letters against the clock — by 48.4 seconds (−77.2 to −19.6).

Mild cognitive impairment means measurable memory or thinking problems worse than normal ageing but not severe enough to be dementia, with independence preserved.

The qualification that governs every number above: the comparator is a wait-list. All reported estimates are intervention versus wait-list or usual care, routed through that single hub node. The paper presents no head-to-head comparison between active exercise types. In a trial where nobody can be blinded to whether they were exercising, a wait-list arm controls for the passage of time and for nothing else — not attention, not expectation, and not the practice effect of sitting the same cognitive test twice. MoCA, MMSE, Digit Span and Trail Making B are all learnable, and no adjustment for repeat administration is reported.

So "mind–body exercise ranks first" means it sat furthest from doing nothing, not that it beat anything. The SUCRA rankings — mind–body 96.3% on MoCA, resistance training 92.7% on ADAS-Cog, digital exercise 96.7% on Trail Making B — are recorded here and not adopted, consistent with this vault's standing refusal of ranking leaderboards (exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported). A telling detail: a different modality takes first place on each of the eight outcomes.

Graded suggestive, and the certainty distribution is why. The source is appraised mixed, capping strength. The authors grade 359 estimates and rate 240 of them low or very low certainty (mci-exercise-network-certainty-is-mostly-low-or-very-low), and the two estimates rated high sit on the sparsest networks in the paper (grade-high-certainty-can-sit-on-a-network-of-eight-studies). Within-node heterogeneity reaches I² = 84.5% (mind–body on MoCA) and 93.2% (multicomponent on MMSE). Every outcome is a test score — there is no incident-dementia endpoint anywhere in the paper.

Read against the same night's opposite result. walking-training-null-on-cognition-in-older-adults pooled eight randomised walking trials in healthy older adults, largely against active comparators, and found nothing in any domain. Two syntheses, opposite populations, opposite comparators, opposite answers. Both are consistent with the benefit-on-demand structure this vault has now seen in three separate literatures (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers): the effect concentrates in people who already have the problem.
