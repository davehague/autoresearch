Of five ways a context can be consistent, two predicted doing more exercise

Zhu 2026 asked which kind of contextual stability predicts physical activity, disaggregating what the habit literature usually pools. Five cue dimensions were separable: temporal (same time of day), location (same place), social (same people), routine (same slot in a sequence of other actions), and mood (same affective state). A sixth — object cues — has never been studied.

Against physical-activity behaviour, only temporal and routine consistency were significant. Against habit strength — the felt sense of automaticity — all five were.

⚑ That gap is the paper's most useful line, and it is a warning about an outcome rather than a finding about cues. Three of five cue types were associated with feeling like exercise was a habit without being associated with doing more of it. Any intervention optimised against habit-strength scales would chase all five; only two of them track the behaviour anyone cares about.

⚠ The magnitudes are very small, and the authors' defence of them is not one this vault accepts. r = 0.11 on behaviour is about 1.2% of variance. The abstract's justification — "Although effect sizes were small, they are consistent with population-level benchmarks in the PA literature" — is an appeal to the field's own weakness. social-connection was coined partly on the observation that a discipline's habitual effect size is not a warrant for it. An r of 0.11 sits inside the range confounding routinely produces.

⚠ The largest number in the paper is the least informative. Habit strength correlated with PA behaviour at a medium-to-large effect — but habit-strength instruments in this literature ask people how automatic, frequent and characteristic their exercise is, while physical-activity self-report asks how much they exercise. A large correlation between those two is substantially a questionnaire correlating with a subset of itself (habit-strength-and-physical-activity-are-measured-by-overlapping-self-reports).

⚠ And the causal arrow runs both ways. People who already exercise a lot have more consistent contexts because they exercise a lot. Nothing here randomised anyone's cues, and the paper nonetheless closes by "supporting a shift toward cue-specific PA promotion strategies" (cue-consistency-behaviour-association-is-inside-confounding-range).

What exercise-adherence opens holding, stated as one sentence with its uncertainty attached: of the five ways a context can be consistent, the two that tracked doing more exercise were doing it at the same TIME and in the same ROUTINE slot — not the same place, not with the same people, not in the same mood — as a small correlational association that no trial has tested.

The map's top open gap is named at coining: a randomised trial that manipulates cue consistency and measures device-based physical activity.
