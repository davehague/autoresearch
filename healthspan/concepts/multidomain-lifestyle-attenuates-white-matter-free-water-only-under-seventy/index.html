One brain marker of six responded to a two-year lifestyle programme, and only in the younger half of the cohort

In the prespecified imaging analysis of US POINTER (n = 959, aged 60–79), a structured multidomain lifestyle programme attenuated the two-year rise in white-matter free water relative to a self-guided programme — but only in participants under 70 (β = −0.031, SE 0.012, P = .009). In those 70 and over there was no effect (β = 0.019, SE 0.014, P = .18). The three-way time × arm × age-group interaction was β = 0.050 (SE 0.018, P = .006).

What free water is, and why it is the plausible place for an effect to appear. In the authors' terms, it indexes increased extracellular fluid in white matter arising from "blood-brain barrier dysfunction, impaired interstitial fluid clearance, and disruption of perivascular transport pathways" — "thought to capture early microstructural alterations that may precede overt structural damage." It is the earliest marker in the set, which is the argument for it being the most modifiable one. The same paper's predictor analysis supports that reading: baseline free water forecasts both microbleeds and lesion growth (white-matter-free-water-predicts-microbleeds-and-lesion-progression).

⚠ Read this beside the five nulls, always. White-matter hyperintensity volume — the validated, standard measure of small-vessel disease — was flat at P = .90, as were fractional anisotropy, PSMD, the ALPS index and incident microbleeds (multidomain-lifestyle-null-on-five-of-six-cerebrovascular-mri-markers). There is no multiplicity correction across six markers and two prespecified strata. One hit at P = .006 in a set of at least twelve tests is a different object from one hit out of one test.

Why it is nevertheless graded and not refused — two checks the vault demands and this paper passes. (1) The age stratification was prespecified, unlike Look AHEAD's BMI interaction (descended from a five-subgroup screen) or SUPERBRAIN-MEET's seventeen post-hoc characteristics (superbrain-meet-subgroup-nulls-are-underpowered-not-generalisability). (2) The effect held when age was modelled continuously rather than dichotomised — which is precisely the check that exposes a spurious subgroup, and the authors ran it. single-study is where it lands: one marker, one stratum, one trial.

⚑ It is also the third and smallest data point on this map's comparator rule, and it fits the pattern. multidomain-lifestyle records that "the less the control arm got, the bigger the effect looked" — LatAm-FINGERS against active health advice gave 0.11 SD/year; SUPERBRAIN-MEET against usual care gave 0.28 SD over 24 weeks. POINTER's self-guided arm is the most active comparator of the three — same educational content, six facilitated meetings, annual cardiovascular risk assessment — and it produced the smallest signal: one marker, half the cohort, no clinical anchor. Three points, monotone.

What it does not do: move the map. shared/evidence-model.md bars consolidating while a live rebuttal stands, and Look AHEAD — 10 years, n = 3,655, incident cognitive impairment, primary cardiovascular endpoint null and stopped for futility — is live. This is a third surrogate win, and a weaker one than the two cognitive-composite results that preceded it. The map's rule holds: two (now three) surrogate wins do not retire a hard-endpoint null.

Population and boundary. Aged 60–79, physically inactive, suboptimal diet, ≥2 risk factors. The eligibility criteria describe someone recruited for elevated risk, not a healthy forty-something. And the outcome is an unanchored diffusion metric at two years — no cognitive, functional or clinical endpoint is measured in this paper.
