In one trial with mixed blinding, the effect estimates ranked by visibility rather than by content

MAPT blinded participants and study staff to the capsule (omega-3 vs placebo) and could not blind them to the multidomain programme — 43 visible group sessions. Its three active arms then produced:

ArmWhat the participant could seeDifference vs placebo
Multidomain + omega-3the programme, plus a capsule that might be active0.093 SD
Multidomain + placebothe programme0.079 SD
Omega-3 alonenothing — capsule blinded0.011 SD

Adding a blinded capsule to a visible programme moved the estimate by 0.014 SD. Adding the visible programme to nothing moved it by 0.079. Both are non-significant; what is informative is the ordering.

Why this belongs in the vault as its own note. A single trial cannot demonstrate an expectancy effect, and this note does not claim one — a small genuine multidomain benefit would produce the same ranking. What it claims is narrower and checkable: within one randomised trial, the only contrast protected by blinding was the only contrast at zero. That is a reason to discount small positive estimates from open-label lifestyle trials generally, and it is a rare case where the discount can be calibrated inside a single study rather than argued across a literature.

Where it bites immediately. multidomain-lifestyle's positive evidence is LatAm-FINGERS — single-blind, participants necessarily aware of a two-year structured supervised programme, with a cognitive battery re-administered four times, reporting +0.11 SD/year (latam-fingers-cognitive-benefit-is-differential-improvement-not-prevented-decline). MAPT's open-label multidomain arm produced +0.079 SD over three years — roughly a quarter of LatAm-FINGERS' annualised rate, and non-significant. An open-label multidomain arm producing a small positive drift is now the expected result in this literature rather than a discovery.

shared/study-appraisal.md has the lens and this is the cleanest instance of it. The RCT blinding lens reads "open-label where blinding was feasible (performance bias)." MAPT is the case where blinding was feasible for one component and impossible for the other, and the trial reports both — which turns a qualitative concern into an internal comparison.

Rebutted by: a-three-year-lifestyle-programme-in-midlife-shows-up-in-medicare-claims-twenty-five-years-later — scoped, not overturned: the unblinding gradient explains MAPT's soft cognitive-composite endpoint, and a 25-year Medicare-claims multimorbidity endpoint (HR 0.79, 0.68–0.93) is not something a participant's expectancy can move. The two results are compatible: expectancy inflates what people report, and claims data record what the health system billed for.
