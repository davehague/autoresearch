Magnesium is not a metabolic lever — but the null is borderline, and the vault says so in both directions

Fifteen RCTs, 1,085 adults with diabetes or pre-diabetes — the population in which a glucose-metabolism benefit should be easiest to demonstrate. Oral magnesium supplementation produced no statistically significant change in either primary outcome: insulin MD −1.73 (−3.73 to 0.27, p = 0.09) and HOMA-IR MD −0.74 (−1.58 to 0.09, p = 0.08). The prediabetes subgroup was also null. Subgroups by dose (>250 vs ≤250 mg/day) and by duration (over vs under 12 weeks) did not rescue it — which is the honest way to test whether a null is really a dosing artefact, and it isn't.

This closes an axis on magnesium-glycinate rather than opening one. That map sits at emerging on a small, RCT-corroborated subjective sleep signal (d = 0.2). Magnesium is one of the most reflexively recommended supplements on the internet, and the pitch is rarely about sleep — it is about insulin sensitivity, metabolic health, blood sugar. The best available randomised evidence, in the population most likely to show it, does not support that pitch.

And now the discipline that has run four times in this single night's ingest, applied where it costs the vault nothing — which is why it matters that the vault applies it anyway.

HOMA-IR −0.74, with an upper bound of +0.09. The point estimate favours magnesium. The interval lies almost entirely on the benefit side and misses significance by nine hundredths. This is a borderline null, not a demonstrated absence. It is the same shape as:

  • Shen's lumbar BMD near-miss — SMD 0.28 (−0.02 to 0.58), which the vault refused to read as equivalence (rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd);
  • Liu's walking PSQI interval — MD −1.41 (−3.18 to 0.36), which the vault refused to read as a negative finding (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-unresolved) — and which Kong 2026 then confirmed, the very next day, in the direction the vault protected (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-large-effect).

The rule holds here too: not shown, and not excluded. No power analysis for a clinically meaningful HOMA-IR change is reported, so "magnesium does nothing metabolically" is not what this paper established. What it established is that nobody has demonstrated that it does, across 15 trials, at any dose, over any duration.

The population is not David's, and that alone is disqualifying for the regimen: these are diabetic and pre-diabetic adults, and the paper contains no evidence at all about healthy metabolism. Recorded as a neutral fact (shared/evidence-model.md).

Status of magnesium-glycinate: emerging, unchanged. It stays on the Watch list on its sleep signal alone, where it already was. What the map gains is a firmer boundary: magnesium is a cheap, low-burden, possible sleep aid, and it is not a metabolic intervention. Its logged drug-interaction gap is untouched by this paper and remains open.

Rebutted by: magnesium-lowers-homa-ir-where-a-smaller-pool-could-not — a 78-RCT pool (Mohammadi 2026, 2026-08-13) reports HOMA-IR −0.48 (−0.79 to −0.18) at GRADE moderate certainty, clearing the zero this note's −0.74 (−1.58 to +0.09), p = 0.08 could not. ⚑ The two do not disagree about direction or magnitude — this note's point estimate is the larger of the two and sits inside the rebutting interval; what changed is precision, and this note's own reading ("not shown, and not excluded") is what the larger base resolved. ⚠ Scope condition, so the rebuttal is not read as a reversal: the rebutting pool mixes healthy adults with T2DM, hypertension and metabolic-syndrome patients, while this note's 15 RCTs enrolled diabetic and pre-diabetic adults only — the enriched population is still where the null was found — and the rebutting paper detected no dose-response for HOMA-IR at all (magnesium-dose-response-analysis-found-no-dose-response-for-most-outcomes). The metabolic axis is re-opened as unresolved, not decided.
