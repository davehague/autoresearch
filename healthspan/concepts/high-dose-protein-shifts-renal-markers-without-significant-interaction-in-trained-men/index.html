A second high-protein renal-marker shift, this time in men, still not statistically attributable

A 2×2 factorial RCT in 48 resistance-trained men crossed resistance training (RT) or concurrent training (RT+cycling) with moderate (1.6 g/kg/day) or high (3.2 g/kg/day) protein intake for 16 weeks. No Time×Training×Protein or Time×Protein interaction reached significance for any renal marker or eGFR estimate (all p≥0.182) — the trial could not statistically attribute a renal effect to the high-protein condition specifically. But within the high-protein groups alone, BUN and cystatin C rose and eGFR estimates fell (all p≤0.017), while the BUN/creatinine ratio — which would flag a dehydration/pre-renal pattern rather than a true filtration change — stayed flat. The authors call these within-group shifts "descriptive," explicitly declining to characterize them as organ injury, and name their own sample size as too small to rule a clinically meaningful difference in or out.

This replicates the direction of what protein-target already holds from higher-protein-intake-shifts-kidney-and-liver-markers-without-established-harm-in-older-women (Bagheri's earlier 108-woman factorial trial, same author group, same basic shift) — now in men, at a cleaner 3.2 g/kg/day dose point, with a renal panel built specifically to answer this question (three eGFR equations, not a secondary afterthought). Two independent samples from different populations showing the same directional pattern, each individually underpowered to call it harm, is worth tracking as a strengthening-but-still-open signal — not yet a safety flag, since neither source characterizes the shift as injury and shared/safety.md requires a credible signal beyond an author-caveated null interaction. Capped suggestive: small per-cell sample explicitly flagged by the authors as underpowered, single population (young trained men), conflicts unverified (full text inaccessible). No dose is recommended here — the standing ~1.6 g/kg/day target this map already supports sits at the dose that showed no shift in either trial; only the doubled 3.2 g/kg/day dose showed the within-group pattern.
