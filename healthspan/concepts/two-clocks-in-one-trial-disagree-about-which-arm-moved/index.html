Same blood, same two timepoints, two clocks — and they disagree about which arm of the trial changed

Carroll 2026 ran DunedinPACE, GrimAge and PCPhenoAge on the same peripheral-blood samples from the same 92 people at the same two visits. Two of them produced significant movement. They located it in opposite arms.

Between-group test (the primary analysis)Which arm moved within itself
DunedinPACEsignificant, −0.02, FDR p=0.03the control arm: SET +0.03 (p=0.02); CBT-I flat (−0.01, p=0.42)
GrimAgenot significant, −0.33 y, FDR p=0.11the treatment arm: CBT-I −0.84 years (−1.35 to −0.34, p=0.01); SET flat (−0.16, p=0.52)
PCPhenoAgenot significant, −0.49 y, p=0.26—

Two entirely different narratives are available from one dataset. Read GrimAge and the story is "CBT-I made people ten months biologically younger; the controls held steady." Read DunedinPACE and the story is "CBT-I held the line; the controls aged faster." ⚑ Only one clears the primary test, and it is not the one whose treatment arm moved.

The authors' explanation is reasonable and unfalsifiable from these data. DunedinPACE was built on longitudinal within-person change in system-level markers and should be more sensitive over short windows; GrimAge is trained on time to death and PCPhenoAge on long-horizon morbidity, so both should be sluggish over two years. That account fits. It would also have fitted, with the sign reversed, had GrimAge been the clock that cleared — an explanation that accommodates any outcome is not evidence for the outcome it accommodates.

Why this belongs on biological-age-clocks and not on the sleep map. The finding is about the instruments, not about insomnia. It joins the map's accumulating case that "the clock" is not a single object:

  • most-consensus-aging-biomarkers-did-not-predict-mortality — nine of fourteen consensus aging biomarkers predicted no deaths at all in BASE-II.
  • epigenetic-clock-responsiveness-longevity-interventions-translage-sehgal-2026 — first-generation clocks (Horvath1/Horvath2/Hannum) agree with the majority of other clocks only 0.5 to 0.66 of the time when they fire, and different intervention classes are best read by different clocks; senolytics moved biomarkers in opposite directions within the same study.
  • This note — in a single randomised trial with three clocks on one set of samples, the two that moved disagree about the arm.

What a reader should take from it. A published epigenetic-ageing result is a joint claim about an intervention and about the instrument that detected it, and the second half is rarely stated. A trial that had pre-registered GrimAge as its primary clock would have reported this study as null. One that had pre-registered DunedinPACE reports it as positive. The clock is a researcher degree of freedom, and the only defence is pre-specification, which this secondary analysis did not have.
