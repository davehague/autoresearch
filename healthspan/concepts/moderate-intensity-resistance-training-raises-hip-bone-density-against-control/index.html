The first pooled evidence in this vault that anything moves bone density — and it is training versus not training

Across 34 randomised controlled trials in 1,746 middle-aged and older adults, moderate-intensity resistance training performed three times a week beat control for bone mineral density at the femoral neck, SMD 0.26 (95% CI 0.06 to 0.46) and the trochanter, SMD 0.34 (0.10 to 0.58). On total-body bone density, both high-intensity twice weekly (SMD 0.49, 0.07 to 0.91) and moderate three times weekly (SMD 0.30, 0.01 to 0.59) beat control. A standardised mean difference of 0.26 to 0.34 is roughly a quarter to a third of a standard deviation — small, with lower bounds close enough to zero (0.06, 0.10, 0.01) that the effect's existence is better established than its size.

This corrects a distinction the vault had been eliding. Three prior nulls sit behind the standing position that nothing moves bone: rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd (heavy versus light training — no difference), creatine-bone-bmd-null (creatine plus training versus training alone — no difference), and potassium-citrate-bone-nulls-honestly-underpowered (an alkaline salt versus placebo — below the required information size). Every one of them compares two things people do; none compares doing something to doing nothing. This source runs that comparison and finds a small positive at the hip.

So the revised claim is narrower and better founded than the one it replaces: whether you resistance-train has a detectable effect on bone density; how heavy you train does not, and no supplement in this vault has earned a bone claim at any dose. That is the sentence creatine-monohydrate already reasoned its way to — whatever bone benefit exists comes from training at all — now with pooled randomised evidence under its first half.

Bounded hard. Appraised through a Springer paywall, so network geometry, per-node trial counts, heterogeneity, inconsistency, publication bias and certainty ratings are all unreadable; bone mineral density is a surrogate and no fracture outcome appears anywhere; and the significant contrasts are all versus control, so this does not identify a best programme. The paper's own authors state that at the lumbar spine, total hip and Ward's triangle no intervention differed significantly from any other (bone-density-network-ranks-sites-where-no-intervention-differed). Capped suggestive; no Status moves.

No dose is extracted. "Moderate intensity, three times per week" is the network's category label, assembled from 34 trials that each defined moderate intensity in their own terms; no %1RM band, set count or session length is retrievable. Per shared/safety.md, a category is not a prescription.
