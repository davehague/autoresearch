Loneliness tracks with worse health even in people who aren't already sick — at about two-thirds the effect the field had been reporting

A pre-registered (PROSPERO CRD42019119135) multilevel meta-analysis of 167 articles, 334 effect sizes, 158 independent samples, 303,643 participants across 36 countries, restricted to non-clinical (healthy) samples, finds loneliness associated with poorer health: r = −.22 overall, with general health largest at r = −.23, physical health (functional disability) r = −.20, sleep function r = −.19, and sensory acuity r = −.12.

The contribution is the shrinkage. Prior meta-analyses reported r = −.35 for general health and r = .29 for sleep — but pooled in samples defined by chronic conditions (HIV, Alzheimer's, diabetes, severe mental illness), populations where illness plausibly causes the loneliness rather than the reverse. Removing them cuts the association by roughly a third. This is the rare synthesis whose headline achievement is making a famous effect smaller and better-specified, and it attacks the most obvious reverse-causal pathway in its own field by design rather than by discussion paragraph.

The number to actually carry is smaller still. The same source finds the association roughly halves when health is measured objectively rather than self-reported (r = −.12 vs −.23) — loneliness-health-association-halves-on-objective-measures. The vault treats r = −.12 as the more defensible estimate. Against the framing this claim usually travels with in public health ("as bad as fifteen cigarettes a day"), a correlation explaining a little over one percent of variance is a very different object.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, capped). Source reliability is mixed. The data are cross-sectional — excluding clinically ill samples narrows reverse causation but cannot close it, since sub-clinical illness and early functional decline plausibly cause loneliness in nominally healthy people. Heterogeneity is extreme (over 92% of variance is genuine between-study difference; only 7.2% sampling error). The effect sits inside confounding range — socioeconomic status, employment, and social circumstance could generate an association this size unaided. And there is no hard endpoint anywhere: no mortality, no incident disease. Contrast purpose-in-life, the vault's other psychosocial map, measured against 48,928 actual deaths — same domain, different evidentiary league, and the two should not be collapsed into a single "psychosocial stuff matters" belief.
