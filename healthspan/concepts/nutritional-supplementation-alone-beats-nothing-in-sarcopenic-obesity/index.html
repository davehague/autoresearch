No nutritional strategy beat usual care on anything in sarcopenic obesity — including when added to training

Across a 24-RCT, 1,298-participant network meta-analysis ranking nine strategies on five outcomes, not one nutritional strategy — supplementation or high-protein intake — reached statistical significance against usual care on BMI, handgrip strength, fat mass, percentage body fat, or skeletal muscle index. The single place a nutrition arm ranked favourably was multicomponent training combined with nutritional supplementation for skeletal muscle index (SUCRA 74.1%), and that comparison was not significant (MD 0.41) and rested on the sparsest evidence in the network (SMI reported by just 5 of 24 trials).

Why this pushes back on protein-target without overturning it. The protein map's consolidating status rests on a well-replicated claim about daily total protein and resistance-training adaptations in adults who are training. This source does not touch that claim. What it does is test the adjacent and much more commonly marketed proposition — that nutritional supplementation is itself a lever in a clinical muscle-loss population — and return nothing, including in the arms where supplementation was added to training rather than substituted for it. The protein map's own through-line already holds that total intake, not timing or distribution, is what matters; this adds a second boundary: in sarcopenic obesity, over 3–9 months, the nutritional arm did not distinguish itself from the exercise it accompanied.

An absence of demonstrated benefit, not a demonstrated absence. The paper names its own alternative explanation: age-related anabolic resistance means older adults need larger protein doses to trigger synthesis, and the intervention periods here are shorter than a muscle-mass effect plausibly requires. With 1,298 participants spread across nine strategies, the nutrition nodes are thinly populated. The vault records this as an under-powered null — real enough to constrain claims, not strong enough to downgrade a map. Per shared/evidence-model.md, a suggestive-capped result does not force a Status move on a consolidating map, and protein-target holds at consolidating.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, capped). Source reliability is mixed. Surrogate outcomes only, 3–9 month follow-up, no hard endpoint, CINeMA certainty low-to-moderate across four of five outcomes, and no blinding possible.
