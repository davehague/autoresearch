The one positive pooled signal in this review is one trial wearing a meta-analysis costume

The review's primary meta-analysed outcome — bone-specific alkaline phosphatase (BALP), a bone-turnover marker — showed a significant pooled increase with vitamin D supplementation (SMD 0.96, 95% CI 0.09–1.83, p=.031), at extreme heterogeneity (I²=97.1%). The authors' own sensitivity analysis excludes one study where both the vitamin D arm and the control arm also received risedronate (a bisphosphonate) — a clinically distinct co-intervention that does not belong in a vitamin-D-alone pool — and the effect attenuates to SMD 0.64 (−0.14 to 1.41), no longer significant.

Filed open-question rather than as a finding: this is the review's one positive number, and it is not robust to a standard, pre-planned sensitivity check the authors themselves ran and reported honestly. Even setting the risedronate issue aside, BALP is a biomarker, not a hard bone outcome — the same surrogate-versus-hard-endpoint distinction vitamin-d already applies to its cardiometabolic and telomere surrogate findings.
