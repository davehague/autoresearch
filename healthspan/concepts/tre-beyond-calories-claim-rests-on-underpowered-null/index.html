An underpowered null, sold as a mechanism — the pathology's newest and most seductive costume

TREAD's weight-loss result is real (tre-modest-weight-loss-vs-standard-counseling). The claim everyone will quote from it is not. The authors write:

"our study did not find a significant difference in caloric intake between TRE and SOC groups at post-intervention, which suggests that calorie restriction in TRE is insufficient to explain the differences observed between the groups in this study."

This is the inference that turns a diet trick into a science — the claim that time-restricted eating has a circadian mechanism over and above simply eating less. Look at what it is built on.

The numbers. TRE cut daily intake by 335 kcal; standard care cut it by 180 kcal. The gap is ~155 kcal/day. It is "non-significant" at p = 0.35 — in a comparison of 26 vs 29 people, in a trial powered for weight, not for calories, and measured by 24-hour diet recall.

Now do the arithmetic the paper does not do. 155 kcal/day × 84 days ≈ 13,000 kcal. At the standard ~7,700 kcal per kg of adipose tissue, that is ≈ 1.7 kg — against an observed between-group weight difference of 2.91 kg. The point estimate of the very null being used to exonerate calories would, on its own, account for roughly 60% of the entire effect. And because p = 0.35 means the interval is wide, values that account for all of it — and more — sit comfortably inside the data. (This is the vault's own arithmetic on the paper's published numbers, and it is deliberately crude: it ignores water weight, adaptive thermogenesis, and recall error in both directions. It does not need to be precise. It only needs to show that the "null" is nowhere near small enough to carry the claim.)

The instrument makes it worse, not better. Intake was measured by 24-hour diet recall — the exact instrument shared/study-appraisal.md files under "Recall / information bias — RED: self-report exposure with known large validation error." Under-reporting of food intake is large, it is worse in people with obesity, and it is plausibly differential between an arm doing something novel and effortful and an arm receiving routine counseling. A calorie comparison this imprecise cannot exonerate calories of anything.

Three failures, stacked. The comparison is badly measured (recall), underpowered (n≈55, p=0.35 in a trial powered for a different endpoint), and its point estimate points straight at the thing it claims to rule out. Any one of those would be enough.

This is the vault's underpowered-null pathology in a new costume, and it is the most seductive one yet. Shen sold an underpowered null as safety (rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered). Wu sold four of them as equivalence (rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered). Kong wrapped a biased corpus in a Bayes factor of 800,000 (bayesian-certainty-cannot-repair-biased-inputs). TREAD sells one as a MECHANISM: "we looked for the boring explanation, didn't find it, therefore the exciting one is true." The structure is argument from ignorance, and it is invalid for exactly the reason the others were — failing to detect something with an instrument too blunt to detect it is not evidence of its absence.

And the conflict points the same way. The senior author is time-restricted eating's foremost public advocate (cofounder of a circadian-biology company; author royalties from The Circadian Code), and "TRE beats calorie-matched eating" is the central thesis of those books. Per shared/evidence-model.md this is flagged, never fatal, and it does not touch the randomized weight result — but the disputed claim is precisely the one a conflicted author would most want to make, and it is precisely the one the data do not support.

What would actually settle it: an isocaloric trial — feed both arms the same measured calories, vary only the window. Until someone runs it, "TRE works beyond calories" is unproven, not disproven, and the vault says the first thing rather than the second.
