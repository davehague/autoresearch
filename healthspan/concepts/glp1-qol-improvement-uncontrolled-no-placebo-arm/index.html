A head-to-head trial has no placebo arm — so "quality of life improved" is a before-and-after, not a drug effect

SURMOUNT-5 randomized participants to tirzepatide or semaglutide. It did not randomize anyone to placebo or no treatment. Therefore every result of the form "improved from baseline with both treatments" — including the headline PCS improvement at p < 0.001 — is an uncontrolled pre-post change, and the trial's randomization contributes nothing to interpreting it.

What sits inside an uncontrolled pre-post change in a weight-loss trial. Regression to the mean (people enrol when things are bad). Trial-participation effects (72 weeks of structured contact with clinical staff). Expectation (participants knew they were receiving an effective drug and could see the result on the scale). Seasonal and secular drift. None of these are subtracted, because there is nothing to subtract them with.

The randomization licenses exactly one comparison, and it is small. Tirzepatide vs semaglutide on General Health: 5.45 vs 4.20, p = 0.003 — a difference of 1.25 points on an SF-36v2 norm-based scale whose population standard deviation is 10. That is roughly an eighth of a standard deviation, statistically significant largely because ~750 people were measured. Per shared/study-appraisal.md's significance-versus-effect-size lens, this is a small difference detected precisely, not a meaningful one.

Why the vault flags this rather than treating it as a technicality. The paper is titled "Improved health-related quality of life with tirzepatide versus semaglutide" and its abstract concludes "HRQoL improved with tirzepatide and semaglutide." Both statements will travel as evidence that GLP-1s improve quality of life. The design cannot support that. It can support tirzepatide beat semaglutide by 1.25 points on one domain, and it can support the null — that MCS did not move (glp1-physical-qol-improves-mental-qol-flat), which is a within-arm observation whose flatness is informative precisely because the uncontrolled design should have inflated it.

This is the same class of catch as the vault's bundled-arm series, arriving from a different direction. There, the missing thing was an isolating comparator — no CBT-alone node, no creatine-alone contrast, no normoxic-exercise control. Here the missing thing is a null comparator. In both cases a real, well-run trial answers a narrower question than its title claims, and the vault's job is to record the narrower one.

Consequence: the vault carries no drug-attributable quality-of-life benefit from this source. Nong 2026's 43-trial null (glp1-no-meaningful-quality-of-life-benefit) remains the vault's estimate, at strong, from a reliable source with actual control arms.
