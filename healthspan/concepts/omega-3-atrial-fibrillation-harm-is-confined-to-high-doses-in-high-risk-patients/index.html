The fish-oil arrhythmia risk is real, and it lives in one of four boxes — high doses in people who already have heart disease

Atrial fibrillation is an irregular, often rapid heart rhythm; it matters because it raises stroke risk and because it is the single most-cited harm attached to omega-3 supplements. Three earlier meta-analyses — pooling no more than eight trials each — reported that omega-3 raised its risk, one of them by 25%. This analysis pooled 35 trials and 114,592 participants and asked the question with a pre-specified two-way split: by dose (above or below 1,500 mg/day of EPA+DHA) and by baseline cardiovascular risk.

The harm is confirmed and confined. In people at high cardiovascular risk taking more than 1,500 mg/day, the odds of new atrial fibrillation were 43% higher (OR 1.43, 95% CI 1.14 to 1.79). The interval excludes 1.0, so no-effect is ruled out; the data are compatible with anywhere from a 14% to a 79% relative increase. ⚑ In absolute terms this is 0.8 percentage points — about eight extra cases per 1,000 people treated over trials averaging several years. Small in absolute terms, unambiguous in direction.

The other three boxes returned nothing: odds ratios of 1.07, 1.06 and 1.03. ⚠ They are not equally informative — see the-omega-3-reassurance-strata-differ-sixteen-fold-in-size-and-are-reported-identically — but the two that carry real weight are the ones that matter most for a consumer: 53,676 high-risk participants at ordinary supplement doses (OR 1.07) and 32,049 low-risk participants at ordinary supplement doses (OR 1.06).

Why the 1,500 mg/day line and not some other line. It sits below the lowest approved pharmaceutical omega-3 dose (icosapent ethyl, 1,800 mg/day). It therefore separates what a person gets from food and a supermarket capsule from what a cardiologist prescribes as a drug — the distinction a consumer actually faces, and one no prior analysis had drawn.

The mechanism predicted the pattern rather than being recruited to explain it. Omega-3 raises vagal tone and lowers resting heart rate from doses around 500 mg/day. Vagally-mediated, bradycardia-dependent atrial fibrillation is a recognised phenotype, and the people prone to it are those with structural heart disease, those on beta blockers, those with untreated sleep apnoea or sick sinus syndrome — and, named in the paper, people doing high volumes of strenuous endurance exercise. That is precisely the "high-risk substrate plus high dose" cell where the harm appeared.

Capped at suggestive despite the meta-analysis-of-RCTs tier, because the source is appraised mixed — see the-largest-omega-3-safety-analysis-was-run-by-the-omega-3-industrys-own-institute. ⚑ But note the direction of the conflict. This finding runs against its authors' commercial interest, and it came out larger than the prior estimate it set out to re-examine. A harm that survives an author group's incentive to erase it is the sturdiest part of the paper.
