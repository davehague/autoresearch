Breaking up sitting gives a small, same-day lift to executive function and memory

A PROSPERO-registered three-level meta-analysis of 21 randomized crossover trials (n=433) found that interrupting prolonged sitting with brief standing or physical-activity breaks improved executive function (g = 0.18, 95% CI 0.03–0.32, p=0.016) and memory (g = 0.37, 0.13–0.61, p=0.003) relative to uninterrupted sitting. Both intervals exclude zero. Both effects are small. The authors rate their own certainty as LOW for both, under GRADE.

Small, and acute. Every included trial is a single-session laboratory paradigm with cognition measured immediately afterward. The honest translation is "getting up helps a bit, right now" — not that sitting breaks are a cognitive intervention, and not that any of this accumulates. Whether habitual break-taking produces durable cognitive benefit is untested by this literature, and is logged as an open gap rather than assumed.

The benefit is domain-specific, which is the interesting part. If this were generic arousal — stand up, feel more awake, do better at everything — attention should have moved most. It is the one domain with a tight interval centred on zero (sitting-breaks-null-on-attention). Something narrower than wakefulness is happening.

It is the third independent layer on the same behaviour. sedentary-behavior holds an observational mortality association across 2.8 million people (sedentary-time-independent-of-exercise-mortality-risk) and a randomised postprandial-insulin benefit that beat the trial's own exercise arm (sitting-breaks-add-insulin-benefit-beyond-exercise). Now a same-day cognitive readout. Three designs, three outcome types, one behaviour — and the behaviour costs nothing, needs no equipment, and is entirely knee-safe.

Capped suggestive — small base, GRADE-low certainty, five outcome families with two significant and no stated cross-family correction, exploratory moderators that contradict each other. Status held emerging; an acute cognitive meta-analysis does not touch why that map sits where it does.
