Insomnia's mortality risk sits in the objectively-short-sleep subgroup, not in insomnia itself

In the Wisconsin Sleep Cohort, insomnia symptoms accompanied by objectively short sleep — total sleep time under 6 hours on overnight polysomnography, the reference-standard laboratory recording of sleep — carried all-cause mortality HR 2.57 (95% CI 1.12–5.89) against people with neither insomnia symptoms nor short objective sleep, after adjustment for confounders. Insomnia symptoms with normal objective sleep duration carried no increased mortality risk.

Insomnia symptoms were defined as difficulty falling asleep, difficulty staying asleep, or early-morning awakening more than 5 times per month. Median follow-up was 8 years.

Why this matters for the vault. It splits a category. "Insomnia" is defined by complaint, and the wider literature relating insomnia to mortality has been inconsistent — which this result explains, if it holds: the complaint alone does not carry the risk, and neither does the short sleep alone (objective-short-sleep-without-insomnia-carried-no-mortality-excess). Only the conjunction does. A conjunction effect is what a genuine phenotype claim predicts and is not what two independent additive risk factors would look like.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive). The interval runs 1.12 to 5.89 — from a 12% excess to nearly sixfold. This is a direction, not a magnitude, and the figure should never be quoted as "2.6×" without it. One of four cells reached significance with no multiplicity correction reported; the four-cell structure follows from a pre-existing hypothesis rather than a fishing expedition, which is why the p-hacking flag was weighed and not fired, but nothing retrievable confirms pre-registration. Most importantly, reverse causation is unexcluded: objectively short, fragmented sleep is a downstream marker of undiagnosed cardiovascular disease, pain, depression and early neurodegeneration, and the abstract reports no lag analysis and no exclusion of early deaths. The full text is paywalled, so the covariate list — including whether sleep apnoea, the condition this cohort was founded to study, was adjusted for — is unread.

The practical shape of the claim. It is a reason to treat persistent difficulty falling or staying asleep as more than a comfort problem, and it is not a reason to chase a duration number. The evidence-consistent response to persistent insomnia symptoms in this vault is cbt-i-first-line-sleep-guideline-consensus and a clinician conversation about what else might be causing curtailed sleep — never a supplement, and never a self-set sleep target.
