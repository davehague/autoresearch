Three cells in the UPF–cancer umbrella review cannot be true as printed

Bao 2026's Table 1 is the whole point of an umbrella review: one place where every pooled estimate from the underlying meta-analyses is transcribed so they can be compared. Three of its cells cannot be read as printed. All three were checked against two independent renderings of the published article — the PubMed Central XML deposit (PMC13490970) and Wiley's own HTML — which agree character for character.

  1. Pancreatic cancer — HR 1.11, 95% CI "1.843–1.45." The lower bound is larger than the upper bound, and the point estimate lies outside both. Printed beside it: p (overall effect) = 0.001. Printed in the paper's own prose about the same outcome: "No statistically significant associations were found for pancreatic cancer."
  2. Gastro-oesophageal-junction gastric cancer — HR 0.91, 95% CI "1.62–1.34." Same defect, same shape. Beside it: p (overall effect) = 0.002. In the prose: "no significant association was found for cardia gastric cancer."
  3. Colorectal cancer, developing-countries subgroup — p (heterogeneity) = 2. A probability cannot exceed 1.

A fourth anomaly is structural rather than arithmetic: the breast-cancer subgroup table lists "pre-menopausal breast cancer" twice, with different study counts and different estimates (6 studies, RR 1.12 (0.97–1.29); 5 studies, RR 1.03 (0.96–1.11)). One of those rows is almost certainly post-menopausal and mislabelled.

The most likely benign explanation, stated plainly. "1.843–1.45" and "1.62–1.34" look like leading-digit corruptions of "0.843–1.45" and "0.62–1.34" — both of which would be perfectly coherent intervals around their point estimates, and both of which would make the outcomes non-significant exactly as the prose says. If so, the underlying estimates are fine and the typesetting broke. That reading does not rescue the p-values, which still say 0.001 and 0.002 next to outcomes the same paper calls non-significant, and it does not rescue the duplicated subgroup label.

Why this is recorded as a finding rather than a typo. An umbrella review's only contribution is faithful transcription — it runs no analysis of its own. When the transcription layer visibly fails in three places, every other cell in the table inherits the doubt, including the ones this vault has quoted. That is why ultra-processed-food-cancer-risk-umbrella-review-bao-2026 is graded unreliable and every concept drawn from it is capped at single-study.
