A second reassuring hemodynamic reading — whose one significant result skips its own top dose

Across four occlusion pressures × three timepoints × three cardiovascular measures in a randomized crossover (n = 60), exactly one comparison reached significance: heart rate at 60% LOP, +3.82 bpm (0.72–6.91), about 5.6% above baseline. Blood-pressure changes were characterised as modest. The authors conclude the cardiovascular changes were "within safe ranges for healthy young adults."

The magnitude is reassuring; the pattern is noise. 80% LOP — the highest pressure tested — was not significant. A dose-response that fires at its third dose and not its fourth is not a physiological gradient; under shared/study-appraisal.md's multiple-comparisons lens, one hit in ~36 uncorrected comparisons with a non-monotonic shape is a chance finding. The honest reading of this table is "nothing much happened at any pressure," not "60% is the risky one."

Two populations now agree on the reassuring part. Ren 2025 found heart rate unchanged and modest systolic-BP responses in older adults 55–80 (bfr-cardiovascular-response-data-older-adults); Zhu finds the same small magnitudes in young sedentary adults. The acute cardiovascular load of low-intensity BFR looks tolerable at both ends of the age range the vault cares about.

And the flag still stands, for the fifth time. Across Xu, Hou, Chen, Ren and Zhu — five sources, 50+ randomised trials plus this crossover — there is still no systematic tally of serious adverse events, cuff complications, or thrombotic events (bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials). What accumulates is surrogate reassurance: heart rate fine, blood pressure fine, discomfort now measured. None of those is the occlusion-specific risk the high-dose-risk flag was set on. The flag is refined a second time — hemodynamically tolerable in two populations, occlusion risks uncounted by everyone — not lifted.

The 40–60% pressure recommendation is recorded, not adopted. shared/safety.md forbids converting a published range into a prescribed dose, and this particular range rests partly on the non-monotonic comparison above. Cuff pressure is a physio's call. The same discipline was applied to Ren 2025's HFHP regimen ranking.

Caveats that cap it: a 5-minute acute exposure — the least informative slice of the safety question — and sedentary college students, neither middle-aged nor injured. The objection the vault raised against Xu 2026 holds unchanged: the person most tempted by BFR is the person least represented in its evidence.
