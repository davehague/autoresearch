Against a control that supplies attention, the pain benefit vanishes — on 123 people, which settles nothing

Cochrane's hip osteoarthritis review contains one comparison designed to isolate exercise from the expectation and contact that come with it: exercise versus an attention control or placebo. In that comparison pain did not significantly improve — MD −6.31 on a 0–100 scale (95% CI −12.98 to 0.35) — while physical function did, narrowly (MD −7.44, −13.86 to −1.01). Both are rated low certainty, on 2 studies and 123 participants.

The tempting reading, and why the vault does not take it. Set against the usual-care comparison, where pain did improve at moderate certainty (exercise-hip-osteoarthritis-benefit-real-but-below-clinical-threshold), the obvious story is that the pain benefit is expectation rather than exercise — the unblindable-self-report discount this vault has now measured three separate times. Loneliness's health association halved when the outcome was measured objectively (loneliness-health-association-halves-on-objective-measures). Cold-water immersion was objectively null while its subjective wins failed the authors' own bias tests (cold-water-immersion-damage-marker-benefit-fails-bias-adjustment). Green exercise's entire advantage rests on questionnaires with no objective outcome pooled anywhere (green-exercise-evidence-rests-entirely-on-unblindable-self-report).

But 123 people is not a test. The interval runs from −12.98 — a benefit larger than the usual-care point estimate — to +0.35. It is entirely consistent with the same modest benefit the better-powered comparison found, and entirely consistent with nothing. shared/study-appraisal.md's third fatal red flag exists for exactly this: an underpowered null is not a demonstration of equivalence. The vault has refused eight of these, including twice where accepting the null would have closed a gap it wanted closed (stretching-age-generalisation-rests-on-three-middle-aged-trials) and once where the null was convenient (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit). Refusing it here costs the vault a rhetorically satisfying conclusion, which is the point of having the rule.

Credit where the review earns it. Cochrane rated this comparison low certainty and worded its conclusion as exercise "may have little to no effect on pain" — hedged, not asserted. That is the discipline this vault has had to impose on paper after paper from outside, and it is what makes the source reliable overall despite containing an underpowered cell. Contrast rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered, where an interval reaching 11.11 was written up as "similar safety profiles."

So this is logged as open-question, and the resolution is specified. What would settle it: a hip osteoarthritis trial adequately powered against a credible attention control, with a pre-specified pain endpoint and, ideally, one outcome measurable without asking the participant. The vault's standing observation is that on outcomes measurable without asking, expectation effects shrink; on outcomes that can only be asked about, they are large and unblindable. Pain can only be asked about, which is what makes this the hardest cell in the review and the one most worth powering properly.
