Perfect agreement across sixty heterogeneous trials, four times over — a number that needs explaining

I² estimates the proportion of variation across studies attributable to real between-study differences rather than sampling error. I² = 0% says the trials agree so closely that every difference between them is chance. This meta-analysis reports I² = 0.0% on all four of its primary pooled outcomes — resting heart rate, systolic, diastolic and mean blood pressure — from an inclusion set of 60 controlled trials of resistance training lasting anywhere from four weeks upward, in postmenopausal women of varying age, medication status and training history, across resistance protocols the abstract never characterises.

That is a striking claim about a famously messy literature, and the paper does not account for it. Three explanations are available:

(1) Per-outcome k is far smaller than 60. Most likely. If the meta-analysis for each haemodynamic outcome rests on a handful of trials while the remaining studies feed only the qualitative synthesis, then I² is being estimated at small k — where it is notoriously underpowered and returns 0 by default, not because heterogeneity is absent but because the test cannot see it. The abstract reports one total (60) and no per-outcome counts, so this cannot be checked.

(2) The standardised unit compresses the variance. Dividing each trial's effect by its own pooled SD can suppress heterogeneity that would be plain in mmHg. See standardised-effect-sizes-conceal-whether-a-blood-pressure-change-is-clinically-meaningful.

(3) The effect really is that uniform. Possible, and it would be a genuinely interesting finding — but it is the explanation requiring the most from the data, and the paper does not argue for it.

⚑ The operating rule this establishes. I² = 0 is not a quality signal, and a low I² beside a large inclusion count is a prompt to go looking for the per-outcome k rather than a reason for confidence. When k is small, 0% and unknown are the same number wearing different clothes. Where the pooled k is unreported, the interval widths — not the heterogeneity statistic — are the honest measure of what is known.

⚠ This vault has read I² = 0 the other way before, and correctly. In rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered, I² = 0% was the evidence for an interpretation: the trials agreed almost perfectly, so nothing but sample size was widening those intervals, which convicted the paper of laundering underpowered nulls into equivalence. The two readings are consistent. In both cases I² = 0 means "between-study variance is not what is driving the uncertainty here" — which points at sample size. It is only when a paper offers I² = 0 as a reason to trust a pooled estimate that it is being misused.
