Metformin did not blunt the training response — the muscle axis of the vault's AMPK flag gets its first randomised test, and comes back flat

The mechanistic worry that has sat on metformin since 2026-07-07 is specific: metformin activates AMP-activated protein kinase, the cell's low-energy sensor, and endurance and resistance training recruit the same signalling machinery to build mitochondria and muscle. Pharmacological activation, the argument runs, might crowd out the training signal. The vault flagged this on three axes — cardiorespiratory adaptation, muscle hypertrophy, and the protein anabolic response — and deliberately left the magnitude blank rather than guess it.

DEMFOS tested all three at once and found no attenuation on any of them. For six months, older adults with obesity did three supervised 90-minute sessions a week containing 30 minutes of aerobic work (progressing to 70–85% of peak heart rate) and 30 minutes of progressive resistance training (nine movements, working up to 2–3 sets at about 85% of one-rep max), while taking metformin extended-release or a matching placebo under masked conditions.

Aerobic capacity. VO₂peak — the peak rate at which the body can consume oxygen during exercise, the standard laboratory measure of aerobic fitness — rose +3.6 mL/kg/min on metformin and +3.5 on placebo. Difference: 0.1 (−1.5 to 1.6). In absolute litres per minute, +0.14 versus +0.13, difference 0.01 (−0.13 to 0.16).

Muscle and strength, measured three independent ways. Isokinetic knee extensor peak torque +1.2 N·m (−5.4 to 7.8); peak power −0.6 W (−11.0 to 9.8); MRI thigh muscle volume −12 cm³ (−69 to 45) on a baseline near 1,980 cm³; DXA lean mass −0.3 kg (−1.1 to 0.6). Function followed: 6-minute walk distance +1.0 m (−30.0 to 32.1), gait speed −0.02 m/s (−0.11 to 0.06).

The comparison arm proves the measurements were not simply insensitive. Against the third arm — monthly diet education plus metformin, no training — the same training programme produced +3.4 mL/kg/min of VO₂peak (2.0 to 4.9), +12.2 N·m of knee torque (5.8 to 18.5), +13.5 W of power (3.9 to 23.1) and +44.5 m of walking distance (14.2 to 74.7). Every instrument that failed to separate drug from placebo separated training from no training, decisively, in the same participants and the same six months.

This contradicts the vault's own newest entry on the aerobic axis, and the vault records the contradiction rather than averaging it. Methnani 2026 reported that metformin attenuated the VO₂peak gain from aerobic training — placebo +0.26 L/min against metformin +0.106, group × time interaction p = 0.042 (metformin-attenuates-the-vo2peak-gain-from-aerobic-training). DEMFOS reports +0.13 versus +0.14 L/min on that same comparison. DEMFOS is the better instrument on every axis that separates them: 114 people against 22; six months against five weeks (Methnani's own authors conceded five weeks is shorter than the 8–12 weeks aerobic fitness needs to stabilise); intention-to-treat against per-protocol; a prospectively powered between-group comparison against a trial whose Methods section called its own interaction analysis underpowered and preliminary. The vault graded Methnani mixed and capped it suggestive precisely for these reasons.

What survives from Methnani, and must not be quietly folded into this rebuttal. That trial's largest and most consistent finding was not fitness — it was perceived effort: up to two full Borg units harder at the same relative workload on metformin, with placebo unchanged, on a fully blinded self-report (metformin-raises-perceived-effort-at-the-same-exercise-intensity). DEMFOS did not measure perceived exertion at all, so that finding is untouched tonight. The only adjacent evidence DEMFOS offers is indirect: exercise-session attendance was a median 85% in both the metformin and the placebo training arms across six months, so whatever effort penalty exists did not manifest as skipped supervised sessions in this population.

Why suggestive and not stronger. It is one trial. The secondary-outcome intervals, while all null, are wide enough to permit changes of roughly ±3.5% in thigh muscle volume and about a kilogram of lean mass — so this rules out a large blunting effect, not a small one. And the population is narrow: 89% men, one Veterans Affairs centre, aged 65–85, non-diabetic, sedentary at entry, in an energy deficit throughout. Nothing here describes a trained middle-aged adult taking metformin off-label.
