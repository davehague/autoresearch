Resistance training lowers blood pressure in older adults — a clinically meaningful surrogate benefit

A meta-analysis of 51 RCTs (n=2,025) in adults ≥60 finds resistance training reduces systolic BP by −6.11 mmHg, diastolic by −2.53 mmHg, and mean arterial pressure by −4.10 mmHg (all p < 0.001), with larger reductions in women (SBP −7.95, DBP −4.24) and in shorter (≤12-week) and higher-frequency programs. A ~6 mmHg systolic reduction is in the range that maps onto cardiovascular-risk reduction.

What this adds to resistance-training. A blood-pressure thread on the hub — a mechanistically coherent partner to the map's cardiovascular-mortality cohort signal (Zhang 2026: ~19% lower CV mortality with RT). Lifting plausibly lowers cardiovascular risk in part by lowering blood pressure, in the same older population.

Bounded — a surrogate, verified only at the abstract level. Office blood pressure is not events or mortality, so this adds a thread, not a tier: it does not consolidate the map's hard-endpoint mortality claim (a higher-tier study of a different outcome does not promote a map, shared/evidence-model.md). And the confidence intervals, heterogeneity (I²), and risk-of-bias picture could not be verified (full text paywalled), and the effect was larger in shorter trials — a mild durability caveat. Capped suggestive; Status held emerging. For David: a bonus benefit of the lifting he already does, not a reason to change the program and not a dose to prescribe (shared/safety.md).
