Purpose in life and mortality: an entirely observational literature — no randomized evidence exists

All 25 samples underlying this meta-analysis — the 8 harmonized IPD cohorts and the 17 samples drawn from the systematic review — are observational. Purpose in life has never been randomized against mortality, or against any hard clinical endpoint, in this literature or anywhere else we could locate. This is a ceiling on the entire evidence base, not a weakness specific to this one meta-analysis.

Why this matters for the vault. Every other mortality-linked map in the vault at least has a plausible path to experimental evidence somewhere in its wider literature — a diet can be randomized (PREDIMED, for mediterranean-diet), a step count or a training program can be randomized (daily-steps, resistance-training), a supplement dose can be randomized. Purpose in life is different in kind: you cannot randomly assign a person to "have more meaning in life" the way you assign a pill or a walking target. Some adjacent interventions exist in the wider literature (values-clarification work, logotherapy-style programs) but none that we could confirm has been trialed against mortality as a hard endpoint. That makes the observational ceiling here structural, not just current — it may not lift the way daily-steps or mediterranean-diet could someday lift if a hard-endpoint RCT appeared.

Bounded honestly (why single-study, more conservative than the source's suggestive cap allows). The source's mixed reliability caps concept strength at suggestive (shared/evidence-model.md), but this particular claim — the absence of interventional evidence — is not itself a graded empirical finding the way the headline HR is; it is an evidentiary-ceiling statement drawn from one source's literature base, which this ingest could not independently verify against the full universe of possible trials. single-study is the more honest strength for a claim of this shape, matching the vault's treatment of comparable evidence-gap notes (e.g. taurine-lifespan-evidence-is-animal-only).

What this licenses and what it does not. It licenses treating purpose-in-life as a genuine, well- replicated observational correlate worth tracking — not as a "lever" with a dose or a protocol slot the way a supplement or an exercise target would be. It does not license any claim that deliberately cultivating purpose causes lower mortality; the causal arrow is asserted by the association's plausibility and its survival of partial confounder adjustment, not demonstrated by an experiment.
