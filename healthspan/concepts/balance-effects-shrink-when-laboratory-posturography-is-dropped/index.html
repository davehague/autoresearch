Take away the force plates and three of four balance interventions stop clearing zero

The network pooled two different kinds of outcome under one label. Clinical or field-based balance-performance tests — Berg Balance Scale, Mini-BESTest, Functional Reach, Fullerton Advanced Balance, Star Excursion, the Stork test — measure whether a person can stand, turn, reach and hold position without losing their footing. Instrumented laboratory postural control — limits of stability, centre-of-pressure displacement, the Sensory Organization Test — measures millimetres of sway on a force plate.

Restrict the analysis to the first group and most of the result disappears.

⚑ The instruments producing the significant results are the ones furthest from anything a person experiences. The authors call this "outcome-domain sensitivity" and cite it among the reasons a treatment hierarchy is precluded.

Two readings, and the paper does not choose between them. Force-plate posturography is more sensitive — it detects small changes a clinical scale would miss, so a real but small effect could show up there and nowhere else. Or postural sway under laboratory conditions is a different construct from functional balance, and moving one does not move the other. ⚠ Both are consistent with these numbers, and only the second has consequences for whether anyone falls.

⚑ This map already holds the evidence that settles which construct matters, and it does not involve a force plate. falls-fell-while-timed-up-and-go-did-not-move records a trial where real falls fell and the balance test did not budge — an instrument failing to track the outcome. balance-and-functional-exercise-cuts-actual-falls-and-fall-related-injuries is the cluster-randomised trial across 128 villages that moved falls themselves: any fall 42.7% → 31.6% (odds ratio 0.60), falls rate 1.6 → 0.9 per person-year, fall-related injury 24.9% → 15.9%. Neither depended on posturography, and neither is affected by anything in this paper.

⚠ Compounding fragility: the control node is not inert either. It mixes waitlist, no intervention, usual activity, usual care and education-only or minimal-contact conditions. Removing the single study with an active non-exercise control raised every estimate — conventional exercise 0.33 → 0.45, Pilates 0.64 → 1.01, technology-assisted 0.69 → 0.77. The authors label this "control-node structural sensitivity, not proof of robustness," which is the correct refusal to bank the larger numbers.

The habit this licenses. ⚑ When a synthesis pools laboratory instruments with functional tests, ask what happens if you keep only the functional ones — and treat a result that lives entirely in the laboratory subset as a measurement finding rather than a health finding. Nothing here is a demonstrated null: these restricted analyses run on 389 people at low-to-very-low certainty, and a wide interval crossing zero is not evidence of no effect.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal.
