Low-load BFR may match heavy load for strength in a bad knee — the substitute test the vault wanted, on four trials

For three days blood-flow-restriction has held a gap it was coined to hold: nobody in 21 randomised trials has tested low-load BFR as a substitute for heavy load. Xu added occlusion to training in athletes; Hou added it to rehabilitation in knee-pain patients. Chen 2025 runs the head-to-head at last: four of its trials pit low-load BFR directly against high-intensity (heavy-load) resistance training, and the strength difference is SMD −0.11 (95% CI −0.75 to 0.53), p = 0.74 — no significant difference — in middle-aged and elderly OA/TKA patients, David's own population.

If it held at scale, this would be the key to the knee gate. resistance-training caps heavy lower-limb loading at "proposed — review with your doctor/physio" because heavy load builds the most strength and a compromised knee cannot safely have it. The textbook escape is occlusion training at 20–40% 1RM. Chen is the first evidence that the escape works — that you can get the heavy-load strength result without the heavy load — and it is in the right people.

But it is four trials and 123 participants, and the vault does not launder an underpowered null into equivalence. A "no significant difference" from the smallest cell in the paper is absence of a detected difference, not demonstrated non-inferiority; the CI (−0.75 to 0.53) runs from a meaningful deficit to a meaningful advantage. This is the identical discipline the vault applied to Shen's falls (RR 2.68 → "similar safety"), Wu's four I²=0% adaptation nulls ("as effective"), and Jiao's indistinguishable arms — and it has to apply the same way when the convenient reading is "the gate lifts." A lens that only cuts against inconvenient results is not a lens.

So the knee gate SOFTENS, it does not open. The gate can no longer say the substitute question is untested — it now has direct, in-population evidence pointing toward BFR matching heavy load. What it can still say is that the evidence is four small trials with no adverse-event accounting, which is not enough to act on. The safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] stands, because Chen — like Xu and Hou before it — reports no systematic adverse events: three syntheses, 33 RCTs, zero occlusion-safety tallies.

For David: the first paper that both speaks to his March 2026 patellar contusion and tests the substitute question directly. Not a green light; a sharper physio question — is low-load BFR a sensible way to keep loading this knee, given small in-population evidence that it may match heavy load for strength and given that its safety has never been formally studied?
