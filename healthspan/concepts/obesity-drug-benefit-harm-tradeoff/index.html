Larger weight loss is bought with larger harms — the obesity-drug trade-off, quantified across 19 agents

A BMJ network meta-analysis of 262 randomized trials finds that, across weight-loss drugs, greater benefit is generally accompanied by greater harm and greater discontinuation. Discontinuation because of adverse events was highest with orforglipron, naltrexone-bupropion, liraglutide, phentermine-topiramate, CagriSema, and oral semaglutide (RR 1.9–4.2). Gastrointestinal events were most increased with naltrexone-bupropion, oral semaglutide, orforglipron, and tirzepatide (RR 3.1–4.2). Fatigue rose sharply — naltrexone-bupropion RR 8.9, an absolute increase of 331 per 1,000 people over one year; orforglipron RR 3.4 (+100 per 1,000); CagriSema RR 3.2 (+92 per 1,000).

Why this matters for the vault. It extends glp1-gi-discontinuation-rct-select — SELECT's finding that roughly 1 in 6 stopped semaglutide for adverse events — from one drug to the whole class, and it does so in absolute terms alongside relative ones, which is exactly what the relative-versus-absolute lens in shared/study-appraisal.md asks of a source.

Set against glp1-no-meaningful-quality-of-life-benefit, this note completes an uncomfortable picture that a healthspan ledger should state rather than soften. The harms are measured and real. The quality-of-life benefit is measured and absent. Fatigue in particular is not a laboratory abstraction: an extra 331 fatigued people per 1,000 per year is a direct tax on the energy GOAL.md is trying to protect, and it lands on the same axis — daily lived experience — where the benefit failed to appear.

Bounded honestly (why strong). The source is reliable (independently funded, PROSPERO-registered, GRADE-rated, RoB 2, conflicted authors firewalled), the harms are reported with GRADE moderate-to-high certainty, absolute risks are given, and the base is 262 randomized trials. Honest limits: harms are reported per drug, and the worst offenders here (naltrexone-bupropion, orforglipron) are not the agents David is weighing; subcutaneous semaglutide sits at the milder end of the discontinuation spectrum. Trial follow-up ran 12–172 weeks, so nothing is known about harms over the years a healthspan user would take these.

The practical shape of the claim. safety_flags: [prescription]. Never ranked, no dose set (shared/safety.md). Its use is comparative: it lets the clinician conversation weigh which agent, not just whether — and it prices the more aggressive options honestly, since the one that strips the most weight also strips the most muscle (tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss) and the class as a whole buys no measured improvement in how life feels.
