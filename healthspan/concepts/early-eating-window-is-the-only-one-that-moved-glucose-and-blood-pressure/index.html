Four different glucose-and-pressure endpoints, one window that moved them, and every effect is small

The pattern, not any single number, is the finding. Against a control group, early time-restricted eating — first calories before 10 a.m. — was the only node to clear zero on fasting glucose (−3.64 mg/dL), HOMA-IR (−0.42), HbA1c (−0.37 percentage points) and systolic blood pressure (−2.59 mmHg). Fasting insulin fell too (−1.53 mIU/L). Midday and late windows cleared zero on none of them. Diastolic pressure moved for nobody.

⚑ Four independent endpoints pointing the same way is worth more than any one of the intervals. Each on its own is fragile; together they describe a consistent glycaemic signal attached specifically to eating early. That is what the circadian hypothesis predicted, and this is the first network with the resolution to look.

⚠ Now the size of it, honestly. 3.64 mg/dL of fasting glucose is about a seventh of the width of the prediabetes band. −2.59 mmHg of systolic pressure has an upper credible bound of −0.005 — five thousandths of a millimetre of mercury from nothing. HOMA-IR's upper bound is −0.03. These are effects that exist and are close to the smallest size at which a network can still call something an effect.

⚑ The HbA1c number pushes back on this vault's own standing record, and the resolution is precision rather than contradiction. time-restricted-eating-null-on-hba1c-in-glucose-dysregulation recorded Viple 2026's finding of −0.30 percentage points (−0.63 to +0.02), p = 0.06 — a null. Tonight's estimate is −0.37 (−0.62 to −0.16) — significant. ⚑ Look at the lower bounds: −0.63 and −0.62. The two syntheses agree almost exactly on how large the effect could be, and disagree only about whether the upper end of the interval crosses zero. Viple pooled 10 trials in 599 people with glucose dysregulation; this network drew on 15 trials in a broader cardiometabolic population and isolated the early window specifically. The vault's own tre-hba1c-subgroup-significance-tracks-precision-not-effect-size made exactly this point on Viple's own subgroups two weeks ago — significance tracking precision rather than magnitude — and it now applies across the two syntheses. The honest reading is not "the null was wrong": it is that an HbA1c effect of roughly a third of a percentage point is where both syntheses land, and whether it clears a significance threshold depends on how many trials you have.

⚠ The comparison that matters for a decision is a different one, and it is null. All of the above is against a control group — people doing nothing. ⚑ Against calorie restriction, no TRE pattern differed on any outcome (eating-window-placement-did-not-beat-calorie-restriction-in-a-timing-specific-network). So "early eating lowers your glucose" is compatible with "early eating does not beat simply eating less." Both are in the same paper.

⚠ And the effect is not cleanly separable from weight. Every node that lowered glucose also lowered body weight, and the vault already holds tre-glucose-effect-not-separable-from-concurrent-weight-loss from a different synthesis making that point with numbers. The authors' own summary: improvements "appear to be driven predominantly by weight loss regardless of eating window."

Capped suggestive. stance: pushes-back — on the vault's existing HbA1c null, which is why the back-edge is written there. No eating window, fasting duration or schedule is recommended (shared/safety.md).
