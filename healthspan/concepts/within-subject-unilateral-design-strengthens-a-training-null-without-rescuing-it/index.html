Removing the noise beat adding participants — and then the design hid the only harm the trial found

resistance-training-programming exists in part to carry a pathology: this literature answers its questions with samples that were never large enough, and then writes the failure to detect as a demonstration of equivalence. Seven instances are catalogued on that map. The standing complaint has always been "too few participants," and the implied remedy "enrol more."

Camargo 2026 applied a better remedy, and — uniquely on this map — proved in advance that it worked.

What the design bought

Each participant trained one leg at +120% weekly volume progression and the contralateral leg at +20%, so the comparison runs within a person rather than between two groups of them. Diet, sleep, genetics, hormonal environment, adherence, training age and life stress are not adjusted for — they are identical by construction. Between-person variance in hypertrophy is the dominant noise term in this research and is routinely larger than any programming effect.

The trial quantified that advantage prospectively rather than asserting it. A simulation-based power analysis (10,000 simulations, Superpower in R) assumed a between-limb correlation of r = 0.78 and a deliberately conservative d = 0.50 → Cohen's f = 0.20, and returned a minimum sample of 24 for 80% power. Twenty-five completed. A parallel-group trial of 25 people could not have come near that target, and every prior null on this map is a parallel-group or pooled comparison in which confounders were merely balanced by randomisation rather than eliminated.

So the vault's blanket treatment of "small n" was wrong, and this is where it gets corrected. Design and sample size are separate inputs to precision. Shen's fall-risk interval reaching 11.11, Wu's four adaptation nulls at I² = 0%, Doan's strength and mobility nulls, Chen's four-trial BFR substitution — all between-group. This one is a genuinely better instrument pointed at the same class of question, and the source is appraised reliable as a result.

What the design cost, demonstrated rather than hypothesised

⚠ Of 28 participants who began training, three withdrew "due to persistent muscle or joint pain." That is roughly 11% attrition for musculoskeletal pain from a supervised programme of leg press and leg extension taken to momentary concentric failure on every set, in resistance-trained 18-to-35-year-olds screened to exclude musculoskeletal injury — the most robust population in which this question could be asked.

And it cannot be assigned to either volume. The paper states the mechanism plainly: "As the study used a within-subject unilateral design, these withdrawals occurred at the participant level and therefore both protocol assignments (VOL20 and VOL120) from each withdrawn participant were excluded simultaneously." Every participant did both, so nothing that happens to a person can be attributed to a limb.

This generalises, and it is the reason to hold this concept separately. A unilateral design holds systemic factors constant precisely because both limbs share them — which means fatigue, appetite, sleep disruption, endocrine adaptation, motivation and whole-body joint pain are invisible to it by construction. Whatever a large volume jump does to a person cannot be studied by comparing their legs. The map's most decision-relevant question — what dose of loading starts costing more than it returns — is exactly the question this otherwise-excellent design cannot answer.

The pattern that a better design did not fix

The source's NEW & NOTEWORTHY block phrases the finding correctly — "no evidence that the larger volume progression attenuated adaptation under the present experimental conditions" — and its title does not: "Large increases in resistance training volume do not impair muscle hypertrophy." The careful sentence is in the body; the equivalence claim is in the line everyone will cite.

Across the seven instances this map now holds, the equivalence claim has landed in the title, the abstract conclusion or the press framing every time. A registered, powered, conflict-free, reliable trial did not change that. Which supports the map's growing conclusion that the pathology is a writing-and-incentives problem rather than a statistics problem — the same conclusion reached when a GRADE-rated Cochrane review handled its own underpowered null correctly (exercise-hip-osteoarthritis-pain-null-against-attention-control-underpowered).

One further limit worth recording: "powered" is not a binary. The estimate targeted f = 0.20. The trial can say "no moderate-or-larger difference"; it cannot exclude a small one, and its biopsy and molecular outcomes sit outside the power calculation altogether (abrupt-volume-progression-produced-no-catabolic-signalling-shift).
