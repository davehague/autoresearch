For cancer there is no J-curve and no safe amount — the line goes up from zero

Whatever is argued about alcohol and the heart, the cancer relationship is not in dispute and it has no threshold. Meta-analyses of cohort studies support monotonic increasing dose-response across the seven cancer sites the International Agency for Research on Cancer has formally adjudicated as causally linked to alcohol: lip and oral cavity, other pharynx, oesophagus, colon and rectum, liver, larynx, and female breast. More alcohol, more cancer, at every level examined, with no dip at the bottom of the curve.

And the list is probably too short. The review reports stable dose-response relationships for stomach, biliary tract and pancreatic cancer, plus lung — though the lung association "almost disappeared when estimated in never smokers," which the authors report rather than suppress. Large hospital-based cohorts additionally find stable harmful associations between alcohol dependence and these sites, and a reduction in risk after alcohol treatment interventions — a natural-experiment signal that residual confounding does not easily produce. The authors call for IARC to evaluate the additional sites urgently.

The mechanism is why there is no threshold. Alcohol metabolises to acetaldehyde, which damages DNA directly and in several ways. It also induces oxidative stress and inflammation, and it alters hormone levels including oestrogen — the pathway behind the breast-cancer association specifically. A mutagenic mechanism operating on every molecule metabolised does not have a dose below which nothing happens; it has a dose below which the effect is small. Those are different claims and only the second one is true.

⚑ Why this note exists separately from the cardiovascular argument. Almost every public discussion of "moderate drinking" is, without saying so, a discussion of ischaemic heart disease — the one outcome family where a protective limb is claimed (the-alcohol-heart-benefit-shrinks-as-each-additional-bias-is-controlled). That claim, even if true, does not transfer. A person weighing whether their drinking is fine is usually weighing a cardiovascular argument against a cancer risk they have not been shown. On the cancer axis the direction is unambiguous and the shape is a straight line up.

And the reversibility is the worst of any alcohol outcome (alcohol-harms-reverse-on-very-different-timescales-and-cancer-is-the-one-that-does-not): blood pressure recovers in days, liver risk falls immediately on reduction, but "alcohol-related damage may already be too advanced for reductions in consumption to yield meaningful benefits" in the case of cancer.

⚠ Capped at suggestive because the source is appraised mixed and because the underlying exposure measurement is self-report throughout. The direction and shape are as solid as observational nutrition-and-lifestyle epidemiology gets — IARC adjudication, dose-response, mechanism, and a treatment-intervention signal — but no randomised trial of alcohol and cancer exists or ever will.

No threshold, no unit count and no "safe amount" is stated here. The review supplies risk functions, not cut-points, and shared/safety.md forbids inventing one.
