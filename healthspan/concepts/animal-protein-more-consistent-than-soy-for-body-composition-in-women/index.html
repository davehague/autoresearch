Animal protein looked more consistent than soy — which the vault already believed, from four times the evidence

Fifteen trials, split into animal-based (8: whey, meat, milk) and plant-based (7: mostly soy protein isolate). The animal group showed "more consistent effects on weight and body fat reduction"; the soy group was "more heterogeneous." There is no pooled estimate, no confidence interval, no I², no GRADE. "More consistent" is a description of a vote count.

⚑ The direction agrees with a much stronger source already on this map, and the agreement is worth less than it appears. protein-supplement-type-effects-small-whey-baseline carries Drummond 2026: a 78-RCT network meta-analysis in 4,755 adults, in which whey beat placebo by a small margin (FFM SMD 0.16, 0.05–0.28; strength SMD 0.15, 0.03–0.27) and bovine colostrum, beef, and the plant proteins — pea, soy and rice — did not significantly beat placebo at all.

So this source is a narrative, unpooled, 15-trial echo of a pooled 78-trial finding. The vault has a standing rule against treating a second, softer look at a settled question as an independent win — the same rule that refused to double-bank aerobic-volume-mortality-dose-response-nonlinear and daily-steps-mortality-dose-response as separate mortality levers. The direction is now seen twice; the strength of the claim is still set by the network meta-analysis, and this does not raise it. Graded single-study deliberately below its source's suggestive cap: one unpooled narrative synthesis comparing two heterogeneous bags of proteins is not an instrument that can carry a comparative claim.

⚠ "Animal" and "plant" are not two interventions. Whey, meat and milk protein differ from one another in leucine content, digestion rate and food matrix; so do soy isolate, pea and rice. Two heterogeneous groupings compared by vote count is a weak instrument for a source question, and Drummond's network — which resolved each protein as its own node — is the right shape for it.

⚠ And every comparison here is confounded by the co-interventions. Animal-protein trials showed their effect "especially when combined with calorie restriction and supervised exercise" (protein-source-benefit-unseparable-from-deficit-and-exercise). If the animal-protein trials were more likely to bundle a deficit and supervised training than the soy trials were, then "animal protein is more consistent" is a statement about trial design. The review reports no way to tell, and with no pooling and no meta-regression there is no mechanism in it that could.

⚠ Funding unknown, and it matters here specifically. The full text is paywalled (HTTP 402), so the funding and conflict statements could not be read. Protein-source comparison is the most commercially contested question in this map's subject area — dairy, meat and soy interests sit on opposite sides of this exact result — and protein-target's own anchor already carries a dairy-industry funding flag. The vault alleges nothing and records that it could not check.

What it genuinely adds: the population. 1,161 pre- and postmenopausal women, against a map whose muscle evidence is dominated by young men. Nothing changes about the ~1.6 g/kg/day target, which is a daily-total claim this source does not test.
