Nineteen ways to train balance; eighteen worked, and the one that did not was stretching

The largest randomised evidence base in this vault — 423 trials, 33,901 participants, 2,546 effect sizes, 19 modalities, in older adults recruited without any disease criterion — found that eighteen of nineteen exercise modalities improved overall balance against control, and stretching "demonstrated no clear benefit."

The top four: perturbation training 0.66 (95% credible interval 0.49–0.82), sensory training 0.55 (0.42–0.68), dance 0.54 (0.45–0.64), agility stepping 0.53 (0.39–0.67). By domain: Tai Chi for global balance, perturbation training for static, Pilates for dynamic, agility stepping or aquatic exercise for proactive. Perturbation training performed best supervised and in higher-fall-risk groups; sensory training performed best delivered at home. Dose-response was nonlinear.

⚑ What the stretching null does and does not mean, because it is easy to overread in both directions. It does not contradict stretching's supported claim — chronic-static-stretching-improves-lower-limb-flexibility records Hedges' g 0.851 across 79 RCTs for range of motion, and range of motion is a different construct from balance. It does not make stretching useless for a rehabilitating joint, where restoring range is the goal being met. What it establishes is narrower and worth having: stretching is a range-of-motion intervention and it is not a stability intervention, and the two should stop being bundled.

⚠ Eighteen of nineteen modalities beating control is a warning as much as a result. Exercise trials cannot blind participants, balance instruments are effort-dependent and mostly clinician-scored, and control arms in this literature are usually no-treatment or education. A network in which almost every arm wins is partly measuring the contrast between doing something and doing nothing, which makes stretching's failure to clear even that bar the more notable half of the finding.

⚠ Surrogate endpoint. Balance performance is not falls and not fractures. The review measures the surrogate throughout and never claims otherwise.

⚑ The vault's scattered fall-and-stability concepts reproduce this structure from far weaker bases. unstable-rt-reduces-fear-of-falling-not-falls moved fear without moving falls. exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator found the benefit tracking how little the control group did. bfr-balance-gain-is-visually-dependent found a balance gain that vanished with the eyes shut. Small studies, weak comparators, and the same warning at 1/400th the scale.
