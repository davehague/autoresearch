The absence stops being reported as an absence and starts being reported as safety

The harms method, quoted in full: "Participants were monitored throughout the intervention for any discomfort, numbness, or excessive pain." That is clinical watchfulness, not a protocol — no event definition, no collection instrument, no denominator, no reporting timeline. The result: "No adverse events occurred." The conclusion: the hybrid "may offer a safe and feasible alternative for individuals who cannot tolerate high-load resistance training."

⚠ Two weeks ago, the same evidentiary situation produced the opposite sentence. Jelen 2026's authors wrote that no major adverse events were reported and immediately added that this was "encouraging but not sufficient to draw firm conclusions regarding safety" (bfr-adverse-event-absence-replicates-without-becoming-safety). Same absence, opposite conversion — and this time at 200–350 mmHg, the highest cuff pressures in the corpus (bfr-cuff-pressure-reaches-350-mmhg-under-a-seventh-prescription-convention).

The cumulative count is the argument. Seven sources — Xu, Hou, Chen, Ren 2025, Zhu, Jelen, Ren W 2026 — spanning roughly 57 randomised trials plus two crossovers, across athletes, knee-pain patients, arthroplasty patients, healthy older adults and sedentary young adults. Not one systematic adverse-event tally with a denominator, for a technique whose mechanism is deliberately restricting arterial inflow to a limb.

⚑ What is new is the direction of travel: the field is escalating dose while still not counting harm, and has begun describing the uncounted absence as a safety property. Per shared/safety.md, a missed harm outweighs an over-cautious flag. safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] stands, refined a fourth time.

The contrast available tonight makes the point without needing a bigger trial. A sixteen-person dialysis pilot ingested the same night followed CONSORT Harms 2022 — pre-specified event definition, systematic routine measurement, nephrologist adjudication of severity and relatedness (creatine-tolerated-in-dialysis-patients-on-a-sample-too-small-to-establish-safety). Both studies are far too small to establish safety. Only one of them counted.

Rebutted by: bfr-at-80-percent-occlusion-lowered-mental-quality-of-life-against-sham — the first BFR trial in this corpus to run a validated patient-reported instrument against a randomised sham found the 80%-occlusion arm's mental health-related quality of life falling below the population mean while sham held flat (group × time P = .014), which is the harm an uninstrumented "no discomfort reported" could never have seen; and bfr-mental-health-signal-is-one-of-four-uncorrected-tests bounds how far that rebuttal reaches (one of four uncorrected tests, failing a Bonferroni threshold) without restoring the word "safe."
