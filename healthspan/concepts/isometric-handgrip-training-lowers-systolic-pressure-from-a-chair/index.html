Squeezing a spring in a chair moved systolic pressure about as much as continuous aerobic training did

In a network of 30 randomised trials in adults with hypertension, isometric handgrip training lowered systolic blood pressure by 5.40 mmHg (95% CI −9.97 to −0.83) against standard care — statistically significant, on 6 trials and 315 participants of direct evidence. In the same network, moderate-intensity continuous aerobic training produced −5.90 mmHg (−9.74 to −2.05). The two intervals overlap almost entirely.

What the trials actually did, from the source's characteristics table — reported as description, not as a protocol this vault endorses: four sets of roughly two minutes of sustained grip at about 30% of maximum voluntary contraction, one minute of rest between sets, done at home or at a desk, for 8–12 weeks. Some trials alternated hands; one used 45-second contractions at 50% of maximum. No equipment beyond a gripper, no cardiovascular load, no joint loading, and under ten minutes a session.

⚑ The ratio of effect to burden is the finding. For a vault whose north-star is least amount of work and least discomfort for the maximum benefit (GOAL.md), an intervention that gets within half a millimetre of continuous aerobic training's effect for a fraction of the time, equipment and physical demand is the shape of thing that usually gets buried — because it does not top the leaderboard. It ranked mid-table on SUCRA. The vault records it precisely because rankings hide it.

⚑ It is also the only lever in this network that a knee injury cannot touch. Every other significant modality here — combined training, continuous aerobic work, high-intensity continuous training — puts load or impact through the lower limb. Handgrip does not. GOAL.md makes knee-safety a hard constraint, and this is the one entry on the list that satisfies it unconditionally.

⚠ What it does not establish. The interval runs to −0.83, so the data are compatible with an effect under 1 mmHg — a real result whose lower bound is close to nothing. Only 4 of the 30 trials in the network were at low risk of bias. Blood pressure is unblindable, and handgrip trials are especially exposed to it: participants know they are training, sessions are usually unsupervised at home, and adherence is self-reported. Interventions ran 8–12 weeks with no follow-up, so nothing here says the effect persists, and aerobic-fitness-declined-measurably-over-eight-weeks-of-usual-living is the reason that question is not idle. Capped suggestive.

Rebutted by: isometric-exercise-ranks-last-in-a-midlife-and-older-network — a network five times larger, in mostly-normotensive midlife-and-older adults, puts handgrip LAST of six modalities at −4.18 mmHg (−8.31 to −0.06) with the highest dropout in the network (21.70%); the magnitude agrees, the ranking and the burden argument do not.

⚠ Population. All trials enrolled adults with diagnosed hypertension. The effect in normotensive people is expected to be smaller and is not measured here. This is not a recommendation and no protocol is prescribed (shared/safety.md).
