Beetroot juice raises VO2max — by about a fifth of a standard deviation, in a literature missing its small nulls

Thirty-eight placebo-controlled trials in 703 healthy adults find that beetroot juice increases maximal oxygen uptake by SMD 0.24 (95% CI 0.11–0.37, I² = 48.3%). Egger's test detects significant small-study effects (p = 0.014), and a trim-and-fill procedure imputing five missing studies pulls the estimate to SMD 0.19 (0.07–0.31).

Both numbers belong in the claim, and the second is the one to carry. The interval still excludes zero after correction, so this is not a finding that evaporates — but an effect of 0.19 in a corpus where the missing studies are demonstrably the small null ones is an effect whose lower bound is doing most of the work. The source is appraised mixed and strength is capped at suggestive accordingly.

What the number is measured on matters more than its size. VO₂max here is read off a graded exercise test, usually on a supplement day, usually in a crossover. It is a performance measurement, not a training adaptation and not a health outcome. No trial in this corpus measured anything after a washout, anything sustained, or anything a person would notice off the ergometer. zone-2-cardio holds the vault's reason to care about aerobic fitness at all — cardiorespiratory fitness predicts all-cause mortality with no observed ceiling (cardiorespiratory-fitness-all-cause-mortality) — and that claim comes from a 122,007-person cohort measuring fitness people had, not fitness a drink produced for an afternoon.

⚑ Why this is graded open-question rather than confirms or extends. Nothing in this vault previously claimed a supplement moves VO₂max, so there is no incumbent belief to confirm or extend. The open question this note names is precisely the gap between the two things being conflated: an acute increase in a fitness measurement and an increase in fitness. Everything on zone-2-cardio that raised VO₂max did so by changing the person — sprint intervals over weeks (training-duration-outperforms-session-volume-for-vo2max), HIIT versus continuous training in 60–80-year-olds (hiit-beats-mict-vo2max-elderly). This changes the reading. Whether it changes the person is untested.

The methodological credit sits in a separate note, and it is real — nitrate-depleted-placebo-isolates-the-beetroot-effect records that this corpus, uniquely among the vault's candidate supplements, has a placebo that isolates the active compound. That credit belongs to the design, not to the effect size, and this note is the effect size.
