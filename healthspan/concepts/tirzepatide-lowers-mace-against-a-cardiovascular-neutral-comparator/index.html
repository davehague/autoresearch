Tirzepatide cut major cardiovascular events by about a third against a heart-neutral comparator drug

Over one year, adults with type 2 diabetes and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease who started tirzepatide had a 2.9% (2.5–3.4) risk of a major adverse cardiovascular event — heart attack, stroke, or death from any cause — against 4.4% (3.8–4.9) for those starting sitagliptin. That is an absolute difference of 1.4 percentage points (0.7 to 2.1), a hazard ratio of 0.68 (0.58–0.80), and a number needed to treat of 70 for one year.

The components split: myocardial infarction HR 0.67 (0.52–0.87), NNT 130 — a real reduction. Ischaemic stroke HR 0.91 (0.64–1.28) — an interval running from a 36% reduction to a 28% increase, which is uninformative rather than null.

Why the comparator is the finding. Sitagliptin is a diabetes drug whose randomised trials showed it neither raises nor lowers cardiovascular risk. Using it as the comparison arm means the estimate answers what does tirzepatide add on top of ordinary care — the question the randomised trial SURPASS-CVOT could not answer, because it compared tirzepatide against dulaglutide, a drug that already reduces cardiovascular events, and found non-inferiority rather than superiority. No placebo-controlled cardiovascular outcome trial of tirzepatide exists or is likely to, so this is the closest available answer.

Why this matters for the vault. It is a third independent line supporting the cardiovascular case for this drug class, on a different molecule from the one that carries glp1-mace-benefit-rct-select (semaglutide, randomised, HR 0.80) and by a different design from glp1-lowers-mace-mortality-obesity (target-trial emulation). Different drug, different method, same direction.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive). This is a claims-database cohort study, not a trial: no HbA1c, blood pressure, lipids, weight or smoking status is recorded, so overlap weighting can only balance what billing captures. Follow-up is as-treated — censored at treatment discontinuation or switching — which can quietly remove sick person-time. It is one year long, and the weighted comparison has the precision of about 7,444 per group rather than 53,000. The same paper's much larger secondary results are the reason for the cap (tirzepatide-mortality-and-infection-signals-exceed-the-atherosclerotic-effect): if part of the mortality signal is residual confounding, part of this composite — which contains all-cause mortality — inherits it.

The practical shape of the claim. It is evidence about adults averaging 70 with diabetes and existing heart disease, whose one-year baseline event risk was 4.4% (tirzepatide-cardiovascular-evidence-comes-from-diabetes-with-established-heart-disease). An NNT of 70 does not transfer to a lower-risk person. Prescription and injectable: never ranked into the daily protocol, no dose set here (shared/safety.md).
