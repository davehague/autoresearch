A balance and functional exercise programme cut real falls and real injuries, not just balance scores

A 12-month, village-doctor-led programme of balance and functional exercise plus health education, delivered across 128 rural Chinese villages randomised as clusters, reduced every fall endpoint it measured in adults 60+ with cardiovascular disease.

Whether you fell at all: 31.6% vs 42.7% — OR 0.60 (95% CI 0.43 to 0.84). An odds ratio of 0.60 means the odds of falling in intervention villages were 60% of the odds under usual care; the interval excludes 1.0, so "no effect" is not among the values compatible with this trial.

How often you fell: 0.9 vs 1.6 falls per person-year — RR 0.62 (0.43 to 0.89).

Whether the fall hurt you: 15.9% vs 24.9% — OR 0.57 (0.42 to 0.78).

⚑ The injury result is the load-bearing one, and it is the strongest of the three. A fall-prevention programme can post a good any-fall number while preventing only the harmless stumbles. Here the injury estimate (0.57) is at least as large as the any-fall estimate (0.60) — the falls being prevented are not disproportionately the trivial ones. That is the specific failure mode this result rules out, and it is why the finding is worth more than its tier alone suggests.

⚑ Why this matters to balance-training out of proportion to its size. The map was coined on the largest randomised balance network in this vault — 423 RCTs, 33,901 participants — which nonetheless reported "tentative" for reactive balance, with no point estimate and no interval for any modality (reactive-balance-is-the-least-evidenced-domain-in-the-largest-balance-network). The map recorded the consequence plainly: its best-evidenced domains were the ones furthest from the endpoint. This source does not close that gap — it steps around it, by declining to measure balance and measuring falls instead.

⚠ What it does not establish: which part of the bundle worked. The intervention is balance exercise plus functional exercise plus health education plus twelve months of contact with a village doctor, against a usual-care comparator that received none of it. Nothing here isolates the balance training from the attention. The vault has caught this comparator problem in four separate literatures — exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator, weighted-vest-plus-exercise-beats-usual-activity-on-bone-and-fat, and the 18-of-19-modalities-beat-control shape in balance-modality-rankings-rest-on-overlapping-credible-intervals. Against usual care the bundle works. What in the bundle works is unanswered.

⚠ And the falls were self-reported in an unblinded trial, which is the direction of bias that flatters the intervention. Capped suggestive on that plus the unverifiable pre-specification of the CVD subgroup.
