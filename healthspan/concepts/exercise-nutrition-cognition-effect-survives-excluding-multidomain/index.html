Strip out the multi-domain programs and the cognitive effect holds while heterogeneity goes to zero

A pre-planned sensitivity analysis excluding multi-domain interventions — those bundling cognitive training or behavioural counselling alongside the exercise and nutrition arms — leaves the effect on global cognition intact and eliminates the heterogeneity: d = 0.12 (95% CI 0.02–0.22), I² = 0%, against SMD 0.15 with I² = 50% in the full pool.

Why this is the methodologically most interesting number in the source. A sensitivity analysis usually functions as a robustness check that a result survives. This one does more: it tightens the estimate. Two things follow. First, the small global effect belongs to the exercise + nutrition pairing itself, not to cognitive-training or counselling components smuggled in under a "multi-domain" label — which is a real attribution gain, because multi-domain trials are exactly where cognitive benefits are most likely to come from practice on cognitive tasks. Second, I² = 0% means the clean two-lever comparison is homogeneous across its constituent trials: this is a small effect measured consistently, not a small effect averaged out of noisy disagreement. Those are different epistemic objects, and the vault values the former considerably more.

The offsetting honesty. The isolated estimate is smaller than the headline (0.12 vs 0.15) and its lower bound is 0.02 — a hair from nothing. So the sensitivity analysis buys precision at the cost of magnitude: what survives is more trustworthy and even less impressive. And it does nothing about this literature's central structural limitation, which no analysis of these trials can fix — the comparator is control, never exercise alone, so the nutritional contribution remains unmeasured (exercise-plus-nutrition-cognition-small-global-benefit).

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, capped). The source's reliability verdict is mixed, capping strength per shared/evidence-model.md. Open-label expectancy on an effort-sensitive cognitive outcome is untouched by any sensitivity analysis.

Rebutted by: diet-and-activity-showed-no-interaction-on-midlife-cognition — in 3,028 midlife adults with diet quality and accelerometer-measured activity both captured at age 46, formal interaction terms on cognition at 51 were null, and the diet association held at every activity level. It does not contradict the pooled additive effect recorded here; it pushes back on the stronger reading that combining these two levers produces more than the sum, and it does so in a healthy midlife population rather than a trial cohort. Bounded: interaction terms are chronically underpowered, so this is an open question rather than a demonstrated absence of synergy.
