Heavy drinking carries a bigger dementia risk than anything else on the Lancet Commission's list

"Almost all systematic reviews and meta-analyses concur that heavy drinking is associated with increased risks of cognitive decline and various forms of dementia." The number attached is from a large French population-based cohort of all hospital patients followed for several years: alcohol dependence carried a relative risk of about 3 for all forms of dementia — and the review states that this ⚑ "exceeds all relative risks of all other risk factors for dementia identified by the Lancet commission."

The Lancet Commission list is the standard inventory of modifiable dementia risk — hearing loss, hypertension, physical inactivity, social isolation, depression, smoking, air pollution and the rest. Several of those have their own maps in this vault. On this evidence, heavy drinking outranks all of them.

Two properties of the study design worth understanding. The exposure is a hospital diagnosis of alcohol dependence, used as a proxy for heavy drinking because hospital records do not carry self-reported intake. That is a strength on the measurement axis — it removes the self-report under-reporting that plagues the rest of this literature — and a limitation on the population axis, since dependence is the severe end of the distribution and the estimate does not describe ordinary heavy drinking. It also found that alcohol dependence accounted for the majority of early-onset dementia (before age 65).

The mechanisms are established and multiple: neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, impaired cholinergic function, and damage to the blood-brain barrier. The cardiovascular harms of heavy drinking additionally feed vascular dementia. And chronic heavy drinking and heavy episodic drinking both cause brain atrophy.

⚠ What this note does NOT extend to, deliberately. For non-heavy drinking the evidence is genuinely inconsistent: observational studies suggest harm in younger adults and possible benefit in older ones — an age-specific pattern rather than a single curve — mechanism studies disagree with one another, and brain damage is detectable below heavy-drinking thresholds. ⚑ The review's own recommendation is the conservative one: model only the heavy-drinking harm, and restrict it to early-onset dementia. This vault adopts that framing rather than the more quotable version. The claim here is about heavy drinking and dependence, not about a glass of wine.

Partly reversible. Studies using computed tomography show partial recovery of brain volume after sustained abstinence, and acute conditions such as Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome can reverse if caught and treated. The rest of the brain's response to chronic alcohol is only partly reversible (alcohol-harms-reverse-on-very-different-timescales-and-cancer-is-the-one-that-does-not).
