Days without food collapse the active thyroid hormone while the storage form does not budge — the body throttling conversion, not the gland failing

The thyroid gland mostly makes T4 (thyroxine), a storage form. Tissues convert it locally into T3, which is the hormone that actually sets metabolic rate. Pooling fifteen studies of complete, zero-calorie fasting lasting 24 hours to 10 days, the two forms came apart:

  • Free T3 fell by about two standard deviations — Hedges' g = −1.979 (95% CI −3.054 to −0.905)
  • Total T3 fell too — g = −1.337 (−1.866 to −0.807), and with the least disagreement between studies of any outcome (I² = 26.8%)
  • TSH, the pituitary's signal to the thyroid, fell moderately — g = −0.581 (−0.882 to −0.280)
  • Free T4 did not move — g = 0.073 (−0.237 to 0.382)

⚑ Why the split is the whole finding. If the gland were failing, T4 would fall first. T4 holding while T3 collapses means the conversion step downstream is being turned down — an energy-conservation response, not thyroid disease. The concurrent TSH drop says the pituitary is participating in the same downshift rather than fighting it. In raw numbers: a 10-day fast in 13 healthy men took FT3 from 5.2 to 2.8 and total T3 from 1.8 to 0.8, while FT4 went 18.2 → 18.0.

⚑ Duration matters categorically and not continuously, which is a real and slightly odd result. Fasts longer than 3 days dropped FT3 significantly; fasts of 3 days or less did not (interval −3.092 to +0.100). Yet meta-regression treating duration as a continuous variable found nothing, for any of the four hormones. The adaptation appears to switch on rather than accumulate — though with seven studies, "appears" is doing real work.

⚠ What it does not establish. Most of the underlying studies are single-arm with no control group — the review used the NIH quality tool built for exactly that design — with 5 to 58 participants each. The FT3 estimate carries I² = 92.8% and a prediction interval that crosses zero (−5.232 to +1.273), meaning the next study could plausibly find no change. Pre-post correlations were assumed because most studies did not report them. No outcome anywhere is clinical: nothing links a fallen T3 to how anyone felt, performed, or fared. Capped suggestive.

⚠ This is not evidence that fasting harms the thyroid, and should never be quoted as such. A reversible adaptation to energy withdrawal is not hypothyroidism; the preserved T4 is the evidence for that reading. The review does not follow anyone into refeeding, so how fast it reverses is unmeasured here.

⚑ Scope, and it is unusually tight — see the-fasting-thyroid-evidence-excludes-time-restricted-eating-by-design. Every popular fasting protocol was deliberately excluded from this literature.
