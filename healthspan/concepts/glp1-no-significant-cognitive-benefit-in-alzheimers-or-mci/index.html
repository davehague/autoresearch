GLP-1 receptor agonists (liraglutide, exenatide) show no significant cognitive benefit in Alzheimer's or MCI — on three small trials

A PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO-registered systematic review pooled three RCTs (n=278) of liraglutide or exenatide vs placebo on global cognitive outcomes in adults with clinically or biomarker-confirmed Alzheimer's disease or MCI due to AD. Pooled SMD: −0.21 (95% CI −0.81 to 0.38, I²=47%) — not statistically significant. The authors frame this as inconclusive given the small evidence base, not as a demonstration that the class has no cognitive effect.

Why this matters for the vault. glp-1 has repeatedly found that the class's measured benefits concentrate in weight loss and (in people with established cardiovascular disease) cardiovascular events — and that hoped-for broader benefits don't show up when actually measured: no meaningful quality-of-life gain across 262 RCTs (glp1-no-meaningful-quality-of-life-benefit), mental quality of life flat even after 72 weeks of the most effective pharmacological weight loss on record (glp1-physical-qol-improves-mental-qol-flat). This extends that "markers move, broader benefit doesn't automatically follow" theme into cognition — though here the honest reading is "unanswered," not "answered no," given how few trials exist.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, filed open-question). Severely underpowered — three trials, 278 participants total — with an interval wide enough to span both a clinically meaningful benefit and a clinically meaningful harm. This is read as genuinely inconclusive, not as evidence of absence: the fatal-flag condition for an underpowered null "sold as no effect" does not fire here, because the authors do not sell it that way. Full text not yet retrieved; which three trials, their individual drug/dose/duration, and funding disclosures are unverified. Context (not separately ingested): other 2026 meta-analyses in this space — a 4-RCT non-diabetic pool and a 14-RCT cross-population pool — point the same small-or-null direction.
