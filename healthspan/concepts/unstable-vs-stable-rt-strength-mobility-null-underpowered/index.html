Wobble boards did not out-lift stable loading — and this is the fifth underpowered null on this map

Across 6 pooled RCTs in older adults, unstable resistance training showed no significant advantage over stable resistance training on lower limb strength or short-term mobility, and on the Five-Times Sit-to-Stand test the trend actually favoured stable training.

This is a null the vault would enjoy banking, which is exactly why it does not. Skip the balance gimmick, just lift the weight is simpler, cheaper and knee-safer — everything GOAL.md prefers. But the corpus is seven trials, six pooled, with no total sample size, no power analysis, and no GRADE certainty reported. Per shared/study-appraisal.md that is an underpowered comparison, and reading it as equivalence is a fatal reading error regardless of which direction it flatters. The supportable claim is "unstable surfaces have not been shown to add strength or mobility over stable loading," not "they add nothing."

The count now matters more than any single instance. resistance-training-programming holds:

  • Shen 2026's falls null (RR 2.68, CI to 11.11, called "similar safety") — rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered
  • Shen 2026's bone null (lumbar SMD 0.28, CI −0.02 to 0.58) — rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd
  • Wu 2026's four adaptation nulls at I² = 0% — rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered
  • Chen 2025's BFR-substitute null on 4 trials/n=123 — bfr-matches-heavy-load-strength-in-knee-rehab
  • and now Doan 2026's strength/mobility null on 6 trials

Five underpowered nulls, five conclusions written as equivalence. The most reliable finding about this literature is that it is chronically too small to answer its own secondary questions, and that its authors consistently resolve that in favour of whatever their recommendation already said. Two of the five flattered the vault's own preferences; it refused those too, which is the only condition under which the lens means anything.

What would resolve it: adequately powered head-to-head trials on the secondary outcomes — strength, mobility, falls, bone — rather than further meta-analyses pooling the same handful of small studies.

For David: the practical read is unchanged and modest — there is no demonstrated strength or mobility reason to add unstable-surface work over stable lifting, so it is not a gap in his programme. That is a statement about missing evidence, not about a settled absence.
