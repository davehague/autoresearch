The unmeasured confounder in the GLP-1 eye study is the reason the drug gets prescribed

The study that associated GLP-1 receptor agonists with about three extra cases of ischemic optic neuropathy per 10,000 people over 18 months (glp1-small-absolute-increase-in-ischemic-optic-neuropathy) ends its own conclusion with: "Observed differences may reflect residual confounding." This concept records why that sentence should be taken at face value rather than read as boilerplate.

The specific gap. The analysis adjusted for more than 80 covariates using inverse probability of treatment weighting — thorough by any standard. It could not adjust for body mass index or type 2 diabetes duration, because a claims database does not contain them.

Why BMI in particular is fatal to a causal reading here. GLP-1 receptor agonists are preferentially prescribed to heavier patients — that is their distinguishing clinical advantage over the comparator drugs. Obesity is itself associated with hypertension, sleep apnoea and small-vessel disease, all of which are implicated in the pathway to an optic-nerve infarction. So the analysis has a confounder that is unmeasured, strongly associated with the exposure by design, and pointing in the same direction as the finding. That is not a generic caveat; it is a concrete alternative explanation.

Diabetes duration compounds it. Longer-standing diabetes means more microvascular disease, and microvascular disease is the mechanism in question. Patients escalated to a GLP-1 may differ systematically in duration from those escalated to an SGLT2 or DPP-4 inhibitor.

And the effect is small enough for that to matter. shared/study-appraisal.md's effect-size-versus-confounding lens is explicit that at low single-digit magnitudes "anything unaccounted-for can distort reality." The risk-difference intervals have lower bounds of 0.4 and 1.1 per 10,000. A modest amount of unmeasured confounding by adiposity would consume the entire signal.

What the design does still buy, so this is not a dismissal. Active comparators mean everyone studied needed a second diabetes drug, which removes most confounding by indication. Two different reference drugs agreed on direction and magnitude. A new-user design guarantees exposure preceded outcome. The result is a credible signal that has not been shown to be causal — which is a real epistemic position, not a euphemism for nothing.

And one further limit sits on the outcome rather than the exposure. "Diagnostic codes specifically for NAION were lacking," so incident ischemic optic neuropathy served as a proxy. NAION is diagnosed by examining the back of the eye, which a billing code cannot observe.

How the vault treats it. As an open-question, graded single-study, with the harm concept itself held at suggestive and gated by safety_flags: [prescription]. Per shared/safety.md, a credible harm signal is flagged at the strength it earns — not inflated into a demonstrated risk, and not discounted into silence because it might be confounding. What would resolve it: adjudicated ophthalmic outcomes in a randomised GLP-1 trial, or an emulation in a data source containing BMI.
