The balance rankings are a ranking of overlapping intervals

A network meta-analysis's most quotable output is an ordering, and an ordering is the part of it least supported by the data. Perturbation training 0.66 (95% credible interval 0.49–0.82). Sensory training 0.55 (0.42–0.68). Dance 0.54 (0.45–0.64). Agility stepping 0.53 (0.39–0.67).

⚑ Perturbation training's lower bound is 0.49. That is below dance's point estimate (0.54) and below agility stepping's (0.53). All four intervals overlap across most of their range. On these numbers the top four modalities are not distinguishable from each other, and the word "largest" is describing the order of four point estimates drawn from one posterior distribution rather than a demonstrated difference.

The same objection applies with more force to the domain-specific claims — Tai Chi for global balance, perturbation for static, Pilates for dynamic, agility stepping or aquatic exercise for proactive. Those are per-domain rankings across nineteen modalities, and no interval is published for any of them. A 19-arm ranking on a subdomain of a surrogate outcome is where ranking noise is largest.

⚠ A credible interval is not a confidence interval, and the distinction cuts the same way. A 95% credible interval is the range containing the true effect with 95% probability given the model and the data. It inherits the model's assumptions — including consistency between direct and indirect evidence, which this review's retrievable text does not report testing.

⚑ Why this note exists rather than being a caveat inside another one. The ranking is the part of this paper that will be quoted, repeated, and turned into recommendations, and it is the part the evidence supports least. shared/study-appraisal.md's "statistical vs clinical significance" lens applies directly: an ordering that is real in the arithmetic and absent in the intervals is exactly the kind of finding this vault exists to catch. The vault has caught its shape before — bone-density-network-ranks-sites-where-no-intervention-differed records a network meta-analysis ranking bone sites where no intervention actually differed, and sarcopenia-exercise-network-muscle-mass-result-is-locally-inconsistent records a network whose local estimates disagreed with each other.

What survives the objection. That balance training works is supported by 423 trials and is not in doubt (stretching-was-the-only-exercise-modality-that-did-not-improve-balance). Which one is best is not established. The practical consequence is permissive rather than restrictive: pick the modality someone will actually do.
