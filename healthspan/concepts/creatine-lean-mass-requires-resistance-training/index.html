Creatine's lean-mass gain requires resistance training; the anaerobic-power gain does not

Across 37 RCTs in healthy men aged 18–30, creatine's effect on body composition depends entirely on whether the person is training. Paired with resistance training, creatine added fat-free mass +3.39 kg (p < 0.001), lean body mass +2.70 kg (p = 0.0001), and squat 1RM +9.58 kg (p < 0.001). Without resistance training, the same outcomes were flat and non-significant — FFM −0.89 kg (p = 0.566), LBM −0.54 kg (p = 0.39). Anaerobic power behaved differently: Wingate peak power rose in both contexts (+61.35 W with RT, +119.72 W, p = 0.010 without RT; pooled +71.27 W), consistent with creatine's phosphocreatine-buffering mechanism being available whether or not you lift. GRADE certainty: high for Wingate peak power, moderate for squat 1RM / FFM / LBM.

This is the direct evidence behind the map's standing clause "additive to training, not a substitute." The muscle gain is the training's — creatine amplifies it and does not create it. Hand creatine to someone who does not lift and their lean mass does not move. The practical headline for a healthy adult: creatine is a resistance-training amplifier, and its body-composition benefit is contingent on the training stimulus it amplifies.

Read at its true strength. This confirms, from a new (context-stratified) angle, the strength/lean-mass thread the vault already holds as consolidating (creatine-resistance-training-lean-mass-strength, creatine-dose-response-strength). It does not change the dose answer (~5 g/day; no dose signal recoverable here) and does not touch the bone, safety, or cognition threads. The anaerobic-power result is real but a young-athlete surrogate, not a healthspan endpoint — recorded, not promoted, same treatment the vault gave Bai 2026's ergogenic findings.

Rebutted by: creatine-without-training-raised-lean-mass-in-a-thirteen-person-cell — a 12-week trial in adults aged 45–65 reports a fat-free-mass gain in its non-exercise creatine arm, the outcome that is −0.89 kg (p = 0.566) here. The rebuttal does not land and the clause is unchanged: that cell held 13 completers, the trial declined multiplicity correction across 22+ outcome tables and used Fisher's LSD, the gain is never quantified, and the source is appraised unreliable and capped single-study. Recorded so this note cannot be read as unchallenged, and because the population it was challenged in — middle-aged, sedentary, mixed sex — is the one this 18-to-30-year-old male cohort does not cover.

Population caveat. Young men (18–30); the kilogram magnitudes are theirs, not a 43-year-old's. The direction (RT-dependence of the lean-mass effect) is mechanistically robust across ages; the magnitudes are calibrated to this cohort.
