Training improved a cognitive test in older adults; creatine, again, added nothing on top

The Trail Making Test improved significantly in every trained group (elastic band and aquatic alike) and stayed flat in both no-training controls — but creatine did not provide additional cognitive benefit on top of training, at a modest 3 g/day dose over 16 weeks.

Why this matters for the vault. creatine-cognition already holds a healthy-adult cognitive null at a much higher dose (Chun 2026, 10 g/day, no training arm) — this adds a second healthy/community-dwelling-adult null, this time nested inside a trial where the training itself demonstrably improved cognition, giving the "training already accounts for the gain" reading direct support rather than an absence of comparison. The map's standing bottom line is unchanged: creatine's cognitive case rests on a narrow older-adult memory benefit and an acute sleep-deprivation rescue, neither of which this trial tested; a general "creatine helps cognition" claim for a healthy, well-rested, already-training adult continues to have no supporting evidence.

Appraised mixed, capped suggestive — single trial, modest dose relative to the map's higher-dose evidence, no power calculation retrievable for this specific outcome.
