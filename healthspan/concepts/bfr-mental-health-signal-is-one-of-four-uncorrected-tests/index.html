The harm signal misses a Bonferroni threshold by 0.0015 — recorded, not laundered

The trial reports four group-by-time interaction tests: knee function (AKPS, P = .26), pain (VAS, P = .58), physical quality of life (PCS, P = .57), and mental quality of life (MCS, P = .014). One is significant. A Bonferroni-corrected threshold across four families is α = .0125, and P = .014 does not reach it. No family-wise correction is applied or discussed anywhere in the paper.

This note exists because the finding it qualifies is one the vault wants to be true. blood-flow-restriction has spent seven sources documenting that BFR trials declare safety without counting harms (bfr-adverse-event-absence-replicates-without-becoming-safety), and this is the first trial with an instrument that could have found one (bfr-at-80-percent-occlusion-lowered-mental-quality-of-life-against-sham). A vault that has been asking for exactly this evidence for a month is the last party that should relax its standards when the evidence finally arrives in the direction it expected. The discipline is applied here for the same reason it was applied to the walking-cognition null, which was inconvenient rather than convenient: the standard cannot depend on which answer it produces.

Four specific bounds, stated plainly. One of four, uncorrected, and it fails correction. The single significant test does not survive the simplest multiplicity adjustment. No power calculation for this outcome. The parent trial was designed for strength and physical function; the authors say so and lead their limitations with it. Blinding is unverified and probably broken. No blinding-success check was run, and 45-minute cycles at 80% limb occlusion are unlikely to be mistaken for 20 mmHg. The magnitude is contested by the authors themselves — "the relatively small changes in the MCS scores… may or may not hold clinical importance." A 5-point fall is half a standard deviation on this scale and it crosses the population mean, but no minimal important difference is invoked.

What survives all four bounds, and it is not nothing. The comparison is randomised and sham-controlled — more than any prior safety claim in this corpus has been. The effect survived adjustment for the one baseline imbalance (age, P = .04) and for cumulative adherence. It runs in the same direction as two of the authors' own prior rehabilitation trials, where physical quality of life improved while mental quality of life stayed flat or fell. And it appears in an arm that gained no compensating benefit on any efficacy outcome (bfr-added-nothing-to-pain-or-physical-function-against-a-sham-cuff).

So the honest grade is open-question, and the honest sentence for the map is asymmetric in exactly the way shared/safety.md requires. "A missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious flag." This is not sufficient to claim BFR harms mental wellbeing. It is more than sufficient to retire the claim that BFR's harms have been shown to be absent — because seven trials reporting no adverse events had no instrument that could have detected this one, and the first trial that did, detected something.
