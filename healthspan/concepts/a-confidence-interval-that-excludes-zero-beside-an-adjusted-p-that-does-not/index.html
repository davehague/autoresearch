MAPT's best arm reports an interval that excludes zero and an adjusted p value of 0.142, in the same line

The combined multidomain-plus-omega-3 arm is published as 0.093 (95% CI 0.001 to 0.184; adjusted p=0.142). Read the interval and the arm is significant. Read the p value and it is nowhere close. Both numbers are correct.

The reconciliation, stated plainly because this pattern will recur. The confidence interval is unadjusted; the p value is adjusted for the three simultaneous comparisons MAPT made against its single placebo arm. Testing three arms against one control inflates the chance that at least one clears 0.05 by luck, and correcting for that is what moves a borderline result to 0.142.

⚑ The consequence: MAPT's own multiplicity correction is what turned its best arm from positive into null. Had the trial published the unadjusted interval alone, the headline would have read as a successful three-year prevention trial of a lifestyle bundle plus fish oil in at-risk older adults — and the abstract's formatting, which prints the unadjusted interval immediately before the adjusted p, makes that misreading easy. The authors did the conservative thing and their Interpretation rests on the corrected number.

Why the vault records this as an open question rather than a flaw. shared/study-appraisal.md's p-hacking lens is written to catch a paper reporting only the winners of many uncorrected comparisons. MAPT is the mirror image: many comparisons, corrected, and the correction reported honestly even though it cost the trial its positive result. There is no lens in the current set that names this as a strength — and there is no lens that warns a reader to check whether a quoted interval and a quoted p value were computed under the same adjustment. ⚑ The vault has now met the reverse case too (the-significant-estimates-live-in-an-unregistered-reanalysis-in-a-supplementary-figure, where a registered and an unregistered analysis of the same data returned inverted verdicts). Proposed lens (Universal): "Are the reported interval and the reported p value computed under the same multiplicity adjustment? RED — a significant-looking unadjusted interval is printed beside a non-significant adjusted p and the paper's conclusion is not stated. GREEN — the adjustment status of every quoted quantity is explicit and the conclusion names which one it rests on." MAPT is GREEN under that wording; it is recorded because the next paper with this shape may not be.
