The only longitudinal sleep-and-cognition signal here dissolved under its own multiplicity correction

Paquet 2026's cross-sectional analysis found five of thirteen sleep composites associated with cognition. Its longitudinal analysis — the one that could speak to direction — found that actigraphy-based sleep continuity and variability tracked within-person cognitive decline, "although significance decreased after multiple comparison adjustment."

Sleep variability is sleep-regularity's exposure, measured objectively, in a longitudinal model, against cognition. This is the first time in the vault that combination has been tested. The result did not hold.

The vault's rule, stated plainly: a result that does not survive its own paper's correction is not a finding. The authors deserve credit for reporting the attenuation rather than the uncorrected estimate — and then the abstract's conclusion restates it as "actigraphy-based sleep continuity was associated with cognition longitudinally." The correction governs, not the summary. This is the same discipline applied to epigenetic-clock-signal-rests-on-one-of-five-clocks (one hit among five correlated tests, uncorrected) — here the correction was actually run, and it did its job.

Read symmetrically, which shared/evidence-model.md requires. This is not a demonstrated absence. No surviving effect estimate or confidence interval is reported, so the vault cannot say how much power the longitudinal analysis had or how large an effect it could have excluded — the abstract gives neither the longitudinal n nor the multiplicity method (UNVERIFIED, paywalled). It is an unsupported claim, not a refuted one: the honest statement is tested, did not hold, magnitude unknown.

What it changes on the map. sleep-regularity's case rests on mortality — sleep-regularity-predicts-mortality, wrist accelerometry, n=60,977, a 20–48% spread — and was broadened across cognitive and cardiometabolic domains by a 2025 systematic review (sleep-regularity-multi-outcome-review). This source is the first primary test of the cognitive limb with an objective exposure and a temporal design, and it comes back fragile. The mortality claim is untouched. The cognitive limb is now explicitly weaker than the review's summary implies. Status holds emerging.

What would resolve it. A longitudinal analysis powered on sleep regularity specifically — one exposure, pre-registered, rather than thirteen composites screened against one outcome — with a reported effect size whether or not it clears correction.

For David: no change. Anchor consistent sleep and wake times because of the mortality evidence; do not expect a measurable cognitive dividend from it, and do not let a systematic review's "cognitive domain" bullet stand in for a result that a primary study just failed to reproduce.
