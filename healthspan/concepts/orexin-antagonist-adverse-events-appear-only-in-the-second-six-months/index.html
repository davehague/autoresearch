The class's tolerability profile is a six-month profile — at twelve months the adverse events show up

Dual orexin receptor antagonists are described, marketed and short-term-reviewed as well tolerated. At six months, that is exactly what the data say. Pooled across three trials (n = 1,490), any adverse event ran at RR 0.98 (95% CI 0.90–1.08) versus placebo — an interval so tight around 1.0 that it is close to a demonstration of equivalence rather than a mere absence of difference.

At twelve months (2 trials, n = 1,454), the same comparison is RR 1.09 (1.06–1.13), I² = 0%. Statistically unambiguous, and in the direction of more harm.

What "RR 1.09" is and is not. It means adverse events occurred about 9% more often on drug than on placebo over a year — a small relative increase, precisely estimated. It is not a dramatic safety signal. What makes it worth a first-class note is not its size but that it is absent at six months and present at twelve, in the same drugs, in the same review.

⚑ The serious-adverse-event picture carries the same shape and one specific number. Pooled SAEs were not significant at either timepoint (6 months RR 1.79, 0.96–3.33; 12 months RR 1.62, 0.50–5.19). But daridorexant alone at twelve months reached RR 2.78 (1.76–4.39) — an interval clear of 1.0 by a wide margin. ⚠ From one trial, and the individual-dose analyses did not reach significance. One drug, one trial, is not a class safety conclusion. It is the thing to look at next.

⚑ Somnolence is the constant, and it is large: RR 5.96 (3.19–11.11) at six months, RR 4.85 (2.52–9.33) at twelve. Roughly five- to six-fold. This is the mechanistically expected effect of blocking a wake-promoting signal, and it does not attenuate with time. It sits directly alongside the class's weakest efficacy result — orexin-antagonist-daytime-function-benefit-is-the-weakest-result-in-its-own-evidence.

⚑ Discontinuation is where dose bites. Overall, quitting for adverse events did not differ from placebo at either timepoint. At the top of the dose range at six months it did: lemborexant 10 mg RR 2.20 (1.13–4.28), suvorexant 40/30 mg RR 9.22 (2.20–38.67). The suvorexant interval is extremely wide — few events — but its lower bound is 2.20. ⚠ The review notes in passing that the highest approved suvorexant dose in the United States and Japan is 20 mg, while the pooled trials mostly used 40/30 mg. A substantial share of this class's efficacy data comes from doses above what is prescribed.

⚑ Why this is the finding the review exists to produce. Short-term DORA data already existed. The specific thing a ≥6-month synthesis can catch, and a twelve-week trial cannot, is harm that accumulates — and it caught some. A reader carrying forward "well tolerated" from the short-term literature is carrying a claim this paper bounds to the first six months.

⚠ Two trials at twelve months is thin, and reverse-direction caution applies: longer follow-up also means more time for background events in an ageing cohort, which randomisation should balance but small numbers may not.

⚠ INTERACTION FLAG (mechanistic tier, raised not measured). safety_flags: [prescription, interaction]. A five- to six-fold somnolence signal that does not attenuate over twelve months is a class-level central-nervous-system depression caution, and this vault holds three other sleep agents on or near the same axis — melatonin, l-theanine and chamomile. No trial in this source tested co-administration with any of them, and none of their sources tested co-administration with a hypnotic. Per shared/safety.md — a missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious flag; a mechanistic signal is enough to flag — this is raised, tiered as mechanistic, and deliberately kept out of any Benefit score. The plausible harm is additive next-day sedation, which composes with driving and childcare rather than with any biomarker.
