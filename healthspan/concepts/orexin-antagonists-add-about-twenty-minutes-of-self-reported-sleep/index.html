Six months of a dual orexin receptor antagonist buys about twenty extra minutes of self-reported sleep

Pooling the six randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials that ran the three FDA-approved dual orexin receptor antagonists — suvorexant, lemborexant, daridorexant — for six months or longer, in 3,546 adults with diagnosed primary insomnia:

Outcome at 6 monthsMean difference vs placeboI²
Subjective total sleep time+19.87 min (95% CI 14.15 to 25.60)0%
Subjective time to sleep onset−12.69 min (−19.78 to −5.60)0%
Subjective sleep efficiency+4.61 pp (3.85 to 5.37)0%
Subjective wake after sleep onset−15.08 min (−45.57 to 15.41) — null0%

These are good estimates of a modest effect. I² = 0% means the contributing trials agree closely; the intervals are tight and clear of zero. The class does what it claims: it shortens the time to fall asleep by about twelve minutes and extends the night by about twenty.

⚠ At twelve months the total-sleep-time estimate persists but degrades: +16.38 minutes with an interval of 0.08 to 32.68 — a lower bound of five seconds — and heterogeneity rising to I² = 49%.

⚠ Every one of these outcomes is patient-reported. The review's own outcome definitions are "subjective total sleep time," "subjective sleep efficiency," and so on. There is no polysomnography and no actigraphy anywhere in the pooled analysis. In a drug class whose commonest adverse effect is next-day somnolence at 5–6× placebo, the entire efficacy case rests on how people rate their own nights. Double-blinding limits how far that undermines the result; it does not make the measure objective.

⚠ Every trial is manufacturer-run — Merck (suvorexant), Eisai (lemborexant), Idorsia (daridorexant). Randomised, blinded and placebo-controlled, so not disqualifying under shared/study-appraisal.md, but there is no independent replication in this literature.

⚠ And no trial used an active comparator. The review states it plainly: "None directly compared different insomnia drugs, as all evaluated DORAs versus placebo." So twenty minutes cannot be compared against any alternative — not against older hypnotics, and not against cbt-i-first-line-sleep-guideline-consensus, the behavioural treatment guidelines rank ahead of drugs.

⚑ The number is worth stating plainly because it will be summarised as "improves sleep." It does. By about twenty self-reported minutes, from a nightly prescription drug, in trials run by the companies selling it.
