A tomato company's meta-analysis found +1.36 points of arterial dilation, and published the analysis that removes it

Four randomised trials, 182 healthy adults, continuous intake of lycopene-containing products: flow-mediated dilation rose by 1.36 percentage points (95% CI 0.20–2.51), p = 0.02, at low heterogeneity (I² = 24%). Flow-mediated dilation is an ultrasound reading of how much an artery widens after blood flow is briefly occluded and released — an early marker of endothelial function.

⚠ The result is load-bearing on one trial, and it is the sponsor's. Leave-one-out analysis: removing Yoshida et al. makes the pooled effect non-significant; removing any of the other three does not. All three review authors are employed by Kagome Co., Ltd., the tomato-products company that sponsored the review, and they were investigators and co-authors on that trial. It is also the only one of the six reviewed studies they rated at low risk of bias, and the largest.

⚑ They print the objection themselves, verbatim: "the statistically significant pooled effect was lost after exclusion of the authors' own recent study… the possibility of unintended reviewer bias cannot be ruled out because the review authors were involved in conducting that study." Their counter-argument is also on the record and is reasonable: the remaining pool "is based solely on studies with greater methodological limitations." Both things are true. The finding is still one trial deep.

⚑ Why this is filed on anthocyanins and not on a map of its own. The vault's coining bar — set on beetroot-juice and reused for anthocyanins — is an isolating contrast, a machine-measured outcome, low risk of bias, a pre-specified dose. This meets one of four: the outcome is machine-measured; the contrast is foods rather than the compound; one study of six is low-risk; and there is no pre-specified dose and no dose-response. ⚠ Lycopene is a carotenoid, not a flavonoid, so the host map is chosen by claim shape rather than chemistry — a plant-pigment FMD claim with an industry-funded evidence base — and should be carved out if the evidence grows. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

The structural parallel is the useful part. anthocyanins holds an AJCN review part-funded by the US Highbush Blueberry Council, whose one durable randomised finding was FMD +1.41 (0.90–1.91) with lipids, blood pressure and glucose all null (anthocyanins-improve-flow-mediated-dilation-at-dietary-doses). Two coloured plant compounds, two food-industry sponsors, two near-identical FMD effects, no hard endpoint in either. The base rate for what happens next is already on the shelf: cocoa-flavanols carried the same halo into a randomised cardiovascular trial and the pre-specified primary analysis came back null (cosmos-prespecified-primary-cvd-analysis-remained-null).
