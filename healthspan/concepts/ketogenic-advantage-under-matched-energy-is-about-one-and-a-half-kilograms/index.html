Hold the calories constant and the metabolic advantage is a kilogram and a half

The popular case for ketogenic eating is a metabolic advantage — that restricting carbohydrate produces more weight loss than the calorie arithmetic predicts. That claim is only testable when both arms are prescribed the same energy, and Żurawski 2026 is built to enforce exactly that condition. Of 216 full texts assessed, 208 were excluded; after failure to meet the intervention definition, the single largest exclusion reason was lack of energy matching (n = 57 trials).

What survived: six trials, 259 people. Pooled difference −1.49 kg (95% CI −2.41 to −0.58), p = 0.008.

The estimate is unusually stable. I² = 0.0%, τ² < 0.0001. It held under alternative imputations of the change-score standard deviation (−1.46 at r = 0.25; −1.59 at r = 0.75), under exclusion of the one trial rated high risk of bias (−1.42, −2.01 to −0.83), and under leave-one-out. A small result that does not move when you push it.

⚠ And the authors refuse to let their own I² = 0% be over-read, which is the caveat this vault usually has to supply itself: "An I² value of 0% should therefore not be interpreted as evidence of complete clinical homogeneity, particularly given the small number of studies and the limited power of heterogeneity tests." Six studies cannot detect heterogeneity; its absence carries no information.

Three limits keep 1.49 kg from meaning what it appears to mean:

(1) Matched prescription is not matched intake. Verbatim: the criterion "did not establish that achieved energy intake was equivalent throughout the intervention." Two of six trials controlled feeding; the other four ran at least partly free-living with self-reported intake.

(2) The horizon is short and the tissue is unidentified. Five of six trials ran 6–12 weeks and body composition was never synthesised, so the authors state the difference "does not necessarily reflect a proportionally greater reduction in adipose tissue" — glycogen and its bound water remain a live explanation at exactly this magnitude and timeframe (ketogenic-weight-difference-cannot-be-attributed-to-fat-loss).

(3) Match protein as well and the estimate stops saying anything (ketogenic-advantage-becomes-uninformative-when-protein-is-also-matched).

GRADE: LOW, downgraded by the authors themselves for risk of bias and imprecision. Capped suggestive, and it coins ketogenic-diet at emerging on precisely this: a small, stable, low-certainty, short-horizon difference in a quantity that has not been shown to be fat.
