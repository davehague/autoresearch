Aerobic exercise at the end of a long fast is followed by ~547 more kcal over 24 hours

In a registered, conflict-free randomized crossover trial (n=35 healthy young adults), the same 30-minute treadmill run at 65% VO₂peak was performed in the morning after a 12-hour fast and in the evening after a 12-hour fast. Against the morning condition, evening fasted exercise was followed by 400 kcal more acute intake (286–515) at an ad libitum meal and 547 kcal more over 24 hours (335–759), both p < 0.001, with hunger, desire to eat and prospective consumption all higher and fullness lower.

This is a graded interaction between two levers this vault holds on separate maps, and it is flagged as such. intermittent-fasting holds a fasting/time-restricted eating format; zone-2-cardio holds aerobic training. Each is scored on its own. Combined in one order they compose; combined in the other they appear to cancel — an intake surplus of 547 kcal over a day is larger than a compressed eating window plausibly removes. shared/safety.md requires the flag on any credible signal, and this is a randomized within-subject effect with intervals that exclude trivial magnitudes.

The class of interaction is efficacy-defeating, not hazardous. Nobody is harmed; the two practices simply undo each other's point. The vault has graded this class before — cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy (cold water after lifting blunts adaptation, SMD −0.22) and bfr-occlusion-reduces-walking-volume-and-comfort (cuff pressure cuts step count). Like those, it gates naive co-recommendation and does not enter any benefit score.

Read at its true strength — and the load-bearing caveat is a missing arm. mixed, capped suggestive. The abstract describes no non-exercise control condition. Humans eat more in the evening than in the morning regardless of exercise, so the design cannot separate a circadian appetite gradient from exercise-induced compensation. The vault therefore carries the number and declines the word "compensatory." Also: one 30-minute bout, a 24-hour window, no weight or body-composition outcome, 35 healthy young adults, one modality (running, which GOAL.md's knee constraint disfavours), and self-reported appetite ratings in an unblindable protocol.

What it does not establish. Not that evening exercise causes weight gain — nothing was weighed and one day is not a trajectory. Not that morning training is superior for anything other than next-meal intake in young adults. Not that walking, cycling or resistance training behave the same way.

For David: a free scheduling observation, not a rule. If aerobic work and a compressed eating window are both in play, the earlier session is the version less likely to be followed by a large evening intake. Offered at suggestive strength from an acute young-adult study; no schedule and no eating window is prescribed here, per shared/safety.md. Notably, this points the same direction as the appetite suppression he is weighing pharmacologically on glp-1 — and costs nothing.
