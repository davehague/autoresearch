Doing both beats doing one, on a hard endpoint, at high certainty

In a network of 30 randomised trials and 2,137 adults with diagnosed hypertension, combined training — aerobic and resistance work inside the same programme — lowered systolic blood pressure by 9.52 mmHg (95% CI −13.47 to −5.56) against standard care, and lowered diastolic by 5.84 mmHg (−8.51 to −3.16). Both estimates carry high GRADE certainty under CINeMA — 2 of only 17 high-certainty ratings among the network's 90 comparisons.

⚑ Why the magnitude is worth stating plainly. Systolic pressure is the top number: arterial pressure while the heart contracts. A ~9.5 mmHg reduction is in the range a single first-line antihypertensive drug produces. And blood pressure is not a surrogate — it is on the causal path to stroke and heart attack, and it is the endpoint the drug class is licensed on. This is a real clinical effect on a real endpoint, reported in millimetres of mercury rather than standardised units, which is exactly the reporting failure standardised-effect-sizes-conceal-whether-a-blood-pressure-change-is-clinically-meaningful flags in most of this literature.

⚑ It is the fourth independent literature to give combined training the same answer. The vault already holds: combined-aerobic-resistance-tops-endothelial-dose-response (vascular function), combined-aerobic-resistance-tops-glycemic-control-in-older-adults (glycaemia), aerobic-plus-resistance-lowest-mortality (all-cause mortality), and adding-resistance-training-to-aerobic-training-raises-peak-vo2 (fitness). Four outcomes, four separate evidence bases, one answer: the combination beats either component alone. That convergence is worth more than any single estimate.

⚑ And it settles a number the vault refused four days ago. stretching-blood-pressure-effect-is-larger-than-the-better-evidenced-levers recorded a claim of −10.43 mmHg from stretching, ranked first at SUCRA 91.8%, which the vault declined as its implausible-magnitude signature. Tonight the properly graded, transitivity-tested literature puts its best-evidenced intervention at −9.52 mmHg. The declined estimate sat above what a full programme of aerobic plus resistance training achieves, from a modality the same authors defined as having no external load and no cardiopulmonary demand. The refusal was right.

⚠ The bounds. The network is thin for ten nodes: 2,137 participants total, and 7 trials / 284 participants of direct combined-versus-usual-care evidence. Only 4 of the 30 trials were at low risk of bias (21 some concerns, 5 high risk). Blood pressure is unblindable and measurement-sensitive, and only a minority of trials used 24-hour ambulatory monitoring rather than office readings. The "combined training" node pools genuinely different programmes — an elastic-band circuit plus walking, strength plus cycling, a DASH-diet-plus-aerobic-counselling arm — under one label, and no transitivity test detects a definitional problem. Most interventions ran 8–12 weeks, so persistence is unmeasured. Capped suggestive by the source's mixed appraisal.

⚠ Population, stated because it is the main transfer limit. Every trial enrolled people with diagnosed hypertension. Blood-pressure interventions produce much smaller effects in normotensive people, because there is less to lower. These millimetre figures do not transfer to someone with normal blood pressure, and the vault's regimen layer computes that match at stage 05, not here.

⚠ INTERACTION — flagged. This lever and tirzepatide are additively blood-pressure-lowering by different mechanisms and were never studied together; see tirzepatide-lowers-blood-pressure-events-and-raises-hypotension-events-dose-dependently and both maps.
