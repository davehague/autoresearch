Creatine's brain effect in dementia models may not be the same effect in both sexes

A JBI/PRISMA-ScR scoping review of creatine and dementia-related cognition (8,317 records screened, 6 studies/7 publications eligible — 1 in vitro, 4 animal, 1 human pilot, no RCTs anywhere in the literature) surfaces one preclinical finding not previously in this vault: in one rodent dementia model, female animals showed cognitive benefit from creatine supplementation while male animals trended toward harm. The review notes this was paralleled by sex-divergent bioenergetic responses in the human pilot (creatine-brain-alzheimers-pilot-smith-2025, the same n=20 CABA trial already in this vault) — i.e., brain creatine uptake itself may differ by sex, not just the downstream cognitive effect.

This is a single rodent study, capped single-study regardless of how the review frames it, and it is an open question, not a graded harm — the review does not claim male harm is established, only that the direction diverged from the female result in this one model.

Why this matters for the vault, not for David specifically. David is not in a dementia population and this signal has not been tested for a functional consequence in anyone. It is filed as a watch item: if a future human trial in any population reports sex-stratified cognitive outcomes for high-dose creatine, this is the prior thread to check it against.
