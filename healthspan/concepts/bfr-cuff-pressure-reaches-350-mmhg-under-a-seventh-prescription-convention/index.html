A seventh cuff-pressure convention, and the highest numbers yet

Pressure was set by thigh circumference on 5-cm cuffs: 200, 250, 300 or 350 mmHg, held bilaterally and continuously for the entire 30-minute session. Every previous source in this vault prescribed either a percentage of arterial or limb occlusion pressure (60–80% LOP) or absolute values in a 50–143 mmHg band; the highest-pressure regimen the vault had recorded was ≥120 mmHg (bfr-cardiovascular-response-data-older-adults).

Bounded honestly: this is not straightforwardly two to three times the occlusion. Narrow cuffs require higher absolute pressure to achieve the same arterial occlusion than wide ones, and 5 cm is narrow. 350 mmHg on a 5-cm band is not comparable to 120 mmHg on a wide one in either direction.

⚑ That incomparability is the claim, not an objection to it. The map's standing position is that cuff pressure IS the dose — the axis along which harm would scale — so a literature that cannot standardise it cannot bound its own risk. Seven sources have now used seven conventions, and the newest adds a cuff-width variable that makes even absolute numbers non-comparable across the corpus.

The authors agree with the diagnosis. They list it among their own limitations: circumference-stratified absolute pressure is defensible and widely used, but "individualized AOP/LOP-based prescription is acknowledged as the methodological gold standard," and future work "should incorporate individualized pressure assessment to enhance precision and reproducibility."
