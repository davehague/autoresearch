Three months of strength training specifically remodels long-range white-matter tracts in elderly brains — balance training does not show the same signature

A randomized, passively-controlled trial (n=60, 64-82y) found that 3 months of strength training, but not the balance-training arm of the same trial, was associated with changes in diffusion metrics consistent with white-matter microstructural remodeling — specifically increased extra-axonal axial diffusivity in the bilateral inferior fronto-occipital and longitudinal fasciculi, long-range tracts connecting frontal regions to occipital and temporal regions.

This is a surrogate, structural-imaging outcome — no cognitive or functional correlate was reported alongside it in the retrievable abstract. It extends resistance-training's existing brain-structure thread (previously built on a cross-sectional accelerometer-and-MRI association, Saxler 2026, where most of the signal was mediated by BMI/cardiometabolic risk rather than running directly to the brain) with a small but genuinely randomized data point specific to strength training as a modality.

Read cautiously: n≈20 per arm, no stated power calculation, and a trial testing multiple tracts and diffusion metrics carries real multiple-comparison risk. reliability: mixed caps this at suggestive.
