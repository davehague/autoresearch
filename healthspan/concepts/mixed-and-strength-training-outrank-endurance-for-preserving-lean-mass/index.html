For keeping lean mass through a diet, mixed training ranked first and endurance alone did not clear the bar

A frequentist network meta-analysis (33 RCTs, 41 comparisons) ranked training modes against calorie restriction alone for preserving fat-free mass: mixed training +1.20 kg (95% CI +0.67, +1.73, P-score 0.93), strength training +0.83 kg (+0.17, +1.49, p=0.013, P-score 0.66), endurance training +0.51 kg (−0.04, +1.05, p=0.067 — short of significance, P-score 0.41). Network inconsistency was not significant for this outcome (Q=6.56, df=6, p=0.363), so direct and indirect evidence agree.

Read it as an ordering, not as three numbers. P-score rankings from a 33-study network with moderate within-design heterogeneity (I² = 60.5%) are fragile, the pairwise subgroup test found no significant difference between modes (only the point estimates favoured strength), and the parent estimate carries self-detected publication bias (exercise-preserves-fat-free-mass-during-calorie-restriction). Graded single-study on that basis. The defensible statement is: doing something that includes resistance work beats doing cardio alone, which beats doing nothing — not that mixed training buys 0.37 kg more than strength training.

The body-weight ranking from the same network should not be quoted at all. For body mass, inconsistency between direct and indirect evidence was substantial (Q=35.52, df=6, p<0.001). The network is trustworthy for the muscle question and not for the weight question, and the vault carries only the former.

It is the vault's existing advice, arriving from a new direction. resistance-training and protein-target have been the muscle-preservation answer here from the beginning, and creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training made the same point about supplements: the training is what does the work. This is the first time the fat-free-mass map itself carries randomized evidence for the ordering. Status held emerging on fat-free-mass.
