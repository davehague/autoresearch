A weight difference measured at the exact horizon where glycogen and its water dominate

Żurawski 2026's outcome is body weight in kilograms. It is not fat, and the paper says so directly:

"Early body weight reduction may partly reflect depletion of glycogen stores and the water associated with them rather than loss of adipose tissue alone... The observed difference in body weight between groups does not necessarily reflect a proportionally greater reduction in adipose tissue. Because body composition was not synthesized, the relative contributions of changes in adipose tissue, glycogen, and body water remain uncertain."

The timing makes this the leading explanation rather than a technicality. Five of the six pooled trials ran 6–12 weeks. Carbohydrate restriction depletes muscle and liver glycogen within days, and roughly three grams of water leave with each gram of glycogen — a shift of one to two kilograms, arriving early, permanent for as long as the restriction lasts, and entirely non-adipose. The pooled difference is 1.49 kg. The confound is the same size as the finding and lands on the same schedule.

The authors add a second, independent reason not to read the kilograms as fat: "Controlled feeding studies also indicate that the increase in fat oxidation that follows carbohydrate restriction does not necessarily translate into greater body-fat loss." Burning more fat and losing more fat are different claims, and the first has been demonstrated while the second has not.

⚠ And adherence to the exposure itself is inconsistently verified. Ketosis was confirmed by plasma ketones in two trials, by urinary reagent strips in two, not reported in two, and "not clearly described" in one. A third of the base has no biochemical evidence that the intervention arm was ever in the metabolic state the intervention is named for. The vault has a standing companion habit for exactly this, written on magnesium-glycinate on 2026-08-02: when a number does not move, ask whether the intervention arrived. The mirror applies here — when a number does move, ask the same question, because a diet that never reached ketosis and still lost weight is a statement about calories, not carbohydrate.

This is why the new map opens without a body-composition claim of any kind. ketogenic-diet records a weight difference, its size, its certainty and its unknown tissue composition — and records that the vault holds no evidence at all about what a ketogenic diet does to fat mass, lean mass, or anything measured at twelve months.

⚑ Carried forward as a flagged open question, not a graded finding: the authors note that "in some studies, ketogenic diets were associated with increases in LDL cholesterol concentrations, which is clinically relevant given the established causal role of LDL-C in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease." This synthesis measured no lipids.
