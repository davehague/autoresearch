The protective association is biggest exactly where you would expect it to be an artefact

Alzheimer's disease has a preclinical course measured in decades. People in that phase move less, years before anyone diagnoses them. That is reverse causation, and it produces a protective-looking association between activity and dementia without activity doing anything.

Feter 2026 built two guards against it: cohorts with dementia or mild cognitive impairment at baseline were excluded, and older cohorts had to have a valid baseline cognitive assessment. Then two of its moderator analyses came back pointing the wrong way.

  • Follow-up duration moderated the effect: studies with under 5 years of follow-up found a larger protective association than studies with 5 or more. If activity genuinely prevented dementia, longer exposure windows should if anything show more benefit, not less. A signal that shrinks as you move the measurement further from the diagnosis is the classic shape of reverse causation.
  • Diagnostic criteria moderated it too (p=0.0061): studies using cognitive screening tests alone found a stronger protective association than studies using clinical DSM criteria. Screening instruments pick up mild, early, and reversible states that a clinical work-up would not call dementia — the states most likely to be caused by whatever is also reducing activity.

⚑ The fix exists and almost nobody ran it. A lag analysis — dropping everyone diagnosed in the first few years of follow-up — is the standard remedy. Across 74 studies, exactly one long-follow-up study reported one, and only for leisure-time activity. It agreed with the main result. The authors' own words: the scarcity of such analyses "precludes a definitive assessment of reverse causation," and they recommend it become a standard methodological safeguard in this literature.

⚠ This pushes back on the strength of the headline protective estimate, not on its direction. Two independent things point the other way and are worth stating: the association held after adjusting for baseline age, education, chronic disease and APOEε4 status; and for Alzheimer's disease specifically, follow-up exceeding 20 years showed the lowest risk (RR 0.68, 0.57–0.81) — the opposite of what pure reverse causation predicts. So the honest position is that reverse causation is inflating an effect that is probably real, by an amount nobody has measured.

⚑ And it does not touch the domain finding at all. Reverse causation predicts that people sliding toward dementia do less of everything — including work. It does not predict that they do more occupational activity. The sign-flip in physical-activity-domain-flips-the-sign-of-the-dementia-association is the one claim in the paper this failure mode cannot manufacture, which is a reason to weight the structural finding above the magnitude.
