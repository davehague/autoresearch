Lifting twice a week roughly halves vegan-diet muscle loss in older adults — it does not stop it (RCT arm, n=23)

Within the same 72-person trial, the arm that combined the self-selected vegan diet with supervised progressive resistance training twice weekly (VEG-RE) lost significantly less thigh muscle volume than diet alone — ‒0.35 L (95% CI ‒0.47, ‒0.24 L) vs ‒0.61 L in VEG-only (P=0.0038) — and less appendicular lean mass (‒0.62 kg vs ‒1.15 kg, P=0.0033). Muscle protein synthesis rate was also higher in VEG-RE (1.24%/day) than VEG-alone (1.09%/day, P=0.0246).

The catch: VEG-RE still lost muscle. Protein intake fell by roughly the same amount in the exercise arm as in the diet-only arm — the training stimulus arrived without the substrate to fully use it. Muscle strength trended in opposite directions between arms (VEG ~5–10% weaker; VEG-RE ~3–8% stronger), which is a real-looking and biologically coherent signal, but the between-group contrast lost statistical significance after Benjamini–Hochberg correction for multiple comparisons — the paper reports this honestly rather than hiding it, so it is filed as a trend, not a demonstrated strength benefit.

Why this matters beyond this map. It is a direct empirical test of a claim protein-target has made from indirect evidence: that resistance training raises sensitivity to protein but cannot substitute for it. Training attendance was high (21±3 of 24 sessions) and training volume increased as prescribed — so the null-ish strength result is not an adherence artifact, it is a substrate-limitation result.
