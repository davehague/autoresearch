Vitamin D does not raise testosterone — the fourth extra-skeletal claim to fail randomisation

Across 11 randomised comparisons in adult men, vitamin D supplementation produced no clear effect on total testosterone: MD 0.47 nmol/L (−0.50 to 1.44), I² = 24.1%. Every other androgen model was null too — SHBG, free androgen index, calculated free testosterone, bioactive testosterone.

This is an unusually informative null by this vault's standards. Heterogeneity is low (I² = 24.1%) — the trials agree with each other rather than cancelling out — the analysis used Hartung-Knapp adjustment, which widens intervals conservatively in small meta-analyses, and the upper bound of +1.44 nmol/L sits against a typical total-testosterone range of roughly 10–35 nmol/L. Even the most favourable end of the interval is too small to matter. Most nulls the vault ingests are underpowered intervals doing an impression of absence (rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered); this one is closer to the real thing, and the vault credits it as such — the same symmetry applied to bfr-knee-function-null-in-patients.

What holds it back from "excluded": a disclosed retrieval hole. Embase was not searched (institutional access unavailable) and 27 of 162 assessable reports could not be obtained. GRADE certainty is low for the primary. An absence assembled from an incomplete search carries an asterisk — and to the authors' credit, they are the ones who put it there.

Four for four. vitamin-d now holds randomised nulls on cancer and CVD (vitamin-d-no-cancer-cvd-benefit), fractures (vitamin-d-no-fracture-benefit), telomere length as a mixed/positive surrogate (vitamin-d-preserves-telomere-length), and now androgens. Every extra-skeletal outcome family the supplement is popularly sold on, tested randomly, comes back flat — while the observational signal that made it popular persists. This is exactly the consensus-flip shape shared/evidence-model.md warns about, and vitamin D is the vault's cleanest live instance.

It does not touch the map's live question, and that is recorded deliberately. vitamin-d stays contested rather than fading because deficiency correction remains open (vitamin-d-deficiency-mortality-signal). This paper pools adult men with no baseline vitamin-D-status stratification readable from the abstract, so it says nothing about correcting a genuine deficiency. A future run must not close the deficiency gap on this citation.

For David: the population is finally right — adult men — and the endpoint is still a biomarker, several steps from anything GOAL.md names. The practical content is a non-purchase: vitamin D is not an androgen lever, and the muscle route runs through resistance-training and protein-target (protein-supplementation-augments-resistance-training). Status contested holds — a fourth null on an already-downgraded map confirms it rather than moving it.
