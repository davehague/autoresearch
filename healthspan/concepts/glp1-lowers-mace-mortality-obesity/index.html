GLP-1 receptor agonists lower MACE and all-cause mortality in obesity (target-trial emulation)

In 140,169 propensity-matched pairs of adults with obesity and no diabetes, GLP-1 receptor agonists (liraglutide, semaglutide, tirzepatide) versus other anti-obesity medications were associated with lower major adverse cardiovascular events (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.72–0.81) and all-cause mortality (HR 0.49, 0.42–0.57), plus lower acute coronary syndrome (0.70), heart failure (0.81), and stroke (0.66).

Why this matters for the vault. It is the vault's first ingested evidence on GLP-1 — the intervention David is personally weighing (GOAL.md) — and it lands in the right population (obesity without diabetes). The direction and magnitude are consistent with the randomized SELECT program, which lends the association weight.

Bounded honestly — and gated by safety, not ranking: this is observational (EHR emulation) with residual confounding, shorter follow-up in the GLP-1 arm, and proportional-hazards violations that likely inflate the raw hazard ratios — so it is supportive, not RCT-grade. Per shared/safety.md, GLP-1 is a prescription/injectable: it never enters the ranked daily protocol (safety_flags: [prescription]) and no dose is set. The interaction flag marks its overlap with intermittent-fasting (both suppress intake) and its tendency to undercut the protein-target and accelerate lean-mass loss — countered by resistance-training + protein.
