Semaglutide reduces MACE in obesity with established CVD, without diabetes (SELECT RCT)

In the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled SELECT trial (n=17,604, BMI ≥27, established cardiovascular disease, no diabetes), once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg cut the primary MACE endpoint (CV death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke) to 6.5% vs 8.0% — HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.72–0.90), P<0.001 over a mean 34 months (NNT 67). Nonfatal MI HR 0.72 (0.61–0.85) and the heart-failure composite 0.82 (0.71–0.96) moved with it; mean weight change −9.3% vs −0.8%.

Why this matters for the vault. This is the RCT-tier corroboration logged as an open gap when the map was coined on Tang 2025 (observational emulation). A randomized hard-endpoint win lifts glp-1 off purely observational footing and moves its Status to consolidating.

Bounded honestly. (1) Secondary prevention — every participant already had cardiovascular disease, so the benefit is proven in that population, not in a metabolically healthy primary-prevention adult (a residual gap). (2) The pre-specified hierarchical testing chain broke at CV death (HR 0.85, P=0.07), so downstream endpoints — all-cause mortality (HR 0.81, 0.71–0.93) and the HF composite — are nominally positive but not formally significant; this recalibrates Tang 2025's much larger observational all-cause mortality HR 0.49 toward a more modest, unconfirmed effect. (3) Novo Nordisk-sponsored registration trial. Per shared/safety.md, GLP-1 is a prescription/injectable: it never enters the ranked daily protocol (safety_flags: [prescription]) and no dose is set here. The interaction flag carries its overlap with intermittent-fasting (both suppress intake) and its tendency to undercut the protein-target and accelerate lean-mass loss — countered by resistance-training + protein.
