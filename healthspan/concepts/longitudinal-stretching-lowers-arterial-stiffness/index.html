Four to twelve weeks of stretching lowered arterial stiffness — on six studies, so the direction is the finding and the size is not

Pooling six longitudinal studies, stretching programmes of 4 to 12 weeks reduced pulse wave velocity by SMD −1.02 (95% CI −1.79 to −0.25), p = 0.012.

What the outcome is. Pulse wave velocity measures how fast a pressure wave travels down an artery. Stiff arteries carry it faster, so a lower velocity means more compliant vessels. It is the best-validated measure of arterial stiffness and it predicts cardiovascular events — but it is a marker, not an event. Nobody in these studies had a heart attack, a stroke, or died. This is a surrogate, which shared/evidence-model.md ranks below the hard outcomes the rest of this vault's exercise maps are built on.

Why the direction is taken and the magnitude is not. A standardised mean difference of −1.02 puts the average stretcher a full standard deviation below the average control. From stretching. Over four to twelve weeks. Set that against what this vault holds for far better-evidenced interventions: resistance training lowers systolic blood pressure −6.11 mmHg across 51 trials (resistance-training-lowers-blood-pressure-older-adults); passive heat is null on most cardiometabolic markers (passive-heat-null-on-most-cardiometabolic-markers). An implausibly large effect from a handful of small studies is a signature this vault has flagged four times and been right each time — twice on collagen (collagen-ranking-rests-on-indirect-evidence, collagen-bone-signal-heterogeneous-postmenopausal), on ashwagandha (ashwagandha-effect-sizes-implausibly-large), and on every ranking leaderboard it has declined.

Two structural weaknesses, stated plainly. The pooled set permitted non-randomised controlled designs, and the retrieved record does not say how many of the six were randomised — so this may not be an RCT pooling at all. And heterogeneity, publication-bias assessment and risk-of-bias ratings are all unrecoverable from the available record, on a literature where pulse wave velocity is measured across different arterial segments with different instruments.

The interesting part is the contrast with the acute result. A single stretching session moved nothing (acute-stretching-does-not-change-arterial-stiffness). If the longitudinal effect is real, that makes it a training adaptation rather than a transient vascular response — biologically the more substantial claim, and the harder one to establish on six studies.

What this does not do. It does not give stretching a hard-endpoint case, and it does not overturn the finding that objectively-measured flexibility is a weak-to-null independent predictor of later health (flexibility-weak-predictor-of-health-outcomes-adults). It supplies a candidate mechanism where the map previously had none. Mechanism sits below outcome, and the map's Status is unchanged.

No dose follows from this — see stretching-dose-recommendation-rests-on-one-of-four-moderator-contrasts, and shared/safety.md on not synthesising doses.
