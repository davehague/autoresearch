Creatine (not beta-alanine) improves sprint, repeated-sprint ability, and upper-body endurance in athletes

A network meta-analysis of 52 RCTs finds creatine — not beta-alanine — is the supplement with a real signal across acute, high-intensity athletic performance tasks, versus placebo:

  • Sprint time: SMD = −0.64 (95% CI −1.27 to −0.01; p = 0.04; P-score 0.91). Negative SMD here means faster sprint time — a benefit, not a harm; sprint is a "lower is better" outcome.
  • Repeated-sprint ability (RSA): SMD = −0.78 (−1.40 to −0.17; p = 0.01; P-score 0.98). Same direction convention: negative SMD = faster total/average sprint time across repeated efforts = benefit.
  • Upper-body muscular endurance (UME): SMD = 0.43 (0.09 to 0.77; p = 0.01; P-score 0.92). This is the cleanest of the four positive-leaning findings — the only one where Egger's test showed no publication-bias signal (p = 0.30).
  • Jump performance: SMD = 0.33 (0.11 to 0.56; p = 0.002) — nominally significant, but its own P-score ranking is weak: creatine ranks only 0.34 (beta-alanine ranks higher, 0.48; creatine+beta-alanine lowest, 0.23), and none of the three arms beat each other with statistical confidence. Treat jump as the softest of the four.
  • Agility and lower-body muscular endurance: no significant effect from creatine or beta-alanine (p > 0.05 both).
  • Beta-alanine alone never reaches significance in any domain.

Why suggestive, not strong. Sprint and RSA — two of the three cleanest-looking wins — both carry a statistically significant Egger's-test publication-bias signal (sprint p = 0.02, RSA p = 0.01), which the source's own Discussion attributes to "the prevalence of small-sample trials reporting positive Cr outcomes." The source's reliability: mixed verdict (full reasoning in creatine-beta-alanine-combined-nma-bai-2026) caps this note at suggestive regardless of tier.

What's new for the map. creatine-monohydrate already carries strong evidence for creatine's lean-mass/strength benefit (Chilibeck 2017, Ashtary-Larky 2025) and a bone-null. This is a different outcome domain — acute, sport-specific athletic performance (sprint speed, repeated-sprint capacity, muscular-endurance reps) rather than resistance-training adaptation. stance: extends because it is a new sub-question, not a reconciliation of an existing claim.

Population note (neutral fact, not a personal-fit judgment). This evidence comes from athletes and regularly physically active adults tested on sprint/jump/RSA tasks — an athletic-performance surrogate, not a healthspan endpoint (muscle mass, function, bone, cognition).
