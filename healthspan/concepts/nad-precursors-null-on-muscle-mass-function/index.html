NAD precursors (NMN + NR) don't improve muscle mass or function in older adults

A meta-analysis of 10 randomized controlled trials found NMN and nicotinamide riboside had no significant effect on any pooled measure of muscle or physical function in adults over 60: skeletal muscle index (MD −0.42 kg/m², 95% CI −0.99 to 0.14, p=0.14), handgrip strength (both hands ns, I²=0%), gait speed (MD −0.01 m/s, ns), and five-time chair-stand (MD −0.21 s, ns). Removing diabetic/prediabetic and high-risk-of-bias trials left the null unchanged. The authors' conclusion: "current evidence does not support the use of NMN and NR as effective interventions for improving muscle function and mass in adults above 60 years old."

Why this matters for the vault. This is a meta-analysis of RCTs — the top evidence tier — landing null on the exact "rebuild aging muscle" claim that markets these supplements. It does not contest that NMN/NR raise blood NAD⁺; it shows the biomarker doesn't convert into function. That biomarker-moves-but-outcome- doesn't pattern is the vault's recurring discipline line (cf. omega-3 surrogate clocks, taurine failed premise). Contrast with creatine-monohydrate, the one supplement in the vault with real strength/lean-mass RCT support — the boring lever works where NAD boosting doesn't. Sets nad-precursors to contested.
