Dairy protein tops a second independent network — and the paper prints the interval that says a future trial could still find nothing

The ranking. Across 36 randomised trials, whey produced +1.24 kg of lean body mass versus placebo (95% credible interval 0.61 to 1.87), milk +1.18 kg, casein +0.92 kg. Rice, blends, soy and pea all had positive point estimates and none of their intervals cleared zero. Whey also topped the exploratory strength outcome (+5.8 kg on the one-repetition maximum, 1.9 to 9.7), and a cluster-ranking plot put it in the best quadrant on both outcomes at once.

⚑ This is a replication, and the vault already held the bigger version. protein-supplement-type-effects-small-whey-baseline came from a 78-RCT, 4,755-participant network reporting whey vs placebo at a standardised mean difference of 0.16 (0.05 to 0.28) for fat-free mass, with plant proteins not clearing zero. Tonight's network is independent — different team, different search, Bayesian rather than frequentist, half the size — and it reproduces both halves. Two networks agreeing on type is the more useful fact than either ranking on its own.

⚑ The number to actually read is the predictive interval, and it is why this note is suggestive rather than strong. A credible interval says where the average effect plausibly sits. A predictive interval says where a single future trial would plausibly land. Whey's credible interval is 0.61 to 1.87 kg — clearly positive. Whey's predictive interval is −0.18 to 2.66 kg — it includes nothing at all. Milk's and casein's do too. The paper publishes this and states what it means: "although the mean effects point in the direction of supplementation being beneficial, the results of the studies may vary." A league table with SUCRA values of 0.91 and 0.78 sits directly above a table saying a future trial might find zero, and both are true.

⚠ How much of this ordering is real. CINeMA certainty: whey vs placebo moderate (downgraded for imprecision and imperfect blinding); milk, casein and soy low; pea, rice and blend very low. The threshold analysis — how many additional null trials would displace each rank — gives whey 8 and everything from rice downward 1 or 2. ⚑ So the top of this table is reasonably stable and the bottom of it is an ordering printed on noise. Excluding the five high-risk-of-bias trials moved whey's estimate by less than 0.1 kg; restricting to the 22 double-blind trials increased it (+1.31 kg, 0.58 to 2.04).

⚠ What breaks it. Whey was tested mostly in young trained men (mean age 34.1), while casein and milk nodes carried more older and untrained participants — an effect-modifier imbalance the authors name, which means every indirect comparison inherits it and transitivity is explicitly not established. The consistency model beat the inconsistency model by only ΔDIC = 2.8 against the paper's own 5-point threshold, so the assumption underpinning the indirect estimates failed its pre-specified test. 72% of trials recruited men only. Median trial length 12 weeks.

What the magnitude is worth. The authors decline to call +1.24 kg clinically important, correctly: "No universally accepted minimal clinically important difference exists for lean body mass." Over roughly twelve weeks, about a kilogram, against a placebo, in people who were lifting anyway.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal. stance: confirms — it agrees with the map's existing belief that supplemental protein adds a small real amount on top of training, and with the vault's existing whey-baseline finding.
