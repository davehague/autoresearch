The supplement stops paying at about a scoop and a half — and about five grams later if you are over 65

What was fitted. The authors converted every trial arm to grams of supplemental protein per day and fitted a restricted cubic spline — a curve flexible enough to bend but constrained enough not to chase noise — through the whole network. The whey curve rises steeply from 15 to 30 g/day and then flattens at roughly 30–35 g/day. Past 35 g/day the incremental lean-mass return diminishes. Casein and milk look similar over about 25–35 g/day, with more uncertainty. Age-stratified curves push the plateau right in adults aged 65 or older, to 35–40 g/day — an extra ~5 g/day, which the authors attribute to anabolic resistance: ageing muscle needs a larger leucine signal to trigger the same protein-synthesis response.

The same finding in linear form. The Bayesian meta-regression puts +0.024 kg of lean mass per extra g/day (95% credible interval 0.004 to 0.044) — about a quarter of a kilogram per extra 10 g/day. Longer programmes helped too (+0.035 kg/week). Mean participant age did not credibly predict the effect.

⚑ Why this is a stop signal rather than a start signal. The authors write the practical version themselves: for people already eating adequately (they name 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day), "it is unlikely that further muscle development will be achieved by consuming more than 35 g/d of a supplement" and "resources such as money or calories can be redirected to other performance factors such as sleep hygiene, periodization of training, or diet quality." ⚑ This is a synthesis of 36 supplement trials telling its readers when to stop buying supplement.

⚠ It is exploratory, and its authors say so three times. The whey spline rests on 10 dose-arm data points. The plateau is called exploratory in the abstract, in the results and in the conclusion. Casein and milk had fewer points still; pea and rice had too few dose levels to fit a spline at all, and only a linear model could be run for them. The age-stratified curve rests on the nine trials in adults ≥65, which the authors call "hypothesis-generating rather than definitive."

⚠ And the deeper limit sits underneath the whole curve. It is a dose-response in supplemental grams, fitted without knowing what participants habitually ate — see protein-dose-response-cannot-separate-supplementing-from-replacing-without-baseline-intake. maps/protein-target's standing belief is that the lever is the daily total near ~1.6 g/kg/day. For an 84 kg adult that is about 134 g/day, and a 30 g scoop is 22% of it. So the plateau this curve found is a plateau in the fifth of the total that comes from a tub — compatible with the map's belief, and not an extension of it.

⚠ It also does not say when to take it. The authors' practice bullet mentions a peri-workout window; the vault's own higher-tier evidence says timing and distribution are null (protein-timing-distribution-null-on-lean-mass). Nothing about the timing clause is supported by anything in this paper's own analysis — it fitted daily grams, not clocks. The vault records the dose and declines the window.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal. No dose is recommended here; this describes where a curve fitted to ten points stopped rising (shared/safety.md).
