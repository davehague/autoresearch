The first published effect sizes for this practice — and only two of seven clear a clinical threshold

tai-chi was coined on 2026-08-18 with a specific complaint: Chen 2026's umbrella review graded 547 associations and named twenty-three at high certainty, publishing no point estimate, no interval and no standardised mean difference for any of them (tai-chi-knee-osteoarthritis-function-is-high-certainty-evidence). Certainty and magnitude had been separated, and only certainty was published. This synthesis supplies the magnitudes, for sarcopenia.

⚑ The split is the finding: the two outcomes that clear a clinical threshold are both about how someone moves — walking speed and a composite of balance, gait and chair-rise. The two that do not are the two about tissue — grip strength and muscle mass.

Grip is the starkest. Sarcopenia is diagnosed at handgrip below 30 kg for men and 20 kg for women. A pooled gain of 1.13 kg moves a person about four to six percent of the way across that line. It is a real, tightly-estimated, statistically robust difference, and it does not reclassify anybody. ASMI is worse: +0.20 kg/m² against a cutoff of 8.90 is roughly two percent.

⚠ Every one of the seven rows is "versus not exercising." Sixteen of the seventeen trials used health education, non-intervention, conventional therapy, routine care or remote guidance as the comparator; exactly one, with sixteen participants per arm, compared against resistance training (the-sarcopenia-tce-literature-has-one-trial-against-real-exercise). So even the two thresholds that are cleared are cleared against a control group that did nothing, and some unknown share of the gain is the benefit of attending a supervised programme three to five times a week.

⚠ Three further limits. The intervals above come from fixed-effects models justified by an implausible I² of 0–2% across trials running 4 to 72 weeks in participants aged 62 to 88 (near-zero-heterogeneity-across-wildly-heterogeneous-trials-is-a-signal-not-a-reassurance) — a random-effects model would have produced wider intervals. Five of the seven outcomes are effort-dependent timed or repetition tests performed in front of unblinded assessors. And 25 of 72 eligible full-text reports could not be obtained and were dropped, so about a third of the literature is unread.

GRADE certainty across all outcomes: low to moderate, downgraded for risk of bias, imprecision and limited generalisability. Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal.
