The cold-pack manufacturer paid for the trial that found cold packs do nothing

Axanova AG makes topical cooling products. It funded this trial, paid its publication charge, and supplied the gel pack under test. The trial concluded that "no evidence was found for accelerated physiological recovery" on either primary outcome.

shared/study-appraisal.md is explicit that industry funding is a RED flag to record and never fatal on its own — "a clean randomized, blinded, adequately-powered, hard-endpoint RCT can still be reliable despite heavy sponsor control." shared/evidence-model.md's bias watch says conflicted studies are flagged and never laundered into higher apparent certainty. Both rules are written for the case where the money and the result point the same way. This is the other case, and the vault records it explicitly so the rule is visibly symmetric.

A sponsor-funded null is the most credible form a null can take. Every ordinary incentive — selective outcome reporting, a friendlier comparator, quiet non-publication — pointed toward a positive result or no result at all, and none of them operated. The trial was prospectively registered (NCT06813690) before recruitment, both primary outcomes were named in advance, both were reported, and the finding that reached print is the one that damages the funder's product category.

⚑ Why this matters beyond one trial: the vault has been accumulating the opposite examples. The creatine safety review carried an AlzChem-funded author group and advisory-board roles, and its reassuring direction was weighted down as review-tier (creatine-safety-profile). The Magtein® L-threonate trial was industry-adjacent with borderline p-values and its map was flagged for human review. The bone-geometry letter carried severe industry conflicts with author–trial overlap (creatine-high-dose-bone-indices). sponsor-adverse-findings-and-sponsor-friendly-framing-can-coexist is the vault's reference case for the subtler version. Against that ledger, a sponsor publishing against itself is genuinely rare and is worth naming as a green flag rather than passing over in silence.

⚠ What this does not do. It does not make the trial well-powered — the sample size was calculated for effects of d ≈ 1.2–1.4 and the authors concede smaller effects were undetectable (local-cooling-does-not-speed-recovery-at-any-timing). It does not verify that the cooling reached the muscle (gel-pack-cooling-may-not-deliver-a-thermal-dose-to-deep-muscle). Credible funding does not repair a design, and this note grades the funding, not the finding.

The generalisable habit, recorded as a habit and not a finding: check which direction the money points relative to the result, not merely whether money is present. A conflicted study reporting an inconvenient answer has cleared a bar an independent study never had to.
