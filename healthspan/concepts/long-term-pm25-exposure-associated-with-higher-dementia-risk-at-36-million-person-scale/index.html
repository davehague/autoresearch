A second, larger PM2.5-dementia synthesis lands — and it reinforces the vault's refusal to coin an air-pollution map, not overturns it

air-pollution-cognitive-harm-is-consistent-and-unrandomised recorded the vault's tenth candidate refusal: PM2.5 was the most consistent exposure in a 54-review umbrella (dementia HR 1.225–1.592), and the vault still declined to coin a map because there is no intervention (modeled ambient concentration at a residential address, nobody assigned anything), exposure is inseparable from socioeconomic position by construction, and the better-measured effects sit inside confounding range.

Huang 2025 is a larger, methodologically different synthesis — a Burden of Proof meta-analysis (the GBD programme's deliberately conservative, heterogeneity-explicit framework) pooling 28 cohorts, ~36.3 million people, ~2.73 million incident dementia cases/deaths — and it lands at a comparable magnitude: RR 1.37 (1.13–1.69) at 15.5 vs 2.0 µg/m³, rated only ★★ (weak-to-moderate) by the framework's own conservative star system. None of the refusal's grounds are resolved. The exposure is still modeled ambient concentration, not something a person is assigned; the review's own diagnostics found only one covariate significant enough to retain across the whole pool, meaning most between-study heterogeneity — plausibly including socioeconomic confounding — is unexplained; and significant publication bias was detected by Egger's regression for both all-cause dementia and Alzheimer's disease specifically. A second large synthesis reaching a similar number by a different method is a replication of the association, not a resolution of why the vault refused to act on it.
