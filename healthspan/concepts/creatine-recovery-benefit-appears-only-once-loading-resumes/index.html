Creatine is an adjunct to reloading, not a substitute for it — and it disappears again wherever loading is blocked

The same review that finds creatine useless during strict immobilization (creatine-does-not-prevent-atrophy-during-strict-immobilisation) finds a different picture once progressive loading resumes. Hespel 2001 — ineffective through a 14-day cast — then found creatine accelerated recovery of quadriceps cross-sectional area and maximal knee-extension power across ten weeks of resistance training. Additive signals appear in knee osteoarthritis (function, lean mass, paired with strengthening), polymyositis/dermatomyositis (functional performance and phosphocreatine ratios on ³¹P-MRS, 20 g/day loading → 3 g/day with home exercise) and Parkinson's disease (upper-body strength, chair-rise, with resistance training).

⚠ And it vanishes wherever loading is blocked. Null at ACL reconstruction (6 months), total knee arthroplasty (30 days), acute inpatient stroke, COPD exercise capacity, and peripheral arterial disease (6-minute walk distance, calf oxygen saturation). The exercise-induced-muscle-damage literature is inconsistent.

The authors' explanation is coherent and untestable as stated. Early pain, swelling, restricted weight-bearing and tissue healing "may limit the high-effort loading through which creatine is most likely to add value." That predicts the null pattern well — but any null can be attributed to insufficient loading, and no trial in this set measured or reported the rehabilitation dose. It becomes a real claim only when one does; the authors list that as their first recommendation for future work. The nulls are recorded as nulls; the explanation is recorded as a hypothesis.

⚑ The operating shape, and it is the same shape three times over now. Creatine has no independent anabolic action in humans at rest. It amplifies a mechanical stimulus. So the question is never "does creatine work" but "is there a stimulus for it to work on." In training: yes (creatine-resistance-training-lean-mass-strength). In a cast: no. In rehabilitation: only once the rehabilitation is actually loading the muscle.

⚠ Discount this half of the review harder than the other half. These positive findings run with the senior author's disclosed thirty-year supplement-industry funding and creatine-manufacturer advisory role, and they are drawn from small trials selected without a systematic search (creatine-disuse-review-reports-the-null-that-runs-against-its-own-disclosure). Populations are clinical — knee OA, Parkinson's, myositis — not healthy adults. No dose is recommended here; those listed are the ones the cited trials used (shared/safety.md).
