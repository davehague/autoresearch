Both arms of the intra-workout protein trial were already above the daily plateau — which is what turns an underpowered null into a confirmation of the dose–response curve

The trial that found no benefit from drinking protein during resistance training reported the number that makes its null readable: total protein intake. In week 8, measured by four-day weighed food record, the arm receiving intra-workout protein was eating 1.65 g/kg/day (IQR 0.46) and the arm receiving carbohydrate alone was eating 1.81 g/kg/day (IQR 0.40).

Read that again in the direction that matters: the group with no protein in its workout drink ate more protein overall. Neither difference was significant, and the carbohydrate-only arm also tended to higher total energy intake and higher total energy expenditure. Per workout — counting the shared post-exercise beverage both arms received — the protein arm took in 32.9 g and the carbohydrate arm 19.4 g, so the intended contrast in protein at the session was real; it simply did not translate into a difference in the daily total.

Why this single table entry decides how the trial should be cited. protein-target holds consolidating on a dose–response claim: the muscle benefit of protein added to resistance training plateaus around 1.62 g/kg/day, established by Morton 2018's meta-regression across 49 randomised trials (protein-supplementation-augments-resistance-training). Both of these arms were on the flat part of that curve. So the correct sentence is not "an 8-week randomised trial failed to detect a benefit of intra-workout protein" — it is "an 8-week randomised trial found no benefit from redistributing protein in people who had already exceeded the intake above which the map predicts no further benefit." The first sentence is a weak null. The second is a confirmation of the curve, delivered at the resolution of a muscle biopsy.

⚑ And it is the fix for a defect the vault flagged two days ago on the previous timing source. Ikeda 2026's 7-RCT meta-analysis of morning protein was carried at a weakened reading precisely because its comparator was "morning protein versus NO morning protein" rather than the same daily total taken later — meaning timing was confounded with total intake, and the record did not say whether totals were equalised (morning-protein-comparator-is-no-protein-not-redistribution). This trial does not have that hole. Both arms were fed a pre-exercise meal, both received an identical post-exercise protein drink, intake was weighed rather than assumed, and the totals are printed. It is the first timing source in this vault where the reader can verify that the daily total was not the thing being varied.

⚠ The symmetric cost, stated because it is the real limit on how far this travels. A trial in which both arms exceed the target cannot tell you what happens to someone whose intake is inadequate — which is the only condition where the timing of protein is plausibly consequential, and which the authors name as their own open question: "particularly when protein intake is suboptimal or training is performed in the fasted state." The strength of this design in one direction is exactly its blindness in the other. Nothing here licenses the claim that intra-workout protein is useless for a person under-eating protein; the claim is that it adds nothing to a person who is not.

A general lesson the vault should carry, because it recurs. In nutrition trials, the meaningful contrast is almost never the one named in the title. A "timing" trial in which both arms hit the target is a test of redistribution above a plateau. A "supplement" trial in which the control arm also gets the supplement's active nutrient from food is a test of a marginal increment. The number that tells you which trial you are reading is the measured total intake in each arm — and most papers in this corpus do not report it. This one does, which is why a 17-person study earns a place on a consolidating map.

Graded single-study. This is a reading of one trial's diet table, not an independent finding, and it bounds a claim rather than making one.
