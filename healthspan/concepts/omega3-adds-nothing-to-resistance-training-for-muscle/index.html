Adding omega-3 to resistance training does not build more muscle or strength — a clean null

A PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis (9 RCTs, n=286, healthy older adults ≥65) tested whether adding omega-3 PUFAs to resistance training beats RT + placebo (or RT alone). It does not: muscle mass was a clean null — non-significant (p = 0.30), I² = 0%, no publication bias (Egger p > 0.05, symmetric funnel), robust to excluding design outliers — and neuromuscular function and strength were null too (lower-body SMD −0.05, p = 0.72 after removing a no-placebo outlier; upper-body strength SMD 0.09, p = 0.52). No dose-response, no sex or duration moderation.

This is a well-behaved null, not an underpowered shrug. Zero heterogeneity and no publication bias make "no measurable extra muscle from adding fish oil to lifting" a genuine (if modest-base) statement rather than an absence of evidence. It closes the omega-3 muscle-thread gap the map's July log opened, toward null.
