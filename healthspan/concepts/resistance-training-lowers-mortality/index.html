Resistance training lowers mortality, with an early dose-response plateau (large prospective cohort)

Across three prospective cohorts (147,374 adults, up to 30 years, 35,798 deaths), ~90–119 minutes of resistance training per week was associated with 13% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.87, 0.81–0.95), 19% lower cardiovascular mortality (HR 0.81, 0.67–0.97), and 27% lower neurological-disease mortality (HR 0.73, 0.58–0.92) — with no additional benefit above ~120 min/week. Cancer mortality fell only at low doses (1–29 min/week HR 0.91; 30–59 min HR 0.88).

Why this matters for the vault. It puts a direct mortality number on the resistance-training base that the vault previously touched only indirectly — via creatine-monohydrate and protein-target, both of which add strength/lean mass on top of a training stimulus rather than substituting for it. The early plateau (~90–120 min/week) fits GOAL.md's least-work philosophy: a modest, sustainable dose captures most of the benefit, so "lift more, forever" is not required.

Bounded honestly: observational. Resistance-training volume is self-reported and can't be randomized, so residual confounding (the "healthy exerciser") is possible despite adjustment, and the cohorts are narrow (US health professionals). Per the evidence model's tier gating, this opens the map at emerging — strong signal, observational ceiling, the same honest gate as sleep-regularity.
