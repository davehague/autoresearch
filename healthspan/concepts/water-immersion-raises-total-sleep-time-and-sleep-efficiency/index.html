Water immersion raises total sleep time and sleep efficiency, on two marginal p-values and no reported intervals

Ten randomised trials of water immersion thermotherapy in healthy adults pooled two positive results: total sleep time (SMD 0.63, p = 0.0438) and sleep efficiency — the share of time in bed actually spent asleep — (SMD 0.64, p = 0.0419). Sleep-onset latency did not move (p = 0.0877).

This is the first randomised, pooled evidence on sleep quantity that sauna-heat holds. The map's previous sleep claim measured subjective quality in young male athletes from an unreliable source; nothing had measured minutes.

Two things bound it, and both are load-bearing. ⚠ Neither estimate carries a confidence interval — the abstract reports standardised mean differences and bare p-values only, so it is not possible to say what magnitude of effect these results rule out (water-immersion-sleep-estimates-are-reported-without-confidence-intervals). ⚠ And both positives sit four thousandths inside 0.05, among eleven reported comparisons with no visible correction, nine of which were null.

The thermal dose is unreadable. The review states no temperature, no duration, no immersion depth and no timing before bed, and its full text is paywalled. No protocol is derived from this and none may be — shared/safety.md forbids synthesising one.
