A dose-dependent vitamin D supplementation/mortality association that doesn't track skin cancer and survives a causal-inference check — still not a randomized trial

Modeling vitamin D supplementation longitudinally from pharmacy-dispensed purchases (calibrated to predicted serum 25(OH)D change), supplementation sufficient to raise levels by more than 10 ng/mL was associated with mortality HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.68–0.76), dose-dependently, in the same two-cohort EHR analysis that found severe deficiency tracks with higher mortality (severe-vitamin-d-deficiency-tracks-higher-mortality-in-two-independent-ehr-cohorts). Two design features argue against the most obvious confounders: no corresponding association with skin malignancy (a negative control that would be expected to move if more sun exposure, not the supplement, were driving the result), and the association was consistent under inverse-probability-weighted marginal structural Cox models, a causal-inference technique built to address time-varying confounding by indication.

What this does not resolve: baseline healthy-adherer bias. People who purchase and take vitamin D supplements differ systematically from people who do not — more health-engaged, more likely to see a doctor regularly — independent of any time-varying confounding the marginal structural model addresses. This is the same "healthy-adherer" pattern this vault has discounted other supplement-mortality observational claims for, and the retrievable abstract does not disclose the baseline confounder-adjustment list.

Filed as open-question, not banked. This sharpens vitamin-d's deficiency-correction thread with the largest observational base the map holds, but it is still an observational association, not the randomized deficiency-selected trial the map's gap has been waiting for since Wang 2026's target-trial emulation. Status unchanged.
