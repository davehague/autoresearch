Acid suppression may move aspirin's bleeding downstream rather than remove it — and the evidence for that is not randomised

The same synthesis that shows a proton pump inhibitor cuts aspirin-associated UPPER gastrointestinal bleeding by about 70% on randomised evidence (ppi-substantially-reduces-upper-gi-bleeding-in-aspirin-users) also reports that PPI use was associated with roughly a threefold increase in LOWER gastrointestinal bleeding: RR 2.85 (95% CI 1.85–4.38).

That number comes from three non-randomised studies. That is the whole story, and it cuts both ways.

Why the association is probably not what it looks like. People are prescribed a proton pump inhibitor because a clinician judged them at elevated gastrointestinal risk — prior ulcer, older age, anticoagulant or steroid co-medication, Helicobacter history. The exposure is a marker for the outcome's own risk factors. shared/study-appraisal.md's observational lens set fires on this directly: there is no true non-user comparator, the allocation mechanism is clinical judgement about bleeding risk, and confounding by indication is the single most parsimonious explanation available. The one randomised contrast in the paper — PPI against H₂-receptor antagonists, 2 RCTs — returns RR 1.88 with an interval from 0.30 to 11.82, which excludes nothing in either direction.

Why it is nevertheless flagged rather than dismissed. shared/safety.md is explicit: "Interaction evidence is asymmetric: a missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious flag. Raise the flag on any credible signal (case report / mechanistic is enough to flag) and tier it in the note." Three observational studies plus a named, pre-existing mechanism — acid suppression altering the small-bowel microbiome, the "PPI-associated enteropathy" literature — clear that bar comfortably. The flag is raised in the cautious direction and the grade is set honestly at single-study.

The evidence asymmetry is the finding, and it must always travel with the benefit. One drug, one co-medication, two gastrointestinal sites, two completely different tiers of evidence: a randomised 70% reduction upstream, an unrandomised threefold association downstream. Citing either number alone misrepresents the trade. The source's own authors get this right — they call the lower-GI evidence "from non-RCTs alone" and the harm "potential" — and the vault adopts their framing rather than sharpening it.

⚠ And there are no absolute numbers on either side. shared/study-appraisal.md's first universal lens fires cleanly: RR 0.30 on upper-GI bleeding and RR 2.85 on lower-GI bleeding cannot be weighed against each other without baseline event rates, and the paper reports none. Whether this is a good trade, a bad trade, or a rounding error depends entirely on numbers that are not published. The vault records a trade of unknown sign.

What would resolve it. A randomised trial of PPI versus no-PPI in aspirin users with lower GI bleeding as a pre-specified endpoint, reporting absolute event rates. Failing that, any analysis that separates acid-suppression exposure from the clinical reasons it was prescribed — a new-user active-comparator design, or an instrument. Three retrospective comparisons of PPI users against non-users cannot do it, and no number of additional ones would.
