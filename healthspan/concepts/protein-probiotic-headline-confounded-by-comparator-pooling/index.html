When a meta-analysis pools "vs placebo" with "vs the active ingredient," its headline answers no question at all

Chang 2026 advertises Hedges' g = 0.45 (0.21–0.67) for protein-plus-probiotic supplementation on physical performance. That number is built by pooling two comparisons that mean different things:

  • protein+probiotic vs placebo — measures the combination against nothing, which is mostly the protein.
  • protein+probiotic vs protein alone — measures what the probiotic adds, which is the only thing anyone buying such a product is paying for.

Averaging them produces a number that describes neither. And because this vault already holds protein's effect as consolidating from a 49-RCT meta-analysis (protein-supplementation-augments-resistance-training), the pooled headline is substantially a restatement of something already known, sold as something new.

The paper's own Control model separates them, and the separation is devastating — nine of ten contrasts null (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance).

And there is a second tell, quieter and worse. Look at the placebo column alone: cardiorespiratory endurance 0.18 (−0.40 to 0.74), functional performance 0.10 (−0.47 to 0.66), muscle strength 0.19 (−0.19 to 0.55), muscular endurance 0.38 (−0.29 to 1.10), power 0.08 (−0.34 to 0.50). Every one crosses zero.

This corpus cannot detect protein's effect on strength — an effect established across 49 RCTs and held as consolidating in this vault. A literature that underpowered is not in a position to detect a smaller, novel increment layered on top of it. The failure of the placebo column is the proof that the lone positive in the protein-alone column should not be believed. That inference — using a study's failure on a known effect to discipline its claim about an unknown one — is the most useful thing this ingest teaches, and the vault should reach for it again.

Why this lands mixed and not unreliable. Nothing is hidden. The authors pre-registered (PROSPERO CRD420251165192), built the correct analysis, and printed every null in it. The failure is one of emphasis: the abstract advertises "signals of benefit… for muscle strength" when the added-value estimate for muscle strength is g = 0.26 (−0.18 to 0.66), null, and the conclusion never carries the Control model's answer up to where a reader will see it. The full text is honest; the abstract is not, and the abstract is what gets read.

Pair it with the same week's other sophisticated-model catch. bayesian-certainty-cannot-repair-biased-inputs records Kong 2026 producing a 99.97% posterior probability over an evidence base its own GRADE table rates very low. Both papers use advanced Bayesian machinery. Both are honest in full text. Both generate headline numbers that will be quoted without their caveats. The vault's three earlier laundering catches this week (Shen, Wu, Jiao) all required an author to oversell. These two do not — and the inflation happens anyway. That is a distinct and harder failure mode, and naming it is the point of this note.
