The "GLP-1 spares muscle" reassurance rests on 2-3 comparative studies, not the dozens the field's size implies

A pre-registered, physiology-first meta-analysis built separate pools for adiposity and muscle-quantity change during GLP-1-based therapy — and found only 3 comparative studies feeding the adiposity pool and 2 feeding the muscle-quantity pool, both with confidence intervals crossing the null and GRADE certainty rated low to very low. The field has 19 relevant studies in total; almost all of them are single-arm (no comparator), which this review deliberately excludes from its primary pooled estimates.

This pushes back on confidence, not direction. glp-1's existing body-composition syntheses (Laverde 2026, Sawicka-Gutaj 2026) both report fat mass loss predominating over lean mass loss, in the same direction this source's single-arm data also shows. What this source adds is a count: when restricted to genuinely comparative evidence, the number of studies backing the "preferential fat loss" framing is far smaller than either prior synthesis's citation count suggested, and the comparative estimates themselves are imprecise enough to be compatible with no real differential at all.

Why suggestive, not stronger. Appraised mixed — pre-registered and methodologically careful (the Hartung-Knapp CI adjustment specifically avoids overstating precision from few studies), but the primary estimates are built on 2-3 studies each and the pooled abstract gives no effect-size magnitude, only direction and certainty grade.

This narrows the confidence behind glp1-fat-mass-loss-predominates-over-lean-mass-across-time-points and glp1-pooled-smd-lean-mass-loss-across-sixty-studies: both stand, but the comparative evidence under the first is thinner than its citation count implied, and the second's 28-study lean-mass pool (Beaudart 2026) draws mostly on single-arm-derived heterogeneity rather than placebo-controlled comparison — a back-edge is recorded on each.
