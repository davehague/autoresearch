SUCRA is a probability of ranking well, not a measure of benefit — and here the two came apart completely

SUCRA ("surface under the cumulative ranking curve") turns a network meta-analysis into a league table. It answers how likely is this treatment to be near the top? — not how much does it help? An intervention with a tiny, precisely estimated effect can outrank one with a large, uncertain effect. And SUCRA has no way to return "we don't know": it will always produce a confident-looking ordering, including from evidence that supports none.

Two demonstrations in a single paper:

One — the ranking inverts its own effect sizes. On WOMAC total, celecoxib ranks first (91.2%) on MD −0.97 (−5.41 to 3.46) — an interval containing zero and extending into worse-than-placebo. Glucosamine/chondroitin ranks second (75.6%) on MD −3.14 (−5.75 to −0.53) — three times the effect, and the only estimate in that outcome whose interval clears zero. A reader of the league table learns the reverse of what the estimates say.

Two — a ranking built on an explicit null. The paper states that the network meta-analysis "did not reveal any statistically significant differences between interventions for the risk of adverse events," then publishes a safety league table running from probiotics (88.5%) to celecoxib (15.6%). The abstract promotes this to "probiotics and metformin had the most favorable safety profiles." The ranking exists; the difference it ranks does not.

Compounding it, the results text asserts significance its own tables refute — "compared with placebo, all active interventions demonstrated statistically significant improvements" on WOMAC total, where three of four printed intervals cross zero.

⚑ The operating rule this establishes for the vault. A SUCRA number is never evidence on its own. Read the effect estimate and its interval first; treat the ranking as a summary of that, and discard it when it disagrees. Where a paper reports only SUCRA and not the estimates, the ranking is unverifiable and should be recorded as such.
