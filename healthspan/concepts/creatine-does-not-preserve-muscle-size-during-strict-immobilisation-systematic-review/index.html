Creatine does not stop muscle shrinking while a limb is immobilised

Across the two included trials that measured muscle size directly by imaging (CT or NMR), creatine supplementation did not preserve quadriceps cross-sectional area during 1–2 weeks of experimental limb immobilisation: reductions were comparable between creatine and placebo (−5.6% vs −5.5%; −11.0% vs −9.4%). Two other trials using BIA/DXA found smaller lean-mass losses with creatine — but the authors of this systematic review flag that difference as possibly a hydration-measurement artefact rather than true tissue preservation, since BIA/DXA are sensitive to fluid shifts in ways direct imaging is not.

Why this null is credible rather than a shrug. Three of this review's five authors disclose ongoing paid relationships with creatine manufacturers (industry-sponsored research, product donations, scientific-advisory and expert-witness roles). A null on disuse-atrophy prevention, reported by authors with that profile of disclosed interest, is the result bias would predict against — per shared/evidence-model.md's bias-watch, credited rather than discounted.

Why suggestive, not strong. No meta-analytic pooling — six small trials narratively synthesised, not combined into a single effect estimate with a confidence interval. Population is young and healthy (mean ages 15–24), not the older-adult or clinical disuse populations the question matters most for. Appraised mixed.

Where this sits on the map. This replicates, at systematic-review scale, the same null a narrower narrative review and a small pilot RCT already carried on creatine-monohydrate — see creatine-does-not-prevent-atrophy-during-strict-immobilisation (Stout 2026) and creatine-may-accelerate-pain-reduction-and-jump-recovery-during-patellar-tendinopathy-rehab (Sánchez-Gómez 2026). No dose is recommended (shared/safety.md); the map's standing ~5 g/day figure is unchanged.
