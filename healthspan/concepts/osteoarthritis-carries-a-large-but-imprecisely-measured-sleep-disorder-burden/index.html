People with osteoarthritis carry a large comorbid sleep-disorder burden, but the exact rate is very uncertain

A PROSPERO-registered prevalence meta-analysis (18 observational studies, n=202,847) of people with knee, hip, or hand osteoarthritis found insomnia in roughly 42% (95% CI 34–51%), obstructive sleep apnea in roughly 37% (95% CI 6–67%), and restless legs syndrome in roughly 35% (95% CI 10–61%). No study reported narcolepsy. Certainty was graded "very low" throughout, driven by diagnostic-criteria heterogeneity and mixed study designs across the pooled literature.

Why this matters for the vault. This is a comorbidity-burden finding, not an interventional one — it does not test whether treating osteoarthritis improves sleep, or whether treating a sleep disorder changes OA outcomes. Filed primarily on exercise-for-osteoarthritis because of its relevance to GOAL.md's knee-recovery/pickleball context: someone managing a painful joint is also disproportionately likely to be contending with a sleep disorder. Cross-linked to sleep-duration and cbt-insomnia for the sleep-disorder side of the comorbidity.

Bounded honestly. The sleep-apnea and RLS intervals are wide enough (spanning 50+ percentage points) that the point estimates should be read as "common" rather than as precise rates. The insomnia estimate, on the largest and tightest interval, is the most trustworthy of the three.

The practical shape of the claim. Not a reason to start or avoid anything — a reason to read a future sleep-quality complaint or a future joint-pain complaint as potentially connected, worth naming to a clinician rather than treating as independent. No treatment or dose implied.
