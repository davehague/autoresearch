Strength went up, balance went up, walking went up — and how people felt about their lives did not move at all

In the same 14-trial meta-analysis that found benefits on muscle mass, strength, balance, walking and physical function, quality of life was flatly null: SMD 0.08 (−0.19 to 0.35), p = 0.53.

⚑ This is a tight null, not an underpowered one, and the distinction is the whole point. shared/study-appraisal.md names "an underpowered null sold as no effect" a fatal reading error — the failure the vault has caught repeatedly (rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered, rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered). This null does not have that shape. Its interval runs −0.19 to 0.35: it excludes any moderate or large effect in either direction. And it sits in the same paper, on the same trials, as an outcome (physical function) whose prediction interval spans 6.45 SD. The QoL estimate is the most precisely-bounded number in the analysis, and what it bounds is nothing.

⚠ Why it pushes back rather than merely reporting. The implicit argument running through the exercise-for-sarcopenia literature is that moving the surrogates — grip, mass, gait, function — moves the thing people care about. Here every surrogate moved and the thing people care about did not. That is a live tension the vault should carry rather than bury, and it converges with tonight's other sarcopenia synthesis from an independent group: no-exercise-intervention-beat-control-for-gait-speed-in-sarcopenia, where the most functional outcome was the one nothing beat.

Three readings remain open, and 14 trials cannot separate them:

  1. The gains are too small to feel. A muscle-mass MD of 0.25 and a balance SMD of 0.31 may be real and beneath the threshold of noticing.
  2. QoL instruments are too blunt. Generic quality-of-life questionnaires are dominated by mood, pain, social circumstance and comorbidity; a strength change may be invisible inside them.
  3. The gains genuinely do not translate. The least comfortable reading, and not excluded.

⚠ Reading (2) deserves the caution the vault normally applies in the other direction. "The instrument was too blunt" is the standard rescue for an inconvenient null, and it is unfalsifiable unless someone reports which instruments were used and pre-specifies a responsive one. The abstract does not. Recorded as a genuine null with three live explanations, none of them adopted.
