For lower-limb strength, load is the variable: ≥70% 1RM beats lighter training (18 RCTs)

A PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis of 18 randomized trials (n=1,283, adults ≥50) compared high-intensity resistance training (≥70% of one-rep max) against low-to-moderate intensity (<70% 1RM). High intensity won on leg press, SMD 0.95 (95% CI 0.48–1.43) and leg extension, SMD 0.63 (0.09–1.17).

Why this matters for the vault. resistance-training was coined on a mortality cohort (Zhang 2026) that could say how many minutes per week — ~90–119, plateauing above ~120 — but nothing at all about how heavy. This is the first vault source to supply the load axis, and it is RCT-tier. Strength, not training volume, is what carries independence, fall resistance, and the ability to keep playing pickleball at sixty; and the vault's muscle amplifiers (creatine-resistance-training-lean-mass-strength, protein-supplementation-augments-resistance-training) only work on a stimulus that is actually sufficient.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive). The source's reliability verdict is mixed (shared/study-appraisal.md), capping strength. Eighteen trials share 1,283 participants, split across two regimens and four outcomes; the leg-extension interval (0.09–1.17) barely clears null; and blinding participants to how heavy their weights are is impossible, so performance bias is structural in every constituent trial. The population is adults ≥50 broadly — healthy, sarcopenic, and osteoporotic cohorts pooled — not middle-aged adults, and emphatically not adults in orthopaedic recovery. The outcome is a strength surrogate, not a hard endpoint: it does not upgrade the map's emerging mortality claim, which is gated by an observational ceiling this paper does not touch.

Why safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] — read this before adding plates. The same meta-analysis reports fall incidence RR 2.68 (95% CI 0.65–11.11) and adverse events RR 2.42 (0.66–8.88) for high-intensity training, and concludes these suggest "similar safety profiles." They suggest nothing — both intervals are consistent with substantial harm, and both point estimates sit above 2 (rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered). GOAL.md names knee/joint-safe as a hard constraint and records a March 2026 patellar contusion. Per shared/safety.md — a missed safety signal is worse than an over-cautious flag — this benefit carries the flag, which caps any regimen expression of it at "proposed — review with your doctor / physio." The evidence says heavy lifting builds more strength. It does not say heavy lifting is safe for this knee, and this study cannot say so.
