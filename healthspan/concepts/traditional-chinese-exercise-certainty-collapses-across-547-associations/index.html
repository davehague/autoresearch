Ninety-six percent of what this literature has published does not reach high certainty — and the paper says so itself

An umbrella review sat one level above the primary literature and graded every published association it could find on traditional Chinese exercise for musculoskeletal disorders: 547 unique outcome associations, from 92 meta-analyses, built on 1,744 randomised trials.

⚑ This is the finding, and it is not a criticism of the paper — it is what the paper is for. An umbrella review's characteristic job is triage: taking a large, enthusiastically-published field and sorting which of its claims survive scrutiny. Twenty-three high-certainty associations is a real haul — most exercise literatures this vault has ingested produce none at all. What it is not is a general licence. The correct reading is a short list, not a demonstration that these practices broadly work.

⚠ The authors' own conclusion sentence reads the other way, describing the review as consolidating "high-quality evidence demonstrating that TCEs, especially Tai Chi, provide multidimensional benefits… improving function, pain, mental health, sleep and BMD with sustained practice." Every one of those domains does appear among the 23. The sentence is true of the 23 and silent about the other 524, and a reader who saw only the conclusion would carry away a different paper than a reader who saw the numbers.

⚑ The methodological quality of the inputs was high, which makes the certainty collapse more informative rather than less. AMSTAR-2 rated 65 of 92 (70.7%) of the included meta-analyses as high quality. So this is not a case of bad reviews producing uncertain conclusions — well-conducted syntheses of this literature mostly cannot reach high certainty, which points at the underlying trials: small, unblindable, heterogeneous, with self-reported outcomes and weak comparators.

⚠ And the true evidence base may be smaller than 1,744. Ninety-two meta-analyses of one field inevitably pool many of the same primary trials. The standard overlap metric for an umbrella review — the corrected covered area — is not in any retrievable text. "1,744 primary RCTs" is a sum across meta-analyses, not a count of distinct trials.

⚠ Over a third of the pool does not say what it studied. 37.3% of associations came from "mixed/unspecified TCEs." Tai Chi, Baduanjin, Qigong, Yijinjing, Wuqinxi and Daoyin are different practices with different loading, different tempo and different balance demands, and more than a third of this evidence declines to distinguish them.

⚑ Why this note exists as a first-class claim rather than a caveat. This vault has repeatedly recorded that headline synthesis findings survive better than the qualifiers attached to them (frailty-network-ranks-bundles-without-reporting-effect-sizes, sucra-rankings-can-invert-the-effect-sizes-they-are-built-from). The 4.2% is the number a future run should meet first when it reads anything positive about this literature.
