The compound got in, and nothing happened

Dynamic cerebral autoregulation is the brain's shock absorber. Arterial pressure fluctuates constantly; the brain tolerates neither too much flow nor too little; small cerebral vessels adjust their tone to keep flow steady while pressure moves. It is measured with two numbers — gain (how much of a pressure swing gets through to flow; lower is better) and phase (how far ahead of the pressure the vessels respond; larger is better).

The mechanistic case for testing nitrate on it is genuinely good. Metabolic syndrome reduces nitric-oxide availability; nitric oxide relaxes blood vessels; dietary nitrate is reduced in the body to nitrite and then to nitric oxide; impaired autoregulation is documented in mild cognitive impairment and dementia. Every link in that chain is real.

⚑ Four weeks of 6.4 mmol nitrate per day did not move it. Treatment effect on global frontal gain: p = 0.83, partial η² = 0.002 — an effect size indistinguishable from zero. On phase: p = 0.23, η² = 0.07. And no cognitive outcome moved either (all p > 0.11).

⚑ The delivery worked, which is what makes the null informative rather than ambiguous. Plasma nitrate rose by +57 ± 34 µM in the beetroot arm against −5 ± 21 µM in placebo (p<0.001, η² = 0.56). Adherence was high and palatability neutral. Most failed supplement trials cannot distinguish "the compound does nothing" from "the compound never got in." This one can.

⚠ One mechanistic caveat cuts the other way, and it is the authors'. Plasma nitrite — the intermediate through which nitrate is supposed to become nitric oxide — did not change significantly (p = 0.077). Blood was drawn 24 hours after the final dose, and nitrite has a short half-life, so the authors flag their own single-timepoint sampling as a limitation. So delivery to the bloodstream is proven; delivery to the active metabolite is not.

⚠ This is a pilot and it cannot rule anything out. n = 22, no a priori power calculation — the authors say so outright, and their own future-directions calculation says a properly powered trial needs 40 participants. This trial was roughly half-sized for its own effect estimates. Randomisation also failed on two relevant lipid variables (HDL cholesterol p<0.001, triglycerides p=0.049). ⚑ So this is a failure to demonstrate, not a demonstration of failure, and it is recorded as confirming the map's existing position rather than downgrading it — a Status move needs a well-powered null, and this is not one.

Where it sits on the map. beetroot-juice already holds a pattern of small acute surrogate wins and larger nulls: a modest VO₂max gain that beetroot-juice-vo2max-effect-disappears-in-trained-athletes shows vanishes in the trained, a null across sprint, jump and exertion outcomes, and an isokinetic torque signal with nitrate-torque-gain-has-no-emg-or-lactate-signature and no mechanism. ⚑ This adds a cerebrovascular null on a properly isolating control, and it is the first source on this map to test a brain outcome at all.

In one sentence: nitrate reached the bloodstream and the brain's blood-flow buffer did not change, in a trial too small to settle the question but well enough controlled to be worth recording.
