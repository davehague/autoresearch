The quality gradient and the results gradient run in opposite directions

The review's abstract says exercise-based interventions "consistently improved" frailty status, strength and gait speed. Its own per-study risk-of-bias grid rates two of thirty-four trials as low risk across all six domains — and both are nulls. Latham 2003 (n = 243) is recorded in the review's own table as "Negative trial... no functional benefit; ↑ injury risk." Taaffe 1994 (n = 18) is recorded as "No effect... no strength gain... ↑ lean mass only." Every one of the other thirty-two trials — which is to say every positive result — carries "some concerns."

⚑ The review states the correct facts and draws no inference from them. Its text reads "No study was judged to be at high overall risk of bias" and "Overall methodological quality was moderate." Both are true. Neither observes that restricting to the best-graded evidence leaves a literature with no effect in it.

⚠ What this note does and does not claim. It does not claim exercise fails to improve frailty — two low-risk trials, one of them n = 18, cannot establish that, and one of them tested growth hormone rather than exercise. It claims the review's word "consistently" is not supported by the review's own quality grading, and that a synthesis reporting a favourable summary over a corpus whose best-graded members are null owes its readers that restriction analysis.

⚑ Third instance of this shape in this vault, across three unrelated literatures. the-only-low-risk-student-sleep-trial-studied-good-sleepers — the one low-risk sleep trial enrolled people already sleeping fine. integrated-aerobic-resistance-training-survives-the-quality-filter-that-sinks-circuit-training — the blood-pressure leaderboard's winner collapsed once high-risk trials were dropped. Tonight is the version where nobody ran the filter. An appraisal lens for quality-gradient inversion is requested in the source digest and logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
