Sleep efficiency reported in minutes — a units error in the headline result, and the fourth execution tell of its kind

The abstract reports "increased SE (+2.58 min, 95% CI: 2.01–3.16)". Sleep efficiency is time asleep divided by time in bed, expressed as a percentage. It does not have minutes. Whatever quantity was pooled, the label is wrong, and it is wrong in the abstract, in the paper's headline results, past peer review.

On its own this is trivial. As a class of signal it is not, and the vault has now caught it four times.

The rule the vault applies is consistent across all four: when a reported number cannot be the quantity it claims to be, discount the magnitude and keep the direction. A units error is weaker evidence of carelessness than an impossible effect size — mislabelling is cheap, and the underlying analysis may be fine. So this note is graded single-study, the weakest grade available, and it is a caution attached to one outcome, not a verdict on the paper. The paper is otherwise cleanly registered, publicly funded, honestly GRADEd, and reports its own null.

Why record it at all rather than letting it pass. Because execution details are the only evidence a reader outside a field ever gets about how carefully work was done, and because the specific number affected — sleep efficiency — is the one outcome in this paper the vault would otherwise weight most heavily. Sleep efficiency is closer to an objective measure than PSQI, and the vault has spent a month noting that in this literature "every paper's strongest result is the one its design is weakest at measuring." An objective sleep result would be worth something. This one cannot be interpreted, because the vault cannot tell what was measured.

So the practical consequence is narrow and specific: the vault does not carry a sleep-efficiency magnitude from this source. The PSQI result, the small-effect finding, and the category-level conclusion all stand on their own (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold).
