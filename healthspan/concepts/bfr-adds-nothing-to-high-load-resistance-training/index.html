Bolt a BFR cuff onto training you can already do heavy, and nothing happens

Eight studies of high-load (≥60% 1RM) resistance training with blood flow restriction versus conventional high-load resistance training in healthy adults:

  • Muscle strength: g = 0.11 (−0.11 to 0.33)
  • Muscle size: g = 0.08 (−0.18 to 0.34)
  • Certainty: very low — the authors' own rating

Their explanation is mechanically satisfying: adding occlusion to heavy load is not beneficial "likely because of comparable physiologic demands." Heavy load already produces the metabolic stress that BFR exists to manufacture. You cannot stack the same stimulus on itself.

And the vault applies its own lens to a null it would very much like to keep. "Don't bother with the cuff if you can lift heavy" is a convenient conclusion — it simplifies the regimen, removes a purchase, and fits GOAL.md's least-work philosophy exactly. Which is precisely when the underpowered-null rule has to hold. A strength interval running to g = 0.33 is not zero; 0.33 is a small-but-real effect, from eight studies, at the authors' own "very low" certainty. So the recorded claim is "no evidence that BFR adds to heavy load," NOT "evidence that BFR adds nothing to heavy load" — the identical formulation the vault used for beta-alanine stacked on creatine (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy), and for the same reason.

This is the third night running the lens has been aimed at a null the vault wanted to bank — after Wu's four adaptation nulls and, tonight, DHA's cognitive null (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit). The rule is worthless if it only fires on papers you dislike.

What the null nonetheless establishes, and it is a lot. Combined with the vault's two other BFR syntheses, it identifies what BFR is actually for: not amplifying training you can already do, but substituting for load you cannot tolerate (bfr-value-is-load-tolerance-not-load-addition). The cuff is a workaround, not an amplifier — and if David's knee heals to where heavy load is safe again, this paper says the simplest possible answer wins: just lift.
