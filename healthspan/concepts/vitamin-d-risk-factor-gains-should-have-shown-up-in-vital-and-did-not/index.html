If vitamin D really lowered blood pressure and LDL by these amounts, VITAL would have seen it — and VITAL was flat

Two randomised results on the same supplement have to be held together, and they do not fit.

Result one. A meta-analysis of 45 randomised placebo-controlled trials finds vitamin D lowering systolic blood pressure by 2.79 mmHg (95% CI −4.65 to −0.94) and LDL cholesterol by 0.136 mmol/L (−0.215 to −0.056) (vitamin-d-small-improvements-in-cardiometabolic-risk-markers).

Result two. VITAL randomised 25,871 people to vitamin D₃ 2,000 IU/day or placebo, double-blind, and found major cardiovascular events flat — hazard ratio 0.97 — along with cancer at 0.96 (vitamin-d-no-cancer-cvd-benefit) and fractures at roughly 0.98–1.01 (vitamin-d-no-fracture-benefit).

Why these are in tension rather than merely unrelated. Blood pressure and LDL are not arbitrary numbers; they are the two best-established modifiable inputs to cardiovascular risk in medicine. Interventions that move them by these amounts and sustain it — thiazides, statins at low dose, sodium reduction at population scale — are interventions whose event reductions have been repeatedly demonstrated in trials smaller than VITAL. A trial of 25,871 people over years is not a small instrument. If vitamin D were durably shifting both of those inputs in the direction reported, the event count is where it would show, and it did not.

Three explanations survive, and the vault records all three rather than choosing.

One — the marker changes do not persist. Most trials in a risk-factor meta-analysis are short. A blood-pressure or lipid change measured at 8, 12 or 26 weeks may regress; VITAL ran for years. A transient shift in a marker produces no events.

Two — the marker changes are real and do not translate. This is the possibility that matters most to this vault, because it is the same finding stated in a third literature. nad-precursors holds a trial in which blood NAD⁺ doubled and nothing clinical moved (nicotinamide-riboside-raises-nad-without-cognitive-benefit); omega-3 holds a 24-month trial in which DHA verifiably entered cerebrospinal fluid with no cognitive or structural benefit (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit); biological-age-clocks holds the general rule that a marker predicting an outcome is not the same as moving the marker moving the outcome (dunedinpace-strongest-mortality-predictor-among-consensus-biomarkers). Vitamin D would be the fourth molecule in that pattern, and the one with the largest hard-endpoint trial behind the null.

Three — the marker effects are artefacts. Four significant results out of at least eight to ten endpoints, with no recoverable multiple-comparison correction, subgroup directions that contradict each other across outcomes on a shared pathway, and — because the source's full text is not yet published — risk of bias, heterogeneity and multiplicity handling all UNVERIFIED. This is the least charitable reading and it cannot be excluded.

⚑ What the vault does with an unresolved tension: it applies the hierarchy rather than splitting the difference. shared/evidence-model.md sets Status by the strongest evidence, not by vote: "One well-powered meta-analysis-rct null result outweighs three cohort benefit signals." Here both sides are randomised — so the tiebreaker is the endpoint, and a hard outcome outranks a surrogate. The map's contested Status holds, set by the event data, and this source is recorded as the sixth outcome family behaving as the map's stated pattern predicts rather than as a challenge to it.

The consistency check that makes this principled. vitamin-d-preserves-telomere-length is a flattering surrogate that came from VITAL itself — the same trial, the same participants, hard endpoints null and a biomarker moving — and the vault declined to let it rehabilitate the map. Declining a flattering surrogate from an unrelated meta-analysis is the same decision applied twice. A ledger that discounted the telomere result and banked this one would not be reasoning; it would be picking.

Graded single-study. This is a reconciliation between two sources the vault already holds, constraining how the newer one is read. It establishes no new fact and moves nothing on its own — which is exactly what a note like this should do.

What would resolve it. A trial that measures both: risk markers and events, in the same participants, over a horizon long enough for events to accrue. VITAL was that trial for events. Nobody has run the version that also tracks whether the markers moved and stayed moved.
