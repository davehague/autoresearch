Air pollution is the most consistent cognitive-harm signal in the urban-environment literature — and the vault refuses to coin a map for it, on narrower grounds than it refused greenspace

The signal. Fine particulate matter — PM2.5, airborne particles under 2.5 micrometres across, small enough to cross from lung into bloodstream — was significant on 10 of 11 meta-analysed outcomes in an umbrella review of 54 systematic reviews, the most consistent exposure in the entire set. Against dementia: HR 1.225–1.592, graded highly probable. Against cognitive impairment: RR 1.089–1.220, also highly probable. Nitrogen dioxide, comparing the most-exposed third of people with the least, reached the top convincing tier at RR 1.029–1.069. Traffic noise and cognitive impairment came in at OR 1.194–1.641. Air pollution also attracted the highest-quality reviews in the umbrella — the domain with the most rigour behind it, not just the most papers.

⚑ Flagged as a candidate intervention and NOT adopted — the vault's tenth candidate refusal, after whole-body vibration, beta-alanine, probiotics, collagen (twice), hypoxic training, cocoa flavanols, green exercise and residential greenspace. This one came closest to being accepted, and the reasons it did are worth stating before the reasons it was still refused.

Where it beats the greenspace refusal of 2026-07-30, on two of that refusal's four grounds. The outcome here is dementia — a hard clinical endpoint, not the epigenetic-clock surrogate this vault has already graded unflatteringly (dunedinpace-strongest-mortality-predictor-among-consensus-biomarkers, most-consensus-aging-biomarkers-did-not-predict-mortality). And the signal does not collapse where the evidence is strongest — greenspace failed in the largest pooled analysis in its literature (greenspace-epigenetic-age-signal-fails-in-the-largest-pooled-cohort), while PM2.5 is most consistent in the best-evidenced corner of this one.

Why the vault still refuses, on the two grounds that survive and one that is new.

There is no intervention. Every study measures modelled ambient concentration at a residential address. Nobody was assigned to breathe anything, nothing was administered, and no trial exists. This atlas grades levers a person can pull; an exposure map of a city is not one.

Exposure is inseparable from socioeconomic position by construction. The poorer you are, the closer to the arterial road you live — and socioeconomic position independently predicts dementia through education, occupation, healthcare access and every mediator in between. This is the same objection that killed the greenspace map (residential-greenness-is-confounded-with-socioeconomic-position-by-construction), and it is not weakened by the outcome being harder. A confounded exposure is confounded whatever it is regressed against.

And the effect sizes on the better-measured outcomes are inside confounding range. NO₂ at 1.03–1.07 and PM2.5 cognitive function at 1.04–1.19 are the low-single-digit to low-double-digit percentage differences that shared/study-appraisal.md describes as ones "anything unaccounted-for can distort." Only the PM2.5 dementia estimate (1.23–1.59) is large enough to argue past that, and it is the one resting on the most heterogeneous pooling. The review's own diagnostics found small-study effects in 19 of 34 significant outcomes and excess significance bias in 7 of 34.

What would close it, stated so a future run can recognise the right citation. Any design that separates air quality from socioeconomic position: a regulatory difference-in-differences (a clean-air rule that changed one city and not its neighbour), a within-person change on moving, a plant-closure natural experiment, or — best of all — a randomised indoor air-filtration trial with a cognitive endpoint, which is entirely feasible and would be an actual intervention. The 54 reviews synthesised here contain none of these.

⚑ One thing worth recording about the refusal itself. Unlike whole-body vibration (a machine to buy) or the supplement candidates, nothing here is being sold, and GOAL.md's cost tiebreaker never came into play. This is the second refusal in two nights decided purely on evidence and actionability — which means the vault's candidate gate has stopped functioning as a price filter and is now doing the harder job it was designed for.
