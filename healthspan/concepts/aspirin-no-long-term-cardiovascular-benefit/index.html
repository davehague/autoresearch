Nine years after randomisation, aspirin had prevented no cardiovascular events

Across ASPREE's combined randomised and post-trial follow-up — roughly nine years in total — major adverse cardiovascular events were no lower in the group randomised to 100 mg/day aspirin: HR 1.04 (95% CI 0.94–1.15), with 741 events (10.0 per 1,000 person-years) against 721 (9.6).

This is the number that matters for the primary-prevention question, and it is a well-populated one. 19,114 randomised people and over 1,400 hard cardiovascular events, in a cohort recruited specifically because they had not had a cardiovascular event. The interval — 0.94 to 1.15 — excludes any benefit worth taking a daily drug for.

It also answers the natural objection to the shorter trial. A common defence of a null primary-prevention result is that the follow-up was too short for a slow-acting preventive to show itself. Extending observation by a further median 4.3 years does not rescue the effect; if anything the point estimate drifts the wrong way.

Why suggestive rather than strong, given the design. The combined-period estimate spans a randomised, blinded phase and a non-randomised extension in which both groups took similar amounts of aspirin, and the source is appraised mixed on that basis. The randomised phase alone would carry more weight, but its MACE estimate was not recoverable in an internally consistent form from the retrieved text and is therefore not recorded anywhere in this vault. What is graded here is the combined-period result the paper reports directly.

It confirms rather than extends. The same trial's healthspan analysis already returned HR 0.98 (0.92–1.05) on a composite of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, dementia, cancer and death (aspirin-no-effect-on-healthspan-loss-older-adults). This is the cardiovascular component of that answer, measured over a longer horizon, agreeing with it.

And it is the benefit column against which a real harm now sits. Major haemorrhage in the same trial: HR 1.24 (1.10–1.39) (aspirin-increases-major-haemorrhage-older-adults).

Not about secondary prevention. People who have already had a cardiovascular event are a different population with a different, guideline-endorsed indication, and that literature is not ingested in this vault. No dose is recommended (shared/safety.md); aspirin stays clinician-side.
