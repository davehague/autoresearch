Benefit starts at about 500 MET-minutes a week and stops growing around 1,300 — which is the public-health guideline, rediscovered from blood sugar by people who were not looking for it

A MET-minute is one minute of activity multiplied by how many times resting metabolic rate it demands. A brisk walk is roughly 3.5 METs, so thirty minutes of it costs about 105 MET-minutes. The metric exists to put running, lifting and tai chi on one axis so a dose-response curve can be fitted across modalities.

This network fitted one, and it has a shape rather than a slope:

  • HbA₁c (average blood sugar over ~3 months): nothing until about 520 MET-minutes a week, then −0.39% at 600, −0.69% at 1,200, and flattening beyond about 1,400 — the slope past that point falls to 0.012 per additional 100 MET-minutes, which is arithmetically indistinguishable from nothing.
  • Fasting glucose: nothing until about 500 MET-minutes, then −0.58 mmol/L at 600, −0.95 at 1,200, flattening beyond about 1,300.

⚑ The threshold is the interesting part, because of where it lands. 500 MET-minutes a week is approximately what 150 minutes of moderate activity costs — which is the standard public-health physical activity guideline, derived decades ago from mortality and cardiovascular epidemiology. This network arrived at essentially the same number from HbA₁c, in randomised trials, in a diabetes population, without setting out to reproduce anything. Independent rediscovery of a threshold from an unrelated outcome is a much stronger argument for that threshold than either derivation alone.

And the shape confirms what the vault already holds. zone-2-cardio carries aerobic-volume-mortality-dose-response-nonlinear and moderate-activity-75min-week-mortality-minimum from mortality cohorts: benefit appears early, and the curve bends. The same early-onset-and-plateau structure now appears in randomised trials on a metabolic marker. daily-steps holds the third instance — a non-linear inflection around 5,000–7,000 steps a day with ten thousand not required (daily-steps-mortality-dose-response). Three literatures, three outcomes, one shape.

⚑ Why the plateau is the part that matters here. GOAL.md optimises for "least amount of work and least discomfort for the maximum benefit," with adherence as an explicit constraint. A curve that flattens is a direct answer to how much is enough: on this outcome, the marginal return above roughly 1,400 MET-minutes a week is close to zero, and the difference between 600 and 1,200 is about 0.3 percentage points of HbA₁c. The evidence keeps saying the same thing — most of the benefit is bought early, and the rest of the curve is expensive.

Four reasons this is capped suggestive. The dose metric flattens exactly what distinguishes the modalities — the authors say so, noting MET-minutes cannot capture load and set structure in resistance training or peak intensity in interval training, so the curve is most trustworthy for aerobic-type work and least for the two nodes it compresses hardest. Physiological response to the same MET dose varies with fitness, muscle mass and comorbidity in older adults. Only 6 of 34 trials are at low risk of bias, with network certainty rated very low to moderate. And the authors bound their own numbers, calling the thresholds "approximate reference ranges rather than precise individualized prescriptions" — a caution the vault adopts rather than overrides.

Population limit, stated plainly. Every trial enrolled adults with type 2 diabetes at a mean age of 60 or over. The threshold is what transfers; the HbA₁c magnitudes are not a healthy person's numbers.
