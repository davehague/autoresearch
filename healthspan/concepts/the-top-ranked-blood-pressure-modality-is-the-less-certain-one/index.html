The winner of the diastolic leaderboard is the estimate you should trust least — and the authors say so

SUCRA — the surface under the cumulative ranking curve — is the number network meta-analyses use to order treatments. It is a ranking statistic, and it is computed from point estimates without regard to how certain any of them is. This paper is a clean demonstration of what that costs.

⚑ The first-place finisher carries a low-certainty estimate built on 127 people; the runner-up carries a high-certainty one built on more than twice that. Anyone reading the abstract's ranking takes away "high-intensity continuous training is best for diastolic pressure." The paper's own GRADE assessment says that conclusion is not supported at that strength.

⚑ What makes this source unusual is that its authors wrote the warning themselves. In their results, unprompted: SUCRA values "reflect only the relative ordering of interventions and do not establish statistically significant or clinically meaningful differences between them"; "a higher SUCRA ranking does not necessarily indicate greater certainty of evidence"; and — twice, in two sections — a non-significant comparison "should not be interpreted as evidence that [it] is equivalent to SOC." That last is the null-versus-equivalence distinction, stated correctly by authors who had every incentive to skip it.

⚑ The network-wide certainty distribution belongs on this note because it is the honest headline. Of 90 comparisons: 17 high certainty, 4 moderate, 20 low, and 49 very low. More than half the league table this paper produces is not interpretable at all, and the paper prints that fact rather than burying it. Risk of bias is similarly unflattering and similarly published: 4 of 30 trials low risk, 21 some concerns, 5 high risk.

⚑ Why this note exists as its own atom. The vault has declined at least five SUCRA leaderboards for exactly this defect — stretching-blood-pressure-effect-is-larger-than-the-better-evidenced-levers, exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported, the-top-ranked-cognitive-training-arm-cannot-separate-software-from-social-contact among them. This is the first time the vault has ingested a leaderboard whose authors diagnose the defect in their own paper. Recorded as the reference case: the problem is not SUCRA, it is SUCRA reported without certainty. A ranking is a claim about order. A certainty rating is a claim about whether the order is real. They are different, and only one of them belongs in a headline.
