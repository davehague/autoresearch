Eating before the evening session fixes the meal and not the day

Höchsmann 2026 tested the obvious mitigation for the evening-fasted-exercise intake surplus: shorten the fast. Cutting the pre-exercise fast from 12 hours to 6 hours before evening exercise attenuated acute intake at the post-exercise meal — and did not attenuate 24-hour intake. The fully fed conditions went further in the same direction: pre-exercise feeding suppressed fat oxidation but did not reduce energy intake or appetite at all.

The half that gets fixed is the half that is easy to see. An ad libitum test meal immediately after exercise is the measurement most protocols take, and it is the one that responds. The weighed 24-hour record — the measurement that corresponds to anything a person cares about — does not. A study that had stopped at the test meal would have reported a working mitigation.

This is what makes the finding useful rather than merely cautionary. It closes off the intuitive fix. If the problem were simply "you exercised hungry," feeding beforehand should solve it; feeding beforehand suppressed the metabolic signature and left the behaviour untouched. The authors read this as a dissociation between metabolic and behavioural regulation and "the primacy of timing" — and the vault records that reading as the paper's, held at the same suggestive grade as everything else drawn from it.

Read at its true strength. mixed, capped suggestive. The fed conditions rest on a subsample of 17. There is no non-exercise control arm, so how much of any of this is exercise rather than evening remains undetermined (see evening-fasted-exercise-followed-by-higher-24h-energy-intake). One acute bout, one modality, one day, healthy young adults, unblindable self-reported appetite. And the 6h-vs-12h contrast is a within-evening comparison — nothing here says a 6-hour fast before evening exercise matches morning exercise on 24-hour intake; the abstract reports only that it did not restore it.

Read symmetrically. These are attenuation failures, not demonstrated absences: no power analysis for the 24-hour comparison is recoverable (paywalled, UNVERIFIED), and n=17 for the fed arm is small. The honest statement is the obvious mitigation was tested and did not work on the outcome that matters, not nothing can help.

For David: the practical consequence is that "just have a snack first" is not a fix supported by this evidence. If the aim is to avoid a large evening intake, the lever this trial supports is moving the session earlier, not eating before the late one. Offered as an observation at suggestive strength — no eating window, meal timing or dose is prescribed here per shared/safety.md.
