GLP-1 agonists cost ~1.7 kg of lean mass — and semaglutide ~5.4 kg (7 RCTs)

Pooling 7 placebo-controlled RCTs (821 patients with obesity) on GLP-1 receptor agonists at obesity doses:

  • Absolute lean mass: −1.74 kg (95% CI −3.04 to −0.45; p<0.00001; I²=98%)
  • Relative lean mass: −3.06% (−5.10 to −1.02; p<0.00001; I²=98%)
  • Semaglutide subgroup: −5.44 kg (−7.07 to −3.81; p<0.00001) — the largest absolute loss of any agent

Why this matters for the vault. glp-1 has carried "substantial lean-mass loss" as an explicitly un-numbered claim — flagged as an open gap with a standing instruction not to fabricate a magnitude. This is the first randomized, placebo-controlled source to supply one. The number is what the map assumed, and the semaglutide figure is worse than the pooled average.

The interaction it grades (safety_flags: [interaction]). Lean mass is exactly the tissue resistance-training and protein-target exist to build, and GLP-1's appetite suppression can simultaneously crowd out the ~1.6 g/kg/day protein target that protects it. The countermeasure — resistance training plus adequate protein (± creatine-monohydrate) — is not the vault's inference alone; the paper's own authors conclude drug treatment must "be accompanied by nutritional and physical exercise interventions to preserve or improve muscle mass."

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, capped). Only 7 trials / 821 patients, and I²=98% on both absolute outcomes — the pooled point estimates summarize trials that disagree profoundly, so treat −1.74 kg as a direction with a wide, unstable magnitude rather than a precise expectation. The endpoint is lean mass (an imaging compartment), not muscle strength, quality, or function — no pooled trial measured what the muscle could do. And the authors' framing that lean-mass loss "should not be considered a limitation" rests on a different outcome (glp1-body-composition-proportion-improves) than the one reported here; the vault records the kilograms, not the reassurance.

Prescription — clinician-only, never ranked (shared/safety.md). This concept gates a conversation with a doctor about muscle preservation; it does not rank an intervention and it sets no dose.
