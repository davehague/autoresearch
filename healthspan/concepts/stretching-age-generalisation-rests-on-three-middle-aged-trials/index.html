"Stretching works at any age" rests on three middle-aged trials

Oba 2026 tested whether flexibility adaptations to chronic static stretching are moderated by age, sex, training status, baseline flexibility, or target muscle group, found no significant moderation on any of the five, and concluded that stretching "may improve lower-limb flexibility across diverse healthy individuals."

The cell counts behind the age null: of 79 trials, 13 enrolled children/adolescents, 59 enrolled young adults, 3 enrolled middle-aged adults, and 4 enrolled older adults. A moderator test returning p = 0.853 on that distribution is reporting the shape of the literature, not the biology of ageing muscle. The same applies to training status (3 sedentary trials, p = 0.221) and sex (9 female-only trials, p = 0.990).

This is shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 — an underpowered null presented as absence — and the vault refuses it here for the same reason it refused it when the conclusion was inconvenient. The running count on this pathology now includes Shen 2026 (fall-risk RR 2.68, CI to 11.11, "similar safety profiles"), Wu 2026 (four adaptation nulls, "as effective as training to failure"), Doan 2026 (unstable vs stable resistance training), Chen 2025 (BFR as a heavy-load substitute), and Yassine 2026 (a cognition null the vault declined to call equivalence even though the null was convenient). Accepting Oba's version would have closed stretching's standing population gap, which is exactly why it gets the same treatment as the rest.

What the vault actually believes. Stretching very probably does work in middle-aged adults — that is a reasonable prior from mechanism, and every age cell pointed the same direction. But a prior is not a finding, and this paper does not supply one. The population gap on stretching, logged when the map was coined from young-athlete evidence on 2026-07-15, was partly closed on the outcome side by Ingram 2026 (45 prospective cohorts, mean age 47.6) and remains open on the intervention side: nobody has adequately tested whether a middle-aged, previously-injured knee responds like a twenty-year-old's.

What would resolve it. Trials of chronic static stretching in adults aged 40–65, ideally including post-injury knees, powered on range of motion. Three trials is not that.

For David: no change to the behaviour, one change to the confidence attached to it. Stretching stays the GOAL.md gate on returning to pickleball. The vault records that the evidence for it in his age bracket is three trials, so that a future run does not read "no moderation by age" as "demonstrated in middle-aged adults."
