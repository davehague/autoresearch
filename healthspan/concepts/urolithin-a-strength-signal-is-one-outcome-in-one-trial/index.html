The "~12% strength gain" is one muscle group, in one trial, alongside two measures that did not move

Read against the full outcome table, urolithin A's strength claim resolves to a single significant result: hamstring average peak torque, +19.6% (2.0 to 37.2), p = 0.029, in Singh 2022. In the same trial, on the same people, measured at the same visit, quadriceps peak torque rose +7.2% (p > 0.05) and hand-grip +2.7% (p = 0.080). One of three strength measures reached significance, with no correction for having taken three.

The vault's second strength trial does not rescue it. In Zhao 2024, 1RM bench press (p = 0.462) and 1RM back squat (p = 0.710) were null — and the review states that the significance markers previously shown for those two outcomes "were incorrect and have been corrected." Only quadriceps MVIC reached p = 0.048.

So the human strength evidence for urolithin A is: two significant outcomes, in two small trials, each sitting beside same-trial measures of the same construct that did not move — one of them at p = 0.048.

This is not a claim that urolithin A does not build strength. It is a claim that the number the vault has been carrying ("~12% strength in one trial") was a summary of the winning outcome rather than of the trial. The narrative "directionally favours urolithin A" reading survives; the effect estimate does not exist.

⚑ Note where the correction came from. Both the mis-marked 1RM significance and the per-outcome breakdown were surfaced by an independent group with no commercial relationship to the manufacturer — the exact gap urolithin-a had recorded as the reason it could not consolidate.
