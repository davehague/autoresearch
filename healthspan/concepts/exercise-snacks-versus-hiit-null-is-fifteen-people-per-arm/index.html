Fifteen people per arm found no difference between two exercise formats, which is what fifteen people per arm do

Matched on weekly duration (75 min), exercise menu and prescribed intensity, exercise snacks and HIIT were statistically indistinguishable on every physiological outcome the trial measured: body weight, BMI, body fat percentage, estimated VO₂max, standing long jump, systolic blood pressure, total cholesterol and LDL-C.

This is not evidence that the formats are equivalent. shared/study-appraisal.md separates failing to detect a difference from demonstrating there is none, and n = 15 per arm with no sample-size calculation could not have detected anything short of a very large effect. The trial is one of the smallest randomised comparisons in the vault.

⚑ The one outcome that DID separate the arms favoured the comparator, and it is worth stating first. Diastolic blood pressure showed a significant time × group interaction (p = 0.02, η²ₚ = 0.19): it fell meaningfully in HIIT (d = 0.86, p = 0.002) and not at all in exercise snacks (d = −0.04, p = 0.86). In a paper with 13+ outcomes, no multiplicity correction and no trial registration, one significant interaction is roughly what chance produces and should not be over-read. But a vault that would have foregrounded this result had it pointed the other way is obliged to foreground it now — the same discipline resistance-training-programming applied when three low-effort training options flattered GOAL.md's least-work philosophy and were each refused.

Why fatal flag #3 does not fire, and the credit is genuine. The paper's own conclusion disclaims the equivalence reading: exercise snacks "may be a promising strategy that warrants confirmation in adequately powered randomized controlled trials." That is the correct sentence, and it is the same disclaimer that spared Svensson 2026 and Camargo 2026. The vault applies the rule consistently: an author who states the limit gets a mixed rather than an unreliable.

Two measurement problems make even the nulls hard to read. VO₂max was estimated from a 12-minute Cooper run, a field test people improve at by learning to pace it; body composition came from bioelectrical impedance, not DXA. And intensity was never objectively verified — no heart rate, no gas analysis, no lactate — so it is unknown whether the two arms delivered comparable physiological work at all.

What would answer the question this trial asks. A registered trial with a non-exercising control arm, objectively verified intensity, a pre-specified primary outcome, and enough participants to detect a difference worth caring about — in adults over 40, since the whole promise of micro-bouts is for people whose barrier is time rather than capacity.
