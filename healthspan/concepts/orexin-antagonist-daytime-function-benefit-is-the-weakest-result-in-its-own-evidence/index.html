The outcome that justifies treating insomnia is the one this drug class evidences worst

Insomnia is not treated because a sleep diary shows a small number. It is treated because of the next day — the fatigue, the mood, the concentration, the sense of having not slept. The instrument built to measure exactly that is the IDSIQ (Insomnia Daytime Symptoms and Impacts Questionnaire), and it is the outcome on which dual orexin receptor antagonists perform worst in their own long-term evidence.

At six months: −5.51 (95% CI −13.25 to 2.22). Null. The interval crosses zero comfortably.

At twelve months: −6.37 (−12.31 to −0.43). Significant — with an upper bound of −0.43, meaning the data remain compatible with a benefit of almost nothing.

⚑ Set that against the same paper's sleep-quantity results and the ordering is exactly backwards from what anyone would want. Subjective total sleep time at six months was +19.87 minutes (14.15 to 25.60) at I² = 0% — tight, consistent, unambiguous (orexin-antagonists-add-about-twenty-minutes-of-self-reported-sleep). The class's best-measured effect is on the outcome that matters least, and its weakest effect is on the outcome that matters most.

⚑ And there is an adjacent number that makes this uncomfortable rather than merely disappointing. Somnolence ran at RR 5.96 (3.19–11.11) at six months and RR 4.85 (2.52–9.33) at twelve — roughly five to six times placebo, consistently, at both timepoints. A drug that blocks a wake-promoting signal at night is still blocking some of it in the morning. Whether the daytime-function null exists because the extra twenty minutes is being paid back as next-day grogginess is not tested by this paper. It is the obvious hypothesis, the two findings sit in the same tables, and the data are consistent with it.

⚠ Both IDSIQ estimates come from a single daridorexant trial (n = 677). This is not a pooled null across a literature; it is one trial's result appearing at two timepoints. A single-trial null is a failure to demonstrate, not a demonstration of no effect — which is why shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 was weighed and did not fire: the authors report it as non-significant and do not claim daytime function is unaffected.

⚠ The GRADE certainty rating for this outcome is in supplementary material that could not be retrieved, so how much confidence the reviewers themselves placed in it is unknown.

What survives: the class reliably adds a modest amount of self-reported sleep, and whether that translates into a better day is the least-settled question in its own evidence base, twelve years after the first approval.
