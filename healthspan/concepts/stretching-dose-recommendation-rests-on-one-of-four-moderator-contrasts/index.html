The recommendation to repeat each stretch three or more times is the winner of four uncorrected contrasts

Logan 2026 tested four dose moderators of stretching's effect on arterial stiffness. One reached significance: repeating each stretch three or more times rather than fewer (SMD contrast −1.46, p < 0.001). The other three did not — frequency of 5–7 days/week (contrast −1.05, p = 0.149), sessions of ≥30 minutes (−0.79, p = 0.169), and supervision (−1.11, p = 0.130).

The paper's conclusion promotes the survivor into a prescription: "We recommend stretching each major muscle-tendon group three or more times."

Three reasons the vault records the number and refuses the recommendation.

Four contrasts, no correction, six studies. Each subgroup comparison is drawn from a pooled set of six longitudinal studies, so the cells are fractions of six. With no adjustment for testing four moderators, the single significant result is a winner's-curse candidate — the largest of several noisy estimates, promoted because it crossed a threshold the others missed by margins (0.149, 0.169, 0.130) that a slightly different sample would have reshuffled.

The three failures point the same way as the success. All four contrasts favour more stretching. That consistency is either a real dose-response the study is too small to resolve, or a heterogeneous literature in which the bigger-dose studies were also the studies with bigger effects for other reasons. Six studies cannot distinguish those, and the paper does not claim to.

And 79 randomised trials could not do what six did here. stretching's largest source, Oba 2026, pooled 79 RCTs and 3,287 participants and reported that stretch volume, intensity and frequency were inconsistently reported and were not examined as moderators at all (stretching-dose-remains-unspecified-across-79-trials). A literature that cannot specify a dose from 79 trials on its own primary outcome has not specified one from six on a surrogate. shared/safety.md forbids synthesising a dose that no adequate source establishes, and GOAL.md's least-work-for-maximum-benefit principle makes an unfounded volume prescription actively costly.

Credit where it is due, though. The authors reported all four contrasts with their p-values, including the three that failed. That is why this source escapes shared/study-appraisal.md's second fatal red flag — p-hacking requires that only the winners be reported, and these are not. The problem is the conclusion, not the analysis, which is the pattern this vault has now catalogued repeatedly: the tables are the paper and the abstract is the argument (sponsor-adverse-findings-and-sponsor-friendly-framing-can-coexist).

So the prescription is recorded, not adopted — the same treatment the vault gave Zhu 2026's blood-flow- restriction cuff pressure range (bfr-cuff-pressure-prescription-remains-unstandardised). stretching continues to say stretch consistently and to refuse to name an amount.
