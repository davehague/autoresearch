A cohort recruited because insomnia should accelerate ageing was ageing at 0.89 years per year

DunedinPACE is scaled so that 1.00 means one year of biological ageing per calendar year. Carroll 2026's trial enrolled adults aged 60 and over with structured-interview-diagnosed DSM-IV insomnia disorder, recruited on the explicit hypothesis — stated in the Introduction and in the Research-in-context box — that insomnia in later life accelerates biological ageing. Their mean baseline DunedinPACE was 0.89 (CBT-I 0.91, SET 0.87).

They were ageing about eleven percent slower than the reference norm before anyone treated anything.

Two explanations, and the vault cannot separate them from these data.

(1) The cohort, not the clock. These participants were 84% White, averaged 16.5 years of education, lived within fifteen miles of a major university campus in Southern California, and were screened to exclude major depression, severe medical conditions and specified medications. That is a selection filter for advantaged, comparatively healthy people. The paper's own limitations section calls them "healthy older adults" — while its Interpretation calls them a "clinically vulnerable population." Both sentences are in the same article, and which one is true decides how this result should be read.

(2) The clock, not the cohort. If a well-validated pace-of-ageing measure reads below the norm in a population defined by a condition that measure is supposed to be sensitive to, that is a calibration question about the instrument.

Why it matters beyond this trial, and it is not a small point. epigenetic-clock-responsiveness-longevity-interventions-translage-sehgal-2026 found that biomarker responsiveness is concentrated in disease populations — across its 41 studies in people without a diagnosed disease, not one biomarker survived multiple-testing correction, while in its 10 disease studies several did. Carroll's cohort has a formal DSM-IV diagnosis, which files it as "disease," and its significant clock response fits Sehgal's pattern. But its baseline pace of 0.89 and its own authors' "healthy older adults" phrasing file it as "healthy," under which the same result sits in mild tension with Sehgal instead.

The vault records the tension rather than resolving it, and names what would resolve it: a study reporting DunedinPACE in an insomnia cohort against a matched non-insomnia comparison group from the same population — the baseline contrast this trial did not have, because both of its arms had insomnia.
