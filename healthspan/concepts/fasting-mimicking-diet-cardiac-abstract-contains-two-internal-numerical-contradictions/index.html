The two twelve-month numbers the paper's conclusion is built on contradict themselves, and an abstract can't say which is wrong

The FIT-trial cardiac MRI substudy's Conclusion rests on two twelve-month diastolic-filling findings. Both carry an internal defect that abstract-level access cannot resolve:

(1) An impossible confidence interval. Mitral annular early peak velocity (Ea) is reported as +0.84 cm/sec (95% CI −0.02 to 1.67), P = .04. An interval spanning zero says the data are compatible with no effect; P = .04 says they are not. Both cannot be correct — one number in the abstract is wrong, and there is no way to tell which from the published summary alone.

(2) A sign flip between sections. Results state: "ΔE/A was positively associated with change in HbA1c (standardized β = 0.47, P = .01)." Conclusion states: "E/A improvements were associated with reduced HbA1c." A positive coefficient between two change scores means they moved together — so either the Conclusion inverts its own Results, or HbA1c is sign-coded somewhere the abstract doesn't specify. If the Results wording is correct, the filling measure the paper calls an improvement moved in step with worsening blood sugar.

The vault marks both UNVERIFIED rather than adjudicating from an abstract, and notes only that the direction the Conclusion asserts is the flattering one.
