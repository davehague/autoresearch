A single paper supplies both columns: this marker predicts strongly and responds weakly

The most robust results in US POINTER's imaging analysis are not about the intervention at all.

Baseline white-matter free water predicted incident cerebral microbleeds — OR 1.63 (1.24–2.14), P < .001 — and faster white-matter lesion progression (β = 0.014, P = .02). Baseline PSMD predicted microbleeds at OR 1.40 (1.08–1.81), P < .001. Lower baseline fractional anisotropy predicted faster lesion progression (β = −0.018, P = .004). These are the tightest intervals in the paper, and they are internally consistent: the markers that should point one way point that way, and the one that should point the other way does.

⚑ Why this concept lives on biological-age-clocks rather than on the trial's own map. That map exists to grade instruments — and it holds the vault's governing rule for them: predicting a decline is not modifying it. aspirin carries the same rule from the other direction, where ASPREE's predictors of healthspan loss turned out to be "the boring ones" — age, male sex, smoking, hypertension, frailty (healthspan-loss-predictors-in-randomised-cohort) — while the drug moved nothing at all (HR 0.98).

What makes this source unusual is that it supplies both columns from the same 959 people, in the same paper. Free water predicts microbleeds at OR 1.63 with a tight interval — and responds to two years of a structured multidomain programme only in participants under 70, on an unstandardised beta with no clinical anchor, while five other markers do not respond at all (multidomain-lifestyle-attenuates-white-matter-free-water-only-under-seventy, multidomain-lifestyle-null-on-five-of-six-cerebrovascular-mri-markers). The vault has been asserting the predictor/responder gap as a principle. This is the first study in the ledger that exhibits both halves of it at once, in one cohort, on one marker.

Graded cohort and suggestive, deliberately. These are observational analyses inside a randomised trial — nobody was assigned a baseline free-water value — so the tier is observational regardless of the trial that housed them. shared/study-appraisal.md's observational lens set applies: adjustment covers sex, hypertension, diabetes and smoking and nothing else, and reverse causation is not excluded — early undetected vascular pathology could plausibly raise baseline free water and cause the later microbleeds, which is one process seen twice rather than a prediction. The source's mixed verdict caps the strength.

The practical use is diagnostic reasoning, not action. A marker that forecasts who will accrue small-vessel injury is useful for selecting trial participants and stratifying risk. It is not, on this evidence, a target: the only intervention tested against it moved it slightly, in half a cohort, on one of six measures. Nothing here licenses acting on a free-water value, and no such measurement is available or recommended outside research imaging.
