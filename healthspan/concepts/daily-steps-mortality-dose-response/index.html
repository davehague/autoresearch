~7,000 daily steps ≈ 47% lower all-cause mortality, with a plateau (dose-response meta-analysis)

Pooling 24 prospective cohorts (>160,000 adults) with device-measured steps, walking ~7,000 vs ~2,000 steps/day tracked with:

  • All-cause mortality HR 0.53 (0.46–0.60) — ~47% lower
  • CVD mortality HR 0.53 (0.37–0.77); cancer mortality HR 0.63 (0.55–0.72)
  • CVD incidence HR 0.75 (0.67–0.85)

The association is non-linear with an inflection around 5,000–7,000 steps/day for all-cause mortality, CVD, dementia, and falls (roughly linear for CVD/cancer mortality, diabetes, and depressive symptoms). Practically, most of the mortality benefit is banked well before 10,000 steps — the popular 10,000-step target is a marketing figure, not an evidence threshold.

Why this matters for the vault. A hard-endpoint signal (mortality, not a surrogate) from the lowest-burden, most joint-friendly activity there is — directly aligned with GOAL.md (least work, knee-safe during patellar-contusion recovery). It is the low-intensity floor beneath the cardiorespiratory-fitness-all-cause-mortality fitness→mortality story.

Bounded honestly (why emerging, capped suggestive): every cohort is observational — step count can't be randomized — so reverse causation (subclinical illness lowers step count) inflates part of the HR, and high vs low steppers differ on many mortality-relevant confounders. The magnitude and dose-response argue against pure confounding, but the observational ceiling holds it short of reliable.
