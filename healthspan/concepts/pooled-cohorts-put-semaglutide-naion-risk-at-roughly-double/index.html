Eight cohorts and 5.9 million people put the semaglutide eye signal at about double — as a ratio, with no baseline attached

A prospectively registered meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42025649542) pooled eight observational cohorts, 5,916,559 participants, comparing semaglutide users against non-semaglutide-treated patients for nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy — a sudden, painless, usually permanent loss of vision in one eye caused by an infarct at the optic nerve head. Pooled relative effect 1.93 (95% CI 1.22–3.08), p=0.01, I²=91%.

⚠ The number is a ratio and the paper reports no absolute risks at all. NAION runs at roughly 2–10 cases per 100,000 person-years in the general population. Doubling a rate that small leaves it small, and "roughly doubles the risk" is not a decidable statement without the baseline. The vault's earlier source on this question did report absolutes — glp1-small-absolute-increase-in-ischemic-optic-neuropathy found 8.5 vs 5.5 events per 10,000 over 18 months, number-needed-to-harm 3,333, against two active comparators. More data arrived tonight and less usable information came with it.

⚠ I² of 91% means the eight studies substantially disagree. Almost all of the between-study variation exceeds what sampling error explains, so the pooled estimate is an average over cohorts that may not describe the same thing. Heterogeneity stayed high in every subgroup (88.3% in diabetes, 84.0% in overweight/obesity).

⚑ What it adds to the vault, precisely. The map's optic-neuropathy thread rested on one target-trial emulation, with the vault's own note that the signal might be residual confounding (glp1-optic-neuropathy-signal-may-be-residual-confounding). The direction now replicates across eight independent cohorts. What has not changed is the objection: semaglutide is prescribed for diabetes and obesity, both independent NAION risk factors, so pooling eight studies that share a confounder makes the confounded estimate more precise rather than less confounded. The authors decline the causal claim in their own conclusion — "suggests a possible association… Further randomized clinical trials are required."

Status implication: none. Highest-tier-wins (shared/evidence-model.md) — a mixed pooled-observational harm cannot outrank SELECT's randomised MACE HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90), NNT 67 (glp1-mace-benefit-rct-select). glp-1 holds at consolidating; the cost ledger grows.

⚠ Retrieval limit, stated because it bounds the grade. This source is abstract-bounded — Wiley serves only the abstract. No risk-of-bias assessment, no per-study table, no publication-bias test, no funding statement and no GRADE rating are retrievable. Whether the eight cohorts draw on overlapping US claims databases — a live concern in this literature — cannot be checked. Capped suggestive.
