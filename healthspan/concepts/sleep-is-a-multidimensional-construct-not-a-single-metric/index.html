"Sleep quality" is at least thirteen dimensions — and no single metric in this vault is the construct

Paquet 2026 assembled 39 objective and subjective sleep variables in MESA — polysomnographic EEG (power spectral density, spindle density, spindle–slow-oscillation coupling, REM), wrist actigraphy (continuity, variability) and questionnaires — and reduced them by principal components analysis to 13 composites explaining roughly 71% of the sample's variance.

This is a measurement finding, and it is the paper's most durable contribution. The sleep literature routinely picks one number — PSQI total, total sleep time, sleep efficiency, a regularity index — and calls it sleep quality. This analysis says the construct does not compress that far: thirteen substantially independent dimensions were needed, and even they leave ~29% of variance unexplained.

It cuts both ways for this vault, and both edges are recorded.

The favourable edge. It vindicates the standing refusal to treat sleep as one exposure. sleep-regularity and sleep-duration are deliberately kept apart because when you sleep and how long you sleep are different variables; this paper says there are at least eleven more besides. The vault's "count sleep once at regimen time" rule is about not double-counting the same behaviour across maps — it survives intact, and is now better justified.

The unfavourable edge, which is sharper. If sleep is thirteen-dimensional, every single-metric sleep finding in this vault is measuring a slice. That includes the Sleep Regularity Index behind sleep-regularity-predicts-mortality; it includes the PSQI totals behind the entire supplement shelf (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold); and it includes the accelerometry-derived sleep-period-time that sleep-period-time-outperforms-total-sleep-time-for-mortality found out-predicts total sleep time. Nobody's favourite metric is the construct, and a metric that out-predicts another is winning a race between two slices.

Read at its true strength. mixed, capped suggestive. PCA structure is sample-dependent — 13 composites in 1,628 older adults with full polysomnography is not a universal decomposition, and the loadings are UNVERIFIED (paywalled). The 71% figure describes variance in sleep measurements, not in anything that matters clinically. And a richer instrument set is not the same as a more actionable one: three of the five composites this paper found predictive are EEG microstructure, which no intervention in this vault targets and no person can practise.

The reading discipline the vault takes from it. The number of things you can measure about a system is not evidence about how much of it you can change. The same shape appears in biological-age-clocks: 14 consensus aging biomarkers, 5 predicted mortality, the whole panel discriminating at C-index 0.65. Two literatures, two rich panels, a handful of small hits, and no demonstrated capacity to guide an intervention.

For David: a reason not to buy sleep instrumentation. A device that scores more dimensions of his sleep measures more of a construct whose actionable part remains consistent sleep and wake timing — free, zero-burden, and already in the protocol.
