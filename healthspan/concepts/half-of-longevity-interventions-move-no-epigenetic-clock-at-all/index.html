More than half of fifty-one "longevity interventions" moved none of sixteen epigenetic clocks

The database was assembled on purpose to be favourable. TranslAGE collected 51 human intervention studies selected because the intervention "has been hypothesised for the purposes of longevity or healthspan extension as defined in literature." Every study had blood DNA methylation measured before and after. All 51 were pushed through one harmonised pipeline that recomputed the same 16 prominent epigenetic clocks — three generation-1 chronological-age clocks, six generation-2+ mortality and pace-of-aging clocks, four reliability-optimised "PC" rebuilds, and three "explainable" multi-score clocks — so that discordant findings could no longer be blamed on different labs computing different panels.

⚑ Twenty-six of fifty-one — more than half — did nothing to any of sixteen clocks. And five went the wrong way.

The direction check makes the pooled number legible. Four control datasets and nine "pro-aging event" datasets were run through the identical pipeline. The aging events moved the clocks up by +0.14 SD; the longevity interventions moved them down by −0.06 SD (both P < 0.0001). ⚑ The instruments register aging insults more than twice as strongly as they register anti-aging interventions.

Two of the authors' own consistency rules were failed by prominent categories. Their proposal: a genuine effect should move biomarkers of the same generation in the same direction within a study (rule 1) and reproduce in a second study of the same intervention (rule 2). Senolytics failed both across five studies — GrimAgeV1/V2 moving opposite to the rest within a study, and PCHorvath2 moving in opposite directions between studies. Two studies of the same supplement (SRW) failed rule 1, with PCHorvath1 and PhenoAge falling while PCHorvath2, PCHannum and DNAmEMRAge rose. Anti-TNF therapy and two Mediterranean diets passed both.

⚠ What this does not show. The per-intervention statistic is a paired within-arm before-versus-after t-test, not a randomised between-arm contrast, so a null here can be an underpowered study as easily as an inert intervention, and a positive can be regression to the mean. Per-study significance is called at an uncorrected P < 0.05. The count above is a description of an instrument panel's behaviour across a heterogeneous convenience database — not an efficacy verdict on any of the 51 interventions. Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal.

What it does show, and it is the useful half. ⚑ If a clock panel fails to fire for more than half of the interventions a field has nominated as its best candidates, the panel is either insensitive or the candidates are inert, and the study cannot tell you which. Either reading argues against treating a clock movement — or a clock non-movement — as informative on its own. This is the same asymmetry greenspace-dementia-signal-outruns-its-epigenetic-surrogate established from the other side: a null clock result does not license a null outcome claim.
