The case for stopping was never really about survival — it was about burden, and the trial measured the burden and did not find it

The argument for deprescribing goes like this: in old age the benefit of a preventive drug shrinks while its costs — side-effects, drug interactions, polypharmacy, expense, the daily reminder of being a patient — do not. So at some point the ledger flips. This trial is the first randomised test of that ledger for statins, and the cost side came back empty.

Adverse events were the same. 73.1% on continuation, 74.0% after stopping. Serious adverse events 44.4% vs 44.0%. Myopathy — the side-effect statins are best known for — 5.7% vs 5.1%. Cognitive disorders 5.2% vs 5.1%. New diabetes 1.1% vs 0.9%. Cataract 4.9% vs 4.4%. Not one of the harms the drug class is criticised for got better when the drug was removed.

Quality of life was the same, and did not change over three years in either arm. SF-12 physical component 42.3 vs 42.5; mental 48.5 vs 49.0. The authors call this "surprising" and write that it "does not support the rationale for deprescribing."

⚑ The explanation is survivorship, and it sharpens rather than dissolves the finding. Everyone here had taken a statin for at least a year and most for over a decade. Anyone who was going to get myalgia had already got it and stopped years ago. So this is not evidence that statins are free of side-effects. It is evidence about a specific and very common clinical situation: the person in front of you is 80, has been on a statin for eleven years, and is not complaining about it. For that person — and there are millions; roughly 30% of Canadians over 65, 33% of Americans over 80 and 18% of Swedes aged 75–84 take statins for primary prevention — stopping removes a burden that was not being felt.

⚠ One row did separate and the vault records it rather than tidying it away. Haematological events: 2/631 (0.3%) on continuation versus 10/527 (1.9%) after stopping, difference +1.58 percentage points (0.37 to 3.18). The interval clears zero. It is also one interval among several dozen in a table with no correction for multiplicity, in a paper whose own instruction is that "all our secondary analyses should be interpreted cautiously", and the authors do not discuss it anywhere. The most likely explanation is chance. It is recorded because a ledger that only reports the rows that fit the narrative is not a ledger.

Where this lands. ⚑ The first trial of statin deprescribing found neither a survival cost nor a burden benefit — which makes it a decision without a winner, and explains why the National Lipid Association and the American Geriatrics Society landed on shared decision-making rather than a rule. Verbatim from the paper: "the rationale for deprescribing becomes less clear for individuals who are asymptomatic, adherent, and comfortable continuing treatment."

⚠ Open-label caveat, and it bites hardest exactly here. Adverse-event reporting and quality-of-life scores are subjective outcomes collected by unmasked general practitioners from unmasked patients, some in person and some by telephone. Mortality is robust to that; these endpoints are not. Capped suggestive.
