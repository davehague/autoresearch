Cooling a sore muscle did not speed anything up, whether you started immediately or waited a day

Three arms, seventy-two hours, six outcomes. Soreness resolved on an identical trajectory in all three groups. Strength recovered identically. Creatine kinase, erythrocyte sedimentation rate and muscle thickness moved with time in every arm and separated in none. Neither immediate cooling (nine sessions) nor delayed cooling (six sessions, starting 24 h post-exercise) beat doing nothing.

The damage protocol worked — soreness, CK and ESR all changed significantly over time and MVIC dropped acutely — so this is a null against a stimulus that demonstrably landed, not a null from a trial where nothing happened.

⚑ This confirms a higher-tier finding the vault already holds. cold-water-immersion-does-not-speed-strength-recovery carries Zhu 2026: 22 RCTs, n = 375, cold-water immersion versus passive recovery, MVIC g = 0.08 (−0.11 to 0.26, p = 0.42), no time-dependence at 0/24/48/72 h, no publication bias, and GRADE MODERATE — the only moderate-certainty outcome in that source. A 48-person trial cannot add much to a 22-trial meta-analysis, and it is not claimed to. What it adds is a different delivery method, a different timing question, and an independent replication of the same answer.

⚠ Two limits, both large, both stated by the authors. (1) Power. The sample size was calculated from postoperative cooling data, assuming between-group differences of 2.5–2.9 pain points (d ≈ 1.2–1.4) — giving 13 per group. With 16 per arm, "the study was therefore powered to detect large between-group effects, while smaller effects may not have been detectable," and the authors write explicitly that "non-significant findings should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of the absence of effect." That refusal is the discipline this vault made a reference case of (subgroup-nulls-reported-as-insufficient-evidence-not-equivalence). (2) Dose. Reusable gel packs deliver surface-limited cooling and no intramuscular temperature was measured (gel-pack-cooling-may-not-deliver-a-thermal-dose-to-deep-muscle).

What makes the null unusually credible despite both limits: who paid for it. The trial was funded by Axanova AG, the manufacturer of the cold packs it tested, and published the finding that they did nothing (sponsor-funded-cooling-trial-published-its-own-null).

Where this leaves the map's practical answer. cold-water-immersion already holds that post-workout cold blunts the hypertrophy it is layered on (cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy, safety_flags: [interaction]), that cold transiently impairs explosive power (cold-water-immersion-transiently-impairs-explosive-power), and that inflammation rises acutely after immersion (cold-water-immersion-acute-inflammatory-spike). The recovery rationale printed on the packaging now has a second randomised failure against it, and there is still no measured benefit to weigh against the known cost. The map's wellbeing and sleep signals are a separate claim and are untouched — none of them was measured here.
