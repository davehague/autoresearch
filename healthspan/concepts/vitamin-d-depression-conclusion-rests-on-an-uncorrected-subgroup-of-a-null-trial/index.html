The primary outcome was null; the conclusion was written from a subgroup of thirty-four people

The trial's structure, in order: the pre-specified, clinician-administered primary outcome was null (B = −0.30, 95% CI −1.81 to 1.21, p = 0.698). A self-reported secondary anxiety measure was significant (B = −1.78, −3.35 to −0.32). An uncorrected subgroup — participants with moderate-to-severe depression, roughly 18 randomised versus 16 placebo — showed reductions in both depression and anxiety. The abstract's conclusion is built from that subgroup: "Vitamin D supplementation may benefit young people with moderate-to-severe depression and vitamin D insufficiency/deficiency."

And the subgroup does not survive its own subdivision. Restricted to adolescents aged 10–19, the moderate-to-severe cell showed no difference on any outcome.

Why the vault records this rather than dismissing it. This is the same shape it flagged on cocoa-flavanols two weeks ago — a null pre-specified primary, a secondary or alternative analysis that wins, and the winner travelling (cocoa-flavanol-cvd-benefit-is-estimator-dependent, cocoa-flavanol-primary-cvd-outcome-null-cosmos-parent) — arriving in a trial roughly two hundred times smaller. The mechanism is not fraud; it is the ordinary gravity of publication. A null primary is hard to write a conclusion from, and there is always a cell that reached significance.

What separates this from the COSMOS re-analysis, and it earns the authors real credit. The COSMOS win-ratio paper was appraised unreliable because the winning estimator was promoted without the losing one being given equal weight. Here the null primary is reported prominently, every outcome appears, and the authors label their own reading exploratory — "sample size constraints favor an exploratory interpretation of these findings." shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #2 targets concealment, and there is none. That is why this source is appraised mixed rather than unreliable.

⚠ The honest counterweight, because the subgroup is not an absurd place to look. A benefit concentrated in the more severely affected is the benefit-on-demand structure this vault has now independently established in four literatures: exercise helps sleep in poor sleepers (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers); multidomain programmes work best on people already losing something; and — the same night this landed — exercise moves cognition in people with mild cognitive impairment (exercise-improves-cognition-in-mci-against-wait-list-controls) and does not move it in healthy older adults (walking-training-null-on-cognition-in-older-adults). A deficiency-correction effect appearing only where the deficit is largest is the expected shape, not a suspicious one. That is an argument for a properly powered, severity-stratified trial. It is not an argument for banking this subgroup, and the vault does not.
