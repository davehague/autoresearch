Short-term combined aerobic+resistance exercise is the largest effect in a 35-RCT knee-OA network, at low certainty throughout

A Bayesian network meta-analysis of 35 RCTs (n=3,808) ranking aerobic, resistance, and combined aerobic+resistance exercise against each other for knee osteoarthritis pain, function, and quality-of-life, at short-term (≤12 weeks) and long-term (>12 weeks) follow-up. Short-term combined aerobic+resistance training was the largest effect in the entire network on all three outcomes: pain SMD −0.937, function SMD −0.590, quality of life SMD 0.663 — all GRADE low certainty. Long-term aerobic exercise alone ranked second on function (SMD −0.540, very-low certainty).

Why this matters for the vault. exercise-for-osteoarthritis already holds a Cochrane review (Hall 2026) establishing a small, clinically-marginal benefit of exercise for hip OA, and a non-drug-therapy network (Chen X 2025) placing exercise 2nd–3rd among knee-OA treatments. This source extends that into a knee-specific, modality-and-duration-specific ranking: combining aerobic and resistance work beats either alone, and short-term beats long-term — the same "quality-gradient" shape this vault has flagged across other exercise-OA literatures (Schleimer 2026: benefit shrinks with study size and duration).

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, not higher). Every single estimate in the network is GRADE low or very-low certainty — there is no moderate- or high-certainty result anywhere in what the abstract reports. The authors' own conclusion hedges: exercise's long-term clinical significance is "limited." No adverse-event or safety data is reported. Full text is paywalled (JOSPT subscription journal, no PMC deposit), so risk-of-bias detail, funding, and conflicts are unverifiable.

The practical shape of the claim. If exercising a painful knee, combining aerobic and resistance work in the short term appears to help more than either alone — but the certainty is low throughout, and this says nothing new about safety or about whether exercise beats other conservative care, which this map's existing sources (manual therapy, chondroitin) already address more directly. No dose, equipment, or supplement recommended.
