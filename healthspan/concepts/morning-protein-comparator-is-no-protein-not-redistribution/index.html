This is an "add protein" experiment wearing a "when to eat protein" label

Ikeda 2026 compares morning protein or amino acid intake against no morning intake. That is not the same question as "does taking the same daily total at breakfast beat taking it later?" — and the difference decides what its nulls mean.

Two readings, and the retrieved record does not distinguish them. If the trials equalised total daily protein and only shifted its timing, the nulls are about distribution, and they corroborate protein-timing-distribution-null-on-lean-mass directly. If they did not — if the intervention arm simply ate more protein overall — then a null on muscle is a null on adding protein to an older adult's diet, which is a different and arguably more surprising result, and one protein-target's ~1.6 g/kg/day claim would not have predicted.

The vault carries the source at the weaker reading and logs the ambiguity rather than assuming it away. That is why morning-protein-null-on-muscle-mass-and-strength-in-older-adults is graded single-study and stances as confirms on direction only.

Why the ambiguity is plausible rather than pedantic. The same paper reports that morning protein reduced total daily energy intake (protein-at-breakfast-displaces-total-daily-energy-intake) — which is evidence that participants were not holding their daily totals fixed. If overall intake fell, total daily protein may have fallen too, in which case the intervention arm may have ended up with less daily protein than the label implies. That would make a muscle null nearly uninterpretable as a timing result.

This is the vault's recurring comparator problem, and naming it is the point. It caught the same shape in Chang 2026's protein-plus-probiotic pooling, where a headline pooled versus placebo (which measures the protein) with versus protein alone (which measures the probiotic) and therefore answered neither (protein-probiotic-headline-confounded-by-comparator-pooling); in insomnia networks lacking a CBT-alone node (cbt-sleep-effect-not-isolable-no-cbt-alone-node); and, in tonight's run, in a fasting trial whose glucose benefit cannot be separated from the weight it removed (tre-glucose-effect-not-separable-from-concurrent-weight-loss). Trialists bundle, and bundling destroys attribution — sometimes by combining two treatments, sometimes by choosing a comparator that lets two variables move at once.

The gap is specific and closable: a trial randomising the same daily protein total to a front-loaded versus an evenly-distributed schedule in older adults would answer the question this paper is read as answering. Nothing in this vault has that.
