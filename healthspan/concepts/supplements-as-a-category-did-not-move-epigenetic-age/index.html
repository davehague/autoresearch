Sorted by category, the clocks moved for drugs and for whole-diet change — and not for supplements

The 51 interventions were binned into four categories and each bin tested against zero effect across the 16-clock panel.

CategoryMean effect on epigenetic ageSignificant?

Pharmacological interventions also produced significantly larger effects than every other category (versus lifestyle P = 0.0063, versus supplements P = 0.0009, versus medical procedures P = 0.0517), and the pharmacological bin contained significantly more responsive biomarkers than any other.

⚑ The two categories that moved these instruments are the two that change systemic biology wholesale — a drug acting on inflammation or metabolism, or a change to the whole diet. The category that did not is the one sold on a shelf.

Within-supplement incoherence, not just a null. Two studies of the same supplement (SRW) moved the same biomarkers in opposite directions — PCHorvath1 and PhenoAge down, PCHorvath2, PCHannum and DNAmEMRAge up — failing the authors' rule that a real effect should move biomarkers of the same generation the same way. ⚠ Senolytics, the most-hyped geroscience supplement category of the past five years, failed both consistency rules across five studies: biomarkers moving opposite ways within a study, and PCHorvath2 moving opposite ways between studies. Anti-TNF therapy and two Mediterranean diets were the only interventions in the database to pass both rules.

Why this is pushes-back. omega-3 holds omega3-slows-epigenetic-aging — a post hoc DO-HEALTH analysis in which 1 g/day of omega-3 slowed PhenoAge, GrimAge2 and DunedinPACE by 0.16–0.32 SD, about 3–4 months over three years. That is the single most favourable epigenetic result the vault holds for any supplement, and this synthesis puts omega-3's whole category on the non-responding side of the ledger, in a pipeline that recomputed every clock consistently and in which more than half of all interventions moved nothing (half-of-longevity-interventions-move-no-epigenetic-clock-at-all).

⚠ Read the push-back precisely, in both directions. This is not a refutation of the DO-HEALTH result — DO-HEALTH was a randomised factorial trial and this is a pooled set of within-arm before-and-after comparisons with no randomised contrast, at a lower evidence tier for causal claims. What it does is remove the reassurance of company: DO-HEALTH's omega-3 effect no longer sits inside a category that behaves consistently, and the category's failures are not quiet nulls but sign reversals between studies of the same product. Neither result moves the other's Status. And per epigenetic-clocks-barely-respond-in-healthy-populations, a supplement aimed at healthy adults is being measured in exactly the population where these instruments detect the least.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal — an appraisal that includes the fact that three of this paper's authors are employed by a company selling epigenetic-age tests.
