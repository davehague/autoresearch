Measured against the clocks themselves, only smoking and body weight survived — physical activity did not

Zhao 2026 tested six modifiable lifestyle factors against five epigenetic aging biomarkers in the 778 participants who had actual methylation data, adjusting all lifestyle factors simultaneously. After FDR correction, two survived: current smoking and overweight/obesity, both against DunedinPACE.

Physical activity did not. Alcohol did not. Sleep quality did not. Dietary habits did not.

Physical activity reappears later in the paper — negatively associated with the metabolic signatures of DunedinPACE and DNAmPhenoAA, in the 5,439-person phase. ⚑ But those are the proxies, not the clocks, and they explain 4.4%–13.0% of their targets' variance (a-metabolic-proxy-for-epigenetic-age-out-associates-the-clock-it-proxies). The exercise finding in this paper lives entirely at the proxy layer.

Why the vault records this rather than passing over it. Several maps here carry an epigenetic-clock result as supporting evidence for an intervention:

  • zone-2-cardio carries Shan 2026 — physical activity associated with lower GrimAge and Horvath in 145,465 people.
  • omega-3 carries the DO-HEALTH post hoc — omega-3 slowing three next-generation clocks by ~2.9–3.8 months (omega3-slows-epigenetic-aging).
  • multidomain-lifestyle carries an epigenetic arm in which one clock of five moved in twenty-four people.
  • cbt-insomnia now carries cbt-for-insomnia-slowed-the-pace-of-epigenetic-aging-against-an-active-comparator.

A 778-person cross-sectional null is not a refutation of any of them — it is small, it is one region of one country, it is cross-sectional, and Shan 2026 has 187 times the sample. What it is, is a reminder that the physical-activity-to-clock association is not robust to a modestly sized, heavily adjusted analysis, and that in this dataset the two factors that did survive — smoking and body weight — are the two with the largest and least contested effects on health generally. When a clock only detects the biggest exposures, it is not sensitive enough to adjudicate between the interventions this vault actually grades.

The one positive worth carrying. BMI was the most consistent modifiable factor across all five signatures, with both overweight/obesity and underweight associated with faster metabolomic-epigenetic ageing — matching a prior 61-study meta-analysis that also found BMI the most consistently associated modifiable factor. The adverse end is at both extremes, which is a mild argument against pursuing weight loss past the normal range for its own sake.
