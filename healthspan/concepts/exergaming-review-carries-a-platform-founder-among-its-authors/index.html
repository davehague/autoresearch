A co-founder of an exergame company helped review the evidence for exergames — declared, mitigated, and pointing the wrong way for a conflict

The review's competing-interests statement names it directly: one author co-founded Dividat, an ETH Zurich spin-off that built an exergame platform, and advised the company, with no revenue paid or promised in the 36 months before the work. Four authors also co-wrote four of the nine included trials. The stated mitigation is a workflow exclusion rather than a reassurance: "These 4 authors played no part in data extraction, data analysis, or quality assessment."

shared/evidence-model.md's bias watch says such ties are flagged and never laundered into higher apparent certainty. shared/study-appraisal.md says industry ties are a RED flag to record and not fatal on their own. Both apply, and applying them honestly means recording something inconvenient for a simple reading: the conflict is real, and the paper's findings are unflattering to the category the conflicted author has an interest in.

The review concludes that exergaming's only moderate-certainty benefit is against a control group with no fall-prevention evidence base, that against real strength-and-balance training every estimate is low certainty with one pointing the wrong way (exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator), and that injurious falls were measured once, non-significantly (exergaming-lowers-fall-rate-without-lowering-the-number-of-fallers). A conflicted review team that publishes those three results has not obviously been captured.

⚑ So why record it at all? Because the vault's rule is that disclosure is assessed before the result is known, not calibrated to whether the answer looks convenient. The same principle produced the note on the industry-funded creatine safety review and the Magtein®-adjacent threonate trial, and it produced the green flag on the cold-pack manufacturer that funded a null against its own product (sponsor-funded-cooling-trial-published-its-own-null). Applied symmetrically, this is a red flag whose predicted direction did not materialise — which is information about this paper, and is recorded as such.

⚠ The four author–trial overlaps are the less-discussed half and arguably matter more. Four of nine included trials were co-written by review authors. In a nine-trial review, that is a large fraction of the evidence base assembled by people with a stake in how it reads. The mitigation — exclusion from extraction, analysis and quality assessment — is the right one and is stated specifically. It cannot address inclusion criteria, framing, or which questions the review asks. Graded open-question at single-study because it names an unresolvable uncertainty about one paper rather than a finding about exergames.

This note is part of why no exergaming map was coined — the fourteenth candidate-intervention refusal — but it is the least weighted of the four grounds, precisely because the mitigation was specific and the results run against the interest. The decisive grounds are the comparator gradient and the unquantified support bundled around the intervention in several trials.
