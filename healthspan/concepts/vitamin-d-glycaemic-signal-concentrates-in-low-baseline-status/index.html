The first pooled randomised evidence to touch vitamin D's one surviving thread — and it is an uncorrected subgroup on a surrogate

vitamin-d holds contested rather than fading on a single surviving argument: that correcting genuine deficiency might do what supplementing already-replete people does not. Five randomised outcome families have come back flat, all of them in populations that were largely replete at baseline, and the map has recorded twice in the last twelve days that a source "does NOT touch the map's live question" — of Paez-Allendes on 2026-07-18 and, with more nuance, of Lee on 2026-07-29.

This source does touch it. In a meta-analysis of 45 randomised placebo-controlled trials, the favourable effects on fasting blood glucose and HbA₁c were reported where baseline serum vitamin D was below 50 nmol/L — insufficiency, the population the thread is about. It is the first pooled randomised evidence bearing on the deficiency-correction question that the vault holds. Everything before it was either an observational target-trial emulation (vitamin-d-deficiency-mortality-signal) or a single small trial in a different outcome family and a different decade of life (vitamin-d-null-on-clinician-rated-depression-in-deficient-young-people).

And the vault refuses to close the gap on it, for four reasons it can state precisely.

One — wrong outcome family, and this is the same refusal made three times now. The map's deficiency thread is about all-cause mortality in adults. This is fasting glucose and glycated haemoglobin — two surrogate measurements. The exposure matches; the outcome does not. That is verbatim the reason the map declined Paez-Allendes (androgens) and declined Lee (adolescent mood). A glycaemic marker in insufficiency is not a mortality result in insufficiency, and the vault will not let one stand for the other.

Two — it is a subgroup, and the review's multiplicity handling cannot be checked. The full text is not yet published: the journal's August 2026 issue is unposted and the DOI is unregistered as of 2026-07-30. So the number of trials in the <50 nmol/L stratum, the subgroup effect sizes, whether any interaction test was performed, and whether any correction was applied are all UNVERIFIED. A subgroup whose interaction test cannot be inspected is a hypothesis.

Three — the sibling subgroup analysis in the same paper is incoherent. Systolic blood pressure and LDL improved in participants aged ≥55 while fasting glucose improved in those aged <55 — opposite age-dependence for outcomes on a shared pathway. A paper whose age subgroups contradict each other has not earned belief in its status subgroups, however biologically plausible the latter is.

Four — the overall estimates the subgroup sits inside are trivially small. Fasting glucose −0.11 mmol/L ≈ 2 mg/dL; HbA₁c −0.164% with an upper bound of −0.01%. A subgroup effect is larger than the overall pooled one by construction, but it is being carved out of numbers this small.

⚑ What makes this worth recording rather than dismissing, and it is not politeness. Vitamin D's deficiency thread is not an isolated escape hatch — it is a shape this vault has now seen twice. nad-precursors holds nad-precursors-benefit-confined-to-deficiency-populations: the same structure on a different molecule, where supplementing the replete does nothing and the surviving case is repleting a deficit. The map recorded that parallel on 2026-07-29 and named the standing question it raises — is this nutrient a lever, or a deficiency correction? A benefit that appears only where baseline status is low is the expected shape of a deficiency correction, not a suspicious one. That is precisely the argument for a properly powered, status-stratified trial with a hard endpoint — and precisely why the vault will not bank it from an unverifiable subgroup on a surrogate.

Graded single-study and open-question. The map's Status is unchanged. The gap narrows for the second time in two days, by a fraction, and stays open — and this is now the third distinct citation the vault has declined to close it on, which is a record worth keeping so a future run does not close it on the fourth.

What would settle it. A randomised trial that enrols only people with baseline 25-hydroxyvitamin D below the insufficiency threshold, measures achieved concentrations to confirm the deficiency was actually corrected — a thing Lee 2026 could not do — and runs long enough on a hard endpoint. No such trial exists in this vault, and none of the three near-misses substitutes for it.
