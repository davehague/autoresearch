The fitter you are, the less beetroot juice does — and it is sold to the fittest people

The subgroup gradient is monotonic and it runs the opposite way to the marketing. Recreationally active adults: SMD 0.38 (0.21–0.55), rated high certainty under GRADE. Moderately trained: 0.17 (0.03–0.31). Highly trained and elite athletes: 0.07 (−0.09 to 0.23) — not significant, rated low certainty.

The commercial market for nitrate supplementation is competitive endurance sport. That is the subgroup in which the effect is not demonstrable.

⚠ Read the athlete result precisely, because this vault has enforced the distinction twelve times. The elite subgroup is small, heterogeneous, and rated low certainty by the authors' own GRADE assessment, with a confidence interval spanning −0.09 to 0.23. That is no evidence of benefit, not evidence of no benefit. The same discipline the vault applied to five underpowered nulls on resistance-training-programming and to the beta-alanine combination node (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy) applies here, and it applies even though the direction is convenient.

What is solid is the other end of the gradient, and it is the end that matters for this vault. Recreationally active adults carry the effect at high GRADE certainty — the only high-certainty rating anywhere in the paper. GOAL.md describes a recreationally active adult, not an athlete, so the population where the evidence is strongest is the relevant one. That is an unusual and favourable alignment; most findings in this vault come from populations David is not in.

Why a shrinking effect with training status is mechanistically ordinary rather than suspicious. Trained endurance athletes already have elevated nitric-oxide bioavailability and highly adapted vasculature; there is less headroom for an exogenous nitrate load to fill. The gradient is what a real, ceiling-limited effect looks like — which is a point in the finding's favour, and is recorded as such rather than as a defect.

The parallel the vault should keep in view. creatine-monohydrate holds the mirror image: creatine's lean-mass benefit exists only alongside resistance training and vanishes without it (creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training), and its strength effect replicates in trained athletes (creatine-strength-benefit-replicates-in-trained-athletes). Creatine needs the training; beetroot juice is displaced by it. Two supplements, two opposite relationships to the same behavioural lever — and in both cases the behaviour is the thing doing the work.
