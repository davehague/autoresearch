"7,000 steps" is a number plus the device that measured it

daily-steps recommends walking on the strength of ~7,000 versus ~2,000 steps/day tracking with roughly 47% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.53), pooled from 24 prospective cohorts (daily-steps-mortality-dose-response). Those cohorts used a mixture of devices and wear locations, and Paluch 2026 shows those devices disagree by up to 30% depending on where they sat (wrist-worn-step-counts-run-15-to-30-percent-above-hip-worn).

So the inflection the map reports near 5,000–7,000 steps is not a sharp physiological boundary. It is a threshold plus the measurement mix that produced it — and a wrist reading of 7,000 is plausibly a hip reading nearer 6,000. The biology did not acquire a round number; the instruments did.

This does not weaken the case for walking, and the distinction is worth being precise about. The exposure gradient is real and large, and misclassification of this kind blurs a dose-response curve rather than manufacturing one. What it weakens is treating any specific step count as a target to hit exactly — which is a conclusion daily-steps already reached from the opposite direction on 2026-07-08, when it recorded that "the culturally-mandated 10,000-step target is a marketing figure, not an evidence threshold." Tonight that scepticism gains a second, independent, purely metrological justification, and the two arguments do not share a premise.

⚠ The direction of the error is not neutral. Consumer wrist devices read high (consumer-wearables-and-pedometers-agree-within-ten-percent), and most people wear one. The everyday error therefore flatters — a person is somewhat more likely to be told they cleared a threshold than to have cleared the threshold as the cohorts measured it.

The right response is not a better threshold. It is to read the number less precisely: the map's value has always been walking is free, joint-friendly and strongly associated with living longer, and that survives a device disagreement intact. pushes-back on the precision, not on the lever, and no Status moves.

Graded suggestive — the underlying comparison design is unusually clean (within-person, simultaneous), but the headline figures are published as ranges without confidence intervals, so the size of the correction is directional rather than exact.
