The balance domain that decides whether a stumble becomes a fracture is the one nobody has adequately studied

The review splits balance into domains and reports a winner for each: Tai Chi for global, perturbation training for static, Pilates for dynamic, agility stepping or aquatic exercise for proactive. For reactive balance it reports only "tentative" signals — from balance-specific training, aquatic exercise and conventional resistance training — with no point estimate and no credible interval for any of them.

⚑ The inversion is the finding. Static balance is holding still, and it is what most instruments measure. Reactive balance is what the body does in the half-second after it is already falling — the trip on a kerb, the foot that catches, the recovery step. Reactive balance is the domain closest to the outcome anyone actually cares about, and in a network of 423 trials and 33,901 people it is the one the evidence could not support a claim about.

⚠ This is a gap in the literature, not a null. "Tentative" means too few trials, too heterogeneous, or too indirectly connected in the network to estimate — not that the modalities failed. The trial counts that would show which are behind the paywall.

⚑ Why it matters more than the rankings above it. The whole review measures a surrogate: balance performance, not falls and not fractures. The link from surrogate to outcome runs through the reactive domain — a person with excellent static balance who cannot execute a recovery step still falls. So the review's best-evidenced domains are the ones furthest from the endpoint, and its domain nearest the endpoint is the one it cannot speak to. That is a structural limit on how far any of this can be pushed, and it is why balance-training opens at emerging despite the largest evidence base in the vault.

The vault's small existing fall literature sits in exactly this gap and says the same thing. unstable-rt-reduces-fear-of-falling-not-falls — an intervention that changed how afraid people were of falling without changing how often they did. exergaming-lowers-fall-rate-without-lowering-the-number-of-fallers — fall rate moved and the number of people who fell did not. Both are the surrogate-to-endpoint gap showing up as an inconsistency in the results.
