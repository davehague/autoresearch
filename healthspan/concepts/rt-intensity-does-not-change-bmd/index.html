Training heavier does not detectably raise bone mineral density — but the null is underpowered

Comparing high-intensity (≥70% 1RM) against low-to-moderate-intensity resistance training in adults ≥50, a meta-analysis of 18 RCTs found no significant between-regimen difference in lumbar-spine BMD (SMD 0.28, 95% CI −0.02 to 0.58) or femoral-neck BMD (SMD 0.13, −0.08 to 0.33).

Read the lumbar interval before believing the null. It misses significance by two hundredths, and its upper bound (0.58) is a moderate effect. This is a failure to demonstrate a difference, not a demonstration of no difference — the distinction shared/study-appraisal.md treats as a fatal reading error when it is elided (fatal flag #3, an underpowered null sold as "no effect"). The authors' abstract elides it. This vault does not.

Why this matters for the vault. It completes a two-source convergence on a stubborn tissue. The vault already holds creatine-bone-bmd-null — creatine plus resistance training produces no greater BMD than resistance training alone (Forbes 2018, 5 RCTs) — and behind it creatine-high-dose-bone-indices, the thin, conflicted geometry claim that tried to rescue the higher dose. Now: doubling the training intensity produces no detectable BMD advantage either.

Two independent attempts to move bone density in a training older adult; two nulls. The honest synthesis is not "bone is unmovable" — both nulls are underpowered, and Shen's lumbar near-miss is exactly the kind of estimate a larger trial converts. The honest synthesis is: BMD is stubborn, the vault has twice failed to find a lever that moves it, and nobody should buy a supplement or add plates for their spine on this evidence. Whatever bone benefit resistance training confers appears to come from training at all, not from training heavy — and certainly not from creatine.

Bounded honestly (why single-study / stance: extends). The parent source is mixed, which would cap this at suggestive; it is capped further because the fatal-flag lens fires on precisely this outcome. An underpowered null cannot carry suggestive weight in either direction — it is closer to no information than to evidence of absence. Note the asymmetry the vault preserves: this concept is not permitted to strengthen the bone case, and it is not permitted to close it either.

The practical shape of the claim. Do not choose your training load for your skeleton. Choose it for strength (high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength) — where the same paper found a real effect — and respect the high-dose-risk flag that finding carries.
