Where someone lives is not an exposure you can adjust your way out of — and the corpus that claims to demonstrates the problem instead

Every study in this literature measures the same thing: how much vegetation a satellite sees around a person's home address. Nine of the 14 measure it at the same moment as the outcome. The exposure is therefore, in every case, also a measurement of housing cost, neighbourhood, and everything that travels with them.

The authors know this and say so — residual socioeconomic confounding is named as a principal limitation, on the grounds that "individuals with higher socioeconomic status are more likely to reside in greener environments and exhibit healthier behaviors." And then the same review rates 13 of its 14 studies "low risk of bias." Those two statements cannot both be right. The Newcastle–Ottawa Scale used for the rating is a lenient instrument, and grading cross-sectional studies of where people live as low risk of confounding is not credible on its face.

⚑ The result that makes the problem concrete rather than theoretical. In the CARDIA cohort (n = 482), greenness tracked slower GrimAge acceleration in White participants (β = −3.03 years per interquartile-range increase, 95% CI −5.63 to −0.43) and not at all in Black participants (β = −0.80, −4.75 to 3.13). The authors report that this persisted after socioeconomic adjustment and offer two candidate explanations: "differential access to high-quality greenspace" or "genetic ancestry."

The first of those is not an explanation of the finding. It is a restatement of the confounding. If the same satellite-measured greenness corresponds to different actual environments — a maintained park versus an unmaintained lot, a safe street versus an unsafe one — then the exposure variable is measuring different things in different populations, and no covariate in the model captures that. A stratified result in which the association appears in the more advantaged group and vanishes in the less advantaged one is precisely the shape residual structural confounding takes. Reading it as biology requires ruling that out first, and nothing in a cross-sectional design can.

Two further routes that stay open in this corpus. Reverse causation — healthier people move to, stay in and can afford greener neighbourhoods, so health precedes greenness rather than following it; shared/study-appraisal.md names this the "cart before horse" failure, and nine of 14 studies cannot exclude it by design. Healthy-mover selection — the people who leave declining neighbourhoods are systematically different from those who cannot. Neither is a hypothetical objection; both are the default explanation a place-based cross-sectional association has to beat.

What the studies did control for, credited honestly. Most adjusted for age, sex, smoking and socioeconomic status, and most accounted for cell-type composition in the blood samples — which matters, because a methylation signature can shift simply because the mix of white blood cells shifted. Several also adjusted for air pollution and proximity to major roads and reported the greenspace estimates unchanged, which addresses one specific alternative explanation properly. These are real adjustments and they are not sufficient for the claim being made.

⚑ The transferable rule this vault should carry, because it is not about trees. A place-based exposure is a bundle, and adjusting for the named components of a bundle does not isolate the one you are interested in. The same structure appears wherever this ledger reads observational lifestyle evidence — the reason fat-free-mass-mortality-association-observational is carried as a marker association rather than a protein argument, and the reason mind-diet-adherence-associates-with-cognition-post-randomization and null-diet-trial-recovered-by-adherence-subsetting are held at arm's length: people who do the healthy thing differ from people who don't, in ways the covariate list never fully names. shared/study-appraisal.md calls this healthy-user/adherer bias, and residential greenness is its purest form — the "user" did not even choose the exposure as a health behaviour.

Why the vault records this and coins no map. Greenspace was flagged as a candidate intervention and refused (the ninth such refusal, logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md) — partly because the signal fails at scale (greenspace-epigenetic-age-signal-fails-in-the-largest-pooled-cohort), and partly because of this note: an exposure that cannot be disentangled from socioeconomic position is not a lever a person pulls. The actionable version of the hypothesis already lives in the vault as a randomised comparison — green-exercise-mental-health-benefit-over-indoor-exercise on zone-2-cardio, outdoor exercise versus indoor exercise, which asks a question a participant can be assigned to.

Graded single-study. This is a methodological reading of one review's corpus, constraining what that corpus can support rather than establishing a finding of its own.
