"Non-inferior" is the headline; the confidence interval and the raw event count both point the other way

STREAM's primary-outcome confidence interval (-3.7 to -0.3 weeks of event-free time) does not cross zero — a statistically real difference favoring continuation, not a null — even though it was smaller than the trial's own pre-specified -5.2-week acceptability margin. The raw numbers behind that analysis show a composite-event rate of 12.7% after discontinuation versus 9.2% on continuation over a median 103-week follow-up, roughly 3.5 percentage points higher after stopping. Serious adverse events ran numerically higher after discontinuation too (43.0% vs 39.8%). None of this was hidden by the authors — the raw numbers are reported alongside the non-inferiority framing — but "non-inferior" undersells a result whose point estimate and confidence interval both ran against stopping.

Why this matters for the vault. statins-primary-prevention was coined on Bonnet 2026/SAGA-SITE, whose mortality-difference confidence interval straddled zero — a genuine null. STREAM's does not. This is a credible result pushing back against the map's founding "stopping did not raise risk" reading, and per shared/evidence-model.md moves the map to contested: the map's two best RCTs on its central question now disagree in direction, and the tension is stated rather than buried.
