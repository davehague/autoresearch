The only study in the corpus that varied cuff pressure found the benefit flat from 30% to 70%

Twenty healthy young men performed the same dynamic-stretching warm-up under four conditions — no cuff, and thigh cuffs at 30%, 50% and 70% of estimated arterial occlusion pressure — in a randomised crossover with a 48-hour washout. Countermovement jump height improved more with a cuff than without in all three occluded conditions (all p < 0.01): +5.80, +5.95 and +5.15 cm against +3.50 cm. The three pressures did not differ from each other, and the highest one produced the smallest gain.

Why a null on pressure is the useful result. blood-flow-restriction carries bfr-cuff-pressure-prescription-remains-unstandardised across eight sources and eight incompatible prescription conventions — percentages of limb or arterial occlusion pressure, flat absolute values from 50 to 350 mmHg, on cuffs of different widths that change how much occlusion a given pressure produces. The map's sharpest statement about this is that cuff pressure is the dose, and is the axis along which harm would scale, so a literature that cannot standardise it cannot bound its own risk. This is the first study in the corpus to vary pressure while holding the protocol, the cohort and the session constant. Across more than a doubling of pressure, the measured acute benefit is flat.

Two explanations, and the paper cannot separate them. Either the acute effect genuinely does not scale with occlusion over this range, or the estimated AOP — computed as (systolic blood pressure + 10 mmHg) / a thigh-circumference coefficient, never verified against Doppler ultrasound — did not deliver three distinguishable degrees of occlusion. The second is entirely plausible and the authors do not test it.

⚠ And the inference does not run in the reassuring direction. "Higher pressure buys nothing" is a statement about benefit only. This study, like the eight before it, collected no adverse-event data whatsoever. The one source in the corpus that brought a validated harm instrument found the 80%-occlusion arm's mental quality of life fall below the population norm against a sham cuff (bfr-at-80-percent-occlusion-lowered-mental-quality-of-life-against-sham). So: more pressure has not been shown to buy more, and the only proper harm count was taken above this range and came back negative. Both point the same way, and neither is a protocol. safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] stands.

Magnitude recorded, not adopted. A 3.50 cm jump gain from a warm-up alone — roughly 8% of baseline — rising to 5.95 cm with a cuff, is very large for a single session in a design with no passive control and four repeated jump tests. This is the familiarisation-shaped pattern the map flagged in Jelen 2026's 54% 1RM gains. The direction is kept; the numbers are not.
