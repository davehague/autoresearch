BFR does not improve the knee function patients actually report — and unlike the vault's other nulls, this one is informative

Self-reported knee function on the Anterior Knee Pain Scale: MD 1.93 (−0.52 to 4.38), P = 0.12, I² = 0%. Quadriceps morphology: also null (SMD 0.30, −0.20 to 0.80). The surrogate moved (bfr-reduces-patellofemoral-pain-during-loaded-tasks, and knee extensor strength SMD 0.28); the thing the patient would tell a physio did not.

This is the second independent BFR synthesis to find the functional endpoint flat while a surrogate moves. bfr-sport-performance-null found the same shape in athletes (sport-specific performance SMD 0.11, −0.18 to 0.40). Two meta-analyses, two different populations, two different functional instruments, both null. The vault holds a standing position on this shape — urolithin-a (strength up, function null) and nad-precursors (mechanism engaged, function null) were both read as watch, don't buy — and it applies here even though the intervention is one the vault would like to work.

And now the distinction that has been doing heavy lifting all week, applied in the direction that costs something. The vault has repeatedly refused to accept an underpowered null as evidence of equivalence — falls RR 2.68 with a CI reaching 11.11 sold as "similar safety" (rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered); four I²=0% nulls sold as "as effective" (rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered); a PSQI interval containing both zero and a meaningful benefit (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-unresolved). The rule cuts both ways, which means the vault also has to recognise an adequately informative null when one arrives.

This is one. The AKPS runs 0–100, and its minimal important change is conventionally around 8–10 points. Hou's interval runs −0.52 to 4.38 — the entire upper bound lies below a clinically meaningful improvement, at I² = 0% (the trials agree with each other). So this is not "we couldn't tell." It is: a large functional benefit is unlikely. Contrast Xu's sport-performance null, whose upper bound (0.40) was a genuinely meaningful effect and which the vault therefore refused to call a debunk. Same lens, different verdict, because the numbers are different — which is what having a lens rather than a preference means.

The authors explain their own null, and their explanation is the most useful sentence in the paper. They observe that other syntheses find combined PFPS interventions "often yield only small and non-significant between-group differences in Kujala scores, indirectly suggesting that functional improvement in PFPS may depend more on the overall quality of exercise delivery and adherence than on any single technique."

In plain terms: the exercise fixes the knee. The cuff makes the exercise hurt less while you do it. That reframing is what stops this map from being read as a strength shortcut, and it is why BFR is recorded as an adjunct (bfr-is-adjunct-not-substitute-for-heavy-load) rather than as the escape from the heavy-load knee gate that the vault went looking for.
