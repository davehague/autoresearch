Once resistance training resumes after disuse, creatine amplifies the recovery — a signal, not yet a pooled finding

In the subset of included trials that followed disuse with a resistance-based rehabilitation phase, creatine supplementation produced larger gains than placebo across every measured outcome: quadriceps cross-sectional area (+21.3% vs +14.3%), lower-limb lean mass (+5.6% vs +3.8%), power (+52.2% vs +35.2%) and strength (+28.7% vs +22.8%).

The shape of the finding matters as much as the numbers. Creatine did not protect muscle during immobilisation itself (creatine-does-not-preserve-muscle-size-during-strict-immobilisation-systematic-review) — but once training resumes, it appears to amplify the training response, exactly matching this map's standing rule that creatine's benefit requires an active training stimulus to act on (creatine-recovery-benefit-appears-only-once-loading-resumes, and the dose-response finding that creatine is null without resistance training — creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training).

Why suggestive, not strong. Two trials, no pooled effect size or confidence interval (narrative synthesis only), small samples within an already-small total (n=118 across all six included trials), and a young, healthy population rather than the older-adult or clinical-rehab population this question matters most for. The review's authors themselves call this "warrants further investigation," not an established finding. Appraised mixed.

For David, concretely. This is the closest population this vault holds to his own 2026 patellar contusion recovery. It does not change the map's standing recommendation (~5 g/day, additive to resistance training, no benefit during a non-training disuse period) — it modestly strengthens the case that resuming creatine once rehabilitation exercise resumes is worth considering, on now-systematic- review-level (if still small and unpooled) evidence rather than a single pilot RCT. No dose above the map's standing ~5 g/day is implied (shared/safety.md).
