Tai Chi raises bone density in osteopenia — and it does not contradict this vault's four bone nulls, it completes them

Among the twenty-three high-certainty associations in the umbrella review, one is Tai Chi-specific and skeletal: Tai Chi increased bone mineral density in participants with osteopenia or osteoporosis.

⚑ On its face this collides with the most consistent pattern in this vault. Four times now, a bone claim has been tested with the training held constant, and four times the answer was nothing:

  • creatine-bone-bmd-null — creatine plus resistance training gives no greater BMD than training alone, at any site (5 RCTs, all p > 0.25).
  • rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd — raising resistance-training intensity does not change BMD.
  • weighted-vest-adds-no-bone-density-once-the-exercise-is-controlled-for — adding a weighted vest to exercise you already do adds no bone benefit.
  • bone-density-network-ranks-sites-where-no-intervention-differed — a network meta-analysis ranking sites on differences that were not there.

The standing reading across all four was: loading the skeleton is what moves bone, and the accessories sold alongside the loading do not add to it.

⚑ Tai Chi is not an accessory. It is the loading. It is weight-bearing throughout, spends long periods in single-leg stance, and moves through slow, continuously-loaded eccentric transitions. Mechanically it belongs with moderate-intensity-resistance-training-raises-hip-bone-density-against-control — the vault's record of what does move bone — not with creatine or a vest. This finding does not break the rule; it is the rule seen from the other side, and it is the first non-resistance-training instance of it in the vault.

⚑ And the dose direction fits the mechanical story. The review's subgroup analyses found greater gains for osteoporosis with longer duration, higher frequency and moderate-to-high weekly volume — the accumulation pattern bone adaptation would predict, rather than an on/off supplement effect. ⚠ The thresholds are behind the paywall and this vault sets no dose (shared/safety.md).

⚠ The usual reservations, and one specific to bone. No effect estimate is retrievable, so the magnitude is unknown — and BMD is a surrogate: the endpoint that matters is fracture, which nothing here measured. This vault has already recorded a large literature ranking bone interventions on differences of no clinical consequence. A certain BMD gain of unknown size in osteopenia is not a fracture claim.

⚠ Population is people with diagnosed osteopenia or osteoporosis. Whether a skeletally healthy adult gains anything is a different question this source does not address.

⚑ Machine-measured, at least. Unlike the pain, function, mental-health and sleep findings elsewhere in this review, BMD is DXA-derived — the one high-certainty association here that cannot be moved by expectation effects. That makes it the most robust single finding in the source even though its magnitude is hidden.
