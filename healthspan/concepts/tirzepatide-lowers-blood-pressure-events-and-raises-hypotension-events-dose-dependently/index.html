Tirzepatide is quietly an antihypertensive, and the bill for that arrives as hypotension

Across 32 randomised trials and 47,332 participants, tirzepatide cut hypertension-coded adverse events to RR 0.40 (95% CI 0.26–0.60), p<0.001 — a 60% lower rate — and raised hypotension-coded events to RR 2.45 (1.35–4.45), p=0.003, worsening to 2.58 (1.38–4.81) at higher doses.

⚑ The dose gradient is what makes this read as pharmacology rather than noise. A harm that gets larger as the dose gets larger is behaving the way a drug effect behaves. Combined with random allocation in every contributing trial, this is the first randomised-tier harm evidence on glp-1 — everything else in its cost ledger (glp1-small-absolute-increase-in-ischemic-optic-neuropathy, tirzepatide-mortality-and-infection-signals-exceed-the-atherosclerotic-effect) is observational and carries an unresolved confounding-by-indication objection that randomisation removes by design.

⚠ The outcome is a coded adverse event, not a measured pressure. "Hypotension-related treatment-emergent adverse event" is what a trial recorded and coded — which could be syncope, or orthostatic symptoms, or a low reading at a scheduled visit. The source is abstract-bounded and the definitions are behind the paywall, so the direction is trustworthy and the magnitude is not pinned down. There are also no absolute event rates, so 2.45× has no baseline attached.

⚑ ⚠ INTERACTION — flagged on a credible mechanistic signal (shared/safety.md). exercise-for-blood-pressure records combined aerobic-plus-resistance training lowering systolic pressure by 9.52 mmHg (95% CI −13.47 to −5.56) at high GRADE certainty (combined-training-is-the-best-evidenced-exercise-lever-for-systolic-pressure), and isometric handgrip and moderate continuous training each lowering it by 5–6 mmHg. Those act through different mechanisms than an incretin agonist and are additive in direction. The combination's characteristic presentation — orthostatic symptoms standing up during or after a session — is measured by neither literature: exercise trials do not code drug hypotension events, and drug trials do not prescribe exercise. shared/safety.md is explicit that a mechanistic signal is enough to raise the flag, because a missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious one. The edge is recorded in both maps.

⚠ What this does not say. It does not say to avoid exercise on a GLP-1, and the vault holds no evidence for that position. It says the two levers push the same direction, that nobody has measured them together, and that they should therefore not be silently co-recommended — which is precisely what the across-protocol check in stages/05-refresh-regimen exists to catch.

⚑ And it splits the class. Semaglutide, in the same synthesis, was flat on both endpoints (semaglutide-is-blood-pressure-neutral-on-intervals-too-wide-to-prove-it). On this endpoint the two molecules differ in direction, not just in degree — so a claim about "GLP-1 receptor agonists and blood pressure" would be wrong about half its subject. ⚠ The comparison is indirect (each drug against its own trial controls, no head-to-head network), so the difference between 0.40 and 0.81 is suggestive of a real split rather than a measurement of one.

Status implication: none. No randomised efficacy result moved. glp-1 holds at consolidating; the cost ledger grows and gains its first randomised entry. Capped suggestive by the source's mixed appraisal.
