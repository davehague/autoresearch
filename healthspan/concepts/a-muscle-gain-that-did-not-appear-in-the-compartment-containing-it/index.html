Skeletal muscle mass rose 4.79 kg and fat-free mass did not move — the parts do not add up

Fat-free mass is everything in the body that is not fat: muscle, bone, organs, water. Skeletal muscle mass is its largest single component. The relationship is definitional, not empirical — skeletal muscle is inside fat-free mass.

This trial reports both, from the same device, at the same visits.

Outcome (between-group ANOVA on 8-week change)Result
Skeletal muscle massF = 12.02, P < 0.001 — combined arm +4.79 ± 2.42 kg vs +0.91 to +1.03 kg elsewhere
Fat-free mass⚠ F = 2.04, P = 0.126 — not significant
Body fat massF = 3.46, P = 0.026 (combined arm −1.49 kg)
Body fat percentageF = 2.79, P = 0.049

⚑ If one arm gained nearly five kilograms more skeletal muscle than the others, fat-free mass in that arm had to rise by at least as much, and the fat-free-mass test should have been at least as significant. It was not significant at all. Both results cannot be true of the same bodies.

What the discrepancy points to. Bioelectrical impedance does not measure muscle. It passes a small alternating current through the body, measures resistance and reactance, and then applies proprietary regression equations to partition the result — first into fat and fat-free mass, then fat-free mass into skeletal muscle, body cell mass, water and mineral. The partition step is where this result lives. ⚑ A shift in hydration or glycogen storage — routine after eight weeks of new resistance training, since glycogen binds roughly three grams of water per gram — moves impedance values and reshuffles the internal partition without total fat-free mass changing much. That produces exactly this signature: a large "skeletal muscle mass" change with a quiet fat-free mass.

The authors half-concede it. Their discussion notes that multifrequency impedance devices "may show systematic deviation relative to criterion methods such as DXA" and recommends that "future studies should confirm these findings using criterion-based body-composition methods or repeated-device reliability analyzes within the same laboratory." ⚠ They do not connect that caveat to the fat-free-mass null sitting three paragraphs earlier in their own results.

The magnitude is its own argument. ⚑ 4.79 kg of skeletal muscle in eight weeks in untrained young men is roughly three to five times what the best resistance-training literature produces, and this vault's best-evidenced supplement — creatine, across a 61-RCT dose-response meta-analysis — adds about 1.35 kg of fat-free mass alongside training (creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training). A dietary-reference-level dose of folic acid producing three and a half times creatine's effect, in eight weeks, in ten people, on an impedance device, is a measurement failure before it is a discovery.

⚑ The transferable check, which requires no statistics. When a body-composition result is reported, look for its sibling compartments and ask whether they moved consistently. Fat-free mass should contain skeletal muscle mass; body weight should roughly equal fat mass plus fat-free mass; a large gain in one and silence in another is an instrument artefact. And ask what measured it: impedance infers, DXA and criterion methods measure. The vault has caught this before (lean-mass-gain-measured-by-impedance-in-a-fasting-trial).
