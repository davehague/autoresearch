A third population, a third instrument, the same U — with a nadir at seven hours

Modelled continuously rather than in bands, nighttime sleep duration and all-cause mortality traced a U-shaped curve in 11,054 Chinese adults over 60 — p overall < 0.001, p for nonlinearity = 0.0017 — with the lowest risk at approximately 7 hours.

The shape is now the most replicated finding in this map, and it has been reached three different ways. sleep-duration was founded on a 79-cohort meta-analysis of self-reported duration (short-sleep HR 1.14, long-sleep HR 1.34). It was then re-measured in 76,811 UK Biobank adults wearing wrist accelerometers, which found the same inverse-J with an optimum at ~7.2 h by self-report and ~7.7 h by device (self-reported-sleep-duration-preserves-shape-loses-magnitude). This adds a nationally representative Chinese cohort, a different population, a different questionnaire, and a nadir in the same place. Different continents, different instruments, same curve.

What replicates is the shape. What does not replicate is any of the categorical numbers. After adjustment, none of the four non-reference bands reached significance in the overall sample — the ≥10 h estimate landed at HR 1.795 (0.986–3.267, p = 0.056), and the 8–10 h association present in the unadjusted model disappeared once covariates were added. So the continuous spline says the curve is real and the categorical table says this cohort cannot resolve where on it anyone sits. Both are true and they are not in conflict — splines borrow strength across the whole range, bands do not.

Capped single-study because the source is appraised unreliable, on a two-year follow-up that cannot separate causation from reverse causation for an exposure whose whole difficulty is reverse causation (shared/study-appraisal.md fatal flag #5). That verdict is symmetric and it applies here even though this finding is confirmatory: an unreliable study cannot move a map, including in the direction the map already leans.

What this does and does not add to the map's standing position. It adds generalisability — the curve is not an artefact of Western cohorts or of one questionnaire. It adds nothing to the causal question, because it uses the same instrument and a shorter window than the sources already held. And it leaves the map's two-tailed reading intact: the short-sleep tail is a modest, credible association, and the long-sleep tail remains most parsimoniously explained as illness causing sleep rather than sleep causing death (long-sleep-mortality-likely-reverse-causal).

The practical translation is unchanged, and deliberately not a number to hit. GOAL.md rejects health-as-a-second-job optimisation; sleep-regularity holds the better-measured, larger-effect lever. Anchor consistent sleep and wake times, and let roughly seven hours be the floor that falls out of doing so.
