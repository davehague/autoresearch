Chios mastic does not lower blood pressure in people whose hypertension is already well-controlled

A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (HYPER-MASTIC, n=106) gave low-dose (1,500 mg/day) or high-dose (2,800 mg/day) Chios mastic gum for 3 months to adults with already-well-controlled hypertension. The primary endpoint — 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure, the harder-to-game measurement — showed no significant between-group difference at either dose. Adherence was objectively confirmed via dose-dependent plasma masticadienonic/isomasticadienonic acid levels, ruling out non-adherence as the explanation. Oxidative-stress markers and PBMC/RBC proteasome activity also stayed flat.

A pre-specified high-cardiovascular-risk subgroup showed distinct transcriptomic changes in endothelial-junction/vascular-integrity pathways — exploratory and hypothesis-generating for a higher-risk population, not evidence this trial's largely low-risk sample experienced a vascular benefit. The authors' own explanation for the primary null is a ceiling effect: well-controlled hypertension with low baseline cardiovascular risk leaves little room to detect an antihypertensive signal even if one exists elsewhere.
