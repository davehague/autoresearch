Ranking first among options that do not differ is a statement about the ordering of noise

Zhang 2026 ranks resistance-training programmes by intensity and frequency across six skeletal sites using SUCRA — the surface under the cumulative ranking curve, a percentage summarising how often a programme lands near the top of the ordering across simulations. Moderate-intensity training three times a week ranked first at five of six sites: lumbar spine 80.3%, femoral neck 75.2%, total hip 68.2%, trochanter 86.0%, Ward's triangle 80.5%.

The paper's own closing sentence dismantles three of them. Verbatim: "no statistically significant differences were observed between interventions at the LS, TH, or WT, and these ranking results should be interpreted with caution." At the lumbar spine, total hip and Ward's triangle, no programme in the network beat any other programme. A SUCRA of 80.3% at a site where nothing differed from anything tells you which of several indistinguishable options happened to land highest, not which one works.

This is the sixth instance of the same defect in this vault, and the second where the authors caught it themselves. The class already spans exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported and glycemic-exercise-network-is-sparse-under-a-precise-leaderboard, supplement-outcome-specific-ranking-too-tidy-for-a-sparse-network, traditional-chinese-sports-ranking-is-unseparable-from-its-comparator and frailty-network-ranks-bundles-without-reporting-effect-sizes — the last of which is the extreme version, a leaderboard published with the effect sizes removed entirely. Zhang is the opposite case: the effect sizes are printed, and they are printed against the ranking. So is Jin-Hao 2026, whose authors concluded that "the current evidence does not support identifying a single best exercise approach across all outcomes" (sarcopenia-exercise-network-muscle-mass-result-is-locally-inconsistent).

The reading discipline, restated because it keeps earning its keep: read the confidence intervals or do not read the ranking. A ranking is a summary of an ordering; it carries no information about whether the ordered quantities differ. When a network paper publishes both, the intervals are the finding and the leaderboard is the abstract.
