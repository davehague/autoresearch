The vault's first harm-direction lipid signal from an eating schedule — small, isolated, and on the one outcome whose consistency could not be checked

What was found. Among 31 trials reporting LDL cholesterol, midday time-restricted eating raised it by 3.99 mg/dL (95% credible interval 0.43 to 7.90) against control. Midday also ranked lowest or second-lowest of seven nodes on total cholesterol (13%), LDL (16%) and triglycerides (11%) — a consistent bottom-of-the-table position across the lipid panel, in contrast to its unremarkable performance elsewhere.

⚠ Four reasons the vault records this as an open question rather than a harm.

  1. The interval nearly touches zero. Lower bound +0.43 mg/dL. A rounding error from non-significant.
  2. The magnitude is clinically small. ~4 mg/dL is about 0.1 mmol/L, roughly a twentieth of what stopping a statin produced in the same night's other ingest (ldl-rose-56-mg-dl-after-statin-withdrawal-without-a-mortality-signal recorded +56 mg/dL).
  3. ⚑ The check that would test it was structurally unavailable. The authors report that node-splitting "could not be applied to TC and LDL-C because no closed loops were present in the network" — meaning for these two outcomes there is no comparison where direct and indirect evidence can be compared at all. The estimate rests entirely on an untestable transitivity assumption.
  4. Eighteen outcomes across seven nodes with no multiplicity adjustment. Some intervals clearing zero by chance is expected.

⚑ Why record it anyway. Because the alternative is a ledger that only keeps the results pointing the way the map already leans. maps/intermittent-fasting currently sits at emerging with a belief that fasting is a delivery format for eating less; a small lipid harm attached to one specific window is the kind of thing that would be quietly dropped and then not be there when a second source arrives. The vault's rule is that Status moves both ways, which requires keeping the observations that could move it.

⚑ There is also a coherence argument in its favour that the paper does not make. If eating placement matters at all, the mechanism proposed everywhere in this literature is circadian alignment — insulin sensitivity and fat oxidation higher earlier. A midday window is the one that aligns worst with a normal breakfast and worst with an early dinner: it front-loads nothing and back-loads nothing. A midday window doing worse than both its neighbours on the lipid panel is at least the shape the hypothesis predicts. That is a reason to watch it, not evidence for it.

What would close it. A trial comparing midday against early windows with lipids as a pre-specified primary outcome and a connected network — or any synthesis in which the LDL network contains a closed loop.

Capped suggestive. stance: open-question — the map does not move, and no safety flag is raised: a 4 mg/dL LDL difference from a self-chosen eating schedule is not an interaction, a prescription or a high-dose risk under shared/safety.md.
