TREAD's cardiometabolic panel is a wall of nulls with one survivor — and the survivor is the least specific marker in medicine

TREAD moved body weight (tre-modest-weight-loss-vs-standard-counseling). It moved almost nothing else.

The glucose null is a pushes-back on this map's own incumbent claim. intermittent-fasting's only prior source — Kibret 2025, a 56-RCT network meta-analysis — ranked TRE best among fasting variants for fasting glucose (−3.74 mg/dL) (intermittent-fasting-improves-cvd-risk-factors). TREAD measured glucose, insulin and HbA1c directly, over 12 weeks, in the population most likely to move them, and every one of them was flat.

Per shared/evidence-model.md, Status moves both ways — but highest-tier-wins, and a single 12-week trial of 52 people does not overturn a 56-RCT network. So the map records the tension rather than resolving it, and downgrades its confidence in the specific glucose ranking rather than the whole claim. What this does settle is that the glucose benefit, if real, is not large or fast enough to show up in three months in the obvious population.

And the one marker that did move has a signature the vault already recognizes. sauna-heat: passive heating was null on FMD, arterial stiffness, glucose and lipids, with only a modest systolic-BP drop (−4.11 mmHg) (passive-heat-null-on-most-cardiometabolic-markers, passive-heat-modest-bp-drop-in-at-risk). Two completely unrelated interventions — sitting in a hot room, and not eating after 7pm — produce the identical result: blood pressure nudges, everything else sits still.

Which is worth stating as a general caution: systolic blood pressure is the easiest number in medicine to move and the least specific about why. It responds to weight, salt, sleep, heat, stress, hydration, posture, and the act of being in a trial. A lone SBP drop with a null glucose, lipid and inflammatory panel is not a cardiometabolic benefit — it is what a cardiometabolic panel looks like when not very much happened. The vault will treat an isolated SBP finding as weak evidence of anything beyond blood pressure, in any map.
