Fourteen trials, fourteen diagnoses, and nobody who was simply not sleeping well

The review did not filter for illness — the literature is just like that. Its eligibility criteria admitted "adults >18 years of age, regardless of sex or disease status." What came back was fourteen trials, every one in a diagnosed clinical population: breast and lung and mixed cancer (5), hemodialysis, coronary artery bypass graft, lung resection, restless legs syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis, fractures, epilepsy, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, obstructive sleep apnoea.

⚑ Two populations are entirely absent and they are the two that would matter most for a general reader. No trial in healthy adults. No trial in primary insomnia — that is, in people whose problem is sleep rather than a disease that disturbs sleep. So the intervention with the largest reported sleep effect in this vault has never been tested in anyone whose presenting complaint is poor sleep.

⚑ Why that changes what the effect can mean. In these populations, sleep is disrupted by pain, breathlessness, dialysis schedules, post-surgical recovery, treatment side-effects and disease-related anxiety. A relaxation technique plausibly acts on the anxiety and arousal layer of that stack — which the authors themselves propose, citing the "mediating effect of anxiety and depression" and noting that "all participants in this study had different degrees of illness, which can lead to depression and anxiety." ⚑ If the mechanism is anxiety reduction in the medically ill, the effect does not transfer to a healthy adult with a normal amount of life stress — and nothing here tests whether it does.

⚠ The nearest available proxy points the wrong way. The four trials with mean age ≥55 — the subgroup closest to a middle-aged adult without an acute illness — pooled to −1.17 (−2.67 to +0.32), not clearing zero. ⚠ That is not a demonstration of no effect: the formal subgroup-difference test was null (χ² = 0.17, p = 0.679) and four trials cannot resolve much. But it is the only slice of this evidence base nearest a general reader, and it is the slice that does not hold.

⚑ This is a load-bearing reason the candidate progressive-muscle-relaxation map was refused. GOAL.md describes a healthy adult; shared/evidence-model.md records study_population as a neutral fact precisely so that population-match can be computed at regimen time. A map whose entire evidence base sits outside the reader's population would compute a population match of zero on every item it held.

What would close it. A randomised trial of progressive muscle relaxation in healthy adults or primary insomnia, with an active comparator and an objective sleep measure. Logged to docs/open-gaps-log.md.
