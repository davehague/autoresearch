The practice effect is large and it cancels — provided there is a randomised comparator taking the same test

The demonstration. Having quantified a retest effect roughly equal to one year of cognitive ageing, the authors refitted the parent trial's own treatment comparison with and without adjusting for it. The estimated trajectories moved — adjusted curves sit lower, meaning the raw ones flatter everybody — and the intervention-minus-control difference did not. Verbatim: "Accounting for retest differences increased model-estimated cognitive levels over follow-up but did not meaningfully alter intervention-control contrasts, suggesting repeated testing influenced observed cognitive trajectories more than treatment effects."

⚑ The mechanism is simple and it is the whole point. Both arms sat the same battery on the same schedule. A bias applied equally to two groups subtracts out of the difference between them. The retest-by-intervention interaction confirms it directly: +0.03 (−0.02 to 0.09) minimally adjusted, +0.05 (−0.03 to 0.14) fully adjusted, 0.00 (−0.08 to 0.09) under the alternative parameterisation. Three specifications, three nulls — the practice effect did not differ between arms.

⚑ The reading rule this produces, which is what the vault takes from it.

Study shapeIs the cognitive change contaminated by practice?
Randomised, both arms tested identicallyNo — it cancels in the between-group contrast
Randomised, but arms tested at different frequenciesYes — the asymmetry is the bias
Waiting-list or no-treatment controlLikely — the control group often engages less with the task material between assessments
Single-arm / before-and-after / open programmeYes, fully — roughly a year's worth of "improvement" is the test
Any "participants improved by X" sentence without a comparatorYes, fully

⚑ This is the missing mechanism under a complaint this vault has filed repeatedly. cogtrail-executive-benefit-rests-on-a-waiting-list-comparator and exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator both record benefits measured against inert controls. The vault's argument was expectancy and attention. This paper supplies a second, entirely non-psychological channel: testing exposure. A waiting-list group is not only unblinded and unmotivated — in a cognitive trial it is often the group with less practice at the tasks, and that alone is worth about a year of measured decline.

⚠ The bound. This is one trial, in one population, with an intervention (hearing aids) whose effect on cognition was small or absent to begin with — so "the contrast did not move" is easier to demonstrate when there is not much contrast. A trial with a genuine cognitive treatment effect and asymmetric task exposure (say, brain training against a waiting list, where the intervention is practice) is exactly the case this analysis cannot speak to. ⚑ Note the irony precisely: for cognitive training specifically, the practice effect and the intervention are partly the same thing, which is why cognitive-training is the right home for this note and also the map it least reassures.

Capped suggestive. stance: confirms — it confirms that the vault's randomised cognitive evidence is not undermined by re-sitting-a-cognitive-battery-buys-back-about-a-year-of-decline, while sharpening why its uncontrolled evidence is.
