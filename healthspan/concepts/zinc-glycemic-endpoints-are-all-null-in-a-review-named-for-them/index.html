A review titled for glycemic control found nothing on any measure of blood sugar

Eighteen randomised trials, 1,023 participants with diabetes or prediabetes, pooled under the title "Effects of Zinc Supplementation on Glycemic Control, Insulin Resistance, Inflammation and Oxidative Stress in Diabetes."

Every direct measure of blood sugar was null:

OutcomeStudiesPooled effectGRADE
Fasting plasma glucose5−4.35 mg/dL (−10.80 to 2.11), p = 0.19low
Fasting blood glucose5−0.61 (−1.83 to 0.60), p = 0.32, I² = 95%low
HbA1c4+0.04 pp (−0.26 to 0.35), p = 0.78very low

⚑ HbA1c is the one that settles it. HbA1c is the standard three-month measure of glycemic control; the review's own stated reference frame puts normal below 5.7% and diabetes at or above 6.5%. A mean difference of +0.04 percentage points is not a small effect — against that scale it is indistinguishable from nothing, and it is rated very low certainty on top.

⚠ It is a null, not a demonstration of equivalence, and the authors say so. Their limitations note that the small number of studies for fasting glucose and HbA1c "limited statistical power and may have increased the risk of type II error, precluding exclusion of small but potentially clinically relevant effects." This is honest reporting, and it is why shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 was weighed and did not fire.

⚑ What "improved insulin resistance" actually rests on. The conclusion's positive claim comes from two surrogate indices, not from blood sugar: serum insulin −2.50 µU/mL (−4.69 to −0.31) at I² = 98%, and HOMA-IR −1.10 (−2.05 to −0.15) at I² = 79%. An I² of 98% means essentially all the between-trial variation is genuine disagreement rather than chance — the trials do not agree, and the pooled number is an average across studies answering different questions. The authors concede pooled estimates "should be interpreted as average effects across heterogeneous populations rather than precise estimates."

⚠ And both effects dissolve in the longer trials. HOMA-IR was significant at 6–11 weeks (−1.49, −2.57 to −0.41) and not significant at 12–26 weeks (−0.75, −2.20 to 0.69). The same pattern appears for total cholesterol. A metabolic benefit that shows up in short trials and disappears in longer ones is the shape of a transient or a small-study artefact, not of a durable effect.

⚑ The gap between the surrogate and the endpoint is the finding. Insulin and HOMA-IR are calculated indices of insulin sensitivity; HbA1c is what glycemic control means. The indices moved, at extreme heterogeneity and only in short trials; the thing they are supposed to predict did not move at all. When a surrogate and its endpoint disagree, the endpoint wins.

⚠ And nothing here isolates zinc anyway — several trials co-administered magnesium, vitamin D, calcium, curcumin or vitamin A (zinc-trials-cannot-isolate-zinc-and-the-magnesium-result-shows-why).
