Exercise plus CBT is the largest, most certain sleep effect in this vault — and it contains no supplements

A network meta-analysis of 31 RCTs (n=2,457 adults with insomnia) finds exercise combined with cognitive behavioural therapy (EX+CBT) beat conventional control on three of four sleep outcomes with intervals that exclude zero:

OutcomeEX+CBT vs control
PSQIMD −2.80 (−4.15 to −1.45) — CINeMA certainty: MODERATE
Sleep onset latency−6.95 min (−10.79 to −3.12)
Wake after sleep onset−32.62 min (−62.10 to −3.13)
Total sleep timenot significant — and no intervention in the network moved it

That PSQI moderate-certainty rating is the highest certainty any sleep intervention has achieved in this vault. Set it against the shelf it lands on. l-theanine's highest-tier synthesis (31 RCTs, Molecular Psychiatry) pools no sleep endpoint at all. chamomile's benefit carries I² = 88%. melatonin's +12 minutes was measured in cognitively impaired elders. magnesium-glycinate's signal is d = 0.2, small and self-reported. The strongest sleep lever in the Foundations, sleep-regularity, is observational.

The best-evidenced sleep intervention in this vault is a skill plus a walk, and there is nothing to buy.

Why it does not promote cbt-insomnia anyway — three reasons, and the first is structural.

(1) There is no CBT-alone node in the network. Every CBT arm is CBT plus exercise or qigong. So this paper demonstrates that CBT-combined-with-exercise beats control; it cannot isolate what CBT-I does on its own — which is precisely the gap the map was coined holding open (cbt-sleep-effect-not-isolable-no-cbt-alone-node). The vault does not import claims it has not ingested.

(2) The population is insomnia patients, diagnosed by DSM-5 / ICSD-3 / validated cut-offs. GOAL.md describes David's sleep as a foundation to protect, not a problem to treat. This is the same hold that caps melatonin, ashwagandha and blood-flow-restriction (cbt-i-population-is-insomnia-patients).

(3) The network is shakier than the headline. I² = 91.7% on PSQI; significant local inconsistency by node-splitting in three comparisons — including EX+CBT vs QG+CBT, i.e. the incoherence sits directly on the winning node; most comparisons rate low or very low certainty; outcomes are overwhelmingly subjective, and the authors concede objective measurement "was not consistently available."

What the source gets right, and it is worth naming. This is the same SUCRA-ranking network design the vault appraised unreliable eight days ago in Jiao 2026 (exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported) — and Zhou handles it correctly. The head-to-heads between active arms are mostly non-significant, and the authors say so: "Ranking results were interpreted together with effect estimates and their 95% CIs rather than being used as the sole basis for determining intervention superiority," and, of the TST rankings, "none of the pairwise comparisons reached statistical significance; therefore, these ranking results should be interpreted cautiously." The leaderboard is still noise. The authors simply decline to sell it, and anchor on the comparison the data support. That is the sentence Jiao should have written.

Status of cbt-insomnia: emerging, unchanged. The hold narrows — the vault now has sleep-outcome evidence for CBT in combination — and does not lift.
