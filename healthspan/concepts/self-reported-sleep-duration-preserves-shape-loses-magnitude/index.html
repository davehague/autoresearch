Self-reported sleep gets the curve right and the effect size wrong

Measured in the same 76,811 people, self-report and wrist accelerometry produced the same shape of association between sleep duration and health — an inverse J for mortality, CVD, diabetes and dementia, a U for depression — and put the optimum in nearly the same place: ~7.2 h by self-report against ~7.7 h by device sleep period time. What differed was strength. Against a 5-hour reference, seven hours of sleep carried HR 0.70 (0.61–0.79) on device-measured sleep period time and only HR 0.86 (0.74–1.00) on self-report — an interval whose upper bound sits exactly on the null.

This is measurement-error attenuation, quantified in one population. A noisy exposure biases an association toward nothing; the questionnaire is the noisy instrument, so it finds the smallest effect. The useful consequence is that self-report is unreliable about magnitude while being roughly trustworthy about shape and location.

What this does and does not do to sleep-duration. That map was opened with an explicit measurement penalty — its founding 79-cohort meta-analysis rests on self-reported exposure, and the vault positioned it beneath sleep-regularity partly on that basis. This partly rehabilitates those cohorts: the shape of short-sleep-under-7h-mortality-risk is not an artifact of asking people to estimate. But it also implies every self-reported estimate the vault holds is an underestimate of whatever the real association is — which cuts both ways, since an underestimated association in an observational design is still an association, not a demonstrated cause.

It leaves the other objection untouched. Nothing here addresses reverse causation — no early-death exclusion or lag analysis is reported — so long-sleep-mortality-likely-reverse-causal stands exactly as it was. And the map's subordination to regularity was never mainly about instruments: it is about sleep-regularity-predicts-mortality carrying a larger effect (20–48%) on objective accelerometry. Sleep is still counted once.

Capped suggestive; Status held emerging on sleep-duration.
