Responsiveness is a prerequisite for a surrogate endpoint, not a demonstration of one — and nobody has defined what a meaningful change is

The entire commercial and scientific case for epigenetic clocks is that they let you skip the decade. If a clock stands in for lifespan or healthspan, a two-year trial can substitute for a twenty-year one. That substitution is only legitimate if three conditions hold together:

LegQuestionState of the evidence in this vault
1. PrognosticDo people with worse values go on to do worse?Partly. Five of fourteen consensus biomarkers predicted mortality in BASE-II; whole-panel C-index 0.65 (most-consensus-aging-biomarkers-did-not-predict-mortality, aging-biomarker-panels-discriminate-mortality-weakly)
2. ResponsiveDoes intervening move it?Partly. Five of sixteen clocks moved reliably across 51 interventions, at 0.06–0.09 SD; 26 of 51 interventions moved nothing; in healthy people nothing survived correction (this source)
3. SurrogateDoes moving it move the outcome?⚑ Untested. By anyone.

⚑ This paper is the first serious attempt at leg two, and its authors are explicit that clearing it does not clear leg three. Verbatim from the discussion: "Although it is still unclear whether short-term biomarker changes correspond to long-term improvements in disease, healthspan or lifespan..." And from the limitations, the sentence that should be quoted whenever a clock result is offered as a health claim: "the absence of an empirically defined minimal clinically important difference for DNAm clocks remains a key barrier to their use as surrogate endpoints; future work must anchor clock changes to clinically meaningful functional, physiological or disease-specific outcomes."

There is no threshold. Not "a small threshold," not "a contested threshold" — none has been defined. So a reported movement of −0.089 standard deviations cannot be called meaningful or trivial, because nobody has established what either word would mean on this instrument. The authors' own offered conversion — that ~0.2 SD corresponds to roughly one year of epigenetic age on clocks whose age-deviation standard deviation runs 4–6 years — is a units translation, not a clinical anchor. Knowing a number equals five months of epigenetic age does not tell you whether five months of epigenetic age is worth anything.

⚠ A fourth problem the authors raise against their own best performers. Generation 2+ clocks were trained on composite risk factors — GrimAgeV2 includes C-reactive protein — so "observed responsiveness may reflect the modulation of intermediate health or inflammatory states rather than direct effects on aging biology per se." The clocks that respond best may be responding as inflammation proxies, which is a different and much less interesting claim than measuring aging.

Why this note exists. biological-age-clocks was coined to replace the word "surrogate" with numbers. This is the number that turned out to be missing: there isn't one. The map's operating rule — predictor ≠ responder — is now joined by its completion: predictor + responder ≠ surrogate. A vault that holds epigenetic-age results on omega-3, vitamin-d, zone-2-cardio and multidomain-lifestyle should read every one of them through this note.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal.
