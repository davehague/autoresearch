The quality-of-life gains were measured in the people who stayed on the drug — and GLP-1s are stopped because they make people feel bad

SURMOUNT-5's HRQoL analysis used on-treatment data from participants who received ≥1 dose at their maximum tolerated dose. It is not an intention-to-treat analysis.

shared/study-appraisal.md names this directly: per-protocol or completer analysis that conceals non-random dropout reintroduces adherer bias and sits on the fatal-flag list. Whether it is fatal depends on whether the dropout is random. Here it demonstrably is not.

The dropout in this drug class is symptom-driven, quantified, and pointed at exactly this endpoint. The vault already holds SELECT's number: discontinuation for adverse events 10% vs 2% on placebo, driven by gastrointestinal symptoms (glp1-gi-discontinuation-rct-select). Nong 2026 prices it across the class at RR 1.9–4.2 for AE discontinuation, with GI events RR 3.1–4.2 and fatigue up to RR 8.9 (obesity-drug-benefit-harm-tradeoff). Nausea, vomiting and fatigue are not incidental to quality of life — they are quality of life, and the SF-36v2 has domains for precisely them (Vitality, General Health, Bodily Pain).

So the analysis excludes, by construction, the people whose quality of life the drug damaged most. A person who felt terrible and stopped at Week 20 does not contribute a Week-72 score. What remains is a population selected for tolerating the drug, asked how they feel. The resulting estimate is biased upward by an amount nobody can compute from the abstract, and the direction of the bias is not in question.

Combined with the absence of a placebo arm, the two biases compound. One removes the comparison that would subtract expectation (glp1-qol-improvement-uncontrolled-no-placebo-arm); the other removes the participants who would drag the mean down. Both push the same way. This is why the vault reads the positive findings as upper bounds and reads the MCS null — which survived both pressures — as the paper's most trustworthy output (glp1-physical-qol-improves-mental-qol-flat).

Symmetry note, honestly. On-treatment analysis is defensible for a question like "what does this drug do for someone who can take it?", which is a real clinical question and arguably the one a patient already tolerating a GLP-1 is asking. The vault's objection is not that the analysis is illegitimate — it is that the paper's title and conclusion generalise beyond it, to quality of life in adults with obesity, which is a population that includes the roughly one in ten who stop.
