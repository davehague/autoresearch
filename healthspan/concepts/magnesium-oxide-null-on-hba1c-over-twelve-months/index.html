A year of magnesium, exposure verified, and the blood sugar did not move

Two hundred and forty-seven adults with type 2 diabetes took magnesium oxide 500 mg (302 mg elemental) or placebo, double-blind, for twelve months. The primary outcome — HbA1c — was null: median −0.30% vs −0.05%, p = 0.145, reported in the intention-to-treat table as −0.25% vs 0.00%, p = 0.181. Fasting glucose moved slightly (−0.4 vs 0 mmol/L, p = 0.035). Blood pressure and every lipid fraction were flat.

⚑ The feature that makes this null worth having: the trial demonstrated it delivered the compound. Both ionised and total magnesium were measured, and magnesium status improved. A supplement trial that verifies exposure and still misses its endpoint has told you something a trial with unverified exposure cannot. The same night's cold-exposure trial is the contrast — it found nothing and could not confirm the stimulus ever reached the tissue (gel-pack-cooling-may-not-deliver-a-thermal-dose-to-deep-muscle).

⚑ This is the second independent, higher-tier null on magnesium's metabolic case, and the two agree. magnesium-null-on-insulin-resistance already carried Amiri 2026 — 15 RCTs, 1,085 adults with diabetes or pre-diabetes, null on insulin (p = 0.09) and HOMA-IR (p = 0.08), with dose and duration subgroups failing to rescue it. That is a meta-analysis-rct; this is a single rct, and it lands on the same answer from a different marker over a longer horizon. Graded confirms for exactly that reason. Together they close the metabolic question about as far as evidence of this quality can: magnesium is not a metabolic lever.

⚠ The honest limit, and it is large. The trial was powered to detect a 1.0% absolute HbA1c reduction — the scale of adding a glucose-lowering drug — and observed about a quarter of that (magnesium-glycemic-null-is-powered-for-an-implausible-effect). So the claim is "no drug-sized effect," not "no effect." Attrition was 21.5% and differential by arm (magnesium-trial-dropout-was-differential-by-arm).

⚠ Form and outcome both differ from what this map is about, and neither transfers. The trial used magnesium OXIDE, one of the least bioavailable oral salts, in long-standing type 2 diabetes (median 16 years), on a metabolic endpoint. magnesium-glycinate's claim is about the glycinate form and mild insomnia (magnesium-bisglycinate-insomnia-rct, magnesium-sleep-quality-weak-signal). Nothing here supports or undermines the sleep signal. It lands on this map because the map already carries general-magnesium evidence, and because coining a third magnesium map off one null trial would be proliferation — the reasoning is stated on the map, not assumed.
