Semaglutide came out flat on blood pressure, and flat here means unmeasured rather than equal

In the same synthesis that found tirzepatide moving blood-pressure events sharply in both directions, semaglutide moved neither:

EndpointRR (95% CI)p
Hypertension-related events0.81 (0.57–1.15)0.233
Hypotension-related events1.39 (0.81–2.36)0.232

⚠ Read the intervals, not the p-values. Hypertension runs from 0.57 to 1.15 — compatible with a 43% reduction and with a 15% increase. Hypotension runs from 0.81 to 2.36 — compatible with a 19% reduction and with a more than doubling. Neither is a demonstration of no effect; both are demonstrations that the question is open. The upper bound on the hypotension interval (2.36) sits close to tirzepatide's point estimate (2.45), which is the cleanest way to see that this data cannot rule out the two drugs behaving similarly.

⚑ The distinction that matters: a null result and a demonstration of equivalence are different things. Showing "no difference" requires a pre-specified equivalence or non-inferiority margin and enough events to exclude a meaningful difference on both sides. This analysis has neither, and its authors are appropriately careful — they call the profile "relatively neutral" and flag unreported "potential protective signals … in high-dose subgroups" rather than claiming semaglutide is blood-pressure-safe.

⚑ Why the vault records a null at all. Because the comparison against tirzepatide is the point (tirzepatide-lowers-blood-pressure-events-and-raises-hypotension-events-dose-dependently), and half of a contrast recorded alone becomes a claim about a class. Without this note, the map would carry "GLP-1s raise hypotension risk 2.5-fold," which is false for one of the two molecules it is most likely to be applied to. ⚠ And the comparison is indirect — each drug against its own trial controls, no head-to-head — so what is established is that the tirzepatide signal was found and the semaglutide one was not, in datasets of unstated relative size.

⚑ Where it leaves the interaction flag. The additive-hypotension interaction with exercise-for-blood-pressure is flagged on the tirzepatide concept, not the class. On this evidence, semaglutide neither earns the flag nor is cleared of it — the interval is too wide to clear anything. Recorded so a later run does not read the absence of a flag here as a safety finding.
