Resistance training ranks first for both handgrip strength and fat mass in sarcopenic obesity

A prospectively registered (PROSPERO CRD420251270452) network meta-analysis of 24 RCTs, 1,298 older adults with sarcopenic obesity, ranking nine exercise- and nutrition-based strategies with RoB 2 and CINeMA grading, puts resistance training first on two of the five outcomes:

OutcomeRT effect vs usual careSUCRARunner-up
Handgrip strengthMD +3.96 (95% CI 2.15–5.77)90.9%multicomponent training, MD +2.13 (0.25–4.01), 59.3%
Fat massMD −2.30 (95% CI −3.63 to −0.98)79.0%no other strategy reached significance

Multicomponent training took the other two significant outcomes — BMI (MD −1.08, −1.86 to −0.29; SUCRA 85.1%) and percentage body fat (MD −3.53, −5.70 to −1.36; SUCRA 77.1%), with RT second on PBF.

Why this extends rather than merely confirms resistance-training. The vault's existing resistance-training evidence sits largely in healthy or general older-adult populations. Sarcopenic obesity is a distinct and harder clinical phenotype — low muscle and high fat simultaneously — where the two treatment goals normally pull against each other (caloric restriction costs muscle; anabolic feeding costs fat). That one intervention took both the strength outcome and the fat outcome in the same network is the substantive finding: RT is the strategy that does not force the trade-off.

Ranking recorded, leaderboard declined. Per the vault's standing SUCRA discipline, a SUCRA of 90.9% is a probability of ranking well within this network, not an effect size and not a head-to-head win. The paper itself calls for "direct head-to-head comparisons," conceding the ordering is largely indirect. What is carried forward is the effect estimate and its interval, not the rank.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, capped). The source's reliability verdict is mixed, capping strength per shared/evidence-model.md. Every outcome here is a surrogate over 3–9 months — no fall, fracture, hospitalisation, or mortality anywhere in the network. Grip strength is a predictor, not an event, and the vault has independently ingested evidence this run that grip does predict mortality (dunedinpace-strongest-mortality-predictor-among-consensus-biomarkers) — but predicting is not the same as an intervention-shifted grip conferring the benefit. Blinding is structurally impossible, and grip is effort-dependent, so expectancy runs in the intervention's favour on its most decisive win. CINeMA certainty is majority-moderate for grip (68%) but only 44% moderate + 6% high for fat mass.
