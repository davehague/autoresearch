One diet score, two significant results, and they cannot both be true

In the same 14,776 people over the same sixteen years, the overall plant-based diet index was associated with 27% lower coronary heart disease mortality (HR 0.73, 0.59-0.90, p-trend 0.001) and 60% higher incident coronary heart disease (HR 1.60, 1.09-2.33, p-trend 0.009), comparing highest to lowest quintile. Both intervals exclude one.

⚑ No dietary mechanism produces this pair. A pattern that genuinely caused far more coronary disease would need a spectacular conditional survival advantage to also cause less coronary death, and none is proposed. The remaining explanations are all methodological, and the paper does not choose between them:

  • Ascertainment. Incident CHD is adjudicated from surveillance, hospital records and participant contact; CHD death is adjudicated from death records. Detection bias produces exactly this signature — more diagnosed disease in people better connected to the health system, less death from it.
  • Competing risk. With 944 deaths and 1,047 incident events over sixteen years, whether death was handled as a competing risk decides the sign of the incidence estimate. Not retrievable (paywall).
  • Who eats this way. The excess incidence was confined to non-rural participants (interaction p = 0.006). ⚑ A dietary "effect" present in cities and absent in the countryside is describing the exposure's social correlates, not the food.

⚠ Graded single-study, below the source's suggestive cap. A self-contradicting estimate cannot be suggestive of a direction. What it is good evidence for is a claim about the literature rather than about food: had either row been published alone, it would have travelled as a finding.

⚑ The contrast with the healthy-plant index is the interpretable part. hPDI, which weights plant foods by quality, behaved consistently (only-the-quality-weighted-plant-based-index-behaved-consistently); PDI, which counts any plant food including juice, refined grain and sweets, contradicted itself. That is direct evidence the signal lives in food quality, not in plant content.
