"Visceral fat loss rather than weight loss" is a causal headline resting on 31 events and an adherence confound

Circulation's title says: "Visceral fat loss rather than weight loss emerges as a key target for durable cardiometabolic health." The number behind it: each 10% intervention-induced visceral-fat loss was associated with a 28% lower risk of incident type 2 diabetes (HR 0.72, 0.54–0.94).

That is not a randomized result, and the distinction is the whole ballgame.

What it actually is: an exposure-response association within the pooled cohort. Everyone — both trials, all arms — is thrown into one Cox model, and the model asks: did the people who lost more visceral fat get less diabetes? It does not ask: did the arm that was randomized to the intervention get less diabetes?

Which means healthy-adherer bias is fully live, and it is the vault's named lens. The people who lost the most visceral fat during an 18-month diet-and-exercise program are, definitionally, the people who adhered hardest — and adherence predicts later health through a hundred channels that have nothing to do with adipose tissue. shared/study-appraisal.md lists this under "Healthy-user / adherer bias — RED: compares high vs. low adherence/use, not a true non-user control." Strip the adherence confound out and there may be nothing left of the visceral-fat mechanism at all.

And the structural tell: they had a randomized comparison and did not report it. The paper does not present long-term outcomes by trial arm. It had two RCTs, with arms, and it shows you a pooled exposure- response instead. The vault called exactly this shape on Look AHEAD three days ago — "when a paper leads with a stratified result and never shows you the pooled one, the pooled one is usually null" — and here the same tell appears in a better journal, on the same night the POINTER analysis produced its own version of it (multidomain-lifestyle-attenuates-white-matter-free-water-only-under-seventy). Twice in one run, in two of the most prestigious venues in medicine.

Two further reasons the estimate is fragile. (1) Thirty-one events. That is a very small number of outcomes to carry a multivariably-adjusted Cox model; HR 0.72 has a lower bound (0.94) close to 1 and would move on a handful of reclassifications. (2) Reverse causation is not excluded. Incipient metabolic disease could both blunt a person's fat loss during the trial and cause their later diabetes — manufacturing this exact association with no causal arrow running from fat to disease.

What the vault will and will not take from this. It will take abdominal-fat-loss-partially-persists-despite-weight-regain — the randomized-origin, MRI-measured, 96%-retained observation that abdominal fat stays partly reduced after the weight comes back. It will not take "visceral fat is the causal target" as an established fact. The two findings are in the same paper and they do not have the same evidentiary status, and this vault does not grade them as if they do.
