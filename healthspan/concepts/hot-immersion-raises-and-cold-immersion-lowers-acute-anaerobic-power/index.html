Cold immersion costs about 2.4% of anaerobic power within minutes; hot immersion adds about 2.2%

In a Wingate test performed minutes after immersion, cold-water immersion reduced mean anaerobic power by 2.4% and hot-water immersion raised it by 2.2% — each significant against passive control (p < 0.05) and against each other (p < 0.0001). In the only included study measuring 20 m sprint speed at 21–45 h post-match, neither modality accelerated recovery (p = 0.90).

This independently reproduces a finding the vault ingested three days earlier, with a different instrument. Zhu 2026 (22 RCTs, n = 375, time-stratified) found cold impaired countermovement jump immediately post-exercise at g = −0.68 (−1.16 to −0.20), resolving by 24–48 h (cold-water-immersion-transiently-impairs-explosive-power). Different authors, different design, different test — same direction, same timescale, and here with a symmetric positive for heat. The proposed mechanism is muscle temperature and contraction kinetics: cooling slows them, warming speeds them. Recorded, not endorsed.
