The pooled compression-garment soreness estimate runs from moderate harm to large benefit — that is an absence of a result, not a null

At the primary 24-hour window, wearing a compression garment after endurance exercise gave a pooled effect on muscle soreness of Hedges' g = 0.62, 95% CI −0.57 to 1.82, I² = 74.0%, from four studies. At the immediate-to-2-hour window, g = 0.38 (−0.67 to 1.42), I² = 51.0%, from three.

Read the interval, not the point estimate. A g of 0.62 is a moderate-to-large benefit. An interval from −0.57 to 1.82 is compatible with compression making soreness moderately worse, with it doing nothing, and with it helping a great deal. This is not a demonstration that compression does not work. It is a demonstration that this evidence base cannot tell.

⚑ The width is honest, not sloppy. The reviewers used Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman inference, which deliberately widens confidence intervals when few studies are pooled — precisely to stop four small trials from producing a falsely confident number. A conventional random-effects model over the same four studies would have produced a narrower interval and a more quotable result. The method was chosen to avoid that, and this is what it returned.

⚠ The base is 158 participants across nine trials — a median of about fourteen — with 10 of 11 outcome-level results rated high risk of bias on RoB 2, and overall certainty rated very low. Outcome measurement was the dominant high-risk domain, "particularly for subjective soreness outcomes assessed under unblinded or poorly blinded conditions."

⚑ The one trial that isolated the actual claim found nothing. Bieuzen 2014 separated compression worn during running from compression worn only during recovery, and coded the recovery-only arm at g = −0.08 at 1 hour and −0.05 at 24 hours — while the trial's broader positive finding concerned the during-exercise condition. The garment may do something; the claim that wearing it afterwards is what does it is the weakest part of the package, and it is the part the retail category is built on.

⚑ And where soreness and damage were measured in the same trial, only soreness moved. Hill 2014 found 24-hour soreness dropping sharply on compression (31.0 → 13.9 mm) while the sham arm rose (26.8 → 36.4), with no significant group effect on maximal voluntary contraction, creatine kinase or C-reactive protein. ⚠ That is the signature of a perceptual effect rather than a recovery effect. A garment that makes you feel less sore without making you less damaged is not worthless — it is a different product from the one advertised.

⚠ Provenance, extracted and published by the reviewers. Four of nine trials had garments supplied or funded by Sigvaris, 2XU or AITEX; the remaining five did not report the question at all.

Dose was specified, unusually for this vault's equipment literature: measured or manufacturer-stated pressures from 5–15 mmHg to 23–32 mmHg, coverage from calf to full body, wear durations from 60 minutes to 72 hours. ⚠ No dose-response was modelled, and this vault prescribes none (shared/safety.md).
