Melatonin impairs glucose tolerance and β-cell function in carriers of the common MTNR1B risk variant

In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial with pre-stratified genotype groups, a single 5 mg oral melatonin dose worsened glucose tolerance by 11.7% (95% CI 1.0–22.3) and cut the early C-peptide response by 19.2% (−33.9 to −1.2) in MTNR1B G-allele carriers, both P<sub>adj</sub> < 0.05. The mechanism was a 40% suppression of glucose-stimulated first-phase β-cell responsivity (−52.4 to −24.3, P<sub>adj</sub> = 0.0003). All three are the same physiology measured three ways, and the direction is consistent across them.

This overturns an unsourced claim, not a graded one. melatonin previously asserted that melatonin carried no safety interaction and was well tolerated — an assertion with no source and therefore no tier. Against that, a multiplicity-adjusted genotype-stratified crossover is the strongest available evidence, and it points the other way. The map moves to contested; the safety_flags: [interaction] set here gates co-recommendation per shared/safety.md.

Why this is not a subgroup-mining artefact. Carriers were recruited as a stratum on an a-priori hypothesis, not discovered after the fact; p-values are reported adjusted; and the MTNR1B→type-2-diabetes association is one of the best-replicated findings in metabolic genetics, so the mechanism was pre-committed rather than reverse-engineered from the result. A fishing expedition finds effects scattered everywhere — this one found nothing at all in noncarriers (melatonin-metabolic-effect-is-genotype-dependent).

What it does not establish. Twenty-one people, ten of them carriers, on surrogate endpoints (IVGTT minimal-model parameters, not HbA1c or diabetes incidence), after one acute dose that is not the habitual low-dose bedtime exposure most people actually have. Capped suggestive. Nothing here shows melatonin causes diabetes, and nothing here applies to noncarriers. No dose is recommended — 5 mg is the trial's, not the vault's (shared/safety.md).

Where it bites. Melatonin's benefit case was already thin — +12 min of sleep in cognitively impaired elders (melatonin-population-caveat-and-limits) inside a supplement shelf whose whole pooled effect sits under the clinical threshold (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold). The unspoken argument for taking it anyway was that it costs nothing. For a common genotype, that is now a randomized open question.
