Exercise beats no exercise for aging cognition — direction only, low certainty, and no modality attached

Strip the ranking table out of Jiao 2026 and something ordinary and probably true is left: across 18 RCTs (n=1,316) in older adults, exercise groups outperformed non-exercise controls on cognitive scales. The authors' own GRADE table prices the confidence:

  • MMSE — SMD 0.55 (0.12–0.98), LOW certainty, I² = 86.1%
  • MoCA — SMD 0.93 (−0.03 to 1.89), LOW certainty, I² = 97.2% — the interval includes zero
  • WAIS — SMD 1.26 (0.82–1.69), MODERATE certainty — but from 4 RCTs, n=211
  • WCST — SMD 0.61 (0.11–1.12), LOW certainty — 2 studies, both aerobic training

Graded single-study because the source is unreliable (shared/evidence-model.md — the cap is mandatory, not discretionary). The paper commits two fatal reading errors: it converts a set of statistically indistinguishable modality arms into a ranked prescription, and it reports a different "winning" exercise on each of four scales with no multiplicity correction (exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported). An unreliable source cannot move a map on its own, and this one doesn't.

So what is this note for? To record that the direction is consistent with what the vault already holds from better sources, and to fence off what it cannot support.

Consistent with: cardiorespiratory-fitness-all-cause-mortality and daily-steps-broad-healthspan-outcomes (whose outcome list already includes dementia, HR 0.62 — a hard endpoint, from a far better-powered base than any cognitive scale here), and multidomain-lifestyle-cognitive-impairment-benefit-overweight (where a decade-long randomised lifestyle intervention moved incident cognitive impairment in one BMI stratum).

Cannot support: any statement that aerobic training is better than resistance training for cognition, or vice versa; any dose ("60–90 min, ≥3×/week"); and any promotion of a cognitive claim on zone-2-cardio or resistance-training. The between-arm comparisons were null and the paper says so.

The honest summary the vault will carry: move, because moving is good for the aging brain and for everything else — and stop asking which movement is best, because the literature that claims to answer that is ranking noise. Convenient, given GOAL.md's adherence-first constraint: the best exercise for cognition is, on this evidence, the one David will actually keep doing.
