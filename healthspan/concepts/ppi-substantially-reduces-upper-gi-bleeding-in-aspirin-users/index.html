A proton pump inhibitor removes about 70% of aspirin's upper-gut bleeding risk, on randomised evidence

Across 7 randomised controlled trials, adding a proton pump inhibitor to aspirin reduced upper gastrointestinal bleeding against placebo or no treatment: RR 0.30 (95% CI 0.19–0.46). The margin over every other acid-suppression class tested is larger still — H₂-receptor antagonists RR 0.21 (0.09–0.53), mucosal protective agents RR 0.24 (0.10–0.58) — and the direction replicates in the non-randomised strata (vs no-PPI, RR 0.50, 0.38–0.66).

Why this belongs on aspirin specifically. That map is fading: ASPREE gave a precisely estimated null on healthspan loss (HR 0.98, 0.92–1.05) alongside a quantified bleeding harm (HR 1.24, 1.10–1.39, about 1.7 extra major bleeds per 1,000 person-years — aspirin-increases-major-haemorrhage-older-adults). The standard reply to that harm is "co- prescribe a PPI," and the vault previously had no graded evidence on whether the reply works. It now does, for the upper gastrointestinal tract, at randomised tier.

The effect is out of confounding range and consistent across comparators. A 70% reduction inside randomised allocation, replicated in direction against four different comparators, is not something unmeasured variables manufacture. This is the strongest part of the source paper.

Why suggestive and not strong, on three specific grounds. (1) No absolute risks appear anywhere. shared/study-appraisal.md's first universal lens fires: without baseline event rates, a 70% relative reduction cannot be sized, and it cannot be weighed against the lower-gut signal from the same paper (ppi-lower-gi-bleeding-signal-in-aspirin-users-is-unrandomised). (2) The pools are small — 7, 5 and 4 trials — and heterogeneity, risk-of-bias tooling, GRADE ratings and PROSPERO registration are entirely uninspectable, because the article is confirmed closed access with no free copy anywhere. (3) The source's mixed reliability verdict caps every concept drawn from it, per shared/evidence-model.md. The cap describes what could not be inspected, not doubt about the direction of this benefit.

⚑ safety_flags: [prescription, interaction], and both are deliberate. A PPI is a prescription drug, so per shared/safety.md it never enters a regimen and no dose is recorded — the source publishes none. And this is a genuine drug-drug interaction surface, in the useful direction: the co-medication measurably changes what the first drug does to the patient. It is flagged so that the other side of the same interaction — the unrandomised lower-GI signal — travels with it and is never cited alone.

What it does not say. Nothing about whether the upper-GI bleeds prevented here are the same events ASPREE counted as major haemorrhage — the endpoints are not the same object and the vault does not equate them. Nothing about primary- versus secondary-prevention populations, which is not recoverable. And nothing that makes low-dose aspirin worth taking for healthspan: removing part of a cost does not create a benefit where a 19,114-person randomised trial found none.
