A sixth synthesis finds no adverse events — and is the first to refuse to call that safety

Across six randomised trials in 197 healthy older adults, no major adverse side effects were reported. The authors then do something none of the five preceding syntheses did: they immediately state that this is "encouraging but not sufficient to draw firm conclusions regarding safety", and name the specific hazards nobody measured — acute rises in heart rate and blood pressure that are "particularly relevant for older adults with hypertension or cardiovascular disease," plus increased perceived exertion, discomfort and pain.

"No adverse events were reported" and "no adverse events occurred" are different sentences, and the gap between them is the entire safety question for a technique that deliberately restricts arterial inflow in older limbs. Six small trials of healthy volunteers, none at low risk of bias, none designed to detect harm, none following anyone beyond the intervention period, reporting nothing bad — that is what an unmeasured outcome looks like, not what a safe intervention looks like.

This is the sixth replication of the same absence. The gap has been flagged on blood-flow-restriction five times across four prior syntheses and 51 RCTs (bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials). Ren 2025 supplied the first genuine data — cardiovascular response, not occlusion risk (bfr-cardiovascular-response-data-older-adults) — and Zhu 2026 supplied the first measured cost, a graded interaction with walking volume (bfr-occlusion-reduces-walking-volume-and-comfort). Still, across six syntheses, there has never been a systematic adverse-event tally with a denominator.
