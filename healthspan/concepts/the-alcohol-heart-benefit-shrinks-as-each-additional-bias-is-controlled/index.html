The one-drink-a-day heart benefit is in every cohort study, and it gets smaller every time someone controls another bias

The J-curve — lower cardiovascular risk at low-to-moderate drinking than at zero — is the most consequential claim in alcohol epidemiology and the reason "moderate drinking is good for your heart" became common knowledge. This review finds it in every major cohort meta-analysis it examined, for ischaemic heart disease, ischaemic stroke and type 2 diabetes. It also finds it plausible: there are established mechanisms for a real effect at low doses — higher adiponectin, lower fibrinogen, inhibited platelet aggregation, reduced inflammation — all of which act on ischaemic outcomes specifically, which is exactly where the J appears and not elsewhere.

⚑ And then it reports the sentence that undermines the whole thing: "This J-shape is observed in all major meta-analyses of cohort studies, but the lower risk at low consumption levels becomes less pronounced as more potential biases are controlled."

That is a quality gradient running against the headline, and it is the signature of an artefact rather than an effect. A real biological effect does not systematically shrink as measurement improves; a confounded one does. The specific confounders here are the ones this literature is famous for:

  • Sick-quitter bias. The reference group of "non-drinkers" is contaminated with former drinkers who stopped because they became ill. Their events get attributed to not drinking.
  • Healthy-user bias. Light drinkers in Western cohorts are richer, better educated, more socially connected and more likely to exercise. The review notes most cohorts are middle-class populations.
  • Self-reported exposure, with the well-documented under-reporting that goes with it.

Two further constraints the review puts on its own finding, both of which narrow it substantially:

  • ⚠ The protective limb holds "generally only without heavy episodic drinking." See the-alcohol-j-curve-requires-the-absence-of-heavy-drinking-occasions — this is not a caveat, it is a precondition.
  • ⚠ Cardiovascular relative risks attenuate toward 1 with older age, so whatever the benefit is, it is smallest in the population with the most cardiovascular events to prevent.

The genetic evidence, which was supposed to settle this, does not find the protection (no-low-risk-of-bias-mendelian-randomisation-study-found-alcohol-protective). The authors argue it also cannot refute it, because almost none of those studies tested a non-linear shape. Both statements are true at once, and that is precisely the state a contested Status is for.

⚑ What this vault will and will not say. It will say: a protective effect of light drinking on ischaemic heart disease is possible, is present in every cohort synthesis, is smaller the better the study, and is not corroborated by the one design built to remove its main confounders. It will not say the J-curve is debunked — the review is explicit that the evidence base is not there for that either. And it will not attach a number of drinks to any of it.

The asymmetry that decides what to do with this in practice: whatever survives on the cardiovascular axis, it does not transfer to the cancer axis, where the curve is a straight line up from zero (alcohol-and-cancer-is-monotonic-with-no-threshold).
