The vault's top-ranked sleep dimension is nearly absent from the literature where it would be easiest to test

Across 30 studies and 10,944 adults, a review structured explicitly by the six RU-SATED dimensions of sleep health found that sleep regularity and sleep timing were its thinnest cells: "limited evidence," directions suggestive at best, and firm conclusions explicitly refused — "the limited number of studies across these sleep health dimensions is insufficient to draw firm conclusions." Daytime alertness showed no association at all. The authors name regularity, timing and alertness as the priority for future research.

The reported directions, carried as directions only: lower sleep regularity generally associated with reduced weight or attenuated fat loss; later or more irregular timing with less favourable outcomes in some studies; earlier wake times with greater weight loss in three studies.

This bears directly on sleep-regularity's central claim. That map ranks regularity above sleep-duration — regular sleep-wake timing beating duration for mortality, and quantitatively ahead (20–48% vs 14%, accelerometry vs self-report). Here is a literature on a highly measurable, short-horizon, randomisable outcome, and the dimension the vault ranks first is the one nobody has studied. Meanwhile duration — the dimension this vault ranks lower and distrusts the instrument of — is the one with two randomised manipulation trials (sleep-restriction-reduces-fat-loss-during-calorie-restriction).

An absence of studies is not a refutation, and the map does not move. shared/evidence-model.md sets Status by the strongest available evidence, and regularity's mortality evidence is untouched here. But the primacy claim is visibly narrower than it looked a week ago, and this is the second consecutive day it has narrowed: yesterday Paquet 2026 found that sleep variability's association with cognitive decline did not survive the paper's own multiple-comparison correction (sleep-variability-cognitive-decline-signal-does-not-survive-correction) and that sleep is a roughly 13-dimensional construct, making every single-metric result a slice — including the SRI that map is built on (sleep-is-a-multidimensional-construct-not-a-single-metric). Two days, two literatures: the mortality claim stands; the extrapolations from it do not.

What would resolve it. A randomised trial holding sleep duration constant while manipulating sleep timing regularity during a calorie deficit, with objective sleep measurement and a body-composition endpoint. The review's own recommendations amount to the same request — prospective registration, explicit randomisation, objective sleep measures, intention-to-treat analysis — and it is a straightforward trial to run.

Bounds. The parent review is appraised mixed: preregistered and duplicated, but unable to meta-analyse, so the "limited evidence" judgement is a narrative one; all included studies carried at least moderate risk of bias; sleep was mostly self-reported; and the population is adults with BMI ≥ 25. Graded cohort tier because the regularity and timing evidence within the review is observational. Status held emerging on sleep-regularity; sleep is counted once.
