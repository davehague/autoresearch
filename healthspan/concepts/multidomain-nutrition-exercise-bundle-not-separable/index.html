Exercise and a branded protein supplement in one arm against habitual care — the seventh time the vault has watched bundling destroy attribution

The intervention was supervised multicomponent exercise plus a daily oral nutritional supplement, tested as one arm against habitual care. There is no exercise-only arm and no supplement-only arm, so neither component's contribution is estimable. The authors concede this in their own limitations.

This is the seventh instance in the vault's running series, and the pattern is now a standing methodological finding in its own right:

  1. creatine + beta-alanine — combined arm estimable in 1 of 6 domains (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy)
  2. protein + probiotic — added value null in 9 of 10 contrasts (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance)
  3. exercise + CBT — no CBT-alone node in the network (cbt-sleep-effect-not-isolable-no-cbt-alone-node)
  4. unstable-surface RT — surfaces compared at unspecified intensity (unstable-vs-stable-rt-strength-mobility-null-underpowered)
  5. hypoxia + HIIT — benefit shrinks against exercise controls (hypoxic-hiit-benefit-shrinks-against-exercise-control)
  6. "dietary supplements" — a dozen unrelated substances as one node (supplement-sleep-benefit-not-attributable-to-any-supplement)
  7. this — exercise + a branded supplement

What distinguishes this one is where the money sits. The supplement is Meritene, a Nestlé Health Science product, and Nestlé Health Science part-funded the trial. The design that makes attribution impossible is therefore also the design in which a commercial product shares the credit for a supervised exercise programme's results. The authors state Nestlé had no role in design, analysis or interpretation, and the vault records that statement rather than dismissing it — but shared/evidence-model.md requires the conflict be flagged and never smoothed away, and the structural point stands independently of anyone's intent.

The prior probability is not neutral, either. Six months of supervised, thrice-weekly resistance, balance and aerobic training in frail 80-year-olds is an intervention with a large, well-replicated effect on exactly the outcomes that moved — grip, gait, balance, frailty. 16 g of supplemental protein per day is a modest addition for someone already eating, and the vault's own protein map holds that the target is a daily total (~1.6 g/kg/day), not a branded delivery vehicle (protein-supplementation-augments-resistance-training). The most parsimonious reading is that the exercise did most of this — but "most parsimonious" is not "measured," and the vault grades what was measured, which is nothing about either component.

Consequence: no component claim is carried from this source. The bundle's joint effect on function is real and graded (multidomain-intervention-improves-frailty-and-function-frail-elders). Nothing here supports adding a nutritional supplement to a regimen, and nothing here is needed to support exercise, which is better-sourced elsewhere in the vault on its own.
