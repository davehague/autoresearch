Measurement is not treatment: the arm that only got the tracker did not improve

In a three-arm randomised trial, every participant wore a sleep monitoring device and received a sleep report. The only thing that varied was whether telephone health guidance on sleep hygiene and daytime activity was added on top.

Both arms that received the telephone guidance improved on the Athens Insomnia Scale within-group at 3 months. The arm that received the device and the report and nothing else showed no significant change over time at all. The group-by-time interaction was significant at p = 0.002.

⚑ The result is a deflating one about a heavily-marketed category: being shown your own sleep data, on its own, did not change a sleep outcome. For a vault whose north star is least work for maximum benefit, a daily-compliance intervention that produced no measurable benefit is a poor trade at any price.

⚠ Two structural limits keep this from being a strong claim, and both are about what the trial could not ask. First, there is no arm without a device. Every comparison holds the tracker constant, so the trial cannot compare "tracker" against "no tracker." The defensible statement is that adding human guidance to a tracker beat a tracker alone — not that trackers do nothing. Second, no effect sizes are published anywhere in the retrievable text, so even the guidance benefit is a direction without a magnitude — and with a baseline AIS of 6.5 on a 0–24 scale, there was limited room to move in either direction.

⚠ The primary outcome is a questionnaire in an unblindable trial, which flatters the guidance arms. Participants knew whether a health professional was telephoning them; attention, expectation and social desirability all push the self-reported score the same way the intervention does. ⚑ And the omission is conspicuous: every participant wore a device capable of producing objective sleep data, and the trial reports a subjective scale as its primary outcome.

⚠ Within-group improvement is the weakest comparison available — it cannot separate the intervention from regression to the mean or the passage of time. The vault holds a standing note on that error in another literature: sequential-single-arm-hiit-gains-cannot-be-separated-from-the-passage-of-time.

⚑ Read against the vault's structured-behavioural evidence, the contrast is unflattering to hygiene advice. cbt-insomnia carries 53 RCTs (n = 13,608) for a structured course whose benefit persists after treatment stops (cbt-i-controlled-insomnia-severity-effect-holds-at-eight-months). Unstructured sleep-hygiene advice by telephone is a different and much weaker intervention, and the shared word "behavioural" should not be allowed to blur them. ⚠ Not a clean comparison in the other direction either — cbt-i-large-effect-was-measured-against-a-sleep-diary-control records that CBT-I's own estimate was inflated by a weak comparator.

Scope. Mean age 78.5, Japanese, community-dwelling, mildly symptomatic. Nothing here transfers as a number to a middle-aged adult.
