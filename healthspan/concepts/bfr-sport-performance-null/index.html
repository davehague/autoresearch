BFR's strength gains did not show up in performance — and the authors recommend it anyway

Across the same ten RCTs that produced a large lower-limb strength effect (bfr-adds-lower-limb-strength-in-athletes, SMD 1.09), sport-specific performance did not move: SMD 0.11 (−0.18 to 0.40), I² = 0%. The authors' conclusion, unchanged by this: "It may be prudent to integrate this approach systematically into training cycles."

Two errors are available here. The vault declines both.

Error one — reading this null as a debunk. It is not. The confidence interval reaches 0.40, a meaningful performance benefit, on a base of 181 athletes across ten trials. This is an underpowered null, and the vault has spent this month refusing to let underpowered nulls be sold as equivalence — rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered (falls RR 2.68, CI to 11.11, called "similar safety") and rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered (four nulls called "as effective"). The rule is symmetric or it is worthless. It does not become acceptable to over-read a null just because the over-reading would be skeptical rather than promotional. The honest statement is: nobody has powered a trial to find out whether BFR's strength gains transfer.

Error two — accepting the recommendation. The authors advise systematic adoption on the strength of surrogates (muscle strength, muscle circumference) while the one endpoint that measures doing something sits at zero. That is a pattern this vault has an established position on: urolithin-a-null-on-function-and-many-endpoints — muscle strength up, mitophagy engaged, physical function null — was read as watch, don't buy. nad-precursors-null-on-muscle-mass-function — blood NAD⁺ up, function null — ranked the intervention low. Mechanism engaged is not benefit delivered, and a surrogate that fails to transfer is a warning, not a footnote.

What this leaves. BFR remains the most interesting idea the vault holds for the knee problem — strength adaptation at 20–40% 1RM instead of ≥70% — and it remains unproven on anything a person can feel, in a population that is not David's, with no adverse events collected (bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials). Watch, don't buy. Don't debunk either.
