Statistically robust, sub-perceptual at the centre

Structured exercise improved university students' self-reported sleep quality by 2.43 PSQI points (95% CI −3.72 to −1.14), and the estimate held under leave-one-out, change-from-baseline, restricted maximum likelihood and the Hartung–Knapp adjustment. That is a robust result.

It is also smaller than a person would notice. shared/glossary.md records ~3 PSQI points as the smallest change people register; the point estimate is 2.43 and the most conservative interval (−3.98 to −0.88) puts most of its mass below three. Robustness and importance are different properties, and this literature has the first without the second.

⚠ The 95% prediction interval runs −6.98 to +2.12 and contains zero. The pooled figure is an average across settings, not an effect to expect in any particular one — the authors say so: "a clinically meaningful benefit cannot be assumed in every context." At I² = 91.7%, that is the honest reading.

Two components moved and two did not. Sleep duration (−0.74) and habitual sleep efficiency (−0.43) reached significance; sleep latency (−0.53, −1.36 to 0.30) and sleep disturbances (−0.54, −1.23 to 0.15) did not — on three trials each at I² above 94%. Those are underpowered nulls, not demonstrated absences, and are read here as open questions.

GRADE rated four of the five outcomes very low and the fifth low — the paper's own verdict on its own headline.
