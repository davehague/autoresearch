20 g/day of creatine does load the brain (+11% total creatine) — the dose the vault has no safety evidence for

In the single-arm CABA pilot (NCT05383833), 20 g/day creatine monohydrate (10 g twice daily) for 8 weeks in 20 Alzheimer's-dementia patients raised brain total creatine by ~11% on ¹H magnetic resonance spectroscopy (330.5 ± 36.8 → 366.9 ± 57.5 IU; p<0.001, Cohen's dz=1.01), elevated in 85% of participants. Serum creatine rose at both 4 and 8 weeks.

Why this matters for the vault. It supplies the missing mechanistic step in the vault's longest-running creatine question. creatine-brain-cart-before-horse-review-2026 — authored by Candow's own group, the leading proponents of high-dose creatine — argued that healthy adults likely need ≥20 g/day, not 10 g, for even a detectable brain-creatine rise (only ~3–11% against an MRS measurement error of up to 20%), and conceded the muscle-saturation → brain-uptake mechanism was unproven in humans. CABA now demonstrates that 20 g/day does raise brain creatine, by roughly the predicted magnitude, in most people who take it. That is the first empirical support for the loading threshold.

Why it justifies nothing about dose (safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]). Target engagement is not benefit. The one randomized, placebo-controlled test of the next link — does a high dose then improve cognition in healthy adults? — was null at both 10 g and 20 g (creatine-dose-response-cognition, Moriarty 2023, n=30; placebo numerically beat the 10 g arm). Concluding "20 g loads the brain, therefore take 20 g" is precisely the cart-before-the-horse inference Candow's group named. Three further constraints:

  • 20 g/day is double the highest dose the vault has safety evidence for. The creatine safety thread rests on ~10 g/day being renal-clean over 3 months (creatine-high-dose-renal-safety). Twenty grams is outside that evidence, not inside it.
  • Adverse events included sleep disturbance. Thirteen mild events across the cohort — cramping/muscle pain, diarrhea, constipation, nausea, facial flushing, and sleep disturbance. GOAL.md names "nothing that disrupts sleep" as a hard constraint, and sleep-regularity is a foundation lever. Thin evidence, flagged over-cautiously per shared/safety.md.
  • Population. Alzheimer's patients (mean age 73) with impaired brain bioenergetics are arguably the group most primed to show a creatine effect and least like a healthy middle-aged adult.

Bounded honestly (why mechanistic, why suggestive). The uptake finding is credible despite the absent control arm — it is an objective, large-effect, within-subject biomarker measurement, pre-registered, with zero dropout and independent funding (Alzheimer's Association + NIH; the maker donated powder only). But it is target engagement in a small, sick, uncontrolled cohort, and it is graded as a mechanism, not an outcome.

The gap it leaves, precisely stated: a randomized, placebo-controlled, ≥20 g creatine trial in healthy adults with both MRS brain-creatine and cognitive endpoints. No dose is prescribed here (shared/safety.md).
