Four blood markers moved in the same direction while the outcome the trial was built around did not

PRAMEV's prespecified weight endpoint was null in intention-to-treat (hybrid-lifestyle-programme-null-on-its-prespecified-weight-endpoint). Its secondary metabolic outcomes were not. At 20 weeks the programme arm versus usual care: alanine aminotransferase −4.0 vs −2.0 U/L (P = .01), an enzyme that leaks from liver cells and rises with fatty liver; LDL cholesterol −5.0 vs −0.1 mg/dL (P = .02); HDL cholesterol +3.0 vs +1.1 mg/dL (P = .01); triglycerides −7.5 vs −2.0 mg/dL (P = .01).

The magnitudes are small and should be said so. Five mg/dL of LDL is roughly a 3–4% shift for a typical starting value; three mg/dL of HDL is a nudge. None of these is a hard endpoint — they are blood markers that predict cardiovascular events in epidemiology, not events that happened to anyone in this trial. The vault's standing rule applies unchanged: a surrogate that moves is not an outcome that moved, and converting one into the other using a coefficient borrowed from a different literature is the error catalogued at surrogate-to-outcome-conversion-borrowed-from-a-different-literature.

What makes them the most trustworthy numbers in the paper is structural. They are continuous measures rather than a dichotomised threshold, analysed across the follow-up sample rather than restricted to completers, and four markers moved coherently — falling LDL, falling triglycerides, rising HDL and falling ALT are the pattern a genuine reduction in adiposity and improvement in insulin handling produces, not four independent coin flips. In a trial whose one dichotomous positive is an artefact of who stayed (pramev-per-protocol-benefit-compares-two-differently-filtered-groups), the least filtered result is the one worth carrying.

The awkward version, which is the honest one: this is a weight-loss programme that did not reliably produce clinically meaningful weight loss and did produce a coherent small improvement in metabolic blood chemistry. Whether that is worth 20 weeks of group counselling to any individual person is a different question, and the trial does not answer it.
