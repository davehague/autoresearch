Put diet and device-measured activity in one midlife model, and only diet is still standing five years later

With both exposures measured at age 46 in the same 3,028 people and entered in the same model, Mediterranean-diet adherence was associated with global cognition at age 51 (β = 0.045 per score point, 95% CI 0.025 to 0.065) while device-measured moderate-to-vigorous physical activity was not (β = 0.228 per hour/day, 95% CI −0.026 to 0.481). In combined tertile analyses, the diet association held irrespective of activity level: high-diet/low-activity versus low-diet/low-activity gave β = 0.185 (0.046 to 0.325).

Why this matters for the vault. Diet and activity are almost always studied in separate literatures with separate instruments, which makes ranking them impossible. This is a head-to-head in one cohort, and the direction it came out in is the harder one for a simple measurement-bias story: the activity exposure was a thigh-worn accelerometer and the diet exposure a self-reported questionnaire. Measurement-error attenuation — the mechanism this vault invokes constantly, and which is documented inside this vault at self-reported-sleep-duration-preserves-shape-loses-magnitude — predicts the worse-measured exposure should be the one that loses. It won instead.

The population is the other reason this is worth holding. Age 46 at exposure, 51 at outcome, healthy, general-population — nearly every other cognition source in this vault sits in older adults or people with mild cognitive impairment, and the vault has separately recorded that lifestyle benefits concentrate in people already losing something (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers).

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, and why this is not "exercise doesn't help"). The effect is small: a one-SD better diet score is worth about 0.07 standard deviations of cognition — inside the range that education, childhood cognitive ability, occupation and income can produce, none of which is verifiable because the full text was not retrievable. Critically, whether the age-51 model adjusted for age-46 cognition is UNVERIFIED, and that single fact decides whether this measures cognitive trajectory or merely cognitive level carried forward from childhood. The activity result is a non-finding, not a demonstrated null: its interval runs from a trivial harm to a substantial benefit, in a cohort already averaging 52 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity a day, where low-activity contrast is thin. And the winning exposure is the self-reported one, with healthy-user confounding unaddressable.

The practical shape of the claim. A reordering of emphasis, not a new item: dietary pattern deserves at least as much attention as movement for the midlife brain. It sets no dietary target and is not a reason to walk less.
