Doing both beat doing either — and it is the same answer the vault already had from a mortality cohort, arrived at randomly, on a different outcome, in a different population

A Bayesian network meta-analysis of 34 randomised trials in 2,461 older adults with type 2 diabetes ranked five ways of exercising against glycaemic control. Combined aerobic plus resistance training won all three outcomes, and on the hardest one it was the only thing that worked at all.

HbA₁c — the share of haemoglobin with glucose attached, which averages blood sugar over roughly three months and is the standard outcome in this literature. Combined training: −1.05% (95% credible interval −1.43 to −0.64), against −0.80% for traditional Chinese sports, −0.63% for interval training, −0.48% for continuous aerobic exercise and −0.44% for resistance training alone. Fasting glucose: combined −1.44 mmol/L (−2.16 to −0.66) — and interval training and resistance training alone were not significant here at all. Two-hour post-meal glucose: combined −3.15 mmol/L (−5.42 to −0.83), the only modality to separate from control.

Why the vault records this as confirms rather than as news. resistance-training already holds aerobic-plus-resistance-lowest-mortality — in three prospective cohorts totalling 147,374 adults followed up to 30 years, combining aerobic and resistance training reached the lowest all-cause mortality (hazard ratio around 0.55), lower than either alone. That is observational, and carries the observational ceiling. This source is randomised, on a different endpoint, in a different population, from authors with no connection to that literature — and it lands on the same configuration. A belief that survives contact with an unrelated literature is worth more than one that has only ever been tested where it was born.

What is being confirmed and what is not. The direction — that the combination beats its components — now has randomised support. The magnitude does not transfer: a 1.05-percentage-point HbA₁c reduction is a number about people with type 2 diabetes, whose starting values leave room to move, and it says nothing about what combined training does to blood sugar in someone with normal glucose. And no mortality claim is transferred in either direction: this network measured markers, not events, with no trial recording a diabetes complication, a cardiovascular event or a death.

Why suggestive and not stronger, in four specific ways. The winning node rests on 7 trials. Only 6 of 34 trials in the whole network are at low risk of bias; 24 carry "some concerns" and 4 are high risk. The authors' own CINeMA certainty ratings run from very low to moderate. And what the control arms were asked to do is never characterised, which in this vault's recent experience is the variable most likely to be doing the work (traditional-chinese-sports-ranking-is-unseparable-from-its-comparator). Publication bias was detected on fasting glucose (Egger's p = 0.0337) and left uncorrected.

The ordering below first place should not be read. SUCRA is a probability of ranking best within this particular network; it is not an effect size and it does not know how much evidence sits behind each node (glycemic-exercise-network-is-sparse-under-a-precise-leaderboard). The vault takes the direction — do both — and refuses the ranking of the other four.
