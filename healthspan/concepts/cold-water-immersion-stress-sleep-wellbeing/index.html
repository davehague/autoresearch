Cold-water immersion lowers stress and improves sleep — on a delay, and modestly

Pooling 11 RCTs (3,177 healthy adults) of deliberate cold-water immersion (≤15°C, ≥30 s):

  • Stress dropped 12 hours after immersion (SMD −1.00, p<0.01) but not immediately (SMD −0.09) or at 24–48 h — a delayed, not acute, effect.
  • Sleep quality improved versus passive recovery (p=0.04).
  • Quality of life (SF-36 mental component) was higher at 30 days but not sustained at 90.
  • A narrative signal: ~29% fewer sickness-absence days with regular cold showers.

Why this matters for the vault. It positions cold-water immersion as a plausible wind-down / recovery adjunct — the sleep and stress signals overlap the same outcome space the vault tracks for sleep-regularity and the wind-down cluster (l-theanine, chamomile, melatonin). For David (GOAL.md), it is knee-safe (zero mechanical load), like sauna-heat.

Bounded honestly (why capped suggestive): the base is small (11 RCTs), heterogeneous, and predominantly male; the endpoints are subjective; and the stress benefit shows up only at one of several tested time-windows (a multiple-comparison caution). Real, but thin — a tool to watch, not a foundation.
