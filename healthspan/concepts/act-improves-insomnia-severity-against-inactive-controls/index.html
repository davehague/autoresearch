Acceptance and commitment therapy beats doing nothing for insomnia, by a moderate-to-large margin

Acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) approaches insomnia from the opposite direction to CBT-I: rather than changing sleep behaviour, it trains people to stop struggling against the arousal and unwanted thoughts, on the premise that the effort to sleep is part of what keeps you awake. Against waitlist or standard care it improved insomnia severity by SMD −0.67 (−1.08 to −0.25), with smaller benefits on depression (−0.34) and anxiety (−0.42).

The comparator is doing all the visible work, and the same paper proves it. Against CBT-I instead of nothing, the same therapy produced a non-significant 0.21 (act-versus-cbt-i-difference-is-unresolved-not-equivalent). Nothing about ACT changed between those two numbers — only what it was measured against. This is the vault's cleanest single-paper demonstration of comparator strength, and it re-fires the open request for a comparator lens in shared/study-appraisal.md.

⚠ The size of the evidence base cannot be read. Neither the number of trials nor the total sample appears in the abstract, and the article is embargoed in PMC until 2027-08-03. An SMD of −0.67 from five small trials and from twenty-five large ones are different facts.

⚠ Blinding is impossible and the outcome is a questionnaire — participants know which therapy they got and are then asked how bad their insomnia is. That distorts the waitlist contrast far more than the head-to-head.
