Exercise improves the blood-vessel measures; which exercise is not shown to matter

Forty-seven randomised trials in 1,995 older adults, seven exercise modalities, two standard measures of vascular health. Exercise beats doing nothing on both. Nothing beats anything else on either. The authors state it directly: "no statistically significant differences were identified across any head-to-head comparisons," and "extensive overlap in confidence intervals across active interventions precludes definitive claims of head-to-head clinical superiority."

The two measures, explained. Flow-mediated dilation cuffs the arm to briefly block blood flow, then measures by ultrasound how far the artery widens on release — a healthy vessel lining opens, a damaged one does not, so higher is better. Pulse wave velocity times how fast a pressure wave travels along an artery; stiff arteries carry it faster, so lower is better. Both are surrogates: they predict cardiovascular events well, and neither is an event.

What reached significance against control. Whole-body vibration improved flow-mediated dilation by +5.7 percentage points (95% CI 2.4–9.0), with resistance training and walking also significant. Aerobic training lowered brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity by SMD −0.81 (−1.09 to −0.54), with walking, combined and mind–body exercise also significant. Combined training lowered diastolic blood pressure by −3.48 mmHg (−5.52 to −1.43). On carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity — the measure usually treated as the reference standard for aortic stiffness — nothing was significant at all.

The rankings are recorded and declined. SUCRA scores put whole-body vibration first on dilation (91%), aerobic first on stiffness (83.3%), stretching first on systolic pressure (91.8%) and combined training first on diastolic (86.1%). Four outcomes, four different winners, zero significant differences between any pair of modalities. A SUCRA score is the probability of ranking first given the estimates; when the estimates overlap completely it reports which arm drew the highest point estimate. This is the seventh ranking leaderboard this vault has recorded without adopting.

Why this estimate is worth carrying despite suggestive grading. Every outcome here is machine-measured — an ultrasound probe, a pulse-wave transducer, a blood-pressure cuff. The problem that wrecks certainty across this vault's sleep, mood and self-reported-cognition literatures — an unblindable behaviour scored by asking people how they feel — does not apply. What caps it at suggestive is the mixed source appraisal: 1,995 people spread across seven modality nodes with per-node trial counts never published, only 3 of 47 trials at low risk of bias, and moderate-to-high heterogeneity across most networks.

The practical translation. For a person choosing what to do, this says: supervised exercise of essentially any kind improves these vascular markers over four weeks or more, and the evidence does not support picking one modality over another for that purpose. That aligns with GOAL.md's adherence-first constraint — the best modality is the one that gets done.
