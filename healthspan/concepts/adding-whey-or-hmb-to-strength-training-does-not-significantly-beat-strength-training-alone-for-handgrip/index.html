The one isolating contrast this map always asks for — does the supplement add to training, not just to nothing — lands as a trend, not a finding

Within the same meta-analysis, three-way subgroup contrasts for handgrip strength separate training from supplementation more cleanly than the headline numbers: strength training plus whey/HMB clearly beats supplementation alone (ES=0.63, P=.002) — training is doing most of the work — but strength training plus supplement does not significantly beat strength training alone (P=.06), and training alone does not significantly beat supplementation alone either (P=.06).

Why this matters for the map. protein-target's standing position is that resistance training is the lever and supplementation is a small, additive refinement — and every design that has tried to isolate the supplement's marginal contribution on top of training (Bastos 2026's synbiotic arm, Svensson 2026's intra-workout protein, Casuso 2025's timing meta-analysis) has landed null or borderline. This is another one: a trend in the expected direction (adding a supplement to training looks like it might help) that does not clear P<.05 in a 7-trial pool.

Bounded honestly. A P=.06 in a small subgroup pool is "not yet shown," not "shown to be absent" — the authors say so themselves. This reinforces rather than newly establishes the map's position.
