The paper reports a sex difference and then reports the test that says there isn't one

Ren 2026 presents sex-stratified sleep-mortality results that look like a strong sexual dimorphism. Men sleeping 8–10 hours: HR 3.095 (95% CI 1.545–6.199, p = 0.001) — while the same band in the overall sample was not significant after adjustment. Women sleeping 4–6 hours: HR 0.522 (0.281–0.969, p = 0.039) for premature death — short sleep associated with less early death, which no mechanism predicts.

Then it reports the formal test: sex × sleep-duration interaction, χ² = 7.50, p = 0.112. Not significant. The paper says so in its own abstract, and states plainly that the differences were observed "although the formal interaction test did not reach statistical significance."

This is the cleanest available demonstration of what stratified reporting does to a cohort, and it is worth keeping as a methodological reference point rather than as a finding about sleep. Splitting a sample by sex halves the events in each stratum, doubles the number of estimates, and guarantees that some bands will drift far from the pooled value in both directions. An apparent subgroup effect is only evidence of a subgroup effect if the interaction test says the subgroups differ. Here it does not — so the honest reading is that men and women in this cohort had the same sleep-mortality relationship, and the stratified numbers are the pooled relationship plus noise.

The female short-sleep result is the tell. HR 0.522 implies that women sleeping 4–6 hours died prematurely at roughly half the rate of women sleeping 6–8 — a protective effect of sleep deprivation. Nothing in physiology predicts it, no other cohort in this vault reports it, and the analysis it comes from has 210 premature-death events in total, which the authors themselves flag as underpowered. It is noise, and it is useful precisely because it is obviously noise: it calibrates how much to trust the male 8–10 h result sitting in the same table, produced by the same procedure.

Why the vault records this as pushes-back. Not against a sleep claim — against a reading practice. Sex-stratified findings in observational sleep research are widely reported and widely repeated, and this vault will meet more of them. The standing rule this note anchors: report the interaction test, or treat the strata as descriptive. The same discipline the vault applied to sleep-variability-cognitive-decline-signal-does-not-survive-correction, where the only longitudinal signal on offer dissolved under its own multiplicity correction.

Credit where it is due. The authors ran the interaction test, published the null, and said in the abstract that their sex differences did not survive it. That is more honest than a great deal of the literature this vault ingests, and it is why the paper is readable at all despite its unreliable verdict.
