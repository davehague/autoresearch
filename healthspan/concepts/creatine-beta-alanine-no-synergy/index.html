Stacking beta-alanine onto creatine adds nothing detectable — but the test itself was underpowered

The source's abstract and conclusion state, without qualification, that "combined Cr + BA showed no synergistic benefits" and that co-supplementation "offers no additional advantage beyond Cr monotherapy... across any tested performance domain." Read against the paper's own results and limitations, this needs a narrower, more honest framing.

What the data actually show. A creatine+beta-alanine (Cr+BA) node is only reported for one of the six outcome domains this network meta-analysis modeled: jump performance, where Cr+BA's P-score (0.23) was the lowest of the three active arms (beta-alanine 0.48, creatine 0.34, Cr+BA 0.23) and not significantly different from placebo. For sprint, agility, RSA, upper-body muscular endurance, and lower-body muscular endurance, the results section never reports a Cr+BA comparison at all — the plain implication is that too few trials tested the combination in those domains to generate a network estimate. The Discussion's own Limitations section says this directly: "limited data existed for combined Cr + BA trials, restricting the ability to detect potential synergistic interactions beyond additive metabolic pathways," and separately flags "the scarcity of factorial Cr + BA designs" as a key research gap.

The call: this is "no evidence of synergy," not "evidence of no synergy." Per shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal-flag #3 (an underpowered null sold as "no effect"), a claim this general — "no synergistic benefit... across any tested performance domain" — is not supported by a single, non-significant, thinly-populated node in one of six domains. The vault's honest reading is: no credible evidence has yet shown that adding beta-alanine to creatine helps athletic performance beyond what creatine does alone. That is a real and useful finding — it is just not the same claim as "adding beta-alanine has been shown to do nothing," which would require adequately powered head-to-head Cr vs Cr+BA trials this literature does not yet contain.

Why this still matters for the regimen question even though it's a thin null. Per GOAL.md (adherence-first; fewer items; cost as tiebreaker), the decision at stake is whether to add a second supplement — a genuinely asymmetric bar from "is beta-alanine proven useless." Absence of evidence for benefit is a reasonable basis to not add something, even when it falls short of being evidence of absence. This note should not be read as closing the scientific question of Cr+BA synergy — only as removing any evidence- based case for adding beta-alanine to David's current creatine use today.

Beta-alanine itself remains outside this vault as a candidate, never-adopted intervention — no maps/beta-alanine exists, and this note deliberately does not create one; see the parent ingest's flagged- candidate recommendation.
