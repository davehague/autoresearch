"Fifty-six percent of studies were positive" is not an effect size

The observational half of Duan 2026 reports its findings as counts of studies, because a meta-analysis was impossible: sleep satisfaction positive in 56% of behavioural-intervention studies (5), sleep efficiency positive in 7 of 12 (58%), sleep duration mixed and inconsistent, alertness null. There is no pooled magnitude for any of them. Higher baseline sleep duration predicted greater weight loss in behavioural interventions and less weight loss in physical-activity-only interventions — a sign reversal the authors flag and cannot explain.

Recorded because this vault has a standing rule against exactly this inference. shared/evidence-model.md sets Status by highest-tier-wins, not vote-counting, for the reason on display here: individual studies in this corpus range from n = 10 to n = 2,990, and the review states it could not weight results by sample size or by risk of bias. So a ten-person study counts the same as a 2,990-person one, in a corpus where the authors' own assessment found 11 studies at serious and 1 at critical risk of bias, with all studies at least moderate. "Roughly half positive" over that corpus is close to what noise looks like.

And there is a more parsimonious explanation than the causal one. These are observational exposure-to-outcome analyses: sleep is measured at baseline and during a diet, and weight loss is the outcome. People who sleep well while dieting are plausibly people who are adhering to the diet. That is shared/study-appraisal.md's healthy-adherer lens, and the review does not exclude it. It is the same structure the vault refused four days ago, when MIND's null randomised trial was recovered by subsetting on post-randomization adherence (null-diet-trial-recovered-by-adherence-subsetting) — the operating rule recorded then was that "it works if you actually adhere" should be treated as an adherer-bias claim by default. Applied here, the sleep-satisfaction and sleep-efficiency associations are hypotheses, not findings.

The one thing this half of the review does establish is a contrast. The randomised sleep manipulation in the same paper found a clear, consistent effect (sleep-restriction-reduces-fat-loss-during-calorie-restriction). Two RCTs beat twenty-eight observational studies — the tier hierarchy doing exactly what it is for.

And the instrument compounds it. Sleep here is mostly self-reported (PSQI, logs, Epworth). Five days ago Chaput 2026 — same senior author — showed in 76,811 people measured both ways that self-report preserves the shape and loses the magnitude (HR 0.86, 0.74–1.00 vs device-measured HR 0.70, 0.61–0.79, self-reported-sleep-duration-preserves-shape-loses-magnitude). So these estimates are probably understatements of whatever is real — which is a reason to want better studies, not a reason to trust these ones. No sleep target is prescribed (shared/safety.md).
