The 25-year lifestyle result rests on just over a third of the people who were randomised

Of the 3,234 adults randomised in the Diabetes Prevention Program, 1,173 appear in this analysis — 36%. The other 64% are absent for three stacked reasons: they had to survive roughly 25 years, they had to reach Medicare eligibility, and they had to consent to claims linkage. The median age of those who made it is 74.

Why this is the dominant limitation and not a footnote. Randomisation buys comparability at baseline. It does not survive arbitrary post-randomisation selection: if the three arms lost different kinds of people, the surviving comparison is no longer the randomised one. A hazard ratio of 0.79 computed on a 36% subset is protected by randomisation only to the extent that the 64% left in a way unrelated to arm.

⚠ Whether they did is precisely what cannot be checked here. ⚑ Retrieval limit is doing real work: this note is written from the structured abstract, because the PMC deposit is embargoed until 2026-12-15 and the publisher's site is bot-gated. A comparison of consenters against non-consenters, and a breakdown of loss by arm, are exactly the kind of table a JAMA paper of this weight would carry — and they are not retrievable tonight. The vault records this as an unresolved question, not as a defect.

Two things push in the reassuring direction, and they are worth stating precisely because the selection is severe.

  • ⚑ The internal contrast is confounder-symmetric. Lifestyle and metformin were randomised in the same people, followed the same way, and subjected to the same consent filter. Whatever the selection did, it did to both arms. So the comparison between HR 0.79 and HR 0.91 is far better protected than either number standing alone — and that comparison is the paper's most useful output.
  • Survival selection here biases toward the null on the outcome measured, not away from it. The people who dropped out by dying are disproportionately the sickest; removing them compresses the multimorbidity contrast rather than inflating it.

Two things push the other way. Consent to data linkage is a behaviour, and behaviours correlate with engagement — the lifestyle arm spent three years being coached into engagement. If that made its members more likely to consent, the arms are not selected identically. And 85% of the retained cohort reached multimorbidity anyway, so the analysis is discriminating among people who nearly all got sick.

What would resolve it: the full text's comparison of the 1,173 against the 2,061, and loss-to-follow-up by randomised arm. ⚑ Both become available on 2026-12-15 when the PMC embargo lifts, and this source is flagged for re-appraisal then.
