Three years of coached diet and exercise in midlife, still visible in the health system's billing records a quarter of a century later

The Diabetes Prevention Program randomised 3,234 US adults with prediabetes to intensive lifestyle intervention, metformin, or placebo in 1996-1999. Twenty-five years on, Medicare claims for the surviving, consenting participants were used to count multimorbidity — two or more of 15 chronic conditions defined in the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services Chronic Condition Data Warehouse.

The lifestyle arm reached multimorbidity at a 21% lower rate: hazard ratio 0.79 (95% CI 0.68 to 0.93). The interval excludes 1.0, so no-effect is ruled out; the data are compatible with a 7% to 32% relative reduction. Restricted to dyads of the costliest conditions, the estimate strengthens to HR 0.57 (0.38 to 0.85).

⚑ The benefit is not the trial's original endpoint counted again. The Diabetes Prevention Program was built to prevent diabetes and did. The obvious deflationary reading of this result is that lifestyle prevented diabetes, diabetes is one of the 15 conditions, therefore the multimorbidity count fell mechanically. The authors tested that: the relationships persisted with diabetes removed from the definition.

Why this matters more than almost anything else on this map. multidomain-lifestyle is built from trials whose endpoints are cognitive test scores, frailty indices and cardiometabolic surrogates — measured by researchers, in people who knew which arm they were in. This endpoint is a billing record. Nobody self-reported it, nobody scored it, and there was no way to try harder on it. It is also a 25-year horizon, where the rest of the map's evidence runs 6 to 36 months.

Three properties make it unusually well-protected for an observational analysis:

  • The exposure was randomised. The single largest advantage over the observational lifestyle literature — nobody selected themselves into the lifestyle arm, so healthy-user bias is not available as an explanation.
  • The comparator was a real placebo arm, not a waiting list or a leaflet. This map's recurring embarrassment is that the size of a multidomain benefit tracks the weakness of what the control group got; that escape hatch is closed here.
  • The contrast was actively diluted, and survived. During the follow-up study the placebo was discontinued and unmasked, and lifestyle classes were offered to every arm quarterly until 2014. That dilution pushes toward the null. The 0.79 is what remained.

⚠ The limit that governs how far it travels. This analysis holds 1,173 of the 3,234 randomised — 36% — because Medicare linkage required both surviving to Medicare age and consenting (the-dppos-multimorbidity-result-carries-36-percent-of-the-randomised-cohort). And the benefit is delay, not avoidance: 82% of the lifestyle arm reached multimorbidity anyway (eighty-five-percent-of-this-cohort-reached-multimorbidity-and-the-benefit-is-delay-not-avoidance).

⚠ It does not move this map's Status, and that is a rule rather than a judgement. shared/evidence-model.md sets Status by highest-tier-wins. The result that made multidomain-lifestyle contested is MAPT — a randomised trial with a null primary endpoint. This is cohort tier. A cohort-tier positive does not outrank an RCT-tier null, however much more interesting its endpoint is. Recorded as the strongest thing on the other side of a live tension, not as its resolution.

And it inherits the bundle problem whole. The intervention was diet, activity and sustained coaching delivered together. This says the package is durable. It says nothing about which part of it did the work.
