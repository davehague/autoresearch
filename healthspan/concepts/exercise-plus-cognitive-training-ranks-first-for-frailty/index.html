Exercise plus cognitive training tops the frailty leaderboard — and the leaderboard has no scores on it

A network meta-analysis of 22 randomised trials in community-dwelling older adults with frailty or prefrailty ranked non-drug bundles by SUCRA — the surface under the cumulative ranking curve, which is the probability a treatment lands among the better ones, averaged over all possible ranks. For frailty level: exercise + cognitive 84.7%, exercise + cognitive + social support 83%, exercise + nutrition 72%. For motor ability: exercise + nutrition + psychological 77.1%.

⚠ A SUCRA percentage is not an effect size. 84.7% does not mean "84.7% effective" and does not mean it beat the next option by 1.7 points — and a leaderboard can stay sharply ordered while every credible interval overlaps every other one (frailty-network-ranks-bundles-without-reporting-effect-sizes).

The population is a clinical one. Frailty is a measured syndrome — weight loss, exhaustion, weakness, slow gait, low activity — identifying older adults at high risk of falls and dependence. Nothing here transfers to a healthy middle-aged adult without an argument this source does not make.

What the ranking does support is narrow and worth having: every-top-ranked-frailty-bundle-contains-exercise. What it does not support is any claim about a component on its own — multidomain-lifestyle's standing rule (multidomain-nutrition-exercise-bundle-not-separable) holds, and a bundle leaderboard is exactly the artefact most likely to tempt someone into breaking it.

Rebutted by: adding-cognitive-training-to-vr-exercise-made-memory-worse-than-plain-remote-exercise — a 51-trial Bayesian network found the exercise-plus-cognitive-training combination significantly inferior to plain remote exercise for memory (SMD −1.54, 95% CI −2.89 to −0.24). The tension resolves into a scope condition rather than a contradiction: the combined bundle's advantage holds where it was measured — frailty — and does not transfer to memory, where adding the cognitive task appears to cost something.
