A protein supplement lowers LDL by about three milligrams per decilitre, which is a real direction and a small quantity

Pooling 32 randomised trials (39 arms, n=1,357) of high-quality protein supplements — milk protein, casein, whey, soy — in adults with overweight or obesity, LDL cholesterol fell 3.45 mg/dL (95% CI −5.77 to −1.13, P=.004) and total cholesterol fell 5.70 mg/dL (−10.20 to −1.20, P=.013).

The two estimates cohere, and coherence is the reason to believe the direction. LDL-C is a major constituent of total cholesterol; a genuine LDL reduction should appear inside a total-cholesterol reduction of similar or larger magnitude, and −3.45 sitting inside −5.70 is precisely that. This is the internal-coherence check the vault proposed on 2026-08-24 after meeting a source whose two significant estimates could not both be true; this source passes it.

Now read the magnitude. Against a typical LDL of 120–130 mg/dL in this population, 3.45 mg/dL is about a 3% reduction. statins-primary-prevention holds a drug class that moves the same number by 30–50%. Protein supplementation is not a lipid intervention and this note should not be read as an argument for using it as one.

What it adds to protein-target. That map is seventeen dated bullets about a single question — how much total daily protein drives the training adaptation, with the answer near 1.6 g/kg/day and everything above it flat. This is the map's first source on a non-muscle outcome, and its practical content is a reassurance rather than a recommendation: hitting a protein target does not appear to cost anything on the lipid panel, and may nudge it favourably. Status on the map does not move; a new outcome family at suggestive strength does not disturb a consolidating verdict built on a different endpoint.

Why capped at suggestive. Source appraised mixed. The estimate attenuates when high-risk-of-bias trials are excluded and the attenuated value is not published in the abstract (the-protein-lipid-effect-attenuates-when-high-risk-of-bias-trials-are-removed); the review advertises a GRADE assessment in its title and prints no certainty rating; the choice between fixed- and random-effects models is unstated; and the constituent trials average about 35 people per arm.
