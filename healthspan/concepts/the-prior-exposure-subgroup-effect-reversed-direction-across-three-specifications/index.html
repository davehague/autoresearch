The same hypothesis, on the same data, came out positive, null and negative depending on how the model was written

The hypothesis was reasonable and pre-stated. People who have never taken a cognitive battery should get a bigger boost from taking it a second time than people who have taken it many times before, because the naive group has more to learn. ACHIEVE was unusually well-placed to test it: 238 participants came from the ARIC cohort and had sat the identical battery before; 739 were test-naive. The authors wrote the prediction into their introduction: "retest effects are larger among de novo than ARIC participants due to no prior exposure of cognitive testing."

⚑ Three specifications of the same model gave three different answers.

Minimally adjusted, binary retest+0.08 (0.02 to 0.15)Hypothesis confirmed

Fully adjusted, √(cumulative prior assessments)−0.26 (−0.61 to 0.09); on the global score including auditory tests −0.38 (−0.73 to −0.04); on memory −0.59 (−1.04 to −0.15)Hypothesis reversed, significantly

The two "significant" rows point in opposite directions. And the reversal is not a fringe result — under the square-root parameterisation it reaches −0.59 for memory, a large effect in the direction opposite to the prediction.

⚑ Why this note exists, and it is not a criticism of the paper's headline. The main retest finding — re-sitting-a-cognitive-battery-buys-back-about-a-year-of-decline — is stable across all three specifications, in both strata, and in the complete-case sensitivity analysis. It is the subgroup structure that is unstable, and the paper does not discuss the reversal. The authors state that interaction analyses were "exploratory," that "no adjustment was made for multiple comparisons," and that "isolated statistically significant findings were interpreted cautiously" — all correct, and all stated before the results rather than after. ⚑ But a specification that flips a pre-stated hypothesis from confirmed to refuted, reported in a table and left uncommented, is exactly the kind of thing a ledger should record rather than a reader should rediscover.

⚑ What it means substantively. Prior testing experience does not reliably predict how large someone's practice effect will be — at least not in this dataset, at this sample size, with this many prior-assessment counts to model. That in turn means a study cannot reassure itself that its cohort is "already practised" and therefore immune. Both strata here showed the effect: ARIC +0.11 (0.03 to 0.18), de novo +0.12 (0.07 to 0.17). People who had done the battery before still improved on it again.

The generalisable lesson. ⚑ When a subgroup result flips sign across reasonable specifications of the same model, the honest summary is "we do not know," not whichever version the abstract reports. The main effect earned its suggestive grade; this subgroup earned nothing.

Capped suggestive. stance: pushes-back — against its own source's stated hypothesis and against the minimally-adjusted result a casual reader would take from the table.
