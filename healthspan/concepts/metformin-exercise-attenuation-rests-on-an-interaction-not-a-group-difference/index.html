"Metformin blunted the training response" is a claim about rates of change, and the two groups never actually differed

The trial that gave the vault its first number for the metformin–exercise interaction reports three contrasts on the same underlying measurement, and they do not agree. Keeping them separate is what stops a real but fragile signal from being read as a settled one.

Contrast one — the interaction term, which is what the paper leads with. A group × time interaction asks whether the amount of change over time differed between arms. On absolute aerobic capacity it did: p = 0.042 (η²p = 0.191).

Contrast two — the same measure, scaled to body weight. Aerobic capacity is conventionally reported per kilogram, because a bigger person consumes more oxygen at the same fitness. On that version the interaction does not reach significance: p = 0.088. Same people, same test, same five weeks; one denominator apart, and the finding disappears. Neither expression is wrong, and the paper reports both — but a result that survives one normalisation and not the other is, by construction, a borderline result.

Contrast three — the one a reader intuitively assumes was tested. Were the metformin and placebo groups different at the end? No, on either measure: p = 0.159 absolute, p = 0.225 scaled. Both arms improved significantly from their own baseline; neither ended up measurably fitter than the other.

Why this distinction is not pedantry. "Metformin blunts the cardiorespiratory adaptation to training" is a sentence that reads as people on the drug end up less fit. What the data support is narrower: over five weeks the drug arm's fitness rose more slowly, on one of two ways of expressing it, in a trial too small to separate the groups. The authors state the limitation themselves in their Methods — the sample size was computed for a within-group before-versus-after comparison (9 per arm for 80% power), and "with n = 11 per group, the study is underpowered for robust detection of interaction effects." They add that five weeks is shorter than the 8–12 weeks conventionally required for cardiorespiratory adaptation to stabilise, so the comparison is taken while the outcome is still in motion.

This is not the same failure mode as the vault's usual one, and the difference is worth naming. The recurring pattern this ledger polices is a null primary outcome with a winning secondary that travels — the COSMOS shape (cocoa-flavanol-cvd-benefit-is-estimator-dependent, vitamin-d-depression-conclusion-rests-on-an-uncorrected-subgroup-of-a-null-trial). Here nothing is concealed and nothing is swapped: every contrast is reported in the same paragraph, the disagreeing one included, and the authors flag their own underpowering. The reading discipline is the same, the misconduct is absent. What is required is simply that the claim be carried at the strength its weakest contrast supports.

Graded single-study deliberately. This is a reading of one trial's internal statistics, not an independent finding, and it constrains a claim rather than making one. It cannot move any map by itself and is not intended to.
