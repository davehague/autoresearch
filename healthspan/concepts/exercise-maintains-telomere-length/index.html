Exercise maintains telomere length in pooled RCTs — a real randomized signal on a noisy surrogate

Across 16 RCTs (1,908 adults), exercise intervention maintained telomere length versus control: SMD 0.59 (95% CI 0.14–1.06, P=0.01). Interventions of ≥16 weeks were required for meaningful effects. Egger's test found no publication bias (t=0.46, P=0.66).

Why this matters for the vault. It completes a comparison the vault has been assembling on cellular-aging surrogates without meaning to. omega-3 was null on telomere length in the VITAL sub-study (omega3-no-telomere-effect). vitamin-d did slow telomere attrition in that same sub-study (vitamin-d-preserves-telomere-length) — post-hoc, in a trial null on every hard endpoint it powered for. Now exercise, randomized and independently funded (a Chinese national social-science grant, no supplement or device maker), moves the same marker. The free intervention outperforms the pills on the pills' preferred biomarker.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, and why no map upgrades).

  • The estimate is fragile. I²=92%, meta-regression explains only 25.2% of the variance, and the confidence interval's lower bound (0.14) nearly touches null. Telomere-length assays differ across trials (qPCR, Flow-FISH, Southern blot), which is itself a documented source of noise. The direction is more trustworthy than the magnitude.
  • It is a surrogate. Telomere length is a biomarker of cellular aging, not healthspan. The source's title ("Exercise delays aging") outruns its endpoints. The vault refused to upgrade vitamin-d on a telomere finding and applies the same rule here — zone-2-cardio and resistance-training keep their emerging Status, which is gated by the observational ceiling of their mortality evidence, not by a biomarker.
  • Population mismatch. Most pooled trials enrolled clinical populations (cancer, PCOS, post-MI, menopause), not healthy middle-aged adults.
