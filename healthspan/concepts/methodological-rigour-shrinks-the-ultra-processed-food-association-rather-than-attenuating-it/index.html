The UPF review says its flaws hide a bigger effect; its own results say its flaws produce the effect

Mekonnen 2026 concludes that "methodological limitations may attenuate the observed associations" — i.e. that the true harm of ultra-processed food is larger than the pooled RR 1.09 it reports. That is a theoretical argument, and it has a real basis: 38 of 46 studies (82.6%) adjusted for variables that are plausibly mediators — baseline hypertension, diabetes, BMI, overall diet quality. Adjusting for the pathway through which processed food would cause heart disease does bias the estimate toward the null.

But everywhere the same paper can measure which way rigour pushes, it pushes the other way.

  1. Energy adjustment. Studies using the crudest handling of total calories (standard model only) report estimates RR 1.04 (1.002–1.07) higher than studies using other methods, and estimates are significantly more attenuated where both the residual and standard methods were applied (p = 0.009) — the more careful practice. See ultra-processed-food-effect-size-tracks-the-energy-adjustment-method.
  2. Risk of bias. In the ROBINS-E subgroup analyses, "the pooled risk ratio for CVD events was slightly attenuated among studies with high or some concerns regarding bias in exposure measurement and participant selection, compared with those at low risk of bias" — and, in the other direction and more starkly, "the association with hypertension was stronger in studies judged at high risk of bias due to selection of participants."

The CVD row of (2) is the one point that supports the authors, and they note the subgroup differences were not statistically significant. The hypertension row runs against them. The energy-adjustment result — the only moderator in the whole meta-regression that reached significance — runs against them.

This is not an accusation of dishonesty; the paper publishes every number used here. It is a note that the review's abstract states a direction of bias that the review's results section does not support, and that a reader who only reads the conclusion will carry away the opposite of what was measured. This vault records both and prefers the measurement.

What would settle it. A randomised feeding trial of matched diets differing only in processing level, with a hard cardiovascular endpoint — which does not exist and probably cannot be run at the required duration. Failing that: a synthesis restricted to the handful of studies using a causal diagram to select confounders (there are two in 46) and reporting absolute risks.
