The dynamometer that measures the trained joint moved; the one that generalises did not

protein-target carries protein-supplementation-augments-resistance-training from Morton 2018's 49-trial meta-analysis, and that claim is well founded. This trial adds a boundary to it rather than a rebuttal, and the boundary is visible in the disagreement between two strength instruments in the same participants.

Isokinetic knee testing moved. Peak torque normalised to body weight rose in the Protein arm for extensors (p = 0.002 dominant, 0.003 non-dominant) and flexors (p = 0.02, 0.001), with total work up in the dominant extensors (p = 0.008).

Handgrip strength did not move in anybody. No time effect, no group effect, in any of the three arms.

⚑ Those are the trained muscles and the untrained ones. The resistance-training programme included knee flexion and extension work; the Biodex measured knee flexion and extension. Grip was not trained and grip did not change. What that pattern looks like is a modest, local training-adaptation effect - which is not what "supplemental protein makes you stronger" is usually taken to mean.

⚠ And the increment sits on top of an already-adequate diet, which is the part that transfers. Every arm, including the flavoured-water control, was counselled by nutritionists to 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day. So this is not protein versus no protein; it is 20 g of extra post-workout whey versus hitting a target through food. The control arm hit the same functional endpoint - all three arms improved equally on the SPPB.

⚠ Note the target used here is well below this map's anchor. The trial used the PROT-AG 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day recommendation for adults over 65; the map's anchor from Morton 2018 is ~1.6 g/kg/day. This trial's "adequate" baseline is lower than the vault's, so the measured increment was added on top of a lower floor than protein-target recommends - and a supplement added to an insufficient intake is a different experiment from one added to a sufficient intake.

⚠ Body composition showed no within-group change in any arm, measured by bioelectrical impedance. Between-group differences at twelve weeks (fat mass p = 0.015, BMI p = 0.010, body-fat percentage p = 0.017, Control vs Protein) appeared without any group changing from its own baseline, which points at where the groups started rather than at what happened to them - and the arms were demonstrably imbalanced at baseline on several measures.

⚑ The trial's honesty about its own weakest positive result is worth recording. HOMA-IR fell in the Protein arm (time effect p = 0.001, group effect p = 0.03), and the authors immediately note that this arm started with the highest insulin and HOMA-IR, invoke regression to the mean themselves, and report that no group x time interaction was detected - the interaction being the actual test of a differential treatment effect.
