Hearing aids work well for the thing hearing aids are for

Across 8 randomised trials and 1,683 people, hearing aids improved hearing-specific health-related quality of life — the extent to which hearing loss stops someone participating in their own life — by a standardised mean difference of −1.25 (95% CI −1.63 to −0.87), at GRADE moderate certainty.

What a standardised mean difference is, and why this one is unusual. When trials measure the same construct with different questionnaires, each result is rescaled into "how many standard deviations apart the two groups ended up" so they can be pooled. The convention reads 0.2 as small, 0.5 as moderate, 0.8 as large. ⚑ −1.25 is very large — the largest effect this vault holds on any intervention. The Cochrane review does the thing most reviews skip and converts it back to a real instrument: about a 27-point improvement on the 0–100 Hearing Handicap Inventory for the Elderly, a questionnaire asking how much hearing loss interferes with daily activities.

Moderate certainty means: moderately confident the true effect is close to this, with a real possibility it is substantially different. Cochrane downgraded exactly one level, for risk of bias, and explicitly declined to downgrade again for heterogeneity (I² = 87%) on the ground that the effect was large enough to survive the disagreement between trials.

Listening ability moved by essentially the same amount — SMD −1.28 (−2.41 to −0.15) across 5 trials and 622 people, moderate certainty, roughly a 20-point move on the Abbreviated Profile of Hearing Aid Benefit. Note the interval: it runs from enormous (−2.41) to almost nothing (−0.15), with I² = 96%. The trials do not agree on the size, only on the direction.

⚠ The effect shrinks by a factor of about five when the question widens. General health-related quality of life — not hearing-specific, just life — moved only SMD −0.27 (−0.46 to −0.09) across 1,558 people, at low certainty, which the review back-translates as a 4-point move on a 0–100 disability scale. ⚑ The narrower the question, the bigger the answer. Hearing aids substantially fix the hearing-shaped hole; they nudge the rest of life.

⚑ And the finding must be read next to how it was measured. Every one of these outcomes is a questionnaire answered by a person who knows perfectly well whether they were given a hearing aid — 15 of the 16 trials are at high or unclear risk of performance or detection bias, and where placebo devices were used the blind demonstrably broke (placebo-hearing-aids-failed-to-blind-more-than-eighty-percent-of-participants). That does not make −1.25 wrong; a device that makes speech audible plausibly should produce a large change in self-rated hearing difficulty. It does mean this is the vault's clearest example of a large effect and an unblindable outcome arriving together, and the honest reading is that the number is partly a measurement of the device and partly a measurement of knowing you have one.

In one sentence: the best-evidenced claim about hearing aids is that they substantially reduce self-reported hearing difficulty, which is both a genuinely large real-world benefit and very close to a definition of the device.
