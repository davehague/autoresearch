Against a sham cuff, the occlusion contributed nothing — the rehabilitation did all of it

Eighty-four service members with patellofemoral pain, randomised to 80% limb occlusion pressure or a 20 mmHg sham, on an otherwise identical nine-week programme of neuromuscular electrical stimulation and exercise. Both arms improved substantially and identically. Knee function on the Anterior Knee Pain Scale rose from 66.7 to 79.0 (sham) and 69.5 to 76.9 (real BFR) — time effect F(1,201) = 49.5, P < .0001; group P = .15; interaction P = .26. Pain fell in both. Physical quality of life climbed from well below the population norm to nearly normal in both — 40.6 → 48.2 and 43.4 → 48.5 — time P < .0001, group P = .15, interaction P = .57. The parent trial had already reported no between-arm difference in strength or physical performance.

⚑ The comparator is what makes this different from almost everything else in blood-flow-restriction. The corpus is built overwhelmingly on BFR + light exercise versus light exercise alone, a contrast that confounds the occlusion with the cuff, the equipment, the extra clinical attention and the expectation of receiving a novel therapy. This trial gives both arms the same cuff, the same device, the same session structure, and the same 45-minute inflation cycles — and varies only the pressure. That is the isolating contrast this vault keeps asking supplement and device literatures for, and when it is applied here, the added effect of occlusion is not detectable on any efficacy outcome.

Two further design credits worth naming. The pressure was individualised via the Delfi Personalized Tourniquet System — the limb-occlusion-pressure method that the corpus's own authors call the methodological gold standard, and that the vault's highest-pressure source conceded it did not use (bfr-cuff-pressure-reaches-350-mmhg-under-a-seventh-prescription-convention). And the outcomes are validated, population-normed instruments measured at four timepoints, not two.

Read as pushes-back, and bounded honestly. This does not show that BFR never works — five prior sources in this vault recorded strength gains from adding it to light exercise, and this trial is in an injured, painful, rehabilitating knee rather than in healthy volunteers, which is a genuinely different physiological setting. What it shows is that in the population where BFR's clinical case is strongest — rehabilitation of an injured knee, where heavy loading is not an option — a properly shammed comparison found no added pain relief, no added function, no added physical wellbeing, and no added strength.

The nulls are not sold as equivalence here and the vault does not read them that way. No power analysis exists for these outcomes (this is a secondary analysis), and the paper's own framing is "similar improvements" rather than demonstrated equivalence. shared/study-appraisal.md fatal flag #3 was weighed and not fired because the paper's conclusions do not rest on these nulls.

What makes the pairing consequential. The same trial found the 80%-occlusion arm losing ground on mental health-related quality of life (bfr-at-80-percent-occlusion-lowered-mental-quality-of-life-against-sham). A measured downside with no measured upside is a different object from an unproven benefit — and it is the first time this corpus has been able to put both sides of that ledger in the same randomised comparison.
