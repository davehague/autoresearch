COSMOS's pre-specified analysis was null, and this re-analysis does not change that

COSMOS — a randomised, placebo-controlled, 2×2 factorial trial of 500 mg/d cocoa flavanols in 21,442 healthy older US adults over a median 3.6 years, with adjudicated hard endpoints and intention-to-treat analysis — did not demonstrate a significant reduction in total cardiovascular events in its pre-registered primary analysis. Within the 2026 re-analysis itself, Cox regression on the hierarchical composite returned HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.79–1.03) — non-significant, reported alongside the win-ratio result.

This is the vault's primary record of what COSMOS established about cocoa flavanols and cardiovascular disease: nothing, at conventional significance. The 2026 paper adds a post-hoc estimator that crosses p = 0.05 where the planned one did not (cocoa-flavanol-cvd-benefit-is-estimator-dependent). It does not revise the trial's registered result, and the vault does not let a secondary analysis overwrite a primary one.

Why the null is worth more than the positive here. The evidence quality runs the opposite way to the headline. The null comes from the pre-specified analysis of a large, well-conducted, blinded, hard-endpoint RCT — close to the best design available for a supplement question. The positive comes from a post-hoc re-analysis of the same data. On the vault's own rules, the pre-specified answer from a strong trial is the load-bearing evidence and the re-analysis is exploratory context.

Note also that this is a null on a healthy-primary-prevention population over a relatively short 3.6-year horizon — the same design constraint that limits what the vault reads into omega-3's and vitamin-d's primary-prevention nulls (VITAL, ASCEND).
