In adults with class 1–2 obesity, the same lifestyle intervention shows no cognitive-impairment effect — the hinge of a benefit-to-harm gradient

In the BMI 30–39 kg/m² stratum of Look AHEAD's post-trial cognitive follow-up — the middle and typically largest of the three BMI groups — the Intensive Lifestyle Intervention (ILI) showed no effect on incident clinically significant cognitive impairment versus Diabetes Support and Education (DSE): HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.85–1.16). This is the pivot point of a BMI-ordered gradient that runs from benefit at BMI 25–29 (multidomain-lifestyle-cognitive-impairment-benefit-overweight, HR 0.64) through this null middle to harm at BMI ≥40 (multidomain-lifestyle-cognitive-impairment-harm-severe-obesity, HR 1.40) — a monotonic-looking dose-response by baseline body weight, not a single subgroup anomaly.

Why this matters for the vault. A single benefit-vs-harm subgroup split (two arms) would be much easier to read as a coin-flip artifact of multiplicity. A three-way, ordered gradient — where the middle stratum sits almost exactly at the null between a protective arm and a harmful one — is a somewhat more structured pattern, and it is the shape that lends the interaction p=0.004 more credibility than an isolated two-arm split would. This note exists so the vault does not silently drop the "boring" null arm and only carry the two dramatic ones — the shape of the whole gradient is the finding, not just its extremes.

Bounded honestly (why capped suggestive). Same parent-source caveats as the other two arms of this interaction: no reported overall/main effect, mixed reliability, unconfirmed pre-specification for this 2026 analysis, and an unaddressed competing-risk-of-death threat over 12–14 years. A confidence interval of 0.85–1.16 is reasonably tight and centered near 1.0 — this looks like a genuine null rather than an underpowered non-result, which is itself informative (it is not "no effect because too few events"; class 1–2 obesity was likely the best-powered of the three strata).
