Against calorie restriction, intermittent fasting is a wash — only alternate-day fasting wins, and only short-term

The largest IF synthesis to date (network meta-analysis, 99 RCTs, n=6,582) compared alternate-day fasting (ADF), time-restricted eating (TRE), whole-day fasting (WDF), continuous energy restriction (CER), and ad libitum. Against ad libitum, weight loss sorted by restrictiveness: ADF −3.40 kg (high certainty), WDF −2.36, CER −2.11, TRE −1.72. Against CER — the contrast that decides whether fasting adds anything — only ADF beat calorie restriction (−1.29 kg, 95% CI 0.59–1.99, moderate certainty), TRE and WDF did not, and in trials ≥24 weeks (n=17) no IF strategy differed from CER at all.

Why this is the honest read of the whole map. The strategies line up by how much they restrict intake, and they converge with plain calorie restriction once you compare like with like. That is the signature of an effect driven by the energy deficit, not by meal timing or a fasting-specific mechanism — intermittent fasting is a delivery format for eating less. ADF's short-term edge is real but small and transient, exactly the kind of finding that does not survive to a durable recommendation.

Decision-relevant for GOAL.md. If the aim is fat loss, the choice among fasting formats is an adherence question, not a physiological one — which reinforces the map's standing point that TRE (an eating window, no calorie counting) is the lowest-friction variant and gives up nothing meaningful to the harsher schedules. No protocol is invented (shared/safety.md); the trials' windows are the evidence. Surrogate endpoint (weight), short trials, overweight/obese populations — so this strengthens the map's honest framing without moving it off emerging.
