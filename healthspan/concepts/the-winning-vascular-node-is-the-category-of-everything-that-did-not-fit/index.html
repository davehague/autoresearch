A leaderboard whose first place is occupied by "everything else"

The network's descriptive ranking put hybrid/mixed-modality training first for flow-mediated dilation, interval training first for pulse wave velocity, and continuous endurance plus combined training first for intima-media thickness. Three outcomes, three different winners.

⚑ The FMD winner is a residual category. The methods define HYB by exclusion — the arms that "could not be decomposed into clearly prescribed CET plus RT components," sweeping together vibration platforms, exergames, recreational activity, sedentary-behaviour-reduction programmes and rehabilitation platforms. It is not an intervention. It is the bin. The paper's own caution is that HYB programmes "may not share a uniform physiological mechanism," and CINeMA rates the comparison major concerns for imprecision and some concerns for indirectness.

⚠ The paper disclaims its own rankings, twice, and deserves the credit for it. Rankings are "imprecise," certainty "low," several comparisons "sparse or heterogeneous," and modality differences should be read "as exploratory rather than definitive." The subgroup analyses were deliberately relegated to the supplement "to avoid overemphasizing ecological comparisons in the main text." The problem is not the authors' restraint. It is that a rank order gets quoted and a CINeMA table does not.

⚑ This is the vault's continuing SUCRA discipline, in a new variant. Previously the objection has been that a leaderboard is unstable, or reports no effect sizes at all (frailty-network-ranks-bundles-without-reporting-effect-sizes), or collapses under its own quality filter (integrated-aerobic-resistance-training-survives-the-quality-filter-that-sinks-circuit-training). Tonight's variant is worse: the winner is underspecified not by accident but by construction, so no amount of extra data could make the rank mean anything.

⚠ Ranking recorded, leaderboard declined — the standing rule on exercise-for-blood-pressure, where two far larger networks already disagree about which modality wins while agreeing about the direction and magnitude.
