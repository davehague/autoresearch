The bleeding risk in omega-3 is about grams of EPA, not about fish oil

Within a review that found no overall bleeding excess for omega-3 (omega3-no-overall-increase-in-bleeding-risk), one pre-specified analysis came back positive: high-dose purified eicosapentaenoic acid raised bleeding risk by 50% in relative terms — RR 1.50 (95% CI 1.13–1.99), p = 0.006.

And the relationship was graded, not binary. Across all eleven trials, bleeding risk rose 0.24 (0.05–0.43) per gram of EPA, p = 0.02. That is the mechanism-consistent shape you would want before believing a dose claim: not a threshold discovered post hoc, but a slope.

Now translate the 50%, because the relative and absolute numbers tell different stories and both are true. The absolute increase was 0.6% (0.1–1.0%) — one additional bleed for every 166 people treated. A 50% relative increase on a small baseline is still a small number of people. The authors say so themselves, describing the clinical significance as "very modest," which is the correct reading and also, conveniently, the sponsor-friendly one (sponsor-adverse-findings-and-sponsor-friendly-framing-can-coexist).

Two things weaken the cell. It rests on two trials — REDUCE-IT and JELIS — and REDUCE-IT is the trial the authors themselves flag for having used mineral oil as a placebo, which they say "may have magnified its results." So the vault's strongest dose-related harm signal for omega-3 comes from its most contested included trial. The dose-response meta-regression across all eleven trials is the more reassuring support, because it does not depend on any single comparison.

This corroborates a flag the vault already raised for a different harm. omega3-atrial-fibrillation-gi-harm already carries safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] from Mattumpuram 2025's finding of increased atrial fibrillation and gastrointestinal adverse events at high dose. Two independent syntheses, two different harms, the same dose-dependence. The flag is no longer an extrapolation from one paper.

What it means for how this vault talks about omega-3. The distinction that matters is not fish oil versus no fish oil but supplemental dose versus pharmacological dose. Icosapent ethyl at trial doses is a prescription cardiovascular drug, not the capsule on a supplement shelf. shared/safety.md gates any item carrying a safety flag at "proposed — review with your doctor," and no dose is recommended here — the grams are recorded as what the trials administered.
