The entire accuracy literature for the vault's best predictor is six reviews, and none of them is good

Rated on AMSTAR 2 — the standard instrument for appraising a systematic review's methods — five of the six reviews came out low and the sixth critically low. None reached moderate or high. The overview's own rigour cannot exceed what it is reviewing, and this is the binding constraint on every number drawn from it.

The consequences are concrete. The authors were "limited to qualitatively synthesising the available evidence", so the quoted accuracy figures are ranges across measures rather than pooled estimates. Confidence intervals were missing for three of the four AUC values in the heart-failure review — the source review's failing, inherited and flagged.

The overview itself is well done, and the distinction matters. It is PROSPERO-registered, unfunded, conflict-free, screened in duplicate, and it measured overlap between reviews with the corrected covered area and dropped the lower-quality review where two shared 35% of their primary studies — the double-counting that most overviews of reviews do silently. The weakness is in the field, not in the paper reporting on it, and the authors lead with that finding rather than burying it.

Recorded as a reading discipline. Where a field's entire synthesis layer is rated low, a confident-sounding accuracy figure drawn from it inherits that rating.
