The near-miss that closed an axis is cleared by a larger pool — and the two results never disagreed

On 2026-07-13 magnesium-glycinate closed its metabolic axis on Amiri 2026: 15 RCTs, n = 1,085 in diabetic and pre-diabetic adults, HOMA-IR MD −0.74 (−1.58 to +0.09), p = 0.08, with dose and duration subgroups failing to rescue it (magnesium-null-on-insulin-resistance). The vault read that interval carefully at the time and wrote the honest thing: "the estimate lies almost entirely on the benefit side, and it misses significance by nine hundredths — so: not shown, and not excluded."

Mohammadi 2026 pools 78 RCTs and reports HOMA-IR −0.48 (−0.79 to −0.18), at GRADE moderate certainty.

The critical observation is that these two results do not contradict each other in the slightest. Amiri's point estimate (−0.74) is larger than tonight's (−0.48) and sits comfortably inside tonight's interval. Amiri's problem was never direction; it was width. A five-fold larger trial base narrowed the confidence interval and the same effect cleared zero. This is what an underpowered near-miss looks like when the power arrives — the vault's standing discipline of reading intervals rather than p-values is what makes the two readings compatible instead of contradictory.

⚠ What this does NOT establish. Three things hold the finding down:

(1) Different populations. Amiri enrolled diabetic and pre-diabetic adults exclusively — the group most likely to show a metabolic effect. Mohammadi pools "healthy individuals to patients with obesity, T2DM, HTN, MetS, and other chronic diseases." A significant effect in a mixed pool and a null in the enriched subpopulation is a strange combination if the effect is real and mechanism-driven, and neither paper can resolve it.

(2) No dose-response. Mohammadi is a dose-response meta-analysis, and it found no significant linear or nonlinear relationship between magnesium dose and HOMA-IR (magnesium-dose-response-analysis-found-no-dose-response-for-most-outcomes). If magnesium were lowering insulin resistance causally, more of it should generally do more.

(3) The magnitude. A HOMA-IR shift of 0.48 in a mixed population, with no established MCID, is not a result anyone would notice, and the authors decline to claim clinical relevance for any of their significant findings.

So the map's metabolic axis re-opens as an unresolved question rather than flipping to a positive claim. The suggestive cap — a mixed reliability verdict on a source whose certainty gradient runs backwards — means this cannot move a map on its own, and it does not. What it does do is retire a sentence: the axis is no longer "as closed as evidence of this quality can close it."

Rebutted by is written back onto magnesium-null-on-insulin-resistance, because a reader landing on that note alone would otherwise see a settled null.
