Eighteen hard sets a week grew as much muscle as thirty-three, over eight weeks

Twenty-five trained lifters progressed one leg's weekly quadriceps volume +120% above habitual and the other +20%, twice weekly for eight weeks, with every set taken to momentary concentric failure in a 9–12 rep range on both limbs. The prescriptions delivered a verified difference: 32.8 ± 5.9 versus 18.0 ± 3.3 weekly sets (P < 0.001) and 237,125 versus 138,416 kg of accumulated volume load.

Both legs grew (main effect of time F(1,72) = 61.02, P < 0.001) and their growth trajectories did not differ (protocol × time interaction F(1,72) = 1.70, P = 0.197). Fascicle length and pennation angle were likewise unmoved.

⚑ Why this null is graded above the six that preceded it on this map. resistance-training-programming exists partly to catalogue underpowered nulls written up as equivalence, and the objection has always been power. This trial has a prospective, simulation-based power analysis — 10,000 simulations, 80% power, α = 0.05, an assumed between-limb correlation of r = 0.78, and a deliberately conservative effect assumption (d = 0.50 rather than the d ≈ 0.75 reported in prior volume work), giving Cohen's f = 0.20. It required 24 participants and 25 completed. It was registered before it ran (REBEC RBR-2f836zh), reported a CONSORT flow from 138 screened, achieved 98.5% session adherence, blinded the ultrasound assessors to limb allocation, standardised post-exercise protein at 30 g whey after every session, and took no commercial funding. The source is appraised reliable — the first null on this map to earn it.

⚠ And the boundary that keeps this suggestive rather than strong. 80% power for f = 0.20 means "no moderate-or-larger difference," not "no difference." A small genuine advantage to higher volume — the kind that compounds across years rather than eight weeks — sits entirely inside this trial's confidence region. The outcome is a surrogate measured by ultrasound, not MRI, over eight weeks, in 18-to-35-year-olds. Powered is not the same as powered for everything, and the paper's title ("do not impair muscle hypertrophy") overstates what its own NEW & NOTEWORTHY block correctly hedges as "no evidence… under the present experimental conditions."

⚠ Two things in the results the paper reports and does not interpret. (1) There is a significant main effect of protocol (F(1,72) = 9.71, P = 0.002) — VOL120 limbs were larger overall — with no interaction. In 25 people randomised only by limb dominance, the parsimonious reading is a pre-existing between-limb imbalance, not an effect of training. It does not threaten the interaction null, but it is the sentence that becomes "higher volume produced larger muscles" if nobody writes it down. (2) "+120% versus +20%" is a 6:1 progression ratio and reads like a sixfold difference in work. The achieved difference is 1.82× the sets and 1.71× the load. Substantial, but not the extreme dose the framing implies.

The hypothesis this refuses was not invented for the paper. Prior unilateral work found limbs progressed 83–120% above baseline grew less than contralateral limbs at +20%, and a separate trial found large back-squat volume increases produced no growth at all over six weeks. There was a real prior signal that big volume jumps hurt, and this randomised test did not reproduce it. A null that contradicts its authors' motivating literature flatters nobody.

What it does not say. Nothing about middle-aged or untrained lifters. Nothing beyond eight weeks. And nothing about safety that can be attributed — three of 28 participants withdrew with persistent muscle or joint pain, and the within-subject design cannot say which volume caused it (within-subject-unilateral-design-strengthens-a-training-null-without-rescuing-it).
