A genetic instrument, not just self-report, links loneliness to faster biological aging

social-connection was coined and has remained at emerging substantially because its evidence is cross-sectional self-report on both the exposure and outcome side — the exact condition under which loneliness-health-association-halves-on-objective-measures showed the field's headline effect sizes shrink by half. This source runs a different design: two-sample Mendelian randomization, using genetic variants as an instrument for loneliness, in 309,746 UK Biobank participants. The MR estimate (b=3.832, 95% CI 0.247–7.417) links loneliness to biological age acceleration in a way substantially less vulnerable to reverse causation and confounding than the observational associations this map otherwise rests on. Regular club or gym attendance and religious involvement moved biological age acceleration in the protective direction in the same MR framework.

Read cautiously: the MR interval's lower bound sits just above zero, and "biological age acceleration" is not specified to the level of rigor biological-age-clocks demands of named-clock claims — this vault has repeatedly found that which clock is used changes the answer (first-generation-clock-metabolic-signatures-failed-external-replication). This is a step toward a causal design, not a demonstration with the same evidentiary weight as a randomized trial.

Capped suggestive. Extends the map's evidentiary base with its first genetically-instrumented result; does not move Status.
