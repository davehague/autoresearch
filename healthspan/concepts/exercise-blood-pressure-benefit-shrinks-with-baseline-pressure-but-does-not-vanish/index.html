Exercise lowers blood pressure in people who do not have high blood pressure — about 4 mmHg less, not zero

Of five candidate effect modifiers tested in a 159-trial Bayesian network — age as a number, age as a category, intervention duration, measurement posture, and baseline blood pressure — only baseline blood pressure changed the answer. Every 10 mmHg higher a group started, the more exercise lowered it, by 2.49 mmHg (95% credible interval −3.73 to −1.24). Framed the other way round, groups whose mean starting pressure was at or above 140 mmHg gained 3.86 mmHg more (−6.54 to −1.09) than groups below it.

⚑ Why this is the most decision-relevant number in the source. The obvious objection to every exercise-and-blood-pressure result is that it was measured in people with hypertension, where there is more to lower — so it tells a normotensive person nothing. This network is the test of that objection, because it is mostly normotensive: only 82 of 379 arms, and 2,458 of 10,821 participants, had a mean baseline at hypertension level. Every headline estimate above therefore comes predominantly from people whose blood pressure was already normal — and every modality still lowered it.

So the honest form of the caveat is a discount, not an exclusion. Someone starting near a normal systolic pressure should expect roughly 4 mmHg less than the figures reported in hypertensive-only syntheses — which is why an independent network restricted to diagnosed hypertension put combined training at −9.52 mmHg (combined-training-is-the-best-evidenced-exercise-lever-for-systolic-pressure) while this one puts it at −7.72. Two networks, two populations, and a moderator that reconciles the gap almost exactly.

⚠ What it does not establish. This is a study-level meta-regression, not an analysis of individuals: it compares trial averages, so it is exposed to the ecological fallacy — a relationship between group means need not hold within groups. Regression to the mean pushes in the same direction as the finding and cannot be separated from it at this level, since groups selected for high pressure will drift down regardless. The paper reports no threshold below which the effect disappears, and the analysis was not designed to find one. Nothing here says a normotensive person should exercise for blood pressure, or that a few millimetres of mercury changes anyone's risk. Capped suggestive.

⚠ This pushes back on a claim this vault wrote four days ago. exercise-for-blood-pressure opened with "none of these millimetre figures transfer to someone with normal blood pressure." That was the correct reading of a hypertensive-only network; on a mostly-normotensive one it is too strong. The direction was right, the magnitude was wrong, and the correction is a measured discount rather than a reversal.
