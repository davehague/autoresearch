The literature is most certain exactly where its answer is "nothing"

Mohammadi 2026 pooled 78 randomised trials of magnesium supplementation across twenty-one cardiometabolic outcomes and graded every one of them with GRADE. Ten reached significance. Eleven did not. Read as a headline, that is a broadly positive result for magnesium. Read against the certainty column, it inverts.

The two things this literature is confident about are that magnesium does not change your body fat and does not change your TNF-α. The two results with the largest-sounding numbers — triglycerides −9.24 mg/dL and LDL-C −3.21 mg/dL — sit at the bottom of the certainty scale. And the paper's own publication-bias tests fire on those same outcomes: verbatim, "Egger's test revealed possible publication bias for LDL-C and BW, whereas Begg's test suggested possible publication bias for TG." Publication bias in this literature's direction makes those two estimates more likely over-stated than under-stated.

The magnitudes are small even taken at face value, and the authors say so twice. "These effects were generally modest, and their clinical relevance remains uncertain." And: "these results indicate favorable changes in intermediate cardiometabolic biomarkers rather than definitive evidence of clinically meaningful therapeutic effects." They add that MCIDs have not been established for most of these markers, so no one can say which of these numbers a person would notice. An HbA1c shift of 0.15% and an LDL-C shift of about 0.08 mmol/L are not results anyone would feel.

Why this is filed as an open-question rather than as a confirmation or a rebuttal. magnesium-glycinate wrote on 2026-08-02 that "magnesium is not a metabolic lever, and the axis is as closed as evidence of this quality can close it." That sentence can no longer stand as written — a 78-trial pool reports ten significant cardiometabolic effects, from a larger base than either source that closed the axis. But it is not replaced by the opposite claim either. A suggestive-capped source with an inverted certainty gradient, publication bias on its flagship results, and effects its own authors decline to call clinically relevant does not establish that magnesium is a metabolic lever. The specific rebuttal is carried by magnesium-lowers-homa-ir-where-a-smaller-pool-could-not, which names the claim it contradicts; this note describes the structure of the evidence around it. The axis re-opens as unresolved, and the map's Status does not move — the map's belief has always been about sleep, and this pool contains no sleep endpoint at all.
