The best-supported number in the paper says the winner wins imperceptibly

Of everything in this network meta-analysis, celecoxib's advantage on VAS pain is the estimate that best survives scrutiny: MD −0.48 (95% CI −0.78 to −0.17), an interval clearing zero, on the outcome with the most contributing trials (12).

And it is a difference below the threshold at which a patient reliably notices anything. The conventional minimal clinically important difference for VAS pain in knee osteoarthritis is around 1.5–2.0 points on a 0–10 scale. Half a point is roughly a quarter to a third of that.

So the top of the league table is occupied by a real, precisely-estimated, clinically imperceptible effect — which is exactly the situation SUCRA handles worst (sucra-rankings-can-invert-the-effect-sizes-they-are-built-from): a small, tight effect outranks a large, uncertain one, and the ranking reads like a recommendation.

Bounded hard. The source is appraised unreliable (its results text claims significance for estimates its own tables show crossing zero; its rankings invert between pain instruments). This estimate is recorded because it is the paper's most defensible number, not because it settles anything. Note also that the same drug ranks 4th of 5 on the WOMAC pain subscale in this same network (knee-oa-rankings-invert-between-two-pain-scales-in-one-network).
