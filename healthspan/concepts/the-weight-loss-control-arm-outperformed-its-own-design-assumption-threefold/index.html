The control arm beat its own design assumption threefold — which is what makes this null worth having

The design assumption. DELISH's sample size rested on a specific prediction: the Mediterranean-style arm would lose ≥5% of body weight at 24 months and the control about 1%, for a hypothesised between-arm difference of 4 percentage points. The 1% figure was not invented — it came from a meta-analysis of 15 randomised trials of behavioural weight-loss programmes delivered in primary care, pooling −1.2 kg (95% CI −2.3 to −0.2) at 24 months.

What the control actually did. Two years of free WeightWatchers produced −3.51% (−3.97 kg) by intention-to-treat, and −3.91% among participants who returned for the final visit. ⚑ The comparator outperformed the primary-care weight-loss literature by roughly a factor of three, and the authors flag it as "an unexpected finding." The Mediterranean arm hit its own target almost exactly among per-protocol participants (−5.10%). The intervention did what the designers hoped. The control did far more than they expected, and that is why there is no difference.

⚑ This is the shape the vault keeps asking for and almost never gets. shared/study-appraisal.md has now accumulated seven requests for a comparator-strength lens, every one triggered by the opposite problem — an active intervention banking a large effect against usual care, waitlist, education or nothing. The vault's standing examples: traditional-chinese-sports-ranking-is-unseparable-from-its-comparator, exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator, the-relaxation-sleep-literature-fails-its-own-publication-bias-test (12 of 14 comparators routine care or nothing), nutritional-supplementation-alone-beats-nothing-in-sarcopenic-obesity.

DELISH is the inverse and it demonstrates what the lens is for. A trial that recruits a genuinely effective active comparator converts an uninformative question into a useful one:

  • Against waitlist, the finding would have been "a Mediterranean-style programme causes weight loss" — true, unsurprising, and useless for deciding anything.
  • Against WeightWatchers, the finding is "a dietitian-delivered Mediterranean-style programme is not a better weight-loss tool than a good commercial one" — which is a decision.

⚠ And it forces the null to be read correctly. −0.11 percentage points (−1.79 to +1.57) means "no better than," not "does not work." Both arms lost about 4% of body weight and held it for two years. Anyone converting this trial into "the Mediterranean diet doesn't help you lose weight" has dropped the comparator, which is the only thing the trial measured against.

⚑ The generalisable habit: read the control arm's absolute result before reading the between-arm difference. A null against a comparator that lost 3.5% and a null against a comparator that lost 0.5% are different findings wearing the same P value. This trial prints both numbers; most do not.

⚠ One structural caveat the authors raise. There was no usual-care arm, so the control's unexpectedly good performance cannot be cleanly attributed to WeightWatchers rather than to trial participation, measurement visits, or the secular diffusion of weight-lowering medication through primary care during 2020–2024. The comparator was strong; exactly why is not established.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal.
