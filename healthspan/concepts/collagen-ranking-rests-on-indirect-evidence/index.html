Collagen "beats" whey on a SUCRA chart — built from one direct trial and a mechanism that runs backwards

Drummond 2026's network meta-analysis ranks collagen #1 of 13 protein supplements: fat-free mass SMD 0.94 (0.48–1.40), SUCRA 98.9% (an implied ~3.2 kg over 8–16 weeks), strength SMD 0.41 (0.09–0.73), SUCRA 88.1%, and a direct-looking collagen vs whey FFM SMD 0.78 (0.31–1.25), p = 0.0012. Read the methods and the ranking dissolves: exactly one trial directly compared collagen with whey. The rest of collagen's chart-topping position is indirect network inference, which CINeMA downgrades, and which the authors concede rests on "relatively few trials."

Two independent reasons the vault flags this rather than believing it.

(1) The magnitude fails a plausibility check. ~3.2 kg of fat-free mass in 8–16 weeks is elite-hypertrophy territory — hard to produce with optimal training and a complete protein. Here it is credited to collagen, which has low biological value and low branched-chain amino acids — i.e. it lacks the leucine that triggers muscle protein synthesis. The one property that would make a supplement least able to build muscle is exactly what collagen has, a fact the authors admit before reaching for speculative rescue mechanisms (glycine→endogenous creatine, microcirculation). When the mechanism runs backwards and the number is implausible, the number is the artifact.

(2) A SUCRA rank is not a measurement. It summarises a network's internal ordering and inherits every weakness of the indirect paths feeding it. The vault has seen this exact failure before: in Jiao 2026, stretching — the near-sham control — topped the cognition SUCRA leaderboard (exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported), which was the tell that the leaderboard was noise. Here a low-BCAA protein tops the muscle leaderboard: same tell.

So collagen is flagged as a candidate intervention and NOT adopted (§6.5) — the third such refusal in five days, after beta-alanine (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy) and probiotics (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance). Graded single-study/open-question: one direct trial and an implausible magnitude cannot support a claim, and — given the leucine mechanism — the prior against a ~3.2 kg advantage is strong. What would change the answer: adequately-powered direct RCTs of collagen vs whey (or vs an equal dose of a complete protein) on resistance-training body composition and strength, with a pre-specified primary.

For David: do not switch protein sources on the strength of this chart. Collagen is unproven for muscle, not disproven — and mechanistically unlikely to beat whey. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md; no map coined.
