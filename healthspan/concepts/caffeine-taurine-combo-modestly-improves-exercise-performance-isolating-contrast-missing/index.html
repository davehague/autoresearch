Caffeine-plus-taurine combinations modestly improve exercise performance, but no trial isolates taurine's contribution

An 11-RCT meta-analysis (n=234) of caffeine-taurine co-supplementation versus placebo found modest improvements in time-to-exhaustion (SMD 0.54) and peak anaerobic power (SMD 0.55), and a reduction in perceived exertion — but heart rate was unaffected and the reaction-time result, despite a large pooled SMD, is undermined by very high heterogeneity and a prediction interval crossing zero. A product-level sensitivity analysis including one commercial energy drink produced a smaller estimate the authors say "cannot be attributed specifically to caffeine and taurine."

Why this matters for the vault. Every one of the 11 pooled trials compares the combination against placebo — none isolates caffeine alone versus caffeine+taurine. This is the identical missing-contrast pattern taurine already flagged in Saedmocheshi 2026 (taurine-exercise-trial-omits-the-contrast-that-would-isolate-taurine): a result attributed to taurine (or to the combination) that the trial design cannot actually separate from caffeine's own, well-established acute effect. Filed primarily on caffeine-timing as an acute-performance finding, in the same convention as the Jiannine/Miraftabi/Zhang/Bingol Diedhiou/Chen sources already there — it says nothing about the evening sleep-cutoff claim that is this map's actual case.

Bounded honestly. Small base (234 participants across 11 trials), no isolating contrast anywhere in the pool, and the authors' own product-level sensitivity analysis undercuts attribution to the specific ingredient pair.

The practical shape of the claim. No evidence here that taurine itself contributes anything beyond what caffeine alone already does; the combination's modest benefit may be entirely a caffeine effect. Cross- linked to taurine, extending (not resolving) that map's standing complaint about the missing isolating contrast. No dose recommended.
