Without a training stimulus, creatine alone did nothing measurable in 8 weeks — and its precursor did

An 8-week double-blind pilot gave adults over 65 either creatine monohydrate or an equimolar dose of guanidinoacetic acid (GAA), creatine's direct metabolic precursor — with no resistance-training stimulus in either arm. Creatine alone did not significantly move handgrip strength, fat-free mass, muscle mass, body-cell mass or total body water. GAA did, on all of them, within-group.

This is not new information about creatine — creatine-monohydrate already holds a 37-RCT context-stratified meta-analysis (Gu 2026) showing creatine's body-composition benefit requires resistance training and is flat-to-null without it (creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training). This pilot's creatine arm reproduces that null in a fresh, smaller sample. The genuinely new claim is the comparative one: in the same no-training conditions where creatine does nothing, GAA moved several outcomes, with a between-group handgrip effect (g=0.91) whose 95% CI lower bound sits exactly at zero.

Read cautiously, for three reasons. First, n=21 completers total with no reported power calculation and no located trial registration — the profile shared/study-appraisal.md treats as underpowered-null territory, except here it is an underpowered positive, which deserves the same skepticism. Second, body composition was measured by bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), an instrument this vault has directly caught reporting a phantom hydration-compartment shift as muscle gain elsewhere (a-muscle-gain-that-did-not-appear-in-the-compartment-containing-it) — "total body water" is literally one of the outcomes GAA is credited with moving. Third, both arms' myostatin fell (GAA more), which is the one biomarker reading with a coherent anabolic mechanism behind it, but IGF-1 was undetectable in most participants, so the growth-hormone axis cannot corroborate it.

Not a reason to add GAA to anything. GAA has its own pharmacology — it can raise plasma homocysteine, which this trial measured but did not report by arm in the abstract — and one small, unregistered, BIA-based pilot is a thinner evidentiary base than several candidates this vault has already refused (e.g. beta-alanine, refused on a comparably thin single node). No guanidinoacetic-acid map is coined. Filed as an open question on creatine-monohydrate pending an adequately powered, DXA-based, ideally training-controlled follow-up.
