Front-loading protein at breakfast did not build muscle in older adults — and could not have proved it doesn't

Seven randomised trials in adults over 60 found no benefit of morning protein on lean mass (SMD −0.11), grip strength (−0.01) or lower limb strength (−0.11). The authors' own summary: it "may not improve muscle mass, strength, or physical performance among older adults… But the evidence is very uncertain."

What an SMD of −0.11 with an interval to +0.18 actually excludes. A standardised mean difference expresses the gap between groups in standard deviations, so results measured in different units can be pooled; conventionally 0.2 is small, 0.5 moderate, 0.8 large. An interval running from −0.41 to +0.18 therefore rules out moderate-or-larger effects in either direction and rules out nothing else. A small real benefit sits comfortably inside it. This is a failure to demonstrate benefit, not a demonstration of no benefit — the vault's standing refusal, applied for the eleventh time, and graded single-study accordingly.

What it does legitimately add: a second independent line pointing the same way. protein-target's position — that timing and distribution are near-noise and the daily total is the lever — has rested since 2026-07-07 on Casuso 2025, 5 RCTs and 193 people in general adults (protein-timing-distribution-null-on-lean-mass). This is 7 RCTs and 305 people, an independent Japanese rehabilitation-and-epidemiology group, in adults over 60 — the population where age-related anabolic resistance would most plausibly have made timing matter. Two unrelated literatures, same direction, and the harder population did not break the rule. That is corroboration of direction, which is what the confirms stance records; it is not additional power.

⚠ The comparator caveat is binding and changes what the null is about. These trials compare morning protein against no morning protein — not against the same daily total eaten later. Unless daily intake was equalised, and the retrieved record does not say it was, this confounds timing with total protein (morning-protein-comparator-is-no-protein-not-redistribution). So it is weaker evidence about distribution than its title suggests, and the source is carried at that weaker reading.

A plausible reason the muscle outcomes were flat sits inside the same paper: the added protein displaced other food, reducing total daily energy intake (protein-at-breakfast-displaces-total-daily-energy-intake). A protein strategy that makes an older adult eat less overall is working against itself.

Population, stated so it is not dropped. Healthy and frail adults aged ≥60, pooled together. GOAL.md describes an experienced adult male doing aspirational weight lifting. Nothing here transfers as a finding.
