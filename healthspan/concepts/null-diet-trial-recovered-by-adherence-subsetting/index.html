A null trial's second life: subset by adherence and the effect appears

The MIND trial's pre-specified randomized comparison was null. Its post-hoc analysis, re-partitioning the same participants by how much their diet actually changed, is positive. Both statements describe the same 590 people, the same three years, and the same cognitive battery. The difference between them is that one compares groups formed by randomisation and the other compares groups formed by behaviour.

This is the second null-trial recovery the vault has ingested in five days, by a different technique.

Estimator shopping and adherence subsetting are distinct methodological failures with an identical product: a large, expensive, well-conducted trial reports nothing, and a secondary analysis of it reports something. Neither technique is fraudulent and neither paper hid what it did; both were appraised unreliable here and capped at single-study precisely so that neither can move a map alone.

Why the vault expects more of these rather than fewer. The incentive is structural: the trial is already funded, the null is already published, the dataset persists, and a positive secondary finding is publishable where a re-statement of the null is not. The operating rule this vault adopts: when a source's headline disagrees with the pre-specified primary analysis of the trial it is drawn from, the pre-specified analysis is the finding and the headline is a hypothesis.

The intention-to-treat principle is what is being set aside. shared/study-appraisal.md's RCT lens set already flags per-protocol and completer analyses as RED because they "reintroduce adherer bias." Sorting by a post-randomization behavioural variable is that failure in a more thorough form — not merely dropping non-completers, but making non-adherence the exposure contrast itself.

Read at its true strength. This concept is a methodological reading, not a new empirical result: it takes the parent trial's null (rct tier, genuinely well-conducted, active comparator, 93.4% completion) and the secondary analysis's positive association, and states which one governs. It is capped single-study because its source is the unreliable secondary paper; the underlying null it defends is stronger than this note's own grade.

For David: nothing to do, one thing to expect. Headlines of the form "it works if you actually stick to it" will keep appearing from trials that found nothing, and they are not evidence that it works.
