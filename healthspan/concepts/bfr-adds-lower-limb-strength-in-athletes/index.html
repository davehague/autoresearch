Occlusion added to training raised lower-limb strength — a large effect on a base of ~18 people per trial

Ten RCTs (n=181 athletes) found that adding blood flow restriction to resistance training improved lower-limb muscle strength, SMD 1.09 (0.52–1.66), with I² = 75%, and muscle size (MD 1.09, 0.10–2.09, I²=0%).

Read the base before the number. Ten trials averaging eighteen participants each. An SMD of 1.09 is a very large effect for a training add-on, and the combination of large effect + tiny trials + funnel-plot asymmetry is the textbook signature of small-study bias — which the authors explicitly concede they cannot exclude ("with few studies this approach has limited sensitivity, so small-study effects cannot be completely excluded"). The heterogeneity (I² = 75%) is highest on precisely the outcome being headlined. There is no PROSPERO registration and no GRADE assessment. The direction is credible; the magnitude is not bankable, and this note grades it suggestive for that reason.

Why the vault coined a map for this anyway. resistance-training carries safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]: heavy lower-limb loading (≥70% 1RM) produces the most strength (high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength) but its safety comparison was an underpowered null sold as reassurance (rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered), so against GOAL.md's knee constraint it is capped at "proposed — review with your doctor/physio." Low-load training with occlusion is the standard escape from exactly that trap — heavy-load adaptations at 20–40% 1RM, strength without the joint load. That makes BFR the most interesting idea in the vault for David's actual problem.

And this evidence does not deliver it. The trials added BFR to training in healthy young athletes and compared against RT or conventional training. Not one tested low-load BFR as a substitute for heavy load, in a middle-aged adult, with a knee to protect. The question the vault needs answered is not the question that was asked, and the population is not the population that matters (bfr-sport-performance-null shows the functional endpoint was flat, and bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials shows no adverse events were collected at all).

safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] is set deliberately. Not because harm was demonstrated — but because none was measured, in a technique whose mechanism is deliberately restricting arterial inflow to a limb, with cuff pressure that must be individualized to arterial occlusion pressure and that the trials themselves "rarely reported." shared/safety.md: a missed safety issue is worse than an over-cautious flag. The regimen expression is capped at "proposed — review with your doctor/physio." No pressure, no load, no protocol is recorded anywhere in this vault — none was reliably reported to record.
