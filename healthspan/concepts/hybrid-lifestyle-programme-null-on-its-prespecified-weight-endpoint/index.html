A 20-week supervised lifestyle programme missed its own weight target, and the interval is too wide to call it a failure

In the PRAMEV randomised trial, 20 weeks of a hybrid intensive lifestyle programme — group behavioural counselling, nutrition education, physical-activity promotion and continuous instructor support, added to usual medical care — did not beat usual medical care alone on its prespecified primary outcome. In the intention-to-treat analysis, 20.6% (20 of 97) versus 15.5% (16 of 103) achieved a ≥5% body-weight reduction: odds ratio 1.34, 95% CI 0.61 to 2.92, P = .46.

Read the interval before reading the p-value. An odds ratio of 1.34 with bounds at 0.61 and 2.92 is compatible with the programme cutting the odds of success by 39% and with it nearly tripling them. This is a trial that could not tell, not a trial that found nothing to find — the distinction shared/study-appraisal.md draws between an underpowered null and a demonstration of equivalence, applied here in the direction that is inconvenient for a sceptic rather than for an enthusiast. The trial's power calculation compounds it: 80% power was computed for a continuous contrast of 5 kg versus 2 kg mean weight loss (Cohen d = 0.79 — a very large assumed effect), while the endpoint actually tested was a dichotomised proportion, which discards information and costs power.

Why this is graded pushes-back on the map rather than filed as neutral. multidomain-lifestyle holds three randomised trials with positive main effects on cognitive test scores and one on physical function. This is the first randomised test on the map of an outcome that cannot be moved by attention, expectation or practice at the measured task — body weight on an instrument — and it did not move. That is a real constraint on how far the map's positive results generalise, and it sits alongside Look AHEAD's decade-long null as the second hard-outcome disappointment in this literature.

Bounded honestly. Open-label, single-centre, 20 weeks, 86% women, weight measured by bioelectrical impedance rather than a calibrated scale, and 39.2% of the intervention arm dropped out — which is a separate and larger problem, recorded at pramev-per-protocol-benefit-compares-two-differently-filtered-groups. Capped suggestive; Status on the map is unchanged.
