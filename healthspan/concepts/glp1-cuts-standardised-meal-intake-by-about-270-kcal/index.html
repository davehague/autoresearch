A GLP-1 removes about 270 kcal from a single unlimited meal — measured by weighing the food

Placebo-controlled trials sat people in front of more food than they could eat and weighed what was left. On a GLP-1 receptor agonist they ate 1132 kJ less — about 271 kcal — (95% CI −1449 to −815).

The instrument is unusually good, and that is the main reason to care. This is food weighed, not food recalled. Most dietary evidence in this vault runs on food-frequency questionnaires with known large validation error, and most of its behavioural evidence runs on people reporting how they felt. A weighed laboratory meal against a placebo arm is a genuinely clean measurement, and the vault has few of them.

The number the vault gains, and what it replaces. glp-1 and protein-target have carried the sentence "appetite suppression can crowd out the ~1.6 g/kg/day protein target" since 2026-07-09, backed by nothing but plausibility. This is the first measured magnitude for the drug's intake effect anywhere in the vault.

⚠ Two limits that bound it hard, and neither is optional. First, the pooled estimate rests on 4 arms from 3 trials and 209 people, out of 16 studies reviewed — a very thin base for a precise-looking number. The I² of 0% looks like consistency and is not: adding a single further trial sent it to 70%. The honest expression of the uncertainty is the 95% prediction interval, which the authors publish and which says a new trial would likely land somewhere between about 104 and 437 kcal. Second, this is one meal, in a laboratory, on one day — the review states that real-world habitual intake was too underreported to pool (glp1-intake-reduction-measured-only-at-a-laboratory-lunch). It is a mechanism readout, not a daily deficit.

⚠ safety_flags: [prescription, interaction], and the interaction is the new part. GLP-1 is a prescription/injectable, so per shared/safety.md it is never ranked into a daily protocol and no dose is set here. The interaction flag is raised because a measured reduction in food intake sits directly against the vault's protein target — a daily total that gets harder to reach on less food — while the drug is separately documented to cost 1.74–5.44 kg of lean mass (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude, tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss). The flag gates naive co-recommendation and enters no benefit score (glp1-intake-reduction-makes-the-protein-target-harder-to-hit).

Funding, worth stating because it is the exception. The review is unfunded, and the authors declare no relationship of any kind with Novo Nordisk or Eli Lilly — in a literature where SELECT was Novo-sponsored and SURMOUNT-5's quality-of-life analysis carried Lilly employees holding Lilly equity.
