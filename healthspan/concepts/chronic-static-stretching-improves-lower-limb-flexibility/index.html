Chronic static stretching improves lower-limb range of motion — 79 RCTs, g = 0.851

Pooling 79 randomized controlled trials (n=3,287, healthy individuals), chronic static stretching produced a moderate improvement in lower-limb flexibility: Hedges' g = 0.851 (95% CI 0.710–0.991), p < 0.001, against non-stretching or passive controls. Heterogeneity was substantial (τ² = 0.249, I² = 66.1%) and methodological quality moderate-to-good (PEDro 6.15 ± 0.90, range 4–8).

This is confirmation, not news, and the vault records it as such. stretching already held the range-of-motion claim on nine trials (stretching-improves-range-of-motion, ankle dorsiflexion +1.79 cm at I² = 98%). It now holds it on seventy-nine, with a tighter interval, a graded risk-of-bias assessment, and a conflict-free publicly-funded synthesis behind it. The map's single best-supported claim got substantially better supported and did not change.

By muscle group, the effect is not uniform even though the paper says it is. Knee flexors g = 0.995 (47 trials), ankle plantar flexors g = 0.656 (22 trials), knee extensors g = 0.550 (6 trials) — declared non-significant at p = 0.100. See stretching-quadriceps-evidence-thinnest-and-weakest-of-the-lower-limb for why the thinnest cell matters most here.

Read at its true strength. mixed, capped suggestive. The outcome is near-tautological and unblindable — stretching a muscle and then measuring how far it stretches, with effort-dependent range-of- motion instruments and participants who know what they did. Egger's test detected small-study effects at p < 0.001, and trim-and-fill imputing nothing is weak reassurance under I² = 66%. The pooled instruments (sit-and-reach, straight leg raise, passive knee extension, dorsiflexion angle, distance measures) are not one construct. No PROSPERO registration is stated and no GRADE assessment was performed.

What it does not establish. Not that stretching prevents injury (injuries were not an outcome). Not that being flexible improves health — flexibility-weak-predictor-of-health-outcomes-adults finds it a weak-to-null predictor across 45 prospective cohorts. Not that the effect generalises across ages (stretching-age-generalisation-rests-on-three-middle-aged-trials). And not how much to do (stretching-dose-remains-unspecified-across-79-trials).

For David: the proximate benefit that justifies the slot is now well-evidenced. GOAL.md gates his pickleball return on consistent stretching; this is the strongest confirmation the vault has that the gate buys what it is supposed to buy — range of motion in a stiff, previously-injured knee — while remaining free, knee-safe, and nothing to purchase. Status emerging holds: a large confirmation of a proximate surrogate does not consolidate an intervention with no hard-endpoint case.
