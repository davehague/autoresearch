BFR's first cardiovascular-safety data: hemodynamically tolerable in older adults — but still no adverse-event tally

Across 18 RCTs (n=626, older adults 55–80), a network meta-analysis of blood-flow-restriction regimens added to low-intensity training reports the first cardiovascular endpoints in any BFR source the vault holds. Heart rate did not change significantly across any regimen; systolic blood-pressure responses were modest (LFLP +3.40 mmHg, LFHP +1.51 mmHg); the authors conclude the high-frequency/high-pressure regimen (HFHP, ≥120 mmHg, 3×/week) "maintains cardiovascular health" while judging the low-frequency/low-pressure regimen's stronger physiological responses "may not be suitable for those with cardiovascular issues." On strength, BFR regimens beat the no-cuff low-intensity control (isometric HFHP WMD 1.44, 1RM LFLP 0.58) — replicating the vault's "occlusion adds strength over light load" thread a fourth time, now in healthy older adults.

This narrows the safety absence the high-dose-risk flag was set on — it does not close it. The flag has stood since 2026-07-12 because a technique that deliberately restricts arterial inflow had zero cardiovascular data across 33 RCTs (Xu, Hou, Chen; see bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials). Ren supplies hemodynamic data, and it is reassuring on direction. But blood-pressure response is not the risk the flag is about: there is still no systematic tally of serious adverse events, cuff complications, or thrombotic events, and the authors' own discussion raises "blood stasis, endothelial injury, and hypercoagulability" as concerns for high-risk populations precisely because the trials did not measure them. So the flag stands with refined reasoning: hemodynamically tolerable in older adults; occlusion-specific risks still uncounted; the authors themselves caution against CV-compromised use.

Read at its true strength. Small, sparse network (n=626 across five arms); the regimen ranking (HFHP best and safest) is the kind of underpowered network ranking the vault records as an authors' claim rather than adopting (exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported). The comparison is BFR added to low-load vs low-load control — it does not test occlusion as a substitute for heavy load, so it speaks to the strength and safety threads, not the substitute gap (bfr-matches-heavy-load-strength-in-knee-rehab).

For David: makes the "proposed — review with your doctor/physio" conversation better-informed — in older adults the hemodynamics of low-intensity BFR look tolerable and the higher-pressure regimen was judged CV-safe — without lifting the flag, changing Status (emerging), or making BFR a protocol entry. Cuff pressure is a physio's call; no dose is invented here (shared/safety.md).
