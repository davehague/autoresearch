The worse your sleep, the more walking helps it — which is why this evidence is mostly not about David

The strongest moderator in Kong 2026's model was baseline Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index score (β = −0.302): trials enrolling worse sleepers reported larger benefits. Supervision mode and exercise setting did not matter. Where you start determines what you get.

This single coefficient reorganises the whole finding, and it is why the headline g = −0.76 does not transfer to a healthy adult. Of the 21 trials, 8 enrolled patients with diagnosed sleep disorders and only 3 enrolled healthy adults. The pooled effect is therefore dominated by people for whom sleep is a problem to treat — and the moderator says explicitly that those are the people in whom the effect is biggest.

GOAL.md describes David's sleep as a foundation to protect, not a complaint to fix. Applying an effect size drawn overwhelmingly from insomnia and clinical populations to someone who already sleeps adequately is the population-match error the vault has now refused four separate times: melatonin (+12 min, in cognitively impaired elders), ashwagandha (young athletes), cbt-insomnia (insomnia patients), blood-flow-restriction (collegiate athletes). Population match, not evidence tier, keeps being the binding constraint — and it binds here too.

The honest expected value for a healthy sleeper who already walks: small, unmeasured, and free. Which is not nothing — it is simply not a reason to change anything. daily-steps is already a Foundation on a hard mortality endpoint (~7,000 vs ~2,000 steps/day, all-cause mortality HR 0.53). The sleep effect, if real, is a bonus on a walk already taken, not a second reason to take it, and it is not counted twice in the regimen.

A note on the one GRADE judgment in this paper the vault disagrees with. Kong's GRADE table rates indirectness as "not serious." With 18 of 21 trials in clinical or sleep-disordered populations, and a moderator showing the effect grows with baseline sleep pathology, indirectness for a healthy adult is severe. The vault records the disagreement rather than adopting the rating.

And it converges with the run's other sleep finding. exercise-plus-cbt-improves-sleep-in-insomnia — the highest-certainty sleep effect in this vault — is also measured in insomnia patients. The exercise-and-sleep literature is, almost in its entirety, a treatment literature. The vault holds essentially no evidence about what any of this does for someone whose sleep is already fine, and it will not manufacture that evidence by extrapolation.
