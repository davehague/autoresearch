Two hundred thousand people said stopping a statin was dangerous; twelve hundred randomised people did not reproduce it

The observational picture was consistent and large. Three cohort studies in the past decade — in Italy, Denmark and France, between 29,047 and 120,173 participants each, followed for 20 months to 5.5 years — all reported roughly 30% higher cardiovascular risk among people aged 75 or older who stopped statins taken for primary prevention. A 2024 systematic review of the observational literature agreed, reporting higher all-cause mortality, higher cardiovascular mortality and more cardiovascular events, at low certainty.

⚑ The flaw all three share is the same one, and it is the reason the trial was run. None of them recorded why the statin was stopped. In ordinary practice, a preventive drug is discontinued in someone who has become frail, has developed a serious illness, has entered palliative care, or has accumulated so many other prescriptions that something has to go. The subsequent deaths and events are then attributed to the withdrawal rather than to the reason for it. This is confounding by indication: the treatment decision is itself a marker of prognosis. The authors of the trial name it explicitly as the explanation for why the cohorts and the trial disagree.

What randomisation buys. Assigning the decision by computer breaks the link between "why this person stopped" and "how sick this person is." In the randomised comparison the ~30% excess did not appear: major cardiovascular events 5.0% vs 4.4% (+0.61 percentage points, −1.84 to +3.06), the death-or-event composite 12.0% vs 11.4% (+0.56, −3.29 to +4.41), and mortality numerically lower after stopping.

⚠ The honest bound, in both directions. The trial does not prove there is no excess. Its intervals are wide — 49% of planned enrolment — and every cardiovascular count ran numerically against stopping. What it establishes is narrower and still important: a ~30% relative excess of the size the cohorts reported is not what randomisation produced. A signal that large should have been visible in 1,160 people even at this precision, and it was not.

Why the vault keeps this. ⚑ shared/study-appraisal.md carries an "observational→RCT base rate" lens built on the field's poor replication record. This is that lens firing on a question with deaths in it, where the observational literature was not small, not old, and not obviously sloppy — it was three modern cohorts across three countries agreeing with each other. Agreement across cohorts is not independence when they all share the same unmeasured confounder.

Capped suggestive. stance: pushes-back — against the observational literature on statin discontinuation, not against anything in this vault.
