The global composite moved; not one specific cognitive domain did

In the same 14-RCT, 4,013-participant meta-analysis that found a small benefit on global cognitive function, no specific cognitive domain reached significance — executive function and visual-perceptual ability were both null.

Why a composite-only benefit should lower confidence rather than raise it. A global cognitive score is an aggregate of the domains beneath it. If a combined exercise-and-nutrition program improved cognition through an identifiable mechanism, the expectation is that some domain would carry the signal — executive function being the usual candidate, since it is the domain most consistently linked to aerobic fitness in the wider literature. Finding movement in the aggregate while every measured component stays flat is compatible with a real but diffuse effect, and equally compatible with the composite picking up something that is not cognition at all: practice effects, mood, motivation, or the expectancy of an unblinded participant who knows they were assigned to the program.

This is the source's own push-back on its own headline, and it is recorded here as a distinct claim rather than a caveat clause, because the vault grades nulls as findings. It also constrains what multidomain-lifestyle may claim: a small global effect with no domain-level counterpart is not a mechanism, it is an unexplained aggregate.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, capped). The source's reliability verdict is mixed, capping strength per shared/evidence-model.md. Note the null's own limit, in the direction of caution: the domain-level analyses draw on subsets of the 14 trials that measured each domain, so they are less powered than the global estimate. This is an absence of demonstrated domain effects, not a demonstrated absence — the same discipline the vault applied to Yu 2026's nutrition null in this run.
