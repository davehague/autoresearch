The trial carrying the stroke signal is not a B-vitamin trial — it is a folic-acid-plus-ACE-inhibitor trial in an unfortified population

CSPPT (20,702 Chinese hypertensive adults, no prior CVD, median 4.5–5 years) compared enalapril 10 mg plus folic acid 0.8 mg daily against enalapril 10 mg alone, and found fewer first strokes: 2.72% vs 3.42%, RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.68 to 0.93), Bayes factor 31.9, NNTB 143 (85 to 428) over 5.4 years, absolute risk reduction 0.7%, fragility index 23.

This is the single most persuasive result in the homocysteine literature, and four features stop it generalising.

(1) It is not a monotherapy comparison. Both arms received an ACE inhibitor; the contrast is folic acid added to antihypertensive therapy. The design cannot separate an independent folate effect from an effect that only exists alongside blood-pressure lowering. ⚑ That is an interaction in the pharmacological sense and it is flagged as one — per shared/safety.md, a credible signal is enough to flag, and interaction evidence is deliberately asymmetric. No vault map holds antihypertensive drugs, so the flag is carried here and noted on homocysteine-lowering's connect section rather than mirrored into a drug map that does not exist. exercise-for-blood-pressure and statins-primary-prevention are the adjacent primary-prevention maps and are cross-linked there.

(2) The population had no folate fortification. China did not mandate folic acid fortification of the food supply; the United States has since 1998. In a fortified population, the fraction of people whose folate status is low enough for supplementation to change anything is far smaller. Ten of the review's fifteen trials ran in non-fortified populations and this is by far the largest of them — which means the pooled stroke estimate (the-homocysteine-stroke-benefit-clears-the-p-value-and-not-the-sequential-boundary) is substantially a claim about unfortified food supplies. The review says the generalisation question out loud: "it is unknown whether such treatment would benefit a non-Chinese population."

(3) A fragility index of 23. Reclassifying 23 participants in the 10,348-person treatment arm from no-event to event would push the result past P=0.05. On a trial of that size, that is a thin margin.

(4) The absolute effect is 0.7 percentage points over five years. The review quotes Fuster on exactly this point: a highly significant statistical result "may not represent a clinically important effect when treating patients in our daily lives." Between 85 and 428 hypertensive adults would take folic acid daily for five years to prevent one stroke.

What this is good for. It is a genuine, high-quality, well-powered demonstration that in a specific population — hypertensive, unfortified, on an ACE inhibitor — folic acid does something. It is not evidence for anyone else, and the review does not present it as such.
