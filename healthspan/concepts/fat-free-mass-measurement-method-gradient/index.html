The crudest instrument shows the biggest mortality effect — the opposite of what a clean tissue effect predicts

Within the same 49-study meta-analysis, the low-vs-high fat-free-mass mortality association varies sharply by how fat-free mass was measured: calf circumference RR 2.19 (1.50–3.20), DXA RR 1.52 (1.29–1.79), BIA RR 1.23 (1.07–1.41). The crudest instrument shows the largest effect; the instrument closest to an actual body-composition assay shows the smallest.

Why this pushes back on the headline number. If low fat-free mass itself were the causal driver of death, the instrument that most specifically isolates fat-free tissue (BIA) should show the association at least as strongly as a crude proxy — not less than half as strongly. Calf circumference is a classic frailty/sarcopenia screening measure entangled with overall health status, subcutaneous fat, and edema, not just muscle. A gradient where the crudest, most confounding-prone instrument shows the biggest number is more consistent with confounding and reverse causation inflating the association than with fat-free mass being the causal tissue. This is not a technicality; it is the paper's own internal evidence working against its own headline RR of 1.42.

What survives. Even the best instrument (BIA) shows a statistically significant association (RR 1.23, CI excludes 1) — the direction is not erased, only shrunk substantially. Read fat-free-mass-mortality-association-observational's RR 1.42 (or the trim-and-fill 1.31) as an upper bound inflated by cruder, more confounded instruments, with something closer to BIA's 1.23 as the more defensible estimate of what a body-composition-specific measure actually captures.

Bounded honestly (why single-study). This is a post-hoc subgroup breakdown of one meta-analysis, not an independently replicated finding, and the source's own mixed reliability already caps strength at suggestive — this specific comparison is weaker still, hence single-study. It is recorded because ignoring an instrument-dependent 2x spread in the exact same outcome would be dishonest, not because it stands alone as strong evidence of anything beyond "measurement matters here."
