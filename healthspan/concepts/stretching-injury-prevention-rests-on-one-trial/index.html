The injury-prevention headline is one trial the abstract dressed as a general finding

Weerasinghe 2026's abstract concludes stretching "appears effective in reducing injuries." The evidence underneath is a single RCT: individualised static stretching cut lower-extremity/trunk injuries ~30% (60 vs 101 injuries, p < 0.05). No other trial in the review assessed injury outcomes, and the authors themselves concede that high-quality real-world injury evidence is "limited." Reading "one positive injury trial + eight trials that measured surrogates at I² = 98%" as "effective in reducing injuries" is the abstract outrunning the data — the same over-reach lens the vault has fired repeatedly this month.

The wider literature the paper sits inside is genuinely split, and the vault adopts that honesty rather than the headline. The consensus is that stretching is not an all-purpose injury-prevention strategy; there is a narrower signal that static stretching may reduce muscle injury specifically. So the honest claim is: stretching's injury-prevention reputation exceeds its evidence, though a muscle-injury-specific benefit is plausible. Graded single-study/open-question — one trial cannot establish a general effect, and the vault does not launder it into one.

Why this note exists on a freshly-coined map. The stretching map is coined because GOAL.md names stretching as the gate on David's pickleball return — but coining it must not smuggle in the overstated injury claim. The map's headline is deliberately: range of motion, yes (stretching-improves-range-of-motion); injury prevention, not established here. That keeps the vault from repeating, on its own new map, the exact abstract-over-reach it caught in Jiao, Shen, Wu, and Kong.

For David: stretching is worth doing for range of motion during rehab and because the goal file gates pickleball on it — not because it will demonstrably prevent injury, a claim thinner than its reputation. What would change the answer: adequately-powered injury-endpoint RCTs of stretching in middle-aged / return-to-sport populations, which do not yet exist in the vault.
