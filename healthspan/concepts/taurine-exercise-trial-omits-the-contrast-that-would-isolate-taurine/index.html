The trial reported as showing taurine potentiates exercise never reports taurine against exercise

The abstract concludes that combined training with taurine produced "statistically superior, large-magnitude improvements… relative to either modality applied independently." Superiority over taurine alone is genuinely demonstrated. Superiority over exercise alone — the only comparison that isolates what the supplement adds — is not reported for any of the five outcomes.

The omission is informative rather than accidental. Bonferroni post hoc was applied to all six pairwise between-group contrasts at post-test wherever the Group × Time interaction was significant, and the paper prints the significant ones: asprosin (CON–EX, CON–EX+SUP, EX+SUP–SUP), spexin (CON–EX, CON–EX+SUP, SUP–EX+SUP), body mass (EX and EX+SUP each vs CON and SUP), BMI (CON–EX+SUP, SUP–EX, SUP–EX+SUP), body fat (EX+SUP vs CON and vs SUP, plus CON–EX). EX+SUP versus EX is absent from all five lists, which means it was tested and did not reach significance.

What the reader is offered in its place is a comparison of within-group change scores — −11.20 ng/mL against −7.50, described as "2.4-fold greater than EX alone" — which is not a between-group test and cannot support a between-group claim. The pre-specified 2 × 2 × 2 interaction, described in the methods as "the formal test of factorial synergy," is consigned to Supplementary Table S1, after which the discussion asks for "formal 2 × 2 factorial confirmation in larger trials" as though it had not been run.

The stance here is confirms, not pushes-back: what this rebuts is the paper's own abstract, not any claim this vault held — taurine has said since 2026-07-07 that the human case is unmade, and this tightens that rather than contradicting it.

This is the vault's recurring attribution failure arriving from a new direction: the confound beside the supplement is not 24 g of protein or a missing control arm but twelve weeks of supervised training three times a week — the single lever in this vault with a mortality association attached to it.
