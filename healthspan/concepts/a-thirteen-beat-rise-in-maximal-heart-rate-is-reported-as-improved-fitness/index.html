Maximal heart rate went up 13 beats, which is not what getting fitter looks like — but going further on the treadmill is

Maximal heart rate is among the least modifiable variables in exercise physiology. It is set primarily by age. Eight weeks of endurance training in a healthy adult characteristically lowers resting and submaximal heart rate and leaves HRmax unchanged or slightly reduced. A 13-beat increase in HRmax over eight weeks in healthy 34-year-olds is not a recognised training adaptation, and the paper's description of it as "improved cardiovascular response and exercise tolerance" is not a physiological explanation.

⚑ There is a much more parsimonious reading, and the trial's own data support it. HRmax on a graded treadmill test is not a physiological ceiling — it is the highest heart rate the participant reached before they stopped. And the trial reports that participants went further:

People who run longer reach a higher heart rate. So the chain is most likely: the supplement (or something about the treatment arm) let people continue further into the protocol → they hit a higher peak heart rate → the VO2max formula, which divides HRmax by a near-constant HRrest, converted that into a "10% fitness gain" (the-ashwagandha-vo2max-gain-is-arithmetically-the-rise-in-maximal-heart-rate).

⚑ If that is what happened, the trial may have detected a real endurance-performance effect — and chose an endpoint that obscures it. A doubling of Stage 8 attainment against a placebo arm that did not move is a large, plainly-stated, between-arm difference in a thing that actually happened. It is a better argument for the supplement than the primary endpoint is.

⚠ And the vault cannot grade it either. Stage attainment is reported as two percentages with no between-group statistical test, no confidence interval, and no time-to-exhaustion — the direct, continuous measure of the same thing, which a treadmill test produces automatically and which this trial did not report. Percentages in two arms of 42 and 21 people, with no test, are not a finding.

What would settle it: time or total work to volitional exhaustion, reported with a between-group comparison and an interval. ⚑ The trial almost certainly holds that data. Until it is reported, the honest position is that something plausibly happened to endurance performance and the trial's own reporting choices prevent anyone from saying what.

A note on the alternative that cannot be excluded. Perceived exertion also fell in the treatment arm (the-ashwagandha-fatigue-claim-is-a-within-group-result-whose-between-group-test-was-null), by a clinically negligible 0.35 Borg points. If a supplement makes hard effort feel marginally easier, participants push further, reach a higher peak heart rate, and every downstream number moves — without any change in the cardiovascular system at all. That is a real and potentially useful effect. It is not a fitness improvement, and it is exactly what an adaptogen marketed for fatigue would be expected to do.
