People who exercise a lot have consistent contexts, and this design cannot say which came first

Zhu 2026's behavioural association is r = 0.11 — about 1.2% of the variance in whether people are physically active. The paper closes by "supporting a shift toward cue-specific PA promotion strategies," which is a recommendation to intervene. Nothing in the study intervened on anything.

The reverse-causation account is at least as parsimonious as the causal one, and it is entirely unaddressed in the retrievable record. Someone who runs four mornings a week will report high temporal consistency, high routine consistency and high location consistency because they run four mornings a week. The cues are a description of an established behaviour as much as a candidate cause of it. shared/study-appraisal.md names this as the "cart before horse" red flag, and it is the observational lens this literature most needs and least demonstrates.

A third possibility is neither causal direction: shared confounding by life structure. People with predictable schedules — stable work hours, no shift rotation, childcare that does not move, a body that is not in pain — will show more consistent contexts and more exercise, with no arrow between them at all. A correlation of 0.11 is well inside the range that unmeasured life stability could produce on its own. shared/study-appraisal.md is explicit that at this effect size "anything unaccounted-for can distort reality."

⚠ The moderator finding has the same ambiguity built in. Cue consistency predicted exercise frequency better than exercise duration. A habit account predicts exactly that — a cue triggers an episode and does not govern its length — and so does a measurement account, in which a consistency construct simply shares method variance with frequency counts. Both readings fit; this design separates neither.

⚠ And most of the observational lens set could not be walked at all. The full text is paywalled (Wiley, confirmed non-OA at the browser rung), so the study-design mix, confidence intervals, heterogeneity statistics, publication-bias assessment and quality appraisal of the included studies are all unread. The appraisal below the abstract is not "passed"; it is "unavailable."

Why exercise-adherence is nevertheless coined on this. Not because the evidence is good — it is thin, correlational and partly unreadable — but because the vault has been making adherence judgements without any evidence at all. GOAL.md ranks interventions by least work, least discomfort, low daily maintenance, and dozens of map entries call something "low burden" or "a bad trade" as unsourced editorial assertion. This is the first source attached to any of it, and its own weakness is recorded inside the map so the map cannot be mistaken for a strong one.

Nothing here changes a single recommendation. No dose, no protocol, no Status moved anywhere. The honest summary is that the vault now has a map for a question it has been answering by instinct.
