You get faster at a mechanical task you have done before; you do not get better at remembering the word for "harmonica"

The split is clean and it is mechanistically sensible. Across nine tests, the practice effect concentrated in timed processing-speed and executive tasks and was absent from language:

  • Digit Symbol Substitution (match symbols to digits against the clock): +0.16 SD (0.10 to 0.21) — in raw units, 1.60 more symbols.
  • Trail Making Part A (connect numbered dots as fast as possible): −0.12 SD (−0.22 to −0.02) — 1.95 seconds faster; negative is better here.
  • Delayed Word Recall: +0.14 SD (0.04 to 0.24) — 0.23 more words.
  • Language as a domain — Boston Naming, Word Fluency, Animal Naming — −0.03 (−0.08 to 0.02), no effect at all.

⚑ Each of the three significant individual-test effects is approximately one year of that test's own estimated annual decline.

Why the pattern is the useful part. Digit Symbol Substitution and Trail Making are procedural: the score depends on how efficiently you execute a rule you have been taught. Do it once and you know the layout, the pen, the strategy. Naming a picture of a harmonica is retrieval from a lexicon you either have or do not — there is nothing to get better at between administrations. ⚑ So the practice effect is not a general "test-taking" halo; it is skill acquisition on the specific tasks that reward skill.

⚑ The direct consequence for how this vault reads sources. The tests that carry the artefact — Digit Symbol Substitution and Trail Making — are precisely the paradigms that consumer brain-training products and cognitive-tracking apps re-administer, and precisely the ones that dominate "processing speed" endpoints in supplement and exercise trials. A rising line on a repeated timed task is the expected result of repetition. No product is named or assessed here; the mechanism is general.

⚠ And it generalises past cognition, which is where the vault should be uneasy. The same logic applies to every learned physical test in this corpus: the six-minute walk, the timed up-and-go, the five-times sit-to-stand, grip dynamometry, and one-repetition-maximum testing — all of which improve with familiarity and technique independent of the underlying capacity. ⚑ This vault grades many interventions on exactly those instruments. Nothing in this paper measures that; the extension is the vault's inference and is flagged as such.

⚠ Bounds. Alternate test forms were not used, so this is practice on identical content — the authors cite prior work that alternate forms do not eliminate retest effects but this study cannot demonstrate it. Individual-test analyses were exploratory and uncorrected. And the population is adults 70–84 with untreated hearing loss.
