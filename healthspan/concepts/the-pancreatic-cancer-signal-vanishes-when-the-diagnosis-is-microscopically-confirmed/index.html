The diet-and-pancreatic-cancer association is a property of how the cancer was counted

Pooling ten prospective cohorts and 1.1 million adults, highest-versus-lowest Mediterranean adherence gave HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.82–0.98), p=0.02 for pancreatic cancer. Split by how the diagnosis was ascertained, the finding separates cleanly into a signal and a null:

Outcome ascertainmentComparisonsHR (95% CI)I²
Registry linkage80.87 (0.81–0.93)46.8%
Microscopic confirmation21.00 (0.88–1.13)0%

Between-subgroup heterogeneity p=0.05. The microscopically-confirmed estimate is not a weak effect — it is exactly 1.00, with zero heterogeneity between the two cohorts (EPIC and NLCS, two of the three largest European contributors).

⚑ This subgroup has a mechanism behind it, which is what distinguishes it from the paper's other subgroups. Registry linkage counts pancreatic cancers coded from clinical impression, imaging or death records; microscopic confirmation counts the ones a pathologist verified in tissue. They are different outcome definitions with different error structures. The paper also reports subgroups by geography, sex and exposure index — none of which has a comparable mechanism, and none of which changes what the study means.

⚑ The most parsimonious reading is ascertainment, not biology. High Mediterranean adherence in these cohorts is a marker of education, income, non-smoking and health engagement. Those people also get more diagnostic scrutiny and cleaner coding. If the association were causal you would expect it to be strongest where the outcome is measured most accurately. It is absent there. A signal that survives only under the looser definition of the outcome is the signature of differential ascertainment, not of a protective diet.

⚠ What this does not do. It does not establish that the Mediterranean diet has no effect on pancreatic cancer — two cohorts cannot establish that either, and the confirmed-subgroup interval (0.88–1.13) is wide enough to contain a modest real benefit. It removes the basis for claiming one. The source's own conclusion calls the pooled result "compelling evidence"; this note is the reason the vault does not.

⚑ Status implication: none, and the reason matters. mediterranean-diet rests on a 54-cohort, 1.8-million-adult all-cause mortality synthesis with GRADE moderate certainty and PREDIMED randomised support (mediterranean-diet-lowers-all-cause-mortality). Highest-tier-wins: a mixed cancer-site observational result cannot move that. What it does is bound the map — there is no reliable pancreatic-cancer-specific case here, and the map should not imply that "cancer" is among the outcomes it covers.
