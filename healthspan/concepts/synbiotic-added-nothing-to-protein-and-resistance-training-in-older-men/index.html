The third independent look at protein-plus-a-live-culture, and the third time the culture contributed nothing

This vault has now examined the "add a probiotic to your protein" proposition three times, through three different designs, and the answer has not varied.

  • probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance - a network meta-analysis in which the probiotic arm lost 9 of 10 contrasts.
  • protein-probiotic-headline-confounded-by-comparator-pooling - the apparent benefit in that literature traced to a comparator artefact.
  • This one - the first prospectively registered, randomised, triple-blinded trial in the set, and the first to isolate the add-on properly.

⚑ The isolation is what makes it worth having. All three arms did the same twelve weeks of supervised resistance training - 2 sessions/week, 3 x 8-12 reps at 70% of 1RM to RPE 7-8 - and all three were counselled by nutritionists to the same 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day protein target. So the Control arm is not "nothing"; it is training plus an adequate diet. The Protein arm's contrast is 20 g of supplemental whey on top of that. The Synbiotic arm's contrast is the synbiotic on top of the whey. Four named strains - L. acidophilus NCFM, L. paracasei Lpc-37, B. lactis Bl-07 and Bl-04 - at 10 x 10^10 CFU daily for twelve weeks.

The synbiotic arm gained nothing the protein arm did not. It did not add strength - the isokinetic knee gains belong to the Protein arm alone. It did not add function - every arm improved on the SPPB, including the one drinking flavoured water. It did not move glycaemia; no time, group or interaction effect appeared for AGEs, fructosamine, glucose, insulin, HbA1c or C-peptide in any arm. The redox changes (uric acid up ~2.7-fold, serum iron down ~65%, LDH and CK up) appeared in all three groups and are the signature of unaccustomed resistance training, not of a supplement.

The authors' own conclusions, verbatim: "the addition of synbiotic supplementation did not provide further benefits beyond those achieved with protein supplementation and resistance training alone", and "the routine use of synbiotic supplementation for this population cannot be recommended based on the present findings."

⚠ This is a failure to demonstrate benefit, not a demonstration of no benefit, and the distinction is load-bearing here. Fourteen people were in the synbiotic arm. Only a large effect could have been detected. The authors name three live explanations - "the specific bacterial strains used ... the administered dose ... or the 12-week intervention period" - and call for larger and longer trials.

⚠ And the mechanistic premise went unverified: no microbiome analysis was performed. The rationale was that the synbiotic would correct gut dysbiosis and thereby improve protein utilisation. Nobody checked whether the strains arrived or changed anything. A null with no engagement check cannot distinguish does not work from never got there.

⚑ What has accumulated across the three sources is a consistent absence of demonstrated benefit through three different failure modes. That is a reason not to buy the product. It is not proof that the product is inert, and this vault should keep saying both sentences.
