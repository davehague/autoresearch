Sixteen of seventeen trials asked whether this beats doing nothing; one asked the question that matters

Read the comparator column of Table 1 and every result in the paper changes meaning.

Resistance training⚑ 1 (n = 32)

Sixteen of seventeen comparators involve no structured exercise. So the seven pooled estimates — handgrip +1.13 kg, ASMI +0.20 kg/m², gait speed +0.12 m/s, Timed Up-and-Go −2.80 s, SPPB +1.37 points and the two sit-to-stand results — all answer: "is doing this better than not doing it?"

⚑ That question's answer was never in doubt. Supervised exercise three to five times a week for 4 to 72 weeks, in older adults with diagnosed sarcopenia, against a group receiving a leaflet. The interesting question — whether Traditional Chinese Exercise is a reasonable substitute for the resistance training that is the standard recommendation — is answered here by thirty-two people in one trial, pooled invisibly into the same estimates.

The paper's own introduction makes the case for asking it. It argues that resistance training is the most recommended modality but that older adults struggle to adhere to it — "reduced physical capacity, joint discomfort, or poor exercise compliance," equipment, facilities and supervision — and that Traditional Chinese Exercises are attractive because they are moderate-intensity, low-cost and low-risk. ⚑ That is an argument for a head-to-head trial, and the synthesis it introduces contains one.

The vault holds the comparison that this literature is missing. Two separate networks that included these practices alongside conventional modalities put resistance training first: moderate-intensity-resistance-training-ranks-first-for-handgrip-in-sarcopenia and resistance-training-ranks-first-grip-and-fat-mass-sarcopenic-obesity. Neither this synthesis nor tai-chi contradicts that, and neither was designed to.

⚑ This is the eighth time this vault has recorded the same defect, and the count is the argument for adding a comparator-strength lens to shared/study-appraisal.md. Prior instances: traditional-chinese-sports-ranking-is-unseparable-from-its-comparator (this same literature, from the umbrella review that coined the map), exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator, nutritional-supplementation-alone-beats-nothing-in-sarcopenic-obesity, the-relaxation-sleep-literature-fails-its-own-publication-bias-test (12 of 14 comparators routine care or nothing), weighted-vest-plus-exercise-beats-usual-activity-on-bone-and-fat.

⚑ And the same run supplies the positive counterpart, which is what a well-comparated trial buys you. the-weight-loss-control-arm-outperformed-its-own-design-assumption-threefold describes a 24-month randomised trial whose control arm was two years of free WeightWatchers and lost 3.5% of body weight — turning an uninformative "does it work" into an actionable "is it better than the alternative." The lens, as it should be worded, is two-sided: read the control arm's absolute performance before reading the between-arm difference.

⚠ What this note does not claim. It does not claim the effects are zero. Supervised exercise against nothing is a real intervention with real benefits, and two of the seven outcomes clear a clinical threshold (traditional-chinese-exercise-sarcopenia-effects-are-mostly-below-clinical-thresholds). It claims that an unknown share of every estimate is the benefit of attending a structured programme rather than anything specific to the practice, and that this literature cannot separate the two.

Capped suggestive on a mixed appraisal.
