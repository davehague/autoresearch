Adding ~10-15 g/day of dairy protein to home-based resistance training added no detectable benefit over training alone

A pragmatic 12-week RCT randomized 54 community-dwelling Japanese adults ≥65 to home-based resistance training alone, or the same training plus a dairy-product intervention targeting +10–15 g/day of dairy-derived protein. With exceptionally well-verified adherence on both arms (median 7×/week dairy intake, ~3.1–3.25×/week training, not significantly different between arms, p=0.940), no significant between-group difference emerged in upper/lower-limb strength, handgrip, gait speed, or body composition. Some functional outcomes improved over time in both groups, consistent with the training itself driving the change.

Why this matters for the vault. This is the latest in protein-target's long run of isolating-contrast nulls — joining Khalafi 2025 (whey added nothing to body composition), Bastos 2026 (a synbiotic added nothing to protein+training), and Svensson 2026 (intra-workout protein added nothing when the daily total was already met). The pattern this map has stated repeatedly holds again: the training is the lever; small supplemental top-ups on top of an already-adequate diet are near-noise. This instance is unusually trustworthy because adherence to BOTH the nutrition and exercise arms was measured and reported, not assumed — rare in this literature.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive, not higher). Small completer sample (n=51, ~25/arm) with no power calculation reported for the primary contrast — the null is honest but its precision is unclear. Part of the dairy product was supplied in-kind by Morinaga Milk Industry Co. (disclosed); this is a sponsor-adverse result (a milk-industry-supported trial found no added benefit of milk protein), which per this vault's standing rule is the one funding configuration that argues for trusting the null rather than discounting it. An exploratory, post-hoc subgroup (participants with low baseline dairy intake) showed limb-strength gains in the dairy+exercise arm — hypothesis-generating only, on an already small sample, not graded here.

The practical shape of the claim. For someone already training and eating adequate protein, adding a modest dairy top-up buys nothing measurable. Consistent with GOAL.md's adherence-first, least-work philosophy: one less thing to optimize.
