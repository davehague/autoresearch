Metformin made the identical workout feel meaningfully harder — the trial's largest effect, and the one that matters most to whether exercise keeps happening

Across five weeks of matched supervised cycling, participants taking metformin 1000 mg/day rated the same relative workload up to two full points harder than they had at baseline. Participants taking an identical starch placebo rated it exactly the same as before, at every intensity tested.

The Borg scale, in English. Rating of perceived exertion is a 6-to-20 scale on which a person reports how hard exercise feels: roughly 9 is "very light," 13 "somewhat hard," 15 "hard," 17 "very hard." Two units is the distance from "somewhat hard" to nearly "hard" — not a rounding error, and not a measurement most people would fail to notice week after week.

The numbers, and what makes them the sturdiest result in the paper. At 60% of maximal aerobic power the metformin arm rose +2.0 units (95% CI 1.6 to 2.4); at 50%, +1.5 (0.9 to 2.0); at 40%, +1.1 (0.5 to 1.6). The placebo arm did not change at any of the six intensities measured. The two arms differed from each other directly at the end of the trial at 40%, 50% and 60% of maximal power (p = 0.004, 0.0006, < 0.0001) — which is more than can be said for this trial's better-known fitness finding, where the arms never separated (metformin-exercise-attenuation-rests-on-an-interaction-not-a-group-difference). The effect size for the perceived-effort interaction (η²p = 0.288) is larger than for the fitness interaction (0.191).

Why a subjective outcome is unusually trustworthy here. Perceived effort is self-reported, which normally makes it the softest endpoint in a trial and the most vulnerable to knowing which arm you are in. In this trial nobody knew — participants, investigators, exercise supervisors, outcome assessors and the data analysts were all blinded to drug allocation, the sequence was generated and held by an independent pharmacist, and the placebo was identical in appearance and schedule. A subjective outcome under full blinding is protected in exactly the way an unblindable exercise outcome is not — the standing weakness of most of this vault's exercise literature (resistance-training-depression-effect-heterogeneous-unblindable, green-exercise-evidence-rests-entirely-on-unblindable-self-report).

⚑ Why this is the finding this atlas should carry forward, ahead of the VO₂peak result it is usually bundled with. GOAL.md's stated philosophy is "least amount of work and least discomfort/side-effects for the maximum benefit," with adherence-first as an explicit constraint — "an intervention he won't stick to scores as if it doesn't work." On those terms, a drug that reliably adds two Borg units to the same session is levying a direct adherence tax on the intervention the rest of this vault treats as foundational, and it is doing so on firmer statistical ground than the fitness attenuation.

And the honest bound on that argument: nobody in this trial stopped exercising. Session adherence was 94.7% in the metformin arm versus 95.1% in placebo — indistinguishable — across five supervised weeks with weekly check-in visits. So the effort penalty is measured; the dropout it might cause is not. The hypothesis is that what supervision absorbs over five weeks, voluntary training over years does not. This trial cannot test that, and the vault does not assert it.

Mechanism, stated as the plausible reading rather than a finding. Metformin shifts substrate use toward fat oxidation and raises energy expenditure at matched relative intensity, both measured in this trial (metformin-shifts-training-adaptation-toward-fat-oxidation) — a metabolically costlier way to do the same work is a coherent candidate explanation for it feeling harder. Recorded as coherence, not causation.

Bounded population. Inactive, class II obese adults with metabolic syndrome and dysglycaemia over five weeks. Nothing here describes a healthy trained adult on off-label metformin, which is the use case metformin exists to grade and the one TAME is meant to settle.
