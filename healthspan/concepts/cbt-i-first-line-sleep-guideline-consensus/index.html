CBT-I's isolated sleep efficacy is guideline consensus — the vault's first source that says so

The map's founding source (Fiori) measured depression. The next (Zhou) had no CBT-alone node — every CBT arm was bundled with exercise or qigong. So until now the vault held no source affirming CBT-I's isolated sleep efficacy, the very reason clinical guidelines rank it first-line for chronic insomnia. Yan 2026, a JBI evidence summary of 28 documents (5 guidelines, 3 consensus statements, 12 systematic reviews, 8 meta-analyses), is that source: CBT-I as its own multicomponent therapy — sleep restriction, stimulus control, cognitive therapy, sleep hygiene, ± relaxation, not bundled with exercise — is the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia, with ~7–8 of 10 patients improving, durable benefit, and minimal side effects, superior to controls on the sleep outcomes it targets (sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, total sleep time, sleep efficiency).

This closes the attribution half of the map's gap. The vault can now say, with guideline backing and a conflict-free synthesis of 20 years of reviews, that CBT-I improves sleep on its own — cleanly separated from the exercise-bundled arms that made Zhou uninterpretable (cbt-sleep-effect-not-isolable-no-cbt-alone-node). That is a real advance: three days ago the isolated claim was unsourced; now it is sourced as consensus.

It does not close the magnitude half. This is a narrative evidence summary — it reports no pooled effect size, only interpretive benchmarks (an ISI change of ~8.4 as a marker of moderate improvement, a sleep efficiency <80% cutoff). There is still no gradeable pooled CBT-alone sleep number in the vault (cbt-i-sleep-effect-size-not-pooled-here), which is why this note is capped suggestive and the map's Status does not move on it.

Population is chronic-insomnia patients — the same population-match hold that keeps the map on the Watch list for a healthy sleeper. David is not described as having insomnia; the vault holds no evidence CBT-I helps someone who already sleeps adequately.

For David: strengthens the trigger already written into the regimen — if sleep becomes a problem to treat, CBT-I is the first thing to reach for — with the first vault source affirming its isolated sleep efficacy as guideline consensus. Still Watch, not Protocol; still a plan, not a task. No dose, nothing to buy (shared/safety.md).
