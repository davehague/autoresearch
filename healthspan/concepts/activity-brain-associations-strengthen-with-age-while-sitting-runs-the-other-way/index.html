Sitting and moving pointed opposite ways on the same brain measures, and the gap widened with age

In one model, in one set of 4,188 people, physical activity and sedentary time ran in opposite directions on the same white-matter measures — more sitting was associated with higher fibre dispersion where more activity was associated with lower. The age interactions pointed the same way: with advancing age, more light activity tracked with higher white-matter volume (β = +0.015, p = 0.029) while more sedentary time tracked with lower white-matter volume (β = −0.015, p = 0.025). More moderate-to-vigorous activity tracked with higher fractional anisotropy especially in older people (β = +0.036, p = 0.012).

Two age interactions survived the study's full multiple-comparison correction: mean diffusivity of the external capsule (β = −0.044, p = 0.0005) and neurite density of the splenium of the corpus callosum (β = +0.053, p = 0.0005) — in both cases, the association with activity strengthened with age. The authors conclude that "sedentary and older individuals may benefit most from additional PA."

Why this matters for the vault. sedentary-behavior rests on sedentary-time-independent-of-exercise-mortality-risk — an umbrella review of 30 systematic reviews finding that sitting predicts mortality even after adjusting for physical activity, but which publishes no pooled effect size, so its magnitude is unknown. This is the first time the vault has seen the two behaviours separate on a machine-measured biological outcome rather than in an epidemiological adjustment. Same people, same model, same scanner, opposite signs. It is not a mortality replication and does not supply the missing magnitude, but it is independent support for treating sitting as its own variable rather than as the absence of exercise.

The age pattern also cuts against the most obvious rival explanation for the whole study. If these associations were purely reverse causation — impaired brains producing less movement — you would not particularly expect them to strengthen with age in the direction observed; you would expect the relationship to be dominated by whoever is already ill. That is an argument, not a control.

Bounded honestly (why suggestive). Cross-sectional, so directionality is undetermined and the authors say so. The interaction effects are small (β ≈ 0.015 for the volumetric ones) and most were nominally significant only — the two that survived correction are tract-level microstructural results, and both lost corrected significance in the sensitivity analysis that removed outliers and participants with neurological disease. Sedentary time and activity time are near-mechanically complementary within a fixed waking day, so "opposite directions" is partly arithmetic, not only biology.
