Nutrition did not raise strength-per-unit-of-muscle — alone, or added to exercise

In a review of 41 RCTs and 3,141 adults aged 60+, four intervention families returned nulls on muscle-specific strength: nutritional supplementation alone, aerobic exercise, concurrent training, and combined exercise-plus-nutrition. Only resistance exercise moved it (resistance-exercise-is-the-only-intervention-that-raised-strength-per-unit-of-muscle, SMD 0.61).

⚠ Read this as a failure to demonstrate, not a demonstration of failure — and the reason is specific. No confidence interval, trial count or participant count is published for any of the five null categories. "Did not lead to significant improvements" in a category built from small, short, heterogeneous trials is roughly as likely to mean underpowered as ineffective, and nothing in the retrievable text distinguishes them. shared/study-appraisal.md fatal flag #3 exists for nulls sold as "no effect" without power; it does not fire here, because the authors explicitly write "remain inconclusive" rather than "do not work." This note holds to their wording.

⚑ The category incoherence is the most interesting unresolved thing in the paper. Combined exercise and nutrition contains resistance exercise in most of its trials. A category containing the one thing that works, returning a null, has three live explanations and the abstract distinguishes none of them:

  1. The combined pool is dominated by aerobic or concurrent exercise plus nutrition — in which case the null is about the exercise, not the food.
  2. The category splits are thin enough that the estimates are noise.
  3. ⚠ Adding nutrition changes the ratio's denominator. Muscle-specific strength is strength divided by mass. creatine-resistance-training-lean-mass-strength records creatine adding roughly 1.37 kg of lean mass. If a supplement adds tissue without adding proportional strength, it lowers a muscle-quality ratio while making the person genuinely stronger. That is a real measurement artefact, not a rhetorical one, and it would make this null an argument about the instrument rather than about the nutrition.

⚑ It nonetheless converges with what the vault already holds about protein specifically. morning-protein-null-on-muscle-mass-and-strength-in-older-adults and protein-timing-distribution-null-on-lean-mass both find protein timing moves neither mass nor strength; nutritional-supplementation-alone-beats-nothing-in-sarcopenic-obesity finds nutrition alone clearing only a do-nothing comparator. The standing reading on protein-target is unchanged: hit the total, stop optimising the schedule, and expect the training to do the work.
