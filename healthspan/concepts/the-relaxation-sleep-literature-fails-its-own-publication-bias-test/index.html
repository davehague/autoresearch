A formal test says three studies are missing, and putting them back removes a quarter of the effect

What was run and what it found. The authors examined a funnel plot — effect size against precision, which should be symmetric if the literature is complete — and it was asymmetric. Egger's regression test returned p = 0.004. They then applied trim-and-fill, which estimates how many studies would need to exist on the sparse side of the funnel to restore symmetry, imputes them, and re-pools: three studies imputed, and the estimate fell from Hedges' g = −1.24 to −0.92 (−1.49 to −0.36).

⚑ The authors and the vault read the same result in opposite directions, and both readings are defensible. Theirs: "the adjusted effect size remained statistically significant, and the effect direction remained unchanged, indicating that the overall results of this study were relatively stable." True as far as it goes. ⚑ The vault's: a base of 14 small trials whose largest has 91 people just failed a formal test of its own completeness at p = 0.004, and the correction deleted a quarter of the reported effect. That is not stability; that is an estimate whose size depends on which studies got published.

⚠ Why this is the fatal flag rather than a caveat. shared/study-appraisal.md's flag #2 is "p-hacking — many comparisons, no correction, only significant results reported." ⚑ The synthesis itself did not p-hack — it pre-registered, reported its subgroups, and published its own failing bias test. The flag fires on the literature it pooled, which is what Egger's test measures: only significant results reported. A meta-analysis inherits the selection in its inputs, and there is no analytic fix — trim-and-fill is a sensitivity check, not a repair.

⚑ Why this base is unusually vulnerable to it. Progressive muscle relaxation is unpatentable, free and has no sponsor, so there is no industry pressure — but there is also no registry discipline and no incentive to publish a null. Fourteen trials from four countries, in nursing and rehabilitation journals, with arms as small as 13 people. A small nursing trial of a free relaxation technique that found nothing is exactly the study that never leaves a desk drawer. The eligibility criteria compound it: conference abstracts, commentaries and unpublished studies were explicitly excluded.

⚠ What survives. The direction. Something happened in these trials. What cannot survive is the magnitude — and magnitude is what a decision layer scores. The honest bound is: somewhere between "a large effect" and "a moderate effect measured against nothing," with a formal test saying the published set is not the whole set.
