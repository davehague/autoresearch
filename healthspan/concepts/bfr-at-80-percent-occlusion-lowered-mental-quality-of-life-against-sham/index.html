The first BFR trial to measure a wellbeing outcome against a sham found the occluded arm getting worse

Over nine weeks, mental health-related quality of life on the SF-12v2 MCS — a validated scale normed to a US population mean of 50 — fell from 54.1 to 49.1 in the arm receiving blood flow restriction at 80% limb occlusion pressure, crossing below the population average, while the sham arm (20 mmHg) held essentially flat at 53.2 → 53.5. The group × time interaction was F(1,202) = 6.17, P = .014. Neither the main effect of time (P = .66) nor of group (P = .49) was significant: the whole finding is the divergence.

⚑ Why this matters more than its p-value. blood-flow-restriction has recorded, across seven prior sources, that BFR trials report an absence of adverse events without defining, instrumenting, or counting them (bfr-adverse-event-absence-replicates-without-becoming-safety) — and, most recently, that one set of authors converted that uncounted absence into the word "safe" at the highest cuff pressure in the corpus (bfr-authors-call-uncounted-absence-safe-at-the-highest-recorded-pressure). This is the first source in that corpus with an instrument capable of seeing a harm: a validated, population-normed patient-reported outcome, measured at four timepoints, against a randomised sham cuff. It saw one. The absence is no longer an absence of counting.

Three things bound it, and they are real. It is a secondary analysis of a trial designed for strength and function, with no power calculation for this outcome. It is one of four interaction tests reported without family-wise correction, and P = .014 does not clear a Bonferroni α of .0125 (bfr-mental-health-signal-is-one-of-four-uncorrected-tests). And blinding almost certainly failed in practice — 45-minute inflation cycles at 80% occlusion feel nothing like 20 mmHg, and no blinding-success check was run — which matters for a self-reported outcome.

⚑ The blinding objection is weaker than it looks, and the reason is the authors' own mechanism. They attribute the decline to discomfort: "discomfort resulting from the use of both NMES and BFR, each of which is known individually to cause significant myalgia, may be intensified when combined," compounded by prolonged soreness during a period of reduced readiness. If the proposed cause is the felt experience of the treatment, then "the participants could tell" is not a confound — it is the exposure. For someone deciding whether to spend nine weeks doing this, "occlusion made me feel worse" and "the experience of occlusion made me feel worse" are the same finding.

What makes the direction credible rather than incidental: it cost nothing to gain it. In the same trial, real BFR delivered no advantage on pain, knee function, or physical quality of life (bfr-added-nothing-to-pain-or-physical-function-against-a-sham-cuff), and the parent trial reported no strength or physical-performance benefit either. So the only variable that separated the arms is the one that went the wrong way.

Flagged high-dose-risk, not interaction. This is a dose-of-occlusion signal, not a between-intervention interaction. Per shared/safety.md, a safety flag gates rather than ranks: it caps any regimen appearance of BFR at "proposed — review with your doctor" regardless of benefit score, which is where BFR already sits.

Held suggestive, not strong — the source is appraised mixed, and this note does not claim BFR causes psychological harm. It claims something narrower and better supported: the first proper measurement came back negative, and seven prior declarations of safety had no instrument that could have detected it.
