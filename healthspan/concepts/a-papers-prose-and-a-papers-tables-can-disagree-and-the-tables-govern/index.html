This vault nearly accused a research group of arithmetic that does not exist, and PDF archival is what stopped it

The first draft of this source's digest asserted that five of the paper's twelve pooled estimates were internally impossible. Read exactly as the narrative Results section prints them, they are:

"HDL (WMD= 2.24 mg/dL; 95% CI = − 0.74 to − 3.74; p = 0.003)" — a positive estimate outside an all-negative interval, printed in descending order.

"fasting insulin (WMD= − 0.71 µIU/mL; 95% CI = − 1.82 to − 0.38; p = 0.202)" — an interval excluding zero, declared non-significant.

Table 3 reads 2.24 (0.74, 3.74) and -0.71 (-1.82, 0.38). ⚑ Every one of the twelve pooled estimates is coherent. The prose applies a spurious minus sign to interval upper bounds — an en-dash range separator turned into a minus during typesetting or copy-editing. It is a rendering fault, not an analytical one, and it appears in the HTML and in the published PDF alike.

⚑ The catch came from shared/pdf-archival.md, which says archival "never blocks the ingest." Tonight it did something better: it corrected one. The ladder took three rungs — Europe PMC's isOpenAccess flag reported "confirmed non-OA" for a gold open-access BMC article, the BMC /counter/pdf/ endpoint failed at the browser rung, and link.springer.com/content/pdf/ finally served the bytes. Had any rung been the last one tried, this vault would have published a false accusation of arithmetic incompetence against a named research group.

Three rules follow.

(1) The tables govern. When a paper's narrative and its tables disagree, quote the table. This is not a tie-break heuristic — tables are generated from the analysis output, prose is transcribed by a human under deadline.

(2) A coherence scan is a trigger, not a verdict. A mechanical CI-p coherence check — does the interval's relationship to the null agree with the reported p-value, and does the point estimate lie inside its own bounds? — is genuinely useful and costs seconds. ⚠ Its correct output is "go read the table." Its incorrect output is "the analysis is incoherent." The first draft wrote the second. The lens is worth adding to shared/study-appraisal.md in the first form only, and per CLAUDE.md's propagation rule it is logged for David rather than applied to a file copied across five repos.

(3) A prose-versus-table disagreement is itself evidence — and sometimes the prose error is the real one. ⚑ The same paper contains a second discrepancy where the table does not exonerate it. The Results section states that CoQ10 "did not increase HDL in participants with a baseline of HDL of lower than 40 mg/dL" and, eleven lines later, that it "did not increase HDL in participants with a baseline of HDL of higher than 40 mg/dL." Table 3 shows <40: +1.74 (−0.26 to 3.75), p=0.090 and >40: +2.63 (0.39 to 4.88), p=0.021, so the first sentence is right and the second is wrong — and the error inverts the clinically important half of the finding (the-coq10-hdl-benefit-is-absent-in-the-people-whose-hdl-is-actually-low).

⚠ The uncomfortable open question. This vault holds a substantial number of abstract-only and HTML-only ingests, written without a table to check against. How many carry a defect invented the same way is not knowable without re-reading them. Recorded rather than resolved.
