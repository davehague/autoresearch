The biggest fish-oil safety analysis was run by the fish-oil industry — and it found the harm anyway

The declared conflicts are extensive and directly on-topic. Four authors sit at the Fatty Acid Research Institute. The senior author holds stock in OmegaQuant Analytics, which sells Omega-3 Index blood testing. A co-author is Chief Medical Officer of CardioTabs, a company that sells omega-3 products. The first author holds a research award from GOED, the Global Organization for EPA and DHA — the omega-3 industry's trade association. Fifteen of the 35 pooled trials contributed unpublished atrial-fibrillation data supplied directly by their principal investigators, many of whom appear in the author list, and those data are not publicly available.

shared/evidence-model.md's bias-watch rule says such studies are flagged and never laundered into higher apparent certainty. shared/study-appraisal.md adds that industry funding is not fatal on its own. ⚑ This source is the case that shows why the rule has to be applied asymmetrically rather than as a blanket discount.

The paper's own conclusion runs against its authors' interest. It set out to re-examine a commercially damaging safety signal with four times the previous evidence base — and reproduced it, at a larger magnitude than the estimate it was re-examining (OR 1.43 versus Gencer's 1.25). ⚑ A harm finding that survives an author group's incentive to erase it is stronger evidence than the same finding from a neutral group, not weaker. The conflict, here, is a credibility multiplier on that half of the paper.

The reverse holds for the reassuring half. The three null strata align with the authors' commercial interest; they are where the data are thinnest (the-omega-3-reassurance-strata-differ-sixteen-fold-in-size-and-are-reported-identically); and they are what the Conclusions stretch furthest, extending safety "possibly up to 2400 mg/day" from a 3,264-person cell. Those claims get the discount.

⚑ The rule this vault takes from it: grade the two halves of a conflicted paper differently, by asking which direction each finding pushes relative to the funder's interest. A single reliability verdict on the source is the right unit for capping strength, but it is not a substitute for reading which specific claims the conflict would have bent, and which way.

Not an accusation and no evidence was re-graded. The disclosures are complete and voluntary; the methodological work — six months of investigator outreach, retention of zero-event trials, registry mining — is more effort against publication bias than any other source in this vault. It is recorded so a future run does not read this paper as a neutral referee settling the atrial-fibrillation question, and does not read its reassurances with the same confidence as its warning.
