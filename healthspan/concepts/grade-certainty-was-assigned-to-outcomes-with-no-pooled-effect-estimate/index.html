A certainty rating with nothing underneath it to be certain about

The review declines to meta-analyse, and gives a good reason: populations, intervention doses and outcome instruments differ enough that pooling "would have violated key assumptions required for valid quantitative pooling and risked producing a pooled estimate that was not clinically or statistically interpretable." This vault agrees with that judgement.

⚑ It then applies GRADE across ten outcome families and returns MODERATE certainty for five of them. GRADE rates confidence that a pooled effect estimate is close to the truth. There is no pooled effect estimate. The table's "Summary of effect" column reads "Exercise-centered multidomain interventions consistently improve frailty status" — a sentence. Imprecision is graded "Moderate" for outcomes whose precision was never computed. Publication bias is graded "Possible" for frailty reversal, and the overall rating is Moderate anyway.

⚑ The failure is not that the certainty is wrong; it is that it has no referent. Moderate certainty in what? Not in a magnitude — none exists. Not in a direction, since Moderate is not how GRADE expresses directional confidence. What the rating actually conveys is the authors' overall impression, dressed in a framework that readers correctly trust to mean something more specific.

⚑ Second instance in this literature in three weeks. frailty-network-ranks-bundles-without-reporting-effect-sizes records a frailty network meta-analysis that produced a full SUCRA leaderboard and printed no effect sizes at all. Same field, same failure: an apparatus of quantitative rigour built over an absent quantity. An appraisal lens for "certainty without an estimate" is requested in the source digest and logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

⚠ What this note does NOT claim. Narrative synthesis is legitimate and refusing to pool was the right call. The error is the certainty rating bolted on afterwards, not the decision that made it impossible.
