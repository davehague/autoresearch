Add a weighted vest to exercise you already do and the bone benefit disappears

The review ran two comparisons and only one of them is about the vest.

Against usual activity, vest-plus-exercise raised femoral-neck bone mineral density (SMD 0.59, 95% CI 0.05–1.13, p = 0.03, I² = 37%) and lumbar-spine BMD (SMD 0.73, 0.29–1.16, p = 0.001, I² = 0%). That comparison bundles a weighted vest with a supervised exercise programme and sets the bundle against people carrying on with ordinary life. Nothing in it separates the vest from the exercise.

Against exercise alone — the same training without the vest, which is the only contrast that isolates the thing being sold — the authors report it plainly: "Adding a vest to exercise alone did not confer a significant BMD benefit compared with exercise alone, although this finding is derived from a single study."

⚠ One trial is not a demonstration of no effect. No point estimate or interval is published for this comparison, and a single small trial cannot exclude a real vest effect. The authors label it as one study rather than selling it as equivalence, which is why shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 was weighed and did not fire. This is a failure to demonstrate, recorded as such.

⚠ And the dose is unknown. The vest load — kilograms, or percentage of bodyweight, progressed or fixed — sits in the study-characteristics table behind the paywall. Bone responds to strain magnitude. A review pooling a 2 kg vest with a 15 kg vest is pooling two different interventions under one word, and this note cannot tell which it did.

⚑ Where it sits: this is the fourth time the vault has controlled for the training and found the bone add-on doing nothing. creatine-bone-bmd-null — creatine plus resistance training gives no greater BMD than training alone at any site (5 RCTs, all p > 0.25). rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd — raising training intensity does not change BMD either. bone-density-network-ranks-sites-where-no-intervention-differed — a network meta-analysis whose site rankings sit on differences that were not there. The consistent reading across all four: loading the skeleton is what moves bone, and the accessories sold alongside the loading do not add to it.

⚠ Methodological caveat that cuts against the positive half, not this null. The review pooled with fixed-effect models, which assume a single true effect across nine ~40-person trials and narrow the intervals accordingly. The femoral-neck lower bound is 0.05. Under random effects it would very likely cross zero — so the confounded comparison is weaker than it looks, while this null is unaffected.
