The same pressure band has now been recommended by two papers and adopted by this vault neither time

Zhu 2026 recommended 40–60% of limb occlusion pressure for walking with a cuff. Miao 2026 recommends 40–60% of arterial occlusion pressure for an acute conditioning set. blood-flow-restriction has declined both, and the reasons are different each time.

Zhu's band was declined because the analysis under it did not behave. Across roughly 36 comparisons, exactly one reached significance — heart rate at 60% LOP, +3.82 bpm — and 80% LOP, the highest pressure, did not (bfr-walking-hemodynamics-modest-young-adults). A dose-response that skips its own top dose is a noise signature.

Miao's band is declined because it is a subgroup contrast, not a comparison anyone ran. No trial in the pool randomised a participant to 50% AOP against 70% AOP; the band comes from pooling seven studies that happened to use lower pressures against four that happened to use higher ones, inside a family of about twenty uncorrected subgroup comparisons, with the authors' own warning attached: "some optimal parameters are derived from single or a small number of studies and need further verification."

⚑ What has changed is the reason, not the verdict. shared/safety.md forbids converting a published range into a prescribed dose, and two weak analyses arriving at the same band do not make a strong one. But the corpus now has two independent findings pointing the same practical way — the benefit does not increase with pressure and may disappear above 60% AOP (bfr-above-sixty-percent-aop-loses-the-benefit-and-keeps-the-burden, bfr-warm-up-benefit-showed-no-dependence-on-cuff-pressure) — which is a fact about where the evidence points, not a protocol. The map's standing position holds: nine sources, nine prescription conventions, and the pressure question is "what pressure, set how, measured against what" — a conversation for a clinician or physio.
