The cocoa-flavanol cardiovascular benefit exists in one estimator and not the other — same trial, same events

A secondary analysis of COSMOS (randomised, placebo-controlled, 2×2 factorial, n=21,442 healthy older US adults, 500 mg/d cocoa flavanols, median 3.6 years) re-analysed the trial's total-CVD composite using generalized pairwise comparison ("win ratio"), which weights events by severity in a pre-specified hierarchy (fatal CVD > non-fatal MI > non-fatal stroke > revascularisation > carotid surgery > peripheral artery surgery > hospitalised unstable angina). Under that estimator: cocoa wins 3.41%, placebo wins 2.87%, reciprocal win ratio 0.84 (95% CI 0.72–0.99), p = 0.037.

Cox regression on the identical composite, in the identical participants, gave HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.79–1.03) — non-significant.

The claim graded here is the discrepancy, not the benefit. Two estimates, one dataset, overlapping intervals, both near 1.0 — differing mainly in which side of p = 0.05 they land on. A finding whose existence depends on the choice of statistical model is a finding about the model at least as much as about cocoa. The source's framing is that time-to-first-event models "underestimated benefit" — which presumes the conclusion. The symmetric reading is that a trial with a null pre-specified primary analysis was re-analysed with a different estimator and published once one agreed (cosmos-prespecified-primary-cvd-analysis-remained-null).

In fairness to the method. Win-ratio/GPC is a legitimate, recognised approach that addresses a real defect in composite endpoints: a Cox model scores a revascularisation the same as a cardiovascular death. The hierarchy was pre-specified and a reordering sensitivity analysis was run. The problem is not the technique; it is that the technique was applied after a null result and reported because it was positive, with no way to know how many estimators were considered.

Bounded honestly (why single-study, capped hard). The source's reliability verdict is unreliable — a fatal red flag fired for analytic multiplicity with the significant estimator promoted (shared/study-appraisal.md #2). Per shared/evidence-model.md, an unreliable source caps strength at single-study, which means this claim cannot move cocoa-flavanols or any other map on its own, in either direction. That cap is the point: the underlying COSMOS trial is strong — randomised, blinded, adjudicated hard endpoints, ITT — and its result is null. This re-analysis is not new evidence; it is a new estimator applied to old evidence. The parent trial's cocoa extract was supplied and partly funded by Mars Edge, which is flagged per the vault's bias-watch rule and is not treated as fatal on its own.
