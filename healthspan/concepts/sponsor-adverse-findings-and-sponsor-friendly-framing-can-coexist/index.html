A conflicted review can produce a sponsor-adverse result and a sponsor-friendly framing at the same time

This vault flags industry funding on sight and never launders it into certainty (shared/evidence-model.md's bias watch), while shared/study-appraisal.md is explicit that funding is not fatal on its own — it contributes to a downgrade "only when it compounds a real design weakness." Javaid 2024 is a useful worked example of why that distinction is doing real work rather than hedging.

The configuration. The review was supported by an educational grant from Amarin, which manufactures icosapent ethyl — high-dose purified eicosapentaenoic acid. Of everything the review examined, the one significant harm it found was against high-dose purified EPA: rate ratio 1.50 (1.13–1.99), p = 0.006 (high-dose-purified-epa-increases-bleeding-risk).

Which means the conflict argues for the finding, not against it. A funder-adverse result is the specific outcome a captured review would be expected to suppress. Its survival is weak positive evidence that the analysis was not steered. This is the mirror image of the vault's usual pattern — for instance cocoa-flavanol-cvd-benefit-is-estimator-dependent, where a Mars-funded trial's null was re-analysed into significance by a differently-chosen estimator, and the direction of the conflict lined up exactly with the direction of the rescue.

And yet the framing is precisely what a sponsor would want. The stated conclusion opens "Omega-3 PUFAs were not associated with increased bleeding risk" and describes its own significant harm as of "very modest" clinical significance. Both halves of that are defensible in isolation — the pooled null is real (omega3-no-overall-increase-in-bleeding-risk) and the absolute risk really is small (0.6%, one extra bleed per 166 treated). But a reader who stops at the conclusion learns the reassuring half and not the dose-dependent half.

The transferable rule, stated so a future run can apply it without re-deriving it. Funding predicts the framing more reliably than it predicts the numbers. Appraise the result on its design; appraise the conclusion sentence on its incentives; do not let a clean sponsor-adverse number buy credibility for a sponsor-flattering summary, and do not let a disclosed conflict discredit a number that runs against the sponsor's interest.

Where the vault has needed this before. Every case in which a paper's abstract and its own tables disagreed: supplement-outcome-specific-ranking-too-tidy-for-a-sparse-network (a ranking the paper's own GRADE table rated low), exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported (a leaderboard the Results section contradicts), stretching-injury-prevention-rests-on-one-trial (an abstract claim resting on a single included trial), rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered ("similar safety profiles" from an interval reaching 11.11). The consistent lesson is that the tables are the paper and the abstract is the argument — and funding tells you which way the argument will lean.
