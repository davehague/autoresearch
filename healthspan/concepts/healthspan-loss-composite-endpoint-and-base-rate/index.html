"Healthspan loss" gets a randomised operational definition and a base rate

ASPREE operationalised loss of healthspan as the first occurrence of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, dementia, cancer, or death, and measured it in 19,114 disease-free adults aged ≥70. 30.5% lost healthspan within a median 4.7 years — roughly 82 events per 1,000 person-years, essentially identical in both randomised arms.

Why the vault records the endpoint separately from the drug result. This ledger is full of interventions sold on healthspan while measured on something else — VO₂max, PSQI, telomerase activity, epigenetic clocks, mood inventories, grip strength, a cognitive z-score. Here is a hard, adjudicated, randomised-trial definition of the word, with a number attached. It gives every future longevity claim in this vault a concrete question to be held against: does it move a composite like this one, or does it move a marker someone hopes predicts it?

It is the outcome-side counterpart to biological-age-clocks. That map grades instruments — and found that nine of fourteen expert-consensus aging biomarkers did not predict mortality at all, with the whole panel discriminating at C-index 0.65 (most-consensus-aging-biomarkers-did-not-predict-mortality). This concept supplies what those instruments are meant to be surrogates for. The pairing states the operating rule plainly: the endpoint is countable, so an intervention that only moves the instrument has not been shown to move the endpoint.

Bounds on the number itself. The 30.5% / 4.7-year base rate is specific to disease-free community-dwelling adults aged ≥70 — a population selected for health at entry and then followed at an age of high competing mortality risk. It is not a general-population figure and cannot be applied to a middle-aged adult. Whether this composite was pre-specified or assembled for this secondary analysis is UNVERIFIED (full text paywalled), and no component-wise breakdown is available at abstract level — so a null on the composite could in principle conceal offsetting component effects. Read it as an operational definition with a base rate, not the definition.
