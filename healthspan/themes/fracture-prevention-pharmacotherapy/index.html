Fracture-prevention pharmacotherapy — metabolic & pharmacologic

Status: emerging (2026-09-27) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single Cochrane systematic review testing bisphosphonates, PTH/PTHrP analogues, denosumab, and romosozumab for fracture prevention specifically in men — the vault's first pharmacological (as opposed to exercise-based) bone-health lever, distinct from exercise-for-bone-health and whole-body-vibration. This map is prescription-only end to end: nothing here ever enters a Protocol slot (shared/safety.md) — it can only ever be a "discuss with your clinician" item, and it is coined to hold the evidence honestly, not to recommend anything. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Cochrane's own verdict is "we are very uncertain." Across 17 RCTs (4,132 men aged 50-73), no drug class — bisphosphonates, PTH/PTHrP analogues, denosumab, or romosozumab — has evidence precise enough to say whether it meaningfully cuts hip, vertebral, or other fractures in men. Every fracture-outcome estimate is GRADE "very low certainty," downgraded for risk of bias, serious imprecision (very low event rates in a population this age), and suspected publication bias. The one moderately-confident finding is a safety reassurance, not an efficacy claim: bisphosphonates probably do not raise adverse-event or serious-adverse-event risk.

Why this is a gap worth naming, not a graded null. Osteoporosis pharmacology trials have enrolled predominantly women; this Cochrane review exists because men have been largely excluded from the evidence base these drugs are marketed against. The honest reading is "we don't yet know if this works in men," not "this doesn't work" — the review does not sell an underpowered null as a demonstrated absence of effect, and neither does this map.
