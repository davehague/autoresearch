Ketogenic / very-low-carbohydrate diets — nutrition

Status: emerging (2026-08-13) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined by Żurawski 2026, a PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis that asked the only version of the ketogenic question that is actually answerable — when both arms are prescribed the same energy, does restricting carbohydrate still win? — and answered it at −1.49 kg (95% CI −2.41 to −0.58), GRADE LOW, from six trials and 259 people, most of them under twelve weeks, in tissue nobody identified. The map opens on a deflating answer delivered by an unusually disciplined paper.

Why a new map rather than a note on an existing one. The vault holds intermittent-fasting (when you eat), protein-target (how much protein), mediterranean-diet (overall pattern) and multidomain-lifestyle (everything at once). It holds nothing on macronutrient composition — the single most-argued axis in popular nutrition. The gap is real and this source is good enough to open it honestly.

What we believe now

Under matched energy prescriptions, a ketogenic diet produces a small additional weight reduction of about 1.5 kg, at low certainty, over short horizons, in a quantity that has not been shown to be fat. Match protein as well and the estimate stops saying anything at all.

The primary estimate is stable — I² = 0.0%, robust to SD-imputation assumptions, to excluding the one high-risk-of-bias trial (−1.42, −2.01 to −0.83), and to leave-one-out (ketogenic-advantage-under-matched-energy-is-about-one-and-a-half-kilograms). And the authors decline to let their own I² = 0% be read as homogeneity, since six studies cannot detect heterogeneity in the first place.

Two things dissolve it, and the paper reports both against itself.

(1) Protein. Only three of six trials met the registered protein-matching criterion; in the other three the ketogenic arm ate proportionally more protein. Restricted to the matched three: −1.79 kg (95% CI −5.69 to +2.11), p = 0.187 — directionally the same, and compatible with nothing (ketogenic-advantage-becomes-uninformative-when-protein-is-also-matched). ⚠ An absence of a demonstrated effect, not a demonstration of absence — the interval is uninformative in both directions. This is the most important fact on the map: the cleanest test of the actual proposition is three trials wide and silent.

(2) Tissue. Body composition was never synthesised, and five of six trials ran 6–12 weeks — the window in which glycogen depletion and its bound water produce a one-to-two-kilogram shift that is not adipose. The confound is the same size as the finding and arrives on the same schedule (ketogenic-weight-difference-cannot-be-attributed-to-fat-loss). Ketosis itself was biochemically confirmed in only four of the seven included studies.

The most transferable finding is about the literature, not the diet. Higher-quality syntheses and larger samples report smaller low-carbohydrate advantages — a broad, largely ad-libitum synthesis reports −2.17 kg, this strict energy-matched one −1.49 kg, and a twelve-month-minimum one −0.91 kg (low-carb-advantage-shrinks-as-synthesis-quality-and-sample-size-rise). An effect that shrinks as the design improves is behaving like an artefact of the design.

Why emerging and not something stronger or weaker. One source, mixed reliability, all concepts capped suggestive, GRADE LOW by the authors' own assessment. It is not contested — nothing here rebuts a prior vault position, because the vault had none. It is not fading — the small effect is real and stable as far as it goes.

Sub-threads: none — one question (does carbohydrate restriction beat higher-carbohydrate eating once energy is held constant?) with a single pooled answer and a single subgroup that dissolves it; the map's other content — tissue attribution, the quality gradient, and now the lipid trade-off — are caveats and costs attached to that one estimate rather than separable objects. The long-term-outcome strand remains a gap with no source in it yet. [reviewed 2026-08-13 @ 1 bullets]

⚑ NEW 2026-09-15 — the map's own flagged lipid gap, closed. A 53-RCT meta-regression (Zhao 2026) finds ketogenic diets raise LDL-C (+8.22 mg/dL, GRADE low) and total cholesterol (+8.06 mg/dL, GRADE low) — and the rise is largest in lean, younger adults over shorter trial windows, exactly the profile least likely to be monitored (ketogenic-diet-raises-ldl-c-more-in-lean-younger-adults). The same synthesis also confirms triglyceride and HDL-C improvement (GRADE moderate) — but the HDL-C gain fades with duration rather than growing (ketogenic-diet-triglyceride-and-hdl-gains-attenuate-over-time). This does not move Status — a new outcome domain, not a challenge to the weight-loss claim — but it converts the map's founding-day ungraded lipid flag into a sourced, GRADE-rated finding, and it belongs in the "no recommendation" reasoning below alongside the adherence-cost argument.

⚑ NEW 2026-08-16 — a second outcome tested, and the same answer came back. A systematic review of 52 studies comparing the Mediterranean diet, VLCKD and intermittent fasting on testosterone in men with obesity found VLCKD most consistent (+1.5 to +3.0 nmol/L) — and then reported the mediator: ~3 nmol/L per 10 kg of weight lost, common to every pattern (weight-loss-not-diet-composition-carries-the-testosterone-gain). The whole ketogenic advantage is what 5–10 kg of weight loss buys, however it came off. ⚠ The endpoint is a hormone concentration, not a symptom, no head-to-head trial exists, and most studies measured total testosterone without SHBG (ketogenic-testosterone-advantage-is-biochemical-and-never-tested-head-to-head). ⚠ The source is weak — unregistered, three databases, no meta-analysis — and the map's Status does not move on it. What it adds is corroboration of the map's own founding shape from an unrelated outcome.

No recommendation, and the reason is arithmetic

Against GOAL.md's preference for least work, least discomfort, low daily maintenance, a diet requiring ≤50 g of carbohydrate a day is among the highest-adherence-cost interventions this vault has ever graded. What it buys under fair conditions is 1.5 kg of low-certainty weight over three months, in unidentified tissue, which the authors explicitly decline to call clinically meaningful. The trade is bad on this evidence.

No dose, no macronutrient prescription, no protocol, no regimen slot — not even Watch. Per shared/safety.md this vault does not write eating plans and does not synthesise targets sources have not stated. Now also carrying a real, GRADE-low LDL-C/total-cholesterol increase concentrated in lean, younger adults (ketogenic-diet-raises-ldl-c-more-in-lean-younger-adults) — a further mark against a diet already losing on adherence-cost arithmetic alone.

VLCKD carried the most consistent evidence, at +1.5 to +3.0 nmol/L total testosterone. ⚑ And then the paper reports the mediator that dissolves its own ranking: ~3 nmol/L per 10 kg of weight lost, common to all three patterns (weight-loss-not-diet-composition-carries-the-testosterone-gain, pushes-back, suggestive). The entire ketogenic advantage is the size of the effect predicted by 5–10 kg of weight loss alone — and VLCKD is a diet characterised by rapid early loss. ⚠ No head-to-head RCT with testosterone as the primary outcome exists (the authors call it "a critical evidence gap"), and evidence on symptoms, sexual function, fertility and quality of life is "either absent or inconsistently reported" (ketogenic-testosterone-advantage-is-biochemical-and-never-tested-head-to-head, open-question, suggestive). ⚠ Most studies measured total testosterone without SHBG, which rises with improving insulin sensitivity — so an unknown share of the gain may be a binding-protein artefact rather than usable androgen. Appraised mixed, at the floor of the band: PRISMA-followed, unfunded, and unusually candid in its own limitations — but not PROSPERO-registered, searched only SciSpace, Google Scholar and PubMed (no Embase, Cochrane, Web of Science or Scopus), ran no meta-analysis so there is no pooled estimate or publication-bias test, pooled 7 animal experiments into a human clinical synthesis, and acknowledged that publication bias "may have inflated positive findings, particularly for VLCKD studies" — the exact claim it headlines. Capped suggestive. ⚠ Status HELD at emerging — a non-registered narrative review on a surrogate cannot upgrade a map anchored by a registered matched-energy meta-analysis, and highest-tier-wins means the weaker synthesis does not outrank the stronger one already here.

  • 2026-09-15 — Ingested Zhao 2026 (keto-diet-lipid-profile-meta-regression-zhao-2026, BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, systematic review + meta-regression, 53 RCTs, Cochrane RoB 2 + GRADE, no external funding, no declared conflicts; gold OA, PDF archived via Europe PMC render endpoint after the rung-1 script hit a 403). LDL-C +8.22 mg/dL (5.08–11.40, GRADE low), total cholesterol +8.06 mg/dL (3.04–13.07, GRADE low) — larger in adults ≤50, BMI ≤30, and trials ≤12 weeks. Triglycerides −22.31 mg/dL (GRADE moderate), HDL-C +3.52 mg/dL (GRADE moderate), the latter's gain shrinking with duration (meta-regression coefficient −0.125, p=0.011). Closes the map's founding-day flagged lipid gap. Appraised mixed; both concepts capped suggestive. Status HELD at emerging — new outcome domain, not a challenge to the weight-loss estimate.

Open gaps

  • Top gap — the trial that would settle it. A controlled-feeding RCT matching both energy and protein, running beyond twelve weeks, measuring body composition and confirming ketosis biochemically. The authors ask for exactly this.
  • ✅ CLOSED 2026-09-15 — lipids. Zhao 2026's 53-RCT meta-regression grades the LDL-C/total-cholesterol increase this map flagged at coining and declined to grade from a discussion paragraph. See ketogenic-diet-raises-ldl-c-more-in-lean-younger-adults. Still open: no long-term cardiovascular outcome data exists for this literature — the lipid signal is a surrogate, not an event.
  • Hard outcomes: nothing. No mortality, no cardiovascular events, no diabetes incidence. The map holds one surrogate (body weight) at one short horizon.
  • Diet quality. The source raises reduced fibre and bioactive-compound intake under heavy carbohydrate restriction, with consequences for gut microbiota. Unmeasured here; relevant to fermented-foods and mediterranean-diet.
