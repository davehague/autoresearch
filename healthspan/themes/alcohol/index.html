Alcohol — nutrition / exposure

Status: contested (2026-08-26) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. ⚠ Read the Status word narrowly: almost nothing on this map is contested. The harm side is as settled as observational evidence gets — monotonic, threshold-free dose-response for seven IARC-adjudicated cancer sites, dose-dependent blood-pressure elevation, a relative risk of about 3 for dementia with alcohol dependence, injury risk from a blood alcohol concentration of 0.03 g/dl, and 62 conditions defined as fully alcohol-attributable in ICD-11. ⚑ What is contested is one narrow, heavily marketed claim: that one or two drinks a day protects the heart. Every cohort meta-analysis finds that J-shaped curve. Every well-conducted Mendelian randomisation study fails to — and the protective limb "becomes less pronounced as more potential biases are controlled." The founding source, whose authors build the WHO and Global Burden of Disease alcohol models, argues the genetic evidence is too weak to refute the J-curve while conceding it does not support it. The map opens holding that tension and nothing wider. ⚑ Also unlike every other map here: this is a lever you pull by doing less, not more — which is why a vault with 62 intervention maps, including beetroot juice and weighted vests, did not have one for the largest modifiable exposure in the adult population until tonight.

Sub-threads: none — one dated bullet, so no declaration is owed until ten (shared/evidence-model.md); the natural future strands are already visible in the founding source (cancer · cardiovascular · neurological · injury · reversibility). [reviewed 2026-08-26 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

Four things, in descending order of confidence.

(1) For cancer there is no safe amount and no J. Dose-response is monotonic increasing across the seven cancer sites the International Agency for Research on Cancer has formally adjudicated as causally linked to alcohol — lip and oral cavity, other pharynx, oesophagus, colon and rectum, liver, larynx, and female breast (alcohol-and-cancer-is-monotonic-with-no-threshold). Stable dose-response relationships also appear for stomach, biliary tract and pancreatic cancer, and for lung (though the lung association nearly vanishes in never-smokers). The mechanism explains the shape: alcohol metabolises to acetaldehyde, which damages DNA directly; it also drives oxidative stress and alters oestrogen. ⚑ A mutagenic mechanism acting on every molecule metabolised has no dose below which nothing happens — only a dose below which the effect is small. Those are different claims and only the second is true.

(2) The cardioprotective J-curve is real in cohort data, shrinking under scrutiny, and absent from the genetic evidence. It appears in all major cohort meta-analyses for ischaemic heart disease, ischaemic stroke and type 2 diabetes, and it has plausible mechanisms — higher adiponectin, lower fibrinogen, inhibited platelet aggregation, reduced inflammation, all acting on ischaemic outcomes specifically. ⚠ And the founding review states that the protective limb "becomes less pronounced as more potential biases are controlled" (the-alcohol-heart-benefit-shrinks-as-each-additional-bias-is-controlled) — which is the signature of an artefact, not an effect. The named artefacts are the ones this literature is famous for: sick-quitter bias (the non-drinker reference group is contaminated with people who stopped because they became ill), healthy-user bias, and self-reported exposure throughout. Mendelian randomisation — using inherited ADH1B and ALDH2 variants, assigned at conception, to approximate a trial nobody could run — was supposed to settle it. Across 20 studies: seven null, one harmful, two mixed, one protective; among AUDIT-C-based studies, two harmful and one null. ⚑ The decisive line is a quality gradient: of the studies at low-to-moderate risk of bias, none found alcohol protective; 15 of the 20 were at high risk of bias (no-low-risk-of-bias-mendelian-randomisation-study-found-alcohol-protective). ⚠ The counter-case is fair and is why this is contested rather than fading: 17 of 20 studies fitted only log-linear models and could not have detected a J-shape if one existed, the non-linear methods that exist are known to produce spurious results, and no study separated average volume from heavy episodic drinking — the exact condition the J-curve depends on. A design that cannot see the condition cannot test the claim.

(3) The J-curve, where it holds, requires the absence of heavy drinking occasions — so the weekly average is the wrong unit. The protective limb holds "generally only without heavy episodic drinking," and heavy episodic drinking "confers no cardiovascular benefits — even for otherwise non-heavy drinkers," regardless of average volume (the-alcohol-j-curve-requires-the-absence-of-heavy-drinking-occasions). ⚑ Seven drinks across seven evenings and seven drinks on one Saturday produce the same average and different biology: concentrated intake reverses the platelet benefit into pro-coagulation, raises blood pressure acutely, worsens lipids, and disrupts cardiac conduction — raising atrial fibrillation, arrhythmia and sudden-cardiac- death risk. Guidelines and self-assessment run on weekly totals, and the evidence says the total is the wrong unit for the outcome the guidance is implicitly about.

(4) Heavy drinking is the largest modifiable dementia risk factor on the Lancet Commission's list. Alcohol dependence carried a relative risk of about 3 for all forms of dementia in a large French all-hospital- patient cohort, and the review states this exceeds every other risk factor the Lancet Commission identifies (alcohol-dependence-carries-a-larger-dementia-risk-than-any-lancet-commission-factor). It also accounted for the majority of early-onset dementia. ⚠ This does not extend to non-heavy drinking, where the evidence is genuinely inconsistent and age-specific; the review's own recommendation is to model only the heavy-drinking harm, restricted to early-onset dementia, and this map adopts that conservatism rather than the more quotable version.

(5) The reversibility profile is the most decision-relevant part, and it is very unevenly distributed. Injury and intoxication-mediated risks reverse immediately; blood pressure in days to weeks; liver cirrhosis risk immediately on reduction — demonstrated at population scale by the Gorbachev reforms, the German occupation of Paris, and alcohol tax increases; brain atrophy partially, on CT, with sustained abstinence. ⚠ Cancer largely does not reverse, and established cirrhosis often does not (alcohol-harms-reverse-on-very-different-timescales-and-cancer-is-the-one-that-does-not). ⚑ Reduction pays off fastest on exactly the axes a healthy adult would notice, and not at all on the one that would kill them.

⚠ What this map deliberately does not contain. No threshold. No "safe amount." No units-per-week figure. The founding source supplies risk functions, not cut-points, and shared/safety.md forbids synthesising a recommendation a source does not state. Anyone wanting a number must get it from a guideline.

⚠ And the gap that matters most for this vault's own goal, open on day one. GOAL.md lists "nothing that disrupts sleep" as a hard constraint, and alcohol's suppression of REM sleep and fragmentation of the second half of the night is not covered by the founding source at all. It is real pharmacology with no vault-quality source ingested. Logged as an open gap, not fabricated into a graded concept.
