Beetroot juice (dietary nitrate) — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-08-02) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single systematic review + meta-analysis of 38 placebo-controlled trials (n = 703), on a surrogate endpoint (VO₂max) with detected publication bias. Opened at emerging and it will take a hard endpoint to move it. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Beetroot juice produces a small increase in maximal oxygen uptake in recreationally active adults, and there is no evidence that it changes anything a person would notice off an exercise-testing ergometer.

The pooled effect is SMD 0.24 (95% CI 0.11–0.37), falling to 0.19 (0.07–0.31) once trim-and-fill models the five studies the funnel plot says are missing — Egger's test detects small-study effects at p = 0.014 (beetroot-juice-raises-vo2max-by-a-small-amount). The effect is entirely a function of how untrained you are: recreationally active 0.38 (0.21–0.55) at high GRADE certainty, moderately trained 0.17, highly trained/elite 0.07 (−0.09 to 0.23), not significant at low certainty (beetroot-juice-vo2max-effect-disappears-in-trained-athletes). Dose shows a threshold then a plateau — null below 6.4 mmol nitrate, 0.27 in the standard 6.4–12.8 mmol band, no larger above it — and three or more consecutive days beats a single acute dose (beetroot-nitrate-dose-plateaus-in-the-standard-band).

⚑ Why this map exists when fourteen other candidates were refused, stated plainly because it is the whole justification. This vault has declined beta-alanine, probiotics, collagen (twice), hypoxic training, cocoa flavanols, green exercise, whole-body vibration, traditional Chinese sports, exercise snacks and exergaming. Those refusals turned overwhelmingly on one thing: the design could not separate the thing being sold from the things beside it. This corpus answers that question with its comparator. The standard placebo here is nitrate-depleted beetroot juice — the identical beverage with the nitrate stripped out — used in 34 of the 38 trials, and restricting the analysis to those trials returns SMD 0.25 against the full pool's 0.24 (nitrate-depleted-placebo-isolates-the-beetroot-effect). The isolating contrast exists and the effect survives it. Add 22 of 38 trials at low risk of bias, zero at high risk, a machine-measured outcome, publication bias detected and corrected for, and no funding and no conflicts in a commercially saturated literature — and the bar the refusals failed is cleared.

⚑ NEW 2026-08-03 — the map acquires a second axis, and it is acute, tiny and unreplicated. Jurga 2026 gave 13 physically active men 800 mg of nitrate (≈12.9 mmol) and tested isokinetic knee extension two hours later. Peak torque, maximum single-repetition work and torque at 30° all rose at 60°/s and 180°/s — work up 17 J (p = 0.005, d = 0.94) at the slowest speed (acute-nitrate-raises-isokinetic-torque-at-low-and-moderate-velocity). Under the 240°/s fatigue protocol, nothing moved (nitrate-strength-effect-disappears-under-the-fatigue-protocol), and eight EMG measures and post-exercise lactate were all null, so the trial produced a performance effect and no mechanism for it (nitrate-torque-gain-has-no-emg-or-lactate-signature). Hold this loosely: the only power calculation in the paper is for plasma nitrate, not for any performance endpoint; fifteen comparisons ran without correction; and the registration post-dates the ethics approval by fourteen months.

⚑ On the name — the argument below was settled on 2026-08-02 and is reopened one day later. The obvious alternative is dietary-nitrate, and the depleted-juice placebo arguably licenses it: the contrast really does isolate the nitrate rather than the beetroot. The map was named for the beverage anyway — four of the thirty-eight trials did not use a depleted placebo, the dose is delivered as juice, and what a person buys is a bottle of concentrate. Jurga 2026 moves that balance. Its active arm was 800 mg of nitrate as SODIUM NITRATE added to 5 mL of beetroot concentrate, and its placebo was the identical 5 mL of concentrate alone: both arms drank the beetroot, only one got the nitrate, and the juice was the placebo (beetroot-strength-evidence-comes-from-added-sodium-nitrate-not-juice). That is a stronger attribution design than the depleted-juice comparator — it isolates by addition rather than by subtraction, with no ion-exchange step to argue about. The map is not renamed tonight: one 13-person acute trial does not overturn a 38-trial synthesis whose dose is reported in mmol delivered as juice. But the name is now a live question rather than a settled one, and a second source testing the salt should trigger the rename. Recorded here rather than acted on, because renaming a map breaks every inbound link and is a human's call.

⚠ Why this is nowhere near a healthspan claim, and why emerging is generous rather than harsh. Every outcome in this corpus is VO₂max measured during a graded exercise test, usually on a supplement day, usually in a crossover. No trial measured anything after a washout, anything sustained, or any health outcome. zone-2-cardio holds the reason to care about aerobic fitness at all — cardiorespiratory-fitness-all-cause-mortality, a 122,007-person cohort where mortality falls with fitness and shows no ceiling — and that is fitness people had, not fitness a drink produced for an afternoon. An acute change in a fitness measurement and a change in fitness are different things, and this literature has only tested the first.

The practical bottom line. Beetroot juice is a food, it is cheap, it carries no interaction anywhere in this vault, and its effect is best evidenced in exactly the population GOAL.md describes. Nothing here argues against drinking it. Nothing here makes it a healthspan lever, and against this map's own neighbours it is badly outclassed — training-duration-outperforms-session-volume-for-vo2max says another four weeks of consistent training raises VO₂max by +2.69% (1.25–4.19) on well-powered randomised evidence, which is a better-evidenced route to the same number and free. Not medical advice; no dose is prescribed here — the mmol bands above are the source's, in the source's units.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-02 — ⚑ MAP COINED. The first candidate supplement in this vault to arrive with an isolating placebo, and the effect it isolates is small. Opened at emerging; flagged for human review. Ingested Qin & Kim 2026 (Frontiers in Nutrition 13:1858216, PRISMA-2020 systematic review + random-effects meta-analysis of 38 randomised and crossover placebo-controlled trials, n = 703 healthy adults, six databases from inception to 2026-01-31; no funding of any kind and no declared conflicts, in a literature where the standard nitrate concentrate is a commercial product; PROSPERO CRD420261414721 — registered retrospectively, recorded as a red flag; 22/38 trials at low risk of bias, 16 some concerns, none high; GRADE moderate overall; appraised mixed, capped suggestive; gold-OA PDF archived 4.15 MB via the browser rung after rung 1 reported no free PDF — harvest lag, not a paywall). Pooled VO₂max/VO₂peak: SMD 0.24 (0.11–0.37), p < 0.001, I² = 48.3% (beetroot-juice-raises-vo2max-by-a-small-amount). ⚠ Egger's test p = 0.014, funnel asymmetry in the bottom-left quadrant; trim-and-fill imputed five studies and pulled the estimate to 0.19 (0.07–0.31) — attenuated, still excluding zero, and the corrected number is the one this map carries. ⚑ The subgroup gradient runs monotonically against the marketing. Recreationally active 0.38 (0.21–0.55), GRADE HIGH — the only high-certainty rating in the paper; moderately trained 0.17 (0.03–0.31); highly trained and elite 0.07 (−0.09 to 0.23), not significant, GRADE low (beetroot-juice-vo2max-effect-disappears-in-trained-athletes). The people who buy ergogenic aids to compete are the subgroup it is not demonstrable in — read as no evidence of benefit, not evidence of no benefit, the distinction this vault has now enforced thirteen times. ⚑ Dose: threshold then plateau, the sixth literature in this vault to draw that curve. <6.4 mmol null (0.13); 6.4–12.8 mmol → 0.27 (0.14–0.40), the most consistent band; >12.8 mmol → 0.22, no larger. Chronic ≥3 days 0.29 (0.14–0.44) beats acute 0.19 (0.04–0.34) (beetroot-nitrate-dose-plateaus-in-the-standard-band). ⚠ These are cross-trial bin comparisons, not a fitted dose-response — the pre-specified meta-regression could not be run because primary trials reported continuous moderators too incompletely. ⚑ THE REASON THIS MAP EXISTS: 34 of 38 trials used nitrate-depleted beetroot juice as the placebo, and restricting to them returns SMD 0.25 (0.12–0.38) against the full pool's 0.24 (nitrate-depleted-placebo-isolates-the-beetroot-effect). The comparator is the same drink with the active compound removed. Against a month in which HMB could not be separated from 24 g of protein, hypoxic HIIT's benefit shrank against a normoxic control, green exercise had no objective endpoint anywhere, and traditional Chinese sports could not be separated from comparators the paper never described — this is the first candidate literature where the design answers the attribution question rather than the discussion section arguing about it. ⚠ Bounded honestly, and the bounds are what keep it at emerging: every endpoint is a surrogate measured on a supplement day; publication bias is detected, not merely suspected; registration is retrospective; VO₂max and VO₂peak were pooled without consistent verification criteria; samples are predominantly male; and 703 people across 38 studies is ~18 per trial, which is the corpus shape that produces small-study effects in the first place. open-question. Logged as an auto-coined map in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

  • 2026-08-03 — The map gets a second axis within 24 hours of being coined, and the naming decision it argued yesterday is immediately put under pressure. Status held emerging. Ingested Jurga 2026 (Nutrients 18(14):2377, randomised double-blind placebo-controlled crossover, n = 13 physically active men, 25.0 ± 5.2 y, ≥3–4 sessions/week; public funding only — Polish Ministry of Science Development of Young Researchers grant; no conflicts; ⚠ ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07605455 registered 26 May 2026 against an ethics approval of 3 April 2025, so pre-specification is unverifiable; appraised mixed, capped suggestive; ⚠ pdf_unavailable — gold-OA article whose PDF bytes are bot-gated, four browser-rung routes all returning GATED, with the rendered full text retrieved on the same rung). Acute 800 mg nitrate → isokinetic knee extension, 2 h post-dose, three velocities. At 60°/s and 180°/s: peak torque up; maximum single-repetition work 311 ± 40.5 vs 294 ± 38.3 J (p = 0.005, d = 0.94) and 231 ± 32.0 vs 217 ± 34.3 J (p = 0.029, d = 0.69); torque at 30° up at both speeds (p = 0.029, 0.027) (acute-nitrate-raises-isokinetic-torque-at-low-and-moderate-velocity). Relative peak power hit significance at 60°/s only — the authors call the 180°/s p-value borderline and non-significant, which is the honest label and is credited. ⚑ At 240°/s under the fatigue protocol, every measure was null (work p = 0.513, torque at 30° p = 0.267, torque at 0.18 s p = 0.389) (nitrate-strength-effect-disappears-under-the-fatigue-protocol). That is the second independent way this literature has declined to deliver where it is sold — Qin & Kim found the VO₂max effect absent in the highly trained, and this trial finds the torque effect absent under fatigue, which is the endurance condition the whole nitrate market is built on. Read as no evidence of benefit, not evidence of no benefit — the distinction now enforced fourteen times here. ⚑ The trial found an effect and no mechanism. Eight surface-EMG measures across both vasti, amplitude and frequency domains: eight nulls, largest p = 0.106. Post-exercise blood lactate: supplementation main effect p = 0.780, interaction p = 0.853. The authors write it plainly — "the physiological mechanisms underlying these findings remain to be established" (nitrate-torque-gain-has-no-emg-or-lactate-signature). ⚑ THE METHODS DETAIL THAT REOPENS THE MAP'S NAME: the active arm was 800 mg of nitrate as SODIUM NITRATE added to 5 mL of beetroot concentrate, and the placebo was the identical 5 mL of concentrate alone (≈57 mg nitrate). Both arms drank the beetroot; only one got the nitrate (beetroot-strength-evidence-comes-from-added-sodium-nitrate-not-juice). This isolates by addition where Qin & Kim isolated by subtraction — a cleaner attribution design than the one that coined the map, and it attributes the strength effect to a salt, not a beverage. Yesterday's naming argument stands as written but no longer stands unopposed; rename on a second source, not on this one. ⚠ Bounded hard, and the bounds are why nothing moves: the only a priori power calculation is for plasma NOx, so no performance endpoint in this trial has a stated detectable effect size; fifteen performance comparisons ran with no multiplicity correction and the hits sit at p = 0.027–0.042; d ≈ 0.7–0.9 from 13 paired observations is upward-biased and reported without intervals; the dose is ≈12.9 mmol, at the top edge of this map's own standard band (beetroot-nitrate-dose-plateaus-in-the-standard-band); and everything is acute, surrogate, male, young, and measured on a supplement day with nothing after washout. extends. Status held emerging — the map said it would take a hard endpoint to move, and a 17-joule difference in one repetition is not one.

  • 2026-08-14 — ⚑ THIRD SOURCE, THE LARGEST BY FAR, AND THE THIRD CONSECUTIVE TIME THE EFFECT FAILS TO APPEAR WHERE THE DESIGN GETS STRONGER. Status held emerging; the naming trigger stays unfired. Ingested Eroglu 2026 (beetroot-juice-sprint-performance-fatigue-meta-eroglu-2026, Nutrients 18(15):2513, systematic review + random-effects meta-analysis, 23 randomised trials / n = 401 healthy adults aged 18–40, 22 of them crossovers, trials from 2014 to June 2026; protocol registered on OSF, DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/3KRE4; PEDro range 6–10, mean 8.7, seven trials at 10/10; RoB 2 crossover extension — one trial low risk, 22 some concerns, none high; no funding, no conflicts; appraised mixed, capped suggestive; ⚠ pdf_unavailable — gold-OA article, PDF bytes Cloudflare-gated at the MDPI /pdf endpoint, full text read at the browser rung). ⚑ Eight of ten pooled outcomes are null, and six of those sit at I² = 0.0%. Perceived exertion SMD −0.08 (−0.27 to 0.10) across 13 studies; countermovement jump 0.19 (−0.10 to 0.49) across 7; peak power 0.19; mean power 0.22; 10 m sprint −0.18; 20 m sprint −0.11; heart rate 0.12; blood lactate 0.50 at I² = 77% (beetroot-juice-null-across-sprint-jump-and-exertion-outcomes). GRADE low for eight outcomes, very low for two. The authors' conclusion: "low- to very-low-certainty evidence does not support its routine use to enhance short-distance or repeated-sprint performance." ⚑ The perceived-exertion null is the one that matters to GOAL.md, and it is not an underpowered one. Thirteen trials, zero heterogeneity, an interval too tight to hide a meaningful effect in — beetroot juice did not make hard efforts feel easier. This vault has refused fifteen times to read a wide null as evidence of absence; this is a different object, and the distinction is what makes the refusals honest. ⚑ Two positives, and both are narrow. Time to peak power SMD −0.92 (−1.29 to −0.54) — from four Wingate studies only, while peak power itself was null — and handgrip strength SMD 0.40 (0.05–0.74), p = 0.027, five studies. ⚠ Handgrip and countermovement jump were both added AFTER registration, which the authors disclose in their methods: "added after registration when comparable data became available." Of the two late additions one came back null and one came back positive (beetroot-handgrip-finding-is-a-post-registration-outcome). Credit for printing the deviation where a reader will find it — this vault has spent weeks on sources whose registration status could not be established at all. ⚑ What this does and does not do to the map. It does not push the map's claims back — sprint performance was never one of them — it extends the ledger onto an axis where the compound does nothing. ⚠ It does touch the live strength axis: handgrip is a second positive beside Jurga's isokinetic torque, but obtained by a different method, at GRADE low, in a ten-outcome family with no multiplicity correction, one of them added post hoc. Two weak positives obtained differently are not a replication; the strength axis stays open. ⚠ Ten of 23 trials verified no nitrate or nitrite biomarker, so an unknown share of these nulls may be trials where nothing was absorbed — recorded because it is the honest limit on reading them. On the name: the map said the rename to dietary-nitrate should fire on a second source testing the salt. This is not one — 23 trials of the beverage. Trigger unfired; the beverage name now has the largest corpus on the map behind it.

  • ⚠ 2026-08-02 — the relationship to training status is the mirror image of creatine-monohydrate's, and the pair is instructive. Two supplements, opposite relationships to the same behavioural lever — and in both cases the behaviour is what is doing the work. Recorded as a coherence observation, not a graded finding.
