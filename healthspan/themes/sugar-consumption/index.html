Sugar / added-sugar consumption — nutrition

Status: emerging (auto-coined 2026-10-01) — ⚑ flagged for human review, on the same structural- homelessness ground as ultra-processed-food, and a broader evidentiary base than that map's. Coined by a PROSPERO-registered umbrella review (Woelber 2026) that screened 10,593 records down to 228 systematic reviews across 18 health-outcome domains, finding consistent positive associations between sugar/sugar-sweetened-beverage (SSB) intake and type 2 diabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, all-cause mortality, oral disease, irritable bowel syndrome, and cognitive/mood disorders — plus weaker-evidence associations with cancer, NAFLD, kidney disease, gout, asthma, poor sleep, reduced bone density, and telomere shortening. 71% of the 228 included reviews were AMSTAR-2 rated low or critically low; the evidence is broad, consistent in direction, and mostly weak.

What we believe now

Higher free-sugar / sugar-sweetened-beverage intake tracks, dose-responsively and across at least seven independent outcome domains, with worse metabolic, cardiovascular, oral and cognitive health — in an observational literature the umbrella review's own authors say cannot separate sugar from the broader unhealthy-lifestyle cluster it travels with. No randomised evidence exists for any hard outcome; every number below is observational.

  • The strongest, best-replicated domain: type 2 diabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome. T2DM risk +13–31% per daily SSB serving (dose-response, multiple independent reviews); obesity risk +18% per serving, up to +50% at ≥2 servings/day; metabolic syndrome +14–56% depending on beverage type. This is also where AMSTAR-2 quality concentrated — several moderate/high-rated reviews, a rarer finding in this umbrella. (sugar-consumption-dose-response-with-t2dm-obesity-metabolic-syndrome)
  • Cardiovascular disease, stroke and all-cause mortality move in the same direction, at modest but consistent magnitudes. Hypertension +6–21% per serving; stroke +6–13% per serving; CHD/stroke/heart failure +17%/+7%/+8% per 250ml/day; all-cause mortality +4–12%. Quality here was more mixed (3 of ~22 appraised cardiometabolic reviews rated high AMSTAR-2). (sugar-consumption-cardiovascular-disease-and-all-cause-mortality-risk)
  • ⚠ A striking 2.8-fold dementia-risk number exists, and it is a single-study flag, not a graded finding. The more-replicated cognition signal (17% higher general cognitive-disorder risk, 22–41% higher ADHD-symptom risk in children, 5–25% higher depression risk — each from 2–3 independent reviews) is the trustworthy part of this domain; the dementia estimate needs independent corroboration before it means anything load-bearing. (sugar-consumption-cognitive-decline-dementia-and-mood-signal-flagged-single-study-on-dementia)
  • The evidence base is broad but shallow, and the umbrella's own authors name the confound directly rather than burying it: "the reported negative outcomes are likely related not only to sugar, but to a cluster of lifestyle factors." 71% of 228 included reviews AMSTAR-2 low/critically low; several primary studies within the umbrella carry sugar-industry funding, disclosed rather than laundered. (sugar-consumption-evidence-base-is-broad-but-mostly-low-quality-and-lifestyle-confounded)

What this map does not say. It does not say sugar causes any of these outcomes in isolation. A consistent direction across 18 independent outcome domains, multiple geographies, both sexes, with dose-response in several domains, is not nothing — but no study design here separates sugar from the calories, the displaced fibre/protein, the physical inactivity, or the person who eats it. The same caveat ultra-processed-food carries applies here, more explicitly, because this umbrella's own authors say so themselves.

⚑ Why this was coined, and how to reverse it

Against the four-part coining bar this vault set on beetroot-juice (2026-08-02) — an isolating contrast · a machine-measured outcome · low risk of bias · a pre-specified dose:

  • Isolating contrast — FAIL. Observational sugar/SSB intake, bundled with a broader lifestyle pattern the umbrella's own authors name explicitly.
  • Machine-measured outcome — PARTIAL. Several outcomes are hard and objectively ascertained (T2DM diagnosis, CVD events, mortality, bone fracture); the exposure is self-reported throughout, with no consistent instrument across the 228 reviews.
  • Low risk of bias — FAIL, though less emphatically than ultra-processed-food. 71% of 228 reviews low/critically low (vs that map's 0-of-43-cohorts-low-risk cardiovascular literature); 29% of reviews here rate moderate-or-high, concentrated in the metabolic-disorders and oral-health domains.
  • Pre-specified dose — PARTIAL. WHO's existing <10%/<5%-of-energy thresholds are cited (not derived here); several individual domains (T2DM, obesity) show clean per-serving dose-response gradients from their own underlying reviews.

Score ≈ 1.5 of 4 — weak by the bar, marginally stronger than ultra-processed-food's ~1/4 on disclosure quality and the proportion of moderate/high-AMSTAR-2 evidence.

⚠ This reverses a prior refusal, and the reversal is clean rather than contested. On 2026-08-06 the vault explicitly refused a candidate added sugar map (the 16th refusal) when Zhu 2026's natural experiment on the 1953 end of UK sugar rationing found large cancer-incidence effects — refused specifically because "a 1953 policy applied before the participants' second birthday is not an intervention a 43-year-old can occupy." The refusal's own stated exit criterion was explicit: "what would open it: adult evidence." Woelber 2026 is exactly that — 228 reviews of adult-population, dose-response, today-modifiable SSB/sugar intake. This is not the vault re-litigating a settled question; it is the refusal's own named condition being met four weeks later by an unrelated, much larger source. Filed under sugar-consumption rather than added sugar to track the broader exposure (free sugar in any form) the umbrella review actually studied.

It was coined anyway, on the same structural-homelessness ground as ultra-processed-food (docs/backlog.md, requested since 2026-08-07): "or the claim spans ≥3 existing maps and belongs to none." Sugar/SSB intake bears simultaneously on ultra-processed-food (correlated, overlapping exposure — SSBs are NOVA group 4), intermittent-fasting, mediterranean-diet, ketogenic-diet (all diet-pattern maps where sugar reduction is a shared mechanism), vitamin-d (via the bone-density finding) and glp-1 (via the metabolic-disease overlap) — and was unfindable from any of them before tonight.

⚠ REVIEWER'S GROUNDS FOR REVERSING IT:

  1. This map and ultra-processed-food now cover genuinely overlapping ground (68% of this map's evidence base is specifically sugar-sweetened beverages, a NOVA group-4 category). A reviewer could reasonably argue these should be one map, not two, and that coining a second structurally-homeless map compounds rather than resolves the fragmentation problem the first one was meant to solve.
  2. The coining bar scored ~1.5/4 — still below the bar the vault has refused candidates at before (coenzyme-q10 at 2/4, ginger at 2/4).
  3. No randomised evidence exists anywhere in this map's founding source. Every claim is observational.

The counter-argument, for completeness: sugar/SSB and "ultra-processed food" are related but distinct exposures — not all UPF is sugary (e.g. many savoury snacks, processed meats) and not all sugar sources are NOVA group 4 (e.g. honey, fruit juice are NOVA group 1–2 but still "free sugar" under WHO's definition). A reader asking "does sugar matter" and a reader asking "does processing level matter" are asking different questions that happen to share a lot of underlying literature. Keeping them separate, cross-linked, mirrors how intermittent-fasting and ketogenic-diet stay separate despite both bearing on weight and glycaemic control.

David's call; git is the undo. One git revert removes this map; its four concept notes would then be filed on ultra-processed-food by claim shape, with a scope note distinguishing "processing" from "sugar" claims.

What would move this

  • An isocaloric, macronutrient-matched feeding trial varying only free-sugar content, with a hard cardiometabolic or mortality endpoint. Does not exist and may not be runnable at the required duration — the same structural gap ultra-processed-food names for its own question.
  • A dedicated, independently-conducted meta-analysis of the dementia signal — currently a single review, single estimate, no extractable confidence interval.
  • A re-pooling of the 228 reviews' unique, non-overlapping primary studies — the umbrella's own stated limitation; its authors explicitly deferred this to future work.
