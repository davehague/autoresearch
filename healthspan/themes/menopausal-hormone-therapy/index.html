Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT/HRT) — metabolic

Status: consolidating (2026-11-26, upgraded from emerging) — the vault's first female-specific hormone-replacement map, now carrying its first RCT-tier evidence. A single large Danish cohort opened this map on all-cause mortality (no excess found); a 2026 Cochrane review — pooling 24 RCTs, ~70% of the usable data from the two landmark 1990s trials (WHI, HERS) — now supplies GRADE-rated, moderate-certainty, real (non-trivial) effects on the specific outcomes a cohort can't isolate: combined therapy raises breast cancer and clot risk while both regimens lower fracture risk. Two independent, high-tier bodies of evidence (the WHI combined arm and its concurrently-run oestrogen-only arm) replicate a real, non-null risk/benefit shape with no rebuttal in play — the bar shared/evidence-model.md draws for consolidating. Prescription-class rule applies in full — by shared/safety.md this is never ranked in a daily protocol and no dose is set here. What we believe now

A Danish nationwide register-based cohort (n=876,805 women, median 14.3-year follow-up) found no excess all-cause mortality with past-or-current menopausal hormone therapy use versus never-use — adjusted HR 0.96 (95% CI 0.93–0.98). The association follows a hump shape by cumulative duration: null under 1 year, strongest at 3–9.9 years (HR 0.89–0.90), fading back toward null at ≥10 years. Transdermal delivery shows a larger association (HR 0.85) than oral (HR 0.98) — consistent with known first-pass-metabolism pharmacology, though still observational. Cardiovascular mortality was modestly lower in users under 5 years; cancer mortality was flat. A small subgroup — women with both ovaries surgically removed at ages 45–54 — showed a substantially larger apparent benefit (27–34% lower mortality), which the study's own authors call hypothesis-generating, not confirmed.

The central limitation is healthy-user bias, unresolved by this design. Women who initiate and sustain MHT for years are systematically healthier, more engaged with preventive care, and have access correlated with socioeconomic advantage — this registry's covariate adjustment cannot fully rule that out, and the duration-response hump is equally consistent with "benefit grows then plateaus" and with "healthier, more-adherent users accumulate at longer durations while frailer users stop." This closes the door on the WHI-era mortality-increase fear in a general population, but does not establish a causal mortality benefit.

Cochrane's 2026 update supplies the RCT-tier counterpart this map was missing (new 2026-11-26). Pooling 24 RCTs (n=45,660, ~70% of the usable data from WHI 1998's two concurrently-run arms plus HERS 1998), combined continuous therapy (oral conjugated equine oestrogen + medroxyprogesterone acetate) probably increases breast cancer risk (RR 1.27, 1.03–1.56, moderate certainty) and may increase stroke (RR 1.39) and venous thromboembolism (RR 2.03, both low certainty) (combined-hormone-therapy-increases-breast-cancer-stroke-vte-risk-cochrane-2026) — while both combined and oestrogen-only therapy probably reduce clinical fractures (RR 0.78 and 0.73, moderate certainty) (hormone-therapy-reduces-fracture-risk-both-regimens-cochrane-2026). Oestrogen-only in post-hysterectomy women skips the breast-cancer signal but probably increases stroke and gallbladder disease instead — two different risk/benefit shapes by regimen, not one "hormone therapy" effect. This doesn't contradict the Danish cohort's no-excess-mortality finding (different outcomes: specific morbidity vs. all-cause death); it fills in what the cohort couldn't isolate. The honest caveat, stated three times by Cochrane's own authors: this is overwhelmingly oral-tablet evidence from 1990s trials in women averaging over 60 — transdermal delivery (the form the cohort found had the strongest mortality association) and the "start near menopause" timing hypothesis are essentially untested here.
