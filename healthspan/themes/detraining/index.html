Detraining — exercise

Status: emerging (2026-08-07) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined by a PROSPERO-registered synthesis of 86 randomised controlled trials (n = 5,670), each pairing a supervised exercise block with a defined cessation phase and a passive non-exercising control. This is the vault's first map about stopping rather than doing — the question every other map assumes away, and the one a person with an injury, a deadline or a holiday actually faces. It opens emerging for reasons of source quality and framing, not evidence volume: a single mixed-appraised source caps every concept at suggestive, and a suggestive-capped single source cannot carry a map further.

Sub-threads: none — one argument (exercise adaptations decay unevenly, on an outcome-specific clock, and the reassuring version of that claim is measured against the wrong baseline); the metabolic-versus-structural split is the argument's internal structure rather than a separable strand. [reviewed 2026-08-07 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

Stopping does not delete the file — but the reassurance is measured against the wrong gap, and the metabolic layer goes first. Across 86 RCTs, exercise-induced improvements did not snap back to baseline at cessation. Aerobic fitness was the most durable thing built: VO₂max remained above baseline in short-, medium- and long-term (>6 months) strata (SMD 0.29–0.62). Body mass (−1.12 to −2.09 kg), waist circumference (−1.43 to −2.17 cm) and systolic blood pressure (−2.69 mmHg long-term) also persisted past six months off. The metabolic gains did not: HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, fasting glucose, HOMA-IR, fasting insulin and diastolic blood pressure held only inside the ≤2-month window (exercise-benefits-decay-unevenly-after-stopping).

⚑ The mechanism explains the ordering and makes it credible. Glucose and lipid handling run on recent muscle contraction — skeletal-muscle lipoprotein lipase activity and GLUT4 translocation to the cell membrane are switched on by the last few sessions, so insulin sensitivity falls within days of stopping. Vascular and cardiac adaptations — arterial elasticity, left-ventricular remodelling, capillary density — are structural and erode over months. The subgroup pattern lands where that physiology predicts, without special pleading.

⚠ And the headline measures the wrong gap, which the authors state as their own eighth limitation. The pooled contrast is baseline versus post-detraining — so "residual benefit" means still better than before you started, not still near where you got to. Someone who trained twelve weeks, gained five VO₂max points, stopped for a year and kept one is counted here as retaining a benefit (detraining-residual-effects-measured-against-baseline-not-peak). That is the difference between "your break was survivable" and "your break was cheap," and this evidence supports only the first.

⚑ One outcome went the wrong way, and it is the one a bathroom scale hides. Fat-free mass fell significantly below baseline at medium-term (2–6 month) follow-up — the only harmful-direction result in a twenty-item panel — while body mass and waist circumference in that same window still read as improved (fat-free-mass-falls-during-detraining-while-scale-weight-still-looks-good). The authors name the risk as "occult sarcopenia during detraining." Weight is the wrong instrument to track a layoff with.

Why emerging, since 86 trials will suggest otherwise. The pooled evidence is 85% "some concerns" and 10% high risk of bias by the review's own RoB 2 assessment, with ~65% of trials failing to report allocation concealment or pre-register. Roughly twenty outcomes × three time strata were tested without multiplicity correction — the authors ask that subgroup findings be read as exploratory, and several persistence claims sit a whisker from zero. Habitual activity during the break went unmonitored in most trials, so an unknown share of every "residual effect" may be people who did not fully stop, or who kept a dietary habit acquired during the programme. The evidence base is large; the map is new, single-sourced, and built on trials most of which did not say how they concealed allocation. What would move it: a synthesis measuring loss from peak rather than gain over baseline, stratified by modality, with monitored activity through the break.

What this map does not yet contain, and will not invent. ⚠ No maintenance dose. The obvious question — what is the minimum that preserves the gains? — is listed by the authors as an open question for future research, explicitly including whether "exercise snacks" suffice. shared/safety.md forbids synthesising a prescription no source states, and none does.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-07 — Ingested Zhang 2026 (Frontiers in Physiology, systematic review + meta-analysis of 86 RCTs, n = 5,670, PROSPERO CRD420251149494, six databases to 2025-12-27, dual blinded independent screening, RoB 2, GRADE; no conflicts declared). Supervised exercise blocks of 2–24 weeks followed by complete cessation for 1 week to 5 years, every trial against a passive non-exercising control; ages 8.3–83.5, healthy through pulmonary fibrosis; aerobic, resistance, handgrip, combined, stretching and mind-body modalities pooled together. Persisting >6 months off (vs baseline): VO₂max (SMD 0.29–0.62 across all three strata), body mass (−1.12 to −2.09 kg), waist circumference (−1.43 to −2.17 cm), systolic BP (−2.69 mmHg). Persisting only ≤2 months: HDL-C, triglycerides, fasting glucose, HOMA-IR, fasting insulin, diastolic BP, mean arterial pressure, body-fat percentage (subgroup difference p = 0.04). Never persisting: LDL-C, resting heart rate, muscle mass, lean body mass, skinfold thickness, waist-to-hip ratio. ⚑ Fat-free mass significantly decreased at medium-term follow-up — the only harmful-direction result. Auto-coins the map at emerging — a large registered synthesis with a properly isolating comparator, held down by 85% some-concerns/10% high-risk trials, an uncorrected twenty-outcome panel, and a baseline-not-peak contrast the authors flag themselves (limitation eight). Publication bias detected on four outcomes; for resting heart rate, trim-and-fill flipped the inference (null → significant). ⚑ The authors also print a fasting-insulin result they refuse to believe — improving during detraining — calling it likely "methodological or statistical artefacts," which is the behaviour that makes the rest of the paper readable.

  • 2026-08-08 — ⚑ The map's top open question gets its first answer, and the answer is a subtraction. This map opened one day ago naming the maintenance-dose gap — what is the minimum that preserves the gains? — and refusing to invent one. Tonight's creatine-monohydrate ingest (Stout & Pabian 2026, narrative mini-review, tier mechanistic, mixed) strikes the most obvious candidate off the list: during strict immobilization creatine demonstrably raises intramuscular creatine and does not preserve muscle cross-sectional area or strength (Backx 2017, n = 30, 7-day full-leg cast, 20 g/day) (creatine-does-not-prevent-atrophy-during-strict-immobilisation). The mechanism explains it and makes it credible: creatine amplifies a mechanical stimulus, and a layoff supplies none. Its value returns only once progressive loading resumes (creatine-recovery-benefit-appears-only-once-loading-resumes), which is a claim about rebuilding, not about holding. ⚑ Second, smaller signal, pointing the other way. Xu 2026 on resistance-training finds remote, screen-delivered exercise produces measurable strength and balance gains in a deficient population (digital-exercise-improves-strength-and-balance-in-sarcopenia-at-low-certainty). No trial there studied maintenance through a break — but it is the vault's first evidence that the delivery constraint, which is what makes a layoff a layoff, is not by itself fatal. ⚠ The maintenance-dose gap remains OPEN and no dose is synthesised (shared/safety.md). Status held emerging — a mechanistic-tier mixed review cannot move this map, and the second signal is an inference about an untested question. Net: one candidate eliminated, one channel kept open, nothing prescribed.
