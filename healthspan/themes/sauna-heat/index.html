Sauna / passive heat — recovery & mobility

Status: emerging (2026-07-07) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's first Recovery & mobility lever. Coined by a meta-analysis of RCTs that, unusually, deflates the popular sauna-for-heart-health story: passive heating did not improve most cardiometabolic or vascular markers, leaving only a modest systolic-BP effect. Opened emerging on that honest, tension-carrying evidence.

What we believe now

For a healthy adult, passive heat is a pleasant, low-impact recovery habit with — at best — a small blood-pressure benefit, not a demonstrated longevity lever. In 20 RCTs (2–15 weeks), passive heating had no effect on flow-mediated dilation, arterial stiffness, resting heart rate, glucose, or lipids. The only surviving signal was a systolic-BP drop with whole-body heating (−4.11 mmHg) and in at-risk / established-CVD subgroups (−2.52 mmHg); local heating did nothing.

The tension worth keeping visible. The enthusiasm for sauna rests largely on observational cohorts — the Finnish Kuopio data associate frequent (4–7×/week) sauna use with markedly lower cardiovascular mortality — but that association has not been reproduced in randomized trials on mechanism markers, and no RCT has tested sauna on hard cardiovascular outcomes or mortality. This is the same observational-ceiling honesty applied to sleep-regularity and resistance-training, but here the RCT layer pushes back on the cohort hype rather than confirming it.

Why emerging, not higher: high evidence tier (meta-analysis of RCTs), but the result is mostly null on surrogate endpoints, from small, unblinded, biased trials — and the one positive signal is least applicable to a healthy person. A knee-friendly comfort/recovery option to watch, not adopt on cardiometabolic grounds.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Hamaya 2025 (Am J Prev Cardiol, meta-analysis, 20 RCTs): passive heating null on FMD, PWV, HR, glucose, lipids; modest systolic-BP drop with whole-body heating (−4.11 mmHg) and in at-risk adults (−2.52 mmHg); surrogate endpoints, moderate-to-high risk of bias. Auto-coins the map at emerging — RCT reality-check on the observational sauna-mortality hype.

  • 2026-07-25 — Ingested Bustos Carvajal 2026 (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, systematic review, 8 trials, no meta-analysis, PROSPERO CRD420251156387, conflict-free, CC BY; appraised unreliable — fatal flag #2) as the map's first head-to-head test against cold, and the map's first SLEEP thread. Two things arrive. (1) An acute performance signal: in a Wingate test minutes after immersion, hot-water immersion raised mean anaerobic power +2.2% while cold lowered it −2.4%, each p<0.05 vs control and p<0.0001 vs each other (hot-immersion-raises-and-cold-immersion-lowers-acute-anaerobic-power, safety_flags: [interaction], noted in both this map and cold-water-immersion) — the mirror image of the cold finding two independent syntheses now agree on. (2) A sleep signal: HWI beat control (p<0.001) and beat cold (p=0.017) on a 0–100 mm VAS sleep-quality scale (hot-water-immersion-sleep-quality-single-trial-signal). ⚠ Both are capped single-study, and the sleep one especially: it is one significant cell among dozens of uncorrected comparisons, on a visual-analogue scale, in 14–40 young athletes with 6 of 8 studies male-only, from a review whose commonest quality failing is unblinded assessors and which could not obtain 14 of its 22 shortlisted full texts, and whose abstract contradicts its own Table 5 on creatine kinase (chronic HWI lowered CK; acute HWI RAISED it vs control, p=0.046). ⚠ And note the null it sits inside: across the same eight studies no perceptual outcome responded to either temperature (water-immersion-does-not-improve-perceived-recovery-either-temperature) — soreness, fatigue, RPE and general recovery all moved with time in every arm including control. The comparison the sleep claim would have to survive: the entire supplement shelf pools to PSQI −0.70, below the ~3-point MID (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold), while exercise + CBT reaches −2.80 at moderate certainty (exercise-plus-cbt-improves-sleep-in-insomnia). extends; Status held emerging — an unreliable source cannot move a map in either direction. No temperature, duration or timing prescribed. OA PDF archived.

  • 2026-08-05 — The map's sleep thread goes from one unreliable cell to ten randomised trials, and the answer splits by instrument. Ingested Chen 2026 (water-immersion-thermotherapy-sleep-meta-chen-2026, International Journal of Biometeorology, 10 RCTs in healthy adults, eleven databases including three Chinese-language ones, no competing interests; appraised mixed, confirmed paywall). Water immersion raised total sleep time (SMD 0.63, p = 0.0438) and sleep efficiency (SMD 0.64, p = 0.0419); sleep-onset latency null (water-immersion-raises-total-sleep-time-and-sleep-efficiency). ⚑ And the PSQI questionnaire is null on its total and on all seven components — subjective sleep quality p = 0.3721, its own sleep-efficiency item p = 0.6793 against the pooled efficiency result's p = 0.0419 (water-immersion-sleep-benefit-is-absent-on-every-subjective-measure). Same construct, two instruments, opposite answers. ⚑ This rebuts the subjective half of the map's 2026-07-25 sleep claim: a higher tier, a better population (healthy adults, not 14–40 young male athletes) and a mixed rather than unreliable source tested that construct across ten trials and found nothing. Back-edge written onto hot-water-immersion-sleep-quality-single-trial-signal; the tension resolves into a scope condition — what survives is minutes counted, not quality reported. ⚠ Held loosely and for stated reasons: no confidence interval is published for any estimate, both positives clear 0.05 by four thousandths, and eleven comparisons ran with no correction and no pre-specified primary outcome (water-immersion-sleep-estimates-are-reported-without-confidence-intervals). ⚠ The review states no water temperature, duration, immersion depth or timing before bed — a thermotherapy review whose thermal dose is unreadable — so no protocol is derived and none may be (shared/safety.md). Status held emerging: a suggestive-capped source cannot move a map, and the two positives are marginal. No safety_flags set.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠ INTERACTION — heat and cold are opposite acute modifiers of power, and both are timing gates (new 2026-07-25). hot-immersion-raises-and-cold-immersion-lowers-acute-anaerobic-power finds HWI +2.2% / CWI −2.4% mean Wingate power minutes after immersion (p<0.0001 between them). Flagged in both this map and cold-water-immersion per §6.5, interaction, efficacy-defeating rather than hazardous, and it enters no benefit score. ⚠ The positive half is deliberately NOT converted into a recommendation to bathe before training — one Wingate result in young male athletes, from an unreliable source, does not license a protocol, and shared/safety.md forbids inventing one. The cold half is actionable because it is independently corroborated by cold-water-immersion-transiently-impairs-explosive-power and points at avoiding something.
  • ⚠ The sleep thread lands in an already-redundant cluster, and its only real advantage is price (new 2026-07-25). melatonin, magnesium-glycinate, l-theanine and chamomile all compete for the same bedtime slot with effects that are small, below the MID, or unattributable inside a pooled node (supplement-sleep-benefit-not-attributable-to-any-supplement). A hot bath's distinguishing feature is not efficacy — it is that there is nothing to buy, which is the shape GOAL.md favours. Recorded as a redundancy edge, not a co-recommendation, and not counted at regimen time.
  • ⚑ Contributes to the environmental-add-on pattern carried on zone-2-cardio — and to its 2026-07-25 amendment. Heat's objective cardiometabolic results are null (passive-heat-null-on-most-cardiometabolic-markers); its two new signals here are an acute performance surrogate and a self-reported sleep score. That is the same split the amended rule now states: on outcomes measurable without asking, the environment is decoration; on outcomes that can only be asked about, it looks large and cannot be blinded. Compare green-exercise-evidence-rests-entirely-on-unblindable-self-report.
  • Complementary with — not a substitute for — the aerobic/strength levers. Passive heat is sometimes marketed as "exercise-mimetic," but its randomized cardiometabolic evidence is far weaker than that for zone-2-cardio and resistance-training (both with mortality-grade signals). It is a low-impact, knee-friendly adjunct that pairs harmlessly with them; it does not replace them. One RCT in the review combined sauna with exercise — the intended framing is add-on, not alternative.
  • David-specific (GOAL.md): genuinely knee-safe. Zero mechanical loading makes passive heat compatible with patellar-contusion recovery — its honest appeal is comfort/recovery, not cardiometabolic payoff.
  • ⚑ The map's own 2026-07-25 rule is now contradicted from inside the map, and both cases are kept (new 2026-08-05). The amendment above states that for environmental add-ons, "on outcomes measurable without asking, the environment is decoration; on outcomes that can only be asked about, it looks large and cannot be blinded" — built from green-exercise-evidence-rests-entirely-on-unblindable-self-report and the map's null cardiometabolic results. Chen 2026 inverts it exactly: the countable outcomes moved and the asked-about ones did not. The rule is not repealed — it was built on a different outcome family and one 10-trial review does not overturn it — but it is no longer unopposed. A rule that only ever accumulates confirming instances is a habit, not a finding, so the map carries both directions from here.
  • ⚑ Third literature, same measurement split — this is now a fact about sleep instruments, not about baths (new 2026-08-05). daily-steps holds walking-improves-objective-sleep-duration-and-wakefulness beside walking-subjective-sleep-quality-unresolved, and separately exercise-sleep-objective-and-subjective-converge-weakly. Two syntheses of the exercise-and-sleep literature and now one of the immersion literature have landed in the same place: the device says something changed and the questionnaire says it did not. Cross-linked to daily-steps; the transferable claim is about how sleep is measured, and it applies to any future sleep source this vault ingests.
  • The redundancy edge from 2026-07-25 is unchanged and slightly better grounded. melatonin, magnesium-glycinate, l-theanine and chamomile still compete for the same bedtime slot with effects that are small or unattributable (supplement-sleep-benefit-not-attributable-to-any-supplement), and exercise + CBT still reaches PSQI −2.80 at moderate certainty (exercise-plus-cbt-improves-sleep-in-insomnia) against everything here. A hot bath's advantage remains that there is nothing to buy, not that it works better. Recorded as redundancy, not co-recommendation, and not counted at regimen time.
