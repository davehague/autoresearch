Sauna / passive heat — recovery & mobility

Status: emerging (2026-07-07) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's first Recovery & mobility lever. Coined by a meta-analysis of RCTs that, unusually, deflates the popular sauna-for-heart-health story: passive heating did not improve most cardiometabolic or vascular markers, leaving only a modest systolic-BP effect. Opened emerging on that honest, tension-carrying evidence.

What we believe now

For a healthy adult, passive heat is a pleasant, low-impact recovery habit with — at best — a small blood-pressure benefit, not a demonstrated longevity lever. In 20 RCTs (2–15 weeks), passive heating had no effect on flow-mediated dilation, arterial stiffness, resting heart rate, glucose, or lipids. The only surviving signal was a systolic-BP drop with whole-body heating (−4.11 mmHg) and in at-risk / established-CVD subgroups (−2.52 mmHg); local heating did nothing.

The tension worth keeping visible. The enthusiasm for sauna rests largely on observational cohorts — the Finnish Kuopio data associate frequent (4–7×/week) sauna use with markedly lower cardiovascular mortality — but that association has not been reproduced in randomized trials on mechanism markers, and no RCT has tested sauna on hard cardiovascular outcomes or mortality. This is the same observational-ceiling honesty applied to sleep-regularity and resistance-training, but here the RCT layer pushes back on the cohort hype rather than confirming it.

Why emerging, not higher: high evidence tier (meta-analysis of RCTs), but the result is mostly null on surrogate endpoints, from small, unblinded, biased trials — and the one positive signal is least applicable to a healthy person. A knee-friendly comfort/recovery option to watch, not adopt on cardiometabolic grounds.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Complementary with — not a substitute for — the aerobic/strength levers. Passive heat is sometimes marketed as "exercise-mimetic," but its randomized cardiometabolic evidence is far weaker than that for zone-2-cardio and resistance-training (both with mortality-grade signals). It is a low-impact, knee-friendly adjunct that pairs harmlessly with them; it does not replace them. One RCT in the review combined sauna with exercise — the intended framing is add-on, not alternative.
  • David-specific (GOAL.md): genuinely knee-safe. Zero mechanical loading makes passive heat compatible with patellar-contusion recovery — its honest appeal is comfort/recovery, not cardiometabolic payoff.
