Stretching / flexibility training (mobility) — recovery & mobility

Status: emerging (2026-07-15) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from Weerasinghe 2026 (9 RCTs, athletes), the vault's first real stretching source — replacing a statistical ghost with modest evidence. The map's headline is deliberately split: range of motion, yes; injury prevention, not established here.

What we believe now

Stretching reliably improves range of motion, modestly. Its injury-prevention reputation exceeds its evidence. And this map exists because the goal file names stretching, not because the evidence is strong.

(1) Range of motion — the supported claim, now on 79 RCTs instead of 9 (updated 2026-07-24). Chronic static stretching improves lower-limb range of motion: Hedges' g = 0.851 (0.710–0.991), p < 0.001, across 79 randomized controlled trials, n = 3,287 (Oba 2026, conflict-free, PEDro 6.15) (chronic-static-stretching-improves-lower-limb-flexibility). That confirms the map's founding claim at roughly nine times the evidence weight and a far tighter interval — the earlier reading (Weerasinghe: ankle dorsiflexion +1.79 cm, isometric strength +3.6 N, both at I² = 98%, stretching-improves-range-of-motion) stands and is superseded in weight, not contradicted. Held suggestive, not promoted: the outcome is near-tautological and unblindable — stretch a muscle, then measure how far it stretches, with effort-dependent instruments — heterogeneity remains substantial (I² = 66.1%), Egger's test detected small-study effects at p < 0.001 (trim-and-fill imputed nothing, which is weak reassurance at this heterogeneity), and the paper carries no PROSPERO registration and no GRADE assessment. Restoring range is mechanistically expected and among the least controversial findings in exercise science. For a joint being rehabilitated, it is a reasonable, low-risk aim.

(1a) And after 79 trials the dose is still unknown (new 2026-07-24). Oba's authors state that stretch volume, intensity and frequency were inconsistently reported and were not examined as moderators (stretching-dose-remains-unspecified-across-79-trials). GOAL.md optimises for least work for maximum benefit and shared/safety.md forbids synthesising a dose no source states — so this map says stretch consistently and refuses to name an amount. Note the contrast: daily-steps and zone-2-cardio both have specifiable dose-response curves from observational data, while stretching, with a randomized base of 79 trials, is less prescribable than either. Design quality and prescriptive usefulness are different axes.

(2) Injury prevention — the overstated claim. The paper's abstract says stretching "appears effective in reducing injuries." The evidence is a single trial (individualised static stretching, ~30% fewer injuries, p < 0.05); no other included RCT measured injuries, and the authors concede the real-world injury evidence is "limited" (stretching-injury-prevention-rests-on-one-trial). The wider literature is genuinely split — stretching is not an all-purpose injury-prevention strategy, though a muscle-injury-specific benefit is plausible. The vault adopts that honesty, not the headline.

(3) Population — the binding caveat, PARTIALLY closed on the outcome side and STILL OPEN on the intervention side (updated 2026-07-24). Weerasinghe's 9 trials enrolled athletes aged 13.5–27. Ingram 2026 supplies the missing population on the outcome side — apparently healthy adults, mean age 47.6, David's bracket — so the map is no longer only young-athlete evidence. What Ingram closes on population, it opens on ambition: see (4). ⚠ And the paper that looked like it closed the intervention side does not. Oba 2026 reports no moderation of the flexibility effect by age (p = 0.853), sex (p = 0.990), training status (p = 0.221) or baseline flexibility (p = 0.260) and concludes stretching works "across diverse healthy individuals." The cell counts: of 79 trials, 59 were in young adults, 13 in children/adolescents — and 3 in middle-aged adults, 4 in older adults, 3 in sedentary people (stretching-age-generalisation-rests-on-three-middle-aged-trials). A moderator null on three trials reports the distribution of the literature, not the biology. This is shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3, and the vault refuses it here for the same reason it refused it in Shen 2026, Wu 2026, Doan 2026, Chen 2025 and Yassine 2026 — including when the null was convenient. Accepting Oba's version would have closed this very gap, which is precisely why it doesn't get a pass. What the vault actually believes: stretching very probably does work in middle-aged adults; that is a prior from mechanism and from the consistent direction of every cell, and a prior is not a finding.

(5) The quadriceps are the thinnest and weakest cell in the whole literature (new 2026-07-24). Oba's muscle-group split: knee flexors g = 0.995 (47 trials), plantar flexors g = 0.656 (22 trials), knee extensors g = 0.550 — on SIX trials — declared non-significant at p = 0.100 (stretching-quadriceps-evidence-thinnest-and-weakest-of-the-lower-limb). A near-twofold spread called equivalence on six trials. The compartment attached to GOAL.md's March 2026 patellar contusion is simultaneously the smallest effect and the smallest evidence base in the largest stretching synthesis ever assembled, and all six of those trials were in healthy knees. This is not an argument against stretching the quadriceps — the point estimate is a moderate benefit with the same sign as every other cell. It is a refusal to manufacture a compartment-specific claim in either direction.

(4) Does flexibility actually predict health? Barely, at low certainty (new 2026-07-16). A meta-analysis of 45 prospective cohorts (n=48,020, mean age 47.6) finds objectively measured flexibility a weak-to-null independent predictor of later health — modest, trivial-magnitude associations with lower pain/disability (RR 0.99) and frailty/mobility limitation (RR 0.98); null for cardiometabolic disease, musculoskeletal disease, and injury; and mortality/falls too sparse to pool (flexibility-weak-predictor-of-health-outcomes-adults). Certainty very low to low; the source is a preprint. Read in both directions: this is limited support, not a debunk — the point estimates favor benefit and mortality was under-evidenced, not null — but it refuses the inflated version. The two sources compose: stretching reliably buys range of motion (2); being flexible is not, on current evidence, a health lever the way fitness or strength is (4). So the case for stretching rests on the proximate ROM/rehab benefit and the goal file — not a longevity or disease-prevention claim it cannot support. And Ingram's null on injury independently deflates (2)'s already-thin injury-prevention headline.

(6) The map's first cardiovascular thread arrives, and it is a surrogate from six studies (new 2026-07-27). Logan 2026 offers stretching a mechanism where (4) says it has no outcome: 4- to 12-week stretching programmes lowered pulse wave velocity — SMD −1.02 (95% CI −1.79 to −0.25), p = 0.012, nominally "large" (longitudinal-stretching-lowers-arterial-stiffness), while a single bout did essentially nothing — SMD −0.22 (−0.62 to 0.17) (acute-stretching-does-not-change-arterial-stiffness). Pulse wave velocity is how fast a pressure wave travels down an artery: stiffer arteries carry it faster, so a lower value means more compliant vessels. It predicts cardiovascular events; it is not one. ⚠ The direction is taken and the magnitude is not. A full standard deviation of vascular improvement from stretching, on six studies per estimate, with non-randomised controlled designs eligible and heterogeneity, publication-bias and risk-of-bias results all unrecoverable, is the implausible-magnitude signature this vault has flagged four times and been right each time — twice on collagen, once on ashwagandha, and on every SUCRA leaderboard it has declined. Compare what better-evidenced levers buy: resistance-training lowers systolic blood pressure −6.11 mmHg across 51 trials; sauna-heat is null on most cardiometabolic markers. ⚑ And the paper's dose recommendation is refused. Of four moderator contrasts, exactly one cleared significance — ≥3 repetitions per stretch (−1.46, p < 0.001) — and it became "we recommend stretching each major muscle-tendon group three or more times," while frequency (p = 0.149), session duration (p = 0.169) and supervision (p = 0.130) failed, uncorrected, on six studies (stretching-dose-recommendation-rests-on-one-of-four-moderator-contrasts). Six studies do not settle what 79 randomised trials could not — see (1a). Recorded, not adopted. Credit where due: all four contrasts were reported with their p-values, so this is a conclusion problem rather than a p-hacking problem.

Why this map was coined despite thin evidence — and why it was NOT coined three days ago. Stretching first appeared in the vault as a statistical artifact: it topped the SUCRA leaderboard for cognition in Jiao 2026 (exercise-type-cognition-ranking-not-supported), which was the tell that the leaderboard was noise, not a finding about stretching. The vault explicitly refused to coin a stretching map from that artifact and tasked the next sweep with finding a source about stretching as stretching. This is that source. Coining now honours that discipline: the vault waited for real evidence, and here it is — modest, honest, and finally about the thing itself.

For David. GOAL.md gates his return to pickleball on consistent stretching. This map does not change that gate; it gives it an evidence base — a real (if modest) range-of-motion benefit that is exactly what matters when returning a stiff, previously-injured knee to sport — and an explicit note that stretching's injury-prevention reputation is thinner than commonly believed. Free, knee-safe, goal-named, nothing to buy. A low-burden mobility behaviour, not a purchase and not a claim to oversell.

Appraised mixed (strong prospective design, objective exposure — held down by preprint status, very-low certainty, trivial effects); capped suggestive. Full text unretrievable (Research Square bot-wall); pdf_unavailable logged. extends; Status unchanged.

  • 2026-07-24 — The map's founding claim gets nine times its evidence, and the paper's advertised contribution — that it works for everyone — is refused; Status held emerging. Ingested Oba 2026 (Sports Medicine - Open 12(1):95, systematic review + meta-analysis of 79 RCTs, n = 3,287, healthy individuals, lower limb, RCTs published before June 2025 vs non-stretching/passive controls; PEDro 6.15 ± 0.90; JSPS KAKENHI public funding, no competing interests; no PROSPERO registration stated, no GRADE; gold OA CC BY, PDF archived 1.77 MB). Chronic static stretching → lower-limb flexibility Hedges' g = 0.851 (0.710–0.991), Z = 11.845, p < 0.001, τ² = 0.249, I² = 66.1% (chronic-static-stretching-improves-lower-limb-flexibility) — confirmation that changes nothing except confidence. Three refusals, all from the paper's own tables. (1) The five moderator nulls sold as generalizability rest on 3 middle-aged trials, 4 older-adult trials, 3 sedentary trials and 6 knee-extensor trials against 59 young-adult trials; the age null (p = 0.853) is shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3, and accepting it would have closed this map's own population gap (stretching-age-generalisation-rests-on-three-middle-aged-trials). (2) The muscle-group cell most relevant to David — knee extensors, g = 0.550 on six healthy-knee trials against knee flexors' g = 0.995 on 47 — is a near-twofold spread declared non-significant at p = 0.100 (stretching-quadriceps-evidence-thinnest-and-weakest-of-the-lower-limb). (3) After 79 randomized trials the dose is still unspecified — volume, intensity and frequency inconsistently reported and explicitly not moderated (stretching-dose-remains-unspecified-across-79-trials); per shared/safety.md none is invented here. Appraised mixed, capped suggestive — fatal flag #3 weighed and deliberately not fired on a narrow ground: it targets the secondary moderator analyses, while the primary estimate is a large, precise, well-powered positive rather than a null being sold as absence. So the vault keeps the effect and refuses the generalization, rather than discarding both. Held short of reliable by unresolved Egger p < 0.001 (trim-and-fill imputing nothing is weak reassurance at I² = 66%), an unblindable near-tautological outcome, heterogeneous pooled instruments, English-only search, and the missing registration/GRADE. Status emerging HELD — a large confirmation of a proximate surrogate does not consolidate an intervention with no hard-endpoint case (and flexibility-weak-predictor-of-health-outcomes-adults says there isn't one to find). For David: keep the behaviour, lower the certainty about his own bracket — three trials in middle-aged adults, six in the compartment attached to his patella.

  • 2026-07-27 — The map gets a cardiovascular mechanism and immediately discounts it; the map's dose refusal survives a fourth paper; Status held emerging. Ingested Logan 2026 (Sports Medicine, online ahead of print, systematic review + meta-analysis, 12 studies — six acute, six longitudinal, adults 18+, PubMed / Scopus / Embase / Web of Science searched 1974–2025, 242 records screened; randomised, non-randomised controlled AND crossover designs all eligible; 3-level model with REML; no PROSPERO, no GRADE, data/code "on reasonable request"; senior author carries a private-entity affiliation alongside a no-conflicts declaration; appraised mixed; CC-BY OA PDF archived 2.25 MB via manual import after the Europe PMC rung mis-reported it as paywalled). Status held — shared/evidence-model.md puts mechanistic below outcome, and flexibility-weak-predictor-of-health-outcomes-adults says there is no hard-endpoint case to find; a surrogate from twelve studies cannot promote a map held at emerging precisely for lacking one. For David: nothing changes. He already stretches for a better-evidenced reason (the goal file, plus a 79-trial range-of-motion result), and this must not become "stretching is good for your heart."

  • 2026-07-29 — Two days after this map took its first cardiovascular thread, a second independent synthesis lands on the same territory — and comparing them DEFLATES both. Status held emerging; nothing added to the map's case (global-ripple from exercise-modalities-vascular-function-nma-wen-2026 — Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, network meta-analysis of 47 RCTs, n = 1,995 adults 60+, PROSPERO CRD420251236872, CINeMA, publicly funded, conflict-free; home map zone-2-cardio). In that network, stretching ranked FIRST for systolic blood pressure at SUCRA 91.8%, MD −10.43 mmHg (−18.88 to −1.99) — and was non-significant on brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity and non-significant on carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (stretching-blood-pressure-effect-is-larger-than-the-better-evidenced-levers). ⚑ On the surface that corroborates Logan 2026. Look at what it corroborates and it is the opposite. Logan found stretching moving pulse wave velocity by SMD −1.02 (longitudinal-stretching-lowers-arterial-stiffness); Wen finds stretching doing nothing to pulse wave velocity by either measure while producing a large blood-pressure effect Logan's network did not feature. Each paper's large stretching effect lands on the outcome the other finds nothing on. That is not replication — it is two draws from a small, noisy literature, and it is the strongest available argument for the discount this map applied on 2026-07-27 rather than against it. ⚠ And the magnitude fails the same plausibility check, harder. resistance-training lowers systolic pressure by 6.11 mmHg across 51 randomised trials and 2,025 people (resistance-training-lowers-blood-pressure-older-adults) — less, on twenty times the evidence, from an intervention with an obvious mechanism. Wen's own definition of the stretching node is "no application of load… no sustained cardiopulmonary metabolic demand," and the number of stretching trials contributing is never published. This is the implausible-magnitude signature for the fifth time, and it is the second time it has fired on this map in three days. Graded single-study, deliberately below its source's suggestive cap; recorded, not adopted. ⚠ The downstream error is the contagious one, and it is why this bullet exists. Wen's abstract converts "systolic decreases exceeding 10 mmHg" into "an approximate 20% down-risk in major adverse cardiovascular events" using a conversion imported from separate blood-pressure epidemiology — no participant in those 47 trials had a cardiovascular event counted (surrogate-to-outcome-conversion-borrowed-from-a-different-literature). The single sentence this map must never generate is "stretching is good for your heart." It earns its slot from GOAL.md's pickleball gate and a 79-randomised-trial range-of-motion result, and from nothing cardiovascular. For David: nothing changes.
