Rapamycin (sirolimus) — metabolic / pharmacologic

Status: emerging (2026-07-07) — coined this run from a human-greenlit sweep of the longevity trio. Prescription immunosuppressant → by rule it is never ranked in the daily protocol and no dose is set here (shared/safety.md). The vault's second clinician-only pharmacologic lever, alongside glp-1.

What we believe now

Rapamycin has, for the first time, a randomized human longevity trial — and it missed its primary endpoint. What the trial actually establishes is short-term safety, not benefit. PEARL (n=114, ages 50–85, 48 weeks, weekly compounded 5 or 10 mg vs placebo) found no change in visceral fat (the primary outcome, p=0.94). Its defensible result is tolerability: adverse-event rates were comparable to placebo over a year, with no immune/infection/lipid signal — intermittent low-dose weekly dosing appears to avoid daily-immunosuppression toxicity.

Three things cap the map hard:

  • (1) The longevity benefit is unproven. Secondary signals (lean-mass and pain improvement in women on 10 mg; general-health/well-being on 5 mg) are modest, sex-specific, and partly matched by placebo.
  • (2) Severe conflict of interest. All authors but one are employees/shareholders of AgelessRx, the company that ran the trial and sells rapamycin — read efficacy claims through that lens.
  • (3) A bioavailability confound. The compounded drug was discovered mid-trial to have ~⅓ the bioavailability of commercial rapamycin, muddying every dose-response read.

Regimen implication. Clinician-only; lives in the regimen's "discuss with your clinician" section with the evidence summarized, never scored against Benefit/Burden. The honest one-liner: at these intermittent low doses, one year looks safe; the anti-aging payoff remains unproven in humans.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Moel 2025 / PEARL (Aging (Albany NY), RCT, n=114, 48 wk): primary endpoint (visceral fat) null; modest sex-specific secondary gains (women, lean mass + pain); well tolerated at 5–10 mg/week with no clear safety signal. Coins the map at emerging — first human longevity RCT, primary null, industry-conflicted, prescription.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠ Interaction with the muscle levers — mTOR antagonism. Rapamycin inhibits mTOR; resistance-training and protein-target work by activating mTOR to drive muscle protein synthesis. Chronic inhibition could in principle blunt training/protein adaptations. PEARL's paradoxical lean-mass gain in women on intermittent low-dose suggests the weekly pulse may spare this — but the interaction is a real clinician-conversation item, flagged in both directions (safety_flags: [interaction]). Magnitude is an open gap — flagged, not fabricated (no vault-quality source quantifies it).
  • Coherence with glp-1. Both are clinician-only pharmacologic longevity/metabolic levers held to the same "never ranked, evidence summarized for the clinician conversation" discipline. Not an interaction.
  • Sibling to metformin in this run. Both are repurposed prescription drugs with hyped longevity narratives whose best recent human trials underdeliver — rapamycin's primary null, metformin's functional null-with-harm. A coherence pattern for the pharmacologic-longevity space.
  • Open gap: rapamycin is an immunosuppressant — real pharmacology (infection risk, wound healing, vaccine timing, glucose/lipid effects at higher/daily doses) that a healthspan protocol must weigh; no vault-quality source ingested yet, logged rather than fabricated into a graded flag. Larger/longer RCTs on hard endpoints, and a full-bioavailability formulation, are what would move this off emerging. </content>
