Rapamycin (sirolimus) — metabolic / pharmacologic

Status: emerging (2026-07-07) — coined this run from a human-greenlit sweep of the longevity trio. Prescription immunosuppressant → by rule it is never ranked in the daily protocol and no dose is set here (shared/safety.md). The vault's second clinician-only pharmacologic lever, alongside glp-1.

What we believe now

Rapamycin has, for the first time, a randomized human longevity trial — and it missed its primary endpoint. What the trial actually establishes is short-term safety, not benefit. PEARL (n=114, ages 50–85, 48 weeks, weekly compounded 5 or 10 mg vs placebo) found no change in visceral fat (the primary outcome, p=0.94). Its defensible result is tolerability: adverse-event rates were comparable to placebo over a year, with no immune/infection/lipid signal — intermittent low-dose weekly dosing appears to avoid daily-immunosuppression toxicity.

Three things cap the map hard:

  • (1) The longevity benefit is unproven. Secondary signals (lean-mass and pain improvement in women on 10 mg; general-health/well-being on 5 mg) are modest, sex-specific, and partly matched by placebo.
  • (2) Severe conflict of interest. All authors but one are employees/shareholders of AgelessRx, the company that ran the trial and sells rapamycin — read efficacy claims through that lens.
  • (3) A bioavailability confound. The compounded drug was discovered mid-trial to have ~⅓ the bioavailability of commercial rapamycin, muddying every dose-response read.

Regimen implication. Clinician-only; lives in the regimen's "discuss with your clinician" section with the evidence summarized, never scored against Benefit/Burden. The honest one-liner: at these intermittent low doses, one year looks safe; the anti-aging payoff remains unproven in humans.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Moel 2025 / PEARL (Aging (Albany NY), RCT, n=114, 48 wk): primary endpoint (visceral fat) null; modest sex-specific secondary gains (women, lean mass + pain); well tolerated at 5–10 mg/week with no clear safety signal. Coins the map at emerging — first human longevity RCT, primary null, industry-conflicted, prescription.

  • 2026-09-13 — The preclinical literature weighs in for the first time, and it points the direction this map's one human trial left open. Ingested Ivimey-Cook 2025 (rapamycin-metformin-dr-lifespan-meta-ivimeycook-2025, Aging Cell, meta-analysis, 911 effect sizes from 167 papers across 8 vertebrate species, appraised mixed — significant publication bias and heterogeneity self-reported by the authors). Rapamycin produced a lifespan extension comparable in magnitude to dietary restriction; metformin did not (rapamycin-matches-dietary-restriction-lifespan-extension-in-vertebrate-meta-analysis, extends, suggestive; cross-filed on metformin as metformin-shows-no-significant-lifespan-extension-in-vertebrate-meta-analysis). Status emerging HELD — this is animal-model evidence, below the tier of PEARL's human RCT, so highest-tier-wins means it cannot upgrade the map on its own. What it adds is directional corroboration from a different evidentiary base at exactly the point PEARL's primary endpoint came back null. No new safety_flags; prescription — clinician-only, never ranked, no dose set here.

  • 2026-09-22 — ⚑ THE MAGNITUDE GAP FLAGGED SINCE 2026-07-07 IS CLOSED — the first human trial pairing rapamycin with exercise, and it points against the "cycling hypothesis" it set out to test. Ingested Stanfield 2026 / RAPA-EX-01 (rapamycin-exercise-interaction-rct-stanfield-2026, Journal of Cachexia, Sarcopenia and Muscle, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, n=40 adults 65-85, 13 weeks, weekly sirolimus 6mg vs placebo, both arms doing an identical home resistance+endurance programme; appraised reliable). The primary ITT analysis on chair-stand repetitions ran against sirolimus but fell short of significance (-2.13 reps, 95% CI -4.61 to 0.34, p=.089); its two pre-specified sensitivity analyses both reached significance in the same direction — complete-case -2.46 (p=.045), per-protocol -3.44 (p=.007) (rapamycin-may-blunt-functional-gains-from-exercise-in-older-adults, extends, suggestive). Grip strength and 6-minute walk distance moved the same direction, non-significantly. Adverse-event burden was higher on sirolimus (99 events vs 63, equal proportion of participants affected; one possibly-related serious adverse event, pneumonia) (rapamycin-increases-adverse-event-burden-during-a-supervised-exercise-program, extends, suggestive). This is the first quantified human evidence for the mTOR/AMPK interaction this map has carried as an unquantified mechanistic flag since coining. Filed suggestive not strong despite the source's reliable verdict: the primary ITT endpoint itself was non-significant and n=40 is small — the authors call the trial "exploratory" and explicitly do not oversell it. Status emerging HELD — one small RCT on a different question (exercise co-administration) than PEARL's (dose/safety alone) does not itself move the map, per highest-tier-wins; it adds a quantified interaction, which is a different axis from Status. safety_flags: [prescription, interaction] unchanged in kind, now evidenced rather than only mechanistic. Cross-noted, with matching detail, on resistance-training.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-09-22 — titles-scan + global ripple for RAPA-EX-01: the standing interaction gap is now quantified, and no new interaction was found beyond it. Scanned the full vault/concepts/ index and walked every other map. The mTOR/AMPK interaction with resistance-training and protein-target, flagged mechanistically since 2026-07-07, now has its first human RCT evidence: weekly sirolimus did not enhance, and in pre-specified sensitivity analyses may have modestly attenuated, functional gains from a home exercise programme in older adults, with a higher adverse-event burden. Magnitude is no longer purely mechanistic — it is small, exploratory-trial-sized, and directionally consistent across four functional measures. No other drug/supplement co-administration arm existed in this trial (placebo-controlled, single-agent), so no further interaction is claimed. safety_flags: [interaction] stands, now evidenced.
  • ⚠ Interaction with the muscle levers — mTOR antagonism (evidenced 2026-09-22, mechanistic since 2026-07-07). Rapamycin inhibits mTOR; resistance-training and protein-target work by activating mTOR to drive muscle protein synthesis. RAPA-EX-01 is the first trial to test this directly with a functional (chair-stand, grip, walk) rather than biomarker outcome — see the through-line entry above. PEARL's paradoxical lean-mass gain in women on intermittent low-dose (a different question — no concurrent structured exercise programme) is not contradicted by this: different populations, different co-interventions. Flagged in both directions (safety_flags: [interaction]).
  • Coherence with glp-1. Both are clinician-only pharmacologic longevity/metabolic levers held to the same "never ranked, evidence summarized for the clinician conversation" discipline. Not an interaction.
  • Sibling to metformin in this run. Both are repurposed prescription drugs with hyped longevity narratives whose best recent human trials underdeliver — rapamycin's primary null, metformin's functional null-with-harm. A coherence pattern for the pharmacologic-longevity space.
  • Open gap: rapamycin is an immunosuppressant — real pharmacology (infection risk, wound healing, vaccine timing, glucose/lipid effects at higher/daily doses) that a healthspan protocol must weigh; no vault-quality source ingested yet, logged rather than fabricated into a graded flag. Larger/longer RCTs on hard endpoints, and a full-bioavailability formulation, are what would move this off emerging. </content>
