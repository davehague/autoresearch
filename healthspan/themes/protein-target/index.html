Protein target (~1.6 g/kg/day) — nutrition

Status: consolidating (2026-07-07) — high-tier meta-analytic evidence with a clear, dose-honest target on the daily-total lever. A newly ingested timing/distribution sub-thread lands largely null, which reinforces rather than complicates the message: total daily intake — not meal timing or per-meal size — is what moves muscle. A nutrition foundation lever.

Sub-threads: none — one argument (daily total protein ~1.6 g/kg/day drives the training-adaptation benefit); timing/distribution is a repeatedly-closed null refinement of that same claim, not a separable object; the candidate-supplement refusals (probiotics, collagen, synbiotics) are notes about other, uncoined interventions; and the lipid family opened 2026-08-25 rests on one source, so it is a second question this map answers rather than a second strand it is made of. What we believe now

For adults doing resistance training, protein added to training gives small but real gains in strength and lean mass. The evidence now separates into two threads:

(0) New 2026-08-25 — a second outcome family arrives, and it is a reassurance rather than a reason. Every source on this map until tonight treated protein as an input to muscle. Khosravi 2026 (Nutrition Reviews, 32 RCTs, 39 arms, n=1,357, adults with overweight or obesity) is the first to treat a protein supplement as an input to blood lipids: LDL cholesterol −3.45 mg/dL (95% CI −5.77 to −1.13, P=.004) and total cholesterol −5.70 mg/dL (−10.20 to −1.20, P=.013), with triglycerides and HDL unmoved (protein-supplementation-lowers-ldl-and-total-cholesterol-by-a-few-milligrams, protein-supplementation-did-not-move-triglycerides-or-hdl). Read the magnitude before the p value: about a 3% LDL reduction, against a drug class on statins-primary-prevention that moves the same number by 30–50%. And the authors report that both estimates attenuated when high-risk-of-bias trials were excluded, without publishing the attenuated values (the-protein-lipid-effect-attenuates-when-high-risk-of-bias-trials-are-removed). The practical content: hitting a protein target does not cost you anything on the lipid panel. It is not a reason to supplement protein for cholesterol. Status unmoved — a new outcome family at suggestive strength does not disturb a consolidating verdict built on a different endpoint.

(1) Daily total — the lever. The benefit plateaus around ~1.6 g/kg/day of total protein (Morton 2018 meta-regression). It's a target, not "more is better": past ~1.6 g/kg/day there is no further muscle payoff. Effect is additive to training and shrinks with age.

(2) Timing & per-meal distribution — a refinement, largely null. The popular "spread protein across ≥4 meals" advice traces to a proposed ~0.4 g/kg per-meal MPS ceiling (Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018, derived as ~0.25 g/kg + 2 SD) — but that is a mechanistic/opinion claim whose own authors called the practical threshold "speculative and questionable." Two newer studies deflate it. A quadruple-tracer RCT showed a 100 g single bolus is used, not wasted — a bigger and >12 h-prolonged myofibrillar response vs 25 g, with negligible oxidation (Trommelen 2023) — refuting a hard per-meal ceiling at the mechanistic level. And the top-tier timing meta-analysis (Casuso 2025, 5 RCTs) found protein timing/distribution does not meaningfully change lean mass (SMD −0.08) or chest-press strength. Net: hit your daily total; when and in how many servings you take it is near-noise for a healthy adult. One unreplicated hint — pre-training protein edged out post- for leg-press 1-RM (SMD 0.70, just 2 studies) — is a watch-item, not a rule. Honesty cap: the bolus evidence is an acute surrogate (MPS), single-study, young men — it loosens the "concentrated protein is wasted" worry without proving concentrating is better than spreading the same daily total.

Bias watch: the daily-target anchor (Morton) carries dairy-industry funding; the bolus RCT (Trommelen) has a dairy-manufacturer co-author; Schoenfeld sits on a protein-brand advisory board. All three directions are consistent with the wider literature — note the funding, don't launder it into extra certainty on the magnitudes.
