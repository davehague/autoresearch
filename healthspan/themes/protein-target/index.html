Protein target (~1.6 g/kg/day) — nutrition

Status: consolidating (2026-07-07) — high-tier meta-analytic evidence with a clear, dose-honest target on the daily-total lever. A newly ingested timing/distribution sub-thread lands largely null, which reinforces rather than complicates the message: total daily intake — not meal timing or per-meal size — is what moves muscle. A nutrition foundation lever.

What we believe now

For adults doing resistance training, protein added to training gives small but real gains in strength and lean mass. The evidence now separates into two threads:

(1) Daily total — the lever. The benefit plateaus around ~1.6 g/kg/day of total protein (Morton 2018 meta-regression). It's a target, not "more is better": past ~1.6 g/kg/day there is no further muscle payoff. Effect is additive to training and shrinks with age.

(2) Timing & per-meal distribution — a refinement, largely null. The popular "spread protein across ≥4 meals" advice traces to a proposed ~0.4 g/kg per-meal MPS ceiling (Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018, derived as ~0.25 g/kg + 2 SD) — but that is a mechanistic/opinion claim whose own authors called the practical threshold "speculative and questionable." Two newer studies deflate it. A quadruple-tracer RCT showed a 100 g single bolus is used, not wasted — a bigger and >12 h-prolonged myofibrillar response vs 25 g, with negligible oxidation (Trommelen 2023) — refuting a hard per-meal ceiling at the mechanistic level. And the top-tier timing meta-analysis (Casuso 2025, 5 RCTs) found protein timing/distribution does not meaningfully change lean mass (SMD −0.08) or chest-press strength. Net: hit your daily total; when and in how many servings you take it is near-noise for a healthy adult. One unreplicated hint — pre-training protein edged out post- for leg-press 1-RM (SMD 0.70, just 2 studies) — is a watch-item, not a rule. Honesty cap: the bolus evidence is an acute surrogate (MPS), single-study, young men — it loosens the "concentrated protein is wasted" worry without proving concentrating is better than spreading the same daily total.

Bias watch: the daily-target anchor (Morton) carries dairy-industry funding; the bolus RCT (Trommelen) has a dairy-manufacturer co-author; Schoenfeld sits on a protein-brand advisory board. All three directions are consistent with the wider literature — note the funding, don't launder it into extra certainty on the magnitudes.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Morton 2018 (meta-analysis + meta-regression, 49 RCTs): protein + resistance training → +2.49 kg 1-RM, +0.30 kg FFM; no further FFM gain above ~1.62 g/kg/day. Sets Status → consolidating. Funding conflict noted.

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018 (narrative review): proposes a ~0.4 g/kg/meal MPS ceiling (0.25 g/kg + 2 SD) → the "distribute across ≥4 meals" heuristic; the authors themselves call the practical threshold "speculative and questionable." Opens the timing/distribution sub-thread at mechanistic/opinion tier. → protein-per-meal-ceiling-04g-kg

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Trommelen 2023 (parallel-group RCT, n=36, quadruple stable-isotope tracer): a 100 g post-exercise bolus beat 25 g for myofibrillar synthesis, response prolonged >12 h, oxidation <15% — no hard per-meal ceiling at the mechanistic level. Surrogate endpoint (acute MPS); pushes back on the 0.4 g/kg ceiling but not the daily-total belief. → protein-bolus-no-anabolic-ceiling

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Casuso 2025 (systematic review + meta-analysis, 5 RCTs, n=193): protein timing null for lean mass (SMD −0.08) and chest-press 1-RM; a lone unreplicated pre-training leg-press signal (SMD 0.70, p=0.048, 2 studies). Highest-tier anchor for "timing/distribution is a refinement, not the lever." Low-to-very-low GRADE certainty. → protein-timing-distribution-null-on-lean-mass

  • 2026-07-11 — Cross-linked Haas 2026 (fat-free-mass-mortality-meta-haas-2026, 49 observational cohorts, n=1,149,807, 83,798 deaths): low vs high fat-free mass → all-cause mortality RR 1.42 (1.30–1.55), RR 1.31 (1.20–1.44) after the authors' own trim-and-fill correction for self-detected publication bias. Corroborating context for why the tissue matters — not evidence for the protein lever itself, and explicitly not a reason to exceed the target. Observational marker association; reverse causation (illness → wasting) unaddressed; effect shrinks as the measurement instrument improves (calf circumference 2.19 > DXA 1.52 > BIA 1.23), which is what a frailty marker looks like. Status unchanged (consolidating) — this does not touch the map's own RCT-tier evidence. Concepts filed on resistance-training.

  • 2026-07-13 — ⚑ Probiotics flagged as a candidate and NOT adopted — the bundled upsell that turns out to be selling you protein; Status unchanged (Chang 2026, Frontiers in Nutrition, Bayesian multilevel meta-analysis, 20 RCTs, n=679, PROSPERO CRD420251165192, PRISMA, no funding, no conflicts). Headline: protein-plus-probiotic improves physical performance, g = 0.45 (0.21–0.67); body composition null (g = 0.02). The headline pools two comparisons that answer different questions — vs placebo (which measures the protein) and vs protein alone (which measures the probiotic) — so it answers neither (protein-probiotic-headline-confounded-by-comparator-pooling). The authors built the model that separates them — a Control model, explicitly "so that the added value question could be examined more directly" — and it kills the claim: nine of ten contrasts cross zero. Versus protein alone: cardiorespiratory endurance 0.06, functional performance 0.37, muscle strength 0.26 (−0.18 to 0.66), power 0.25 — all null; only muscular endurance survives (0.70, 0.17–1.20), one hit among ~10 uncorrected comparisons on a between-study τ = 0.67, larger than the effect itself (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance). And the tell that breaks the paper: every placebo-controlled contrast is ALSO null — including muscle strength (0.19, −0.19 to 0.55). This corpus cannot reproduce protein's own effect, which this map holds as consolidating from 49 RCTs (protein-supplementation-augments-resistance-training). A literature too underpowered to detect the large, established effect it is mostly measuring has no business claiming a smaller, novel one stacked on top of it. ⚑ No probiotics map coined (§6.5: candidates are flagged, never auto-adopted) — the second such refusal in three days, after beta-alanine (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy), and the vault can say more here: beta-alanine's combination arm was estimable in 1 of 6 domains (no evidence of synergy), whereas Chang's added-value model is estimable in all five and is null in four — a genuine, if imprecise, absence. Still not a debunk (wide HDIs, 6 of 20 trials high risk of bias). Nothing to add, nothing to buy; the stack stays at one supplement.

  • 2026-07-15 — ⚑ Collagen flagged as a candidate and NOT adopted — a SUCRA leaderboard beaten by its own mechanism; Status unchanged. Ingested Drummond 2026 (Translational Sports Medicine, network meta-analysis, 78 RCTs, n=4,755 healthy adults resistance-training, 13 supplement types, PROSPERO CRD42024510914, CINeMA; no funding, no conflicts). The headline ranks collagen #1 — fat-free mass SMD 0.94, SUCRA 98.9% (~3.2 kg implied), strength SMD 0.41, SUCRA 88.1% — over whey. The ranking is a network artifact: exactly one trial directly compared collagen with whey, the rest is indirect inference CINeMA downgrades, and a ~3.2 kg FFM gain is biologically implausible for a low-BCAA, low-biological-value protein that lacks the leucine to trigger muscle protein synthesis — a fact the authors concede (collagen-ranking-rests-on-indirect-evidence). Same tell as Jiao's stretching win: a SUCRA chart topped by the mechanistically-least-likely option is noise, not a finding. The trustworthy read of the same network confirms this map: whey beats placebo small (FFM SMD 0.16), no protein source reliably beats another on direct evidence (protein-supplement-type-effects-small-whey-baseline) — hit the daily total; the specific supplement is near-noise. ⚑ No collagen map coined (§6.5) — the third candidate-not-adopted refusal in five days after beta-alanine and probiotics. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md; collagen is unproven for muscle, not disproven, and mechanistically unlikely.

  • 2026-07-16 — ⚑ Collagen flagged AGAIN — now on a bone line, and it is the vault's first positive-looking bone result, distrusted; Status unchanged. Ingested Sun 2025 (Frontiers in Nutrition, meta-analysis, 20 studies (17 RCTs + 1 observational + 2 clinical), predominantly postmenopausal women with osteopenia/osteoporosis; publicly funded — Chinese NSF — no conflicts). Collagen → BMD gains (spine SMD 0.58–2.00, femoral neck 0.46–2.67, I²=80.1%), bone-turnover markers (SMD 0.40–0.58, I²=0%), muscle strength (SMD 0.60), stronger when combined with Ca + vitamin D (collagen-bone-signal-heterogeneous-postmenopausal). The vault does not bank it — a BMD SMD above 2 at I²=80% is the fingerprint of small-study effects, not a true density change (the same magnitude-tell as Drummond's SUCRA and Jiao's stretching leaderboard); the design is mixed, the population is postmenopausal women with bone disease, and the strongest subgroup is confounded by co-supplements. Appraised mixed; graded single-study/open-question. ⚑ No collagen map coined — the fourth candidate refusal in a week (a flagged close call: collagen now has two evidence lines, muscle + bone). The vault's "no supplement has earned a purchase for David's bone" pattern holds (wrong population, implausible magnitude), now carried with the honest tension that a positive-looking bone result exists and is distrusted. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

  • 2026-07-19 — ⚑ A fifth SUCRA crown, declined — and this time the paper's own GRADE table does the declining; Status held consolidating (Wang 2026, Nutrients 18(6):909, systematic review + network meta-analysis, 35 RCTs, n = 1,211 trained athletes; six databases, RoB 2.0 + NMA-adapted GRADE, network consistency tested; no external funding, no competing interests; not PROSPERO-registered; OA PDF archived). Protein ranked first for endurance performance: SMD 0.28 (0.08–0.48), SUCRA 85.2%, alongside creatine for strength (0.46) and omega-3 for recovery (0.40) — an "outcome-specific efficacy pattern" in which each supplement wins exactly one outcome and none places second. That tidiness is the tell. Thirty-five trials across three interventions × three outcome families is roughly four trials per cell. The pattern is what you get when creatine trials mostly measure strength, protein trials mostly measure endurance, and omega-3 trials mostly measure soreness — an evidence-availability map presented as an efficacy map (supplement-outcome-specific-ranking-too-tidy-for-a-sparse-network). ⚑ And the vault did not have to infer the discount. The paper's own GRADE table rates certainty moderate for each supplement versus placebo and LOW for the indirect active-vs-active comparisons — "primarily due to imprecision." The leaderboard is built on the low-certainty half; the CI-backed vs-placebo contrasts are the moderate-certainty half. That is precisely this map's standing rule — trust the contrast against control, distrust the ordering, learned from Jiao's stretching crown, Drummond's collagen SUCRA 98.9%, Sun's collagen BMD and Zhou's insomnia network — restated in the source's own summary-of-findings table, and then ignored by its own conclusion. The strongest external validation the rule has received. Nothing changes here. The map's claim is a daily total (~1.6 g/kg/day) and this paper says nothing about totals; its population is trained athletes and its endpoints are athletic surrogates. "Targeted supplementation aligned with training objectives" is a purchasing framework, not a health finding. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive.

  • 2026-07-20 — A boundary marked, not a claim overturned: in sarcopenic obesity, no nutritional strategy beat usual care on anything. Ingested Yu 2026 (Frontiers in Public Health 14:1775783, network meta-analysis, PROSPERO CRD420251270452, 24 RCTs, n=1,298 older adults with sarcopenic obesity, follow-up 3–9 months; RoB 2 + CINeMA). Across nine strategies and five outcomes — BMI, handgrip strength, fat mass, percentage body fat, skeletal muscle index — neither supplementation nor high-protein intake reached significance against usual care on any of them, including in arms where nutrition was added to training. The one favourably-ranked nutrition node (MC + supplementation for SMI, SUCRA 74.1%) was not significant (MD 0.41) on the sparsest evidence in the network (5 of 24 trials) (nutritional-supplementation-alone-beats-nothing-in-sarcopenic-obesity). Nothing changes here, and the reason is specific. This map's claim is a daily total (~1.6 g/kg/day) supporting resistance-training adaptations in adults who are training — a different proposition from "supplementation is itself a lever in a clinical muscle-loss population," which is what Yu tested and what came back empty. What the source adds is a boundary, alongside the timing/distribution null already here: total intake in trainees, yes; supplementation as a standalone clinical intervention in sarcopenic obesity over 3–9 months, not demonstrated. An under-powered null, not a clean one — the paper names age-related anabolic resistance and too-short intervention periods as its own explanation. Per shared/evidence-model.md a suggestive-capped result does not force a Status move; consolidating holds. Appraised mixed.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Why the tissue matters — corroborating context, and its honest limits (new 2026-07-11). A 49-cohort meta-analysis (n=1,149,807, 83,798 deaths) finds low vs high fat-free mass associated with all-cause mortality RR 1.42 (1.30–1.55), and RR 1.31 after the authors' own publication-bias correction (fat-free-mass-mortality-meta-haas-2026, fat-free-mass-mortality-association-observational). That is the tissue this target exists to build — but it is not a test of protein, or of diet, or of anything you can do. It is a body-composition marker association in untreated adults, with reverse causation unaddressed (illness destroys lean mass before it kills) and an instrument gradient that runs the wrong way for a clean tissue effect (the crudest measure shows the biggest effect). It adds no support to the ~1.6 g/kg/day target's own RCT evidence, and the map's consolidating Status is untouched by it. Recorded because the vault should know why lean mass is worth defending — not as a reason to eat more protein than the target, which the map's own evidence says plateaus. Concepts live on resistance-training.
  • ⚠ The glp-1 countermeasure now scales with the drug (updated 2026-07-10). A BMJ network meta-analysis of 262 RCTs finds tirzepatide removes the most fat (−25.7%) and the most lean mass (−8.3%) of 19 agents (tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss) — extending the vault's pooled figures (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude, −1.74 kg; semaglutide −5.44 kg) into a gradient: the harder a drug pulls weight off, the more of that weight is muscle. Hitting the ~1.6 g/kg/day target matters more, not less, on a more potent agent — and appetite suppression makes hitting it harder. A clinician-conversation item (GLP-1 is a prescription, never ranked); flagged on both maps. The pooled endpoint remains lean mass, never muscle function — a standing gap, not a number to invent.
  • Stack synergy with creatine-monohydrate — same resistance-training adaptation; treat as one muscle-building stack. Note the parallel: creatine's benefit plateaus (~5 g/day) and protein's daily benefit plateaus (~1.6 g/kg/day) — both "hit the target, then stop."
  • Supports zone-2-cardio and general training adaptation (protein availability aids recovery), a minor complementary edge.
  • Additive to resistance-training — the base lever. The protein benefit is additive to a resistance-training stimulus and shrinks without it; resistance-training now carries its own direct mortality data (resistance-training-lowers-mortality), so it is the foundation and protein is its amplifier. ⚠ Timing tension with intermittent-fasting — now eased by the timing/distribution sub-thread. A compressed eating window can crowd out the ~1.6 g/kg/day target, but the new evidence shows the fix is simply hitting the daily total inside the window, not spreading it across many meals: a large single bolus is well-used (Trommelen 2023) and timing/distribution is null for lean mass (Casuso 2025). So IF is compatible with the protein target as a total-intake problem, not a meal-spacing one — manageable, not a safety flag.
  • ⚠ Muscle-preservation countermeasure for glp-1 — the cost is now a number (updated 2026-07-09). A meta-analysis of 7 placebo-controlled RCTs (n=821) puts GLP-1 lean-mass loss at −1.74 kg, and −5.44 kg for semaglutide (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude). GLP-1 appetite suppression can simultaneously crowd out the ~1.6 g/kg/day target — so hitting protein (with resistance-training + creatine-monohydrate) is the evidence-based way to preserve muscle during GLP-1 treatment, a recommendation the meta-analysis's own authors make explicitly. Flagged in both maps. The body-composition gap is closed; what remains open is muscle function — no pooled trial measured strength or physical function during treatment, so the functional cost of that lean-mass loss is still unmeasured (not invented).
  • ⚠ mTOR/AMPK interaction with the pharmacologic-longevity drugs (added 2026-07-07). The protein anabolic response runs through mTOR; rapamycin (mTOR inhibition) and metformin (AMPK activation) act on that pathway and could blunt it. Both prescription/clinician-only; flagged in both directions, magnitude a logged open gap.
  • Global-ripple: no other interaction or contraindication found against the remaining maps.
  • ⚑ Probiotics: flagged as a candidate, NOT adopted — and the link to fermented-foods is the point (new 2026-07-13). Chang 2026's added-value model finds a probiotic adds nothing demonstrable on top of protein (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance) — 9 of 10 contrasts null. Six of its twenty trials delivered the supplement as yogurt, which is the headline food of fermented-foods, where a 50-cohort meta-analysis found fermented milk/yogurt tracking with ~6% lower all-cause mortality and the vault concluded that "fermentation" isn't the active ingredient (fermented-foods-signal-is-food-specific-and-small). Randomised evidence now agrees from the other direction: hand people the live organisms directly, on top of protein, and the performance benefit does not arrive. The probiotic keeps failing to be the thing that matters. Cross-linked on both maps. No map coined; logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
  • ⚑ Collagen: flagged as a candidate, NOT adopted — the third supplement in five days to ride in on a headline that outruns its evidence (new 2026-07-15). Drummond 2026's 78-RCT network crowns collagen #1 for muscle, but the ranking rests on one direct collagen-vs-whey trial plus indirect inference, and a ~3.2 kg FFM gain is implausible for a low-BCAA protein (collagen-ranking-rests-on-indirect-evidence). The pattern is now a trio — beta-alanine, probiotics, collagen — each a novel supplement whose synthesis-level headline the vault refused because the trustworthy (direct) evidence did not support it. And the credible read of Drummond reinforces this map: no protein source reliably beats another, whey's own effect is small (protein-supplement-type-effects-small-whey-baseline). The lever is the daily total, not the brand. No map coined; logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. ⚠ Updated 2026-07-16 — collagen now has a second, bone, evidence line, and it does not change the answer. Sun 2025 (mixed-design meta, postmenopausal women) reports collagen improving BMD — but at implausible magnitude (femoral neck SMD to 2.67, I²=80%), confounded by co-supplemented Ca + vitamin D (collagen-bone-signal-heterogeneous-postmenopausal). It is the vault's first positive-looking bone result and its fourth candidate-refusal; the "no supplement earns David's bone" pattern holds. Collagen has appeared on two axes now (muscle + bone) — a flagged close call on whether to coin a map; the auto run declined, consistent with refusing the muscle line.
  • ⚠ The bundled-arm attribution trap, hit three times in three days. The vault cannot infer a component's effect from a combination arm — not creatine's from creatine+beta-alanine (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy), not a probiotic's from protein+probiotic (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance), and not CBT-I's from exercise+CBT (cbt-sleep-effect-not-isolable-no-cbt-alone-node, cbt-insomnia). Trialists bundle, and bundling destroys attribution. Worth naming as a recurring failure mode of this literature rather than a coincidence.
