Caffeine timing — sleep

Status: consolidating (2026-07-04) — the claim that late-day caffeine degrades that night's sleep is high-tier and replicated (a meta-analysis of 24 RCTs plus a timing RCT). Framed as an intervention, the lever is when you stop caffeine, not whether you drink it.

What we believe now

Caffeine measurably harms subsequent sleep — cutting total sleep time (~45 min), sleep efficiency (~7%), and deep sleep — and the effect persists hours after intake. Evidence-based cutoffs: stop coffee ~8.8 h before bed, and pre-workout (~217 mg caffeine) ~13.2 h before bed; a timing RCT found even a dose 6 hours before bed disrupts sleep. direction: harm refers to the sleep outcome — caffeine's daytime alertness benefit is a separate, untracked outcome, so this is a timing lever, not an "avoid caffeine" claim.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Drake 2013 (crossover RCT, 400 mg): caffeine at 0/3/6 h before bed each disturbed sleep vs placebo. Within-intervention finding.
  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Gardiner 2023 (meta-analysis, 24 RCTs): −45 min total sleep time, −7% efficiency, less deep sleep; cutoffs coffee ≥8.8 h / pre-workout ≥13.2 h before bed. Status → consolidating.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠ Interaction — sleep-regularity. Late caffeine directly undermines the sleep-regularity lever — the strongest foundation in the vault. Flagged on caffeine-evening-cutoff-for-sleep (safety_flags: [interaction]) and noted in both maps. This is exactly the interaction path the seed set exists to prove: an evening stimulant vs. sleep. Resolution is a timing rule, not exclusion; per shared/safety.md the flag gates co-recommendation without entering the benefit score.
  • The l-theanine half of the daytime pairing now has a number (added 2026-07-10). A 31-RCT meta-analysis in Molecular Psychiatry finds a single 200 mg L-theanine dose, 30–60 min before a task, improves choice reaction time (SMD 0.51) (l-theanine-acute-attention-benefit) — and finds its stress/relaxation effect modest and bias-driven (l-theanine-stress-benefit-driven-by-biased-trials). That firmly locates L-theanine in the daytime, alongside caffeine, and away from the bedtime wind-down cluster. The evening caffeine cutoff below is untouched: an alertness co-agent does not make late caffeine safe.
  • Pairing with l-theanine. The popular L-theanine + caffeine combination is a daytime alertness/cognition pairing and does not soften the evening cutoff: caffeine late in the day still degrades sleep even when taken with L-theanine. Noted in both maps; the timing rule is unchanged.
  • ⚠ Candidate interaction — creatine (creatine-monohydrate). Old, thin evidence (Vandenberghe 1996, Hespel 2002) suggests high co-ingested caffeine may blunt creatine's ergogenic benefit, plus mild GI distress when the two are taken together. This is a performance-timing nuance on the creatine side (distinct from caffeine's sleep axis here), not a safety hazard, and rests on pre-2003 non-dose-graded studies — flagged as a candidate, not gated. A dedicated modern source is the open gap. Noted in both maps.
