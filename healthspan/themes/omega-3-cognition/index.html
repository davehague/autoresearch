Omega-3 and cognition — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-08-26) — forked from omega-3 on 2026-08-26 by the nightly auto run, flagged for human review. ⚠ The Status word and the evidential state disagree here, and the disagreement is worth stating in the first paragraph rather than hiding. emerging means early / mechanistic / single group; thin through-line, and this map's through-line is neither early nor thin: three structurally different designs — a pooled meta-analysis of 11 placebo-controlled RCTs, a 24-month target-engagement trial with cerebrospinal-fluid confirmation, and a three-year four-arm prevention trial in 1,680 people — all return a null, and they fail in different ways from one another. The map opens at emerging because it holds three dated bullets and one intervention-outcome pair, not because the evidence is weak. ⚑ The substantive answer is: for cognitively-unimpaired and at-risk older adults, omega-3 supplementation does not improve or preserve cognition, the delivery excuse has been tested and removed, and the vault treats this question as closed. What we believe now

The answer is no, and it is bounded on three sides.

(1) The pooled randomised evidence is null. A 2025 meta-analysis of 11 placebo-controlled RCTs in cognitively-unimpaired older adults found no effect on global cognition: SMD −0.02 (95% CI −0.07 to 0.04) (omega3-no-global-cognition-benefit). A standardised mean difference of −0.02 with an interval that narrow does not merely fail to find a benefit — it excludes any benefit large enough to notice.

(2) The delivery excuse has been tested and it fails. The standing defence of the null was always a transport story: the dose was too low, the participants were already replete, or APOE ε4 carriers cannot get DHA into the brain. Yassine 2026 was built to test precisely that — a registered, NIA-funded, double-blind 24-month RCT giving 2 g/day DHA to 365 non-demented adults aged 55–80 selected for low dietary DHA (<200 mg/day), stratified by ε4, measuring cerebrospinal fluid. Delivery worked decisively and independently of ε4: CSF DHA/AA ratio difference 0.19 (0.16–0.21), p < 0.0001 (dha-reaches-the-brain-regardless-of-apoe4, graded strong — the trial's own powered primary). Two years later there were no differences in cognition and no differences in brain volumes (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit). ⚠ The vault is strict about what that second result is: a secondary endpoint in a trial powered for a biochemical primary, with 38% dropout — an underpowered null, held at suggestive, and not banked as equivalence. What it does is remove the excuse from the well-powered null in (1). The authors' own recommendation: "Future research should prioritise brain DHA metabolism over further supplementation trials."

(3) A three-year prevention trial in the at-risk population agrees, with a blinded contrast. MAPT (Andrieu 2017, Lancet Neurology, n=1,680 randomised / 1,525 modified intention-to-treat, non-demented adults aged 70+ with a memory complaint, an IADL limitation or slow gait, 13 memory centres in France and Monaco) gave 800 mg DHA + 225 mg EPA daily for 36 months against a pre-specified four-test composite. The omega-3 arm differed from placebo by 0.011 standard deviations, 95% CI −0.081 to 0.103, p=0.812 (omega3-no-cognitive-benefit-in-a-three-year-placebo-controlled-prevention-trial, strong). ⚑ A bounded null, not an unachieved one — a narrow interval, a point estimate at zero, three years of exposure, and, uniquely among MAPT's four arms, a blinded contrast.

⚑ Why three nulls are worth more than three times one null. They fail differently. A pooled meta-analysis can be dragged down by short, small, heterogeneous trials. A target-engagement trial can be dismissed as underpowered on cognition. A single prevention trial can be dismissed as the wrong population or the wrong dose. None of those objections applies to all three at once, and the objection that used to apply to all three — the molecule never reached the brain — is the one Yassine measured and refuted.

⚑ This map is one of three places in the vault holding a verified delivery paired with an absent outcome, alongside creatine-cognition (≥20 g raises brain creatine ~11%, cognition unproven — creatine-20g-raises-brain-creatine) and nad-precursors (nicotinamide riboside doubles blood NAD⁺, cognition and cerebral blood flow unmoved — nicotinamide-riboside-raises-nad-without-cognitive-benefit). Three molecules, three confirmed deliveries, three absent outcomes: proving a supplement arrives is evidence that the pill works, not that it helps. That is the same discipline biological-age-clocks enforces as predictor ≠ responder; here it reads engagement ≠ outcome.

What is NOT settled by this map. Cognition in people who already have dementia (not tested here). Cognition in people with genuine omega-3 deficiency severe enough to be a clinical state. Whether dietary fish differs from capsules. And any cognitive question in adults under 55 — every trial here is in older adults.
