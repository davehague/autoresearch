Curcumin / turmeric (Curcuma longa and its curcuminoids) — nutrition

Status: emerging (2026-11-19) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a Cochrane systematic review (Shirinsky 2026, 9 RCTs, n=1,245, all knee osteoarthritis, no external funding) — the first direct, curcumin-specific evidence this vault has held strong enough to found a map on. A prior candidacy was explicitly declined on 2026-09-11, logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md: curcumin topped a diabetes-prevention network's HbA1c-reduction ranking, but as a single indirect network node with no independent literature to weigh it against — the same standard that has refused beta-alanine and collagen elsewhere in this vault. This is a different kind of evidence: a Cochrane synthesis of nine direct placebo/NSAID/paracetamol-controlled RCTs of the compound itself, which clears the bar the network-ranking candidacy did not. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Turmeric extract does not clear its own trial's clinical-significance threshold for knee osteoarthritis pain, function, or quality of life — and its safety over longer use is simply unmeasured, not reassuring. Against placebo (4 RCTs, n=291–379), pain fell 0.72 points more on a 0–10 scale (95% CI 1.39 lower to 0.06 lower) — short of the review's own 1.5-point bar for a patient to notice; function and quality-of-life intervals both cross zero (curcumin-no-clinically-meaningful-benefit-for-knee-oa-pain-function-or-qol). The one outcome that reached significance is a subjective global "treatment success" question (RR 1.78, 95% CI 1.19–2.67, on half the participant count of the other outcomes) — this vault has repeatedly found that unblindable self-impression measures diverge from more concrete ones, and declines to read this as the more trustworthy half of the pair. Safety is a genuine open question, not a clean bill of health: every adverse-event estimate is rated very-low certainty, and no trial ran longer than 90 days (curcumin-safety-profile-remains-uncertain-in-knee-oa-trials).

⚠ Curcumin's known pharmacology — CYP3A4/P-glycoprotein modulation and anticoagulant/antiplatelet potentiation at higher doses — is real but has no vault-quality source cited yet. Logged as an open interaction gap in docs/open-gaps-log.md, not fabricated into a graded safety_flags entry — the same handling berberine (CYP3A4/P-glycoprotein) and resveratrol (CYP3A4/CYP1A2, anticoagulant potentiation) apply to their own known-but-unsourced mechanisms. A credible mechanistic signal is enough to flag per shared/safety.md's asymmetric rule, but this vault does not invent the graded citation to go with it.

Why this map exists rather than a note on exercise-for-osteoarthritis. The founding source is about a compound, scoped to oral curcumin/curcuminoids specifically (combination herbal products excluded by the review's own design) — a nutrition/supplement lever, not an exercise one. Folding it into exercise-for-osteoarthritis would mix a pill with a movement practice inside one map; this vault keeps levers separate by mechanism even when the outcome (knee osteoarthritis symptoms) overlaps, the same decision already made between exercise-for-osteoarthritis and the pharmacological/supplement network (metformin, omega-3 via krill oil) it tracks.
