Creatine for cognition — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-07-08) — auto-forked this run from creatine-monohydrate (which became fork-due at 12 bullets / 4 sub-threads). The cognition thread is conceptually distinct from creatine's strength/bone/safety story — a separate outcome, a separate (and unresolved) dose question — so it earns its own map. The parent creatine-monohydrate remains the hub for the consolidating strength anchor plus bone and safety; this child carries the suggestive, dose-contested cognition evidence. Flagged for human review.

What we believe now

Creatine may help cognition in two narrow situations, but the case is suggestive — and higher doses do not help healthy cognition. Two angles:

  • (1) Chronic older-adult memory (suggestive). A small benefit concentrated in adults 66–76 (Prokopidis 2023, memory SMD 0.29 overall / 0.88 in elders); a 2026 systematic review concurs (Marshall 2026: 5/6 studies positive on memory/attention, but only 2 double-blind RCTs and 3/6 rated "poor"). Real direction, weak base.
  • (2) Acute stressed brain (mechanistically anchored, single small RCT). A single 0.35 g/kg dose rescued processing speed/memory during 21 h sleep deprivation, with ³¹P-MRS confirming acute brain phosphocreatine/ATP uptake (Gordji-Nejad 2024, n=15) — a rescue use (jet lag, a bad night), not a daily regimen.

Higher dose does not buy healthy cognition — though 20 g/day genuinely does load the brain (2026-07-09). A direct 10 g vs 20 g vs placebo RCT in young adults found no benefit and no dose-response on any measure (Moriarty 2023, n=30; placebo numerically beat the 10 g arm). A 2026 critical review first-authored by Candow himself concluded healthy adults likely need ≥20 g/day, not 10 g, for even a detectable brain-creatine rise (only ~3–11% vs MRS error up to 20%), and that the muscle-saturation-forces-brain-uptake mechanism was unproven in humans.

That mechanistic step is now demonstrated — and it changes nothing about the dose. The single-arm CABA pilot (Smith 2025, n=20 Alzheimer's patients) gave 20 g/day for 8 weeks and measured brain total creatine +11% on ¹H MRS (p<0.001, dz=1.01), elevated in 85% of participants (creatine-20g-raises-brain-creatine). So the 20 g threshold is real: creatine gets in. The trial's cognition improvements, however, came from an uncontrolled, open-label, 20-person pre-post on a practice-effect-prone battery, and the authors themselves say efficacy conclusions are impossible (creatine-cognition-uncontrolled-ad-pilot). Target engagement is not benefit — and the one randomized test of that exact link, at that exact dose, in healthy adults, was null. Concluding "20 g loads the brain, therefore take 20 g" is the precise cart-before-the-horse error Candow's own group named.

So the cognition thread stays emerging: a suggestive older-adult/acute signal, a healthy-adult randomized null, and a high-dose brain hypothesis that is now mechanistically confirmed and clinically unvalidated.

⚠ safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]. Twenty grams per day is double the highest dose the vault's safety thread has evidence for (~10 g/day renal-clean over 3 months, creatine-high-dose-renal-safety on creatine-monohydrate), and CABA's mild adverse events included sleep disturbance alongside GI effects — against GOAL.md's explicit "nothing that disrupts sleep" constraint and the sleep-regularity / sleep-duration foundation. Thin evidence, flagged over-cautiously (shared/safety.md: a missed harm is worse than an over-cautious flag).

For David (GOAL.md): the strength case (creatine-monohydrate) is the reason to take creatine; the cognition benefit is a possible bonus, not a reason to raise the dose. The vault's answer remains ~5 g/day, where it has both efficacy (creatine-dose-response-strength — flat above ~5 g) and safety. The one dose-responsive idea is the acute sleep-deprivation rescue (~0.35 g/kg once), not a higher daily maintenance dose. No dose prescribed (shared/safety.md).

Through-line

  • 2026-07-09 — Ingested Smith 2025 / CABA (Alzheimer's & Dementia: TRCI, single-arm open-label pilot, n=20 Alzheimer's-dementia patients, mean age 73.1, 20 g/day × 8 wk; NCT05383833): brain total creatine +11% on ¹H MRS (p<0.001, dz=1.01), elevated in 85% of participants — the first empirical demonstration that the ≥20 g brain-loading threshold actually loads the brain, confirming the mechanistic prediction of Candow's 2026 review. Cognition also rose on several NIH Toolbox measures (total p=0.02, fluid p=0.004, List Sorting p=0.001, Oral Reading p<0.001) with MMSE unchanged (p=0.33) — but with no control arm these are uninterpretable against practice effects, as the authors state outright. extends the mechanism, not the benefit. Status unchanged (emerging); adds safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] (20 g is double the vault's documented safety ceiling; AEs included sleep disturbance). Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-08-27 — the mechanism behind this map's central refusal now has a name, and it cuts both ways. Bučević Popović 2026 (oral-creatine-bioavailability-rct-reporting-meta-research-bucevic-popovic-2026, a census of all 343 published creatine RCTs, ingested tonight on creatine-monohydrate) reports that supplementation raises brain creatine by 5–15% against muscle's 20–40%, and attributes the gap to sparse SLC6A8 (CT1) transporter density at the blood–brain barrier. That supports this map's premise — the brain really is roughly threefold harder to load than muscle — while removing the mechanism the popular version rests on: muscle is not out-competing the brain for a shared pool, so there is no surplus to push past it. Uptake is gated by a transporter that down-regulates as the cell fills, and past saturation the extra creatine leaves in the urine. ⚑ And the structural reason the 20 g brain-loading dose remains unproven is now quantified: only 2 of 343 creatine RCTs ever aimed at raising brain creatine at all. The trials that would settle it have essentially never been run. This map's refusal of the high brain-loading dose is unchanged and better grounded.
