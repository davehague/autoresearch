GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide / tirzepatide / liraglutide) — metabolic

Status: consolidating (2026-07-07) — upgraded from emerging this run: the landmark SELECT RCT now corroborates the observational emulation the map was coined on. The vault's first Metabolic / pharmacologic lever, grounding the regimen's "discuss with your clinician" section on the compounded GLP-1 David is weighing (GOAL.md). Prescription/injectable → by rule it is never ranked in the daily protocol and no dose is set here (shared/safety.md).

Sub-threads: (1) the hard-outcome ledger — whether the class prevents cardiovascular events and death, anchored by SELECT and its emulations · (2) the cost ledger — harms and burdens the benefit is bought with, spanning gastrointestinal tolerability, lean-mass loss, quality of life and the optic-neuropathy signal. Delivery route (injectable vs oral, and titration schedule) is a property of how each agent is administered inside both ledgers, not a third object: it changes who tolerates the drug, which is the cost ledger, and changes nothing about whether it works. [reviewed 2026-08-16 @ 10 bullets]

What we believe now

In adults with overweight/obesity, GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce cardiovascular events — now shown in a randomized trial, not just observationally. The SELECT RCT (n=17,604, established CVD, no diabetes; semaglutide 2.4 mg × 34 mo) cut the primary MACE endpoint to 6.5% vs 8.0% — HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90), P<0.001, NNT 67. A large target-trial emulation (Tang 2025; 140,169 matched pairs, obesity without diabetes) points the same way — MACE HR 0.76, AKI 0.74, favorable mental-health signals, no coded safety excess. Two independent lines, high tier, real effect: consolidating.

⚑ NEW 2026-08-16 — a needle-free route enters development, and the class's dominant harm turns out to be partly a scheduling variable. A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial (n = 250, 13 Chinese sites, 16 weeks) of VCT220 — a nonpeptide small-molecule GLP-1 agonist taken once daily by mouth, with food — produced weight loss of 5.75% to 9.73% vs 1.61% on placebo (oral-nonpeptide-glp1-matched-injectable-class-weight-loss-over-sixteen-weeks). Unlike oral semaglutide it needs no absorption enhancer and no fasted-dosing rule. Status consolidating HELD — highest-tier-wins means a 16-week surrogate cannot move a map anchored by SELECT's hard-outcome MACE result, in either direction. ⚠ And the trial is manufacturer-funded with two company employees as co-authors, has no active comparator (vct220-has-no-active-comparator-so-it-cannot-be-ranked-against-semaglutide), and enrolled adults averaging 32.4 years. It is unapproved and available nowhere. Nothing about it justifies waiting for it.

⚑ The line worth carrying to a clinician is not about VCT220 at all. At an identical 160 mg dose, fast titration produced gastrointestinal adverse events in 80.6% of participants and slow titration in 64.5% (glp1-gastrointestinal-harm-tracks-titration-speed-not-only-dose). Nausea and vomiting are why people quit this class (glp1-gi-discontinuation-rct-select), and GOAL.md scores adherence and discomfort as first-class factors. How fast a dose is escalated appears to be a real lever on the harm most likely to end the therapy. ⚠ It rests on 31 participants per subgroup with no interaction test — a question to raise, not an answer, and this vault sets no titration rate (shared/safety.md).

⚠ 2026-07-27 — the map takes on a HARM category it did not previously hold: a rare eye event. Status held consolidating. A target trial emulation — an observational analysis built to imitate a randomised trial, with a defined treatment start and active comparator drugs rather than untreated controls — found GLP-1 starters developing ischemic optic neuropathy more often than starters of other second-line diabetes drugs (Reynolds 2026, Annals of Internal Medicine, NIH-funded, no manufacturer involvement).

What the event is: a sudden, usually painless loss of vision in one eye, caused by blood supply to the optic nerve head being interrupted. In most cases it does not recover.

The numbers, in the currency that belongs in a decision. 8.5 vs 5.5 cases per 10,000 over 18 months against SGLT2 inhibitors — risk difference 3.0 (0.4 to 5.7), number needed to harm 3,333; 7.8 vs 4.2 per 10,000 against DPP-4 inhibitors — risk difference 3.6 (1.1 to 6.1), number needed to harm 2,778 (glp1-small-absolute-increase-in-ischemic-optic-neuropathy). Roughly three thousand people would have to take the drug for eighteen months for one additional case. The risk was concentrated in men over 50 and people with existing cardiovascular or eye disease — 85.2% of events over 50, 70.3% in men, "minimal differences in women and those younger than 50" (glp1-optic-neuropathy-risk-concentrates-in-older-men).

⚠ And the authors decline to call it causal, naming the confounder that would explain it: body mass index — which is why the drug gets prescribed. BMI and diabetes duration were unmeasured; obesity travels with the hypertension, sleep apnoea and small-vessel disease implicated in optic-nerve infarction. Their conclusion ends "Observed differences may reflect residual confounding" (glp1-optic-neuropathy-signal-may-be-residual-confounding).

Why Status holds. shared/evidence-model.md aggregates by highest-tier-wins, and this map's Status rests on randomised hard-outcome evidence (SELECT, HR 0.80, NNT 67). A mixed observational safety signal at three events per 10,000, which its own authors decline to call causal, cannot outrank that. What changed is the map's cost column, not its Status — and the population is type 2 diabetes patients aged 18–65, not people taking a GLP-1 for weight alone.

Three framing points the vault must carry:

(1) Prescription — clinician-only, never a ranked pill. Per shared/safety.md, GLP-1 lives only in the regimen's clinician subsection with the evidence summarized. It is not scored against Benefit/Burden and no dose is invented. The Status reflects evidence strength, not a recommendation to rank it.

(2) The certainty has real edges — read them before the headline.

  • Secondary prevention, not primary. Everyone in SELECT already had cardiovascular disease. The CV benefit is proven in that population — not in a metabolically healthier adult taking GLP-1 for weight/longevity (closer to David's situation). This primary-prevention gap parallels the open omega-3 one; it is logged, not closed.
  • The mortality signal is not formally confirmed. SELECT's pre-specified hierarchical testing chain broke at CV death (HR 0.85, P=0.07), so all-cause mortality (HR 0.81, 0.71–0.93) and the HF composite are nominally positive but not statistically confirmed. This sharply recalibrates the emulation's eye-popping all-cause mortality HR 0.49 — the randomized truth is a more modest, unconfirmed mortality effect on top of a solid MACE reduction.
  • A real tolerability cost. SELECT: GI-driven discontinuation 10% vs 2% (any-AE discontinuation 17% vs 8%) — roughly 1 in 6 stopped the drug. This nuances Tang's "no GI excess": an EHR emulation misses the lived intolerance a randomized comparison surfaces. (Serious AEs were lower, 33% vs 36%.)
  • Sponsor trial. SELECT was funded and co-run by Novo Nordisk, semaglutide's maker.

(3) ⚠ It interacts with the muscle and fasting levers — and the lean-mass cost now has a number (2026-07-09). A meta-analysis of 7 placebo-controlled RCTs (821 patients) puts absolute lean-mass loss at −1.74 kg (95% CI −3.04 to −0.45), and the semaglutide subgroup at −5.44 kg (−7.07 to −3.81) (Laverde 2026, glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude). The same trials show lean mass rising as a proportion of total weight (+1.81%) — a denominator effect, because fat falls faster than muscle does (glp1-body-composition-proportion-improves), not muscle gained. Appetite suppression can simultaneously undercut the protein-target intake, so resistance-training + adequate protein (+ creatine-monohydrate) are the evidence-based muscle-preservation countermeasures during treatment — a recommendation the meta-analysis's own authors make. And GLP-1 overlaps with intermittent-fasting — both suppress intake, so stacking them raises under-eating / nutrient-adequacy questions. Both are clinician-conversation items, flagged here so they are never missed.

(4) It does not improve quality of life — and that is the outcome GOAL.md names (new 2026-07-10). A BMJ network meta-analysis of 262 RCTs (n=99,791, 19 drugs) found that across 43 trials and 45,663 participants, no drug convincingly improved quality of life — every mean difference under 5 points against a minimally important difference of 10 (glp1-no-meaningful-quality-of-life-benefit). The same synthesis prices the harms in absolute terms: discontinuation for adverse events RR 1.9–4.2 across leading agents, GI events RR 3.1–4.2, fatigue up to RR 8.9 (+331 per 1,000 per year, naltrexone-bupropion) (obesity-drug-benefit-harm-tradeoff). The vault states this plainly rather than softening it: the randomized evidence supports weight loss, and — in established cardiovascular disease — fewer cardiovascular events. It does not support "you will feel better." That is not an argument against a GLP-1; it is an argument for saying, in the clinician conversation, which promises have been measured.

(5) Among 19 drugs, only one is associated with living longer — and it isn't the strongest one. Subcutaneous semaglutide alone showed lower all-cause mortality (RR 0.81, 0.72–0.93) and myocardial infarction (0.72, 0.61–0.85) (semaglutide-mortality-benefit-not-independent-of-select). Tirzepatide removes the most weight (−14.9%) and the most fat (−25.7%) — and the most lean mass (−8.3%) (tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss). The harder a drug pulls weight off, the more of that weight is muscle.

Why consolidating, not consensus — and why a 262-trial meta-analysis does not change that: the BMJ network meta-analysis is the largest, best-conducted synthesis in the field, and it adds precision, not independence. Its mortality and MI estimates are, by the authors' own statement, "largely informed by cardiovascular outcome trials in high risk populations" — that is, by SELECT, which the vault already holds. SELECT's pre-specified hierarchy broke at CV death (p=0.07), leaving all-cause mortality nominal; re-pooling the same deaths inside a different statistical frame does not repair that. shared/evidence-model.md requires ≥2 independent groups for consolidating and a higher bar for consensus; this adds neither a new group nor a new population. Design quality and pooled precision cannot substitute for independence — the same discipline the vault applied to urolithin-a. Solid CV-benefit signal, real edges; and prescription status caps its regimen role regardless.

  • 2026-07-10 — Ingested Nong 2026 (BMJ, systematic review + network meta-analysis, 262 RCTs, n=99,791, 19 drugs, 24 outcomes, follow-up 12–172 wk, PROSPERO CRD42024507993, GRADE + Cochrane RoB 2, independently funded with conflicted authors firewalled from extraction/RoB — appraised reliable): weight loss tirzepatide −14.9% > CagriSema −14.8% > oral semaglutide −10.9% > subcutaneous semaglutide −9.8% vs lifestyle; subcutaneous semaglutide the only drug with lower all-cause mortality RR 0.81 (0.72–0.93) and MI 0.72 (0.61–0.85), heart failure 0.43 (tirzepatide 0.49); tirzepatide loses the most fat (−25.7%) AND the most lean mass (−8.3%); AE discontinuation RR 1.9–4.2, GI RR 3.1–4.2, fatigue up to RR 8.9; and across 43 trials / n=45,663, no drug convincingly improved quality of life (all MDs <5 points vs MID 10) — nor kidney failure. Status unchanged (consolidating): this is precision, not independence — the mortality events come largely from SELECT itself. Adds the QoL null (pushes-back), the harm gradient (extends), the lean-mass gradient (extends), and the comparative mortality finding (confirms). prescription — clinician-only, never ranked.

  • 2026-07-09 — Ingested Laverde 2026 (International Journal of Obesity, systematic review + meta-analysis, 7 placebo-controlled RCTs, n=821, obesity doses vs placebo): absolute lean mass −1.74 kg (−3.04 to −0.45), relative lean mass −3.06%, semaglutide −5.44 kg (−7.07 to −3.81) — while lean mass as a proportion of total weight rose +1.81% (I²=7%), a denominator effect. Closes the logged "no vault-quality GLP-1 body-composition source" gap — the lean-mass loss is now a graded concept (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude, safety_flags: [prescription, interaction]) rather than an un-numbered warning. extends; Status unchanged (consolidating — this quantifies a known cost, it does not challenge the CV benefit). Caveats: I²=98% on both absolute outcomes; lean mass, never muscle function; the authors' "not a limitation" framing rests on the proportional outcome, not the absolute one.

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested SELECT (Lincoff 2023, NEJM, RCT, n=17,604, overweight/obesity + established CVD, no diabetes; semaglutide 2.4 mg/wk × 34 mo): primary MACE 6.5% vs 8.0%, HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90), P<0.001, NNT 67; nonfatal MI HR 0.72; HF composite 0.82; all-cause mortality HR 0.81 (0.71–0.93) and CV death HR 0.85 (P=0.07) nominal only — hierarchy broke at CV death; GI-driven discontinuation 10% vs 2%. RCT-tier corroboration of Tang 2025 → upgrades the map emerging → consolidating. Caveats: secondary prevention; mortality not formally confirmed; Novo Nordisk-sponsored.

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Tang 2025 (Diabetes Obesity & Metabolism, target-trial emulation, n=140,169 matched pairs, obesity without diabetes): GLP-1RA associated with MACE HR 0.76, all-cause mortality HR 0.49, AKI HR 0.74, favorable mental-health signals, no safety excess. Auto-coins the map at emerging — closes the GLP-1 open gap; observational, prescription, RCT-consistent.

  • 2026-07-11 — Cross-linked Haas 2026 (fat-free-mass-mortality-meta-haas-2026, 49 observational cohorts, n=1,149,807, 83,798 deaths) as a BOUNDARY NOTE, not a gap-closer, and the vault is explicit about why. Low vs high fat-free mass tracks with all-cause mortality RR 1.42 (1.30–1.55) — RR 1.31 after the authors' own publication-bias correction. The tempting chain — the drug strips 1.7–5.4 kg of lean mass + low lean mass predicts death = the drug's muscle loss will kill you — does not hold, and this map refuses it. Haas measured untreated community-dwelling adults, where low lean mass is substantially a marker of occult illness and frailty (its own instrument gradient argues that: the effect is largest with the crudest measure, calf circumference RR 2.19, and smallest with real body composition, BIA RR 1.23). GLP-1 lean-mass loss is pharmacologic, deliberate, and accompanies large fat loss — a different mechanism in a different population. And the vault's own RCT-tier evidence runs the other way: SELECT found lower mortality on semaglutide despite the lean-mass cost. What this legitimately does is raise the stakes of the muscle-preservation countermeasure (lift + protein-target + creatine-monohydrate) — nothing more. No new safety_flags; Status unchanged (consolidating); the muscle-FUNCTION gap is NOT narrowed — still no trial has measured strength, power, or physical function during GLP-1 treatment. Concepts filed on resistance-training; the non-transfer is logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

  • 2026-07-19 — The QoL null gets a randomised head-to-head, and the head-to-head CONFIRMS it despite its own title; Status held consolidating (Shukla 2026, Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism 28(1), prespecified HRQoL analysis of SURMOUNT-5, tirzepatide vs semaglutide at maximum tolerated dose, ~750 adults with obesity/overweight WITHOUT T2D, SF-36v2 + PHQ-9 + PGI-S at Week 72; sponsored by Eli Lilly, several authors Lilly employees holding Lilly equity, lead author funded by Novo/Lilly/Viking; OA PDF archived). Results: PCS improved from baseline in both arms (p < 0.001); MCS did NOT improve in either arm (p > 0.05); all domains improved (p ≤ 0.008); General Health 5.45 (tirzepatide) vs 4.20 (semaglutide), p = 0.003. Two structural facts decide how much of this is bankable, and both are severe. (1) There is NO placebo arm. SURMOUNT-5 randomised drug-vs-drug, so every "improved from baseline" result is an uncontrolled pre-post change absorbing regression to the mean, 72 weeks of trial contact, and the expectation of people who could see the scale move (glp1-qol-improvement-uncontrolled-no-placebo-arm). The randomisation licenses exactly one comparison: 1.25 points of General Health, on a scale normed to SD 10. (2) The analysis is ON-TREATMENT, not ITT — restricted to those still taking the drug (glp1-qol-analysed-on-treatment-not-itt). GLP-1 discontinuation is symptom-driven and quantified (SELECT 10% vs 2%; AE-discontinuation RR 1.9–4.2 class-wide), and nausea and fatigue are not incidental to quality of life — they are quality of life. The people the drug made feel worst are absent from the denominator by construction. So a paper titled "Improved health-related quality of life" produces numbers consistent with the vault's existing null. Nong 2026's 43-trial, n=45,663 finding — no drug convincingly improved QoL, every mean difference under 5 points against a MID of 10 — stays strong and unmoved; Shukla's largest figure is 5.45, uncontrolled (glp1-qol-gains-remain-below-clinical-threshold). The new note arrives as confirms. ⚑ The most trustworthy result in the paper is the null nobody headlined. After 72 weeks of the most effective pharmacological weight loss ever achieved, mental quality of life did not move (glp1-physical-qol-improves-mental-qol-flat) — and sponsor bias, placebo absence and on-treatment selection would all have pushed MCS up. A null that survives biases pointing the other way is worth more than a positive that rides them. Third instance of the vault's markers-move-life-doesn't pattern, after ashwagandha (cortisol −2.36, QoL unchanged) and Nong's 19 drugs. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive. safety_flags: [prescription] — never ranked into the Protocol.

  • 2026-07-27 — A new HARM category joins the map — rare, serious, and not shown to be caused by the drug; Status held consolidating. Ingested Reynolds 2026 (Annals of Internal Medicine, online ahead of print, target trial emulation in a large US commercial claims database, January 2017 – December 2022, patients aged 18–65 with type 2 diabetes initiating a GLP-1 receptor agonist vs an SGLT2 inhibitor vs a DPP-4 inhibitor; inverse probability of treatment weighting over 80+ covariates; NIH-funded, no manufacturer involvement; appraised mixed; pdf_unavailable — Unpaywall confirms no open-access copy of any kind, full ladder documented). 18-month ischemic optic neuropathy: 8.5 vs 5.5 per 10,000 vs SGLT2 inhibitors, risk difference 3.0 (0.4–5.7), NNH 3,333; and 7.8 vs 4.2 per 10,000 vs DPP-4 inhibitors, risk difference 3.6 (1.1–6.1), NNH 2,778 (glp1-small-absolute-increase-in-ischemic-optic-neuropathy, suggestive, safety_flags: [prescription]). The risk concentrated in men over 50 and in those with existing cardiovascular or ophthalmic disease — 85.2% of 81 events over age 50, 70.3% in men, minimal in women and under-50s (glp1-optic-neuropathy-risk-concentrates-in-older-men, suggestive). ⚠ The authors decline the causal claim and name the confounder: body mass index, which is the indication (glp1-optic-neuropathy-signal-may-be-residual-confounding, single-study, open-question). Real strengths — two active comparators agreeing, a new-user design, absolute risks and numbers needed to harm led with rather than relative risks, public funding. Real limits — a small effect whose interval lower bounds are 0.4 and 1.1, BMI and diabetes duration unmeasured, and the outcome a claims-code proxy because "diagnostic codes specifically for NAION were lacking." Status held: highest-tier-wins means a mixed observational harm cannot outrank SELECT's randomised HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90), NNT 67. The cost column grew; the Status did not move.

  • 2026-07-28 — The map's oldest un-numbered warning finally gets a measurement, and it is the drug's own mechanism; Status held consolidating (Quan 2026, Diabetes & Metabolic Syndrome 20:103466, systematic review + meta-analysis, six databases to 31 March 2026; randomised or crossover placebo-controlled trials ≥4 weeks; no funding, and a competing-interest declaration that names Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly explicitly and declares nothing — rare in this literature; paywalled, pdf_unavailable after Unpaywall returned a definitive is_oa FALSE / oa_status closed with zero OA locations). GLP-1 therapy cut energy eaten at a standardised unlimited laboratory lunch by −1132 kJ (−1449 to −815) — about −271 kcal, I² = 0%, 95% prediction interval −1828 to −436 kJ (glp1-cuts-standardised-meal-intake-by-about-270-kcal, suggestive, safety_flags: [prescription, interaction]). The instrument is unusually good: food weighed, not recalled, against a placebo arm. ⚑ Why this matters to the map rather than merely being interesting. Since 2026-07-09 this map and protein-target have both carried "appetite suppression can crowd out the ~1.6 g/kg/day protein target" with nothing behind it but plausibility. Read against the map's documented lean-mass cost — −1.74 kg overall, −5.44 kg for semaglutide (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude) — the warning is now measured rather than inferred, and Quan's authors independently reach the same countermeasure: "structured nutritional counseling emphasizing adequate protein should complement pharmacotherapy" (glp1-intake-reduction-makes-the-protein-target-harder-to-hit, suggestive, safety_flags: [prescription, interaction]). ⚠ Two limits that stop this being over-read, and they are severe. (1) The pooled estimate is 4 arms from 3 trials, n = 209 — out of 16 studies reviewed, and the I² = 0% is a small-sample artifact: the paper's own exploratory addition of one further trial sent heterogeneity to 70%. (2) It is one meal, in a laboratory, on one day — the authors state that real-world habitual intake was too underreported to pool (glp1-intake-reduction-measured-only-at-a-laboratory-lunch, suggestive). This is the vault's fifth well-measured surrogate standing in for an unmeasured outcome this month, after creatine's brain loading, NR's doubled NAD⁺, DHA's CSF entry and fasted exercise's fat oxidation — and the first where the authors say so themselves. ⚑ Semaglutide versus tirzepatide: not significant, p = 0.154 — on four arms, so refused as equivalence (semaglutide-and-tirzepatide-acute-intake-difference-untested, single-study). Appraised mixed; capped suggestive. extends; Status unchanged (consolidating) — this quantifies a mechanism and a cost; it does not touch SELECT's randomised MACE HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90). prescription — clinician-only, never ranked, no dose set.

  • 2026-08-09 — The other molecule finally gets its own cardiovascular evidence, against the comparator that makes it interpretable — and the same paper hands the vault a mortality number it should not take. Ingested Krüger 2026 (tirzepatide-cardiovascular-events-cohort-kruger-2026, BMJ, new-user active-comparator cohort, 52,971 US adults aged 40+ with type 2 diabetes AND established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, two national claims databases May 2022 – May 2025, propensity-score overlap weighting to an effective 7,444/group, mean age 69.8; pre-registered as NCT07203677 with a posted protocol and SAP; appraised mixed, capped suggestive). The comparator is the design. Sitagliptin has trial-proven cardiovascular neutrality, so it acts as a placebo stand-in that everyone is nonetheless taking — which answers the question SURPASS-CVOT could not, because that trial compared tirzepatide against dulaglutide, an already-effective drug, and returned non-inferiority without superiority. No placebo-controlled cardiovascular outcome trial of tirzepatide exists or is likely to. Result: one-year MACE 2.9% (2.5–3.4) vs 4.4% (3.8–4.9), risk difference −1.4 pp (−2.1 to −0.7), HR 0.68 (0.58–0.80), NNT 70. Myocardial infarction HR 0.67 (0.52–0.87); ischaemic stroke 0.91 (0.64–1.28) — uninformative, not null (tirzepatide-lowers-mace-against-a-cardiovascular-neutral-comparator). Third independent line for the class, on a second molecule, by a third design — beside SELECT's randomised glp1-mace-benefit-rct-select and Tang's emulation (glp1-lowers-mace-mortality-obesity). ⚠ And then the numbers keep going, which is the finding the vault records against the paper's own reading. All-cause mortality HR 0.55 (0.42–0.72), infection hospitalisation 0.64 (0.55–0.75), infection-related mortality 0.40 (0.26–0.61) — at one year, larger than SELECT produced randomised over four times the follow-up. This is the exact mirror of glp1-optic-neuropathy-signal-may-be-residual-confounding — the same design class produced an observational harm signal the vault discounted; tonight it produces an implausibly large benefit and gets discounted the same way. Symmetry is the whole rule. ⚠ Scope recorded explicitly (tirzepatide-cardiovascular-evidence-comes-from-diabetes-with-established-heart-disease): baseline one-year event risk in the comparator arm was 4.4%. NNT 70 belongs to that baseline, not to the drug — a healthy adult without diabetes or diagnosed cardiovascular disease sits one to two orders of magnitude lower, so the same relative reduction buys a far smaller absolute benefit. ⚑ Credit where the vault has been complaining for weeks: this is a pre-registered observational study with a public protocol and analysis plan — which is why the unhelpful stroke result and the awkward infection result are both in print. confirms; Status consolidating HELD — a mixed cohort cannot lift a map already anchored by a randomised trial. prescription — clinician-only, never ranked, no dose set. pdf free at the publisher per Unpaywall but unreachable this run (bmj.com Cloudflare rate-limit at the browser rung on three attempts) — logged for retry, not as non-OA.

  • ⚑ 2026-07-31 — THE LEAN-MASS TAX IS A PROPERTY OF THE DEFICIT, NOT OF THE DRUG, and this map should stop implying otherwise. DEMFOS (metformin-lifestyle-physical-function-demfos-rct-nava-2026) ran six months of a 500–750 kcal/day deficit at ~1 g/kg/day protein with supervised progressive resistance training three times a week, in older adults, with no weight-loss drug anywhere in the trial — and DXA lean mass still fell 2.1–2.3 kg, MRI thigh muscle volume fell 79–92 cm³, and total hip bone mineral density fell 0.011–0.012 g/cm², the last significantly more than in the non-dieting arm (weight-loss-with-supervised-training-still-costs-lean-mass-and-hip-bone). Roughly a fifth to a quarter of the weight lost was lean tissue, with the countermeasure this map recommends fully supervised and still insufficient. Stated as a rule the vault can now carry across two literatures: an 8–10% intentional weight loss in an older adult costs lean mass and hip bone whether a drug, a deficit, or both produced it. ⚠ Magnitude is explicitly NOT transferred — DEMFOS had no GLP-1 arm and its participants were 72 and frail; the standing non-transfer refusal on fat-free-mass holds. ⚠ And the bone finding is new to this cluster: the vault has repeatedly priced GLP-1 lean-mass loss and has no comparable hip-bone evidence for pharmacologic weight loss at all. Logged as a gap. No safety_flags added — this is the known cost of an intended effect, not an interaction between two interventions.

So the clinician question below survives in its adherence form and not in its adaptation form — does adding this make the training I am relying on to keep muscle harder to actually do, rather than less effective.

  • ⚠ CLINICIAN-CONVERSATION EDGE, not a graded transfer — metformin is co-prescribed with GLP-1 agonists, and it may tax the very exercise this map depends on (SUPERSEDED IN PART by the entry above — 2026-07-30).
