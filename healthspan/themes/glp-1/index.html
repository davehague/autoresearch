GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide / tirzepatide / liraglutide) — metabolic

Status: consolidating (2026-07-07) — upgraded from emerging this run: the landmark SELECT RCT now corroborates the observational emulation the map was coined on. The vault's first Metabolic / pharmacologic lever, grounding the regimen's "discuss with your clinician" section on the compounded GLP-1 David is weighing (GOAL.md). Prescription/injectable → by rule it is never ranked in the daily protocol and no dose is set here (shared/safety.md).

What we believe now

In adults with overweight/obesity, GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce cardiovascular events — now shown in a randomized trial, not just observationally. The SELECT RCT (n=17,604, established CVD, no diabetes; semaglutide 2.4 mg × 34 mo) cut the primary MACE endpoint to 6.5% vs 8.0% — HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90), P<0.001, NNT 67. A large target-trial emulation (Tang 2025; 140,169 matched pairs, obesity without diabetes) points the same way — MACE HR 0.76, AKI 0.74, favorable mental-health signals, no coded safety excess. Two independent lines, high tier, real effect: consolidating.

Three framing points the vault must carry:

(1) Prescription — clinician-only, never a ranked pill. Per shared/safety.md, GLP-1 lives only in the regimen's clinician subsection with the evidence summarized. It is not scored against Benefit/Burden and no dose is invented. The Status reflects evidence strength, not a recommendation to rank it.

(2) The certainty has real edges — read them before the headline.

  • Secondary prevention, not primary. Everyone in SELECT already had cardiovascular disease. The CV benefit is proven in that population — not in a metabolically healthier adult taking GLP-1 for weight/longevity (closer to David's situation). This primary-prevention gap parallels the open omega-3 one; it is logged, not closed.
  • The mortality signal is not formally confirmed. SELECT's pre-specified hierarchical testing chain broke at CV death (HR 0.85, P=0.07), so all-cause mortality (HR 0.81, 0.71–0.93) and the HF composite are nominally positive but not statistically confirmed. This sharply recalibrates the emulation's eye-popping all-cause mortality HR 0.49 — the randomized truth is a more modest, unconfirmed mortality effect on top of a solid MACE reduction.
  • A real tolerability cost. SELECT: GI-driven discontinuation 10% vs 2% (any-AE discontinuation 17% vs 8%) — roughly 1 in 6 stopped the drug. This nuances Tang's "no GI excess": an EHR emulation misses the lived intolerance a randomized comparison surfaces. (Serious AEs were lower, 33% vs 36%.)
  • Sponsor trial. SELECT was funded and co-run by Novo Nordisk, semaglutide's maker.

(3) ⚠ It interacts with the muscle and fasting levers — and the lean-mass cost now has a number (2026-07-09). A meta-analysis of 7 placebo-controlled RCTs (821 patients) puts absolute lean-mass loss at −1.74 kg (95% CI −3.04 to −0.45), and the semaglutide subgroup at −5.44 kg (−7.07 to −3.81) (Laverde 2026, glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude). The same trials show lean mass rising as a proportion of total weight (+1.81%) — a denominator effect, because fat falls faster than muscle does (glp1-body-composition-proportion-improves), not muscle gained. Appetite suppression can simultaneously undercut the protein-target intake, so resistance-training + adequate protein (+ creatine-monohydrate) are the evidence-based muscle-preservation countermeasures during treatment — a recommendation the meta-analysis's own authors make. And GLP-1 overlaps with intermittent-fasting — both suppress intake, so stacking them raises under-eating / nutrient-adequacy questions. Both are clinician-conversation items, flagged here so they are never missed.

(4) It does not improve quality of life — and that is the outcome GOAL.md names (new 2026-07-10). A BMJ network meta-analysis of 262 RCTs (n=99,791, 19 drugs) found that across 43 trials and 45,663 participants, no drug convincingly improved quality of life — every mean difference under 5 points against a minimally important difference of 10 (glp1-no-meaningful-quality-of-life-benefit). The same synthesis prices the harms in absolute terms: discontinuation for adverse events RR 1.9–4.2 across leading agents, GI events RR 3.1–4.2, fatigue up to RR 8.9 (+331 per 1,000 per year, naltrexone-bupropion) (obesity-drug-benefit-harm-tradeoff). The vault states this plainly rather than softening it: the randomized evidence supports weight loss, and — in established cardiovascular disease — fewer cardiovascular events. It does not support "you will feel better." That is not an argument against a GLP-1; it is an argument for saying, in the clinician conversation, which promises have been measured.

(5) Among 19 drugs, only one is associated with living longer — and it isn't the strongest one. Subcutaneous semaglutide alone showed lower all-cause mortality (RR 0.81, 0.72–0.93) and myocardial infarction (0.72, 0.61–0.85) (semaglutide-mortality-benefit-not-independent-of-select). Tirzepatide removes the most weight (−14.9%) and the most fat (−25.7%) — and the most lean mass (−8.3%) (tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss). The harder a drug pulls weight off, the more of that weight is muscle.

Why consolidating, not consensus — and why a 262-trial meta-analysis does not change that: the BMJ network meta-analysis is the largest, best-conducted synthesis in the field, and it adds precision, not independence. Its mortality and MI estimates are, by the authors' own statement, "largely informed by cardiovascular outcome trials in high risk populations" — that is, by SELECT, which the vault already holds. SELECT's pre-specified hierarchy broke at CV death (p=0.07), leaving all-cause mortality nominal; re-pooling the same deaths inside a different statistical frame does not repair that. shared/evidence-model.md requires ≥2 independent groups for consolidating and a higher bar for consensus; this adds neither a new group nor a new population. Design quality and pooled precision cannot substitute for independence — the same discipline the vault applied to urolithin-a. Solid CV-benefit signal, real edges; and prescription status caps its regimen role regardless.

  • 2026-07-11 — Cross-linked Haas 2026 (fat-free-mass-mortality-meta-haas-2026, 49 observational cohorts, n=1,149,807, 83,798 deaths) as a BOUNDARY NOTE, not a gap-closer, and the vault is explicit about why. Low vs high fat-free mass tracks with all-cause mortality RR 1.42 (1.30–1.55) — RR 1.31 after the authors' own publication-bias correction. The tempting chain — the drug strips 1.7–5.4 kg of lean mass + low lean mass predicts death = the drug's muscle loss will kill you — does not hold, and this map refuses it. Haas measured untreated community-dwelling adults, where low lean mass is substantially a marker of occult illness and frailty (its own instrument gradient argues that: the effect is largest with the crudest measure, calf circumference RR 2.19, and smallest with real body composition, BIA RR 1.23). GLP-1 lean-mass loss is pharmacologic, deliberate, and accompanies large fat loss — a different mechanism in a different population. And the vault's own RCT-tier evidence runs the other way: SELECT found lower mortality on semaglutide despite the lean-mass cost. What this legitimately does is raise the stakes of the muscle-preservation countermeasure (lift + protein-target + creatine-monohydrate) — nothing more. No new safety_flags; Status unchanged (consolidating); the muscle-FUNCTION gap is NOT narrowed — still no trial has measured strength, power, or physical function during GLP-1 treatment. Concepts filed on resistance-training; the non-transfer is logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠ The fat-free-mass boundary — the inference the vault will NOT make (new 2026-07-11). See the 2026-07-11 through-line bullet. In one line: low lean mass predicting death in untreated adults does not establish that drug-induced lean-mass loss carries the same risk, the populations and mechanisms differ, and SELECT's randomized evidence shows lower mortality on the drug despite the muscle cost. This raises the stakes of the countermeasure without grading a harm, and it does not close the muscle-function gap. No safety_flags added on this basis (shared/safety.md — a plausible chain is not a finding).
  • The behavioral mirror: multidomain-lifestyle pursued the same weight loss and failed the same primary endpoint GLP-1 passed (new 2026-07-11). Look AHEAD randomized a decade-long intensive lifestyle program at weight loss in overweight/obese adults with type 2 diabetes; its cardiovascular primary endpoint was null (HR 0.95, 0.83–1.09) and the trial stopped for futility. SELECT randomized semaglutide at an overlapping population and hit its cardiovascular primary endpoint (MACE HR 0.80). Same target, opposite results — which is a caution against treating "weight loss" as one interchangeable exposure regardless of how it is achieved, in either direction. What neither source supports: Look AHEAD's cognitive-harm signal at BMI ≥40 says nothing about GLP-1 drugs — no source tests drug-driven weight loss against cognition. The comparison is recorded as an open question, not an inference. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
  • ⚠ Interaction with intermittent-fasting — intake suppression overlap. Both GLP-1 and IF suppress food intake; combining them compounds the risk of under-eating and nutrient inadequacy. Noted on both maps; a clinician-conversation item, not a co-recommendation. (safety_flags: [interaction].)
  • ⚠ Interaction with protein-target + resistance-training — lean-mass loss, now graded (Laverde 2026). A meta-analysis of 7 placebo-controlled RCTs (n=821) quantifies what the map previously carried as an un-numbered warning: absolute lean mass −1.74 kg, and −5.44 kg in the semaglutide subgroup (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude, safety_flags: [prescription, interaction]). Suppressed appetite can additionally crowd out the ~1.6 g/kg/day protein target. Resistance training + adequate protein (+ creatine) are the evidence-based countermeasures — and the meta-analysis's own authors recommend exactly that, concluding drug treatment must be accompanied by nutritional and exercise intervention. Flagged in both directions. The remaining piece of this gap is functional: every pooled endpoint is lean mass (an imaging compartment), never muscle strength or function, and I²=98% makes the pooled kilograms a direction rather than a precise expectation. Note the companion finding (glp1-body-composition-proportion-improves) that lean mass rises as a share of body weight (+1.81%) — arithmetically compatible with losing muscle, and not to be read as reassurance for a healthspan goal.
  • ⚠ The lean-mass interaction now has a GRADIENT, not just a magnitude (added 2026-07-10). Across 19 drugs, tirzepatide removes the most fat (−25.7%) and the most lean mass (−8.3%) (tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss). So the countermeasure stack (resistance-training + protein-target + creatine-monohydrate) is not optional garnish on the more potent agents — it scales with them. Carried on all three maps. The new high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength finding says something about how that countermeasure is loaded, and its own high-dose-risk flag says who should decide the load.
  • ⚠ The quality-of-life null reaches past this map (added 2026-07-10). GOAL.md's north star is healthspan, not lifespan — and glp1-no-meaningful-quality-of-life-benefit is a high-tier, well-powered null on precisely that outcome (43 trials, n=45,663). It is a pushes-back note against an inference nobody wrote down: that a drug improving weight, MACE, and body composition must improve lived experience. It does not touch the CV benefit and does not move Status. Note the recurring vault pattern it joins: ashwagandha also lowered its marker (cortisol) while leaving quality of life unchanged (ashwagandha-subjective-stress-anxiety-soft). Two very different interventions, same shape of result — markers move, life doesn't. Worth carrying as a lens, not a law.
  • Overlaps the regimen's existing clinician subsection (§6). The regimen already routed GLP-1 to "discuss with your clinician"; this map now supplies the graded evidence behind that routing.
  • Global-ripple: no interaction with the sleep, stress, or vitamin/omega maps. RCT-tier SELECT evidence now ingested (2026-07-07) — the "SELECT un-ingested" open gap is closed. The body-composition gap is closed too (2026-07-09, Laverde 2026 — see above). Residual gaps, now three and sharper: (a) primary prevention in metabolically healthy adults — SELECT enrolled established-CVD patients only, and the BMJ NMA's mortality/MI estimates are drawn from those same high-risk trials, so this gap is reconfirmed, not narrowed (parallels the omega-3 primary-prevention gap, closer to David's use case); (b) muscle function, not just muscle mass — no pooled trial measured strength, power, or physical function during GLP-1 treatment, so the functional cost of losing 1.7–5.4 kg (or 8.3%) of lean tissue is still unmeasured. Do not fabricate it; (c) quality of life over a healthspan horizon — the QoL null comes from trials of 12–172 weeks using generic instruments, which is long enough to say "no measured benefit" and far too short to say anything about decades.
