Vitamin K2 — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-10-27) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single, well-conducted RCT that found vitamin K2 + D3 slows coronary artery calcification progression in people who already have severe calcification. ⚠ Opens with a safety flag that is not from this trial: vitamin K is the direct pharmacological antagonist of warfarin-class anticoagulants — textbook, label-level pharmacology, flagged per shared/safety.md's "credible signal is enough" rule before any vault-quality interaction source has been ingested.

Sub-threads: none — one trial, one question (does K2+D3 slow coronary calcification progression in a high-calcification population); no separable strand yet. [reviewed 2026-10-27 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

In adults who already have severe coronary artery calcification, adding vitamin K2 (720 μg/d) to vitamin D3 (25 μg/d) for 24 months slows further calcification progression, without shifting plaque toward a riskier (non-calcified) phenotype. This is one RCT, a surrogate imaging endpoint (not a hard cardiovascular event), and a secondary-prevention population — it says nothing about whether vitamin K2 matters for a healthy adult without substantial existing calcification, and the trial's combined K2+D3 dosing cannot separate which nutrient (or the combination specifically) is doing the work.

Regimen implication: none yet, and a standing caution. One RCT in a population David has not been shown to belong to (unknown CAC status) does not clear Protocol or Watch-list placement. The interaction flag is the more immediately actionable fact here: vitamin K2 supplementation is not compatible with warfarin-class anticoagulant therapy without clinician guidance, regardless of what this trial itself tested — shared/safety.md requires this caveat live on the map from day one, not only once a dedicated interaction study is ingested.
