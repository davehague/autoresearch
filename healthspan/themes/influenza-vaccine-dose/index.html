High-dose vs standard-dose influenza vaccine (adults 65+) — preventive / pharmacologic

Status: emerging (2026-07-31) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's second non-supplement preventive-medicine lever after aspirin, and the first that is not a drug taken daily. It enters the atlas because it is a rare thing here: an intervention with hard endpoints, randomised evidence at half-a-million scale, GRADE-high certainty on four outcomes, and no supplement industry attached to the question. ⚠ It is also, for David, entirely field context — every trial enrolled adults 65 or older and the formulation is not offered at his age. It will never be scored in the regimen for him.

⚠ Scope, stated once and binding on every future entry: this map is about DOSE, not about vaccination. Every participant in every trial received an influenza vaccine. Nothing here compares vaccinated with unvaccinated, and no entry on this map may be read as evidence on that question. Adjuvanted and recombinant formulations — the two other ways of strengthening a flu shot in older people — were excluded by design and are equally out of scope.

What we believe now

Four times the antigen prevents hospital admissions and does not prevent deaths. Both halves are the finding.

(1) Hospitalisation moves, decisively and consistently. Across 14 randomised trials and 581,845 adults aged 65+, the high-dose vaccine (60 µg of haemagglutinin per strain, against the standard 15 µg) reduced hospitalisation for influenza — risk ratio 0.61 (95% CI 0.50–0.74), laboratory-confirmed influenza hospitalisation — 0.68 (0.58–0.80), cardiorespiratory hospitalisation — 0.92 (0.86–0.98), and hospitalisation for any cause — 0.97 (0.95–0.99). All four at GRADE high certainty, with I² between 0% and 8.7% — the trials agree (high-dose-influenza-vaccine-reduces-hospitalisation-in-older-adults).

(2) In people rather than ratios, it is modest — and the ranking inverts. Per 10,000 people vaccinated: 4 fewer influenza hospitalisations, 15 fewer cardiorespiratory hospitalisations, and 29 fewer admissions of any kind. The largest absolute benefit belongs to the weakest relative effect — 3% of a baseline of 958 per 10,000 beats 39% of a baseline of 11. Roughly one admission avoided per 345 people vaccinated per season. Anyone reasoning from relative risks alone gets this backwards, and the vault keeps this source largely because it makes that inversion impossible to miss.

(3) All-cause mortality did not move, on the best null the vault holds. RR 0.98 (0.92–1.05), I² = 0.0%, 12 trials, 559,082 people — 1 fewer death per 10,000, interval running from 4 fewer to 3 more (high-dose-influenza-vaccine-null-on-all-cause-mortality). This is not "we could not tell." The interval excludes anything larger than an 8% reduction, and it rules out the substantial mortality benefit enhanced flu vaccines are often discussed as delivering. The review nevertheless downgraded it to moderate certainty for imprecision because the interval crosses no effect, and the vault endorses that restraint — a narrow null is not a demonstration of equivalence. The causal chain — better antibody response → fewer infections → fewer admissions → fewer deaths — holds to the second-to-last link and stops.

(4) No safety signal. Serious adverse events RR 0.97 (0.93–1.01) across 548,464 people, low certainty. The benefit is not being bought with a measurable harm on a base large enough to show a common one.

Why emerging, decided by rule and not by feel. The map holds one source, appraised mixed, with every concept capped suggestive — and shared/evidence-model.md does not let a suggestive-capped source carry a map to consolidating. The appraisal turns on one compound weakness the review states itself: the precision behind the pooled estimates comes disproportionately from two very large open-label pragmatic trials that ascertained outcomes from routine administrative records, in a literature that is mostly industry-sponsored (enhanced-vaccine-evidence-rests-on-open-label-administrative-data-trials) — and the two outcomes most exposed to that are the two with the smallest relative effects. The word emerging describes the thinness of this map's through-line, not the weight of the evidence inside it. ⚠ A reviewer could reasonably argue that four GRADE-high outcomes across 14 randomised trials and 581,845 people deserve a better label than the one the vault gives a single mechanistic study, and that the failure here is in the vocabulary rather than the grading. Logged in docs/backlog.md as a decision.

What would move it. A second independent synthesis; or data on antigenic match (how well each season's vaccine matched the circulating strains — the dominant driver of any flu vaccine's performance, and something this review had too little data to examine); or randomised evidence in the populations this corpus barely touches — care-home residents and immunocompromised adults, where baseline risk is highest and the benefit might be largest.

Regimen implication: none, and that is a decision rather than an omission. Age-restricted, clinician- and public-health-administered, and irrelevant to David's current decade. It is never ranked in the protocol. shared/safety.md applies in full: this map summarises published evidence and recommends no vaccination decision to anyone.

Coins the map at emerging — one source, suggestive-capped, thin through-line. open-question.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Global ripple across all 44 maps: no interaction edge, and the absence is a finding rather than an oversight. An inactivated vaccine given once a season does not interact with any supplement, drug, sleep, recovery or exercise lever in this vault at any evidence tier the vault would accept. The one adjacent question the source addressed — co-administration with a COVID-19 vaccine — was examined as a within-trial subgroup and came back null. safety_flags: [] on all three concepts is deliberate.
  • ⚑ Sibling of aspirin in shape, and the contrast between them is instructive. Both are preventive interventions aimed at older adults, both tested in very large randomised trials with hard endpoints, both arriving in this vault from outside the supplement conversation. Aspirin was coined contested and downgraded to fading within two days once its efficacy null was joined by a quantified bleeding harm (aspirin-no-effect-on-healthspan-loss-older-adults, aspirin-increases-major-haemorrhage-older-adults). This map opens with the mirror image: a real benefit on one endpoint family, a precise null on death, and no harm signal. Same evidentiary machinery, opposite result — which is the clearest available demonstration that the machinery is not producing its answers in advance.
  • ⚑ It supplies the vault's cleanest worked example of relative-versus-absolute effect, and that transfers everywhere. The Universal appraisal lens in shared/study-appraisal.md opens with "only a relative number given; baseline hidden." This source volunteers both for every outcome, and the result inverts the intuitive ranking — the 3% effect delivers seven times the absolute benefit of the 39% one. Cross-linked to cocoa-flavanols (cocoa-flavanol-absolute-cvd-benefit-under-one-percent) as the vault's other case where the absolute number reframed a persuasive relative one. No evidence is transferred — this is a methodological edge, not a clinical one.
  • ⚑ And it adds a second, sharper instance of one of the vault's standing rules: a mechanism that verifiably works is not an outcome that moves. In omega-3, DHA reached the brain and cognition did not change (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit). Here, the higher antigen dose demonstrably prevented influenza hospitalisations — the mechanism worked, on hard events, at GRADE high — and all-cause mortality still did not move. ⚑ This is the stronger form of the rule: the first case shows a surrogate failing to translate; this one shows a genuine clinical outcome failing to translate to a harder one. Cross-linked to omega-3, vitamin-d and multivitamins, whose entire pattern is the same shape. Recorded as a reasoning rule; no Status moved anywhere on the strength of it.
  • Open gap: antigenic match. How well a given season's vaccine matches the strains actually circulating is the dominant driver of any influenza vaccine's performance, and this review states it had too few data to examine it. Every number on this map is an average across well-matched and drifted seasons, and could mask large differences between them. Nothing in the accessible literature closes this; logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
