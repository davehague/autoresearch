Dietary nucleotides — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-09-28) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single industry-funded RCT (TALENTs, NCT05243108) testing dietary nucleotide supplementation (1.2 g/day) in healthy older adults. The vault's first encounter with this compound. Opened emerging on the ordinary grounds: one source, appraised mixed (manufacturer funding, unconfirmed baseline on the primary outcome, an underpowered secondary comparison), so every concept caps at suggestive. git is the undo.

What we believe now

A single, genuinely well-randomized but manufacturer-funded 19-week trial found dietary nucleotide supplementation lowered DNA-methylation age (an epigenetic-clock biomarker) and modestly improved insulin sensitivity in healthy older adults — with no functional or clinical outcome tested at all. This is a biomarker-movement story, not yet a functional-benefit one: the trial measured no strength, mobility, cognition-test, or hard-endpoint outcome, only biomarkers and safety labs. That places it in the same "target engagement without outcome" category this vault already tracks for NAD+ precursors and creatine's brain-loading claim (see biological-age-clocks) — a biomarker moved, and whether that translates into anything a person would feel or benefit from is untested.
