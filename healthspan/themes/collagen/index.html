Collagen peptide supplementation — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-11-25) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. What we believe now

Oral collagen peptide supplementation measurably improves skin elasticity and hydration, and osteoarthritis pain and stiffness, with longer use associated with larger gains — but it does nothing for post-exercise recovery, and the certainty behind even the positive findings is capped by the quality of the underlying evidence base. The founding umbrella review re-pools 16 existing meta-analyses (113 RCTs, 7,983 participants): skin elasticity (SMD 1.01) and hydration (SMD 0.71) both improve significantly; osteoarthritis self-reported pain (SMD −0.35) and WOMAC stiffness (SMD −0.53) both improve significantly, though the more granular WOMAC pain subscale and functional-limitation measures did not; a small maximal- strength gain appears (SMD 0.18) but strength recovery, soreness, and tendon mechanical properties after exercise show no effect. The review's own AMSTAR-2 audit of its 16 ingredients found only one rated high quality — the rest low or critically low — a ceiling on how much weight its "high certainty" GRADE labels should carry. Wrinkles, despite being named in the review's title scope, were not reported as a distinct pooled effect size in the retrievable text.

Why emerging, and why flagged for review. A single umbrella review, built from mostly low-to- critically-low-quality underlying meta-analyses, founds this entire map. The direction of every claim is plausible and not contradicted by anything else in this vault, but nothing here has been independently replicated by a second, differently-sourced synthesis yet, and a meaningful share of this map's specific numbers came from an automated summary of page content rather than a verbatim read of the primary PDF (the source is paywalled; see the source digest). Worth a full-text human read once access is available.

What this costs: an oral supplement, no dose recommended. No dose is set here beyond what the underlying literature reports, and none is invented (shared/safety.md). Not yet actionable for David (GOAL.md requires third-party purity/potency verification before any supplement enters the Protocol; that has not been established here, and the evidence-quality ceiling above argues for watching, not adopting).
