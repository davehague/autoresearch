Magnesium glycinate — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-07-04) — a weak-but-now-RCT-corroborated sleep signal; low burden and well tolerated, so it sits on a watch/foundation footing, not a confident recommendation. A direct glycinate-form RCT (Schuster 2025) confirms the direction but with a small effect (d=0.2) — enough to firm up, not to consolidate.

What we believe now

Supplemental magnesium probably helps mild insomnia, likeliest in people with low baseline magnesium. The signal now rests on two convergent tiers pointing the same way: a systematic review with no pooled estimate (Rawji 2024) and a direct, registered, double-blind RCT of the glycinate form (Schuster 2025: ISI −3.9 vs −2.3, p=0.049, d=0.2 small, biggest in low-dietary-Mg responders). The glycinate form is chosen for tolerability (low GI side-effects) — an adherence/low-burden consideration, not a proven outcome superiority.

Why still emerging, not consolidating: the confirming RCT is a single trial with a small, borderline effect on a subjective measure (ISI only) over 4 weeks. Per the evidence model's effect-size gate (a high tier with a trivial effect does not consolidate), the honest read is a strengthened emerging, not a graduation. Objective sleep measures and replication would move it.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Rawji 2024 (systematic review, 15 interventional trials): magnesium "likely useful" for mild insomnia/anxiety, especially with low baseline Mg; evidence weak and heterogeneous. Status → emerging.

  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Schuster 2025 (RCT, n=155, 250 mg elemental Mg/day as bisglycinate): ISI −3.9 vs −2.3 (p=0.049), small effect (d=0.2), largest in low-dietary-Mg responders. Direct glycinate-form RCT confirms direction; effect-size gate keeps Status at emerging (strengthened, not consolidated).

  • 2026-07-13 — A second axis opens and immediately closes: magnesium is not a metabolic lever; Status unchanged (Amiri 2026, BMC Nutrition, systematic review + meta-analysis of 15 RCTs, n=1,085 adults with diabetes and pre-diabetes, PRISMA 2020, searched to 24 Apr 2026). Oral magnesium vs placebo produced no significant change in insulin (MD −1.73, −3.73 to 0.27, p = 0.09) or HOMA-IR (MD −0.74, −1.58 to 0.09, p = 0.08); prediabetes null too; subgroups by dose (>250 vs ≤250 mg/day) and duration (over vs under 12 weeks) did not rescue it (magnesium-null-on-insulin-resistance). This is the pitch magnesium is actually sold on — insulin sensitivity, metabolic health, blood sugar — and the best randomised evidence, in the population most likely to show it, does not support it. The vault reads the interval honestly in both directions, for the fourth time this run: HOMA-IR's upper bound is +0.09, the estimate lies almost entirely on the benefit side, and it misses significance by nine hundredths — the same shape as Shen's BMD near-miss (rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd) and Liu's walking PSQI interval (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-unresolved, which Kong then confirmed the next day). So: not shown, and not excluded. Where the paper does slip is the pivot after the primaries fail — a post-hoc meta-regression on baseline insulin (one significant modifier among six tested, uncorrected) becomes the conclusion's call for "targeted interventions" (magnesium-insulin-baseline-moderator-post-hoc). Population is diabetic/pre-diabetic; David is neither, and the paper contains no evidence at all about healthy metabolism. The map keeps its sleep signal, gains a firmer boundary, and stays on the Watch list.

  • 2026-07-19 — The whole supplement shelf gets pooled on one instrument, and the ceiling on the entire category is two-thirds of a PSQI point; Status held emerging (Mei 2025, Nutrients 17(24):3952, systematic review + meta-analysis, 28 RCTs, PROSPERO CRD420250656302, six databases to Nov 2024, RoB + GRADEpro in duplicate; publicly funded — Chinese national/Guangdong programmes — no conflicts; OA PDF archived via the browser rung). Pooling tryptophan, L-theanine, GABA, B vitamins, vitamin D, zinc, magnesium, potassium, omega-3, ashwagandha, crocetin and ergothioneine: PSQI MD −0.70 (−1.37 to −0.03), TST SMD +0.23, SL −0.24, WASO −0.30, awakenings not significant. GRADE moderate for SE/SL/TST/WASO, low for PSQI. (1) The headline fails the clinical-significance test: against a PSQI MID of ~3 on a 0–21 scale, an estimate of 0.70 whose upper bound is −0.03 has its entire plausible range below the threshold anyone would notice (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold). On the same instrument exercise+CBT scores −2.80 at moderate certainty — four times larger, at higher certainty, for free. This map estimated that gap at "roughly twice" on 2026-07-13; it was under-stating it. (2) The pooled node is a shopping aisle, not an intervention — a dozen substances with no shared mechanism, while the authors' conclusion silently reassigns credit to "tryptophan, vitamin D, omega-3, zinc and antioxidants" (supplement-sleep-benefit-not-attributable-to-any-supplement). Sixth attribution failure, and structurally the worst — the prior five bundled things that at least co-occur. Magnesium is inside this pool and is NOT among the named drivers — conspicuous, but absence from a narrative sentence is not a subgroup null, and the vault will not read it as one. (3) The base is 28 trials of 7–50 people, I²=67% on PSQI and 85% in the amino-acid subgroup, and the paper's own funnel/Egger assessment reveals "varying degrees of asymmetry" — so −0.70 is more likely an over-estimate than an under-estimate (supplement-sleep-trials-are-small-and-heterogeneous). (4) Execution tell: sleep efficiency — a percentage — is reported in minutes ("+2.58 min"), in the abstract, past peer review (supplement-sleep-efficiency-reported-in-wrong-units); fourth impossible-number catch after Drummond, Sun and Alsanie. Magnitude discounted, direction kept. Nothing here rescues this map and nothing sinks it — the small subjective sleep signal (d = 0.2) remains the map's whole case, now with a measured ceiling on the category it belongs to. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive. Watch list, unbought.

  • 2026-08-02 — ⚑ THE METABOLIC AXIS CLOSES A SECOND TIME, FROM THE LARGEST AND LONGEST MAGNESIUM TRIAL IN THIS VAULT — AND THE SLEEP CLAIM IS UNTOUCHED. Status held emerging. Ingested Al-Maqbali 2026 (Frontiers in Endocrinology 17:1883483, prospective, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group RCT, n = 247, adults with type 2 diabetes and HbA1c ≥ 7.0%, median age 58, median diabetes duration 16 years, single centre, tertiary care, Oman, 12 months with three follow-up visits; prospectively registered NCT05774015 on 2023-07-03 before the September-2023 start; ITT and per-protocol pre-planned; Oman Ministry of Education, Research and Innovation block funding; ⚠ Nova Biomedical donated the Stat Profile Prime Plus® analyser used to measure ionised magnesium — the trial's own exposure biomarker — recorded as a bias-watch item touching the mediator, not the null primary endpoint; appraised mixed, capped suggestive; gold-OA PDF archived 2.71 MB via the browser rung). ⚑ THE PRIMARY OUTCOME WAS NULL. HbA1c at 12 months: median −0.30% vs −0.05%, p = 0.145 — reported in the paper's own ITT table as −0.25% vs 0.00%, p = 0.181. Fasting glucose −0.4 vs 0 mmol/L, p = 0.035 is the only endpoint separating in ITT. Systolic BP p = 0.605, diastolic p = 0.852, triglycerides p = 0.136, LDL p = 0.343, HDL p = 0.062 — every one flat (magnesium-oxide-null-on-hba1c-over-twelve-months). ⚑ What makes this null worth having: the trial proved it delivered the compound. Both ionised and total magnesium were measured and magnesium status improved. A supplement trial that verifies exposure and still misses its endpoint has said something a trial with unverified exposure cannot — and the same night's cold-exposure null is the contrast, having never confirmed the stimulus reached the tissue (gel-pack-cooling-may-not-deliver-a-thermal-dose-to-deep-muscle). ⚑ This is the SECOND independent higher-tier null on the metabolic axis and it agrees with the first. This map closed that axis on 2026-07-13 with Amiri 2026 — 15 RCTs, n = 1,085, null on insulin (p = 0.09) and HOMA-IR (p = 0.08), with dose and duration subgroups failing to rescue it (magnesium-null-on-insulin-resistance). A meta-analysis-rct on insulin resistance and a 247-person 12-month rct on HbA1c now land on the same answer from different markers over different horizons. confirms. Magnesium is not a metabolic lever, and the axis is as closed as evidence of this quality can close it. ⚠ The honest limit is large and cuts against the vault's convenience. The trial was powered to detect a 1.0% absolute HbA1c reduction — the effect size of adding a glucose-lowering drug — and observed about a quarter of that (magnesium-glycemic-null-is-powered-for-an-implausible-effect). So the claim is "no drug-sized effect," not "no effect," the same discipline applied to five underpowered nulls on resistance-training-programming and relaxed only once, for a genuinely well-powered analysis. ⚠ And the paper spins its own null. The abstract's "these findings support Mg supplementation as a safe adjunct… particularly among selected subgroups" rests on a null primary endpoint, one fasting-glucose reading, two non-significant subgroups (p = 0.074 baseline hypomagnesaemia — roughly 18 people, since only 7.3–8.1% of the cohort was hypomagnesaemic — and p = 0.085 for diabetes duration ≤ 15 years), and a goal-attainment figure reported as 14.0% vs 6.0%, p = 0.043 in the abstract and 13.6% vs 6.2%, p = 0.051 in its own ITT table (magnesium-trial-abstract-significance-does-not-survive-the-itt-table). ⚠ Attrition 21.5% and DIFFERENTIAL — 25.6% of placebo against 17.0% of magnesium — with no imputation method stated, so the "ITT" is in practice complete-case on an unbalanced remainder. The direction of that bias is genuinely ambiguous and the vault declines to guess (magnesium-trial-dropout-was-differential-by-arm). ⚑ WHY THIS LANDS HERE, STATED RATHER THAN ASSUMED — because the form is wrong and the risk is real. The trial used magnesium OXIDE 500 mg (302 mg elemental), among the least bioavailable oral salts, on a metabolic endpoint, in long-standing type 2 diabetes. This map is named for glycinate and its claim is about mild insomnia. Filing an oxide result under a glycinate map risks exactly the laundering-by-filename this vault refused when it declined to coin a map called hmb. It lands here anyway for two stated reasons: (1) this map already carries general-magnesium evidence — Rawji 2024 is a review of magnesium supplementation, not of the glycinate salt, and Amiri 2026's metabolic null is already here — so in practice it is the vault's magnesium map with a form in its title; (2) coining a third magnesium map off one null trial would be the map proliferation this vault criticises elsewhere. NOTHING TRANSFERS TO THE SLEEP CLAIM. Different salt, different outcome, different population. The map's belief still rests on magnesium-bisglycinate-insomnia-rct (d = 0.2) and magnesium-sleep-quality-weak-signal, and Status is held emerging — a suggestive-capped single RCT confirming an existing null moves nothing. Cross-noted on magnesium-l-threonate so the non-transfer is visible from there. What the map DOES gain: the magnesium case is now visibly only a sleep case.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-08-02 — the exposure-verification contrast, from two of the same night's five sources. This map's new null verified its exposure — ionised and total magnesium both measured, both improved, endpoint still missed. cold-water-immersion's new null did not, and its authors concede the thermal dose may never have reached the muscle (gel-pack-cooling-may-not-deliver-a-thermal-dose-to-deep-muscle). Same night, same direction of result, different evidentiary weight — and the entire difference is whether the intervention was shown to arrive. The vault already holds the mirror-image habit for positive findings: when a number moves, ask whether the thing moved or the ruler did (creatine-lowers-estimated-gfr-without-lowering-measured-gfr). The companion, recorded as a habit and not a finding: when a number does NOT move, ask whether the intervention arrived. Cross-noted on cold-water-immersion.
  • ⚑ 2026-08-02 — the abstract-versus-results-table discrepancy repeated in an unrelated paper the same night, which promotes it from anecdote to reading discipline. This map's new source reports its one significant secondary as p = 0.043 in the abstract and p = 0.051 in its own ITT table (magnesium-trial-abstract-significance-does-not-survive-the-itt-table). resistance-training-programming's new source describes a result as "moderate-certainty evidence" that its own GRADE table rates low (exergaming-abstract-upgrades-its-own-grade-rating). Two papers, two literatures, one night, the same direction of error — the abstract more confident than the results section, by a margin small enough to survive casual reading. This vault reads results sections; the value of that policy is usually invisible, and tonight it was not. Cross-noted on resistance-training-programming.
  • ⚠ 2026-08-02 — a vasoactive adjacency the vault declines to fabricate into an interaction. The newly coined beetroot-juice map holds a nitrate-based vasodilator, and magnesium has its own blood-pressure literature. Neither this trial nor the beetroot source measured the other's outcome — Al-Maqbali found blood pressure flat (systolic p = 0.605, diastolic p = 0.852), and Qin & Kim measured no blood pressure at all. No safety_flags raised, no interaction graded; logged as an open gap on both maps rather than invented. shared/safety.md permits flagging on a credible mechanistic signal — but a mechanism nobody has tested is a gap, not a flag.
  • 2026-08-02 — global ripple across all 45 maps on the glycaemic null: no interaction, no new flag. Walked every other map. The standing oral-magnesium ↔ drug-absorption chelation gap is untouched by this source and stays open in docs/open-gaps-log.md; this trial reported no difference in adverse drug reactions between arms, which is reassuring about tolerability and says nothing about co-administration timing. No Status moved.
  • Tentative synergy with sleep-regularity — evening magnesium may support sleep quality, so it pairs with (does not compete with) the strongest foundation lever. Weak evidence; not a co-recommendation yet.
  • Sleep-supplement cluster with l-theanine and chamomile — low-burden, mostly subjective-evidence sleep levers. At regimen time treat as candidates within one sleep-support cluster, not independent wins; none rivals sleep-regularity as a foundation. Cluster widened 2026-07-07 to include ashwagandha and melatonin — same one-lever, redundant-sedation rule.
  • Mechanistic edge with vitamin-d — magnesium is a cofactor in vitamin D metabolism. Noted, not acted on.
  • Sibling form — magnesium-l-threonate (auto-coined 2026-07-05). A different magnesium compound targeting cognition rather than sleep (single industry-adjacent RCT). Same element: at regimen time treat glycinate and L-threonate as candidate forms of one magnesium lever, not two independent wins. The oral-magnesium absorption interaction below applies to both forms.
  • ⚠ Interaction watch — OPEN GAP (not yet a graded concept). Oral magnesium can reduce absorption of some oral medications (e.g., tetracycline/quinolone antibiotics, thyroid medication, bisphosphonates) by chelation — the standard fix is to separate dosing by several hours. This is real pharmacology, but the run found no vault-quality source to cite (candidates were dated/tangential), so it is logged as an open gap rather than fabricated into a graded safety_flags: [interaction] concept. Provenance rail: flag the gap, don't invent the finding. → resolve by ingesting a proper magnesium drug-interaction review.
  • ⚠ The wind-down cluster's job now has a randomised alternative (new 2026-07-12). cbt-insomnia — 53 RCTs, n=13,608, benefit still present 12 months after the last session (cbt-i-durable-depression-benefit) — is the guideline-grade answer for insomnia, and unlike every substance in this cluster it keeps working after you stop. This map's own sleep signal is small (d≈0.2) and self-reported. No Status change (CBT-I's population is insomnia patients, not healthy sleepers); recorded as the cluster's evidence ranking.
  • ⚠ Not a metabolic intervention — the boundary, stated so it stops being re-litigated (new 2026-07-13). Magnesium's metabolic case (insulin sensitivity, HOMA-IR, blood sugar) is not supported across 15 RCTs in diabetic and pre-diabetic adults, at any dose, over any duration (magnesium-null-on-insulin-resistance). It has no bearing on intermittent-fasting's glucose surrogates or on glp-1's metabolic effects, and it is not a reason to co-recommend it with either. Sibling form magnesium-l-threonate is untouched by this paper (different form, different outcome, still one conflicted trial). The map's entire case remains the small subjective sleep signal, and nothing else.
