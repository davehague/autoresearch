Balance & stability training — exercise

Status: emerging (2026-08-15) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single source, and that source is the largest body of randomised evidence in this vault by an order of magnitude: 423 RCTs, 33,901 participants, 2,546 effect sizes, 19 exercise modalities, in older adults recruited without any disease criterion. Opened at emerging anyway, and the reasons are specific: the outcome is an unblindable surrogate, the headline is a ranking whose intervals overlap, and every quality check a network meta-analysis lives or dies by is behind a captcha. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Almost any structured way of practising balance improves balance in older adults. Stretching is the exception. Which modality is "best" is not established — and the endpoint that matters, falls, has now been moved once, by a bundle nobody has taken apart.

⚑ NEW 2026-08-18 — and the domain now has its first mortality evidence, in which it out-predicted the aerobic test. Wu 2026 gave seven standardised fitness tests to the same 13,423 Taiwanese adults aged 65+ and followed them for a median 7.0 years (1,631 deaths). Comparing best fifth to worst, adjusted for age, sex, BMI, socioeconomic and geographic factors, medical history and self-reported physical activity, the two balance-and-agility tests came first: 8-foot up-and-go AHR 0.41 (95% CI 0.33–0.51) and one-leg stance 0.50 (0.42–0.59), ahead of lower-body strength 0.55 (0.46–0.65) and cardiorespiratory fitness 0.58 (0.49–0.68) (balance-and-agility-outpredict-cardiorespiratory-fitness-for-mortality). A composite of all seven beat every single test — 0.39 (0.32–0.48).

⚠ Status HELD at emerging, and this time the reason is the design rather than the bundle. This is a cohort, and the specific way it can be wrong fits the specific finding uncomfortably well. The 8-foot up-and-go is the most integrative test in the battery — it needs heart, lungs, legs, joints, vision, cognition and vestibular function working at once — so it is exactly the test that would win if what these numbers detect is undiagnosed illness rather than trainable capacity. Subclinical disease degrades gait and balance before it is diagnosed and then kills. The exclusions (dementia, stroke, amputation, catastrophic illness) blunt that pathway; no lag analysis closes it. Being the best predictor and being the best lever are different claims, and this source can only support the first.

⚑ What makes it more than a prediction curiosity is that the randomised half already exists in this map. balance-and-functional-exercise-cuts-actual-falls-and-fall-related-injuries is a cluster-randomised trial in which balance and functional exercise cut real falls and fall-related injuries. The cohort says the capacity predicts death; the trial says the training moves the intermediate endpoint. Neither closes the loop, and together they are the strongest case this map has.

⚑ The plateau is the practically useful half. Splines flattened at roughly 7–8 seconds on the 8-foot up-and-go and 15 repetitions on the chair stand, and most of the mortality differential sat between the bottom fifth and everyone else (fitness-mortality-gains-concentrate-at-the-bottom-and-then-plateau) — the same front-loaded shape zone-2-cardio already records for aerobic volume, arriving from an entirely different kind of measurement.

⚑ NEW 2026-08-16 — the map's founding gap gets stepped around, one day after it was written down. The map was coined on a 423-trial network that could report only "tentative" for reactive balance, the domain that decides whether a stumble becomes a fracture, with no point estimate and no interval for any modality (reactive-balance-is-the-least-evidenced-domain-in-the-largest-balance-network). Tang 2026 does not close that gap — it declines to measure balance at all and measures falls instead. In a cluster-randomised trial across 128 rural Chinese villages, a 12-month village-doctor-led programme of balance and functional exercise plus health education cut any fall from 42.7% to 31.6% (OR 0.60, 95% CI 0.43–0.84), the falls rate from 1.6 to 0.9 per person-year (RR 0.62, 0.43–0.89), and fall-related injury from 24.9% to 15.9% (OR 0.57, 0.42–0.78) (balance-and-functional-exercise-cuts-actual-falls-and-fall-related-injuries).

⚑ The injury estimate is at least as large as the any-fall estimate, which rules out the specific failure mode that would otherwise be the first suspicion — a programme that posts a good fall number by preventing only harmless stumbles. The falls being prevented are not disproportionately the trivial ones.

⚠ And the instrument stayed still while the endpoint moved. Chair stand moved, balance performance moved, quality of life moved — Timed Up and Go did not (falls-fell-while-timed-up-and-go-did-not-move). A trial that cut real falls by roughly 38% failed to shift the mobility instrument most commonly used as a proxy for fall risk. That is a fact about the instrument. It also bounds the map's founding source: if a real fall reduction can pass through an instrument without registering, a 423-trial ranking built on instruments is ranking modalities on something whose link to falling is looser than the ranking implies.

⚠ Status HELD at emerging, and the reason is attribution, not tier. This is randomised evidence on a hard, patient-important endpoint — on tier alone it would argue for an upgrade. It does not get one because the intervention is a bundle: balance exercise plus functional exercise plus health education plus twelve months of contact with a village doctor, against a usual-care comparator that received none of it. Nothing isolates the balance training from the attention. Falls were self-reported in an unblinded trial, the direction of bias that flatters the intervention, and the CVD-restricted analysis is a subgroup whose pre-specification is behind the paywall. multidomain-lifestyle's founding rule — bundles resist being taken apart — applies at full strength. Against usual care the bundle works; what in the bundle works is unanswered.

Eighteen of nineteen modalities beat control (stretching-was-the-only-exercise-modality-that-did-not-improve-balance). The named leaders: perturbation training Hedges' g 0.66 (95% credible interval 0.49–0.82), sensory training 0.55 (0.42–0.68), dance 0.54 (0.45–0.64), agility stepping 0.53 (0.39–0.67). By domain: Tai Chi for global balance, perturbation training for static, Pilates for dynamic, agility stepping or aquatic exercise for proactive. Perturbation training performed best supervised and in higher-fall-risk groups; sensory training performed best delivered at home — the single most actionable line in the source, because it is the only one that answers "what works without a supervisor."

⚠ The ranking is the part of this that will get quoted and the part the evidence supports least. Perturbation's lower bound (0.49) sits below the point estimates of dance (0.54) and agility stepping (0.53). Those four intervals overlap across most of their range; on the published numbers the top four are not distinguishable from one another (balance-modality-rankings-rest-on-overlapping-credible-intervals). The vault has caught this shape twice before — bone-density-network-ranks-sites-where-no-intervention-differed and sarcopenia-exercise-network-muscle-mass-result-is-locally-inconsistent. What survives is permissive rather than restrictive: pick the modality someone will actually do.

⚠ And eighteen of nineteen winning is a warning, not a triumph. Exercise trials cannot blind participants, balance instruments are effort-dependent and mostly clinician-scored, and control arms in this literature are usually no-treatment or education. A network in which almost every arm wins is partly measuring the contrast between doing something and doing nothing — which is what makes stretching's failure to clear even that bar the more informative half of the result. The vault's own small fall literature reproduces the warning at 1/400th the scale: exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator, unstable-rt-reduces-fear-of-falling-not-falls, bfr-balance-gain-is-visually-dependent.

⚑ The inversion that keeps this map at emerging. Static balance — holding still — is what most instruments measure and where the estimates are strongest. Reactive balance — what the body does in the half-second after it is already falling — is what turns a trip on a kerb into a fracture, and it is the one domain the network could only call "tentative," with no point estimate and no interval for any modality (reactive-balance-is-the-least-evidenced-domain-in-the-largest-balance-network). The whole review measures a surrogate, and the link from that surrogate to falls runs through the domain nobody has adequately studied. Four hundred and twenty-three trials, and the outcome closest to what anyone cares about is missing.

⚑ What the stretching null does and does not mean, because it is the sentence most likely to be overread. It does not contradict stretching's supported claim: chronic-static-stretching-improves-lower-limb-flexibility records Hedges' g 0.851 across 79 RCTs for range of motion, and range of motion is not balance. It does not make stretching useless for a rehabilitating joint, where restoring range is the goal being met. What it establishes is narrower and worth having: stretching is a range-of-motion intervention and it is not a stability intervention, and the two should stop being bundled. stretching's Status is held at emerging on exactly that reasoning — a high-tier null on an outcome the map never claimed narrows its scope rather than rebutting its claim.

⚠ For David (GOAL.md: least work for maximum benefit, adherence-first, knee/joint-safe, returning to pickleball, home-based beats supervised on effort): the population is 60+ and he is 43, so no number here transfers. Two structural findings survive the age gap because they are about what the evidence can support, not about how old anyone is. (1) GOAL.md gates his pickleball return on consistent stretching. That gate is about knee range and rehabilitation, which stretching does support — it should not also be counted as stability or fall insurance, because it is not. (2) Pickleball itself sits close to agility stepping (0.53) in this taxonomy — one of the four top-ranked modalities. The sport he wants to return to is, on this evidence, among the better balance interventions available to him. ⚠ Perturbation training deliberately destabilises a person and is delivered supervised in this literature for that reason; it is not a thing to improvise at home on a recovering knee. Nothing enters the regimen from this map.

No fatal flag fires; the authors hedge where hedging is due. Capped suggestive. ⚑ Coined rather than filed under an existing map because balance is a lever domain this vault does not have. It holds resistance-training (strength), zone-2-cardio (aerobic capacity), daily-steps (ambulation), stretching (flexibility), sedentary-behavior (sitting) — and nothing at all on stability, despite holding four scattered fall-and-balance concepts with nowhere to live. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

  • 2026-08-16 — ⚑ THE MAP'S FOUNDING GAP GETS STEPPED AROUND ON DAY TWO, AND THE ENDPOINT MOVES. Ingested Tang 2026 (fall-prevention-rural-older-adults-cvd-family-rct-tang-2026, Age and Ageing, open-label cluster RCT, 128 villages, rural Chinese adults ≥60 with hypertension or heart disease, 12-month village-doctor-led balance + functional exercise + health education vs usual care, recruited Sept–Nov 2023; OUP paywall at the browser rung, appraised abstract-bounded, PDF confirmed non-OA). Any fall 31.6% vs 42.7% — OR 0.60 (0.43–0.84). Falls rate 0.9 vs 1.6 per person-year — RR 0.62 (0.43–0.89). Fall-related injury 15.9% vs 24.9% — OR 0.57 (0.42–0.78) (balance-and-functional-exercise-cuts-actual-falls-and-fall-related-injuries, extends, suggestive). The injury estimate is at least as large as the any-fall estimate, which rules out the "it only prevented harmless stumbles" failure mode. ⚠ Timed Up and Go did not move, while chair stand, balance performance and quality of life did (falls-fell-while-timed-up-and-go-did-not-move, pushes-back, suggestive) — the third time this vault has caught fall instruments and fall outcomes coming apart, and the first time the endpoint is the half that moved. ⚑ Delivered by village doctors inside routine primary care, not trial physiotherapists (fall-prevention-worked-when-delivered-by-existing-primary-care-staff, extends, suggestive). Appraised mixed — a genuinely well-designed cluster RCT reporting the outcome this literature exists to affect, with large coherent effects over twelve months, undercut by self-reported falls in an unblinded trial and by being a subgroup analysis whose pre-specification the paywall hides. No fatal flag fires. Capped suggestive. ⚠ Status HELD at emerging on attribution, not tier: the intervention is a bundle against a usual-care comparator, so nothing isolates the balance training from the attention.

  • 2026-08-18 — ⚑ THE DOMAIN GETS ITS FIRST HARD-ENDPOINT EVIDENCE, AND IT OUT-RANKS THE AEROBIC TEST. Ingested Wu 2026 (physical-fitness-domains-mortality-older-adults-cohort-wu-2026, JAMA Network Open, nationwide prospective linked-record cohort, n = 13,423, community-dwelling Taiwanese adults ≥65, seven administered fitness tests 2015–16 linked to the national death registry to end-2022, median 7.0 y, 1,631 deaths (12.2%); full text read at the browser rung, no commercial funding, conflicts none). Highest vs lowest performance quintile, adjusted for age, sex, BMI, socioeconomic and geographic factors, medical history and self-reported physical activity: 8-foot up-and-go 0.41 (0.33–0.51), one-leg stance 0.50 (0.42–0.59), 30-s chair stand 0.55 (0.46–0.65), 2-minute step test 0.58 (0.49–0.68), 30-s arm curl 0.63 (0.53–0.75), chair sit-and-reach 0.79 (0.67–0.93); composite of all seven 0.39 (0.32–0.48), all P < .001 (balance-and-agility-outpredict-cardiorespiratory-fitness-for-mortality, extends, suggestive). ⚑ The two balance-and-agility tests beat the cardiorespiratory test in the one cohort that measured both in the same people — an ordering this vault's mortality shelf, which was entirely aerobic and volume-based, did not predict. ⚑ Most of the differential sits between the bottom fifth and everyone else, and three tests plateau — ~15 chair-stand repetitions, ~−10 cm back scratch, ~7–8 s up-and-go (fitness-mortality-gains-concentrate-at-the-bottom-and-then-plateau, extends, suggestive). ⚠ Flexibility came last and only mattered up to a threshold (flexibility-predicts-mortality-worst-and-only-up-to-a-threshold, pushes-back, suggestive, filed on stretching) — the second independent hit on flexibility in three days, after the 423-trial balance network. Appraised mixed — prospective, objectively-exposed, objectively-outcomed, activity-adjusted, with large graded effects; held short of reliable by the observational ceiling, smoking entirely unmeasured (the authors concede it), healthy-volunteer sampling at fitness-testing stations, and a single-timepoint exposure. No fatal flag fires — flag #1 requires a small effect with major unadjusted confounders, and these effects are large and monotonic. Capped suggestive. ⚠ Status HELD at emerging on design, not tier: the winning test is the most integrative one in the battery, which is precisely what reverse causation would produce. Best predictor ≠ best lever.
