Berberine — metabolic syndrome (supplements)

Status: emerging (2026-09-26) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from one meta-analysis of placebo-controlled RCTs testing berberine against components of metabolic syndrome.

What we believe now

Berberine improves several cardiometabolic markers — triglycerides, fasting glucose, LDL-C, total cholesterol, waist circumference, BMI, and 2-hour OGTT glucose — but not blood pressure or HDL-C, with no significant safety signal versus placebo in the pooled trials. Effect sizes are real but modest (e.g. fasting glucose −0.515 mmol/L, triglycerides −0.367 mmol/L), and meta-regression suggests short-term treatment (≤90 days) outperforms longer courses for HDL-C/LDL-C. No hard cardiovascular-outcome or mortality trial exists — every finding here is a surrogate metabolic marker.

A known, unsourced open gap. Berberine is pharmacologically established to inhibit CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, which can raise blood levels of co-administered statins, cyclosporine, and other CYP3A4-metabolized drugs. No vault-quality source has been ingested that grades this interaction, so it is logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md rather than fabricated into a safety_flags entry — the same discipline the vault applies to the magnesium/oral-drug-absorption gap on magnesium-glycinate.

Regimen implication. One meta-analysis, real but modest surrogate-marker benefit, no hard outcomes, and an unsourced-but-real drug-interaction question. Does not clear the bar for Protocol placement; a Watch-list candidate at best, pending the interaction gap and a hard-outcome trial.
