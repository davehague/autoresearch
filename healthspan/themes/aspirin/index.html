Aspirin (low-dose) — metabolic & pharmacologic

Status: fading (2026-07-27 — DOWNGRADED from contested, two days after the map was coined, because the safety data the map was explicitly waiting for arrived and closed the question). The vault now holds, from a single 19,114-person double-blind randomised trial: a precisely estimated null on healthspan loss (HR 0.98, 0.92–1.05), a null on cardiovascular events over roughly nine years (HR 1.04, 0.94–1.15), and a quantified harm — major haemorrhage HR 1.24 (1.10–1.39), about 1.7 extra major bleeds per 1,000 person-years.

contested no longer describes it. shared/evidence-model.md defines contested as "a credible higher-tier result pushes back; state the tension, never bury it" — and tension requires two live sides. After this run there is no live positive result for low-dose aspirin as a healthspan or primary-prevention intervention in this population: a null on benefit, a null on the cardiovascular component over a longer horizon, and a harm, all from the same randomisation. fading — "superseded / debunked / gone quiet" — is the honest word.

⚠ A human decision is being informed here, not overridden. docs/backlog.md carries an open item from 2026-07-25 asking whether contested was the right word, noting that "fading is also defensible." That item was a request for a judgement in the absence of safety data. The safety data arrived. The auto run has applied the evidence rule; the backlog item stays open and unticked, because only David closes an item, and because a reviewer may still prefer a different word for a map whose secondary-prevention literature remains un-ingested.

What we believe now

Low-dose aspirin does not extend the healthy part of life in healthy older adults. ASPREE (Zheng 2026, Age and Ageing, secondary analysis) randomised 19,114 community-dwelling adults aged ≥70 — free of cardiovascular disease, dementia and physical disability at entry — to 100 mg/day aspirin or placebo, and followed them a median 4.7 years against a composite of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, dementia, cancer, or death. 5,835 (30.5%) lost healthspan. 82.1 vs 82.7 events per 1,000 person-years. HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.92–1.05) (aspirin-no-effect-on-healthspan-loss-older-adults).

This null is accepted at strong, and the vault's own rules are why. shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 — an underpowered null sold as "no effect" — has fired five separate times on resistance-training-programming and held dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit to suggestive even where the null was convenient. The objection was always power. Here there are 19,114 randomised people, 5,835 hard events, and an interval of 0.92 to 1.05. Declining this null would be the mirror image of accepting Shen 2026's "similar safety profiles" from an interval reaching 11.11. Accepted, and graded accordingly.

⚑ 2026-07-27 — THE LEDGER NOW HAS BOTH COLUMNS, AND THE MAP'S TOP OPEN GAP IS CLOSED. Two days ago this map recorded a null on benefit and then stated, in bold, that it held zero safety data and must not be used for anything until the haemorrhage paper was ingested. The run that wrote that refused to import ASPREE's well-known bleeding findings from memory, because shared/safety.md forbids an unsourced harm claim exactly as it forbids an unsourced benefit claim. That refusal is now paid off with provenance rather than recall (Wolfe 2025, European Heart Journal, extended follow-up of the same trial):

  • Major haemorrhage, in-trial + post-trial: HR 1.24 (1.10–1.39) — 650 events (8.8 per 1,000 person-years) on aspirin vs 537 (7.1) on placebo (aspirin-increases-major-haemorrhage-older-adults).
  • During the randomised, blinded phase the excess was larger: HR 1.38 (1.18–1.62) — the part no observational study could produce and no confounder can explain.
  • After the drug stopped it faded: HR 1.08 (0.91–1.29) (aspirin-bleeding-excess-tracks-exposure-not-history). The harm tracks exposure, not history — a materially different clinical object from a cumulative injury, though the interval reaches 1.29 so "clears completely" is not established.
  • No cardiovascular benefit over roughly nine years: HR 1.04 (0.94–1.15), 741 vs 721 events (aspirin-no-long-term-cardiovascular-benefit). Extending follow-up does not rescue the null — the standard defence of a short primary-prevention trial fails here.

⚠ And the paper's most novel claim is the one this map refuses. A post-trial cardiovascular excess, HR 1.17 (1.01–1.36), drawn from a non-randomised window in a consent-selected subset, with the authors stating they applied no adjustment for the multiple outcomes tested, and the companion post-trial bleeding signal reaching borderline significance only in trial non-adherers (aspirin-post-trial-cardiovascular-excess-is-fragile, graded single-study, open-question). Refusing a barely-significant positive is the same rule this vault applies to underpowered nulls, pointed the other way — and two days ago it accepted two nulls at full strength on exactly that reasoning.

Three things this map does NOT claim, recorded so a future run cannot quietly add them.

  1. It does not claim aspirin is useless. Secondary cardiovascular prevention is a real, guideline-endorsed indication. That literature is un-ingested, so the map holds no view on it. fading describes low-dose aspirin as a healthspan intervention in people who are healthy when they start it — nothing wider.
  2. It no longer claims to know nothing about safety — but the safety it knows is bounded. The bleeding harm is now sourced at randomised tier. It is graded suggestive rather than strong because the paper is a hybrid of a randomised phase and a non-randomised extension and is appraised mixed; the cap describes the paper's design, not doubt about the direction of the harm, and per shared/safety.md harm evidence is treated asymmetrically rather than discounted into silence.
  3. It does not test middle age. Entry age ≥70. Nothing here speaks to a 40- or 50-year-old.

⚑ 2026-08-01 — THE LEDGER GAINS A MITIGATION COLUMN, AND THE COLUMN HAS NO UNITS. The obvious reply to a quantified bleeding harm is "then take a PPI with it," and until now this map could neither confirm nor refuse it, because shared/safety.md forbids importing well-known pharmacology from memory. It is now sourced (Jia 2026, 16 RCTs + 14 non-RCTs), and the answer is genuinely two-sided:

  • Upper GI bleeding: a proton pump inhibitor removes about 70% of it. RR 0.30 (0.19–0.46), 7 RCTs — and the PPI beats H₂-blockers (RR 0.21), mucosal protective agents (RR 0.24) and no-PPI (RR 0.50) (ppi-substantially-reduces-upper-gi-bleeding-in-aspirin-users).
  • ⚠ Lower GI bleeding: PPI use was associated with roughly a TRIPLING — RR 2.85 (1.85–4.38) — on three NON-randomised studies, where people receive acid suppression because a clinician judged them likely to bleed. Confounding by indication is the most parsimonious explanation, and the flag is raised anyway per shared/safety.md's asymmetry rule, graded honestly at single-study (ppi-lower-gi-bleeding-signal-in-aspirin-users-is-unrandomised, safety_flags: [interaction, prescription]).
  • ⚑ And the PPI lost one randomised head-to-head — to potassium-competitive acid blockers — reported as two bare log-rank p-values with no effect size (potassium-competitive-acid-blockers-may-outperform-ppis-on-aspirin-ulcer-bleeding).

⚠ THE OMISSION THAT BOUNDS ALL OF IT: Jia reports no absolute risks anywhere. shared/study-appraisal.md's first universal lens fires cleanly. This map can say, from ASPREE, that aspirin causes about 1.7 extra major bleeds per 1,000 person-years. It cannot say what fraction a PPI would remove, or how many lower-GI bleeds it might add — RR 0.30 and RR 2.85 cannot be weighed without baseline event rates, and none are published. The trade is of unknown sign.

None of this makes aspirin worth taking. Removing part of a cost does not create a benefit where a 19,114-person randomised trial found none. Status holds fading — contested still requires a live positive side, and a mitigation for a harm is not one.

Nothing enters the regimen, and no dose is recommended. Aspirin is pharmacologic, so per shared/safety.md it is clinician-territory only, never ranked into a daily stack. 100 mg/day is recorded as the dose the trial administered, not as advice. The practical content of this map is negative and useful, and it is now complete: if low-dose-aspirin-for-longevity is ever suggested to David, the answer is a 19,114-person randomised null on benefit AND a quantified bleeding harm from the same 19,114 people. As of 2026-07-25 that sentence had to end "a bleeding risk this vault cannot yet quantify." It doesn't any more.

What the trial DID find is that the modifiable predictors were the boring ones. Within the same cohort, healthspan loss was more likely in participants who were older, male, smokers, hypertensive or frail (healthspan-loss-predictors-in-randomised-cohort) — graded suggestive, because that analysis is observational inside a randomised trial and reports direction only. The authors' own closing sentence: "Future studies should explore lifestyle and behavioural interventions to extend healthspan."
