Sensory impairment as a dementia/cognition risk marker — sensory

Status: emerging (2026-11-22) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's third Sensory-domain lever, alongside hearing-aids and vision-nutrition — but a different kind of lever from both. Those two are intervention maps (does a hearing aid help; does a nutritional supplement slow glaucoma). This map holds vision and hearing impairment themselves as risk markers for dementia and cognitive impairment — a condition/exposure map, structurally like sleep-duration or the newly-coined insomnia, not an efficacy map. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Across a 62-study, 14-country pooled analysis, dual sensory impairment (vision + hearing) carries the largest dementia association (OR 1.66), ahead of visual impairment alone (OR 1.60) and hearing impairment alone (OR 1.26) — the first time this vault has quantified vision loss and dual loss against the same dementia outcome as hearing loss, which until now was the only sensory-dementia association this vault held a number for. The same ordering (dual > visual > hearing) repeats for the softer cognitive-impairment outcome at larger magnitudes but with substantially more heterogeneity and a real publication-bias signal. None of this tests whether correcting vision or hearing changes dementia risk — that is hearing-aids's question, and its own Cochrane-tier answer there is "where this literature is precise it finds nothing; where it finds something it is imprecise." This map is upstream of that one: it establishes that the risk marker is real and quantifiable, not that fixing it helps.
