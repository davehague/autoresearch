Sleep duration — sleep

Status: emerging (2026-07-09) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined by a 79-cohort meta-analysis putting a mortality number on habitual sleep duration. Opened emerging and deliberately positioned beneath sleep-regularity: the association is real, but it is measured with a worse instrument (self-report vs accelerometry), it is smaller, and its scarier tail is probably measuring illness rather than causing death.

What we believe now

Sleep duration follows a U-shaped mortality curve, and neither tail says what people think it says. Pooling 79 cohort studies, habitual short sleep (<7 h/night) carried all-cause mortality HR 1.14 (95% CI 1.10–1.18) and long sleep (≥9 h/night) carried HR 1.34 (1.26–1.42), both against a 7–8 h reference; the long-sleep association was stronger in women (1.44) than men (1.36). Read carefully, these are two different findings with two different levels of trust:

(1) The short-sleep tail is a modest, credible association. HR 1.14 with moderate heterogeneity (I²=53%). A ~14% relative increase is small in absolute terms, sits inside the range residual confounding can move, and rests on self-reported sleep — but it is directionally stable across dozens of cohorts. "At least ~7 hours" is a defensible floor.

(2) The long-sleep tail is most likely reverse causation. HR 1.34 with I²=92%. Sleeping ≥9 hours is a well-established downstream marker of undiagnosed disease, frailty, depression, and inflammation — all of which cause both the long sleep and the death. The meta-analysis reports no lag analyses and no exclusion of early deaths, so it cannot separate "long sleep kills" from "dying people sleep more." Nothing here supports restricting sleep. The evidence-consistent action is diagnostic: a persistent need for ≥9 hours is a reason to ask why — a clinician conversation, never a protocol item (shared/safety.md).

(3) The self-report objection is now half-answered — and it lands better than expected (new 2026-07-23). The whole map has carried a measurement penalty because its exposure is self-reported. Chaput 2026 measured 76,811 UK Biobank adults both ways at once — 7-day wrist accelerometry and a questionnaire, same people, same follow-up — and found that self-report gets the curve shape and the optimum roughly right, and the effect size badly wrong. All metrics traced the same inverse-J; the optimum sat at ~7.2 h by self-report against ~7.7 h by device sleep period time. But at 7 h versus a 5 h reference, mortality was HR 0.70 (0.61–0.79) on device sleep period time and only HR 0.86 (0.74–1.00) on self-report — an upper bound sitting exactly on the null (self-reported-sleep-duration-preserves-shape-loses-magnitude). That is textbook measurement-error attenuation, quantified in one population. Consequence for this map: its founding cohorts are less wrong about where the curve bends than the vault assumed, and every self-reported estimate it holds is probably an under-estimate. It does not rescue the long-sleep tail — no early-death exclusion or lag analysis is reported — so (2) above stands untouched.

Why this map sits below sleep-regularity. The vault already holds a better sleep claim, measured better. sleep-regularity-predicts-mortality used objective wrist accelerometry in ~61,000 adults and found the Sleep Regularity Index out-predicted sleep duration for mortality, with the most-regular sleepers showing 20–48% lower all-cause mortality — roughly triple this map's short-sleep effect, on the consistency axis, with a real instrument. Duration is the weaker lever, measured worse.

For David (GOAL.md: adherence-first, nothing that disrupts sleep, least work for maximum benefit), the practical translation is short: anchor consistent sleep and wake times; let ~7 hours be the floor that falls out of doing so, rather than a number to chase. Chasing a duration target is the kind of health-as-a-second-job optimization GOAL.md explicitly rejects — and the regularity lever raises duration as a side effect anyway.

Why emerging, not higher: the design ceiling. Sleep duration cannot be randomized, the exposure is self-reported across nearly every cohort, retrospective cohorts are pooled with prospective ones, the short-sleep effect is small, and the long-sleep effect has an unexcluded rival explanation. The same observational ceiling that holds sleep-regularity, daily-steps, and resistance-training at emerging holds here — with an extra measurement penalty those maps don't pay.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-09 — Ingested Ungvari 2025 (GeroScience, meta-analysis of 79 cohort studies, searched to Oct 2024): short sleep <7 h → all-cause mortality HR 1.14 (1.10–1.18), I²=53%; long sleep ≥9 h → HR 1.34 (1.26–1.42), I²=92%; sex-stratified (long sleep: women 1.44, men 1.36). Auto-coins the map at emerging — a U-shaped association on a hard endpoint, from self-reported exposure, with reverse causation unexcluded on the long-sleep tail. Editorial-proximity flag recorded (first author is the journal's Editor-in-Chief).

  • 2026-07-23 — Ingested Chaput 2026 (SLEEP, UK Biobank prospective cohort, adults 40–69 who both wore a 7-day wrist accelerometer and self-reported habitual sleep; up to 76,811 analysed for mortality, 69,153 for CVD; mean follow-up 8.0 ± 0.9 y; outcomes all-cause mortality, incident CVD, T2D, dementia, depression). The vault's own instrument objection gets tested head-to-head in the same people, and comes back split. Shape survives: all three metrics traced a similar inverse J (U-shaped for depression), optima ~7.2 h self-report / ~7.7 h sleep period time / a wide 6.8–9.3 h total sleep time. Magnitude does not: at 7 h vs a 5 h reference, sleep period time HR 0.70 (0.61–0.79), total sleep time HR 0.83 (0.78–0.89), self-report HR 0.86 (0.74–1.00) — the last touching the null (self-reported-sleep-duration-preserves-shape-loses-magnitude). And the sleep window out-predicted time actually asleep; the authors suggest sleep period time "may be more relevant than total sleep time for future device-based recommendations" (sleep-period-time-outperforms-total-sleep-time-for-mortality). Appraised mixed — full text paywalled, so registration, funding, conflicts, covariates and sensitivity analyses are all UNVERIFIED; reverse causation is not addressed in anything retrievable; UK Biobank healthy-volunteer bias; exposure measured once over a week for an 8-year follow-up. Capped suggestive. Status held emerging, and the ordering beneath sleep-regularity is unchanged — arguably reinforced, since the winning metric is the one closest to a timing/opportunity variable.

  • 2026-07-25 — Ingested Duan 2026 (Obesity Reviews, systematic review, 30 studies — 13 RCTs + 17 quasi-experimental, n=10,944 adults with BMI ≥25, 13 countries, registered a priori on OSF (osf.io/mvd5x), PRISMA 2020, duplicate screening/extraction/RoB, RU-SATED framework, no meta-analysis possible; conflict-free, no commercial funding; appraised mixed) as the map's first body-composition thread — and its first finding that is about WHAT you lose rather than how much. In the two RCTs that randomised sleep restriction alongside calorie restriction, restricted sleep produced consistently reduced fat-mass loss, one also with significantly lower total percentage weight loss (sleep-restriction-reduces-fat-loss-during-calorie-restriction, safety_flags: [interaction], noted in intermittent-fasting and glp-1). ⚑ This pairs with a finding graded three days earlier from a completely independent literature: Deller 2026 found adding exercise to calorie restriction preserves +0.87 kg of fat-free mass without costing weight loss (exercise-preserves-fat-free-mass-during-calorie-restriction). Two literatures, two co-factors, one rule: during an energy deficit, what you do alongside the deficit determines which tissue you give up. ⚠ The observational 90% of the review is much weaker and is vote-counted: sleep satisfaction positive in 56% of studies, sleep efficiency in 7 of 12 (58%), sleep duration mixed and sign-reversing (higher baseline duration predicted less weight loss in physical-activity-only interventions), alertness null — reported as proportions of studies because pooling was impossible, unweighted by sample size (n=10 to n=2,990) or by risk of bias, with healthy-adherer bias unexcluded (sleep-efficiency-satisfaction-weight-loss-evidence-is-vote-counted). ⚠ Corpus quality is poor by the authors' own assessment — 11 serious + 1 critical risk of bias among the non-randomised studies, all studies at least moderate, selective-reporting concerns in 11 of 13 RCTs — and sleep was mostly self-reported, which Chaput 2026 showed five days ago makes such estimates understatements (self-reported-sleep-duration-preserves-shape-loses-magnitude). ⚠ Non-independence recorded: senior author Chaput also authored that paper and one of this review's own included studies. extends; Status held emerging; sleep still counted once. No sleep target prescribed. OA PDF archived.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠ INTERACTION (new 2026-07-25) — short sleep during a deliberate energy deficit shifts the loss away from fat, and it is graded in three maps. Two RCTs that randomised sleep restriction alongside calorie restriction both found reduced fat-mass loss (sleep-restriction-reduces-fat-loss-during-calorie-restriction, safety_flags: [interaction], noted here and in intermittent-fasting and glp-1). Efficacy-defeating rather than hazardous — the same class as cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy and evening-fasted-exercise-followed-by-higher-24h-energy-intake. Raised on two trials because shared/safety.md makes interaction evidence asymmetric (a missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious flag); it enters no benefit score. This is the second schedule-versus-weight-loss interaction in five days, and neither is yet in the regimen — logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md for the next refresh's across-protocol check.
  • ⚑ The two-source rule on body composition during a deficit (new 2026-07-25). fat-free-mass holds Deller 2026: exercise alongside calorie restriction preserves +0.87 kg fat-free mass — about half the loss — without costing weight loss, with meta-regression rejecting the obvious confound (exercise-preserves-fat-free-mass-during-calorie-restriction, mixed-and-strength-training-outrank-endurance-for-preserving-lean-mass). This map now holds the sleep half. Neither is about how much weight comes off; both are about which tissue. Directly relevant to glp-1's documented lean-mass cost (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude, tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss) — and the sleep term is free.
  • ⚑ 2026-07-24 — a measurement result that reframes this map's own instrument comparison. sleep-regularity ingested Paquet 2026 (MESA), which decomposed 39 objective and subjective sleep variables into 13 substantially independent dimensions covering ~71% of variance (sleep-is-a-multidimensional-construct-not-a-single-metric). This map recorded yesterday that sleep-period-time out-predicted total sleep time for mortality (sleep-period-time-outperforms-total-sleep-time-for-mortality) and read that as an argument for sleep-regularity's primacy. Paquet adds the honest qualifier: that was a race between two slices of a thirteen-dimensional construct, not a contest to represent sleep. The reading stands, at lower confidence. Paquet's cognitive results were also cross-sectional and tiny (β ≈ 0.05 per SD) with the longitudinal signal lost to correction, so nothing here strengthens a duration→cognition claim either. No Status move; sleep still counted once.
  • Subordinate to sleep-regularity — same domain, weaker claim, worse instrument. Count sleep once. Regularity out-predicts duration for mortality on objective accelerometry, and anchoring sleep/wake times raises duration as a by-product. At regimen time these are one sleep-foundation lever, not two: the actionable item is regularity, and duration is the floor it produces. Do not double-count them, and do not present a duration target as an independent intervention.
  • ⚠ Eroded by caffeine-timing — the same interaction that gates sleep regularity. Late caffeine reduces total sleep time, efficiency, and deep sleep (Gardiner 2023 meta-analysis), which pushes directly toward the <7 h tail of this map's curve. Flagged on caffeine-evening-cutoff-for-sleep (safety_flags: [interaction]) and noted in caffeine-timing and sleep-regularity; fixable by timing (coffee ≥~8.8 h before bed).
  • Recalibrates melatonin — puts its effect size in perspective. Melatonin's pooled benefit is +12.4 minutes of sleep, in cognitively impaired elders (melatonin-modest-sleep-cognition-gain-in-impairment). Against a 7-hour floor, twelve minutes is noise. This map is the reason melatonin is framed as a short-term circadian tool, not as a way to buy sleep duration. Same logic applies to the wind-down cluster — l-theanine, chamomile, magnesium-glycinate, ashwagandha — whose evidence is for subjective sleep quality, not duration or mortality. None of them are duration levers; none should be sold as one.
  • Complementary with the exercise levers. daily-steps and zone-2-cardio improve sleep quality in the wider literature and are the vault's other lowest-burden foundation behaviours; no conflict, no redundancy, and no interaction. cold-water-immersion shows a sleep-quality benefit (cold-water-immersion-stress-sleep-wellbeing) on a subjective measure — again quality, not duration.
  • ⚠ Watch (thin, over-cautiously flagged): high-dose creatine and sleep disturbance. The 20 g/day CABA pilot lists sleep disturbance among its mild adverse events (creatine-20g-raises-brain-creatine, safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]). The base is an uncontrolled AE tally in 20 dementia patients — far too thin for a graded interaction concept — but GOAL.md names "nothing that disrupts sleep" as a hard constraint, and shared/safety.md says a missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious flag. Logged here and on creatine-cognition as a watch item, and recorded as an open gap. It is one more reason the vault's creatine answer stays at ~5 g/day.
  • ⚠ daily-steps may defend the ~7 h floor this map sets (new 2026-07-12, watch item). Liu 2026 (8 RCTs, n=683) found walking → +34.23 min total sleep time, device-measured (walking-improves-objective-sleep-duration-and-wakefulness). This map's short-sleep tail (HR 1.14 below 7 h, short-sleep-under-7h-mortality-risk) is the credible half of its U-curve, and +34 minutes is a meaningful fraction of the gap for someone sitting just under the floor. Not graded, and not counted twice: the objective effect rests on 2–3 trials, and this map's exposure is self-reported throughout while walking's is not — the two literatures are not measured on the same instrument. Logged, watched.
  • Global-ripple / safety: no other interaction found. Duration is not a substance and interacts with nothing pharmacologically. Its only real edges are the ones above: the caffeine timing gate, the redundancy with sleep regularity, and the sedative-cluster framing correction.
  • ⚠ Non-independence flag on this run's ingest (new 2026-07-23). Chaput 2026 is one of two UK Biobank accelerometry-sub-sample analyses ingested the same night, alongside device-measured-movement-postures-cancer-mitchell-2026 on sedentary-behavior. They share four authors (Biswas, Ahmadi, Cistulli, Stamatakis), the same cohort and the same accelerometry processing pipeline. Different exposures and different outcomes, so neither is redundant — but they draw on overlapping participants, and where they agree (notably that device-measured behaviour beats self-report) that is precision, not independent replication — the same distinction the vault drew between SELECT and the Nong network meta-analysis on glp-1.
  • ⚑ The sleep-period-time result quietly argues FOR sleep-regularity — recorded as a reading, not a finding (new 2026-07-23). Sleep period time is largely a function of when you go to bed and when you get up — an opportunity/timing variable — whereas total sleep time is contaminated by fragmentation, itself a marker of illness. The metric that won is the one closest to regularity; the metric that lost is the one most polluted by health status. Nothing in this source tests that interpretation, and it never compares sleep period time against the Sleep Regularity Index directly. Logged as a gap, deliberately not graded (sleep-period-time-outperforms-total-sleep-time-for-mortality).
  • ⚠ Exercise does not reliably buy you more sleep TIME, and the largest analysis does not change that (new 2026-07-19). Liang 2026 (200 RCTs, n=23,523) found exercise improved objective sleep EFFICIENCY in the 20 trials measuring it, consistent with subjective outcomes (exercise-sleep-objective-and-subjective-converge-weakly) — efficiency, not duration. That neither confirms nor rescues Liu 2026's device-measured +34 min total sleep time, which Zhou's 31-RCT network already failed to corroborate (exercise-does-not-increase-total-sleep-time). So the ~7 h floor this map sets is still defended by nothing in the exercise literature, and the benefit that does exist concentrates in poor sleepers (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers). Sleep is still counted once, beneath sleep-regularity.
