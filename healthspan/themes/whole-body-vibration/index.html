Whole-body vibration — bone density (exercise)

Status: emerging (2026-09-26) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from one meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,447) on whole-body vibration (WBV) training and bone mineral density in elderly adults. Distinct modality from resistance-training (a passive mechanical-stimulus platform, not a loaded exercise) and adjacent to exercise-for-bone-health's broader modality-comparison question — not merged with it because this map's only source treats WBV as a standalone comparator against "conventional exercise" as a whole, not as one modality inside that network.

What we believe now

Whole-body vibration training raises bone mineral density at some skeletal sites in older adults, not others, and every result to date is a surrogate (DXA), not a fracture or fall outcome. The founding meta-analysis (14 RCTs, n=1,447; good TESTEX reporting quality, 9.5/10; but 79% "some concerns" on RoB 2) found significant BMD gains at the Ward's triangle, greater trochanter, femoral neck, and lumbar spine L2–L4, and no significant effect at the total hip or lumbar spine L1–L4.

The site-inconsistent pattern is the honest read: this is a real but partial signal, not a clean uniform skeletal benefit — and with no hard fracture or fall-incidence outcome anywhere in the literature yet, it cannot be weighed against resistance-training or exercise-for-bone-health's more established modalities on the outcome that actually matters.

Regimen implication. One meta-analysis, surrogate-only, majority "some concerns" risk of bias — this does not clear the bar for a Protocol or even Watch-list placement yet. Logged as an open question, not a recommendation.
