Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) — sleep

Status: emerging (2026-07-12) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined by a PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis of 53 RCTs (n=13,608) in Sleep Medicine Reviews showing that CBT-I's effect on depressive symptoms persists 3, 6 and 12 months after treatment ends. It is the vault's first randomised, drug-free sleep intervention — and the only intervention anywhere in the vault whose benefit is measured after you stop doing it.

It opens at emerging for one blunt reason: the vault has ingested a paper about the wrong outcome. This source measures depression. CBT-I's sleep efficacy — the reason clinical guidelines rank it first-line for chronic insomnia, ahead of hypnotics — is not sourced in this vault at all. A map whose headline claim is unsourced does not get a tier for the strength of its secondary evidence. Fixing that is the map's top open gap.

What we believe now

A time-limited course of behavioural therapy produces a sleep-domain benefit that is still measurable a year later — and that is a shape nothing else in this vault has. Across 53 randomised trials, CBT-I beat control conditions on depressive symptoms at 3 months (d = −0.35, −0.49 to −0.21), 6 months (−0.32, −0.59 to −0.05) and 12 months (−0.25, −0.39 to −0.12), decaying steadily but never vanishing (cbt-i-durable-depression-benefit).

⚠ Updated 2026-07-13 — the sleep evidence arrives, and it is bundled. The map was coined with a blunt admission: this vault has ingested a paper about the wrong outcome — Fiori measured depression, not sleep — and docs/open-gaps-log.md tasked the next sweep with going to fetch CBT-I's actual sleep evidence. The sweep came back with Zhou 2026 (network meta-analysis, 31 RCTs, n=2,457 insomnia patients), and there is no CBT-alone node in the network. Every CBT arm is CBT plus exercise or qigong.

What the vault now knows, and it is real: exercise + CBT beats conventional control on subjective sleep quality — PSQI MD −2.80 (−4.15 to −1.45), at MODERATE CINeMA certainty — plus sleep onset latency (−6.95 min) and wake after sleep onset (−32.62 min) (exercise-plus-cbt-improves-sleep-in-insomnia). That is the highest certainty any sleep effect has achieved anywhere in this vault, and its PSQI effect is roughly twice the size of anything on the supplement shelf. The best-evidenced sleep intervention here is a skill plus a walk, and there is nothing to buy.

What the vault still does not know: what CBT-I does on its own. The bundled arm cannot answer a question about the component (cbt-sleep-effect-not-isolable-no-cbt-alone-node) — the identical attribution failure that stopped the vault inferring creatine's effect from a creatine+beta-alanine arm (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy) and a probiotic's from a protein+probiotic arm (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance). Three times in three days, the same structural trap: trialists bundle, and bundling destroys attribution.

Hold #1 narrows; it does not lift. Hold #2 (population) is reconfirmed — Zhou's patients are diagnosed insomniacs too. Status stays emerging, and the gap is rewritten: CBT-I as a standalone intervention, sleep as the primary outcome, ideally in adults without a formal insomnia diagnosis.

One thing did change, though, and it sharpens the trigger below. If sleep ever does become a problem to treat, the vault's own best evidence now says the answer is not "CBT-I" but "CBT-I and the exercise you are already doing" — which is the cheapest possible upgrade to a recommendation.

⚠ Updated 2026-07-15 — the consensus arrives, without the number. The sweep was tasked with fetching CBT-I's isolated sleep evidence, and it came back with Yan 2026 — a JBI evidence summary of 28 documents (5 guidelines, 3 consensus statements, 12 systematic reviews, 8 meta-analyses). It closes the attribution half of the gap: this is CBT-I as its own multicomponent therapy, cleanly de-bundled from exercise, and it affirms — from guideline consensus — that CBT-I improves the sleep outcomes it targets, with ~7–8 of 10 patients responding, durably, minimal side effects (cbt-i-first-line-sleep-guideline-consensus). The vault's first source that says CBT-I's isolated sleep efficacy is real. It does not close the magnitude half: it is a narrative summary that reports no pooled effect size — only interpretive benchmarks (an ISI change ~8.4 = moderate improvement; sleep efficiency <80% cutoff), which are how to read a result, not a measured CBT effect (cbt-i-sleep-effect-size-not-pooled-here). A map held down by a missing magnitude cannot be lifted by a source that supplies consensus without magnitude. Status stays emerging; population is chronic-insomnia patients again. The gap is rewritten one more turn: a primary meta-analysis of CBT-I alone, sleep as the pooled primary outcome, ideally in adults without a formal insomnia diagnosis.

⚠ Updated 2026-07-21 — the magnitude gap PARTIALLY closes: a de-bundled CBT-I-alone number arrives, and it is a real-world one. Öst 2025 (Cognitive Behaviour Therapy, 32 studies, n=5,231, CBT-I in routine clinical care) is the first source to give CBT-I as its own therapy an actual effectiveness magnitude — the thing Yan's consensus omitted and Zhou's bundled network could not isolate: remission 45% post-treatment, 51% at ~12-month follow-up, with within-group insomnia-severity Hedges g 1.87 / 1.68 (cbt-i-effectiveness-in-routine-care). Nearly half of insomnia patients remit and stay remitted a year on, in ordinary clinics — the durability a skill (not a dose) should show. But the vault takes the number for what it is: these are within-group (uncontrolled) effect sizes — the paper's whole design benchmarks real-world effectiveness against controlled efficacy — so g ≈ 1.87 is not a placebo-controlled effect, and the remission rates are the honest currency (cbt-i-routine-care-effect-sizes-within-group). And the population is diagnosed insomnia patients again. So the magnitude half is partially, not fully, closed — what still binds is a controlled pooled sleep-continuity estimate and evidence in adults without an insomnia diagnosis — and the population hold is untouched, so Status stays emerging. The full text was paywalled (pdf_unavailable), so per-outcome pooling and risk-of-bias detail could not be verified. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive.

Two threads, and the second is the one that governs.

(1) The evidence is the best in the sleep domain — on an outcome the domain wasn't asking about. Every other sleep item here is a substance, and every one of them has had a bad month: l-theanine's highest-tier synthesis (31 RCTs, Molecular Psychiatry) pools no sleep endpoint at all; chamomile's benefit carries I² = 88%; melatonin's +12 minutes was measured in cognitively impaired elders; magnesium-glycinate's signal is small and self-reported. The domain's strongest lever, sleep-regularity, is observational. CBT-I is randomised, replicated, free of pharmacology, and — the part that matters — a skill, not a dose. Substances stop working the day you stop taking them. This didn't.

(2) The population is patients, and David is not one — which is the binding constraint. All 53 trials enrolled adults with insomnia (cbt-i-population-is-insomnia-patients). The vault holds no evidence that CBT-I does anything for someone who already sleeps adequately, and it will not extrapolate one. This is the same hold the vault placed on melatonin (gains in impaired elders) and ashwagandha (gains in young athletes): population match, not evidence tier, decides. And the costs are real — a multi-week structured course whose sleep-restriction component characteristically makes people feel worse before better. Against GOAL.md (least work, least discomfort, low daily maintenance), that is a bad trade for a person with no presenting complaint.

So the honest placement is the Watch list, with a trigger written down in advance: if sleep stops being a foundation to protect and becomes a problem to treat, CBT-I is the first thing in this vault to reach for — the only sleep intervention with randomised evidence, the only one that doesn't require buying anything, and the only one whose benefit survives stopping. Knowing that before it is needed is exactly what a research ledger is for.

Why emerging, not consolidating: it would be easy to argue otherwise — 53 RCTs, 13,608 participants, three independent time horizons, no live rebuttal. The vault declines, for the reason it declined on urolithin-a and on glp-1's mortality claim: a Status is set by what the vault can actually source for the claim the map is about. Here that claim is sleep, and the vault has ingested one paper, about depression. Add to that a mixed reliability verdict — pooled effects reported only after outlier removal with no unfiltered estimate, unblindable trials with a self-reported endpoint, and control conditions never characterized — and emerging is where it belongs until the sleep literature is actually read in.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-07-24 — a measurement caveat that lands on this map's outcome instrument. sleep-regularity ingested Paquet 2026 (MESA), which showed "sleep quality" requires ~13 dimensions and that no single metric represents the construct (sleep-is-a-multidimensional-construct-not-a-single-metric). This map's headline magnitudes — the PSQI MD −2.80 exercise-plus-CBT effect and Öst's within-group remission rates — are measured on one questionnaire slice. That does not weaken the direction or the moderate-certainty finding, and remission is a clinical status rather than a metric. It does mean a PSQI point is a narrower thing than the phrase "sleep quality" suggests, and the vault records it here so the comparison against the supplement shelf's PSQI −0.70 is read as one dimension, compared like for like — which is still the fair comparison. No Status move; no safety_flags.
  • Global-ripple: no safety interaction with any vault intervention. CBT-I is behavioural. It has no pharmacology, so it cannot interact with magnesium-glycinate, melatonin, l-theanine, chamomile, creatine-monohydrate, or any prescription item. There is nothing to buy, no dose, no third-party-testing question, and no safety_flags — on GOAL.md's terms the cleanest profile an intervention can have. Its cost is time and discomfort, not risk.
  • ⚠ It would REPLACE, not stack with, the wind-down cluster — and that is the point. melatonin, l-theanine, chamomile and magnesium-glycinate are all aimed at the same job (get to sleep, stay asleep) on far weaker evidence, and the vault has already flagged that cluster as internally redundant. CBT-I is the guideline-grade answer to that job. If sleep ever becomes a live problem, the correct move is not to add a fifth item to the cluster — it is to reach for the one intervention with 53 randomised trials behind it. Recorded on this map; the redundancy note stands on the others.
  • ⚠ It now composes with the exercise Foundations, and that is where its best sleep evidence actually lives (new 2026-07-13). The only CBT arms with vault-sourced sleep evidence are CBT + exercise (exercise-plus-cbt-improves-sleep-in-insomnia) — which means the trigger written into this map is now cheaper to act on than it was: David already has the exercise half (daily-steps, zone-2-cardio, resistance-training are all Foundations). If sleep ever becomes a problem to treat, the move is to add the therapy to the walk he is already taking, not to start from zero — and not to buy anything. Cross-linked to daily-steps.
  • ⚠ And the free intervention keeps beating the shelf on the shelf's own instrument (new 2026-07-13). On PSQI, exercise+CBT lands −2.80 at moderate certainty, and walking alone lands g = −0.76 (daily-steps, though at GRADE very low with publication bias at p < 0.001). Neither is bankable for a healthy sleeper, and the vault promotes neither — but together they are one more reason the wind-down cluster (melatonin, l-theanine, chamomile, magnesium-glycinate) stays exactly where it is: on the Watch list, unbought.
  • Sits above sleep-regularity on evidence design, beneath it on relevance today. Sleep regularity is observational but applies to David now, as a foundation to protect. CBT-I is randomised but applies only if something breaks. Complementary, not competing: regularity is the maintenance; CBT-I is the repair. Neither is double-counted.
  • Adjacent to the caffeine-timing gate. Stimulus control and caffeine cutoffs are components of standard CBT-I protocols in the wider literature — but this source does not decompose the intervention, so the vault grades no component claim and invents no protocol. Logged, not fabricated.
  • The behavioural sibling to purpose-in-life and sedentary-behavior: free, unbuyable, zero-interaction, and unrankable against supplements on cost. The vault's best-evidenced items keep turning out to be the ones with nothing to sell.
  • ⚠ The supplement shelf finally gets a number, and the gap is FOUR to one (new 2026-07-19). This map has carried "exercise+CBT's PSQI effect is roughly twice the size of anything on the supplement shelf" since 2026-07-13. dietary-supplements-sleep-quality-meta-mei-2025 pooled 28 RCTs of the entire shelf — tryptophan, L-theanine, GABA, magnesium, zinc, vitamin D, omega-3, ashwagandha — on the same instrument: PSQI MD −0.70 (−1.37 to −0.03), whose entire interval sits below the ~3-point MID (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold). Against exercise+CBT's −2.80 at MODERATE certainty, that is four times, not two — the vault was under-stating it. The "don't add a fifth item to the wind-down cluster, reach for CBT-I plus the walk" trigger written into this map is now quantitatively stronger, and it still costs nothing to buy.
  • ⚠ Liang 2026 supplies the population half of this map's own hold (new 2026-07-19). This map is held at emerging partly because all its evidence is in insomnia patients and it will not extrapolate to a healthy sleeper (cbt-i-population-is-insomnia-patients). Liang 2026 (200 RCTs, n=23,523) now shows, on the exercise side of the same question, that benefit concentrates in poor sleepers and largely disappears in good ones — "benefit-on-demand" (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers). Two independent literatures, same structure: these are treatment interventions, and David has nothing to treat. The trigger stands; the population hold is now corroborated rather than merely asserted. Cross-linked to daily-steps.
