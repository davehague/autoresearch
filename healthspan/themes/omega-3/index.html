Omega-3 (fish oil) — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-07-06) — three honestly-bounded threads: a single post-hoc RCT shows omega-3 slowing epigenetic-aging clocks (surrogate, small effect); a high-tier meta-analysis of RCTs returns a null on global cognition; and a new meta-analysis of 42 RCTs shows a cardiovascular hard-outcome benefit — but only in high-risk / established-CVD populations, and paired with an atrial-fibrillation harm. A lever opened low on the ladder, with the healthy-adult primary-prevention case still unestablished.

What we believe now

Two threads, both honestly bounded. (1) Biological-aging surrogate: omega-3 (1 g/day) may modestly slow DNA-methylation aging clocks — the only vault evidence is a post hoc analysis of the DO-HEALTH RCT (n=777 with methylation data), where omega-3 alone slowed three next-gen clocks (PhenoAge, GrimAge2, DunedinPACE) by ~2.9–3.8 months over 3 years, additive with vitamin D + exercise on PhenoAge. A small effect on a surrogate endpoint from a single trial's post hoc analysis. (2) Cognition — now a high-tier null: a 2025 meta-analysis of 11 placebo-controlled RCTs in cognitively-unimpaired older adults found no effect on global cognition (SMD −0.02, 95% CI −0.07 to 0.04). So on the functional cognitive outcome, pooled RCT evidence says no benefit. (3) Cardiovascular hard outcomes — benefit, but population-restricted and with a harm: a 2025 meta-analysis of 42 RCTs (176,253 participants) found omega-3 significantly reduced CV mortality, CVD, CHD, MI, fatal MI, and revascularization — in people with established CVD or high CV risk (secondary prevention) — while significantly increasing atrial fibrillation and GI adverse events. A subgroup signal favored EPA monotherapy over EPA+DHA.

(4) The cognition null's last excuse is gone (new 2026-07-23). The standing defence of thread (2) has always been a delivery story — the dose was too low, the participants were replete, or APOE ε4 carriers fail to transport DHA into the brain. Yassine 2026 was built to test exactly that: a registered, NIA-funded, double-blind 24-month RCT gave 2 g/day DHA to 365 non-demented adults aged 55–80 selected for low dietary DHA (<200 mg/day), stratified by ε4, and measured cerebrospinal fluid. Delivery worked, decisively and independently of ε4 — CSF DHA/AA ratio difference 0.19 (0.16–0.21), p < 0.0001 (dha-reaches-the-brain-regardless-of-apoe4). Two years later there were no differences in cognition and no differences in brain volumes (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit). The vault is strict about what this is: those were secondary endpoints in a trial powered for a biochemical primary, with 38% dropout — an underpowered null, and this ledger has refused five of those on the resistance-training literature, so it refuses to bank this one as equivalence too. What it does is remove the excuse from the well-powered null the map already holds. Cognition is now bounded at both ends: no benefit at the top tier, and no delivery story underneath it.

(5) Bleeding — the map's oldest un-sourced note becomes a graded finding, and it is mostly reassuring (new 2026-07-27). Since 2026-07-06 this map carried a clinician-note warning that higher-dose omega-3 "can potentiate anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy," marked explicitly as well-established pharmacology the vault had no source for, with instructions not to fabricate a graded concept. Javaid 2024 (11 randomised trials, 120,643 patients) supplies the source, and the generic worry does not survive it: no overall increase in bleeding, rate ratio 1.09 (0.91–1.31) — 2.6% vs 2.4%, an absolute difference of 0.07% — with haemorrhagic stroke, intracranial and gastrointestinal bleeding all individually non-significant (omega3-no-overall-increase-in-bleeding-risk). The exception is dose, not co-medication. High-dose purified EPA (icosapent ethyl; two trials, REDUCE-IT and JELIS) raised bleeding RR 1.50 (1.13–1.99) — in absolute terms +0.6% (0.1–1.0), number needed to harm 166 — and risk scaled at 0.24 per gram of EPA (0.05–0.43) (high-dose-purified-epa-increases-bleeding-risk, high-dose-risk). ⚠ The antiplatelet interaction itself is neither shown nor ruled out: the authors' analysis is an ecological between-trial regression across eleven trials at p = 0.056, read as absence — the ninth underpowered-null-as-equivalence this vault has refused, and the first refused in the cautious direction (omega3-antiplatelet-bleeding-interaction-not-demonstrated-or-excluded, safety_flags: [interaction]). So the honest shape is: the ordinary dose looks clean, the pharmacological dose does not, and the variable is grams of EPA rather than the presence of another blood thinner. Two independent syntheses now find dose-dependent omega-3 harms — this one and Mattumpuram's atrial-fibrillation and gastrointestinal signal — so high-dose-risk is no longer an extrapolation from a single paper. ⚠ Bounded by I² = 65% over bleeding definitions that were not standardised across the eleven trials, and by an educational grant from Amarin, which makes the one product the review indicts (sponsor-adverse-findings-and-sponsor-friendly-framing-can-coexist).

The three threads bound each other: a surrogate biomarker moved, a functional cognitive endpoint did not, and a cardiovascular benefit appears only where baseline risk is high — the large primary-prevention trials (VITAL/ASCEND) were null, and the pooled benefit is paired with a real AF/GI harm. So this does not establish a healthspan outcome for a healthy, low-risk adult, it pushes back on "fish oil for the brain," and it reframes the cardiovascular case as secondary-prevention, not everyone-should-supplement — the same baseline-dependence pattern as vitamin-d.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Bischoff-Ferrari 2025 / DO-HEALTH (RCT post hoc, n=777): omega-3 1 g/day slowed PhenoAge, GrimAge2, DunedinPACE (0.16–0.32 SD ≈ 2.9–3.8 months); additive with vitamin D + exercise on PhenoAge. Opens the map at emerging (surrogate endpoints, post hoc, small effect).

  • 2026-07-05 — Ingested Mulargia 2025 (meta-analysis, 11 placebo-controlled RCTs pooled of 19 reviewed): no effect on global cognition in cognitively-unimpaired elders, SMD −0.02 (95% CI −0.07 to 0.04). High-tier null pushes back on the cognition claim; partly closes the "omega-3 hard-outcome" open gap on the cognition axis. Status stays emerging (surrogate-aging thread unchanged).

  • 2026-07-06 — Ingested Mattumpuram 2025 (meta-analysis, 42 RCTs, n=176,253, established-CVD/high-risk): significant reductions in CV mortality, CVD, CHD, MI, fatal MI, revascularization; significant increase in atrial fibrillation + GI events; EPA monotherapy > EPA+DHA. Adds a third thread (cardiovascular hard outcomes) — closes the CVD side of the hard-outcome gap for secondary prevention, leaves the healthy-primary-prevention case open. Status stays emerging (benefit is population-restricted and paired with a harm).

  • 2026-07-08 — Ingested Zhu 2025 / VITAL telomere sub-study (RCT, n=1,031, 4 y): marine omega-3 1 g/day had no significant effect on leukocyte telomere length (while vitamin D3 in the same trial did). Adds one more flat surrogate to the aging-biomarker thread — omega-3's surrogate signals are inconsistent (epigenetic clock nudged post-hoc, telomere null, cognition null). Status stays emerging.

  • 2026-07-19 — The map's biggest number arrives on its softest outcome; Status held emerging (Wang 2026, Nutrients 18(6):909, systematic review + network meta-analysis, 35 RCTs, n = 1,211 trained athletes; RoB 2.0 + NMA-adapted GRADE, network consistency tested; no external funding, no competing interests; not PROSPERO-registered; OA PDF archived). Omega-3 ranked first for recovery: SMD 0.40 (0.18–0.62), SUCRA 88.7% (omega3-recovery-signal-athletes-surrogate-only) — the largest effect this map carries anywhere, and its least consequential. Three discounts, and the vault applies all of them. (1) "Recovery" is a composite of three different constructs — serum creatine kinase (n=12 trials), delayed-onset muscle soreness on visual analog scales (n=14), and functional recovery via repeated performance testing (n=8). A blood enzyme, a self-reported pain rating and a performance test are not one outcome, and the largest contributor is the self-reported one, in trials where a fishy aftertaste famously undermines blinding. (2) The ranking is low-certainty by the paper's own GRADE table — moderate for omega-3 vs placebo, LOW for the indirect active-vs-active comparisons that produce the SUCRA. The vault keeps the vs-placebo interval and discards the crown. (3) Population is trained athletes; post-exercise soreness is not a healthspan outcome. This does not offset the map's hard findings and must not migrate into its summary line. Omega-3 remains: null on global cognition at high tier, CVD benefit confined to high-risk/secondary prevention and paired with an atrial-fibrillation harm, epigenetic clock nudged post-hoc, telomere null. A soft win on a soft endpoint does not offset a hard null on a hard one — and "this supplement's best-looking result is always on its softest outcome" is exactly the pattern this map exists to resist. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive. Nothing bought.

  • 2026-07-21 — The muscle-thread gap closes toward null; the training does the work, not the capsule; Status held emerging (Dam 2025, J Ageing Longev 5(1):4, systematic review + meta-analysis, 9 RCTs, n=286, healthy older adults ≥65, PROSPERO CRD42020221271; no funding, no conflicts; CC-BY OA PDF archived). Adding omega-3 PUFAs to resistance training did not beat RT + placebo on muscle mass (clean null: p = 0.30, I² = 0%, no publication bias), neuromuscular function (lower-body SMD −0.05, p = 0.72) or strength (upper-body SMD 0.09, p = 0.52) (omega3-adds-nothing-to-resistance-training-for-muscle). The one positive — chair-rise SMD 0.77 (p = 0.02) — was I² = 78% and outlier-driven (a no-placebo trial, SMD 3.16); removing it dropped the effect to SMD 0.40 or p = 0.06 (omega3-rt-chair-rise-signal-outlier-driven). A fourth honestly-bounded thread: omega-3 does not distinguish itself as a muscle supplement on top of training — the same pattern as creatine-without-RT (null) and Yu 2026 (no nutritional strategy beat usual care). Trials 6–18 wk (many below omega-3 steady state), none met quality standards → appraised mixed; muscle-null concept suggestive, chair-rise single-study. Nothing bought.

  • 2026-07-23 — The brain-delivery excuse dies; the cognition null is now bounded on both ends; Status held emerging (Yassine 2026, EBioMedicine 129:106316, randomised double-blind placebo-controlled phase IIa trial, NCT03613844, n=365, 24 months, USC, Sept 2018–May 2024; NIA R01AG057684 + ADDF funded, no omega-3 manufacturer involvement; appraised reliable; OA but bot-walled, pdf_unavailable recorded with the full ladder). Participants were 55–80, non-demented, low dietary DHA (<200 mg/day), ≥1 dementia risk factor; 47% APOE ε4 carriers, 58% female, 39% Hispanic. Pre-specified primary — CNS target engagement — was met decisively: CSF DHA/AA ratio +0.17 (0.15–0.18) on 2 g/day DHA vs −0.02 (−0.04 to −0.0004) on placebo, difference 0.19 (0.16–0.21), p < 0.0001, and no interaction with APOE ε4 (p = 0.71) (dha-reaches-the-brain-regardless-of-apoe4, graded strong — the trial's own powered primary). Over 24 months there were no treatment differences in cognitive performance or brain volumes (dha-brain-delivery-without-cognitive-or-structural-benefit, held suggestive on power grounds — secondary endpoints, 38% dropout, mostly COVID). The authors' own recommendation: "Future research should prioritise brain DHA metabolism over further supplementation trials." This is the vault's THIRD verified target engagement without benefit — after creatine's +11% brain loading (creatine-20g-raises-brain-creatine) and NR's doubled blood NAD⁺ (nicotinamide-riboside-raises-nad-without-cognitive-benefit). Three molecules, three deliveries, three absent outcomes: proving a supplement arrives is evidence the pill works, not that it helps. Status held — a single rct cannot outrank the map's meta-analysis-rct cognition null, and the primary result is a biochemical surrogate. Nothing bought; no dose recommended.

  • 2026-07-27 — The map's oldest un-sourced note becomes evidence — and the answer is reassurance plus a dose ceiling; Status held emerging (Javaid 2024, Journal of the American Heart Association 13(10):e032390, systematic review + meta-analysis of 11 randomised trials, n=120,643 (60,360 exposed), searched to May 2023; trials JELIS, GISSI-HF, ORIGIN, R&P, AREDS, ASCEND, VITAL, REDUCE-IT, STRENGTH, OMEMI, DO-HEALTH — two already in this vault; educational grant from Amarin; appraised mixed; OA PDF archived 1.68 MB). Overall bleeding: RR 1.09 (0.91–1.31), p = 0.34, I² = 65% — 2.6% vs 2.4%, absolute difference 0.07% (−1.4 to 1.6); haemorrhagic stroke RR 1.29 (0.92–1.81), intracranial RR 0.90 (0.64–1.26), gastrointestinal RR 1.07 (0.95–1.21), none significant (omega3-no-overall-increase-in-bleeding-risk, suggestive). High-dose purified EPA is the exception: RR 1.50 (1.13–1.99), absolute +0.6% (0.1–1.0), NNH 166, with a dose-response of 0.24 per gram of EPA (0.05–0.43) (high-dose-purified-epa-increases-bleeding-risk, suggestive, high-dose-risk). ⚠ The antiplatelet interaction is REFUSED in both directions — an ecological between-trial regression at p = 0.056 cannot demonstrate or exclude an individual-level interaction (omega3-antiplatelet-bleeding-interaction-not-demonstrated-or-excluded, single-study, safety_flags: [interaction], noted on aspirin too). ⚑ And an unusual funding observation, recorded because it cuts against the vault's normal rule: the sponsor makes icosapent ethyl, and the review's only positive harm finding is against icosapent ethyl — a sponsor-adverse result paired with a sponsor-friendly framing (sponsor-adverse-findings-and-sponsor-friendly-framing-can-coexist). Held mixed and not reliable on I² = 65% across non-standardised bleeding definitions, a two-trial high-dose cell resting on the mineral-oil-placebo trial (which the authors themselves flag), and the sponsor relationship. Status held — a bleeding null does not move a map whose Status rests on a cognition null and a population-restricted cardiovascular benefit. Nothing bought; no dose recommended.

  • 2026-08-07 — Ingested Chen 2026 (Frontiers in Medicine, network meta-analysis, 23 RCTs) for the vault's first krill-oil evidence — and krill oil is an omega-3 delivery vehicle, at EPA 240–600 mg + DHA 110–280 mg/day across three trials (Laslett ×2, Stonehouse, Suzuki), 2–4 g/day krill oil. In knee osteoarthritis it ranked 1st on the WOMAC pain subscale (SUCRA 0.99, MD −4.12, −5.61 to −2.63) and 4th on VAS pain (0.51) — the single most rank-unstable result in the paper (knee-oa-rankings-invert-between-two-pain-scales-in-one-network). Status unchanged; source appraised unreliable, capped single-study, cannot move this map. open-question — the first omega-3 joint-pain signal the vault has met, arriving inside a network whose rankings reverse with the choice of questionnaire.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Surrogate signals track with vitamin-d, but diverge. On the PhenoAge clock in DO-HEALTH the two were additive; on telomere length in the VITAL sub-study (Zhu 2025) they diverged — vitamin D3 preserved telomeres, omega-3 was null (omega3-no-telomere-effect vs vitamin-d-preserves-telomere-length). Both are surrogate-endpoint signals co-existing with null/contested hard outcomes; a nuance, not a rehabilitation of the "supplement everyone" case (see vitamin-d).
  • Cognition-lever landscape (not an interaction). The new high-tier cognition null (omega3-no-global-cognition-benefit) sits alongside creatine's suggestive cognition thread (creatine-monohydrate) and magnesium L-threonate's single-RCT cognition signal (magnesium-l-threonate): same outcome, three different compounds, three different verdicts. A framing note for regimen-time honesty, not a safety edge.
  • ⚠ Muscle thread joins the "training does the work" pattern — resistance-training and protein-target (new 2026-07-21). Dam 2025 finds omega-3 adds nothing to resistance training for muscle mass or strength (omega3-adds-nothing-to-resistance-training-for-muscle). This is not an interaction — it is the same shape the vault keeps finding: creatine-monohydrate builds lean mass only with training and is null without it (creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training); Yu 2026 found no nutritional strategy beat usual care on any outcome, even added to training. At regimen time, resistance training is the muscle lever; omega-3 is not an amplifier of it. Logged as a framing note, no safety edge.
  • Global-ripple: no safety interaction with other vault interventions found. Yassine 2026 adds none either — adverse events were comparable between arms with no serious events attributed to treatment, which is reassuring on tolerability without touching the AF/GI harm graded below (a different dose regime and a different population).
  • ⚑ The target-engagement pattern is now a cross-map rule, not an omega-3 quirk (new 2026-07-23). Three vault maps now hold a verified delivery paired with an absent outcome: omega-3 (CSF DHA/AA moved, cognition and brain volume did not — this map), creatine-cognition (≥20 g raises brain creatine ~11%, cognition unproven — creatine-20g-raises-brain-creatine), and nad-precursors (NR doubles blood NAD⁺, cognition/CBF/BP unmoved — nicotinamide-riboside-raises-nad-without-cognitive-benefit). This is the same species of discipline biological-age-clocks imposes with predictor ≠ responder; here it reads engagement ≠ outcome. Recorded as a reading rule across maps — no Status moved anywhere, because a pattern across three literatures is a heuristic, not evidence about any one of them.
  • ⚑ And a mechanistic result from the same run cuts the same way (new 2026-07-23). Jacques 2026 (dna-methylation-aging-signatures-17-tissues-jacques-2026) finds most aging methylation networks are "fragile and resistant to beneficial modulation" (aging-methylation-networks-resist-beneficial-modulation) — which is the molecular reason to keep discounting this map's own epigenetic-clock thread (omega3-slows-epigenetic-aging), already the weakest evidence it carries (post-hoc, single trial, surrogate). No Status moved; the discount is now cited rather than asserted.
  • ⚠ Safety — atrial-fibrillation / GI harm (now graded). Mattumpuram 2025 makes the omega-3 harm signal a graded concept: omega3-atrial-fibrillation-gi-harm carries safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] for the significant increase in incident AF and GI adverse events. This gates naive high-dose recommendation at regimen time without changing the benefit score (shared/safety.md).
  • ⚑ 2026-07-27 — THE TWENTY-ONE-DAY-OLD BLEEDING GAP IS CLOSED, AND THE DISCIPLINE THAT KEPT IT OPEN IS WHY THE ANSWER IS WORTH HAVING. This bullet previously read: "higher-dose omega-3 can potentiate anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy — well-established pharmacology, but this run still found no vault-quality source to cite," with the instruction to "resolve by ingesting a proper omega-3 drug-interaction / bleeding-risk review." That instruction was written on 2026-07-06 and followed on 2026-07-27. Three things came back, and the first of them contradicts what the note assumed: (a) At the doses these trials used, omega-3 does not measurably increase bleeding at all — RR 1.09 (0.91–1.31) across 120,643 randomised people (omega3-no-overall-increase-in-bleeding-risk). The generic fish-oil-thins-your-blood concern is not supported, and anyone reading this map should now read that former clinician-note as narrower than it was written. This is what it looks like when a vault refuses to assert remembered pharmacology and then finds out the pharmacology it remembered was over-general. (b) The real variable is grams of EPA: RR 1.50 (1.13–1.99) for high-dose purified EPA, absolute +0.6%, NNH 166, dose-response 0.24 per gram (0.05–0.43) (high-dose-purified-epa-increases-bleeding-risk). Together with Mattumpuram's AF/GI signal (omega3-atrial-fibrillation-gi-harm), two independent syntheses now put dose-dependent harms on this map, so high-dose-risk rests on replication rather than inference. (c) The interaction itself is still untested — an ecological between-trial regression at p = 0.056 is not an interaction test (omega3-antiplatelet-bleeding-interaction-not-demonstrated-or-excluded, safety_flags: [interaction]). The flag stays up in the cautious direction (shared/safety.md: a missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious flag), and the gap is now correctly shaped rather than empty — what is missing is individual-patient-data pooling stratified by baseline antiplatelet use.
  • ⚑ 2026-07-27 — the other half of the feared combination is now graded, on aspirin. Low-dose aspirin raised major haemorrhage HR 1.24 (1.10–1.39) in a 19,114-person double-blind trial, with the excess larger during the randomised phase (1.38) and fading after the drug stopped (1.08) (aspirin-increases-major-haemorrhage-older-adults, aspirin-bleeding-excess-tracks-exposure-not-history). Both halves sourced in one run, after three weeks in which neither was. The practical translation for regimen time: the question to bring to a clinician is "how many grams of EPA," not "does fish oil interact with a blood thinner." Cross-noted on aspirin.
  • Two of Javaid's eleven included trials are already in this vault, which is a small consistency check worth recording. VITAL supplies this map's telomere null (omega3-no-telomere-effect) and vitamin-d's fracture and cancer/cardiovascular nulls; DO-HEALTH supplies this map's epigenetic-clock signal (omega3-slows-epigenetic-aging). The same trials that failed to move omega-3's efficacy endpoints also failed to show a bleeding harm — the null is coming from the same evidence base as the disappointment, not from a friendlier one.
  • CVD hard-outcome gap — closed for secondary prevention, open for primary. Mattumpuram 2025 supplies the high-tier cardiovascular evidence the gap wanted — but only in established-CVD / high-risk populations. The healthy-adult primary-prevention question (David's situation) remains unestablished (VITAL/ASCEND null), so the gap narrows to primary prevention rather than closing.
  • Context on the telomere null (added 2026-07-09). Omega-3 was flat on telomere length in VITAL (omega3-no-telomere-effect). A 16-RCT meta-analysis now shows aerobic exercise does move that class of marker — telomerase activity SMD 0.35, telomere length SMD 0.59 (aerobic-exercise-raises-telomerase-activity, on zone-2-cardio) — randomized and independently funded. On the cellular-aging surrogates omega-3 is marketed against, the free intervention outperforms the capsule. All of these are biomarkers, so this changes no Status; it is a framing note for regimen time, where omega-3's real case remains the epigenetic-clock post-hoc signal and secondary- prevention CVD benefit, not telomeres.
  • ⚑ Krill oil enters the vault as an omega-3 question, not a new supplement (2026-08-07). Chen 2026's krill-oil arms deliver EPA 240–600 mg + DHA 110–280 mg/day — the same fatty acids this map already tracks, in a different carrier (phospholipid-bound rather than triglyceride, plus astaxanthin in one trial). No trial in that network compared krill oil against ordinary fish oil, so nothing establishes the carrier matters. Its top ranking on one pain scale and 4th on another is recorded as an instability, not a finding. Cross-noted on exercise-for-osteoarthritis.
