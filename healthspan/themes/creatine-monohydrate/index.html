Creatine monohydrate — supplements

Status: consolidating (2026-07-07; forked 2026-07-08) — high-tier RCT evidence, replicated, real effect, no live rebuttal. The best-evidenced supplement in the seed set. This map is now the hub for three sub-threads — strength/lean mass (strong), bone (BMD-null / geometry-open), and safety (reassuring); the cognition thread was auto-forked 2026-07-08 into its own child map, creatine-cognition (the map had reached 12 bullets / 4 sub-threads — fork-due). The live question across the tissues here is dose, and the evidence says the benefit of >5 g/day is unproven.

What we believe now

Creatine monohydrate is safe and effective at ~5 g/day. The popular case for a higher 10 g/day maintenance dose — that "greedy" muscle saturates first, so a surplus is needed to reach brain and bone — does not survive the evidence, and has been undercut largely by its own leading proponents.

  • (1) Strength & lean mass (strong) — flat above ~5 g/day. Creatine alongside resistance training reliably adds a modest increment of lean mass and strength (Chilibeck 2017: +1.37 kg lean mass). A 61-RCT dose-response meta-analysis (Ashtary-Larky 2025, n=1,457) shows fat-free-mass gain is flat across dose — +1.35 kg at ≤5 g/day vs +1.37 kg at >5 g/day (p=0.965). For muscle, 10 g buys nothing over 5 g. Additive to training, not a substitute — and now sourced directly (Gu 2026, 37 RCTs, context-stratified): with resistance training FFM +3.39 kg / LBM +2.70 kg / squat 1RM +9.58 kg; without training the same outcomes are flat and null (FFM −0.89 kg, p=0.566). The muscle gain is the training's, amplified by creatine, and it vanishes when the training does. Anaerobic (Wingate) power rose in both contexts (creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training).
  • Cognition → forked to creatine-cognition (2026-07-08). The cognition sub-thread — suggestive older-adult memory + an acute sleep-deprivation rescue, but a healthy-adult null and an unproven ≥20 g brain-loading dose — is now its own child map. Bottom line carried forward: cognition is a possible bonus, not a reason to raise the dose. See creatine-cognition.
  • (2) Bone (BMD null; geometry an open, thin question). The highest-tier evidence — a meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (Forbes 2018, n=193) — finds creatine + resistance training gives no greater BMD than training alone at any site (whole-body, hip, femoral neck, lumbar spine; all p>0.25), including in higher-dose arms. The "≥8 g builds bone" claim traces to a single narrative Letter to the Editor (Forbes 2022) that tallies 5 g → 0/4 trials positive vs ~7–9 g → 5/6 "positive" — but on heterogeneous bone geometry/ content outcomes (not BMD), with one high-dose trial flatly null, severe industry conflicts, and authors who co-wrote the very trials they grade. Per highest-tier-wins, BMD-null anchors; high-dose geometry is a thin, non-independent open question, not established.
  • (3) Safety (reassuring). No renal impairment at ~10 g/day for 3 months (Gualano 2008; cystatin C fell/GFR trend up) or across 1–80 g/day over 5 d–24 mo (Longobardi 2025 review). The creatinine caveat is a labeling artifact, not kidney damage — supplemental creatine degrades to creatinine (~2%/day), raising the serum marker without lowering true GFR; tell a clinician you supplement before a kidney panel. GI distress is dose-dependent (worse at single doses >10 g; split ≤5 g/serving). The DHT/hair-loss fear is retired by the first direct RCT (Lak 2025): no change in DHT, testosterone, or 8 hair measures.

On the 10 g/day question (David's use case) — the honest bottom line. Take creatine: it is safe and well-evidenced. But the specific case for 10 g over 5 g has collapsed across all three target tissues, and mostly by the hand of the researchers who advanced it (Candow's group authored the dose-flat muscle meta, the null cognition-dose RCT, and the review saying 10 g is too little for the brain). The evidence says: ~5 g/day is the maintenance dose that delivers the muscle, and there is no reliable human evidence that 10 g adds muscle, cognition, or bone in a healthy adult. 10 g remains safe — if David prefers it as a saturation buffer or "insurance," nothing here argues against it (renal/hair clean; split AM/PM for GI) — but it should not be expected to outperform 5 g. The two genuinely dose-responsive ideas are (a) the acute sleep-deprivation "stress dose" (~0.35 g/kg single dose, not a daily regimen) and (b) an unproven ≥20 g brain-loading threshold that no healthy-adult outcome trial has validated. Not medical advice; no dose is prescribed here — this informs a clinician conversation.

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Forbes 2022 (Letter to the Editor, narrative dose-stratified tally of 10 RCTs): 5 g → 0/4 trials positive on bone; ~7–9 g → 5/6 with ≥1 positive outcome (heterogeneous geometry/BMC, 1 high-dose trial null). The textual origin of "8–10 g for bone," but thin — narrative not pooled, severe industry conflict, author–trial overlap. extends the bone sub-thread with dose nuance; in tension with the stronger Forbes 2018 BMD-null (which anchors). Status unchanged (mechanistic tier).

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Gualano 2008 (RCT, n=18, healthy sedentary men 18–35): ~10 g/day for 3 months → no renal dysfunction (cystatin C fell/GFR trend up; serum creatinine flat vs placebo's training- related rise). Opens the safety sub-thread — first renal-safety evidence, directly answering the literal 10 g/day dose question. extends; Status unchanged (single small study).

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Longobardi 2025 (narrative safety review, doses 1–80 g/day, 5 d–24 mo): no renal/ hydration/cramp harm; GI distress dose-dependent (mitigated by splitting ≤5 g); serum creatinine rises as a benign lab artifact, not true kidney impairment. Broadens the safety sub-thread (extends). ⚠ industry tie (AlzChem-funded APC; author advisory-board roles) — reassuring direction, weighted as review-tier.

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Lak 2025 (RCT, n=38, double-blind, 12 wk, 5 g/day, resistance-trained males): no group×time effect on DHT, testosterone, or any of 8 hair-follicle measures vs placebo. First direct test of the DHT/hair-loss fear (vs van der Merwe 2009's uncontrolled 3-wk DHT-rise signal, which never measured hair). Highest-tier evidence on the safety thread; retires the hair-loss myth. extends; consolidating.

  • 2026-07-11 — Ingested Bai 2026 (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, network meta-analysis, 52 RCTs, all PEDro ≥6, 48/52 double-blind, healthy adult athletes, ≥2 wk) opening a thin athletic-performance sub-thread and answering a stacking question: creatine alone improved sprint (SMD −0.64, negative = faster = better), repeated-sprint ability (−0.78), upper-body muscular endurance (0.43) and jump (0.33) vs placebo; agility and lower-body endurance null (creatine-athletic-performance-benefit). Beta-alanine alone was non-significant or context-specific throughout, and creatine + beta-alanine showed no added benefit over creatine alone (creatine-beta-alanine-no-synergy). Read that null precisely — it is no evidence of synergy, not evidence of no synergy: the Cr+BA arm is estimable in only 1 of 6 outcome networks (jump), and the authors' own Limitations concede "limited data existed for combined Cr + BA trials, restricting the ability to detect potential synergistic interactions." Two reliability catches the abstract gets wrong: Egger's test is significant for sprint (p=0.02), jump (p=0.01), and RSA (p=0.01) — exactly the domains creatine "wins" — despite the abstract claiming no substantive publication bias; and the claimed "P scores ≥0.90 across these domains" is false for jump, where creatine ranks only 0.34 (below beta-alanine's 0.48). mixed → capped suggestive. extends; Status unchanged (consolidating) — athletic-performance surrogates in young athletes neither strengthen nor threaten the strength/lean-mass, bone, or safety threads that ground it, and nothing here touches the vault's ~5 g/day answer (no disaggregated creatine dosing was recoverable; pooled range across both supplements was 2.3–20 g/day).

  • 2026-07-16 — Ingested Gu 2026 (Frontiers in Nutrition, systematic review + meta-analysis, 37 RCTs, healthy men 18–30, RoB 2 with 25/37 low risk, GRADE moderate–high; PROSPERO CRD420261283973; no funding, no conflicts): stratifying by training context shows creatine's lean-mass/strength benefit exists only with resistance training (FFM +3.39 kg, LBM +2.70 kg, squat 1RM +9.58 kg) and is flat/null without it (FFM −0.89 kg, p=0.566), while anaerobic power rises in both contexts (Wingate peak +71.27 W pooled) (creatine-lean-mass-requires-resistance-training). The direct evidence behind the map's standing "additive to training, not a substitute" clause. Appraised reliable; graded strong. confirms; Status unchanged (consolidating) — population is young men, and nothing here touches the ~5 g/day answer, the bone thread, or the safety thread.

  • 2026-07-19 — A third independent synthesis lands the same strength answer; Status held consolidating (Wang 2026, Nutrients 18(6):909, systematic review + network meta-analysis, 35 RCTs, n = 1,211 trained athletes in structured training ≥6 months; six databases, frequentist random-effects, RoB 2.0 + NMA- adapted GRADE, network consistency tested; no external funding, no competing interests; not PROSPERO-registered; OA PDF archived). Creatine ranked first for muscle strength: SMD 0.46 (0.29–0.63), SUCRA 82.4% (creatine-strength-benefit-replicates-in-trained-athletes). This is confirmation that changes nothing, which is what a healthy map looks like. Three independent lines now agree: Gu 2026 (37 RCTs — creatine's lean-mass gain exists only with resistance training, FFM +3.39 kg vs −0.89 kg, p=0.566 without), Bai 2026 (52-RCT athlete NMA), and this. The strength thread was already the map's best-supported one. What the vault declines from the same paper. Its headline — an "outcome-specific efficacy pattern" in which each supplement wins exactly one outcome — is built on the low-certainty half of its own GRADE table: certainty is moderate for each supplement vs placebo and LOW for the indirect active-vs-active comparisons that generate the ranking (supplement-outcome-specific-ranking-too-tidy-for-a-sparse-network, filed on protein-target). Fifth SUCRA discount, and the first where the source's own summary-of-findings table states the vault's standing rule for it. Also: funnel asymmetry on strength (Egger p = 0.08) that the authors describe as "not a cause for concern." Nothing moves for David. Population is trained athletes, outcome is a strength surrogate, and no dose is stated for this population — the ~5 g/day answer is untouched, as are the bone and safety threads. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ CANDIDATE INTERVENTION FLAGGED, NOT ADOPTED: beta-alanine (new 2026-07-11). Bai 2026 is the vault's first encounter with beta-alanine, and per the connect contract (spec §6.5) a candidate intervention is flagged, never auto-adopted. No beta-alanine map was coined, deliberately. The reasoning: beta-alanine is "non-significant or context-specific" everywhere this source tests it, and the only evidence on stacking it with creatine is a combination node estimable in 1 of 6 domains. The vault has coined debunk-maps before (taurine, nad-precursors) — but each of those weighed a real, positive, independent literature against a strong null. Here there is no independent beta-alanine literature in the vault to weigh at all, and a map whose entire content would be "adds nothing, and we are not even confident of that" would overstate what one thin sub-node supports. Practical consequence for the regimen: do not add beta-alanine to the stack — but the reason is "unproven," not "disproven." Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md; revisit if a sweep surfaces beta-alanine evidence outside the creatine-stacking context.
  • The athletic-performance sub-thread is real but nearly irrelevant to this vault's question (new 2026-07-11). Sprint times, jump height, and repeated-sprint ability in trained young athletes are not healthspan endpoints, and GOAL.md names no athletic goal — David's aerobic targets are knee-safe steps and Zone-2. The sub-thread is recorded because it is high-tier and it answers the stacking question; it is not a reason to take creatine, and it does not alter the strength/lean-mass case that already justifies it. Note the parallel with ashwagandha, whose own ergogenic evidence the regimen declined for the same reason: an ergogenic result in young athletes is among the least transferable findings in sports nutrition.
  • The bone thread is now closed from a second direction (added 2026-07-10). creatine-bone-bmd-null established that creatine + resistance training gives no greater BMD than training alone. A new 18-RCT meta-analysis on resistance-training finds that doubling the training intensity (≥70% vs <70% 1RM) does not detectably move BMD either (rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd; lumbar SMD 0.28, −0.02 to 0.58). Two independent attempts to shift bone density in a training adult, two nulls — both underpowered, so neither closes the question, but together they retire the practical case: the creatine-for-bone thesis has no remaining route. Whatever bone benefit exists comes from training at all. The thin, conflicted high-dose geometry claim (creatine-high-dose-bone-indices) stands alone and unreplicated. This changes no Status — the map's strength thread is untouched, and the vault's creatine answer stays ~5 g/day.
  • Stack synergy with protein-target — both amplify the same resistance-training adaptation; count them as one muscle-building stack, not two independent wins. The dose-flat muscle finding reinforces this: the marginal muscle win from more creatine is ~zero, so protein does the incremental work, not creatine dose.
  • Additive to resistance-training — the base lever, not a substitute — and this now extends to bone: creatine adds no BMD benefit beyond resistance training alone (Forbes 2018, meta-analysis-rct), and whatever bone-loading benefit exists comes from the training stimulus, not the supplement. RT is the foundation; creatine is its (strength-only) amplifier. Count creatine + protein as one stack on top of the training.
  • Complementary to zone-2-cardio — creatine serves the strength/anaerobic axis, Zone-2 the aerobic axis; different levers, no conflict.
  • Complementary to the sleep levers sleep-regularity and melatonin — an acute buffer, not a substitute. Gordji-Nejad 2024 positions a large acute creatine dose as a rescue for cognition under sleep deprivation (jet lag, a bad night); sleep-regularity is the preventive foundation and melatonin a circadian-timing tool. Situational use, not a nightly-stack item.
  • ⚠ Candidate interaction — caffeine (caffeine-timing). Old, thin evidence (Vandenberghe 1996, Hespel 2002) suggests high co-ingested caffeine may blunt creatine's ergogenic benefit, plus mild GI distress on co-ingestion. A performance-timing nuance, not a safety hazard; flagged as a candidate, not gated. A dedicated modern source is the open gap. Noted in both maps.
  • ⚠ The safety thread covers ~5–10 g/day, not the 20 g brain-loading dose (added 2026-07-09). This map's safety evidence tops out at ~10 g/day being renal-clean over 3 months (creatine-high-dose-renal-safety). Nothing about that finding argues for raising the dose: strength is flat above ~5 g (creatine-dose-response-strength), and the randomized cognition test at 20 g in healthy adults was null.   • The bone sub-thread is now 0-for-3 across the whole vault (updated 2026-07-12). This map's BMD null (creatine-bone-bmd-null) was joined on 2026-07-10 by resistance-training's null on heavy vs moderate loading (rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd — itself an underpowered miss, not equivalence), and now by potassium-citrate, a non-mechanical alkaline-salt mechanism whose own trial sequential analysis declares the evidence below the required information size (potassium-citrate-bone-nulls-honestly-underpowered). Three mechanisms, three failures to demonstrate anything a person could feel. The vault does not conclude bone is unmovable — training and protein remain what a person actually does for their skeleton — but it does conclude that no supplement in this vault has earned a purchase for bone, and that includes creatine at any dose.
