Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-07-04) — a meta-analysis of chamomile trials shows a subjective sleep-quality benefit, but with extreme heterogeneity (I²=88%) and no effect on objective measures. Ingested as the best available apigenin proxy — labelled honestly as chamomile, a multi-compound botanical, not an isolated-apigenin claim.

What we believe now

Chamomile extract probably improves subjective sleep quality and reduces night awakenings, but does not improve sleep duration, efficiency, or daytime functioning. The signal is meta-analysis-tier but heavily qualified: I²=88% heterogeneity, benefit confined to subjective/awakening outcomes, and only 1 of 10 trials verified product purity/potency.

The apigenin framing (why this map is "chamomile," not "apigenin"). The popular "apigenin for sleep" narrative rests almost entirely on chamomile trials — chamomile is only ~0.8–1.2% apigenin plus many other bioactives (α-bisabolol, chamazulene, terpenoids), so these results cannot be attributed to apigenin. Isolated-apigenin human sleep trials are essentially absent; that is a genuine open gap, tracked in gaps-and-todo.md.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Kazemi 2024 (systematic review & meta-analysis, 10 trials, N=772): PSQI improved (WMD −1.88, 95% CI −3.46 to −0.31, I²=88%), fewer awakenings; no change in duration, efficiency, or daytime function. Opens the map at emerging (heterogeneous; chamomile ≠ apigenin).
