Exercise adherence — the variable every other map assumes

Status: emerging (2026-08-13) — ⚑ auto-coined this run on a deliberately weak base, flagged for human review, and the weakness is recorded here rather than hidden. Coined by Zhu 2026, a three-level meta-analysis of 14 studies, n = 6,069, asking which kinds of contextual cue predict physical activity. Its behavioural answer is r = 0.11 — about 1.2% of variance — correlational, self-reported on both sides, with reverse causation unaddressed and two-thirds of the appraisal unreadable behind a paywall.

Not an intervention — a map about the mechanism by which every other map either works or doesn't. It joins biological-age-clocks (which grades instruments), fat-free-mass (a marker literature) and detraining (about stopping) as maps that are not things to take.

Why coin a map on evidence this thin

Because the vault has been making adherence judgements without any evidence at all, dozens of times, on every map.

GOAL.md ranks interventions by least work, least discomfort, low daily maintenance — so adherence is already inside this vault's objective function. And every dose here is a behaviour repeated for years: ~7,000 steps/day (daily-steps), ~1.6 g/kg/day protein (protein-target), ~90–119 min/week of lifting (resistance-training), a nightly supplement, a consistent sleep window. Phrases like "low burden," "high daily maintenance" and "a bad trade against least-work" appear across this vault as unsourced editorial assertion. Tonight is the first time any of it is attached to a citation.

⚠ The counter-argument, recorded so it is not lost. This is a thinner base than several candidates the vault has refused — and the honest distinction is that those refusals concerned interventions with a commercial form, where coining a map risks laundering a product into apparent legitimacy. There is nothing to buy here. The failure mode of coining too eagerly on a supplement is a bad purchase; the failure mode here is an empty map, and git is the undo.

What we believe now

Of five ways a context can be consistent, the two that tracked doing more exercise were doing it at the same TIME and in the same ROUTINE slot — not the same place, not with the same people, not in the same mood. That is a small correlational association that no trial has tested.

Across 14 studies, context consistency correlated with physical-activity behaviour at r = 0.11 and with habit strength at r = 0.20. Only temporal and routine consistency were significant against behaviour; all five dimensions (adding location, social and mood) were significant against habit strength (only-temporal-and-routine-cue-consistency-predicted-physical-activity). Object cues have never been studied by anyone.

⚑ The most useful line is a warning about an outcome, not a finding about cues: three of five cue types were associated with feeling like exercise was a habit without being associated with doing more of it. An intervention optimised against habit-strength scales would chase all five; only two track the behaviour.

Three reasons the map opens at emerging and holds no magnitude it trusts:

(1) The effect is inside confounding range. r = 0.11 leaves ~98.8% of the variance unexplained, and shared/study-appraisal.md is explicit that at this size "anything unaccounted-for can distort reality."

(2) Reverse causation is at least as parsimonious as the causal reading, and unaddressed. People who exercise four mornings a week report consistent times, routines and locations because they exercise four mornings a week. A third account — shared confounding by life structure (stable hours, no shift work, childcare that doesn't move, a body that isn't in pain) — would produce the same correlation with no arrow at all (cue-consistency-behaviour-association-is-inside-confounding-range).

(3) Both sides of every association are self-report, and the paper's biggest number is the most inflated by it. Habit strength correlated with behaviour at a medium-to-large effect — but habit-strength instruments ask how frequent and automatic exercise is, while the outcome asks how much someone exercises. A questionnaire correlating with a subset of itself (habit-strength-and-physical-activity-are-measured-by-overlapping-self-reports). social-connection has already measured what this inflation costs: r = −.23 subjective against r = −.12 objective, a near-halving (loneliness-health-association-halves-on-objective-measures).

Sub-threads: none — the map holds one question (what predicts whether a person keeps exercising?) from one source, and its three concepts are a finding plus two appraisals of that same finding, not separable strands. [reviewed 2026-08-13 @ 1 bullets]

What this changes in the regimen: nothing

No dose, no protocol, no Status moved anywhere, no safety_flags. A correlational r of 0.11 is not an instruction. What the map is for is to stop the vault asserting adherence claims without evidence, and to hold the gap open until a trial exists.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-13 — ⚑ MAP COINED on a weak base, flagged for human review. Ingested Zhu 2026 (context-cue-consistency-physical-activity-habit-meta-zhu-2026, Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being 18(4):e70204, systematic review + three-level random-effects meta-analysis, 14 independent studies, n = 6,069, six databases, no funding, no conflicts; ⚠ paywalled — confirmed non-OA at the browser rung, ABSTRACT-ONLY appraisal). Context consistency ↔ PA behaviour r = 0.11; ↔ habit strength r = 0.20. Only temporal and routine consistency predicted behaviour; all five dimensions predicted habit strength; object cues unstudied. PA operationalisation moderated the association — stronger for frequency than duration, which fits a habit account and a method-variance account equally well. ⚠ Correlational, self-reported both sides, reverse causation unaddressed, and registration / design mix / intervals / heterogeneity / publication bias all unreadable. The authors nonetheless close by "supporting a shift toward cue-specific PA promotion strategies." Appraised mixed; all concepts capped suggestive. Status opens at emerging.

  • 2026-08-14 — ⚑ THE MAP'S SECOND DATAPOINT ARRIVES ONE DAY AFTER IT WAS COINED, FROM AN UNRELATED LITERATURE, AND IT AGREES IN SHAPE. Status held emerging. The adherence half of Zhang 2026 (hiit-vo2max-cancer-bayesian-meta-zhang-2026, BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation, Bayesian three-level meta-analysis, 16 RCTs / 35 effect sizes, cancer patients; no funding, no competing interests; ⚠ confirmed non-OA — abstract-bounded; appraised mixed, capped suggestive). Mean adherence 85.7%, and no prescription parameter showed a statistically significant association with it (hiit-adherence-was-high-and-unrelated-to-how-it-was-prescribed). ⚑ Two literatures, one instruction. Zhu found weak evidence that when and where a behaviour sits predicts doing it (temporal and routine cue consistency, r = 0.11); Zhang finds no evidence that how the sessions are written — intensity, interval count, session length, frequency — predicts doing them. And the vault's third adherence datum points the same way: 96% adherence to home HIIT achieved by changing the delivery context, not the prescription (remotely-supervised-home-hiit-achieved-96-percent-adherence-in-older-adults). Three sources, none strong, all pointing away from programme design as the lever. ⚠ Bounded hard, and the map says so rather than banking it. 85.7% is a supervised-trial rate in people with a cancer diagnosis and a powerful reason to comply, reported as a bare mean with no dispersion, no dropout figure and no definition of adherence; the null association comes from the very moderator analysis the authors call "exploratory and underpowered." A null from an underpowered analysis is no information, not demonstrated absence — the distinction this vault enforces in both directions, applied here where it is inconvenient. This map's top gap is unchanged: a trial that manipulates the thing and measures behaviour with a device.

Open gaps

  • Top gap — the trial that would make this map worth having. A randomised trial that manipulates cue consistency and measures device-based physical activity. Everything the map currently holds is correlational self-report.
  • Object cues: zero studies. A cue dimension with the most obvious practical form — shoes by the door, bar loaded, poles in the hall — has never been examined. Named by the source itself.
  • Adherence in the vault's own interventions is almost entirely unmeasured. The only other adherence datum here is remotely-supervised-home-hiit-achieved-96-percent-adherence-in-older-adults — and it comes from supervision, not from cues, in a trial designed to test delivery format. No source in this vault reports long-run adherence to a supplement, a protein target, or a step count.
  • Dropout as an adherence signal is currently read only as a bias problem. magnesium-trial-dropout-was-differential-by-arm and the detraining literature both contain adherence information the vault reads as methodology rather than as evidence about whether people keep doing things. Worth revisiting.
