Urolithin A — supplements

Status: contested (2026-08-07) — ⚠ DOWNGRADED this run from emerging, flagged for human review. Auto-coined 2026-07-08 for urolithin A, a gut-microbiome postbiotic (from ellagitannins in pomegranate, berries, walnuts) that stimulates mitophagy. It opened emerging on a genuine mechanism with a modest functional payoff, and its recorded gap was independence: every human evidence line traced to Amazentis, the manufacturer of Mitopure. On 2026-08-07 an independent group with no commercial relationships re-synthesised the same five randomised trials — and the functional claim did not survive the re-analysis. Only one outcome could be pooled at all; fixing a unit-of-analysis error cut it by 38% and took it below significance. The mechanism is not contested. The function is.

Sub-threads: none — one contested question (does a demonstrated mitophagy mechanism produce any measurable functional benefit in humans?) with rival answers from the manufacturer's trials and an independent re-synthesis of those same trials; the immune-cell strand shares the same surrogate-vs-function tension rather than separating from it. [reviewed 2026-08-07 @ 3 bullets]

What we believe now

⚠ Urolithin A engages its mechanism in human muscle, and after five randomised trials it has still not been shown to change anything a person can do. That is a rewrite, not a restatement: until 2026-08-07 this map read "mechanistic target engagement plus a modest muscle signal." The mechanism half stands. The muscle half did not survive an independent re-pooling of the same five trials — one poolable functional outcome, its significance dependent on a unit-of-analysis error, and every other muscle measure unpoolable and explicitly exploratory (see the 2026-08-07 section below).

What the original synthesis reported, recorded as the superseded reading. Across 5 RCTs (250 adults, 10–1000 mg/day, 28 days–4 months), Hodzic Kuerec 2024 described UA as increasing muscle strength and endurance ("~12% strength in one trial"), producing a dose-dependent anti-inflammatory effect (CRP, acylcarnitines), and upregulating mitophagy/mitochondrial genes in muscle biopsies — while being null on physical function, body composition, cardiovascular outcomes, mitochondrial maximal ATP, biogenesis, dynamics and gut microbiota. ⚑ The strength clause is the part now known to be a summary of the winning outcome rather than of the trial; the mitophagy and inflammation clauses are untouched.

The nad-precursors contrast needs revising in urolithin A's disfavour. This map has read UA as the inverse of NMN/NR — a biomarker that moves and a function that follows, against NAD⁺ rising with nothing behind it. Half of that holds: UA's target engagement in muscle biopsies is real and NAD⁺ precursors have no equivalent. Half no longer does: UA's functional payoff is one unpoolable strength outcome and one inconclusive walk test, which is closer to nad-precursors-null-on-muscle-mass-function than this map was recording. Better mechanism; comparably absent function. Still a watch slot; still not a buy.

A third domain, same pattern (added 2026-07-09). A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 50 healthy adults aged 45–70 (Denk 2025, Nature Aging) found 1,000 mg/day UA for 28 days expanded naive-like, less exhausted CD8⁺ T cells (+0.50 pp, P=0.0437) and raised CD8⁺ fatty-acid oxidation (+14.72 pp, P=0.0061), with coherent secondary movement across mitochondrial biogenesis, NK cells, monocytes, TNF secretion, and ex vivo bacterial uptake (urolithin-a-immune-cell-rejuvenation-rct). It is the methodologically cleanest UA evidence the vault holds — and it changes nothing, for three reasons. It is not independent (three authors are Amazentis employees, one is its president, Amazentis funded the trial and supplied Mitopure). Every endpoint is a surrogate (no infection, no vaccine response, no function). The lead result is thin (a 0.50-percentage-point subset shift at P=0.0437, from a large immune panel, over 28 days, in 50 people).

⚠ The independent look arrived, and it dissolved the functional claim rather than disputing it (2026-08-07). Dao 2026 (Frontiers in Nutrition, PROSPERO CRD420251270987, no commercial or financial relationships declared) went back over the same five randomised trials. Quantitative pooling was possible for exactly one outcome — the 6-minute walk test, from two trials. And correcting a unit-of-analysis error (the earlier pooling used only the 1,000 mg arm of a trial with two dose arms sharing one placebo group) took it from +27.46 m (4.54 to 50.38), p = 0.019 to +17.03 m (−5.33 to 39.40), p = 0.135 — a 38% reduction, produced by arithmetic rather than new data, and a point estimate at or below the ~14 m minimal clinically important difference (urolithin-a-pooled-walk-test-loses-significance-on-arm-combination). Everything else — strength, endurance, VO₂peak, lean mass, every mitochondrial biomarker — could not be pooled at all and is labelled by the authors "exploratory signals rather than reproducible effects" (urolithin-a-muscle-outcomes-are-unpoolable-and-exploratory).

⚑ And the strength number this map had been carrying needs restating. "~12% strength in one trial" resolves, on the full outcome table, to hamstring average peak torque +19.6% (2.0 to 37.2), p = 0.029 — while quadriceps torque (+7.2%, p > 0.05) and hand-grip (+2.7%, p = 0.080) in the same trial, on the same people, did not move, with no correction for having taken three measures. The other strength trial's 1RM bench press (p = 0.462) and back squat (p = 0.710) were null and had been mis-marked as significant (urolithin-a-strength-signal-is-one-outcome-in-one-trial).

What is contested, and what is not. ⚑ Not contested: the mitophagy mechanism, the muscle-biopsy gene expression, target engagement. Nothing in the re-synthesis disputes any of it. Contested: whether it produces any measurable functional benefit in a human being. After five randomised trials and 236 people there is one poolable functional outcome, its interval crosses zero, and its best case is roughly the smallest change a person would notice. That is a statement about how little has been established — not a demonstration that urolithin A fails.

Why the Status moved down rather than sideways. shared/evidence-model.md: a credible higher-tier pushback downgrades the map, and Status moves both ways. The incumbent belief rested on a systematic review and the trials beneath it; this is the same tier, from an independent group, recomputing those same trials. The map has spent thirteen months recording that consolidating was unreachable for want of a second independent group. A second group looked, and it did not confirm. For a healthy adult (GOAL.md: least work, proven levers first, low-value spend still cut), the muscle and immune goals route to resistance-training + protein-target + creatine-monohydrate + sleep-regularity + daily-steps — free, functional, poolable, independently replicated, and not sold by the people who studied them. Watch, still don't buy, and now for a better-documented reason.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • The instructive inverse of nad-precursors — both "mitochondrial-aging" supplements. NMN/NR raise NAD⁺ but are functionally null (nad-precursors-null-on-muscle-mass-function); urolithin A shows mechanistic engagement (mitophagy) plus a modest strength signal. Worth ranking side by side at regimen time: UA has the better mechanism-to-function story, but both remain unproven for a healthy adult and both sit below the proven muscle levers. Not a co-recommendation — a comparison.
  • Adjacent to the muscle cluster (resistance-training, protein-target, creatine-monohydrate). UA targets the same goal (muscle strength/quality) via a different mechanism (mitophagy vs training stimulus / substrate). No interaction found; it would be an add-on to, never a substitute for, the training base. No safety_flags set.
  • A discipline marker like taurine — but with more human data. Another hyped longevity supplement, but unlike taurine (human premise debunked) UA has actual randomized muscle data — so it's a watch, not a debunk. The shared caution is the sponsor-generated evidence base.
  • The sponsor-independence problem is now the map's defining gap (2026-07-09). Three human evidence lines — the 5-RCT systematic review, its muscle/mitophagy findings, and now a clean Nature Aging RCT — all trace to Amazentis. This is not a reason to disbelieve any of them (shared/study-appraisal.md: industry funding is not fatal on its own), but it is the reason consolidating is unreachable: the evidence model requires ≥2 independent groups, and there is one. Design quality cannot substitute for independence. Recorded in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
  • ⚑ The independence gap closed on 2026-08-07, and closing it cost the map its functional claim. Dao 2026 is the second group, and it declares no commercial or financial relationships. It did not run a new trial — it re-synthesised the existing five, and found one poolable outcome whose significance depended on a unit-of-analysis error (urolithin-a-pooled-walk-test-loses-significance-on-arm-combination). The gap this map recorded for thirteen months has been answered in the least flattering way available: not by contradiction, but by showing there was less there than the count of trials implied. The sponsor-independence entry above is superseded as a gap; it remains accurate as history.
