Urolithin A — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-07-09) — auto-coined 2026-07-08, flagged for human review. Coined by a systematic review of human RCTs of urolithin A, a gut-microbiome postbiotic (from ellagitannins in pomegranate, berries, walnuts) that stimulates mitophagy. Opened emerging: a genuine mechanism with a modest, inconsistent functional payoff on a small, largely sponsor-generated evidence base. A second, cleanly randomized trial landed 2026-07-09 — and it deepens the same sponsor's evidence rather than replicating it independently, so the Status does not move.

What we believe now

Urolithin A shows more than most longevity supplements — mechanistic target engagement plus a modest muscle signal — but the functional payoff is inconsistent and the evidence is small and manufacturer-led. Across 5 RCTs (250 adults, 10–1000 mg/day, 28 days–4 months), UA increased muscle strength and endurance (~12% strength in one trial), produced a dose-dependent anti-inflammatory effect (CRP, acylcarnitines), and upregulated mitophagy/mitochondrial genes in muscle biopsies. But it did not improve physical function, body composition, or cardiovascular outcomes, and did not change mitochondrial maximal ATP, biogenesis, dynamics, or gut microbiota. The instructive contrast: UA is the inverse of nad-precursors — where NMN/NR raise a biomarker (NAD⁺) with no functional benefit, UA at least engages its mechanism and nudges strength. That earns a watch slot; it does not earn a buy.

A third domain, same pattern (added 2026-07-09). A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 50 healthy adults aged 45–70 (Denk 2025, Nature Aging) found 1,000 mg/day UA for 28 days expanded naive-like, less exhausted CD8⁺ T cells (+0.50 pp, P=0.0437) and raised CD8⁺ fatty-acid oxidation (+14.72 pp, P=0.0061), with coherent secondary movement across mitochondrial biogenesis, NK cells, monocytes, TNF secretion, and ex vivo bacterial uptake (urolithin-a-immune-cell-rejuvenation-rct). It is the methodologically cleanest UA evidence the vault holds — and it changes nothing, for three reasons. It is not independent (three authors are Amazentis employees, one is its president, Amazentis funded the trial and supplied Mitopure). Every endpoint is a surrogate (no infection, no vaccine response, no function). The lead result is thin (a 0.50-percentage-point subset shift at P=0.0437, from a large immune panel, over 28 days, in 50 people).

The tension worth keeping visible (why emerging): the base is small and largely designed/run by the manufacturer (Amazentis / Mitopure) — a real bias-watch flag — with surrogate-dominant endpoints and an internal inconsistency (strength up, physical function null). Adding a third sponsor-linked surrogate study deepens one line of evidence; it does not create a second. Per shared/evidence-model.md, consolidating requires ≥2 independent groups — so the map stays emerging precisely because the new trial's design quality cannot substitute for its independence. Independent, non-Amazentis replication on a functional or clinical endpoint — infection rate, vaccine response, grip strength, walking speed — is what would move it. For a healthy adult (GOAL.md: least work, proven levers first, low-value spend still cut), the muscle and immune goals route to resistance-training + protein-target + creatine-monohydrate + sleep-regularity + daily-steps — free, functional, and not sold by the people who studied them. Watch, don't buy.

Status unchanged (emerging): not the independent replication the map's gap asks for.
  • 2026-07-08 — Ingested Hodzic Kuerec 2024 (Ageing Research Reviews, systematic review, 5 RCTs, n=250): UA increased muscle strength/endurance, lowered inflammation dose-dependently, upregulated mitophagy genes in muscle biopsies; null on physical function, anthropometrics, CV outcomes, and mitochondrial ATP/biogenesis/dynamics/microbiota; small, sponsor-generated base. Auto-coins the map at emerging — real mechanism, modest and inconsistent functional payoff.
