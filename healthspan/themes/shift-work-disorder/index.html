What we believe now

Nearly every treatment tried for shift work disorder shows a real but modest effect at low-to-very-low certainty — a toolkit of partial levers, not a fix. Armodafinil has the best-evidenced alertness benefit (including a high-certainty reduction in self-reported accidents) but also this map's one documented serious adverse event. Melatonin and ramelteon modestly extend daytime sleep (15–76 minutes, timing- dependent) without reliably improving subjective sleep quality. Bright light therapy — despite the largest trial base of any category — is the least consistent result, very-low certainty throughout, with one small trial finding it actively worsened sleep latency. Caffeine, naps, and CBT-I each show small, real, very-low-certainty gains. Nothing tested restores normal sleep architecture or alertness to a non-shift-worker baseline.

⚠ For David (GOAL.md): not directly actionable — David is not a shift worker and GOAL.md names no occupational circadian-misalignment constraint. This map exists to hold the evidence honestly for anyone reading this vault who is, and as the comparator case for sleep-regularity's regularity-matters framework: shift work is the clearest real-world case where consistent sleep timing simply is not available, and this review is the honest account of how little substitutes for it.
