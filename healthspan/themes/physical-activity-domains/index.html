Physical activity domains — where the movement happens (exercise)

Status: emerging (2026-08-19) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined on a single source: a Lancet Public Health meta-analysis of 74 studies and 4.2 million adults finding that the association between physical activity and dementia changes sign depending on the domain the activity occurs in. Opened emerging because the structural claim, however well tested, rests on five occupational studies, two commuting studies and one household study, at the authors' own GRADE certainty of very low to low.

⚑ This map is not an intervention. It is a modifier on every other activity map in the vault. Nothing is done here; the claim is that "physical activity" is not one exposure, and results measured on one domain may not transfer to another.

What we believe now

The vault's activity-and-outcome evidence is almost entirely about discretionary movement, and it has been read as though it were about movement.

Feter 2026 pooled 74 cohort and case-control studies (4,227,297 adults, 64,009 dementia cases, median 10 years). Overall, high activity carried all-cause dementia RR 0.80 (95% CI 0.75–0.86), Alzheimer's 0.79 (0.70–0.90), vascular dementia 0.71 (0.58–0.87) — the familiar result. Split by domain, the direction reverses: leisure-time 0.76 (0.65–0.88) and household 0.85 (0.74–0.98) protective; occupational 1.20 (1.01–1.42) and commuting 1.10 (1.03–1.18) harmful. Domain moderated the association at p=0.0004, the dose-response curves ran opposite ways, and — the test that matters — the occupational risk survived adjustment for total activity volume, so it is not simply "manual workers move more." It also survived leave-one-out and survived deleting every low-quality study.

What is defensible is the negative claim. The protective estimate measured in leisure cohorts does not transfer to occupational ones, and averaging the domains together produces a pooled number that describes neither. What is not established is that occupational movement is the harmful agent. The authors read their own result as largely confounded by what travels with physically demanding work — psychosocial stress, low autonomy, shift work, air and noise pollution, little cognitive novelty — and say so in print. A meta-analysis of observational cohorts cannot separate the lifting from the life.

Why it sits at emerging and stays there until something else lands. One source. Very low to low GRADE certainty on every cell. Considerable heterogeneity (I² 97.4% overall). Self-reported exposure nearly everywhere — and the authors note that accelerometry cannot currently distinguish domains at all, so the defining variable of this map has no objective instrument. Two moderators (shorter follow-up, screening-test diagnosis) run the direction reverse causation predicts, which inflates the magnitude of the protective side without touching the domain claim.

⚑ NEW 2026-08-20 — A SECOND SOURCE, AND IT WORKS THE PROBLEM FROM THE OTHER END: WHAT IS THE LEISURE DOMAIN ACTUALLY DOING? Feter 2026 established where the protective association lives. Iso-Markku 2026 asks how, by measuring the molecules eleven years downstream in 564 men whose activity was recorded at 56. Midlife activity tracked neurofilament light chain and GFAP — general neuronal damage and astrocyte activation — and left both Alzheimer's-specific markers, p-tau217 and the Aβ42/40 ratio, flatly untouched, with tight intervals centred on zero. ⚑ The nulls are the useful half: this is a demonstrated absence over the range the study could detect, not a failure to look. Animal models show exercise clearing amyloid and tau; human studies have largely failed to reproduce it, and this is one more failure with better markers than most. If the dementia association in the big cohorts is real, the mechanism is somewhere other than plaque clearance — most plausibly vascular. ⚠ And this map's own warning applies harder to the new source than to the one that founded it: Feter's exposure was self-reported across 74 cohorts; this one is self-reported for a single week, zero-inflated at 30%, in one cohort of white male US veterans. Prospective design and a laboratory outcome must not launder a one-week activity recall into a fine-grained exposure measure.
