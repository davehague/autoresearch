Exercise for blood pressure — which modality, and how much (exercise)

Status: emerging (2026-08-19) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined on a single source: a network meta-analysis of 30 randomised trials and 2,137 adults with diagnosed hypertension, comparing ten exercise modalities head-to-head with RoB 2 and CINeMA/GRADE certainty for all 90 comparisons. Opened emerging on one source and a thin network, not on weak evidence — the leading estimate is one of only 17 high-certainty ratings the paper issued.

⚑ Coined because the vault had eight blood-pressure findings and no place to compare them — scattered across stretching, resistance-training, anthocyanins, nad-precursors, multivitamins and two ginger notes, each recorded as a side effect of a map about something else. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

What we believe now

Combining aerobic and resistance work in the same programme is the best-evidenced exercise lever for blood pressure, and the ranking tables that get quoted are not the evidence.

Against standard care, in adults with diagnosed hypertension: combined training lowered systolic pressure by 9.52 mmHg (95% CI −13.47 to −5.56) and diastolic by 5.84 mmHg (−8.51 to −3.16) — both at high GRADE certainty under CINeMA. Also significant on systolic: moderate-intensity continuous training −5.90 mmHg (−9.74 to −2.05) and isometric handgrip training −5.40 mmHg (−9.97 to −0.83). A ~9.5 mmHg systolic reduction is in the range a single first-line antihypertensive drug delivers, on a hard endpoint rather than a surrogate.

⚑ The convergence is the strongest thing here. Combined training now tops four independent literatures on four different outcomes — blood pressure (this source), endothelial function, glycaemic control, and all-cause mortality. Four evidence bases, one answer: doing both beats doing either.

What the leaderboard says and why to ignore it. High-intensity continuous training tops the diastolic SUCRA at 93.4% — on low-certainty evidence from 4 trials and 127 people, while second-place combined training is high certainty on 284. Across the whole network, 49 of 90 comparisons are very-low certainty and only 17 are high. The paper's authors state all of this themselves, unprompted, including the null-versus-equivalence distinction, twice. That candour is why the source is graded above the SUCRA papers this vault has declined.

Why it sits at emerging. One source. A thin network — 2,137 participants across ten nodes, with 4–7 trials of direct evidence per key comparison. Only 4 of 30 trials at low risk of bias. An unblindable, measurement-sensitive outcome, with many trials using office readings rather than 24-hour ambulatory monitoring. A three-database search. Modality nodes that pool genuinely different programmes under one label. And interventions running 8–12 weeks, so nothing here measures persistence.

⚠ Population, and it is the binding limit. Every trial enrolled adults with diagnosed hypertension. Blood-pressure interventions produce much smaller effects in normotensive people. None of these millimetre figures transfer to someone with normal blood pressure, and this map recommends nothing to anyone.
