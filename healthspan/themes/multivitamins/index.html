Multivitamin & mineral supplements — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-07-08) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. One of the most widely used supplements in the world, finally scored against a large evidence base — with a genuinely mixed verdict, not a clean debunk. Coined by an umbrella (rapid) review of 19 meta-analyses (5.5M participants). Opened emerging: the longevity case is null, but specific cognition/BP signals are real.

What we believe now

A daily multivitamin does not extend life — but it isn't useless either. Across 19 meta-analyses (5,535,426 participants), multivitamin-mineral (MVM) supplements showed no benefit on all-cause mortality (RCT-inclusive), plus null on COVID-19 outcomes and visual acuity, and a harm signal for age-related macular degeneration progression. So as a longevity lever — the reason it would sit in this vault — it's null, the same "no hard-outcome payoff" pattern as nad-precursors, and a direct counter to supplement-aisle marketing.

The non-null side (why emerging/mixed, not fading): RCT meta-analyses do show MVM improves global cognition, episodic memory, and immediate recall in older/cognitively-intact adults, and lowers systolic blood pressure in at-risk populations; observational data link it to lower colorectal-cancer/CHD/cataract/ hip-fracture risk (but not overall mortality, breast/prostate cancer, or stroke). So multivitamins are not a debunk (taurine) — they're a mixed bag: worthless for longevity, modestly useful for a specific cognition or BP goal. Whether to take one depends on that goal, not on "living longer."

The honest cap. This is a rapid umbrella review of heterogeneous meta-analyses, and its own authors flag that "MVM" is inconsistently defined across trials — different formulations blurred into one label, effects varying by population/age/health status. So the mortality null is robust for "a generic multivitamin," while the positive signals are formulation- and population-specific. Capped suggestive.

For David (GOAL.md, not-maxxing, evidence-first, replete diet): a general multivitamin is a "considered & not doing" as a longevity play; if a specific cognition/BP aim exists, that's a clinician conversation, and any product would need third-party testing. No dose is set here.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-21 — The umbrella-review longevity null gets a single large RCT that lands in the same place; Status held emerging. Ingested the multivitamin arm of the COSMOS parent trial (Sesso 2022, Am J Clin Nutr 115(6):1490–1500 — randomised, double-blind, 2×2 factorial, n=21,442 healthy older adults, Centrum Silver vs placebo, median 3.6 y, ITT; appraised reliable): no significant effect on total CVD, total invasive cancer, or all-cause mortality (multivitamin-no-cvd-cancer-mortality-benefit-cosmos). Where Wang 2026's mortality null was assembled from heterogeneous meta-analyses of inconsistently-defined "MVM," this is one large blinded RCT corroborating the longevity null at RCT tier — graded strong. It does not touch the map's non-null side (targeted cognition/systolic-BP signals), which COSMOS did not test. Shares its 21,442 participants with cocoa-flavanols (the factorial cocoa arm, also null on its primary).

  • 2026-07-31 — The food-side mirror of this map's central null arrives, and the vault reads it as confounding rather than as a case for the food matrix. Status held emerging (Liu 2026, Journal of Nutritional Science 15:e53, prospective cohort analysis of NHANES 2003–2018 linked to the National Death Index; 34,955 US adults, 347,580 person-years, 4,456 deaths (1,368 cardiovascular, 1,038 cancer); exposure from the average of two non-consecutive 24-hour dietary recalls; unregistered, no prespecified analysis plan; ⚠ funded by the research institute of a commercial nutrition company — recorded per the bias-watch rule, though the conclusion does not favour supplementation; CC BY PDF archived from cambridge.org at the browser rung after rung 1 again reported no free PDF). Dietary vitamin E, top vs bottom quintile: all-cause mortality HR 0.69 (0.58–0.83), cardiovascular 0.67 (0.48–0.92), cancer 0.67 (0.46–0.96), all with significant trends; carotenoids 0.77 (0.67–0.88); the six-nutrient composite index 0.82 (0.70–0.97) all-cause and 0.61 (0.45–0.84) cancer (dietary-antioxidant-intake-tracks-with-lower-mortality). ⚑ Set that against what this map already holds from randomised evidence: 21,442 people in COSMOS and 19 meta-analyses across 5.5 million, and no mortality benefit from the pill (multivitamin-no-cvd-cancer-mortality-benefit-cosmos, multivitamin-no-mortality-benefit). Two readings exist — the food matrix does something the isolated nutrient does not, or dietary antioxidant intake is a marker for the kind of life that produces lower mortality. The vault takes the second, because it requires no new biology, explains the randomised nulls, and is what the design can support: the models adjust for income, smoking, alcohol and activity and for none of education, overall diet quality or healthcare access. ⚠ The internal structure settles it, and it is worse than the headline. Four of six nutrients have no significant dose-response trend (p-trend 0.745, 0.573, 0.551, 0.142) while the paper reports significant middle-quintile hazard ratios for each; the paper's own weighted quantile sum analysis names vitamin A or C as the main contributors while the single-nutrient models name vitamin E; the all-cause association reverses in non-Hispanic Black participants (HR 1.03, 1.01–1.06, p-interaction 0.017); and the cancer association vanishes entirely in adults 65 and over (HR 1.00, 0.96–1.05). Roughly 90 uncorrected comparisons (dietary-antioxidant-index-internal-inconsistencies). ⚑ And the paper's headline sentence is one its own design cannot support: it argues diverse dietary antioxidants are "superior to single-nutrient strategies" — while excluding supplement use from the exposure entirely, so it never measured a single-nutrient strategy at all. ✅ What does survive, and it is smaller: the restricted cubic splines are U- or L-shaped for vitamins A and C, zinc and selenium — benefit weakens or reverses at the highest intakes, the same "more is not better" shape this vault carries from three other literatures. ⚠ Fatal flag #1 was the close call and did NOT fire, on one ground only: HR 0.69 is not a small effect. The claim is practice-changing and major confounders are unadjusted — two of the flag's three conditions — and the vault records that a reviewer could reasonably fire it and land unreliable. Appraised mixed, capped suggestive; open-question. Status emerging HELD — a suggestive-capped observational source cannot move a map, and this one points away from supplementation regardless.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-07-20 — this map now shares a trial with a newly-coined one: cocoa-flavanols. COSMOS is a 2 × 2 factorial trial of cocoa extract and multivitamins in the same 21,442 healthy older adults, and the vault has just ingested its cocoa arm (via a secondary re-analysis, appraised unreliable for analytic multiplicity). Two consequences worth recording. (a) Anyone reading either map should know the two interventions come from one trial and one population — the maps are separate, the participants were not. (b) The cocoa arm's story is a cautionary mirror of this map's own: a pre-specified null (Cox HR 0.90, 0.79–1.03) re-analysed by a different estimator until it crossed p = 0.05 at a net absolute benefit of −0.54% (cocoa-flavanol-cvd-benefit-is-estimator-dependent). Two supplements, one trial, one population, two nulls.
  • Same "biomarker/association without the hard outcome" family as nad-precursors. Both raise a proxy or carry associations but fail the longevity/functional endpoint that matters — the vault's recurring supplement caution. Different mechanism, same regimen verdict (not a longevity buy).
  • Redundancy with single-nutrient maps. A multivitamin overlaps vitamin-d (contested; VITAL nulls), magnesium-glycinate, and omega-3 as a bundled, lower-dose delivery of the same nutrients — so it is not additive on top of those, and its generic low doses are unlikely to correct a real deficiency. Targeted single-nutrient correction (if a blood test shows a deficiency) is the clinician-led alternative, not a blanket MVM.
  • Global-ripple: no safety interaction with other vault interventions found. The one harm signal (AMD progression) is population-specific (relates to high-dose antioxidant/zinc formulations in AREDS-type contexts), noted not graded as a cross-intervention edge.
