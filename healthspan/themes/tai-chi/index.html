Tai Chi & traditional Chinese exercise (Baduanjin, Qigong, Yijinjing, Wuqinxi) — exercise

Status: emerging (2026-08-18) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single umbrella review, and that review's own headline number is the reason the map opens at emerging rather than higher: of 547 outcome associations across 92 meta-analyses and 1,744 randomised trials, twenty-three (4.2%) reach high certainty. ⚑ The second reason is sharper — the paper names its high-certainty winners and publishes no effect size for any of them. A finding can be certain and clinically invisible, and this vault has been burned by exactly that shape twice in the same joint. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Tai Chi is the best-evidenced free, equipment-free, joint-sparing exercise practice this vault holds — and the strongest claim anyone can honestly make for it is that five specific things about it are reliably true, with the size of every one of them unpublished.

⚑ The short list, and it is a short list on purpose. Chen 2026 graded every published association it could find on traditional Chinese exercise for musculoskeletal disorders — 547 of them — and twenty-three survived at high certainty (traditional-chinese-exercise-certainty-collapses-across-547-associations). Named among them: physical function in knee osteoarthritis; mental health in knee osteoarthritis and neck pain; sleep quality in fibromyalgia; and, for Tai Chi specifically, chronic musculoskeletal pain and stiffness, and bone mineral density in osteopenia/osteoporosis. Another 179 associations (32.7%) reached moderate certainty. Roughly 63% of everything this literature has published sits at low or very low certainty.

⚠ The map's founding weakness is that certainty and magnitude have been separated, and only one of them was published. GRADE tells you how confident to be that an estimate is near the truth; it says nothing about whether the estimate is worth having. The review names five winners and publishes no point estimate, no interval and no standardised mean difference for any of them (tai-chi-knee-osteoarthritis-function-is-high-certainty-evidence). This vault holds two live examples of why that gap matters, both in the same joint: exercise-hip-osteoarthritis-benefit-real-but-below-clinical-threshold (a Cochrane effect its own authors called "unlikely to be clinically meaningful") and celecoxib-knee-oa-pain-advantage-is-below-clinical-perceptibility. Whether Tai Chi's knee-OA result belongs in that company is unanswerable from anything retrievable.

⚑ The bone finding is the most robust thing here, and it fits a rule this vault wrote four times over. Four separate sources have now tested a bone add-on with the training held constant and found nothing: creatine-bone-bmd-null, rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd, weighted-vest-adds-no-bone-density-once-the-exercise-is-controlled-for, bone-density-network-ranks-sites-where-no-intervention-differed. The standing reading: loading the skeleton is what moves bone, and the accessories sold alongside the loading do not add to it. ⚑ Tai Chi is not an accessory — it is weight-bearing, long single-leg-stance, slow continuously-loaded eccentric movement. It is loading, and its BMD result is the same rule seen from the other side (tai-chi-raises-bone-density-and-it-is-loading-not-an-accessory). It is also the only high-certainty association in the review measured by a machine (DXA) rather than by a person filling in a scale.

⚠ Nothing retrievable isolates Tai Chi from ordinary exercise. Comparators across this literature are usual care, education or waitlist. Some unknown share of every finding above is the benefit of moving at all — the comparator problem this vault has now recorded five times, most recently on balance-training and weighted-vest. Combined with structurally impossible blinding and mostly self-reported outcomes, this is the ceiling on how far the map can move without a head-to-head trial against matched conventional exercise.

⚑ Why a map, when this run refused two other candidates. The vault's four-part coining bar — an isolating contrast · a machine-measured outcome · low risk of bias · a pre-specified dose — set on beetroot-juice (2026-08-02) and applied since to anthocyanins, lycopene, ginger and weighted-vest:

LegVerdict
An isolating contrast⚠ PARTIAL. Randomised throughout, but comparators are usual care / education / waitlist. No contrast against matched conventional exercise is retrievable, so "is it the Tai Chi or is it moving?" is open.
A machine-measured outcome⚠ PARTIAL. BMD is DXA-derived. Function, pain, stiffness, mental health and sleep are self-reported scales in an unblindable intervention.
Low risk of bias✅ PASS, and it is the strongest leg. AMSTAR-2 applied with 70.7% of the 92 included meta-analyses rated high quality; GRADE applied to all 547 associations, not a selected subset; PROSPERO-registered; nine databases including PEDro and SPORTDiscus.
A pre-specified dose✅ PASS on direction, FAIL on numbers. Duration, frequency and weekly volume were analysed as subgroups and reported with a consistent direction — longer, more frequent, higher volume did better for knee OA and osteoporosis. The thresholds are behind the paywall and this map states none.

Two passes and two partials. ⚑ Coined — and the deciding argument is not the score, it is that this vault already had a homeless Tai Chi finding. On 2026-08-15 a Bayesian network of 423 RCTs and 33,901 older adults named Tai Chi the best modality for global balance, and balance-training could only record it as one name in a ranking whose intervals overlap (balance-modality-rankings-rest-on-overlapping-credible-intervals). Three days later a second, independent, differently-designed synthesis puts the same practice in the top 4.2% for a painful knee and for bone. Two large syntheses, one week apart, on a free practice this vault had no home for.

⚑ NEW 2026-08-23 — THE MAP'S FOUNDING COMPLAINT GETS ANSWERED, AND THE ANSWER IS A SECOND PROBLEM. Status held emerging. This map opened five days ago saying that certainty and magnitude had been separated and only certainty was published. Ma 2026 supplies magnitudes — 17 randomised trials, 984 older adults with diagnosed sarcopenia, seven outcomes, PROSPERO-registered, RoB 2 and GRADE applied, combined-modality trials excluded by design. ⚑ And only two of seven clear a clinical threshold. The two that do are about how a person MOVES; the two that do not are about TISSUE. Gait speed +0.12 m/s clears the 0.10 m/s substantial-change bar and SPPB +1.37 points clears the 0.5–1.0-point one; handgrip +1.13 kg is four to six percent of the diagnostic cutoff and ASMI +0.20 kg/m² is about two percent of it (traditional-chinese-exercise-sarcopenia-effects-are-mostly-below-clinical-thresholds). Timed Up-and-Go −2.80 s is large and rests on five trials and 173 people.

⚠ Every one of those seven numbers is measured against people doing nothing. Sixteen of seventeen comparators were health education, non-intervention, conventional therapy, routine care or remote guidance; exactly one trial, with sixteen per arm, compared against resistance training (the-sarcopenia-tce-literature-has-one-trial-against-real-exercise). ⚑ This is the vault's EIGHTH instance of the same defect, and the map's own founding note already carried it (traditional-chinese-sports-ranking-is-unseparable-from-its-comparator).

⚠ And the practice this map is named for does not carry the strength result. Split by practice on handgrip: Tai Chi +0.88 kg (−0.06 to 1.82, p = 0.07) — null, against Yi Jin Jing +2.27 and Baduanjin +1.13. On muscle mass the ordering flips and Tai Chi is the only clearly positive practice (+0.25, 0.12 to 0.38). All between-subgroup tests non-significant. The honest statement is not that one practice beats another — it is that this literature cannot tell them apart, and the umbrella term is doing more work than the evidence supports (tai-chi-does-not-carry-the-strength-result-in-its-own-literature). The map already recorded that 37.3% of its founding evidence does not name its practice; this is the harder version, where the practice IS named and the answer still moves.

⚠ Two diagnostics that should have flagged this literature both returned reassurance, and neither can bear it. I² is 0% for six of seven outcomes across trials running 4 to 72 weeks, in three practices, against seven comparator types, in participants whose group mean ages span 62 to 88 — and that implausible homogeneity is the paper's stated justification for fixed-effects primary models, which narrow every interval quoted above (near-zero-heterogeneity-across-wildly-heterogeneous-trials-is-a-signal-not-a-reassurance). Egger's test is declared negative on pools of 13, 8, 7, 5, 3 and 2 studies, far below where it has power. ⚠ 25 of 72 eligible full texts were unobtainable and dropped — a third of the literature unread. ⚑ And the abstract carries an arithmetically impossible confidence interval (TUG "−2.80, 95% CI −2.55 to −2.05"), alongside a subgroup interval whose upper bound equals its point estimate and a cohort mean age copied verbatim from a single trial.

⚑ What this changes on the map, and what it does not. It closes off a claim nobody should have been making — that these practices build strength or muscle competitively with lifting; the vault's own networks put resistance training first on exactly those outcomes (moderate-intensity-resistance-training-ranks-first-for-handgrip-in-sarcopenia). It leaves the map's actual case untouched, because that case was never about strength: knee-joint function, balance, bone loading, zero cost, no impact and no axial load. And it adds a second population — diagnosed sarcopenia, mean ages 62 to 88 — in which the map still does not extend to healthy adults.

⚠ What this map does NOT claim. It does not claim Tai Chi beats conventional exercise — nothing tested that. It does not claim any of the 23 findings is large. It does not claim anything about fracture (BMD is a surrogate; nobody measured fracture). It does not extend to healthy adults — the populations are people with diagnosed knee OA, neck pain, fibromyalgia and osteopenia. And 37.3% of the underlying evidence does not say which practice it studied, so "traditional Chinese exercise" is doing real work as an umbrella term here.

⚑ For David (GOAL.md: least work for maximum benefit, adherence-first, knee/joint-safe, returning to pickleball, cost a tiebreaker): this is the most GOAL.md-shaped intervention the vault has ingested in weeks, and it still does not enter the regimen tonight.

The fit is unusually clean. No equipment, no cost beyond a class or a video, no impact, no axial load, no eccentric spikes — the exact opposite trade to weighted-vest, which this vault gated on his knee three days ago. Its two best-evidenced targets, knee-joint function and balance, are the two things his knee-recovery gate and his pickleball goal are actually about. And exercise-adherence's standing correction applies in its favour: a practice done at home, on any schedule, at zero cost, outranks a better point estimate that needs a facility.

⚠ Every reason to hold is evidential, not practical. The populations have diagnosed disease and he has a healing March 2026 patellar contusion. No effect size is retrievable for anything. Nothing separates it from ordinary exercise. And the dose that drove the subgroup gains is paywalled, so this map sets none (shared/safety.md). The honest position: a strong thing to raise with his clinician for the knee — specifically whether it fits alongside, not instead of, the rehabilitation already in progress. Nothing changes in the protocol.
