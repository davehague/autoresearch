Exercise and sleep — sleep

Status: emerging (2026-08-09) — ⚑ auto-forked this run from daily-steps (at 10 dated bullets / 3 declared sub-threads), flagged for human review. Six syntheses of exercise and sleep accumulated on a map named for step count between 2026-07-12 and 2026-08-06, and five of them were not about walking. The parent map logged the misfiling against itself on 2026-08-06 and predicted the next fork check would have to resolve it; this is that resolution. Opened emerging and it cannot currently open higher: every source in this strand is capped suggestive or below, the certainty ratings run from very low to low, and the largest synthesis in the field rates its own headline VERY LOW at n = 23,523.

What we believe now

Exercise almost certainly does something for sleep. The magnitude is unknowable from this literature, the instrument you choose determines the answer you get, and the benefit concentrates in people who already sleep badly — which is the opposite of who is usually reading about it.

(1) The instrument decides the answer. Four syntheses, four different instrument profiles, and each one's strongest result is the one its design is weakest at measuring:

The two claims the vault was most tempted by were each undercut from the other side. It recorded, with visible pleasure, that the machine moved (+34 min total sleep time) where every wind-down supplement moves only the questionnaire — and graded it single-study anyway, because those objective outcomes rested on 2–3 trials each. Zhou's 31-RCT network, in the population most likely to show it, then found no total-sleep-time effect at all (exercise-does-not-increase-total-sleep-time). Meanwhile Kong confirmed the subjective direction the vault had refused to write off — g = −0.76, landing inside the very interval that made walking-subjective-sleep-quality-unresolved an open question rather than a null (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-large-effect) — and Kong's own GRADE rating of that finding is VERY LOW: not one of its 21 trials is low risk of bias, all are open-label with self-reported outcomes, and its publication-bias tests fire at p < 0.001. Its headline 99.97% posterior probability describes the model, not the world (bayesian-certainty-cannot-repair-biased-inputs).

(2) The population is the quiet disqualifier, and 200 randomised trials now say so directly. Only 3 of Kong's 21 trials enrolled healthy adults; eight enrolled diagnosed sleep disorders — and the model's strongest moderator is baseline sleep badness (β = −0.302): the worse your sleep, the more exercise helps it (walking-sleep-benefit-scales-with-baseline-sleep-problem). Liang's 200-trial synthesis reports the same thing as its primary finding, calling it a "benefit-on-demand pattern" (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers). This is, almost in its entirety, a treatment literature — and GOAL.md describes David's sleep as a foundation to protect, not a problem to treat. A benefit concentrated in bad sleepers is, for someone who sleeps adequately, a reason to expect less.

(3) ⚠ And the quality gradient runs backwards, which complicates (2). The one trial in Nanjie's set rated low risk of bias — Li's 12-week supervised Baduanjin trial, also the largest at n = 206 and the only one with blinded assessors — enrolled students already sleeping fine (PSQI 3.7/4.1), while the trials with the worst sleepers are the ones nobody blinded (the-only-low-risk-student-sleep-trial-studied-good-sleepers). "The benefit concentrates in poor sleepers" and "the benefit concentrates where measurement is weakest" are not separated anywhere in this literature.

(4) Scale does not launder design. Two hundred open-label trials asking people how they slept produce a very-low-certainty answer for the same reason twenty-one did (exercise-sleep-certainty-very-low-at-200-trials). The pile got bigger; the instrument did not get better. The one genuine methodological gain is that objective sleep efficiency moved in agreement with the subjective outcomes — the first cross-instrument convergence here, in 20 of 200 trials (exercise-sleep-objective-and-subjective-converge-weakly). The one genuine comparator gain is that walking ranked among the top modalities against ACTIVE controls, alongside qigong and HIIT (walking-among-top-sleep-modalities-against-active-controls) — ranking recorded, leaderboard declined, per the vault's standing SUCRA discipline.

What this map does NOT license. No duration prescription (Liang's ~25-week optimum is a meta-regression across heterogeneous trials, recorded not adopted), no session length, no frequency, no modality ranking read off a SUCRA table. shared/safety.md forbids converting a published range into a prescription.

For David, the practical translation is unchanged by the fork: the exercise he already does is the whole intervention, the sleep bonus is small, unmeasured and free, and it is not counted twice in the regimen — daily-steps is recommended on mortality and nothing else. What this map does support is a negative conclusion, which is cheap and safe: on the same subjective instrument the entire supplement shelf is scored on, exercise's effect is several times larger than anything l-theanine, chamomile, melatonin or magnesium-glycinate can show — so it is one more reason not to buy a sleep supplement, and no reason to add anything at all.

  • 2026-07-19 — The field's largest synthesis lands, confirms this strand's own inference, and changes nothing; Status held emerging (Liang 2026, Sleep Medicine Reviews 86, systematic review + Bayesian multimethod meta-analysis, 200 RCTs, n = 23,523 adults ≥18; six databases to Aug 2025; RoB 2 in duplicate; Bayesian multilevel models + MetaForest + structural equation modelling). Four findings. (2) In 20 trials reporting objective sleep efficiency, exercise produced a small but significant improvement consistent with the subjective outcomes — the first cross-instrument agreement in this literature, on 10% of the corpus (exercise-sleep-objective-and-subjective-converge-weakly). (3) Against active controls, qigong, walking and HIIT showed the largest benefits — the isolating comparison this strand requested (walking-among-top-sleep-modalities-against-active-controls); ranking recorded, leaderboard declined per the vault's five-case SUCRA discipline. (4) Certainty of evidence: VERY LOW, stated by the authors, who explicitly caution against strong conclusions (exercise-sleep-certainty-very-low-at-200-trials) — the cleanest demonstration yet that scale does not launder design. The confirmation argues for expecting less, not more: David sleeps adequately, so he sits in the subgroup where this literature's effect is smallest. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive.
