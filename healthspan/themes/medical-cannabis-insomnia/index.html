Medical cannabis for insomnia — sleep

Status: emerging (2026-10-13) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined by the first Cochrane systematic review to ask this question directly (9 RCTs, 537 adults): medical cannabis versus placebo for insomnia. The answer is not a clean negative — every efficacy estimate is too imprecise to call — but the one precise estimate in the whole review is an eleven-fold increase in adverse events.

Why a new map rather than a note on an existing sleep map. The vault already tracks several pharmacological and behavioral candidates for insomnia — cbt-insomnia, melatonin, orexin-antagonists — but holds nothing on cannabis/cannabinoids specifically, despite increasing clinical use and a genuine evidence gap current guidelines acknowledge. A Cochrane-tier source on a previously untracked candidate warrants its own map rather than a stray note.

What we believe now

For a general insomnia population, medical cannabis has no demonstrated clinically meaningful sleep benefit over placebo, and the clearest signal in the evidence is a side-effect cost. Across sleep quality/satisfaction, total sleep time, and daytime functioning, every confidence interval from this Cochrane review is wide enough to be compatible with real benefit, real harm, or nothing at all — an inconclusive result, not a demonstrated null (medical-cannabis-no-clinically-meaningful-sleep-benefit-vs-placebo). The one outcome measured with any precision is adverse events, elevated roughly elevenfold versus placebo (OR 11.25, 95% CI 4.46–28.35) — low-certainty but the tightest, least heterogeneous estimate in the review (medical-cannabis-increases-adverse-events-vs-placebo). A directionally positive quality-of-life signal exists but is explicitly called "very uncertain" by the review's own authors (medical-cannabis-quality-of-life-signal-is-too-uncertain-to-act-on).

Why emerging, not contested or fading. This map opens with its first and only source, so there is no prior belief to contest and nothing established to fade from. The evidence base itself is thin — 9 trials, 537 people, high heterogeneity (I²=91%, 80%, 65% across the efficacy outcomes), and seven of nine trials used non-standardized "magistral" (compounded) preparations rather than licensed medication — exactly the profile this vault opens at emerging for, regardless of the synthesizing review's own methodological tier.

What this means next to the vault's other sleep candidates. cbt-insomnia and melatonin both carry more consistent, larger evidence bases for narrower, better-defined benefits. Against GOAL.md's framing — least work, least discomfort, nothing that trades against sleep — an intervention whose clearest effect is an elevenfold increase in adverse events, with no reliably demonstrated sleep benefit to weigh against it, does not clear the bar for consideration. No dose, no regimen slot, not even Watch — this is prescription/controlled-substance territory per shared/safety.md, and the evidence does not support it regardless.

⚑ Updated 2026-10-18 — a second meta-analysis directly contradicts the founding review's practical conclusion. Asim 2026 (10 RCTs, n=2,134) found cannabinoids significantly reduced insomnia severity and improved sleep quality by margins larger than the ~3-point minimal important difference this vault treats as clinically meaningful elsewhere — the opposite practical readout from Varela 2026's wide, zero-crossing intervals. Neither source is higher-tier once the reliability axis is applied (both land at suggestive), so Status is held at emerging rather than moved to contested — see the Through-line entry and cannabinoids-meta-analysis-finds-clinically-meaningful-insomnia-benefit for the full reasoning. The practical bottom line is unchanged: this remains prescription/controlled-substance territory with no regimen slot regardless of which synthesis is right, but a human reviewing this map should know the efficacy question is now genuinely contested in substance, if not yet in this vault's formal Status vocabulary.

Open gaps

  • No active-comparator evidence against first-line alternatives. One trial in this review compared medical cannabis to an antidepressant (amitriptyline); none compared it to CBT-I, melatonin, or orexin antagonists directly. A reader choosing between sleep-aid candidates has no head-to-head evidence here.
  • No standardization across preparations. Seven of nine trials used magistral (compounded) products of unstated, variable potency — the vault cannot characterize a "typical" cannabis-for-insomnia exposure from this evidence base.
  • No long-term safety data. All included trials are short; the adverse-event signal says nothing about risk beyond trial duration.
