Biological-age clocks & aging biomarkers (the measurement layer) — metabolic

Status: emerging (2026-07-20) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. This map is unlike every other in the vault: it is not an intervention. It grades the instruments — epigenetic clocks, inflammatory markers, functional tests — that a growing number of the vault's own sources use as outcomes. Coined from a single cohort study (Vetter 2026, BASE-II, n=1,083, 316 deaths) that put all 14 aging biomarkers consented by an expert panel for use as outcome measures in intervention studies head-to-head against mortality in one population with one analytic pipeline.

What we believe now

The vault has been citing surrogates it had never graded, and the first grading is sobering. Over recent weeks this ledger has repeatedly recorded epigenetic-age results as evidence — zone-2-cardio carries Shan 2026 (physical activity → lower GrimAge/Horvath, n=145,465), multidomain-lifestyle carries an epigenetic-clock outcome, omega-3 and vitamin-d carry DO-HEALTH's epigenetic-aging analysis — each time with the hand-wave "surrogate." This map exists so that word can be replaced with numbers. Three of them.

(1) DunedinPACE is the best of the panel, and that is a real result. Head-to-head against thirteen rivals in the same people on the same covariate set, adjudicated against 316 actual deaths, DunedinPACE was the strongest and most consistent predictor of all-cause mortality, corroborated across cause-of-death strata (dunedinpace-strongest-mortality-predictor-among-consensus-biomarkers). It is also a pace clock — rate of aging rather than attained age — which is a different and more trial-relevant object than the Horvath/Hannum generation most of the vault's existing epigenetic citations use. Four other markers also predicted: handgrip strength, IL-6, standing balance, cognitive health.

(2) Nine of the fourteen predicted nothing — including staples of geroscience trial design. CRP, gait speed, IGF-1, blood pressure, muscle mass, DNAmGDF15, the frailty phenotype, and Timed-Up-and-Go all failed in adjusted models (most-consensus-aging-biomarkers-did-not-predict-mortality). These are not fringe measures; gait speed and the frailty phenotype are routine primary endpoints. A trial in this cohort powered on either would have been powered on something that does not track the outcome anyone cares about.

(3) The whole panel discriminates weakly — C-index 0.65, and three markers get to 0.63. Fourteen of the best-regarded aging biomarkers, expert-selected, measured together, against real deaths, order a random discordant pair correctly about 65% of the time (0.5 is a coin flip). Eleven of them are collectively worth 0.02 (aging-biomarker-panels-discriminate-mortality-weakly). The field's momentum runs toward bigger panels; this says that after about three markers, more bought a rounding error.

The operating rule this map sets for the rest of the vault — predictor ≠ responder. Nothing here tests whether moving a biomarker moves mortality. A marker that forecasts death in an untouched cohort is not thereby validated as a trial endpoint, because an intervention can shift a marker without shifting the outcome. The consensus panel's stated purpose walks straight into that gap and this source does not close it. So: keep discounting epigenetic-age and biomarker outcomes in intervention studies — now with a citation rather than an instinct — and treat DunedinPACE as the best-validated prognostic marker in the panel that is still not a demonstrated trial endpoint.

(4) The operating rule now has a molecular half, from an entirely different design (new 2026-07-23). Points (1)–(3) are epidemiology: markers that fail to predict. Jacques 2026 attacks the same question from the mechanism side, pooling >15,000 methylomes across 17 tissues and running in silico perturbation modelling on the hub genes of aging co-methylation modules. The result is the least commercially convenient sentence available: most perturbations made the aging profile worse, and "the majority of aging co-methylation networks are fragile and resistant to beneficial modulation" (aging-methylation-networks-resist-beneficial-modulation). So the vault now holds two independent lines — one prognostic, one molecular — saying that aging markers are neither reliably predictive nor readily improvable. Neither is strong alone; the convergence is the point. One module bucked the trend, and it was the NAD⁺ / nicotinamide-riboside salvage pathway — which the vault records and declines, because nad-precursors already holds two higher-tier human nulls on exactly that compound (nad-methylation-module-modifiable-in-silico-only).

(5) ⚑ The operating rule is now complete, and the completion is worse for the clocks than the rule was (new 2026-08-23). Points (1)-(3) are about prediction; the rule drawn from them was predictor ≠ responder. Sehgal 2026 (Nature Medicine, TranslAGE: 51 longitudinal human intervention studies, 3,128 pre/post blood methylation samples, 16 clocks plus 94 further DNA-methylation biomarkers recomputed through one harmonised pipeline) measures the responder half for the first time at scale. More than half of the interventions moved nothing: 19 of 51 significantly lowered epigenetic age (13 after multiple-testing correction), 5 significantly raised it, and 26 had no effect at all (half-of-longevity-interventions-move-no-epigenetic-clock-at-all). ⚠ And the responsiveness that exists is not in the population that buys these tests: across the 41 studies of people without a diagnosed disease, NOT ONE biomarker's response survived multiple-testing correction, while three did in the 10 disease studies (PCPhenoAge −0.32, PCGrimAge −0.22, SystemsAge −0.213), with healthy-versus-disease gaps of 0.14–0.28 SD (epigenetic-clocks-barely-respond-in-healthy-populations). ⚑ The one clock that crossed that line is DunedinPACE — most responsive of the sixteen (down in 16 interventions, up in 1; largest pooled effect −0.089), never in disagreement with the majority, and the only clock responding equally in healthy and diseased people (difference 0.07, P = 0.25). That is the same clock Vetter's 316 deaths named as the best predictor, from a completely different design (dunedinpace-is-both-the-best-predictor-and-the-most-responsive-clock). ⚑ Two of three legs of surrogacy, on one instrument, from independent evidence — and the third leg is empty and its own authors say so. There is "no empirically defined minimal clinically important difference for DNAm clocks," and whether short-term clock changes correspond to long-term healthspan is "still unclear" (clock-responsiveness-does-not-establish-surrogacy-and-there-is-no-defined-minimal-important-difference). So the rule becomes: predictor ≠ responder, and predictor + responder ≠ surrogate.

(6) ⚠ The category split, and the conflict that has to be read alongside it (new 2026-08-23). Sorted into four bins, only pharmacological (−0.093) and lifestyle (−0.039) interventions significantly lowered epigenetic age. Supplements did not, and neither did medical procedures — with pharmacological beating every other category (versus supplements P = 0.0009). Two studies of one supplement moved five clocks in opposite directions; five senolytics studies failed both of the authors' own consistency rules; anti-TNF therapy and two Mediterranean diets were the only interventions to pass both (supplements-as-a-category-did-not-move-epigenetic-age, which writes a back-edge onto omega3-slows-epigenetic-aging). ⚠ And the conflict profile is the heaviest this vault has recorded, pointing at the paper's own recommendations: two authors hold the SystemsAge patent (named among the most responsive), three are employees of TruDiagnostic and developed OMICmAge (analysed favourably), and TruDiagnostic supplied the private half of the dataset. Flagged, not laundered (shared/evidence-model.md).

(7) ⚑ A second independent cohort names the same clock, and sharpens the operating rule with a distinction it did not yet have: predicting that you get there is not predicting how you get there (new 2026-09-03). LaCroix 2026 (WHI Memory Study, n=5,844 women, 15 clocks) replicates Vetter's ranking — third-generation "pace" clocks strongest, DunedinPACE among the top performers (OR 0.77 per SD for survival to 90) — in an independent, much larger, all-female US cohort. But its actual headline is a null the vault did not yet have a name for: none of the fifteen clocks distinguished survival to 90 with preserved cognition from survival to 90 with cognitive impairment. A biomarker validated for quantity of life can carry zero information about its quality. New sharpening of the rule: predictor of longevity ≠ predictor of cognitively healthy longevity.

Why emerging, and why this map is deliberately modest. One convenience-recruited single-city cohort (Berlin, ages 60–80, better-than-average health and SES), n=1,083, no external replication, fourteen simultaneous comparisons with no stated correction, and — most awkwardly — the headline epigenetic finding rests on a DNA-methylation subgroup with only 90 deaths, not the full 316. Confidence is thinnest exactly where the claim is boldest. Appraised mixed; every concept capped suggestive.

What it costs: nothing, and that is the point. This map does not recommend a test, a panel, or a subscription. Consumer epigenetic-age testing is a live commercial market; nothing here supports buying one, and as of 2026-08-23 there are now two reasons rather than one: the panel discriminates individuals weakly (C-index 0.65), and in healthy people the clocks barely move when you intervene — no biomarker response survived multiple-testing correction across 41 healthy-population studies. A measurement that sorts weakly, responds rarely, and has no defined threshold for a meaningful change cannot tell a healthy buyer whether anything they are doing is working. What this map does is make the vault's reading of other people's surrogate outcomes more disciplined.

Ingested LaCroix 2026 (epigenetic-clocks-cognitive-healthy-longevity-whi-lacroix-2026, Journals of Gerontology: Series A, prospective cohort, Women's Health Initiative Memory Study, n=5,844 women, baseline epigenetics 1996–1999, 15 clocks, survival to age 90 with/without cognitive impairment vs death before 90; appraised mixed; PDF archived via a Silverchair-token redirect after a false Europe PMC non-OA negative).
