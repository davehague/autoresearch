Plant sterols / stanols — nutrition

Status: emerging (2026-10-28) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single RCT testing a plant-sterol- and fiber-fortified food (soymilk) against LDL cholesterol. A genuinely new lever (competitive intestinal cholesterol absorption inhibition) with no prior home in this vault — the existing LDL-adjacent evidence (protein-target's protein-supplementation lipid side-note, mediterranean-diet's pattern-level lipid effects, statins-primary-prevention's pharmacological comparison point) each address LDL as a side-outcome of an intervention built for other reasons; this is the first source whose entire purpose is a cholesterol-lowering mechanism.

Sub-threads: none — one trial, one question (does a plant-sterol/fiber-fortified food lower LDL-C more than the same food without fortification); no separable strand yet. [reviewed 2026-10-28 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

Adding plant sterols (1 g/day free sterol) and soluble fiber (5 g/day inulin) to a soy-protein food lowers LDL-C a further ~5.5 mg/dL beyond the base food alone, over 8 weeks. This is a real, mechanistically expected effect — plant sterols competitively block intestinal cholesterol absorption — but it is modest: roughly a tenth the magnitude of what a statin typically delivers (see statins-primary-prevention for the pharmacological comparison point), and it comes from one trial whose data were collected in 2013–2014 and not published until 2026, with a retrospectively registered trial number.

Regimen implication: none yet. David's LDL status is unstated/unknown, and this is a single mid-sized trial of one commercial product formulation, not a general plant-sterol-supplement recommendation. No dose is implied beyond what this trial tested.
