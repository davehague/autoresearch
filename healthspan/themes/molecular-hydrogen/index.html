Molecular hydrogen (hydrogen-rich water, tablets, jellies) — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-08-10) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined on a single source, and coined on a null: a pre-registered, adequately-powered, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found hydrogen supplementation did not beat placebo for sleep quality. Opened emerging because that is what one trial supports — not because the evidence is ambiguous. The evidence is unusually clean; there is just one piece of it.

What we believe now

The best-designed trial of the most-marketed hydrogen claim found nothing — and the manufacturer paid for it.

Molecular hydrogen is sold into the longevity and wellness market as hydrogen-rich water, hydrogen-generating tablets and hydrogen-infused foods, on antioxidant and anti-inflammatory claims, with sleep quality among the most prominent consumer-facing promises. Higashikawa 2026 tested exactly that promise: 44 healthy Japanese adults with poor sleep quality, randomised to 0.9 mg of hydrogen per day (three 0.3 mg jelly packets) or an identical placebo, for 8 weeks. The pre-specified primary outcome was change in the OSA-MA sleep inventory. It was null. So were the VAS for sleep quality, the PSQI and the STAI. Both groups improved substantially from their own baselines; neither beat the other (hydrogen-supplementation-null-on-sleep-quality-in-powered-rct).

Why this null carries more weight than its sample size suggests, and it is worth being specific. Sleep supplement trials are usually unregistered, unpowered, unblinded, or all three. This one was pre-registered before enrolment (UMIN000053237), had a real a priori power calculation — 21 per group at 90% power with a Holm-adjusted 1% alpha — used a placebo matched on appearance, texture, taste and smell, and formally verified that blinding held (40.9% of participants guessed their allocation correctly, i.e. chance). Adherence was 95%. Dropout was zero. shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 exists to stop "we saw nothing" being read as "there is nothing" when no power analysis was done. Here one was done, in advance, and the trial met it.

⚑ And the funding cuts the unusual way. "This study was funded by Shinryo Corporation (Fukuoka, Japan). The experimental jellies were provided by the company" — while the competing-interests statement declares none. Per shared/evidence-model.md that tension is flagged and not laundered. But a sponsor-funded, sponsor-supplied, properly-blinded, adequately-powered trial that reports no effect is evidence FOR the null: the party with every incentive to find an effect, and the resources to design a trial that could, did not (hydrogen-trial-sponsor-made-the-product-whose-primary-endpoint-failed).

Where the funding does show is in the framing, not the numbers. The paper's title, abstract and conclusion are about a post-hoc microbiome analysis, not about the failed primary endpoint. Participants were split into "improved" and "not improved" by whether their VAS sleep score beat the trial-wide median — a variable measured after randomisation and during treatment — and the hydrogen arm's improvers turned out to have fewer hydrogen-producing gut bacteria at baseline (composite "H2-Producers" p = 0.008; linear mixed-effects group × H2-Producers interactions significant for three outcomes). The mechanism is genuinely attractive — if your gut already makes hydrogen, swallowing more should do less — and the authors are honest about it, calling the work exploratory and resting their interpretation on continuous models rather than the median split. It remains a subgroup defined by the outcome it is meant to explain, in cells of roughly eleven people, with no family-wise correction across at least six tested outcomes (hydrogen-responder-subgroup-is-defined-by-a-post-randomisation-outcome). In a trial with no main effect, a responder phenotype describes the noise.

What this map does NOT claim. It does not claim hydrogen is useless. One trial, one dose (0.9 mg/day), one delivery form (jelly), one duration (8 weeks), one population (44 Japanese adults with poor self-reported sleep), one outcome family (questionnaires — no polysomnography, no actigraphy). The antioxidant and anti-inflammatory claims that the wider hydrogen literature rests on are untested in this vault and are this map's largest open gap.

Why emerging and not fading. fading means superseded, debunked, or gone quiet. A single well-run negative trial on one of several marketed claims is none of those. The map opens where the evidence is: one good null, and an open question about everything else.

For David (GOAL.md: adherence-first, least work for maximum benefit, sleep is a foundation to protect): nothing to do, and nothing enters the regimen. He is not a poor sleeper, and the vault's ranked sleep levers are behavioural — consistent timing (sleep-regularity), and cbt-insomnia with a clinician if difficulty falling or staying asleep becomes persistent. ⚠ The practical value of this map is defensive: if a hydrogen product is ever marketed to him with "responder," "personalised," or "microbiome-matched" language, this is the trial behind that language, and its pre-registered endpoint failed.
