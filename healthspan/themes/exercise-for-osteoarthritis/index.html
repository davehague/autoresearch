Exercise for osteoarthritis (therapeutic exercise for a painful joint) — recovery & mobility

Status: emerging (2026-07-27, ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review) — coined from a Cochrane systematic review (Hall 2026, 18 randomised trials, 1,368 people with hip osteoarthritis, no funding, GRADE certainty on every estimate), the highest-tier source this vault has ever ingested on the question GOAL.md keeps implying and no other map answers: what does exercise do for a joint that already hurts?

emerging rather than consolidating is deterministic, not cautious. shared/evidence-model.md states that "a high tier with a trivial effect does not consolidate." The tier here is as high as it gets. The effect is one the authors themselves call "unlikely to be clinically meaningful," and the review's best-powered comparison is null. A close call in the other direction is defensible — a reviewer could argue a moderate-certainty Cochrane result across 18 trials sits above emerging — and it is logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Exercising an arthritic weight-bearing joint helps a little, does not help on top of other care, and does not appear to hurt you. Three comparisons, three answers, and the gap between them is the finding.

(1) Against doing nothing — a real benefit, smaller than a patient would notice. Versus no treatment, usual care or limited education, land-based exercise probably reduces pain (MD −7.19 points on a 0–100 scale, 95% CI −10.70 to −3.68, 9 studies, n=449, GRADE moderate certainty) and probably improves physical function (MD −8.79, −12.00 to −5.41, moderate certainty) (exercise-hip-osteoarthritis-benefit-real-but-below-clinical-threshold). Quality of life did not move (MD 2.31, −1.15 to 5.91, moderate certainty). The review's own words: these improvements "are unlikely to be clinically meaningful." Seven points on a hundred-point pain scale is a genuine between-group difference and a difference most individuals would struggle to feel. This vault normally has to apply the statistical-versus-clinical-significance lens against a paper's conclusion; here the paper applies it to itself.

(2) Added on top of care a person is already receiving — nothing measurable, and this is the best-powered comparison in the review. Exercise plus a co-intervention versus that co-intervention alone: pain MD −3.86 (−8.07 to 0.35), function MD −2.37 (−6.59 to 1.86), quality of life MD 3.60 (−1.30 to 8.36), withdrawals RR 0.85 (0.55–1.32) — all null, all at moderate certainty, on 751 participants (exercise-adds-nothing-on-top-of-existing-hip-osteoarthritis-care). This null is accepted at strong, and the vault's own rules are why: shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 targets an underpowered null sold as no effect, and 751 participants rated moderate certainty by GRADE is not that. Read (1) and (2) together and the honest claim is that the benefit is real, small, and substantially redundant with whatever else the person is already doing.

(3) Against an attention control the pain benefit disappears — on 123 people, so it settles nothing. Versus a control designed to supply the same contact and expectation without the exercise, pain was null: MD −6.31 (−12.98 to 0.35), 2 studies, n=123, low certainty; function narrowly survived (MD −7.44, −13.86 to −1.01) (exercise-hip-osteoarthritis-pain-null-against-attention-control-underpowered). The tempting reading is that the pain benefit is expectation rather than exercise — this vault has measured that discount three times, on loneliness (association halved on objective measurement), on cold-water immersion (objectively null, subjective wins failing the authors' own bias tests) and on green exercise (no objective outcome pooled anywhere in 26 trials). It is refused here anyway. The interval runs from a benefit larger than the usual-care estimate to a small harm, and Cochrane itself rated it low certainty and worded it as "may have little to no effect." An underpowered null is not a demonstration of equivalence even when the conclusion would be satisfying — the ninth time this vault has applied that rule.

(4) ⚠ The safety result, and it is the practically useful one. Adding exercise to existing care probably slightly reduced adverse events: RR 0.75 (0.58–0.97), moderate certainty, 6 studies, n=731 (exercise-slightly-reduces-adverse-events-in-hip-osteoarthritis). This is the first moderate-certainty adverse-event estimate on loading a painful weight-bearing joint anywhere in this vault — against six blood-flow-restriction syntheses across 50+ trials that report no adverse-event tallies at all, and against Shen 2026's fall-risk interval reaching 11.11 written up as "similar safety profiles." Read it as the absence of a harm signal, not the presence of protection — the upper bound is 0.97, and the likeliest mechanism is simply that people whose pain improves report fewer symptomatic events. The review's other two adverse-event cells are uninterpretable in either direction (RR 2.95, 0.62–13.96 on 257 people; RR 8.00, 1.13–56.79 on 18 people), and Cochrane rates both very low.

⚠ The population caveat is binding, and it must not be quietly dropped. These are people with hip osteoarthritis — a degenerative joint disease of a joint David does not have a problem with. GOAL.md records a March 2026 patellar contusion: a different joint, a different mechanism, a different natural history. Nothing here transfers to him as a finding. What transfers is a prior — exercise for a painful weight-bearing joint is safe alongside other care, helps a little, and is not a cure — and, in the phrasing stretching adopted for exactly this situation, a prior is not a finding.

Why this map exists rather than a paragraph on another map. Every exercise map in this vault answers a different question: does training make you fitter (zone-2-cardio), stronger (resistance-training-programming), longer-lived (resistance-training, daily-steps, sedentary-behavior), or more mobile (stretching). None of them asks whether exercise reduces pain in a diseased joint, which is a distinct intervention-outcome pair with 18 randomised trials behind it, measured in patients rather than healthy volunteers. Folding it into resistance-training would have buried a Cochrane review inside a mortality map; folding it into stretching would have mixed range of motion with symptom management. Nothing to buy, nothing pharmacologic, no dose.

  • ⚑ The adjunct-versus-substitute distinction now holds in two joints, from two literatures, and the vault reached it independently in each. blood-flow-restriction's own authors call the cuff an "adjunctive load-management strategy… rather than a replacement" (bfr-is-adjunct-not-substitute-for-heavy-load), and this review finds exercise adds nothing measurable on top of existing care in 751 people at moderate certainty (exercise-adds-nothing-on-top-of-existing-hip-osteoarthritis-care). The two are not in tension and must never be merged: exercise's strongest evidence is for how long you live, and its weakest is for whether your joint stops hurting. That is the reverse of how it is usually sold to someone in pain.

Here, adding exercise to existing care REDUCED adverse events, RR 0.75 (0.58–0.97). Same domain, opposite sign — the difference is almost certainly intensity and supervision structure, not exercise as such. Recorded as a live tension rather than resolved: the vault holds one randomised programme that hurt people and one pooled literature that did not, and cannot yet say what separates them.   • ⚑ The unblindable-self-report problem, and the cleanest natural experiment for it the vault has seen. Every critical outcome here — pain, function, quality of life — can only be obtained by asking, and exercise cannot be blinded. This review contains both a usual-care comparison (benefit) and an attention-control comparison (null on pain) within one literature, which is exactly the contrast that would size the expectation effect if it were powered. It is not: 2 studies, 123 people. The vault's standing rule holds — on outcomes measurable without asking, expectation shrinks; on outcomes that can only be asked about, it is large and unblindable — and pain can only be asked about. Logged as the map's top open gap.
