Vision nutrition — sensory

Status: emerging (2026-11-06) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's second Sensory-domain lever, alongside hearing-aids. Founded on a systematic review and meta-analysis of nutritional interventions (antioxidants, omega-3s, vitamins, CoQ10, ginkgo biloba, citicoline, palmitoylethanolamide) for glaucoma progression, pooling 21 studies (1,209 participants). The founding evidence is a transparently-reported null: no significant effect on intraocular pressure, visual field mean deviation, or retinal nerve fiber layer thickness, with the review's own authors flagging their one statistically significant finding (pattern standard deviation) as not clinically meaningful. git is the undo.

What we believe now

No nutritional intervention this review tested has been shown to measurably slow glaucoma progression. Across 21 studies and three main progression markers (IOP, visual field mean deviation, RNFL thickness), all three pooled estimates were null, with wide, imprecise intervals consistent with an underpowered, heterogeneous evidence base rather than a confident demonstration of no effect. The one statistically significant result — a small reduction in pattern standard deviation from three cohort studies — is explicitly read by the paper's own authors as too small to count as a clinically meaningful neuroprotective effect, not as a counter-signal to the null.
