Intensive blood-pressure targeting (pharmacological) — metabolic / pharmacologic

Status: emerging (2026-11-14) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's fourth pharmacologic map, after glp-1, orexin-antagonists and testosterone-replacement-therapy. This map tracks the question of how aggressively an existing antihypertensive regimen should be titrated — not a new drug to start. Distinct from exercise-for-blood-pressure (an exercise intervention) and potassium-blood-pressure / potassium-citrate (dietary interventions). ⚠ PRESCRIPTION — titration decisions belong to a clinician, never ranked in the protocol (shared/safety.md).

What we believe now

A 12,821-person randomised trial (BPROAD) in Chinese adults aged ≥50 with type 2 diabetes and elevated cardiovascular risk found that targeting systolic blood pressure below 120 mmHg, versus the standard below 140 mmHg, did not reduce the combined risk of all-cause dementia or mild cognitive impairment over a median 3.8 years (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.86–1.06). In a smaller nested MRI substudy, the intensive-target arm accumulated less cerebral white-matter lesion volume — a structural brain-imaging surrogate — than the standard arm. The harder, more clinically meaningful outcome is null; the softer, imaging-surrogate outcome is positive. This is the same engagement-without-outcome pattern this vault has already logged for creatine (brain loading without a cognition gain), NAD precursors (NAD⁺ rise without function gain) and omega-3 (CSF DHA delivery without cognition gain) — a biological signal moved, and the thing a person would actually notice did not.
