Blood flow restriction training (BFR / occlusion training) — exercise

Status: emerging (held 2026-07-15) — auto-coined 2026-07-12 to hold open a gate the vault raised against itself. First the population gap closed (Hou, patients). Now the substitute gap — the one this map was coined to hold — gets its first direct test, in David's own population, and it points the way the vault hoped: low-load BFR shows no strength difference from heavy load (Chen 2025). The knee gate on resistance-training softens — but the test is four small trials, so it does not lift, and the safety absence has now replicated a third time. ⚑ 2026-08-14, at ten sources: the pressure axis has now been moved twice in three days and both tests say the top of it buys nothing, while the one pool that measured burden alongside benefit found them separating at the same threshold in opposite directions.

Sub-threads: (1) the benefit ledger — what occlusion adds, chronic (strength; whether it substitutes for heavy load) and acute (potentiation) · (2) the cost ledger — uncounted harms, measured burden, and a dose unstandardised across nine conventions. Painful knees are the population both questions are asked in, not a third strand. [reviewed 2026-08-14 @ 10 bullets]

⚑ Why two strands and not three — the fork decision of 2026-08-14, stated so a human can overrule it. At ten bullets this map is fork-eligible, and the obvious third strand is the clinical knee (Hou, Chen, Talbot): its own population, its own outcomes, three dedicated sources. Not carved, on one ground: every knee source answers strand (1) or strand (2) inside a knee. Hou is analgesia enabling load; Chen is the substitute test; Talbot is the corpus's only sham and its only proper harm count. Carving them would move this map's reason for existing — David's knee, and the gate on resistance-training — away from the safety flag that is its spine, and would leave the flag needing to live in both halves. The same is true in the other direction: the acute-performance sources (Xin, Miao) look separable until you notice that their most load-bearing findings are contributions to the pressure-dose question the parent owns. ⚠ The counter-argument is real and is recorded rather than argued away: three knee sources would stand alone as a map. NOT forked; git is the undo.

What we believe now

BFR is not a way to skip the load. It is a way to tolerate it — and on that, it now has real evidence in real knees.

(1) In people whose knees actually hurt, BFR is a clinically meaningful analgesic. Hou 2026 pools 11 RCTs, n=508 patients with patellofemoral pain syndrome — no PROSPERO gap, no conflicts, no funding — and finds BFR added to rehabilitation cut pain during exactly the loaded movements a compromised patella fails: shallow single-leg squat MD 2.13 (1.72–2.54), I²=39%; deep single-leg squat 2.38 (1.69–3.08); step-down 2.05 (1.58–2.52) — still measurable 45 minutes after the session (bfr-reduces-patellofemoral-pain-during-loaded-tasks). This clears the bar the paper itself sets: Hou cites the MCID for the NPRS in PFPS at ~1.16 points and for a 10-cm VAS at ~2 cm. Longer term: overall pain SMD 0.37 (0.16–0.57), worst pain 0.38 (0.14–0.62, I²=0%), knee extensor strength SMD 0.28 (0.06–0.49) — small but real.

(2) It does not improve the knee function the patient reports — and that null is informative, not underpowered. Anterior Knee Pain Scale MD 1.93 (−0.52 to 4.38), I²=0% (bfr-knee-function-null-in-patients). The AKPS runs 0–100, and a minimal important change is conventionally ~8–10 points: the interval's entire upper bound sits below a clinically meaningful gain, and the trials agree with each other. Compare bfr-sport-performance-null in athletes (SMD 0.11, −0.18 to 0.40), where the upper bound was a real effect and the vault therefore refused to call it a debunk. Same lens, different verdict, because the numbers are different. Quadriceps morphology: also null.

(3) The authors settle the framing against the vault's hope, in their own words. BFRT is "a potential adjunctive load-management strategy for patients who have difficulty tolerating higher-load exercise, rather than a replacement" (bfr-is-adjunct-not-substitute-for-heavy-load). And they explain their own functional null: improvement in PFPS "may depend more on the overall quality of exercise delivery and adherence than on any single technique."

Which compresses to the finding: the exercise fixes the knee. The cuff makes the exercise possible.

(4) The substitute question — this map's founding gap — now has its first direct test, and it softens the knee gate for the first time (new 2026-07-15). That gate asked: can occlusion buy the heavy-load strength adaptation without the heavy load? For 21 RCTs the answer was "nobody has tested it" — Xu and Hou both added BFR to training. Chen 2025 runs the head-to-head: four of its trials pit low-load BFR directly against high-intensity resistance training, and the strength difference is a flat SMD −0.11 (−0.75 to 0.53), p = 0.74 — no significant difference — in middle-aged/elderly OA and post-arthroplasty knees, David's own population (bfr-matches-heavy-load-strength-in-knee-rehab). And against low-load training without a cuff, occlusion adds real strength (SMD 1.11, p < 0.00001), replicating Xu and Hou a third time (bfr-strength-over-low-load-in-knee-rehab). This is the key the gate was missing — and it is cut from soft metal. The substitute comparison is 4 trials, n = 123, the smallest cell in the paper; a non-significant strength difference on that base is an underpowered null, not demonstrated non-inferiority, and the vault will not read it as equivalence any more than it read Shen's, Wu's, or Jiao's underpowered nulls that way — especially not when the convenient reading is "the gate opens." So the gate can no longer say the substitute question is untested; it now says the evidence is four small trials, pointing the right way, with no adverse events counted — a sharper physio question, not a protocol change.

Why still emerging, when two independent syntheses now agree on direction. Applied deterministically per shared/evidence-model.md — consolidating needs ≥2 independent groups, high tier, a real effect, replicated, and no live rebuttal:

  • The replicated claim (strength: Xu SMD 1.09 in athletes, Hou SMD 0.28 in patients) is small in the population that matters, and "effect size and independent replication gate the top."
  • The large claim (analgesia, MD ~2 points, above MCID) rests on a single synthesis.
  • The functional endpoint is a replicated null — a live tension, carried, not buried.
  • Safety does not exist. See below.

⚠ safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] stands — and the reason to keep it got stronger, not weaker. Xu's 10 RCTs reported no adverse-event data at all. Hou's 11 RCTs report only that BFR groups "commonly reported higher ratings of perceived exertion and greater post-exercise muscle soreness" — with no serious adverse events or cuff complications detailed, because nobody systematically collected them (bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials). Chen 2025 makes it three: thirty-three randomised trials, three independent meta-analyses, three-plus populations (athletes, patellofemoral-pain patients, OA/TKA patients), zero adverse-event tallies, for a technique whose mechanism is deliberately restricting arterial inflow to a limb. The absence has now replicated twice over, which is a stronger reason to flag than the reason the flag was set — and more urgent now that a substitute use, not just an add-on, has in-population support. And it is more urgent now, not less: a technique nobody uses is one thing; a technique with a demonstrated benefit people will now reach for, whose risks nobody has counted, is another.

⚠ Update 2026-07-16 — the absence gets its first crack of light, on the axis that matters least to the worry. Ren 2025 (18-RCT network meta-analysis, older adults 55–80) is the first BFR source in the vault to report cardiovascular endpoints at all — heart rate unchanged, blood-pressure responses modest, and the authors judge the higher-pressure regimen cardiovascularly safe (bfr-cardiovascular-response-data-older-adults). That is reassuring, and it is not the risk the flag is about. Blood-pressure response is not a thrombosis, an endothelial injury, or a cuff complication — and Ren's own discussion raises "blood stasis, endothelial injury, and hypercoagulability" for high-risk populations because the trials did not measure them. So the flag stands with refined reasoning: hemodynamically tolerable in older adults; occlusion-specific adverse events still uncounted across 51 RCTs and four syntheses; the authors themselves caution against CV-compromised use. Narrowed, not removed.

⚠ Update 2026-08-06 — the flag is refined a fourth time, and for the first time the refinement is about the field rather than about the evidence. Ren W 2026 is the seventh source to report no adverse events without counting any, and the first to convert that absence into the word "safe" — at 200–350 mmHg, the highest cuff pressures in the corpus (bfr-authors-call-uncounted-absence-safe-at-the-highest-recorded-pressure). Two weeks earlier Jelen's authors met the identical absence and refused the conversion. Current honest statement: acute cardiovascular load looks tolerable at both ends of the adult age range; the burden of wearing the cuff is real and scales with pressure; across seven sources and ~57 trials nobody has counted a single occlusion-specific adverse event; and the field is now escalating dose while describing the uncounted absence as safety. That is a stronger reason to flag than any of the previous three.

No protocol is recorded here, and the ranges no longer even compare. The trials' own reported figures — cuff 50–143.33 mmHg / 60–80% limb occlusion pressure, and now 200–350 mmHg on 5-cm cuffs stratified by thigh circumference — are quoted as published. Cuff width changes how much occlusion a given pressure produces, so a seventh convention has made the corpus incomparable in absolute terms as well as in relative ones (bfr-cuff-pressure-reaches-350-mmhg-under-a-seventh-prescription-convention). Load 20–50% 1RM, 2–3×/week, 4–9 weeks; the authors state the heterogeneity makes it "difficult to derive clear, quantifiable prescription guidance." Nothing is invented (shared/safety.md).

Where this actually lands for David. GOAL.md names knee/joint-safe as a hard constraint and records a March 2026 patellar contusion, with pickleball gated on consistent stretching. This is the first item in the vault that is specifically about that knee. It is capped at "proposed — review with your doctor/physio" and it is not a protocol entry. What it is, now, is a concrete question worth asking a physio — is low-load BFR a sensible way to keep loading this knee while it heals? — where yesterday it was only a theoretical one. That is the system working.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-12 — Ingested Xu 2026 (Annals of Medicine, systematic review + meta-analysis, 10 RCTs, n=181 trained/collegiate athletes; searched to Nov 2024; Cochrane RoB; no PROSPERO, no GRADE; publicly funded, no conflicts): BFR added to resistance training vs control training → lower-limb strength SMD 1.09 (0.52–1.66), I²=75%; muscle size MD 1.09 (0.10–2.09), I²=0%; sport-specific performance null, SMD 0.11 (−0.18 to 0.40), I²=0%. Authors conclude it is "prudent to integrate this approach systematically into training cycles" — on surrogates, with the functional endpoint at zero and no adverse events collected. Auto-coins the map at emerging with safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]: the strength effect is large but implausibly so for a training add-on given ~18 participants per trial and funnel asymmetry the authors concede they cannot exclude. Capped suggestive; the null and the safety gap capped single-study. Map logs the gap it exists to hold, and predicts where the answer lives: "low-load BFR vs heavy load, in middle-aged adults, with a knee to protect, with adverse events counted… the paper itself points at where that literature lives — BFR in ACL rehabilitation and return-to-sport — and that is where the next sweep should look."

  • 2026-07-13 — The prediction paid off, and the answer is a smaller, better tool than the one that was wanted; Status held at emerging. Ingested Hou 2026 (Frontiers in Physiology, systematic review + meta-analysis, 11 RCTs, n=508 patellofemoral-pain patients, PROSPERO CRD420261296172, six databases to Feb 2026; RoB 2: 5 low / 4 some concerns / 2 high; no funding, no conflicts; no GRADE). The population gap closes — these are people whose knees hurt (mean ages 19.0–46.6; clinical PFPS, recreationally active, military), not collegiate athletes. BFR is a clinically meaningful analgesic during loaded knee tasks — single-leg squat MD 2.13, deep squat 2.38, step-down 2.05, above the paper's own cited MCID, sustained 45 min post-exercise (bfr-reduces-patellofemoral-pain-during-loaded-tasks) — with modest long-term pain (SMD 0.37) and strength (SMD 0.28) gains. And it does not move the knee function patients report: AKPS MD 1.93 (−0.52 to 4.38), I²=0% — an interval whose upper bound falls below the ~8–10-point minimal important change, so unlike Xu's sport-performance null this one is informative rather than underpowered (bfr-knee-function-null-in-patients). The authors' own conclusion is the vault's: "adjunctive load-management… rather than a replacement" (bfr-is-adjunct-not-substitute-for-heavy-load), and their explanation for the functional null — improvement "may depend more on the overall quality of exercise delivery and adherence than on any single technique" — means the exercise does the work and the cuff makes the exercise possible. The knee gate on resistance-training is NOT lifted: no trial in either synthesis tested low-load BFR as a substitute for heavy load, and Hou concedes its contrasts were not dose-matched. Held at emerging deliberately: the replicated effect (strength) is small, the large effect (analgesia) is unreplicated, the functional endpoint is a replicated null, and after 21 RCTs across two syntheses there is still no adverse-event tally (bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials) — so the flag stands and the item stays "proposed — review with your doctor/physio."

  • 2026-07-15 — The substitute gap gets its first direct test — in David's population — and the knee gate softens for the first time; Status held at emerging. Ingested Chen 2025 (PLoS One, systematic review + meta-analysis, 12 RCTs, n=642 middle-aged/elderly patients ≥45 with knee OA or total knee arthroplasty; no competing interests, no funding; registration reported but the printed PROSPERO number is malformed). For the first time in 21→33 RCTs, trials tested low-load BFR against heavy-load resistance training, not added to it: strength SMD −0.11 (−0.75 to 0.53), p = 0.74 — no significant difference — on 4 trials, n=123 (bfr-matches-heavy-load-strength-in-knee-rehab); pain also null vs heavy load (SMD −0.84, p = 0.17). Against low-load control, occlusion added real strength (SMD 1.11, p < 0.00001) but not pain (SMD −0.10, p = 0.46, an active-analgesic comparator); preop BFR improved postop strength (SMD 0.97, p = 0.03) (bfr-strength-over-low-load-in-knee-rehab). The gate on resistance-training can no longer say the substitute question is untested — but the test is four small trials, an underpowered null the vault refuses to read as equivalence (same discipline as Shen/Wu/Jiao), so the gate softens, it does not lift. And the safety absence replicates a third time: 33 RCTs, three syntheses, zero adverse-event tallies (bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials). Capped suggestive; the substitute note graded single-study.

  • 2026-07-16 — The safety absence gets its first data — hemodynamic, not adverse-event — and the flag's reasoning is refined but not lifted; Status held at emerging. Ingested Ren 2025 (Frontiers in Physiology, systematic review + network meta-analysis, 18 RCTs, n=626, older adults 55–80; PROSPERO CRD42024534387; no funding, no conflicts). The first BFR source in the vault to report cardiovascular endpoints: heart rate unchanged across regimens, modest systolic-BP responses (LFLP +3.40, LFHP +1.51 mmHg), and the authors judge the high-frequency/high-pressure regimen (HFHP, ≥120 mmHg, 3×/wk) to "maintain cardiovascular health" while cautioning the low-frequency/low-pressure regimen against those with cardiovascular issues (bfr-cardiovascular-response-data-older-adults). Strength: BFR regimens beat no-cuff low-intensity control (isometric HFHP WMD 1.44), replicating the "occlusion adds strength over light load" thread a fourth time, now in older adults. But this is hemodynamic response, not an adverse-event tally — no serious AEs, cuff complications, or thrombotic events were systematically counted, and the authors themselves raise blood stasis / hypercoagulability for high-risk populations. Appraised mixed (small sparse network, surrogate CV endpoints, an underpowered regimen ranking recorded not adopted); capped suggestive. safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] stands, with refined reasoning: hemodynamically tolerable in older adults, occlusion-specific risks still uncounted. No substitute-vs-heavy-load comparison here.

  • 2026-07-18 — Ingested Zhu 2026 (PLoS One, randomized crossover, n=60 sedentary college students — the largest single BFR trial in the vault; ChiCTR2500097728, no competing interests, OA) as the fifth BFR source, and the first to ask what the cuff costs the activity underneath it. Walking at 0/40/60/80% LOP: perceived exertion and discomfort rose significantly and step count fell significantly with pressure (both p<0.05) — a graded interaction with daily-steps, the vault's lowest-burden free lever, whose mortality claim is denominated in steps (bfr-occlusion-reduces-walking-volume-and-comfort, safety_flags: [interaction], noted in both maps). On hemodynamics, a second reassuring-but-incoherent reading: across ~36 comparisons exactly one was significant — heart rate at 60% LOP, +3.82 bpm (0.72–6.91), ~5.6% above baseline — and 80% LOP, the highest pressure, was not (bfr-walking-hemodynamics-modest-young-adults). A dose-response that skips its own top dose is a noise signature, so the vault banks the reassuring magnitude (concordant with Ren 2025 in older adults) and declines the authors' 40–60% LOP recommendation — shared/safety.md forbids converting a published range into a prescribed dose, and this range leans on a comparison that does not behave. The safety absence replicates a FIFTH time: across Xu, Hou, Chen, Ren and Zhu — 51 RCTs plus this crossover — still zero systematic tallies of serious adverse events, cuff complications, or thrombotic events. Appraised mixed (registered, conflict-free, proper 0%-LOP active control, novel burden measurement — against a 5-minute acute horizon, uncorrected multiplicity, and a young sedentary population two brackets from David's); capped suggestive. safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] stands, refined a second time; extends; Status unchanged (emerging). OA PDF archived.

  • 2026-07-23 — The safety absence replicates a SIXTH time — and for the first time the authors refuse to call it safety. Status held emerging; the flag stands, refined a third time. Ingested Jelen 2026 (European Review of Aging and Physical Activity, systematic review, 6 RCTs, n=197 healthy older adults (mean age ≥60), searched to May 2026, PRISMA-followed but NOT prospectively registered, publicly funded, no competing interests). Every one of the six trials reported improved lower-limb strength (1RM +15.6–54%, quadriceps CSA +3.23–4.2 cm²), which the authors call "comparable to traditional high intensity resistance exercise" — but the review is descriptive only, with no pooled estimate and no confidence intervals, none of the six studies reached low risk of bias, all six were high-risk on RoB 2 domain 2, and the median PEDro IQR was 2–3. A 54% 1RM gain is not physiology; it is familiarisation — the same implausible-magnitude tell the vault flagged on Drummond, Jiao, Sun and Alsanie. Appraised unreliable (fatal flag: a uniformly high-risk-of-bias base with no quantitative estimate to extract); capped single-study, so it cannot move this or any map alone. The value is in the safety sentence. No major adverse events were reported — and the authors immediately add that this is "encouraging but not sufficient to draw firm conclusions regarding safety," then name the hazards nobody measured: acute heart-rate and blood-pressure rises "particularly relevant for older adults with hypertension or cardiovascular disease," plus exertion, discomfort and pain (bfr-adverse-event-absence-replicates-without-becoming-safety). And cuff pressure remains unprescribable across a sixth source — six trials used six conventions (50% AOP + 5 mmHg, 80% AOP, absolute 120–270 mmHg, 1.5 × brachial SBP, femoral-artery SBP, 50% of SBP) (bfr-cuff-pressure-prescription-remains-unstandardised). The knee gate does not lift: the "comparable to heavy load" comparison is descriptive, not dose-matched, and the population is healthy older adults, not knees with something wrong with them.

  • 2026-08-06 — ⚠ THE SEVENTH REPLICATION, AND THE FIRST TIME THE UNCOUNTED ABSENCE IS CONVERTED INTO THE WORD "SAFE" — at the highest cuff pressure this vault has ever recorded. Status held emerging; the flag stands, refined a FOURTH time. Ingested Ren W 2026 (bfr-baduanjin-quadriceps-postural-stability-rct-ren-2026, Scientific Reports 16:23863, randomised controlled trial, n = 38 sedentary young men analysed of 40, 8 weeks, ChiCTR2100054120, ethics Chengdu Sport University 202165; publicly funded, no competing interests; CC BY OA PDF archived). ⚠ Naming hazard: this is Ren W of Chengdu Sport University, NOT the Ren 2025 already on this map — two unrelated Rens, one map. The intervention is Baduanjin qigong with or without bilateral thigh occlusion. ⚑ Cuff pressure was set by thigh circumference on 5-cm B-Strong cuffs — 200 / 250 / 300 / 350 mmHg — and held continuously for the full 30-minute session. Every prior source prescribed 60–80% of limb occlusion pressure or absolute values in a 50–143 mmHg band; Ren 2025's highest-pressure regimen, judged cardiovascularly acceptable, was ≥120 mmHg. This is a seventh prescription convention and the highest absolute numbers in the corpus (bfr-cuff-pressure-reaches-350-mmhg-under-a-seventh-prescription-convention, single-study, safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]) — bounded honestly, because narrow cuffs need higher pressure for the same occlusion, so 350 mmHg on a 5-cm band is NOT three times the occlusion of 120 mmHg on a wide one. The vault cannot say this trial applied more occlusion. It also cannot say it applied less. That is the defect, and the authors concede that individualised AOP/LOP prescription is "the methodological gold standard" and that they did not use it. ⚠ Harms, in full: "Participants were monitored throughout the intervention for any discomfort, numbness, or excessive pain." No event definition, no instrument, no denominator, no timeline — and the conclusion nonetheless states the method "may offer a safe and feasible alternative" (bfr-authors-call-uncounted-absence-safe-at-the-highest-recorded-pressure, single-study, pushes-back). Two weeks ago Jelen's authors met the identical absence and wrote that it was "encouraging but not sufficient to draw firm conclusions regarding safety." Same evidence, opposite sentence, higher dose — back-edge added to bfr-adverse-event-absence-replicates-without-becoming-safety. Strength: BFR added strength over the unoccluded form for a fifth time (peak torque Δ 19.42 vs 6.93 N·m left, 21.71 vs 7.89 right) — but all four interaction tests landed at p = 0.041, 0.043, 0.044 and 0.045 with ηp² 0.10–0.12, no family-wise correction across them, no pre-specified primary outcome, and left/right legs of the same 38 men reported as if they were replications (bfr-augments-low-load-qigong-strength-on-marginal-statistics, single-study). Postural sway improved eyes-open and not eyes-closed — the authors read it as visual dependence, which would make the gain least useful in the conditions balance training is for (bfr-balance-gain-is-visually-dependent, single-study, open question). Appraised unreliable (fatal flag #2: four uncorrected tests clustered immediately below threshold, reported in prose as "substantially augmented," "markedly higher," "compelling evidence"), capped single-study — so it moves nothing on its own, and its genuine virtues (registration, a met a priori power analysis, concealed allocation, machine-measured outcomes, 5% attrition) are recorded and do not rescue it. The knee gate on resistance-training is untouched: this is again BFR added to light exercise, never substituting for heavy load.

  • 2026-08-10 — ⚑⚑ EIGHTH SOURCE, AND THE FIRST ONE THAT LOOKED. Somebody finally ran a validated harm instrument against a randomised sham cuff — and the occluded arm got worse on it, while gaining nothing. Status held emerging; the safety flag is REFRAMED, not merely refined a fifth time. Ingested Talbot 2026 (bfr-nmes-patellofemoral-pain-quality-of-life-rct-talbot-2026, Military Medicine 191(Suppl 1):36-43, secondary analysis of an intent-to-treat RCT, n = 84 active-duty US service members with patellofemoral pain syndrome, 42 per arm, 9 weeks, NCT04086615, blocked randomisation with concealed allocation; USU/DoD funded, no declared conflicts; Oxford PDF endpoint failed at the browser rung, pdf_unavailable logged). ⚑ The comparator is what makes this the most informative source on the map. Every prior source compares BFR + light exercise against light exercise alone — a contrast that confounds the occlusion with the cuff, the device, the clinic time and the novelty. This gives both arms the same Delfi cuff, the same 45-minute inflation cycles, the same NMES-plus-exercise programme, and varies only the pressure: 80% individualised limb occlusion pressure versus a 20 mmHg sham. It is also the first source in the corpus to use individualised LOP — the method Ren W's authors called the gold standard four nights ago while conceding they used thigh circumference and 350 mmHg instead. Result one — the occlusion contributed nothing. Knee function (AKPS group P = .15, interaction P = .26), pain (VAS P = .58 / .58), physical quality of life (PCS P = .15 / .57) — all improved strongly with time (every time effect P < .0001; PCS climbed from well below the population norm to nearly normal in both arms) and not one differed between arms. The parent trial had already reported no strength or physical-performance difference (bfr-added-nothing-to-pain-or-physical-function-against-a-sham-cuff). Back-edge added to bfr-reduces-patellofemoral-pain-during-loaded-tasks — the map's own PFPS benefit claim, which rests on trials without a sham. ⚠ Result two — the only thing that separated the arms went the wrong way. SF-12v2 Mental Component Summary, normed to a US population mean of 50: the 80%-occlusion arm fell 54.1 → 49.1, below the population average, while sham held at 53.2 → 53.5. Group × time F(1,202) = 6.17, P = .014, with both main effects null. Unchanged by sensitivity adjustment for the one baseline imbalance (age, P = .04) and for cumulative adherence (bfr-at-80-percent-occlusion-lowered-mental-quality-of-life-against-sham, safety_flags: [high-dose-risk], pushes-back). ⚑ THIS IS WHAT THE MAP HAS BEEN ASKING FOR SINCE 12 JULY, AND IT IS THE ANSWER IT DID NOT WANT. Seven sources reported an absence of adverse events with no event definition, no instrument, no denominator (bfr-adverse-event-absence-replicates-without-becoming-safety); the seventh converted that absence into the word "safe" at the highest pressure in the corpus (bfr-authors-call-uncounted-absence-safe-at-the-highest-recorded-pressure — back-edge added there). The eighth source brought an instrument and found a signal. The honest statement changes shape: not "harms are uncounted" but "harms are barely counted, and the first proper count came back negative." ⚠ And the vault holds itself to the standard it has been demanding. This is one of four interaction tests, uncorrected, and P = .014 does NOT clear a Bonferroni α of .0125; it is a secondary analysis with no power calculation for the outcome; and blinding is unverified and almost certainly broken on a self-reported measure (bfr-mental-health-signal-is-one-of-four-uncorrected-tests). A vault that spent a month asking for this evidence is the last party that should relax when it arrives in the expected direction — the same discipline applied to the walking-cognition null, in the opposite direction. But the blinding objection is weaker than it looks: the authors' proposed mechanism IS the discomfort — "each of which is known individually to cause significant myalgia, may be intensified when combined." If the cause is the felt experience of occlusion, "participants could tell" is not a confound; it is the exposure. Appraised mixed, capped suggestive. Status emerging HELD — the strength case is untouched, and the knee gate on resistance-training stands: BFR is still only ever tested ADDED TO light exercise, never SUBSTITUTING for heavy load. ⚑ For David this is the closest population the vault holds to his knee — an injured, painful, rehabilitating one — and it says the cuff is the part to skip: nine weeks of exercise and neurostimulation did all the work, in both arms.

  • 2026-08-12 — ⚑ NINTH SOURCE, AND THE FIRST ONE TO VARY THE DOSE. Somebody held the protocol, the cohort and the session constant and changed only the cuff pressure — and the benefit was flat across more than a doubling of it. Status held emerging; the flag stands, unrefined. Ingested Xin 2026 (dynamic-stretching-bfr-pressures-explosive-performance-rct-xin-2026, PeerJ 14:e21614, randomised crossover, n = 20 healthy men aged 18–30, four conditions, 48-hour washout, participants blinded to the pressure value; 13 cm KAATSU Master 2.0 cuffs, both thighs, proximal; ethics Beijing Sport University 2024098H; no funding, no competing interests; an a priori power analysis the trial met — 16 required, 20 recruited; no trial registration retrievable; appraised mixed, capped suggestive). The four conditions: dynamic stretching alone, and dynamic stretching with cuffs at 30%, 50% or 70% of estimated arterial occlusion pressure, followed by countermovement jump, standing long jump, 10 m sprint and the 505 change-of-direction test. ⚑ The result the map has been waiting eight sources for. Countermovement jump improved more in all three occluded conditions than in stretching alone (all p < 0.01) — +5.80, +5.95 and +5.15 cm against +3.50 cm, omnibus F = 9.004, p < 0.001, η² = 0.322 — and the three pressures did not differ from each other, with the highest producing the smallest gain (bfr-warm-up-benefit-showed-no-dependence-on-cuff-pressure). Chen 2025 does — L-BFR vs high-intensity RT, strength SMD −0.11, no difference, in OA/TKA patients (bfr-matches-heavy-load-strength-in-knee-rehab). So the gate can no longer say the question is untested. But the comparison is 4 trials, n=123 — an underpowered null, not demonstrated non-inferiority — so the gate softens to a sharper physio question rather than lifting. Note this is a different mechanism from Hou's finding: Hou showed BFR delivers analgesia (letting you do rehab exercise); Chen suggests BFR may also deliver the strength adaptation at light load. Both point to BFR being genuinely useful for a bad knee; neither is yet strong enough to act on. Cross-linked on both maps.
