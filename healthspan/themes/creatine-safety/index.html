Creatine safety — kidneys, hair, and what a blood test actually says

Status: consolidating (2026-08-02) — auto-forked this run from creatine-monohydrate, which became fork-due at 11 dated bullets with 3 declared sub-threads. The safety/renal strand is the cleanest of the three to separate: it asks a different question from the rest of the parent map — not does creatine work but does creatine hurt you, and does it make your lab results lie — and it answers on its own four sources and eight concepts, none of which bear on strength, lean mass or bone. The parent creatine-monohydrate remains the hub for the consolidating strength anchor and the bone thread. Flagged for human review; git is the undo.

What we believe now

Creatine at the doses this vault discusses is safe, the kidney scare is a measurement artifact rather than kidney damage, and the hair-loss fear is retired. The one thing worth actually doing about any of it is telling a clinician you supplement before a kidney panel.

(1) The kidney question is settled about as well as laboratory evidence can settle it — and the answer is that the instrument is wrong, not the kidney. Almeida 2026 (International Urology and Nephrology, 26 RCTs, n = 1,036, PROSPERO-registered, a nephrology/urology group with no competing interests) measured filtration two ways in the same pooled trials. Estimated from serum creatinine it fell 10.75 mL/min (−17.48 to −4.02), I² = 0%. Measured with the Cr-EDTA tracer, which does not use creatinine as its signal, it did not fall (+5.89, −0.30 to 12.08) (creatine-lowers-estimated-gfr-without-lowering-measured-gfr). Four other kidney markers that bypass creatinine — urea, albuminuria, proteinuria, urinary creatinine — are all flat (creatine-null-on-urea-albuminuria-and-proteinuria), and the artifact also appears in chronic kidney disease and haemodialysis populations (creatine-kidney-marker-shift-includes-ckd-and-dialysis-populations).

Why the equation breaks, in one sentence: filtration is almost never measured directly — a blood test reads creatinine, a waste product muscle produces at a supposedly steady rate, and an equation converts it into an estimate. About 2% of body creatine degrades to creatinine daily, so supplementing puts more creatinine in the blood while the kidney does exactly what it did before.

⚠ Bounded honestly: the exonerating tracer comparison is itself a p = .06 null (interval −0.30 to +12.08) and is refused as equivalence (creatine-reference-method-gfr-comparison-is-underpowered). What carries the artifact reading is the convergence of four independent markers, not that one null. And I² = 0% on the creatinine-based drop is not reassurance about biology — it is what you get when every trial runs the same equation on the same distorted input. The consistency belongs to the equation.

⚑ The practical instruction, and it is the only actionable item on this map: tell a clinician you supplement creatine before a kidney panel. A shift of ~10.75 mL/min is large enough to move a healthy adult into a worse-sounding staging band on a blood test while nothing has happened to the kidney — and a marker that moves for a non-disease reason can trigger a real clinical response.

(2) Direct safety evidence tops out at ~10 g/day and is reassuring within that range. No renal impairment at ~10 g/day for 3 months (Gualano 2008, n = 18; cystatin C fell, GFR trended up — creatine-high-dose-renal-safety), and none across 1–80 g/day over 5 days to 24 months in a narrative review (creatine-safety-profile) — ⚠ industry-tied (AlzChem-funded, author advisory-board roles), reassuring in direction, weighted as review-tier and not laundered. GI distress is dose-dependent and worse at single doses above ~10 g; splitting into ≤5 g servings mitigates it.

(3) The DHT/hair-loss fear is retired. The first direct RCT (Lak 2025, n = 38, double-blind, 12 weeks, 5 g/day, resistance-trained males) found no group × time effect on DHT, testosterone, or any of eight hair-follicle measures (creatine-no-hair-loss-dht) — against van der Merwe 2009's uncontrolled three-week DHT-rise signal, which never measured hair at all.

⚠ The boundary this map must keep visible: the safety evidence covers ~5–10 g/day, NOT the 20 g brain-loading dose. The sibling map creatine-cognition carries a source showing 20 g/day genuinely raises brain creatine (+11%) — double anything the safety thread has evidence for, with sleep disturbance among its reported adverse events (creatine-20g-raises-brain-creatine, safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]). Nothing on this map licenses that dose.

Why consolidating rather than consensus. The renal claim now rests on 26 randomised trials with a reference-method comparison, replicated markers and a mechanistic explanation — that is a real anchor. But every endpoint on this map is a laboratory marker; no hard renal outcome appears anywhere in the corpus, dose and duration are not disaggregated, and the reassurance beyond ~10 g/day is extrapolation. Not medical advice; no dose is prescribed here.

  • ⚑ The instrument-gradient pattern, which this thread supplies the sharpest case of. The vault's other instances are gradients — the instrument changes how big the answer is: fat-free mass predicts mortality nearly twice as strongly by tape measure as by bioimpedance (fat-free-mass-measurement-method-gradient); self-reported sleep duration preserves the shape of the mortality curve and loses its magnitude against a wrist accelerometer (self-reported-sleep-duration-preserves-shape-loses-magnitude); wrist-worn step counters read 15–30% above hip-worn ones in the same person on the same day (wrist-worn-step-counts-run-15-to-30-percent-above-hip-worn). This one is not a gradient but a false signal: the instrument reports a change in a quantity that did not change. The generalisable habit: when a number moves, ask whether the thing moved or the ruler did. Cross-noted on daily-steps and fat-free-mass.
  • ⚠ Global ripple: no interaction, no safety flag, and the reason is worth stating. The creatinine artifact is a laboratory-measurement effect, not a pharmacological one: it does not potentiate, blunt or contraindicate anything in this vault. No map holds a renal outcome, no map holds a drug titrated to estimated filtration, and nothing here changes a timing, dose or stacking decision. So no safety_flags are raised — the concepts carry []. The one adjacency is procedural rather than physiological: glp-1 is the vault's only other map whose management involves routine laboratory monitoring, and the general lesson — a marker that moves for a non-disease reason can trigger a real clinical response — applies there too. Logged, not graded.
  • ⚠ The safety thread covers ~5–10 g/day, not the 20 g brain-loading dose. creatine-cognition carries creatine-20g-raises-brain-creatine at double anything evidenced here, with sleep disturbance among its adverse events. Nothing in the cognition literature argues for raising the dose — strength is flat above ~5 g (creatine-dose-response-strength) and the randomised 20 g cognition test in healthy adults was null.
