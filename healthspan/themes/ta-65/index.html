TA-65 (telomerase activator) — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-11-24) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's first telomerase-activating-compound map, founded on a single 8-RCT meta-analysis. TA-65® is a patented extract of Astragalus membranaceus sold as a telomere-length/longevity supplement (e.g. by TA Sciences). Distinct from biological-age-clocks (which tracks aging-clock measurement validity broadly) — this map tracks a specific intervention claimed to move one biomarker those clocks also use.

Sub-threads: none — one question (does TA-65 lengthen telomeres, and does that translate into function) asked once, from one meta-analysis. No separable strands exist yet. [reviewed 2026-11-24 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

TA-65 reliably and moderately lengthens leukocyte telomeres in RCTs — but that effect does not carry over to anything that looks like a health outcome, and the size of the telomere effect itself depends on who funded the trial. Pooling 8 RCTs (n=750, mean age 63.3), TA-65 produced a real telomere-length increase (SMD 0.47, 95% CI 0.31–0.62, p<0.00001), larger in adults over 60 (SMD 0.63 vs 0.36). The same meta-analysis found industry-funded trials in its own pool reported a larger effect (SMD 0.63) than independent trials (SMD 0.40, p=0.03) — the same magnitude gap as the age split, a useful calibration for how much a funding effect can move a modest evidence base. Despite the biomarker movement, frailty measures (SPPB, grip strength, 6-minute walk) and inflammatory markers (hs-CRP, IL-6) showed no significant improvement (SMD 0.09 and −0.11 respectively, both non-significant). Safety over 12 months showed mild, dose-independent gastrointestinal effects (12.4% incidence) and no severe adverse events, but the authors explicitly flag that long-term carcinogenic risk — a live question for any telomerase-activating compound, given telomerase's dual role in cellular senescence and cancer biology — remains unaddressed.

Why emerging, and why flagged for review. A single meta-analysis, a small pool (8 RCTs, 750 participants), a quantified industry-funding inflation effect in the very source that founds this map, and a headline biomarker result that the same source shows does not translate into anything functional. This is this vault's second aging-biomarker-without-a-functional-correlate finding (after the epigenetic- clock literature on biological-age-clocks) — worth tracking as its own intervention rather than folding into that map, since TA-65 is a specific purchasable product with its own dosing, cost, and safety profile, not a measurement instrument.

What this costs: a patented supplement with an unestablished long-term safety profile, for a biomarker movement with no demonstrated functional benefit. No dose is recommended here beyond the studied 10–50 mg/day range, and none is invented (shared/safety.md). Not actionable for David (GOAL.md requires third-party purity/potency verification and a demonstrated functional benefit before any supplement enters the Protocol — neither is established here).
