Cold-water immersion (cold plunge / cold shower) — recovery & mobility

Status: emerging (2026-07-08) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's second Recovery & mobility lever (sibling to sauna-heat — the hot to its cold). Coined by a systematic review + meta-analysis of RCTs that finds real but small, delayed wellbeing/sleep effects on a thin, mostly-male evidence base — and corrects the popular "anti-inflammatory" framing. Opened emerging.

What we believe now

For a healthy adult, deliberate cold-water immersion is a plausible, pleasant recovery/wellbeing tool with small, time-delayed benefits — not a demonstrated healthspan lever. Across 11 RCTs (3,177 adults, ≤15°C for ≥30 s), CWI lowered stress 12 hours later (SMD −1.00, not immediately), improved sleep quality vs passive recovery, and raised quality of life transiently at 30 days (gone by 90). Crucially, inflammation rose acutely (SMD 1.03 immediate, 1.26 at 1 h) — cold immersion is a stressor before it's a soother, so the "anti-inflammatory plunge" claim is backwards in the acute window; any settling is a later, hormetic rebound. For David (GOAL.md), the honest appeal mirrors sauna-heat: knee-safe, zero mechanical load, a comfort/sleep habit to try one variable at a time.

The tension worth keeping visible (why emerging): the base is small (11 RCTs), heterogeneous (shower vs ice bath vs plunge), predominantly male (one study included women), with subjective endpoints and a stress benefit that surfaced at only one of several tested time-windows. Real signal, thin foundation.

⚠ Interaction — do not stack immediately after lifting (now graded). A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (Piñero 2024) confirms post-workout cold immersion blunts resistance-training hypertrophy (comparative SMD −0.22, 95.7% probability of blunting), with a parallel strength/power attenuation in the wider literature. This is now a graded concept (cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy, safety_flags: [interaction]) rather than a logged flag, and is carried on resistance-training too. Timing rule: keep cold immersion away from the hours right after a strength session (non-lifting days, or well separated). The base is small and young-male, so the magnitude is suggestive — but the direction is well-established and enough to gate co-recommendation timing per shared/safety.md.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-08 — Ingested Cain 2025 (PLOS ONE, systematic review + meta-analysis, 11 RCTs, n=3,177, ≤15°C ≥30 s): time-dependent benefits — stress ↓ at 12 h (SMD −1.00), sleep quality ↑ (p=0.04), QoL ↑ at 30 d (not 90); inflammation ↑ acutely (SMD 1.03→1.26); mostly-male, heterogeneous, subjective-heavy. Auto-coins the map at emerging — a knee-safe recovery tool with small, delayed effects and a flagged resistance-training interaction.

  • 2026-07-08 — Ingested Piñero 2024 (European Journal of Sport Science, meta-analysis, 8 RCTs): post-workout CWI blunts resistance-training hypertrophy (comparative SMD −0.22, P(blunting)=0.957); young-male, low-quality base → capped suggestive. Converts the logged CWI ↔ resistance-training interaction into a graded concept (cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy, safety_flags: [interaction]); Status unchanged (a cross-intervention timing edge, not a change to CWI's own evidence).

  • 2026-07-22 — Ingested Zhu 2026 (PeerJ 14:e21537, systematic review + meta-analysis, 22 RCTs, n=375 — 362 men, 13 women, ages 15.6–48.9; single acute CWI bout vs passive recovery, outcomes stratified at 0/24/48/72 h; PEDro, RoB 2, GRADE, Egger + trim-and-fill, cluster-robust sensitivity; PMID 42473449) as the map's first look at recovery over time — and the source that dismantles cold plunging's central sales pitch. Maximal strength recovery: null, g = 0.08 (95% CI −0.11 to 0.26, p=0.42), no time-dependence, Egger's p=0.626, MODERATE certainty — the paper's cleanest result (cold-water-immersion-does-not-speed-strength-recovery). Explosive power was transiently impaired: CMJ overall flat (g = 0.01) but significantly time-dependent (p<0.01), g = −0.68 (−1.16 to −0.20, p=0.01) at 0 h, resolved by 24–48 h (cold-water-immersion-transiently-impairs-explosive-power, safety_flags: [interaction]). Both apparent benefits failed scrutiny: creatine kinase g = −0.43 → trim-and-fill g = −0.160 (−0.443 to 0.124), non-significant; VAS soreness g = −0.58 with Egger's p<0.001, one outlier driving the asymmetry, and VERY LOW certainty; cluster-robust analysis attenuated both (cold-water-immersion-damage-marker-benefit-fails-bias-adjustment). No adverse reactions in any trial. Appraised mixed — all 22 trials PEDro-high, GRADE per outcome, and the authors run bias tests that defeat their own positive findings; against n=375 with 362 of 375 male (the authors state the results cannot be extrapolated to women), RoB 2 "high" across all trials on blinding grounds, dosing too heterogeneous to define optimal parameters, and acute single exposures only, ≤72 h. Capped suggestive. extends; Status held emerging. PeerJ is fully open access but the PDF endpoint bot-walled (HTTP 403) and the Europe PMC deposit has not propagated; full text retrieved via web-to-md — pdf_unavailable logged as a host failure, not a non-OA source; retry.

  • 2026-07-25 — Ingested Bustos Carvajal 2026 (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, systematic review, 8 trials, no meta-analysis, PROSPERO CRD420251156387, conflict-free, CC BY; appraised unreliable — fatal flag #2) as the first head-to-head hot-versus-cold review, and an independent echo of Zhu 2026 three days earlier. In a Wingate test minutes after immersion, CWI reduced mean anaerobic power −2.4% and HWI raised it +2.2%, each p<0.05 vs control and p<0.0001 vs each other (hot-immersion-raises-and-cold-immersion-lowers-acute-anaerobic-power, safety_flags: [interaction], noted in both this map and sauna-heat) — a different group, a different instrument (Wingate not CMJ), the same direction and timescale as cold-water-immersion-transiently-impairs-explosive-power (g = −0.68 at 0 h). And every perceptual outcome was null for BOTH temperatures: soreness, fatigue, RPE and general recovery moved with time in every arm including control, treatment effects non-significant throughout (p = 0.752, 0.31, 0.68, 0.45) (water-immersion-does-not-improve-perceived-recovery-either-temperature) — the review says so in its own table footnote. So the objective outcomes are null-to-negative and the subjective ones are null here and bias-failing in Zhu; water immersion for recovery is running out of places to be true. ⚠ Appraised unreliable: the abstract's four positive claims are uncorrected single significant cells among dozens of comparisons across 8 studies (mean PEDro 5, commonest failing unblinded assessors), the abstract contradicts the paper's own Table 5 on creatine kinase (Horgan chronic HWI lowered CK p<0.001; Wellauer acute HWI RAISED it vs control, p=0.046), and 14 of 22 full texts were unobtained. Capped single-study — so this can neither promote nor demote a map, which is exactly right for a confirmatory source. Samples 14–40, ages 15–30, 6 of 8 studies male-only. confirms; Status held emerging. No temperature, duration or timing prescribed. OA PDF archived.

  • 2026-08-02 — ⚑ THE COLD-PACK MANUFACTURER PAID FOR A TRIAL OF ITS OWN PRODUCT AND PUBLISHED THE NULL. Status held emerging; no new map coined; one modality boundary recorded. Ingested Buehler 2026 (IJERPH 23(7):887, three-arm parallel RCT, n = 48 healthy young adults — 39 women, 9 men, age 22 ± 3, non-athletes by exclusion — randomised 1:1:1 to immediate cooling (nine 20-min gel-pack sessions over 72 h), delayed cooling (six sessions from 24 h), or no intervention, after a single-leg eccentric leg-extension damage protocol; prospectively registered NCT06813690 before recruitment; ethics BASEC 2024-D0116; linear mixed-effects models with baseline adjustment; ZERO attrition, zero loss to follow-up, no missing data on either primary outcome; appraised mixed, capped suggestive). Both primary outcomes null. DOMS: significant linear time effect (β = −0.568 ± 0.080, p < 0.001) with NO condition effect and NO time × condition interaction. MVIC: no significant time effect (p = 0.146), recovery trajectories identical across all three arms. Creatine kinase, ESR and muscle thickness moved with time in every arm and separated in none; a contrast-specific CRP interaction "did not indicate a systematic recovery advantage" (local-cooling-does-not-speed-recovery-at-any-timing). The damage protocol demonstrably worked, so this is a null against a stimulus that landed. ⚑ FUNDED BY AXANOVA AG — "a commercial manufacturer of topical recovery products (including cooling and warming gels, salves, sprays and patches)" — which also supplied the gel pack under test and paid the article processing charge. shared/study-appraisal.md says industry funding is a RED flag to record and never fatal alone; this vault has applied that rule to the AlzChem-funded creatine review, the Magtein® threonate trial and the conflicted bone-geometry letter, always in the direction of discounting. Applied symmetrically, a sponsor-funded null against the sponsor's own product category is a GREEN flag and is recorded as one (sponsor-funded-cooling-trial-published-its-own-null). Every ordinary incentive pointed at a positive result or non-publication; none operated. ⚑ This CONFIRMS rather than adds — and the map should say so. cold-water-immersion-does-not-speed-strength-recovery already carried Zhu 2026: 22 RCTs, n = 375, MVIC g = 0.08 (−0.11 to 0.26), no time-dependence, no publication bias, GRADE MODERATE. A 48-person trial does not add much to that, and no claim here pretends it does. What it adds is an independent replication via a different delivery method and a different timing question. ⚠ Two limits, both large, both stated by the authors themselves. (1) Power: the sample size was calculated from postoperative cooling data assuming between-group differences of 2.5–2.9 pain points (d ≈ 1.2–1.4), giving 13 per group; at 16 per arm "smaller effects may not have been detectable," and the paper states outright that "non-significant findings should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of the absence of effect." That refusal is the discipline recorded in subgroup-nulls-reported-as-insufficient-evidence-not-equivalence, and it is why fatal flag #3 does not fire. (2) Dose: reusable gel packs give "localized and surface-limited cooling compared with more intensive approaches such as cold-water immersion," and no intramuscular temperature, skin temperature or perfusion was measured — the authors concede the thermal dose reaching deep tissue "may have been insufficient" (gel-pack-cooling-may-not-deliver-a-thermal-dose-to-deep-muscle). A null with unverified exposure is a weaker null, and the same night's magnesium trial is the contrast: it verified exposure and still missed. ⚠ MODALITY BOUNDARY, recorded because this map's title is narrower than its contents. This map holds whole-body immersion, cold showers — and now surface gel packs. They are not interchangeable exposures. The recovery null now replicates across two of them rather than one; nothing licenses treating a pack and a plunge as the same intervention. This is a structural observation, not a fork claim — the map declares no sub-threads and sits below the fork threshold. What it means for the map's practical answer: cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy is a demonstrated, graded cost of post-workout cold (safety_flags: [interaction]), and there is still no measured recovery benefit to weigh against it. The wellbeing and sleep signals (cold-water-immersion-stress-sleep-wellbeing) are a separate claim and were not measured here, so they are untouched. confirms; Status unchanged (emerging) — a suggestive-capped 48-person trial cannot move a map, and it is confirming a meta-analysis-rct the map already holds. PDF NOT archived: gold OA, but the browser rung returned ⛔ GATED on both mdpi.com and the mdpi-res.com mirror; full text was obtained via NCBI PMC efetch, so nothing here rests on an abstract.

Ingested Hagner-Derengowska 2026 (post-exercise-compression-garments-endurance-soreness-meta-hagner-derengowska-2026, Frontiers in Physiology, systematic review + meta-analysis, 9 RCTs, n = 158 analysed, adult endurance athletes, three databases to 19 May 2026, OSF osf.io/46ck9, RoB 2 and GRADE applied, unfunded and unconflicted reviewers; open access, full text read at the browser rung). Capped single-study. ⚑ NO compression-garments map coined — 2 of 4 on the vault's coining bar (isolating contrast ✅, pre-specified dose ✅ in mmHg and wear-hours, machine-measured outcome ❌, low risk of bias ❌).   • ⚠ THE TIMING GATE NOW HAS A SECOND, INDEPENDENT SOURCE — and heat is its mirror image (new 2026-07-25). Bustos Carvajal 2026 finds CWI −2.4% and HWI +2.2% mean anaerobic power in a Wingate test minutes after immersion, each significant against control and p<0.0001 against each other (hot-immersion-raises-and-cold-immersion-lowers-acute-anaerobic-power). Two independent syntheses, two different instruments, one direction: cold makes you acutely less powerful within minutes. The vault's rule keeps both clauses — don't plunge right after lifting (chronic adaptation, cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy) and don't plunge shortly before anything needing power (acute performance). Flagged in both this map and sauna-heat per §6.5, interaction, efficacy-defeating rather than hazardous, and it enters no benefit score. ⚠ The mirror rule for heat is deliberately NOT written as a recommendation — one Wingate result in young athletes does not license a pre-training bathing protocol, and shared/safety.md forbids inventing one.
  • ⚠ And the subjective claims on this map are now held more loosely still. The map opened on stress↓ at 12 h, sleep-quality↑ and transient QoL↑ from a small, mostly-male, self-reported base (cold-water-immersion-stress-sleep-wellbeing). Since then: Zhu 2026 found the subjective positives failed the authors' own bias tests (cold-water-immersion-damage-marker-benefit-fails-bias-adjustment), and now eight further trials find no perceptual benefit for either temperature (water-immersion-does-not-improve-perceived-recovery-either-temperature). Both new sources are capped (suggestive and single-study) so no Status moves — but the map's founding claim is the one under most pressure, and a future run should look for a blinded or sham-controlled immersion trial before treating the wellbeing signal as established.   • ⚠ The timing gate matters MORE the harder the session (sharpened 2026-07-10). The vault now holds RCT-meta evidence that high-intensity loading (≥70% 1RM) is what drives the strength adaptation (high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength). The more adaptation a session generates, the more there is for post-workout cold to blunt. The rule is unchanged — don't plunge in the hours right after lifting — but its cost is now proportional to how hard the lift was. Carried on both maps.
  • ⚠ Interaction with resistance-training — blunts training adaptation (now graded, Piñero 2024). Post-exercise cold-water immersion attenuates resistance-training hypertrophy (meta-analysis of 8 RCTs, comparative SMD −0.22, 95.7% probability of blunting) and, in the wider literature, strength/power — the acute cold suppression of mTOR/satellite-cell signalling and blood flow is the same response that drives muscle adaptation. Now a graded concept (cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy, safety_flags: [interaction]), carried in both maps and the regimen interaction check; gates co-recommendation timing (don't plunge right after lifting — non-lifting days or well separated). Base is small/young-male → magnitude suggestive; direction well-established (shared/safety.md).
  • Hot/cold recovery pair with sauna-heat — same domain, same honesty. Both are knee-safe, low-evidence comfort/recovery tools whose marketed physiological claims outrun their randomized data. For the regimen they are one "thermal recovery" lever expressed two ways, not two independent wins; contrast therapy (alternating) has even thinner evidence and isn't ingested.
  • Sleep/wind-down overlap. The sleep-quality and stress signals overlap the wind-down cluster (sleep-regularity, l-theanine, chamomile, melatonin) — a redundancy to note, not a safety edge: if used for sleep, it's one wind-down approach among several, not additive on top of them.
  • ⚠ The timing rule gains a second clause, with a randomized number behind it (new 2026-07-22). The map already said: don't plunge in the hours right after lifting (chronic hypertrophy blunting, Piñero 2024). It can now add: don't plunge in the hours before anything that needs power — a jump, a sprint, a heavy set — because explosive performance is measurably worse for the first several hours (g = −0.68 at 0 h) and recovers by the next day (cold-water-immersion-transiently-impairs-explosive-power). These are two independent findings, not one confirmed twice, and the vault refuses the double-count: Piñero measured chronic adaptation across training blocks; Zhu measured acute performance within 72 hours and states explicitly that it offers no insight into long-term structural adaptation. Two timescales, two datasets, one direction. Carried on resistance-training.
  • ⚠ The map's own subjective wins should now be held more loosely (new 2026-07-22). Cain 2025 gave this map its stress-at-12-h and sleep-quality signals (cold-water-immersion-stress-sleep-wellbeing), both subjective, in an intervention nobody can be blinded to. Zhu 2026 does not contradict them — different outcomes, different timescale, different source — but it demonstrates within a single dataset that the subjective endpoint was the least trustworthy thing measured: objective outcomes null or negative, biochemical outcome erased by bias correction, and the only survivor the participant-reported one at VERY LOW certainty (cold-water-immersion-damage-marker-benefit-fails-bias-adjustment). That ordering is what an expectancy effect looks like. No Status change — a different study's bias pattern is not evidence about Cain's results — but it is the sharpest available statement of why this map frames cold as a comfort habit rather than a lever. Compare the identical discount the vault applies to ashwagandha and l-theanine, both resting on unblinded subjective scales.
  • Sibling framing with sauna-heat holds and hardens. Both remain knee-safe, low-evidence comfort/recovery tools whose marketed physiological claims outrun their randomized data — and this run strengthens that reading on the cold side specifically: the objective recovery benefit is now a moderate-certainty null. One "thermal recovery" lever expressed two ways, still not two independent wins.
