Statins for primary prevention in older adults — metabolic & pharmacologic

Status: contested (moved from emerging 2026-11-10) — coined 2026-08-22 by the first randomised controlled trial ever to test whether an adult aged 75 or older, already taking a statin for primary prevention, should keep taking it. Before that trial there was no randomised evidence at all on that question. Moved to contested 2026-11-10: STREAM, the larger international trial this map named by name as capable of moving it, has reported — and its confidence interval, unlike the founding trial's, does not straddle zero. The map's two best RCTs on its central question now point in different directions. safety_flags: [prescription, interaction] — this is a prescription medicine and per shared/safety.md nothing here enters the regimen layer.

⚠ This map is about STOPPING an established statin in over-75s without cardiovascular disease. It is not about starting one, not about secondary prevention, and not about anyone under 75. Those are different questions with different evidence, and the temptation to blur them is exactly why this warning is at the top.

What we believe now

⚑ 2026-11-10 — this map is now contested, and the tension is between its own two best trials, not between a trial and an observational literature. Bonnet 2026 found a mortality difference whose confidence interval straddled zero — a genuine null, generously read as "stopping did not raise mortality." STREAM, the larger international trial Bonnet's own authors named as the one that could move this map, found a composite-outcome confidence interval that does not straddle zero (it favors continuing) and a raw event rate roughly 3.5 points higher after stopping (12.7% vs 9.2%) — reported as "non-inferior" only because the difference, while real, was smaller than a pre-specified acceptability bound. Read together, the honest standing answer changed from "one inconclusive trial leans toward 'stopping is fine'" to "two trials, and the newer, larger, more diverse one leans the other way." See the 2026-11-10 through-line entry for the full appraisal. Everything below this paragraph describes the state the map held before STREAM arrived and is retained for its still-valid substance (the margin critique, the LDL dissociation, the observational contradiction, the deprescribing-rationale failure) — read it as "what Bonnet alone established," not as the map's final word.

One randomised trial says stopping did not raise three-year mortality; the trial's own authors say the bar it cleared was too low; and the observational literature that said the opposite is the more likely thing to be wrong.

(1) The result. In 1,160 French adults aged 75+ (median 80), with no cardiovascular disease, established on a statin for at least a year and mostly for over ten, randomised in ordinary general practice: three-year all-cause mortality was 7.9% on continuation and 7.2% after stopping — absolute difference −0.68 percentage points (95% CI −3.95 to +2.60). Non-inferior against a pre-specified 5-point margin.

(2) ⚠ The margin problem, which the paper raises before any critic could. That 5-point margin was chosen against an assumed 15% mortality; the observed rate was 7.6%. A 5-point absolute margin against a 7.6% event rate tolerates roughly a two-thirds relative increase in death. Verbatim from the limitations: the margin "is not acceptable relative to the observed mortality." The authors re-argue from the observed interval, which excludes a 3.5-point difference (≈1%/year). The pre-specified claim is weaker than it sounds; the honest claim is the one constructed afterwards.

(3) ⚑ The exposure change was real and biochemically verified, and the outcome did not follow it. LDL cholesterol rose from 114.6 to 170.6 mg/dL (≈+1.45 mmol/L) within three months of stopping and stayed there; the continuation arm did not move. A change of that size is large in the currency the statin literature is denominated in, and three-year mortality did not diverge. The map records this as a surrogate-versus-outcome dissociation and declines the extrapolation to "LDL doesn't matter" — three years is short, these plaques were already a decade stabilised, and the trial is underpowered.

(4) ⚠ Every cardiovascular secondary ran numerically the wrong way for stopping — heart attack 2.1% vs 1.4%, stroke 2.7% vs 1.9%, major events 5.0% vs 4.4% — with intervals that contain both zero and real harm. Nothing here rules out a modest excess of events.

(5) ⚑ The observational literature is contradicted, and the mechanism of the contradiction is named. Three cohorts (Italy, Denmark, France; 29,047–120,173 each) reported ~30% higher cardiovascular risk after discontinuation. None recorded why the statin was stopped. Confounding by indication — the drug is stopped because the patient is declining — is the parsimonious explanation, and randomisation did not reproduce the excess.

(6) ⚑ Deprescribing failed on its own rationale. Adverse events 73.1% vs 74.0%; myopathy 5.7% vs 5.1%; quality of life unchanged and identical. Stopping removed a burden that was not being felt — in a population selected, by a decade of tolerance, for not feeling it.

Where this leaves the standing question (Bonnet-only view, superseded above). For a person over 75 who has taken a statin for years, has no cardiovascular disease, and is not bothered by it, Bonnet alone did not tell them to stop and did not tell them to continue. STREAM has since reported and leans toward continuing (see the 2026-11-10 paragraph above and through-line entry). STAREE answers the adjacent initiation question (benefit on cardiovascular events, no disability-free-survival gain); PREVENTABLE remains outstanding.

No dose, statin, intensity or stopping rule is stated or implied anywhere on this map (shared/safety.md). Nothing here is medical advice.

  • 2026-08-22 — Map auto-coined by the first randomised trial of statin discontinuation for primary prevention in over-75s; opened at emerging. Ingested Bonnet 2026 (SAGA/SITE, The Lancet Healthy Longevity 7(7):100884, multicentre open-label pragmatic non-inferiority RCT, 1,180 randomised / 1,160 analysed, 297 French general practices, 3-year follow-up, NCT02547883, French Ministry of Health funded, funder had no role, independent statistical team; full text at the browser rung, pdf_unavailable — Europe PMC index lag plus a gated Elsevier PDF endpoint on an article whose text rendered freely). Primary: 3-year all-cause mortality 7.9% (continue) vs 7.2% (stop), absolute difference −0.68% (−3.95 to +2.60), non-inferior against a 5% margin (stopping-statins-after-75-did-not-raise-three-year-mortality). ⚠ The margin is the weak point and the authors say so: 5 points against an observed 7.6% event rate tolerates a two-thirds relative increase in death; they re-argue post hoc from the observed interval, which excludes 3.5 points (≈1%/year) (the-statin-trials-non-inferiority-margin-was-wider-than-its-own-event-rate-justified). ⚑ LDL rose +56 mg/dL (≈1.45 mmol/L) within three months of stopping and stayed there, and mortality did not follow — a surrogate-versus-outcome dissociation on the best-validated biomarker in cardiovascular medicine, recorded and deliberately not extrapolated (ldl-rose-56-mg-dl-after-statin-withdrawal-without-a-mortality-signal). ⚑ Three observational cohorts totalling >200,000 over-75s had reported ~30% more cardiovascular events after discontinuation; randomisation did not reproduce it, and confounding by indication is the named suspect (observational-statin-discontinuation-harm-did-not-survive-randomisation). ⚑ The deprescribing rationale failed on its own terms: adverse events 73.1% vs 74.0%, myopathy 5.7% vs 5.1%, quality of life unchanged in both arms over three years (statin-deprescribing-improved-neither-side-effects-nor-quality-of-life) — with one unexplained, uncorrected, undiscussed haematological row (0.3% vs 1.9%) recorded rather than tidied away. Appraised mixed — green: true randomisation, hard endpoint, pre-registered with a statistical analysis plan frozen before analysis, treatment-policy estimand, 4.9% symmetric crossover, multiple imputation for 6.6% missing outcomes, independent statisticians, public funding, and a limitations section that dismantles its own headline; red: 49% of planned enrolment, a margin its own authors disown, every cardiovascular secondary running the other way over a three-year horizon, open-label subjective endpoints, and no multiplicity adjustment. No fatal flag fires — flag #3 is the close call and does not fire, because the power analysis was pre-specified, the claim is a bounded non-inferiority claim rather than "no effect," and the authors refuse the generous reading themselves. All five concepts capped suggestive. Status emerging — one under-recruited trial, three pending.

  • 2026-08-31 — A safety null on the fear that circulates around this drug class, not an efficacy result — Status HELD emerging.   • 2026-08-31 — One of the three trials this map named as capable of moving it reports — and it answers a different question than the one the map was coined on. Status held emerging. Ingested Zoungas 2026 / STAREE (atorvastatin-cardiovascular-events-disability-free-survival-staree-rct-zoungas-2026, NEJM, multicentre double-blind RCT, 9,971 community-dwelling Australians ≥70 (mean 74.7), no known cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia, atorvastatin 40mg/day vs placebo, median follow-up 5.9 years; digest built from three convergent secondary/press renderings of a same-week publication, not the primary paper — full Methods UNVERIFIED). The primary cardiovascular composite fell from 8.3% to 6.0%, a 30% relative reduction (p<0.001), with LDL falling 48 vs 16 mg/dL (statin-initiation-cuts-cardiovascular-events-in-healthy-70-somethings). Disability-free survival — death, dementia, or persistent physical disability — did not differ: HR 0.94 (0.84–1.05), in a trial powered on this exact composite (statin-initiation-does-not-extend-disability-free-survival). Musculoskeletal, hepatobiliary and diabetes-related adverse events were all more common on atorvastatin; overall serious adverse events balanced. ⚑ STAREE is a statin-initiation trial in adults naive at baseline — this map's scope box says explicitly it is about stopping an established statin in over-75s, not starting one. Highest-tier-wins Status aggregation applies only within a shared claim, so answering the initiation question does not mechanically move a Status set by the discontinuation question (staree-answers-initiation-not-the-discontinuation-question-this-map-was-coined-on) — logged as an open editorial question (whether the map should hold both questions under one Status, or split) rather than silently decided. Appraised mixed (capped below reliable specifically because this vault has not yet read the primary paper); concepts capped suggestive. Status emerging HELD — one of the three named trials has now reported; STREAM and PREVENTABLE remain outstanding, and STAREE speaks to a different question than the one this map was coined to answer.

  • 2026-09-11 — A drug-choice question, not a stopping-or-starting one — and the headline effect is fragile. Status HELD emerging. Ingested Omayer 2026 (atorvastatin-rosuvastatin-inflammatory-biomarkers-meta-omayer-2026, BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, PROSPERO CRD42024579712, systematic review + meta-analysis of 35 head-to-head RCTs, n=6,148, appraised mixed). IL-6, adiponectin and TNF-α were null throughout (rosuvastatin-modestly-outperforms-atorvastatin-on-crp-reduction). ⚠ This map's scope is whether to STOP an established statin in an over-75 — a head-to-head comparison of two agents' CRP effect, in a mixed-age population, touches neither the stopping question nor the starting question STAREE answered. Recorded as agent-choice context. ⚠ The null→exclude-outlier→significant sequence, plus a surviving effect too small to plausibly move a cardiovascular risk category, keeps this at open-question strength — the same caution this vault applies to any SUCRA-style or post-hoc-refined claim. Ingested Long 2026 (statin-heterogeneity-primary-prevention-meta-regression-long-2026, Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy 40:2049-2060, PROSPERO CRD42024579932, systematic review + meta-regression + within-study subgroup meta-analysis, 25 RCTs, n=102,667, appraised mixed).   • 2026-10-02 — A new observational cohort on this map's live question, and it is the discontinuation cohorts' mirror image — same bias, opposite sign. Status HELD emerging. Ingested Lavon 2026 (statin-primary-prevention-over-80-cohort-lavon-2026, J Am Geriatr Soc 74(6):1687-1691, retrospective comparative cohort, 15,745 adults ≥80, 8,413 statin users, mean 4-year follow-up; funding/conflicts unverified; gold-OA PDF archived via the browser rung). Persistent statin use tracked with 31% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.69, 0.34–0.74) and 20% fewer coronary events (HR 0.80, 0.68–0.94), with no excess myopathy, diabetes or dementia — but no benefit for anyone who discontinued before 80 (persistent-statin-use-after-80-tracks-with-lower-mortality-observational). ⚠ Immortal-time/healthy-adherer bias is close to structurally guaranteed by this design — a "persistent user" survived and stayed well enough to keep being prescribed through the whole window, the identical bias this map's own founding RCT (Bonnet 2026) used to dismiss three discontinuation cohorts reporting the opposite-signed effect. "No benefit if stopped before 80" is exactly what that bias predicts, not independent evidence against it. Appraised mixed (large sample, hard endpoint, a genuine safety check; held well short of reliable by the unresolved immortal-time confound and no adjustment-set detail in the retrieved record); capped suggestive. confirms the map's standing point that this literature's observational layer keeps producing large, direction-of-convenience effects an RCT has already shown do not survive randomisation; Status emerging HELD — one RCT still outranks any number of observational cohorts on this exact question. Titles-scan + global ripple: no interaction, no safety_flags change beyond the standing prescription flag.
