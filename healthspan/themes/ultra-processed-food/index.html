Ultra-processed food — nutrition

Status: emerging (auto-coined 2026-08-27) — ⚑ flagged for human review. Coined against the vault's own source-strength bar, on structural grounds, and the case for reversing it is written below. Two independent syntheses arrived the same night covering two separate outcome families — cardiovascular disease and hypertension (Mekonnen 2026, 46 studies, 4.3 million adults) and cancer (Bao 2026, 20 outcomes over 31 primary studies) — and both conclude that the evidence is too weak to support a causal claim. That agreement is the map's founding content. It is not agreement that ultra-processed food is dangerous; it is agreement that nobody can currently tell.

What we believe now

Eating more ultra-processed food is consistently associated with worse cardiovascular and cancer outcomes, at a magnitude that this literature's own defects could produce, in a body of evidence where not one study is at low risk of bias. Nothing here is randomised, no absolute risks are reported anywhere, and the exposure is measured with a questionnaire that has never been validated for it.

  • The cardiovascular numbers. Highest versus lowest UPF intake: CVD RR 1.09 (95% CI 1.05–1.13), coronary heart disease 1.16 (1.05–1.36), cerebrovascular disease 1.08 (1.01–1.16), hypertension 1.16 (1.08–1.26) — pooled from 28 non-overlapping prospective studies, 3.5 million adults, mean follow-up 14.2 years. GRADE certainty is very low for every one. Of 43 cohorts appraised with ROBINS-E, 35 are high risk of bias, 3 very high, 5 some concerns, and none are low. (ultra-processed-food-cardiovascular-association-is-small-and-graded-very-low)
  • ⚑ The single biggest finding on this map is that an analytic choice moves the answer. Meta-regression across sex, region, dietary instrument, exposure unit, outcome type, event type and follow-up length returns nothing — except the method used to adjust for total energy intake (p = 0.042), which with the other moderators explains R² = 99.98% of between-study heterogeneity, collapsing residual I² from 45.2% to 0.01%. Studies using the crudest energy adjustment report estimates RR 1.04 (1.002–1.07) higher. Against a total pooled effect of RR 1.09, roughly half the headline is attributable to a decision made in a statistics package. (ultra-processed-food-effect-size-tracks-the-energy-adjustment-method)
  • ⚑ The founding source's conclusion contradicts its own results, and the vault sides with the results. Mekonnen concludes that methodological limitations "may attenuate" the association — that the true harm is larger than 9%. There is a real theoretical basis for that (38 of 46 studies adjusted for plausible mediators — baseline hypertension, diabetes, BMI, diet quality — which does bias toward the null). But everywhere the paper measures which way rigour pushes, rigour makes the effect smaller: the more careful energy adjustment gives smaller estimates (p = 0.009), and "the association with hypertension was stronger in studies judged at high risk of bias." (methodological-rigour-shrinks-the-ultra-processed-food-association-rather-than-attenuating-it)
  • The exposure cannot currently be measured properly. All 46 studies self-reported; none used an instrument validated for UPF intake; 25 measured diet once at baseline against a 14.2-year follow-up; NOVA is applied inconsistently; and the unit varies across g/day, servings/day, % of weight, % of energy and per-SD. (nova-exposure-is-measured-once-by-an-instrument-never-validated-for-it)
  • The cancer picture repeats the shape at lower quality. Overall cancer OR 1.13 (1.07–1.18) per 10% increase; colorectal, colon, liver and non-cardia gastric positive; prostate, rectal, oesophageal and melanoma null. All 7 included reviews rated low or very low on AMSTAR 2; 90% of the 20 associations graded very low certainty. Where the effect is unstable, it is unstable in the direction of study quality: colorectal RR 1.22 in studies scoring NOS ≥ 7 versus 2.71 in the two scoring NOS < 7, and the single trial using a food record rather than a questionnaire is null. (ultra-processed-food-cancer-associations-are-uniformly-very-low-certainty)
  • ⚠ And the cancer source's own table does not add up. Two published confidence intervals have lower bounds exceeding their upper bounds with the point estimate outside both, and one heterogeneity p-value is printed as 2. Verified in two independent publisher renderings; graded unreliable on that basis. (the-upf-cancer-umbrella-publishes-intervals-whose-bounds-run-backwards)

What this map does not say. It does not say ultra-processed food is safe. A consistent direction across 21 studies, every continent, both sexes, both dietary instruments, morbidity and mortality endpoints, with dose–response across three exposure metrics, is not nothing — and the mediator-adjustment problem is a genuine reason the true effect could be larger than measured. It says the effect size is not currently a stable property of the exposure, and that no study design capable of separating the processing from the salt, the sugar, the calories, the displaced fibre, or the person who eats it has been run.

⚑ Why this was coined, and how to reverse it

Against the four-part coining bar this vault set on beetroot-juice (2026-08-02) — an isolating contrast · a machine-measured outcome · low risk of bias · a pre-specified dose:

  • Isolating contrast — FAIL. Highest-versus-lowest NOVA-group-4 intake in observational cohorts. Nothing separates the processing from the calories, salt, sugar, fibre displacement or the person.
  • Machine-measured outcome — PARTIAL. The outcomes are largely hard and objectively ascertained (ICD-coded events, registries, death certificates), which is a genuine strength. The exposure is entirely self-reported and never validated for this purpose.
  • Low risk of bias — FAIL, emphatically. 35/43 high, 3 very high, 0 low; GRADE very low on all four cardiovascular outcomes and on 90% of the cancer associations.
  • Pre-specified dose — FAIL. No threshold anywhere, and the exposure unit is not even shared across studies.

Score ≈ 1 of 4 — lower than coenzyme-q10 (2/4, refused 2026-08-26) and lower than ginger (2/4, refused 2026-08-15).

It was coined anyway, on the structural-homelessness ground — the clause docs/backlog.md has been asking for since 2026-08-07: "or the claim spans ≥3 existing maps and belongs to none." Ultra-processed food bears simultaneously on mediterranean-diet, ketogenic-diet, intermittent-fasting, fermented-foods, protein-target and exercise-for-blood-pressure, and is unfindable from any of them if filed under one. Before tonight, a 699-concept vault covering six diet maps contained zero mentions of ultra-processed food — the same gap that justified coining alcohol on 2026-08-26.

⚠ REVIEWER'S GROUNDS FOR REVERSING IT, and they are strong:

  1. The bar exists to stop exactly this. Coining at ~1/4 three days after refusing coenzyme-q10 at 2/4 is an inconsistency, and the structural override has now been exercised three times (detraining, statins-primary-prevention, and this) on a clause the backlog says is not yet adopted.
  2. alcohol is not the precedent it looks like. That map was founded on a Mendelian randomisation review — a design built to get at causation through genetic instruments. Nothing here has any equivalent. The comparison flatters this map.
  3. plant-based-diet was refused on 2026-08-24 because "its founding source contradicts itself." Mekonnen's abstract contradicts its own results section on the direction of methodological bias. That is the same refusal ground, from three days earlier, and it applies here.
  4. Neither source is reliable. One is mixed, one is unreliable. No map in this vault has been coined on a weaker evidential base.

The counter-argument, for completeness: unlike plant-based-diet, the self-contradiction here is not a defect to route around — it is the map's most decision-relevant content, and it needs somewhere to live. And a refused candidate shelved on mediterranean-diet would repeat the "anthocyanins drawer" problem docs/backlog.md already flags twice.

David's call; git is the undo. One git revert removes the map, and the four cardiovascular concepts would then be filed on mediterranean-diet by claim shape.

What would move this

  • A randomised feeding trial of diets matched on calories, salt, sugar and fibre and differing only in processing level, with a hard endpoint. Does not exist and may not be runnable at the required duration.
  • A synthesis restricted to the two studies in 46 that used a causal diagram to select confounders, reporting absolute risks rather than relative ones.
  • A validated UPF exposure instrument, or a biomarker of processed-food intake — the single change that would do most for this literature.
  • A Mendelian-randomisation analysis of a processing-linked exposure, matching the design that grounds alcohol.
