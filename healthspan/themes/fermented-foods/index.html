Fermented foods — nutrition

Status: emerging (2026-07-08) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's first whole-food / dietary-pattern lever (as opposed to isolated nutrients). Coined by a very large meta-analysis of prospective cohorts. Opened emerging — and framed deliberately humbly: the effects are small, food-specific, and observational.

What we believe now

A diet that includes fermented dairy is mildly associated with lower mortality — but the effect is small, food-specific, and not causally established. Across 50 cohorts (>3 million adults), fermented milk / yogurt tracked with ~6% lower all-cause (RR 0.941), CVD (0.932), and cancer (0.940) mortality, and cheese with slightly lower all-cause mortality (0.970); chocolate/cocoa was also protective, while miso and bread were neutral. Two honesties define this map: (1) the associations are single-digit percent — squarely inside the range plausible confounding can produce, on self-reported diet; and (2) the signal is food-specific (dairy, cocoa), so "fermentation" is not the active ingredient and the result doesn't license a ferment-everything conclusion. This is an ingest-and-flag entry, not a lever: it supports a food-first pattern that happens to include yogurt, not a prescription to add fermented foods to lower mortality.

Why emerging and framed humbly: high nominal power (>3M) but a small effect on an observational, self-reported exposure — per shared/study-appraisal.md, exactly the profile to read cautiously. Fits GOAL.md as a low-burden dietary preference, not a supplement slot.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-07-24 — a biomarker rule arrives from mediterranean-diet and applies directly to this map's 50-cohort self-reported base. Dhana 2026 validated dietary adherence against plasma lutein-zeaxanthin and β-carotene — real evidence the food was eaten. The vault records what that does and does not buy (diet-adherence-biomarkers-validate-exposure-not-causal-inference): it repairs recall/information bias, one of seven observational lenses, and leaves healthy-adherer bias, reverse causation and residual confounding untouched. This map's ~6% mortality association (RR ~0.94) rests on self-reported intake and would not be promoted by a future paper attaching a nutrient biomarker to it. No Status move; no safety_flags.
  • Overlaps the protein-food space of protein-target. Fermented dairy (yogurt, kefir, cottage cheese) is also a protein source — so a food-first way to hit the ~1.6 g/kg/day protein target doubles as this dietary pattern. A synergy of convenience, not a graded interaction: the vault should present "eat yogurt/dairy" once, serving both maps, not as two separate recommendations.

  • Global-ripple / safety: no interaction with any vault intervention. Standard food-safety cautions (histamine sensitivity, sodium in miso/some cheeses, unpasteurised-product risk in pregnancy) are general nutrition safety, not a cross-intervention edge — noted, not graded. No safety_flags set.
  • ⚠ Randomised evidence now agrees from the other direction: the probiotic is not the active ingredient (new 2026-07-13). This map's founding read was that "fermentation" isn't the active ingredient — the mortality signal is food-specific (fermented milk/yogurt ~6%; cheese/cocoa modest; miso and bread null), which is not how a live-organism mechanism would behave (fermented-foods-signal-is-food-specific-and-small). That was an inference from an observational pattern. It now has a randomised test. Chang 2026 (protein-target) hands people the live organisms directly, on top of protein — 6 of its 20 trials deliver them as yogurt — and finds the probiotic adds nothing demonstrable to physical performance: nine of ten added-value contrasts cross zero (probiotic-adds-nothing-to-protein-for-performance). Two independent lines, two designs, same answer: whatever the fermented-dairy association is picking up, it is not the bacteria. Most likely it is the food — the protein, the calcium, the dairy matrix, or simply the kind of person whose diet contains yogurt. The regimen consequence is unchanged and now better grounded: eat the yogurt if you like it (it is a Mediterranean food and it doubles as protein), and do not buy a probiotic. ⚑ Probiotics remain a flagged candidate, not adopted — no map coined, logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
