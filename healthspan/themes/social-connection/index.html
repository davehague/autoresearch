Social connection (loneliness, isolation) — stress & mental

Status: emerging (2026-07-20) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review, on evidence noticeably weaker than its neighbour purpose-in-life. Coined from a single pre-registered meta-analysis (Zheng 2026, 167 articles, 158 independent samples, 303,643 participants, 36 countries) that asks a question the loneliness literature had been fudging: does loneliness track with worse health in people who are not already sick?

What we believe now

Yes — and at roughly a third less than the field has been claiming, then half that again once you stop asking people to rate their own health. This is a map coined on a shrinkage. Its most useful contribution is subtraction.

(1) The famous number was inflated by including sick people. Prior meta-analyses reported r = −.35 for loneliness and general health — pooling in samples defined by HIV, Alzheimer's, diabetes, and severe mental illness, populations where illness plausibly causes the loneliness. Restrict to healthy samples and the association drops to r = −.22 overall, r = −.23 for general health (loneliness-associated-with-worse-health-in-healthy-populations). Attacking your own field's weakest assumption by design, and publishing the resulting shrinkage, is the mark of a serious synthesis.

(2) The number to actually carry is r = −.12. Measurement type significantly moderated the association (F = 17.01, p < .0001): r = −.23 on subjective health measures, r = −.12 on objective ones (loneliness-health-association-halves-on-objective-measures). The authors' own explanation is that loneliness and self-rated health are both subjective experiences shaped by individual perception — which is a candid description of common-method variance. Much of the headline effect may be one disposition, measured twice, reported as a relationship. The vault carries r = −.12 as the defensible estimate: real, and explaining a little over one percent of variance. Set that against the framing this claim usually travels with — "as bad as fifteen cigarettes a day."

(3) The one behavioural outcome came back null. Health service use — the only outcome in the set that asks whether lonely people actually turn up rather than how they feel — was not associated (loneliness-unrelated-to-health-service-use). Two independent signals in the same paper point the same way: the further this literature gets from self-report, the weaker its central claim becomes.

Why emerging, and why it should not be read as equivalent to purpose-in-life. The vault now holds two psychosocial maps and they are not in the same evidentiary league. purpose-in-life rests on 25 samples, three continents, up to 32 years, and 48,928 actual deaths. This map rests on cross-sectional self-report with no hard endpoint at all — no mortality, no incident disease. Add extreme heterogeneity (over 92% of variance is genuine between-study difference), an effect size squarely inside confounding range where SES and employment could generate it unaided, and reverse causation narrowed-but-not-closed by the healthy-sample restriction. Appraised mixed; concepts capped suggestive. The vault should not let "the psychosocial stuff matters" become one undifferentiated belief when one half is measured against deaths and the other against a questionnaire.

(4) ⚑ THE MAP'S FOUNDING WEAKNESS NARROWS: it now holds a hard endpoint (new 2026-07-31). Everything above was written about self-rated health, measured cross-sectionally — and the map said so, in bold, warning against reading it as the equal of purpose-in-life. An umbrella review of 54 systematic reviews, 66 meta-analysed outcomes and 270 associations on urban-environment exposures and cognitive health now supplies incident dementia, pooled across cohort studies: low social contact RR 1.323–1.853, graded convincing — the top of a five-tier credibility scheme requiring p < 1×10⁻⁶, >1,000 cases, the largest study also significant, I² < 50%, and no small-study or excess-significance bias (low-social-contact-dementia-risk-graded-convincing). Loneliness and dementia RR 1.192–2.092, social participation 1.132–1.745, structural aspects and cognitive decline OR 1.050–1.114; 5 of 8 social outcomes significant. ⚑ And the comparative result is the striking one: across a review of everything an urban environment does to an ageing brain — air pollution, noise, greenness, walkability, built form — the largest effect that also earned the top credibility grade was contact with other people. Every environmental exposure that reached the top two tiers did so at 3–9%.

⚠ Why this narrows the gap and does not close it, and why Status holds emerging. Three reasons, each sufficient on its own. The source is appraised mixed and capped suggestive, and a suggestive-capped source cannot move a Status (shared/evidence-model.md). Reverse causation is live and unaddressed — social withdrawal is a recognised dementia prodrome running years before diagnosis, and the review reports no lag analyses and no early-case exclusion windows, which is the check that would separate cause from symptom. And the social-environment reviews were the umbrella's lowest-quality stratum — exclusively moderate on the Joanna Briggs Institute tool, while air pollution attracted the high-quality ones. A top-tier grade earned on the least-scrutinised evidence in the review, with reverse causation open, is not a consolidating map.

What it costs: nothing, and there is still nothing here to do. Loneliness and social contact have not been randomised. Neither source tests whether increasing connection improves anything — both are associations, not interventions. As with purpose-in-life, the exit from emerging may be structurally unavailable, and the regimen layer should be careful not to turn a life circumstance into a task.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-07-31 — the environment maps that were never coined all live here now, and the vault should say so in one place. Three place-based exposures have been flagged as candidate interventions and refused across two nights: residential greenspace (2026-07-30), and air pollution and traffic noise (tonight, via air-pollution-cognitive-harm-is-consistent-and-unrandomised). All three share one disqualifying property: exposure is measured at an address, nobody was assigned anything, and the exposure is inseparable from socioeconomic position — which independently predicts every outcome in play. This map carries them because the same umbrella review is their only vault-quality source and because its highest-graded finding is the social one. ⚑ What would change the answer for any of them is the same design: a quasi-experiment that separates the exposure from wealth — a regulatory difference-in-differences, a within-person change on moving, a park-construction comparison — or, for air quality specifically, a randomised indoor-filtration trial with a cognitive endpoint, which is entirely feasible and does not exist.
  • ⚠ 2026-07-31 — the greenspace refusal is corrected in its reasoning, not reversed in its conclusion. Last night's refusal cited a null epigenetic-age surrogate as one of four supports. This review reports greenspace associated with lower dementia risk at highly probable across three outcome families (greenspace-dementia-signal-outruns-its-epigenetic-surrogate, filed on biological-age-clocks). The vault's own standing rule — a surrogate does not predict an outcome — runs in both directions, and the vault had been applying it in one. The refusal stands on the grounds that survive; the surrogate argument is withdrawn.
  • Global-ripple: no safety interaction with any vault intervention. Social connection is psychosocial — nothing is ingested or dosed. No supplement, drug, or recovery modality in the vault interacts with it. safety_flags: [] on all three concept notes is a finding, not an oversight.
  • Sibling of, and deliberately not merged with, purpose-in-life. Both are psychosocial exposures in the stress-mental domain; both are structurally hard to randomise. They are kept separate because the evidence quality differs by more than a tier — deaths versus questionnaires — and merging them would let the weaker inherit the stronger's footing. A human reviewer may reasonably decide otherwise; logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
  • Overlaps the sleep cluster, ungraded. Sleep function was one of the five outcome domains (r = −.19), which touches sleep-duration and sleep-regularity. The vault does not grade a loneliness→sleep pathway: the measurement is subjective on both sides, the design is cross-sectional, and poor sleep causing loneliness is at least as plausible as the reverse. Named, not graded (shared/safety.md).
  • The methodological finding generalises well beyond this map — and indicts two of the vault's own. The subjective-vs-objective moderator (r = −.23 → −.12) puts a number on common-method variance: roughly half. ashwagandha and l-theanine both rest on subjective stress and anxiety scales reported by imperfectly-blinded participants, and neither has an objective-measure moderator analysis to check itself against. This does not move either Status — a different literature's moderator is not evidence about theirs — but it is the sharpest available statement of why the vault has been discounting them, and it should inform how their next high-tier source is read.
  • No redundancy and no stacking question. Nothing in the regimen competes with, substitutes for, or potentiates social connection. Worth stating plainly given the neighbouring botanicals: both ashwagandha and l-theanine move subjective stress markers over ~8 weeks, and neither is a substitute for the thing itself.
