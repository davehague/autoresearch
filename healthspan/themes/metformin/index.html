Metformin — metabolic / pharmacologic

Status: contested (2026-07-07) — coined this run from a human-greenlit sweep of the longevity trio. Prescription drug → by rule it is never ranked in the daily protocol and no dose is set here (shared/safety.md). A clinician-only pharmacologic lever, alongside glp-1 and rapamycin.

What we believe now

For the healthspan/anti-aging use case in non-diabetics, metformin's clean human evidence is null — and in frail elders, net-negative. The MET-PREVENT RCT (n=72, mean age 80, sarcopenic + pre-frail/frail; 500 mg TID × 4 months) found no effect on 4-metre walk speed (adjusted difference 0.001 m/s, p=0.96) and a harm signal: hospital admissions 34% (metformin) vs 8% (placebo).

The tension worth keeping visible (why contested, not fading):

  • The pro-longevity case is observational and preclinical. Enthusiasm rests on diabetic-cohort mortality associations, mechanism (AMPK, autophagy), and animal data — not on outcome RCTs in non-diabetics.
  • The population-appropriate landmark hasn't reported. TAME (Barzilai, n≈3,000, aging as the primary endpoint, healthier non-diabetics 65–79) is expected ~2026–27; the long DPPOS follow-up found no mortality difference (HR 0.99). So MET-PREVENT's null is in a specific frail population and doesn't by itself settle TAME's question — but the human outcome data that do exist are null-to-harmful.

⚑ The exercise-interaction flag now has a number on one of its three axes, and it is smaller than the flag implied (new 2026-07-30). For twenty-three days this map carried the AMPK-antagonism concern with its magnitude deliberately left blank — "flagged, not fabricated (no vault-quality source ingested yet)." A triple-blind randomised placebo-controlled trial has now tested it for aerobic exercise, and the honest summary is that both arms got fitter and the drug arm got fitter more slowly on one of two ways of expressing the same measurement (metformin-attenuates-the-vo2peak-gain-from-aerobic-training) — with the direct between-group comparison null (metformin-exercise-attenuation-rests-on-an-interaction-not-a-group-difference). The restraint was vindicated: a magnitude guessed from mechanism would have been larger and more confident than what a blinded trial produced. ⚠ And the trial's sturdiest finding is not the one this map was flagging. Metformin added up to 2 full Borg units of perceived effort at the same relative workload while placebo added nothing (metformin-raises-perceived-effort-at-the-same-exercise-intensity) — a larger, more consistent effect than the fitness attenuation, and the one that bears on GOAL.md's adherence-first constraint.

⚑ ONE NIGHT LATER, THE EXERCISE-INTERACTION FLAG CLOSES ON ALL THREE AXES — AND IT CLOSES AS A NULL, NOT A HARM (new 2026-07-31). DEMFOS (Nava 2026, Lancet Healthy Longevity) randomised 114 older adults with obesity to a six-month supervised programme — 30 min aerobic plus 30 min progressive resistance training, three times a week — with metformin or matching placebo, intention-to-treat, prospectively powered. Metformin changed nothing: VO₂peak +0.1 mL/kg/min (−1.5 to 1.6), knee extensor torque +1.2 N·m (−5.4 to 7.8), MRI thigh muscle volume −12 cm³ (−69 to 45), DXA lean mass −0.3 kg (−1.1 to 0.6) (metformin-does-not-blunt-training-adaptation-in-older-adults). The muscle-hypertrophy axis the map said was "entirely unquantified" now has a randomised number, and it is zero — and the aerobic axis is answered in the opposite direction to Methnani, by the better trial. The AMPK-blunting worry is recorded as rebutted rather than deleted (back-edge written per shared/evidence-model.md). ⚠ The protein/mTOR axis is answered only indirectly — DEMFOS randomised no protein intake, prescribing ~1 g/kg/day to every arm; what it shows is that adding metformin on top did not further damage muscle. ⚠ And the effort penalty is untouched — DEMFOS did not measure perceived exertion at all.

⚑ The same trial is also the map's second independent randomised null on function. Metformin added 0.1 points (−1.2 to 1.4) on a nine-task physical performance battery where the trial was powered to detect 1.8 — so the interval excludes the difference its own designers called meaningful (metformin-adds-nothing-to-lifestyle-intervention-for-physical-function). ⚠ Bounded by a missing fourth cell: with no education-plus-placebo arm, the trial can say metformin adds nothing on top of training but cannot say what metformin does in someone who is not training.

Regimen implication. Clinician-only; lives in the regimen's "discuss with your clinician" section with the evidence summarized, never scored against Benefit/Burden. Honest one-liner: strong observational/mechanistic story, but the human healthspan RCTs are null (and, in the frail, harmful) — wait for TAME. Updated 2026-07-31: the sharper version of the clinician item is now that metformin appears neither to help nor to blunt what training is doing — the best-powered trial of the two together found no difference on any of seventeen outcomes. What is left of the older concern is the subjective one: a smaller, blinded trial found the same workout felt up to two Borg units harder on the drug, and no larger trial has measured perceived exertion since. A drug that changes nothing objectively can still change whether the exercise happens.

Eleven data points. Provocative for a drug whose longevity indication targets people over 65 — the question TAME exists to answer — and a hypothesis, not evidence. ✅ Why mixed and not unreliable, decided by rule.   • 2026-08-24 — ⚑ THE PUREST CASE OF RANK-LAUNDERING THIS VAULT HAS INGESTED, AND THE MAP DECLINES A SIGNAL THAT POINTS ITS OWN WAY. Status HELD contested. Ingested Lee 2026 (antidiabetic-drug-classes-parkinson-disease-risk-bayesian-nma-lee-2026, Journal of Global Health 16:04187, Bayesian network meta-analysis, 9 retrospective cohorts, 712,287 patients with type 2 diabetes, seven antidiabetic classes, PROSPERO CRD420251130232, ROBINS-I, no relevant interests declared). ⚠ No randomised trial of this question exists and the paper says so. The primary result, in the authors' own first result sentence: "No antidiabetic class demonstrated a statistically significant difference in PD risk." ⚑ Table 2 is the receipt — all 21 pairwise credible intervals cross 1, several spanning two orders of magnitude. SGLT2i vs α-glucosidase inhibitors 4.94 (0.44 to 68.64); GLP-1RA vs AGI 3.43 (0.28 to 39.01); SGLT2i vs metformin 4.03 (0.87 to 20.38) (no-antidiabetic-class-differed-significantly-in-parkinson-risk, single-study). ⚑ And the abstract concludes that SGLT2i and GLP-1RA were "consistently associated with lower PD risk, suggesting potential neuroprotective effects." The bridge is SUCRA (the-neuroprotection-claim-rests-on-rank-probabilities-over-an-entirely-null-network, pushes-back). Rank probabilities are a deterministic function of the posterior: feed them a network in which nothing is distinguishable and they still return a first place. "Consistently" means the point estimates kept landing in the same order — which is what point estimates drawn from one shared posterior do. ⚑ This map's SUCRA discipline has five prior instances; this is the first where the underlying comparisons are not merely uncertain but uniformly, exhaustively null and the ranking IS the entire result. ⚑ The consistency claim does not survive the paper's own single sensitivity analysis. Restricting to studies with ≥2 years of follow-up changes the winner: SGLT2i tops the primary ranking, GLP-1RA tops the sensitivity ranking (the-drug-ranking-flipped-under-the-papers-own-follow-up-sensitivity-analysis, pushes-back). A ranking that reorders when you drop the shortest studies is a ranking of study durations. The paper reports the flip in one clause and continues to describe the association as consistent in the abstract, the discussion and the conclusion. ⚑ Significant estimates DO exist in this paper — in an unregistered frequentist re-analysis in supplementary Figure S5, quoted only in the discussion. Versus AGI: SGLT2i RR 0.15 (0.04–0.55), GLP-1RA 0.19 (0.05–0.70), DPP4i 0.25 (0.07–0.82) — against the registered Bayesian model's 4.94 (0.44–68.64) and 3.43 (0.28–39.01) on the same comparisons (the-significant-estimates-live-in-an-unregistered-reanalysis-in-a-supplementary-figure, pushes-back). ⚑ Same data, same comparisons, inverted verdicts, and the version that clears zero lives in the supplement. This vault's rule — when a paper's headline disagrees with the pre-specified primary analysis, the pre-specified analysis is the finding (null-diet-trial-recovered-by-adherence-subsetting) — applies with one adjustment: here it is analysis versus analysis, and the registered one is the finding. ⚠ And the design cannot answer the question in principle. Follow-ups run 427 days (Mui) · ~987 days (Sun) · 1.5–1.7 years (Tang) · 2.2 · 2.97 · 3 · 3.6 · 6.94 years (Brakedal) — against a Parkinson's prodrome of ten to twenty years (parkinson-follow-up-here-is-shorter-than-the-prodrome-it-claims-to-prevent, pushes-back). In a 427-day window essentially every incident diagnosis was already forming before the first prescription. The outcome is an ICD code in every study, unadjudicated. What such a design detects is which drug the prodrome steers people toward and which health system codes it faster — the authors name this exactly ("early prodromal symptoms of PD may affect treatment selection") and proceed. ⚠ Appraised unreliable. Two fatal red flags fire: #1 (a practice-relevant causal claim — "selection of antidiabetic therapies should consider long-term neurological benefits" — from observational data whose central confounder, confounding by indication, the authors themselves call unresolved and unadjustable from claims data) and #5 (reverse causation more parsimonious, design cannot exclude it). Every concept capped single-study. Per shared/evidence-model.md, an unreliable result cannot move a map.   • ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-31, AS A NULL — the exercise interaction, on all three axes. DEMFOS ran metformin against placebo on top of a six-month supervised programme containing 30 minutes of progressive resistance training three times a week and measured muscle three separate ways — MRI thigh volume, DXA lean mass, and isokinetic knee extensor torque and power. All flat, along with VO₂peak, walking distance and gait speed (metformin-does-not-blunt-training-adaptation-in-older-adults).   • ⚠ Interaction with the exercise levers — AMPK antagonism of training adaptation. SUPERSEDED by the entry above; retained as the history. Partially quantified 2026-07-30, on one axis of three. For someone whose foundation is exercise, this is a real trade-off. Flagged in both directions (safety_flags: [interaction]). The cardiorespiratory axis now has a randomised number and it is modest and internally contested — interaction p = 0.042 on absolute VO₂peak, p = 0.088 scaled to body weight, both direct between-group comparisons null, in 11 people per arm over 5 weeks (metformin-attenuates-the-vo2peak-gain-from-aerobic-training, metformin-exercise-attenuation-rests-on-an-interaction-not-a-group-difference). The mechanism is real and the magnitude is smaller than the flag implied. ⚠ The muscle-hypertrophy axis and the protein/mTOR anabolic axis remain entirely unquantified — Methnani tested aerobic exercise only, with no strength, muscle-mass, fibre or protein measurement and no resistance-training arm. Those two halves of the flag stay open, and a future run must not close them on this citation. Noted identically on resistance-training and protein-target.
