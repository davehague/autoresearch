Metformin — metabolic / pharmacologic

Status: contested (2026-07-07) — coined this run from a human-greenlit sweep of the longevity trio. Prescription drug → by rule it is never ranked in the daily protocol and no dose is set here (shared/safety.md). A clinician-only pharmacologic lever, alongside glp-1 and rapamycin.

What we believe now

For the healthspan/anti-aging use case in non-diabetics, metformin's clean human evidence is null — and in frail elders, net-negative. The MET-PREVENT RCT (n=72, mean age 80, sarcopenic + pre-frail/frail; 500 mg TID × 4 months) found no effect on 4-metre walk speed (adjusted difference 0.001 m/s, p=0.96) and a harm signal: hospital admissions 34% (metformin) vs 8% (placebo).

The tension worth keeping visible (why contested, not fading):

  • The pro-longevity case is observational and preclinical. Enthusiasm rests on diabetic-cohort mortality associations, mechanism (AMPK, autophagy), and animal data — not on outcome RCTs in non-diabetics.
  • The population-appropriate landmark hasn't reported. TAME (Barzilai, n≈3,000, aging as the primary endpoint, healthier non-diabetics 65–79) is expected ~2026–27; the long DPPOS follow-up found no mortality difference (HR 0.99). So MET-PREVENT's null is in a specific frail population and doesn't by itself settle TAME's question — but the human outcome data that do exist are null-to-harmful.

⚑ The exercise-interaction flag now has a number on one of its three axes, and it is smaller than the flag implied (new 2026-07-30). For twenty-three days this map carried the AMPK-antagonism concern with its magnitude deliberately left blank — "flagged, not fabricated (no vault-quality source ingested yet)." A triple-blind randomised placebo-controlled trial has now tested it for aerobic exercise, and the honest summary is that both arms got fitter and the drug arm got fitter more slowly on one of two ways of expressing the same measurement (metformin-attenuates-the-vo2peak-gain-from-aerobic-training) — with the direct between-group comparison null (metformin-exercise-attenuation-rests-on-an-interaction-not-a-group-difference). The restraint was vindicated: a magnitude guessed from mechanism would have been larger and more confident than what a blinded trial produced. ⚠ And the trial's sturdiest finding is not the one this map was flagging. Metformin added up to 2 full Borg units of perceived effort at the same relative workload while placebo added nothing (metformin-raises-perceived-effort-at-the-same-exercise-intensity) — a larger, more consistent effect than the fitness attenuation, and the one that bears on GOAL.md's adherence-first constraint.

⚑ ONE NIGHT LATER, THE EXERCISE-INTERACTION FLAG CLOSES ON ALL THREE AXES — AND IT CLOSES AS A NULL, NOT A HARM (new 2026-07-31). DEMFOS (Nava 2026, Lancet Healthy Longevity) randomised 114 older adults with obesity to a six-month supervised programme — 30 min aerobic plus 30 min progressive resistance training, three times a week — with metformin or matching placebo, intention-to-treat, prospectively powered. Metformin changed nothing: VO₂peak +0.1 mL/kg/min (−1.5 to 1.6), knee extensor torque +1.2 N·m (−5.4 to 7.8), MRI thigh muscle volume −12 cm³ (−69 to 45), DXA lean mass −0.3 kg (−1.1 to 0.6) (metformin-does-not-blunt-training-adaptation-in-older-adults). The muscle-hypertrophy axis the map said was "entirely unquantified" now has a randomised number, and it is zero — and the aerobic axis is answered in the opposite direction to Methnani, by the better trial. The AMPK-blunting worry is recorded as rebutted rather than deleted (back-edge written per shared/evidence-model.md). ⚠ The protein/mTOR axis is answered only indirectly — DEMFOS randomised no protein intake, prescribing ~1 g/kg/day to every arm; what it shows is that adding metformin on top did not further damage muscle. ⚠ And the effort penalty is untouched — DEMFOS did not measure perceived exertion at all.

⚑ The same trial is also the map's second independent randomised null on function. Metformin added 0.1 points (−1.2 to 1.4) on a nine-task physical performance battery where the trial was powered to detect 1.8 — so the interval excludes the difference its own designers called meaningful (metformin-adds-nothing-to-lifestyle-intervention-for-physical-function). ⚠ Bounded by a missing fourth cell: with no education-plus-placebo arm, the trial can say metformin adds nothing on top of training but cannot say what metformin does in someone who is not training.

Regimen implication. Clinician-only; lives in the regimen's "discuss with your clinician" section with the evidence summarized, never scored against Benefit/Burden. Honest one-liner: strong observational/mechanistic story, but the human healthspan RCTs are null (and, in the frail, harmful) — wait for TAME. Updated 2026-07-31: the sharper version of the clinician item is now that metformin appears neither to help nor to blunt what training is doing — the best-powered trial of the two together found no difference on any of seventeen outcomes. What is left of the older concern is the subjective one: a smaller, blinded trial found the same workout felt up to two Borg units harder on the drug, and no larger trial has measured perceived exertion since. A drug that changes nothing objectively can still change whether the exercise happens.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-07 — Ingested Witham 2025 / MET-PREVENT (Lancet Healthy Longevity, RCT, n=72, mean age 80): metformin 500 mg TID × 4 mo null on 4-m walk speed (p=0.96) and more adverse events / hospitalizations (34% vs 8%). Coins the map at contested — RCT-null-with-harm against an observational longevity narrative; TAME unreported.

  • 2026-07-30 — The map's oldest un-quantified flag gets its first randomised test, on one of its three axes. Status held contested (Methnani 2026, Biomolecules 16(7):971, randomised placebo-controlled TRIPLE-BLIND trial, PACTR202401506460143, IRB CEFMS 193/2023; 24 randomised → 22 completers, 11 per arm, per protocol; metformin-naïve, physically inactive adults with metabolic syndrome plus prediabetes or controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA₁c ≤ 7.5%), mean age 43 / 47, BMI 37–38 kg/m², Sousse, Tunisia; metformin 500 mg twice daily (1000 mg/day) vs identical 500 mg starch placebo, both arms on the same supervised aerobic programme — 5 sessions/week × 5 weeks at 60% VO₂peak, 500 kcal/session; allocation sequence generated and held by an independent hospital pharmacist, variable permuted blocks, sealed sequentially-numbered opaque envelopes, participants, investigators, supervisors, assessors AND data analysts all blinded until primary analyses were complete; adherence verified at 94.7% / 95.1% of sessions and 90.6% / 92.6% of tablets; no external funding, no competing interests; CC BY OA PDF archived via MDPI's attachment host after the publisher edge returned 403 to both curl and the browser rung). Absolute VO₂peak: group × time interaction p = 0.042, η²p = 0.191 — placebo +0.26 L/min (95% CI 0.16 to 0.37), metformin +0.106 L/min (−0.0004 to 0.212), p = 0.051 (metformin-attenuates-the-vo2peak-gain-from-aerobic-training). ⚠ And here is exactly how far that goes. Scaled to body weight the interaction is NOT significant (p = 0.088), the metformin arm's own interval touches zero, and the direct between-group comparison at the end of the trial is null on both measures (p = 0.159, p = 0.225) (metformin-exercise-attenuation-rests-on-an-interaction-not-a-group-difference). Both arms improved; the claim is about rates of change in a trial too small to separate the groups. ⚑ The trial's largest and most consistent finding is the one this map was NOT flagging: perceived effort. On the Borg 6–20 scale the metformin arm rose +2.0 units at 60% of maximal aerobic power (1.6 to 2.4), +1.5 at 50%, +1.1 at 40%, while the placebo arm did not change at any intensity; between-arm differences at the end were significant at 40%, 50% and 60% (p = 0.004, 0.0006, < 0.0001), three-way interaction p < 0.0001, η²p = 0.288 — a larger effect size than the fitness interaction, and on a self-reported outcome that was fully blinded, which is rare in this corpus (metformin-raises-perceived-effort-at-the-same-exercise-intensity). ⚑ Adaptation was redirected, not removed. Maximal fat oxidation rose +0.127 g/min (0.093 to 0.161) on metformin versus +0.040 (0.006 to 0.074) on placebo (interaction p = 0.001, η²p = 0.413), fat oxidation rose at every submaximal intensity in the drug arm and none in placebo, and the gain was uncoupled from fitness (fitness/fat-oxidation correlation r = 0.612, p = 0.045 in placebo; r = 0.442, p = 0.173 on metformin) — the only contrast in the paper where the arms separate directly (0.067 g/min, 0.007 to 0.127, p = 0.031) (metformin-shifts-training-adaptation-toward-fat-oxidation). ⚠ Recorded as open-question, not a benefit: intermittent-fasting already holds a case where a real fat-oxidation gain was swamped by compensatory intake (fat-oxidation-gain-is-dwarfed-by-compensatory-intake), and this trial measured no food intake, no body composition and no weight. ⚠ Recorded and refused as a finding: in the metformin arm only, older participants gained less fitness (age vs ΔVO₂peak r = −0.775 absolute, r = −0.865 scaled; r = −0.317 / −0.025 in placebo), with Cook's-distance and Spearman robustness checks done properly. Eleven data points. Provocative for a drug whose longevity indication targets people over 65 — the question TAME exists to answer — and a hypothesis, not evidence. ✅ Why mixed and not unreliable, decided by rule. Fatal flag #4 (per-protocol concealing non-random dropout) does not fire — 2 of 24, both reasons published, both outcome-unrelated. Flag #3 does not fire — no null is being sold. Flag #2 does not fire on the concealment test: every outcome family including the flat ones is reported, Bonferroni applied within families, and the authors state in their own Methods that the trial was never powered for the interaction their conclusions rest on and that five weeks is shorter than the 8–12 weeks conventionally required for stable cardiorespiratory adaptation. Capped suggestive. extends; Status contested HELD — a suggestive-capped source cannot move a map (shared/evidence-model.md), and this extends the map's existing null-to-harmful human reading rather than reversing it. TAME is still the result that could move this map either way.

  • 2026-07-31 — The three-axis exercise-interaction flag closes, as a null — and the map takes its second independent randomised null on physical function. Status held contested (Nava 2026, DEMFOS, Lancet Healthy Longevity online-first 100883, randomised placebo-controlled ASSESSOR-MASKED trial, NCT04221750, IRB H-46970, independent DSMB, CONSORT; 114 randomised 1:1:1 → 101 (89%) completing, analysed INTENTION-TO-TREAT with a 50-draw multiple-imputation sensitivity analysis that agreed; community-dwelling adults aged 65–85, mean 72.8, BMI ≥ 30 (mean ~36), mild-to-moderate frailty (physical performance test 18–31), sedentary, non-diabetic and metformin-naive by exclusion, mean 2.7 chronic conditions, 89% men, Michael E DeBakey VA / Baylor, Houston; arms = intensive lifestyle + metformin, intensive lifestyle + placebo, monthly diet education + metformin; metformin extended-release titrated 500 → 1000 → toward 1500 mg/day as tolerated vs matching placebo; the lifestyle programme was a 500–750 kcal/day deficit at ~1 g/kg/day protein plus three supervised 90-min sessions/week — 15 min flexibility, 30 min aerobic at 70–85% peak HR, 30 min progressive resistance training to ~85% 1RM, 15 min balance; randomisation by BMI-stratified block permutation prepared by the biostatistician and implemented by a research pharmacist with no participant contact; adherence 92% diet / 85% exercise sessions, 100% median pill count in BOTH drug arms; Bonferroni across the two primary contrasts and Benjamini–Hochberg within each secondary domain; funded by the US Department of Veterans Affairs, no pharmaceutical sponsor, funder had no role in design, analysis or writing; OA PDF archived from the publisher's own site after the DOI's Elsevier redirect proved captcha-walled). Primary outcome — modified physical performance test, metformin vs placebo inside the identical programme: +0.1 points (95% CI −1.2 to 1.4), p = 0.92, against a trial prespecified to detect 1.8 points at 80% power. ✅ The entire interval lies inside the margin its designers declared meaningful, which makes this a demonstration of no worthwhile effect rather than an inconclusive null (metformin-adds-nothing-to-lifestyle-intervention-for-physical-function). ⚑ And the AMPK-blunting flag closes on all three axes, flat. VO₂peak +0.1 mL/kg/min (−1.5 to 1.6) and +0.01 L/min (−0.13 to 0.16); knee extensor peak torque +1.2 N·m (−5.4 to 7.8); peak power −0.6 W (−11.0 to 9.8); MRI thigh muscle volume −12 cm³ (−69 to 45); DXA lean mass −0.3 kg (−1.1 to 0.6); 6-minute walk +1.0 m (−30.0 to 32.1); gait speed −0.02 m/s (−0.11 to 0.06). Seventeen outcomes, no separation (metformin-does-not-blunt-training-adaptation-in-older-adults). ✅ Why the flat line is readable: a positive control sits inside the same experiment. Against the non-training arm, the same instruments moved decisively — VO₂peak +3.4 mL/kg/min (2.0 to 4.9), knee torque +12.2 N·m (5.8 to 18.5), 6-minute walk +44.5 m (14.2 to 74.7), physical function +2.5 points (1.2 to 3.8) (intensive-lifestyle-intervention-improves-physical-function-in-older-adults-with-obesity). ⚠ This CONTRADICTS the map's own 2026-07-30 entry and the vault records it as a rebuttal, not an average. Methnani reported metformin attenuating the VO₂peak gain (+0.106 vs +0.26 L/min); DEMFOS reports +0.14 vs +0.13 L/min on the same comparison. DEMFOS is larger (114 vs 22), longer (6 months vs 5 weeks, and Methnani's own authors conceded 5 weeks is short of the 8–12 needed for fitness to settle), ITT vs per protocol, and powered for a between-group difference the smaller trial was explicitly not powered for. A Rebutted by back-edge is written onto metformin-attenuates-the-vo2peak-gain-from-aerobic-training per shared/evidence-model.md. ⚠ What does NOT close: the effort penalty. Methnani's largest finding — up to 2 Borg units of extra perceived effort at the same workload, fully blinded — was not measured by DEMFOS at all (metformin-raises-perceived-effort-at-the-same-exercise-intensity). The only adjacent signal is indirect: 85% median exercise attendance in both drug arms over six months. ⚠ Bounded by a missing fourth cell. There is no education-plus-placebo arm, so DEMFOS prices metformin on top of training and cannot price it in someone who is not training. Also 89% men, one centre, six months, diabetes excluded. ✅ Appraised reliable — the vault's cleanest example of an adequately powered null. Fatal flag #3 (an underpowered null sold as "no effect") is the one this design was built to avoid: power was computed in advance for a stated minimally meaningful difference, the analysis was ITT with imputation backup, adherence was verified equal, and the interval excludes that difference. Flags #2 and #4 do not fire. confirms on the map's null-to-harmful human reading; Status contested HELD — the pro-longevity case was never built on function, and TAME remains the unreported result that could move this map either way. ⚠ A reviewer could reasonably push for fading here: this is the third randomised null in three populations. Logged as a decision in docs/backlog.md.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-31, AS A NULL — the exercise interaction, on all three axes. DEMFOS ran metformin against placebo on top of a six-month supervised programme containing 30 minutes of progressive resistance training three times a week and measured muscle three separate ways — MRI thigh volume, DXA lean mass, and isokinetic knee extensor torque and power. All flat, along with VO₂peak, walking distance and gait speed (metformin-does-not-blunt-training-adaptation-in-older-adults). The hypertrophy axis that the entry below called "entirely unquantified" is now quantified at zero, and the cardiorespiratory axis is answered against the smaller trial that had suggested attenuation. The interaction flag is retained on safety_flags for the drug's prescription status and its documented effect on perceived effort, not for adaptation-blunting — which the best available randomised evidence does not support. Noted identically on resistance-training and protein-target. ⚠ The protein/mTOR axis is answered only indirectly: DEMFOS randomised no protein intake and prescribed ~1 g/kg/day to every arm, so what it shows is that metformin on top did not make muscle outcomes worse — not that protein needs are unchanged on the drug. ⚠ And the trial has no non-training arm, so none of this prices metformin for a sedentary person.
  • ⚠ Interaction with the exercise levers — AMPK antagonism of training adaptation. SUPERSEDED by the entry above; retained as the history. Partially quantified 2026-07-30, on one axis of three. Metformin activates AMPK, which can blunt the muscle-hypertrophy and cardiorespiratory adaptations that resistance-training and zone-2-cardio are built on (and can crowd the protein-target anabolic response). For someone whose foundation is exercise, this is a real trade-off. Flagged in both directions (safety_flags: [interaction]). The cardiorespiratory axis now has a randomised number and it is modest and internally contested — interaction p = 0.042 on absolute VO₂peak, p = 0.088 scaled to body weight, both direct between-group comparisons null, in 11 people per arm over 5 weeks (metformin-attenuates-the-vo2peak-gain-from-aerobic-training, metformin-exercise-attenuation-rests-on-an-interaction-not-a-group-difference). The mechanism is real and the magnitude is smaller than the flag implied. ⚠ The muscle-hypertrophy axis and the protein/mTOR anabolic axis remain entirely unquantified — Methnani tested aerobic exercise only, with no strength, muscle-mass, fibre or protein measurement and no resistance-training arm. Those two halves of the flag stay open, and a future run must not close them on this citation. Noted identically on resistance-training and protein-target.
  • ⚠ NEW AXIS 2026-07-30 — the interaction's sharpest edge is adherence, not adaptation. Metformin raised perceived effort by up to 2 Borg units at the same relative workload while placebo moved not at all, with a larger effect size than the fitness attenuation and on a fully blinded self-report (metformin-raises-perceived-effort-at-the-same-exercise-intensity). GOAL.md makes adherence an explicit constraint — "an intervention he won't stick to scores as if it doesn't work" — so a drug that makes the foundation lever feel harder is levying a cost this atlas is built to notice. ⚠ Bounded: session adherence was identical between arms (94.7% vs 95.1%) across five supervised weeks. The effort penalty is measured; the dropout it might cause is not, and the vault does not assert it. Cross-linked to blood-flow-restriction (bfr-occlusion-reduces-walking-volume-and-comfort) as the vault's other case of an intervention whose real cost is discomfort during the exercise rather than a worse outcome after it.
  • ⚑ 2026-07-30 — the fat-oxidation gain is logged as an open question, on precedent rather than reflex. Metformin roughly tripled the training-induced rise in maximal fat oxidation and uncoupled it from fitness gains (metformin-shifts-training-adaptation-toward-fat-oxidation). This vault has already watched that exact surrogate fail to reach 24-hour energy balance in intermittent-fasting (fat-oxidation-gain-is-dwarfed-by-compensatory-intake), and Methnani measured no intake, no body composition and no weight. No safety_flags added for it — it is neither a harm nor a demonstrated benefit. Noted on both maps.
  • ⚑ 2026-07-30 — global ripple across all 43 maps: one new interaction edge, and it runs to the drug David is actually weighing. Metformin is frequently co-prescribed alongside GLP-1 agonists in clinical practice, and glp-1's own muscle-preservation countermeasure is exercise plus hitting the protein target (glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude, glp1-intake-reduction-makes-the-protein-target-harder-to-hit). So a drug that makes the same training session feel harder sits directly on top of a drug whose known cost is lean mass and whose fix is training. ⚠ Recorded as a clinician-conversation item and NOT as a graded transfer: no trial has tested metformin and a GLP-1 together against exercise adaptation, Methnani's participants were on no glucose-lowering co-therapy by design (metformin-naïve, no other agents), and both drugs are prescription-only and never ranked here. Flagged on both maps. No other map gained an edge: the sleep, supplement, immersion and measurement maps are untouched by an aerobic-training drug trial.
  • Coherence with glp-1 and rapamycin. Three clinician-only pharmacologic levers held to the same "never ranked, evidence summarized for the clinician" discipline; metformin is sometimes co-used with GLP-1 clinically (a clinician-conversation item, not a co-recommendation here).
  • Global-ripple: no interaction with the sleep or supplement maps.
  • Open gap: TAME (the population-appropriate aging-endpoint RCT) is un-ingested because unreported — it is the single result that could move this map either way; ingest when it publishes. Established metformin pharmacology (B12 depletion; rare lactic acidosis) is logged here, not fabricated into a graded flag. Narrowed and re-pointed 2026-07-30: the exercise-interaction gap is now partly closed on the aerobic axis and still fully open on hypertrophy and the protein anabolic response. What would settle the aerobic axis is the trial Methnani's own authors ask for — adequately powered for a group × time interaction, run for 8–12 weeks or longer so cardiorespiratory adaptation can stabilise, and in a population taking metformin for the reason this map cares about rather than for metabolic syndrome. None exists.
