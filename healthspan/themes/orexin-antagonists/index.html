Dual orexin receptor antagonists (suvorexant / lemborexant / daridorexant) — sleep

Status: emerging (2026-08-18) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's second prescription/pharmacologic map (after glp-1) and its first sleep medication — every other sleep entry here is a behaviour (cbt-insomnia, sleep-regularity, exercise-and-sleep) or a supplement (melatonin, l-theanine, chamomile, magnesium-glycinate). Coined from a single prospectively-registered meta-analysis of six randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, n = 3,546, at the duration nobody had synthesised: six and twelve months. Prescription/injectable-class rule applies in full — by shared/safety.md this is never ranked in the daily protocol and no dose is set here. git is the undo.

What we believe now

Blocking the orexin wake signal reliably adds about twenty minutes of self-reported sleep. Whether it gives you a better next day is the least-settled question in its own evidence, and its reputation for being well tolerated turns out to be a six-month reputation.

⚑ What the class does, precisely. Orexin is a wake-promoting neuropeptide; a dual orexin receptor antagonist blocks both of its receptors. It does not sedate the way a benzodiazepine or Z-drug does — it removes a wakefulness signal. Three agents are FDA-approved: suvorexant (2014), lemborexant (2019), daridorexant (2022).

At six months, versus placebo: subjective total sleep time +19.87 min (95% CI 14.15–25.60, I² = 0%), time to fall asleep −12.69 min (−19.78 to −5.60), sleep efficiency +4.61 percentage points (3.85–5.37); wake after sleep onset null (orexin-antagonists-add-about-twenty-minutes-of-self-reported-sleep). These are tight, mutually consistent estimates of a modest effect. At twelve months total sleep time holds at +16.38 min — with a lower bound of 0.08 minutes and heterogeneity rising to I² = 49%.

⚑ Now read the same paper in the order a patient cares about, and it inverts. Nobody treats insomnia to improve a sleep-diary line; they treat it to get the next day back. The instrument for that is the IDSIQ, and it is the class's worst result: null at six months, −5.51 (−13.25 to 2.22), clearing significance only at twelve months and barely — −6.37 (−12.31 to −0.43) (orexin-antagonist-daytime-function-benefit-is-the-weakest-result-in-its-own-evidence). The best-measured effect is on what matters least; the least-certain effect is on what matters most.

⚑ And an adjacent number makes that uncomfortable rather than merely disappointing. Somnolence ran at RR 5.96 (3.19–11.11) at six months and 4.85 (2.52–9.33) at twelve — five- to six-fold placebo, without attenuating. A drug that suppresses a wake signal at night is still suppressing some of it in the morning. Whether the daytime null exists because the extra twenty minutes is repaid as next-day grogginess is untested here. It is the obvious hypothesis and the numbers sit in adjacent tables.

⚑ The safety finding is what the review exists to produce, and it reverses with duration. Any adverse event: RR 0.98 (0.90–1.08) at six months — near-equivalence — and RR 1.09 (1.06–1.13) at twelve months, significant, I² = 0%. Daridorexant's serious adverse events at twelve months: RR 2.78 (1.76–4.39) (orexin-antagonist-adverse-events-appear-only-in-the-second-six-months). ⚠ From one trial, with the per-dose analyses non-significant — one drug, one trial, is not a class conclusion. But the shape is the point: the tolerability profile this class is known for is a six-month profile, and this is the first synthesis that could see past it.

⚠ The four structural limits, all of which cap the map at emerging regardless of tier. (1) Every efficacy outcome is patient-reported. No polysomnography, no actigraphy, anywhere in the pool — the outcome names are literally "subjective total sleep time," "subjective sleep efficiency." (2) All six trials are manufacturer trials (Merck, Eisai, Idorsia). Randomised, blinded and placebo-controlled, so not disqualifying — but there is no independent replication in this literature at all. (3) No trial anywhere used an active comparator — the review's own words: "None directly compared different insomnia drugs, as all evaluated DORAs versus placebo." (4) Most estimates rest on one or two trials; sleep efficiency, WASO and IDSIQ at six months each come from a single trial, and the GRADE certainty table is in unretrieved supplementary material.

⚑ The comparison this vault most wants does not exist, and its absence is itself a finding. Guidelines rank CBT-I first-line for chronic insomnia, ahead of hypnotics (cbt-i-first-line-sleep-guideline-consensus), and this vault holds evidence that CBT-I's benefit is still measurable after the treatment stops — at 3, 6 and 12 months post-treatment (cbt-i-durable-depression-benefit) and on insomnia severity at eight (cbt-i-controlled-insomnia-severity-effect-holds-at-eight-months). A DORA works while you take it and stops when you stop; a course of CBT-I is a fixed cost with a residual. ⚠ No head-to-head trial exists, so this is a comparison of shapes and not of magnitudes — and cbt-insomnia's own top open gap, that its sleep efficacy is still unsourced in this vault, is what would make the comparison real.

⚑ Why a map rather than a filing under cbt-insomnia. The vault's four-part coining bar scores this two passes and two partials: isolating contrast ✅ (randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled — the leg nearly every exercise source here fails structurally); pre-specified dose ✅ (FDA-approved doses, analysed per-dose: suvorexant 20/40 mg, lemborexant 5/10 mg, daridorexant 10/25/50 mg); low risk of bias ⚠ partial (RoB 2 applied, most trials low risk, two high risk for missing outcome data, one with reporting concerns); machine-measured outcome ❌ fail (entirely subjective). ⚑ The deciding argument is structural, not the score. Filing a prescription drug class inside the behavioural-therapy map would repeat the drawer-drift problem this vault flagged on anthocyanins when two chemically foreign refusals were shelved there. A prescription hypnotic class is a distinct lever with distinct rules, it is the alternative CBT-I is ranked against, and glp-1 is the precedent for giving a prescription class its own map. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

He carries no insomnia diagnosis, and this entire literature is in adults with diagnosed primary insomnia and no psychiatric comorbidity. These are prescription-only drugs; by rule they are never ranked into the daily protocol (shared/safety.md) — the same rule that confines the compounded GLP-1 to a clinician-only section. No dose, agent or duration is set here.

⚑ What is worth carrying if it ever comes up with a clinician is the ordering, not any number. The non-drug option in this vault has the better shape — guideline first-line, benefit persisting after it ends, no somnolence signal — and the drug class offers twenty questionnaire-measured minutes, a daytime-function result that took a year to clear zero, and an adverse-event profile that worsens in the second half of that year. ⚠ Neither observation is a recommendation, and no trial has put them head to head.
