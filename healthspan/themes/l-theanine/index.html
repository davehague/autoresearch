L-theanine — supplements

Status: emerging (2026-07-10) — unchanged, but the map's shape changed this run. The highest-tier L-theanine evidence in existence (31 RCTs, Molecular Psychiatry) never pools a sleep outcome, finds the relaxation mechanism the sleep claim borrows to be weak and bias-driven, and instead lands a clean acute daytime attention effect. Nothing refutes the subjective-sleep pooling; nothing corroborates it either. Low burden, well tolerated, and now best-evidenced as a daytime tool, not a bedtime one.

What we believe now

L-theanine (an amino acid from tea; reported ranges 200–450 mg/day chronic, 200 mg acute) has two threads pointing in opposite directions, and the vault must carry both:

(1) Sleep — the map's original claim, still emerging and now un-corroborated. L-theanine probably produces a small-to-moderate improvement in subjective sleep quality — onset latency, daytime dysfunction, overall self-reported quality. The anchor is Bulman 2025, a pooled meta-analysis of RCTs (high tier), with two limits: no pooled objective (PSG) outcomes, and the caveat that few trials used pure L-theanine (many co-formulated). A 2025 systematic review (Cotter) claims benefit on objective as well as subjective measures at 200–450 mg/day — which would address the objective-outcome gap, except it is heavily discounted: it is a narrative review (no pooled estimate) that includes uncontrolled open-label trials, and it carries a severe commercial conflict of interest — the authors are employees of a company (a British American Tobacco subsidiary) that sells L-theanine products. Per the provenance/bias rail we flag it, not launder it. Net: the objective-measures question is raised, not resolved.

(2) Daytime cognition — the new, better-evidenced thread (2026-07-10). A meta-analysis of 31 RCTs (n=1,168) in Molecular Psychiatry finds a single 200 mg dose, 30–60 min before a task, improves choice reaction time — SMD 0.51 (0.25–0.77) (l-theanine-acute-attention-benefit). This is the paper's cleanest, most precisely estimated result.

The tension between them is the point. That same meta-analysis tested the mechanism the sleep claim assumes — that L-theanine relaxes you. Its pre-specified primary outcome (acute stress in healthy adults) came back modest, SMD 0.31, and "largely influenced by studies with a high risk of bias" by the authors' own account (l-theanine-stress-benefit-driven-by-biased-trials). Fatigue: null. Anxiety: inconsistent and non-significant, except one trial in psychotic anxiety (l-theanine-null-on-fatigue-and-anxiety). A single-dose antidepressant signal (SMD 0.69) appears only after deleting one outlier and is recorded as an open question, not a benefit (l-theanine-single-dose-mood-signal-fragile).

So the compound whose vault entry exists because it might help you sleep has its strongest evidence for making you more alert, and its weakest evidence for the calming property that motivated the sleep hypothesis in the first place. Note the direction of the conflict of interest: the senior author of the new meta-analysis founded a dietary-supplement company — and reported that his own primary endpoint was bias-driven. That is reporting against interest, which strengthens rather than weakens the pushback.

Why emerging, not higher, and not lower: the evidence tier is high (two meta-analyses of RCTs) but every sleep endpoint is subjective, the objective-sleep question is contaminated by a seller-authored review, the new stress finding is bias-driven, and the one robust effect (attention) is a laboratory surrogate with no healthspan meaning. Both pooled syntheses carry commercial conflicts. Nothing here justifies promotion; the pushes-back findings undercut a rationale, not a result, so nothing justifies demotion either.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-04 — Ingested Bulman 2025 (systematic review & meta-analysis, 18 RCTs pooled, N=897): significant gains in subjective sleep onset latency (SMD 0.15), daytime dysfunction (SMD 0.33), overall sleep quality (SMD 0.43). Opens the map at emerging (subjective-only outcomes; pure-compound confound).

  • 2026-07-05 — Ingested Cotter 2025 (narrative systematic review, 13 trials n=550, incl. 2 open-label): reports benefit on objective + subjective sleep measures at 200–450 mg/day — but severe seller conflict of interest + no pooled estimate + uncontrolled trials. Recorded as a conflicted, low-confidence extension; does not move Status.

  • 2026-07-10 — Ingested Gerolymos 2026 (Molecular Psychiatry, systematic review + meta-analysis, 31 RCTs, n=1,168, oral L-theanine vs placebo): a single 200 mg dose 30–60 min pre-task improved choice reaction time, SMD 0.51 (0.25–0.77); the pre-specified primary outcome — acute stress in healthy adults — was modest (SMD 0.31) and "largely influenced by studies with a high risk of bias"; fatigue null; anxiety inconsistent/non-significant (one psychotic-anxiety trial excepted); a single-dose depressive-symptom effect (SMD 0.69) only after excluding one outlier; no serious adverse events. The highest-tier L-theanine synthesis to date, and it pools no sleep endpoint at all. extends on cognition, pushes-back on the relaxation mechanism; Status unchanged (emerging). Senior author founded a supplement-selling company — disclosed, and the pushback runs against that interest. Capped suggestive; the mood signal capped further to single-study / open-question.

  • 2026-07-19 — L-theanine gets pooled into a "dietary supplements" node and comes out unattributable; Status held emerging (Mei 2025, Nutrients 17(24):3952, systematic review + meta-analysis, 28 RCTs, PROSPERO CRD420250656302, GRADEpro in duplicate, publicly funded, no conflicts; OA PDF archived). The analysis pools tryptophan, L-theanine, GABA, B vitamins, vitamin D, zinc, magnesium, potassium, omega-3, ashwagandha, crocetin and ergothioneine into a single estimate — substances sharing no mechanism, no target and no dose logic — and reports PSQI MD −0.70 (−1.37 to −0.03) (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold). The pooled quantity answers "does taking something help?", which is not a decision anyone makes, while the authors' conclusion performs the per-substance disaggregation the design forbids, crediting "tryptophan, vitamin D, omega-3, zinc and antioxidants" (supplement-sleep-benefit-not-attributable-to-any-supplement). Sixth attribution failure in the vault's series — after creatine+beta-alanine, protein+probiotic, exercise+CBT, unstable-surface RT and hypoxia+HIIT — and the worst of them, because the prior five bundled interventions that at least co-occur in practice; this bundles substances nobody takes as a set. For this map specifically, two things and neither moves it. L-theanine is not among the named drivers — conspicuous, but absence from a narrative sentence is not a subgroup null. And the amino-acid subgroup that contains L-theanine and GABA carries I² = 85%, the worst internal consistency in the paper, so even the subgroup that exists is uninterpretable. The map's existing shape is untouched: its highest-tier synthesis (Gerolymos 2026, 31 RCTs, Molecular Psychiatry) still pools no sleep endpoint at all, and L-theanine still ranks last within the wind-down cluster on the sedating property the cluster is grouped around. What this adds is a measured ceiling on the whole category L-theanine sits in — trials of 7–50 people, funnel asymmetry detected, an effect whose entire interval sits below the PSQI MID. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive. Best-evidenced as a daytime tool; still nothing to buy for sleep.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Sleep-supplement stack with magnesium-glycinate and chamomile — three low-burden, mostly subjective-evidence sleep levers. At regimen time treat them as candidates within one sleep-support cluster, not three independent wins; none is a proven foundation on the order of sleep-regularity.
  • Pairing with caffeine-timing — now the map's best-supported use (strengthened 2026-07-10). The well-known L-theanine + caffeine combination is a daytime cognition/alertness pairing; it does not change the evening caffeine-cutoff guidance for sleep — if anything, taking caffeine (even with L-theanine) late still undermines sleep-regularity. Gerolymos 2026 puts a number on the L-theanine half of that pairing (single 200 mg, 30–60 min pre-task, choice reaction time SMD 0.51, l-theanine-acute-attention-benefit) and locates it squarely in the daytime. Noted in both maps.
  • ⚠ Wind-down cluster widened (2026-07-07), and L-theanine now ranks LAST within it (2026-07-10). The stress/sleep levers overlap this one in use-case; treat the whole set (l-theanine, magnesium-glycinate, chamomile, ashwagandha, melatonin) as one wind-down lever at regimen time. Stacking several is redundant sedation, not additive benefit — ashwagandha carries the cluster's safety_flags: [interaction] for exactly this reason. New ordering information: the cluster is grouped around a sedating/anxiolytic property, and L-theanine now has the weakest evidence for that property of any member — its stress effect is bias-driven, its anxiety effect inconsistent, its fatigue effect null (l-theanine-stress-benefit-driven-by-biased-trials, l-theanine-null-on-fatigue-and-anxiety) — while ashwagandha at least holds a semi-objective cortisol drop (ashwagandha-lowers-cortisol). If one wind-down agent is ever trialed, the evidence does not nominate this one.
  • A shared oddity with ashwagandha (2026-07-10). Both wind-down-cluster botanicals have their best-evidenced effect during the day — L-theanine on acute attention, ashwagandha on aerobic endurance (ashwagandha-aerobic-endurance-performance) — and their weakest on the evening property the cluster is named for. A coherence observation, not a safety edge.
  • ⚠ The sleep job this cluster exists to do now has a randomised, drug-free answer (new 2026-07-12). cbt-insomnia (53 RCTs, n=13,608, benefit persisting 12 months post-treatment — cbt-i-durable-depression-benefit) is the guideline-grade intervention for insomnia. This map's own highest-tier source pools no sleep endpoint at all (l-theanine-stress-benefit-driven-by-biased-trials). The contrast is worth stating plainly: the supplement the vault tracks because it might help you sleep has its best evidence for daytime attention, while the intervention nobody was tracking has 53 randomised trials and a benefit that survives stopping. No Status change here — CBT-I's evidence is in insomnia patients, on depression — but the ranking within the wind-down cluster is now clear.
  • Global-ripple: no safety interaction with other vault interventions found. (L-theanine may be mildly blood-pressure-lowering and additive with sedatives — plausible but no vault-quality source cited; not a vault-internal interaction, so not flagged here.)
