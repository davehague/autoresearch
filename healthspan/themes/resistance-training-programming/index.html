Resistance-training programming — how heavy, how hard, on what surface

Status: emerging (2026-07-18) — auto-forked this run from resistance-training, flagged for human review. The parent map answers whether and how much to lift (mortality, ~90–119 min/week). This child owns the separate question of how to lift: the load, effort and stability axes, all measured on strength and function surrogates, none of them touching the parent's mortality claim.

This map carries safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] and owns the knee gate. The parent restates the gate at full strength — nothing about the fork weakens it — but the evidence behind it lives here.

What we believe now

Keep three axes straight. They are routinely conflated and they are not the same question.

⚑ 2026-08-08 — the load axis acquires a boundary (Jin-Hao 2026, 19 RCTs, n=1,267, sarcopenia). In a sarcopenic population, moderate-intensity resistance training ranked first for handgrip (MD 2.37 kg, 0.76–3.98) — not heavy. This is not a reversal of the Load row. Handgrip is not a trained lift; no programme loads it directly, so a load–response gradient measured in leg press does not transfer to it by default, and the populations differ. What it establishes: "heavier builds more lower-limb strength" has not been shown to govern grip, and has never been tested in sarcopenia. In the same network, nothing beat control on gait speed — the axis table's four questions are all answered on strength surrogates, and the functional outcome remains unmoved.

(1) Heavy loading builds more lower-limb strength — and the same paper's safety comparison cannot say it is safe. Shen 2026 found high-intensity RT (≥70% 1RM) beat low-to-moderate for leg press SMD 0.95 (0.48–1.43) and leg extension 0.63 (0.09–1.17) (high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength). But it also reports fall incidence RR 2.68 (95% CI 0.65–11.11) and adverse events RR 2.42 (0.66–8.88) — both point estimates above 2, both intervals reaching past an elevenfold increase — and concludes "similar safety profiles." shared/study-appraisal.md names that a fatal reading error (an underpowered null sold as "no effect"; rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered). Heavy lifting builds more strength is supported. Heavy lifting is safe for this knee is not, and this evidence cannot say so.

(2) You do not have to train to failure. Non-failure beat failure on dynamic strength, SMD 0.24 (0.06–0.42), 19 trials, n=461 (rt-non-failure-beats-failure-dynamic-strength) — small, and pointing the convenient direction. But the paper's four other outcomes are underpowered nulls it launders into equivalence, and it convicts itself doing so: it reports I² = 0% on isometric strength, hypertrophy and power, meaning the trials agree almost perfectly and nothing but sample size is widening those intervals, and its own Limitations concede the studies "may have limited their statistical power" (rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered).

(3) Unstable surfaces buy confidence, not capability (new 2026-07-18). Doan 2026 found unstable resistance training showed no significant advantage over stable on lower-limb strength or short-term mobility — with Five-Times Sit-to-Stand actually trending toward stable training (unstable-vs-stable-rt-strength-mobility-null-underpowered). What it did improve was fear of falling (FES-I) and 24-week Timed Up and Go. Fear of falling is a self-reported confidence questionnaire in an unblindable balance intervention — close to the most expectation-prone result this design can produce — and no fall incidence, injury count, or adverse event appears anywhere in the paper (unstable-rt-reduces-fear-of-falling-not-falls).

⚠ THE PATTERN THIS MAP EXISTS TO CARRY: five underpowered nulls, five conclusions written as equivalence. Shen's falls null (RR 2.68, CI to 11.11 — "similar safety profiles"). Shen's bone null (lumbar SMD 0.28, CI −0.02 to 0.58 — "no difference"). Wu's four adaptation nulls at I²=0% ("as effective… for most neuromuscular adaptations"). Chen's BFR-substitute null on 4 trials/n=123. Doan's strength/mobility null on 6 trials. The direction of the error is not random: each time, the null flattered the authors' recommendation. Two of the five also flattered GOAL.md's least-work philosophy — and the vault refused those too, which is the only condition under which the lens means anything. The most reliable finding about this literature is that it is chronically too small to answer its own secondary questions. Escalated in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

⚑ 2026-08-01 — THE PATTERN TAKES ITS SEVENTH ENTRY AND ITS FIRST GENUINE EXCEPTION, AND THE MAP HAS TO SPLIT THE TWO APART. Camargo 2026 is a null on the volume axis — and it is the first null on this map that is not underpowered for its own stated target. It carries a prospective simulation-based power analysis (10,000 simulations, 80% power, r = 0.78 between limbs, a deliberately conservative d = 0.50 → f = 0.20, minimum n = 24) and it recruited 25. Add pre-registration (REBEC RBR-2f836zh), a CONSORT flow from 138 screened, 98.5% adherence, verified dose separation, blinded ultrasound assessors, standardised post-exercise protein, public funding and no conflicts — and shared/study-appraisal.md gives it reliable, the first source on this map to earn it. The complaint above was always about power, so when power arrives the grade has to move. ⚠ And the seventh entry is still there, because the title says "do not impair muscle hypertrophy" while the paper's own NEW & NOTEWORTHY block says "no evidence… under the present experimental conditions." Seven for seven, the equivalence claim lands in the line that gets cited. So the pathology is now demonstrably a WRITING-AND-INCENTIVES problem, not a statistics problem — a registered, powered, conflict-free trial committed it anyway. That is the sharper version of what this box has been saying, and it took a good trial to establish it (within-subject-unilateral-design-strengthens-a-training-null-without-rescuing-it).

(4) Bone does not care how heavy you lift, as far as anyone can detect. No significant BMD difference between intensities — lumbar spine SMD 0.28 (−0.02 to 0.58), femoral neck 0.13 (−0.08 to 0.33) — though the lumbar interval misses significance by two hundredths and is underpowered, not equivalent (rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd). Read alongside creatine-bone-bmd-null, the vault has now twice failed to find a lever that moves bone density in a training adult. Do not choose a load, or a pill, for your skeleton.

Why emerging, not higher: every finding here is a strength or function surrogate, in populations ranging from healthy young adults to osteoporotic elders, on trial bases of 6–20 studies with chronic power problems. A higher-tier study of a different outcome does not consolidate anything (shared/evidence-model.md), and none of this touches the parent map's mortality claim.

  • 2026-08-01 — ⚑ THE VOLUME AXIS OPENS, AND IT OPENS WITH THIS MAP'S FIRST reliable SOURCE. Status held emerging. Ingested Camargo 2026 (Journal of Applied Physiology 141:477–493, randomised, single-blind, within-subject unilateral RCT, n=25 resistance-trained adults aged 18–35 (18 men, 7 women), 8 weeks; REBEC RBR-2f836zh, IRB 6.642.786; FAPESP + CNPq public funding, no conflicts declared; CC BY; OA PDF archived 2.90 MB via the browser rung after a rung-1 false negative). Each participant trained both legs twice weekly — 45° leg press then leg extension, every set to momentary concentric failure in a 9–12 rep range, 2-min rest, loads adjusted set-by-set to hold the range — with one limb progressed +120% and the contralateral limb +20% above that person's habitual quadriceps volume, block-randomised stratified by limb dominance with the first-trained limb alternating across sessions. Achieved dose separation, verified and reported: 32.8 ± 5.9 vs 18.0 ± 3.3 weekly sets (P < 0.001); accumulated volume load 237,125 ± 91,078 vs 138,416 kg. ⚠ Note the gap between framing and delivery: a 6:1 progression ratio produced a 1.82× difference in sets and 1.71× in load. Result: mCSA rose over time in both limbs (F(1,72) = 61.02, P < 0.001) with NO protocol × time interaction (F(1,72) = 1.70, P = 0.197); fascicle length, pennation angle, fibre CSA, satellite cell number, myonuclear content and the anabolic/proteolytic signalling panel all null (large-volume-jumps-neither-helped-nor-hurt-hypertrophy-in-trained-lifters, suggestive; abrupt-volume-progression-produced-no-catabolic-signalling-shift, single-study). ⚑ Appraised reliable — a prospective power analysis (f = 0.20, n ≥ 24) that the trial met, plus registration, CONSORT reporting, 98.5% adherence, assessor blinding and standardised post-exercise protein (30 g whey after every session; diet recorded, protein 21.8 ± 5.7% of energy). The primary null is still capped suggestive because 80% power for f = 0.20 excludes a MODERATE difference, not a small one, and the biopsy/molecular outcomes sat outside the power calculation entirely. ⚠ Two things the paper reports and does not interpret, recorded here so nobody else has to find them. (1) A significant main effect of protocol (F(1,72) = 9.71, P = 0.002) with no interaction — VOL120 limbs larger throughout, which in 25 people randomised only by limb dominance reads as a pre-existing between-limb imbalance, not a training effect. (2) ⚠ THREE OF 28 PARTICIPANTS WITHDREW "DUE TO PERSISTENT MUSCLE OR JOINT PAIN" — ~11% attrition for musculoskeletal pain from a supervised leg-press-and-leg- extension programme taken to failure on every set, in healthy trained 18-to-35-year-olds screened to exclude musculoskeletal injury. And the within-subject design makes it unattributable by construction: each withdrawal removes both limbs at once (within-subject-unilateral-design-strengthens-a-training-null-without-rescuing-it, single-study). This is the map's most decision-relevant new fact and it is in the participant flow, not the discussion. ⚑ The hypothesis refused was not invented for the paper — prior unilateral work found +83–120% progressions grew less than +20% contralateral limbs, and Haun et al. found large squat-volume increases produced no growth at all. A randomised test did not reproduce the harm signal. extends; no safety_flags added (an unattributable within-participant withdrawal is not a graded interaction or a dose-specific harm); Status unchanged (emerging) — a surrogate at 8 weeks in trained under-35s does not touch the parent map's mortality claim.

  • 2026-08-02 — ⚑ A FALLS REVIEW ARRIVES WITH A HARD ENDPOINT, AND ITS OWN AUTHORS SHOW THAT THE BENEFIT TRACKS THE WEAKNESS OF THE CONTROL GROUP. Status held; NO exergaming map coined — the fourteenth candidate refusal. Ingested Eost-Telling 2026 (JMIR Aging 9:e89807, PRISMA systematic review + meta-analysis of 9 RCTs, N = 1,385 adults aged 60+, all settings; prospectively registered PROSPERO CRD42020214721; five databases from inception to February 2025; RoB 2.0 and GRADE, both in duplicate; publicly funded by NIHR with a no-role clause; appraised mixed, capped suggestive; gold-OA PDF archived 7.50 MB via the browser rung). ⚑ The pooled headline is IRR 0.53 (0.41–0.68) for fall rate — and the authors immediately take it apart. Their registered protocol planned to pool all active comparators together; during the review they identified "important conceptual and clinical differences between evidence-based fall prevention interventions and non–evidence-based active controls" and split them into three comparisons before final synthesis, declaring it as a protocol deviation. The split is the finding: vs usual care — fall rate IRR 0.60 (0.35–1.01), CROSSES ONE, I² = 86%, GRADE low. vs evidence-based active control (strength and balance training) — IRR 0.41 (0.27–0.62), GRADE low, but number of fallers RR 1.38 (0.62–3.06), nominally the wrong direction, from the single n = 80 trial reporting it. vs NON-evidence-based active control (a leaflet, low-intensity exercise) — IRR 0.58 (0.44–0.77), I² = 41%, GRADE MODERATE — the ONLY moderate rating anywhere in the paper (exergaming-fall-benefit-tracks-the-weakness-of-the-comparator). The impressive pooled number is partly borrowed from the weakest contrast, and the strongest evidence in the review is the comparison against a control the authors themselves classify as having no fall-prevention evidence base. ⚑ THIS IS THE VAULT'S REFERENCE CASE FOR THE COMPARATOR PROBLEM, AND IT ARRIVED FOUR DAYS AFTER THE VAULT LOGGED THAT PROBLEM AS A GAP. docs/backlog.md recorded on 2026-07-29 that comparator strength is a missing lens in shared/study-appraisal.md, after four sources in three days proved separable only by what their control arms did — most sharply traditional-chinese-sports-ranking-is-unseparable-from-its-comparator, where the paper "never characterises what any control arm was asked to do." Here the authors ran that analysis on themselves and published the result that cost them their headline. ⚠ The clinically important dissociation: fall RATE drops, the number of FALLERS mostly does not. Null at 3 months (RR 0.80, 0.40–1.63) and 6 months (RR 0.84, 0.55–1.28) vs usual care; significant only at 12 months (RR 0.75, 0.61–0.92) from one study; null at both timepoints vs the non-evidence-based control. And injurious falls — the outcome that ends independence — were measured in exactly one trial (n = 507): RR 0.81 (0.61–1.08), not significant, and not reported at all in either active-comparator comparison (exergaming-lowers-fall-rate-without-lowering-the-number-of-fallers). "Frequent fallers fell less often" and "fewer people fell" and "fewer people were hurt" are three different claims; this paper supports the first, barely supports the second, and does not reach the third. This map already holds the same proxy substitution one rung down — unstable-rt-reduces-fear-of-falling-not-falls, where unstable-surface training moved fear of falling and not falls. Concerns about falling were null here too (SMD −0.28, −1.18 to 0.61; −0.10, −0.23 to 0.02). ⚠ The abstract inflates its own certainty. It calls the 12-month fallers result "moderate-certainty evidence"; Table 4 rates that cell low (exergaming-abstract-upgrades-its-own-grade-rating). Second instance the same night, after the magnesium trial's p = 0.043/p = 0.051 discrepancy — cross-noted on magnesium-glycinate. ⚠ Conflicts, declared with unusual specificity and pointing the wrong way for a conflict. One author co-founded Dividat, an ETH Zurich exergame-platform spin-off, and advised it (no revenue in 36 months), and four authors co-wrote four of the nine included trials, mitigated by exclusion from extraction, analysis and quality assessment (exergaming-review-carries-a-platform-founder-among-its-authors). Recorded, and weighted lightly: the review's findings are unflattering to the category. ⚑ NO exergaming MAP COINED — the fourteenth refusal, and the case FOR was the strongest yet on one axis: it is the first candidate with a HARD endpoint. Beta-alanine, probiotics, collagen (×2), hypoxic training, cocoa flavanols, green exercise, whole-body vibration, traditional Chinese sports and exercise snacks were all refused on surrogates. Refused anyway on four grounds, the first two decisive: (1) against the comparator that matters — strength and balance training, which this map already recommends — every estimate is low certainty and the fallers estimate points the wrong way, so a map would assert exergaming is a lever when the evidence supports at most that it is a delivery format; (2) in several trials exergames came inside "a wider package of support" the trials never quantified — the ninth bundled-arm attribution failure; (3) nine trials, every pooled estimate on one to three of them, publication bias unassessable, follow-up mostly under the 12 months the field's own ProFaNE standard requires; (4) the platform-founder conflict. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. What would close it: a trial against Otago-standard strength-and-balance training, powered for number of fallers and injurious falls, followed twelve months. No serious adverse events in any trial. extends; Status unchanged — a suggestive-capped review whose own best-certainty result is against a leaflet does not move a map, and the lever this map already holds is the one that won.

Practising executive-function tasks and then walking faster while doing an executive-function task is transfer to a near-identical task, not a physical adaptation; the lower bound of 0.04 means two more trials could erase it. ⚑ The behaviour that makes this source trustworthy, named because it is the contrast of the night.
