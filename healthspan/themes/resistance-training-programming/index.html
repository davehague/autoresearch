Resistance-training programming — how heavy, how hard, on what surface

Status: emerging (2026-07-18) — auto-forked this run from resistance-training, flagged for human review. The parent map answers whether and how much to lift (mortality, ~90–119 min/week). This child owns the separate question of how to lift: the load, effort and stability axes, all measured on strength and function surrogates, none of them touching the parent's mortality claim.

This map carries safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] and owns the knee gate. The parent restates the gate at full strength — nothing about the fork weakens it — but the evidence behind it lives here.

What we believe now

Keep three axes straight. They are routinely conflated and they are not the same question.

(1) Heavy loading builds more lower-limb strength — and the same paper's safety comparison cannot say it is safe. Shen 2026 found high-intensity RT (≥70% 1RM) beat low-to-moderate for leg press SMD 0.95 (0.48–1.43) and leg extension 0.63 (0.09–1.17) (high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength). But it also reports fall incidence RR 2.68 (95% CI 0.65–11.11) and adverse events RR 2.42 (0.66–8.88) — both point estimates above 2, both intervals reaching past an elevenfold increase — and concludes "similar safety profiles." shared/study-appraisal.md names that a fatal reading error (an underpowered null sold as "no effect"; rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered). Heavy lifting builds more strength is supported. Heavy lifting is safe for this knee is not, and this evidence cannot say so.

(2) You do not have to train to failure. Non-failure beat failure on dynamic strength, SMD 0.24 (0.06–0.42), 19 trials, n=461 (rt-non-failure-beats-failure-dynamic-strength) — small, and pointing the convenient direction. But the paper's four other outcomes are underpowered nulls it launders into equivalence, and it convicts itself doing so: it reports I² = 0% on isometric strength, hypertrophy and power, meaning the trials agree almost perfectly and nothing but sample size is widening those intervals, and its own Limitations concede the studies "may have limited their statistical power" (rt-non-failure-nulls-hypertrophy-endurance-power-underpowered).

(3) Unstable surfaces buy confidence, not capability (new 2026-07-18). Doan 2026 found unstable resistance training showed no significant advantage over stable on lower-limb strength or short-term mobility — with Five-Times Sit-to-Stand actually trending toward stable training (unstable-vs-stable-rt-strength-mobility-null-underpowered). What it did improve was fear of falling (FES-I) and 24-week Timed Up and Go. Fear of falling is a self-reported confidence questionnaire in an unblindable balance intervention — close to the most expectation-prone result this design can produce — and no fall incidence, injury count, or adverse event appears anywhere in the paper (unstable-rt-reduces-fear-of-falling-not-falls).

⚠ THE PATTERN THIS MAP EXISTS TO CARRY: five underpowered nulls, five conclusions written as equivalence. Shen's falls null (RR 2.68, CI to 11.11 — "similar safety profiles"). Shen's bone null (lumbar SMD 0.28, CI −0.02 to 0.58 — "no difference"). Wu's four adaptation nulls at I²=0% ("as effective… for most neuromuscular adaptations"). Chen's BFR-substitute null on 4 trials/n=123. Doan's strength/mobility null on 6 trials. The direction of the error is not random: each time, the null flattered the authors' recommendation. Two of the five also flattered GOAL.md's least-work philosophy — and the vault refused those too, which is the only condition under which the lens means anything. The most reliable finding about this literature is that it is chronically too small to answer its own secondary questions. Escalated in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

(4) Bone does not care how heavy you lift, as far as anyone can detect. No significant BMD difference between intensities — lumbar spine SMD 0.28 (−0.02 to 0.58), femoral neck 0.13 (−0.08 to 0.33) — though the lumbar interval misses significance by two hundredths and is underpowered, not equivalent (rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd). Read alongside creatine-bone-bmd-null, the vault has now twice failed to find a lever that moves bone density in a training adult. Do not choose a load, or a pill, for your skeleton.

Why emerging, not higher: every finding here is a strength or function surrogate, in populations ranging from healthy young adults to osteoporotic elders, on trial bases of 6–20 studies with chronic power problems. A higher-tier study of a different outcome does not consolidate anything (shared/evidence-model.md), and none of this touches the parent map's mortality claim.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-10 — Ingested Shen 2026 (Geriatrics & Gerontology International, SR + meta-analysis, 18 RCTs, n=1,283, adults ≥50, PROSPERO CRD420251076841) as the load axis: heavy ≥70% 1RM beat <70% on leg press SMD 0.95 (0.48–1.43) and leg extension 0.63 (0.09–1.17); no detectable BMD difference; falls RR 2.68 (0.65–11.11) and AEs RR 2.42 (0.66–8.88) read by the authors as "similar safety profiles" and not by this vault. Adds safety_flags: [high-dose-risk]. Capped suggestive; bone and safety concepts single-study.
  • 2026-07-11 — Ingested Wu 2026 (BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation, SR + meta-analysis, 20 RCTs, n=556, healthy adults, PROSPERO-registered, GRADE-scored, independently funded, no competing interests) as the effort axis: non-failure beat failure on dynamic strength SMD 0.24 (0.06–0.42). Four remaining outcomes are underpowered nulls laundered into equivalence — isometric −0.12 (−0.48 to 0.24), hypertrophy −0.15 (−0.39 to 0.09), endurance −0.02 (−0.45 to 0.41), power 0.02 (−0.28 to 0.31) — three of four upper bounds meet or exceed the paper's own significant 0.24, at I²=0%. Strength concept suggestive; nulls concept single-study.
  • 2026-07-18 — Ingested Doan 2026 (American Journal of Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation, SR + meta-analysis, 7 RCTs in 8 articles (6 pooled), older adults, searched to July 2025, no conflicts; no PROSPERO or GRADE stated) as the stability axis, and the source that made this map fork-due. Unstable vs stable RT: no significant advantage on lower-limb strength or short-term mobility, with Five-Times Sit-to-Stand trending toward stable (unstable-vs-stable-rt-strength-mobility-null-underpowered); fear of falling significantly reduced short-term and TUG improved at 24 weeks (unstable-rt-reduces-fear-of-falling-not-falls). Two substitutions the vault names: fear of falling for falling (no fall count anywhere in the paper), and surfaces for intensities — so it does not close the parent's standing request for a trial powered on falls comparing training intensities in adults ≥50. Appraised mixed (conflict-free, sensible short/long stratification — against a 7-trial base with no reported N/power/GRADE, an unblindable self-reported primary win, and ~10 uncorrected comparisons); FES-I concept single-study, null concept suggestive. extends; Status unchanged (emerging). Full text confirmed non-OA (Wolters Kluwer; Unpaywall oa_status: closed), pdf_unavailable logged.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-07-27 — the rehab sequence this map terminates now has an outcome measured at its far end, and the outcome is small. A new map, exercise-for-osteoarthritis, holds the vault's first Cochrane review on what exercise does for a painful weight-bearing joint: against usual care, pain MD −7.19 (−10.70 to −3.68) and function MD −8.79 (−12.00 to −5.41) on a 0–100 scale at moderate certainty — which the authors themselves call "unlikely to be clinically meaningful" (exercise-hip-osteoarthritis-benefit-real-but-below-clinical-threshold). This map's heavy-load finding (leg press SMD 0.95) is a strength result; that is a symptom result; and the second is much smaller than the first. Recorded because the sequence range → tolerable load → strength is easy to read as though strength gains convert into pain relief at the same effect size, and they do not. ⚑ And this map's signature pathology gets a counter-example worth naming. This is the vault's home for underpowered nulls written up as equivalence — five instances, five conclusions flattering their own authors' recommendations, including Shen 2026's fall-risk interval reaching 11.11 reported as "similar safety profiles" (rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered). The Cochrane review contains an underpowered null too — pain against an attention control, 2 studies, n=123 — and rates it low certainty and words it as "may have little to no effect" (exercise-hip-osteoarthritis-pain-null-against-attention-control-underpowered). The pathology is therefore not inherent to subgroup analysis; it is a reporting choice, and GRADE-rated reviews make the other one. Cross-noted on exercise-for-osteoarthritis.
  • ⚑ 2026-07-24 — the underpowered-null-as-equivalence series takes its sixth entry, from outside this literature. Oba 2026 (stretching, 79 RCTs, n=3,287) reports no moderation of flexibility gains by age (p = 0.853), sex, training status or baseline flexibility, and concludes stretching works "across diverse healthy individuals" — from cells holding 3 middle-aged, 4 older-adult and 3 sedentary trials against 59 in young adults (stretching-age-generalisation-rests-on-three-middle-aged-trials), plus a near-twofold muscle-group spread declared non-significant at p = 0.100 on six knee-extensor trials (stretching-quadriceps-evidence-thinnest-and-weakest-of-the-lower-limb). This map's standing observation generalises: the five instances catalogued here are not a quirk of the resistance-training literature. Across six papers now, the null a subgroup analysis was never powered to detect is reported as the null it found, and the resulting claim reliably points the way the authors' recommendation already pointed. Accepting Oba's version would have closed stretching's own logged population gap, and it was refused anyway — the same discipline this map applied to Wu 2026. No Status move; no safety_flags.
  • ⚑ 2026-07-24 — an exercise-programme adverse-event count arrives from an unexpected map, and it supports this map's high-dose-risk cap rather than softening it. multidomain-lifestyle ingested LatAm-FINGERS (Crivelli 2026, Lancet, n=1,065, aged 60–77, 2 years), which counted adverse events by arm: 412 in the supervised multidomain arm vs 66 in the health-advice arm, with musculoskeletal symptoms in 113 (21%) vs 13 (2%) (supervised-multidomain-programme-carries-musculoskeletal-adverse-event-burden). This is what the vault has been asking for and not getting: a direct count of musculoskeletal harm in a supervised exercise programme, rather than rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered's fall-risk interval (RR 2.68, CI to 11.11) sold as "similar safety profiles." Two limits travel with it: the count is aggregate across four intervention domains, so exercise is the obvious but unproven source; and an arm with weekly supervised contact reports sore joints more readily, so some of the 6× gap is surveillance. Not a graded interaction — no [interaction] flag. It changes no Status and lifts no gate; it is recorded because the knee cap on heavy lower-limb loading (proposed — review with your doctor/physio, per GOAL.md's March 2026 patellar contusion) should rest on counted harm where counted harm exists.
  • ⚠ THE KNEE GATE LIVES HERE — and it is restated at full strength on resistance-training so the fork cannot weaken it. Against GOAL.md's hard knee/joint-safe constraint and David's March 2026 patellar contusion, any regimen expression of heavy lower-limb loading is capped at "proposed — review with your doctor / physio" (shared/decision-rule.md, hard overrides), on the basis of rt-intensity-safety-comparison-underpowered. Unstable-surface variants inherit the same cap rather than needing their own gate — an unstable surface is precisely the condition under which a healing patella is asked to stabilise unpredictably, and nothing in Doan 2026 enrolled anyone in orthopaedic recovery. Countervailing signal the vault also holds: daily-steps-broad-healthspan-outcomes found fewer falls with more walking, and stronger legs plausibly prevent falls. The tension is unresolved, and that is the point.
  • ⚠ LOAD × EFFORT compose, and the composition is the practical answer. Shen is about how heavy; Wu is about how close to failure within a set. Wu tests performance only — it never measures falls or injury, so it neither confirms nor refutes Shen's fall-risk signal and does not soften the flag. But they compose usefully: the reps that most degrade technique are the last ones before failure, and those are precisely the reps Wu's evidence says you can skip at no cost to dynamic strength. That composition is this vault's inference, not either paper's finding (shared/safety.md — a plausible mechanism is not a graded finding). Offered to the physio conversation, not as a safety claim.
  • blood-flow-restriction is the standing candidate answer to this gate, and it still does not lift it. Low-load training with occlusion is the textbook escape from heavy loading. Across five syntheses the vault now holds: BFR adds strength over light load, is an adjunct rather than a replacement by its own authors' framing (bfr-is-adjunct-not-substitute-for-heavy-load), matched heavy load for strength in David's population on 4 trials/n=123 — an underpowered null, not non-inferiority (bfr-matches-heavy-load-strength-in-knee-rehab) — and carries its own safety_flags: [high-dose-risk] on a five-times-replicated adverse-event absence (bfr-safety-not-established-in-trials). The gate softens to a sharper physio question; it does not open.
  • Bone: two nulls, one conclusion. creatine-bone-bmd-null (creatine adds no BMD beyond training) and rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd (doubling intensity adds no detectable BMD). Both underpowered, neither closing the question — but together: whatever bone benefit exists comes from training at all, not from training heavy and not from a supplement.
  • Muscle-preservation relevance to glp-1 (prescription, clinician-only). Preserving strength, not merely mass, is the goal during GLP-1 treatment, and strength is what responds to load (high-intensity-rt-beats-moderate-for-lower-limb-strength) — which is exactly the intervention this map flags. Both facts land in the clinician conversation together. Full framing on resistance-training and glp-1.
  • Global-ripple: run 2026-07-18 across all 37 maps. No supplement or drug interaction is created by the load, effort or stability axes beyond those already carried on the parent (the cold-water-immersion post-workout timing gate, which bites harder the heavier you train, and the mTOR/AMPK edges with rapamycin / metformin). The stability axis adds no new graded interaction — the unstable-surface knee concern is mechanistic, and per shared/safety.md a plausible mechanism is not a graded interaction, so no new safety_flags were coined on that basis; it is carried as an inherited cap and logged.
