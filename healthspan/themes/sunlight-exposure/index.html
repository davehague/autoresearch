Outdoor sunlight exposure — nutrition

Status: emerging (2026-11-11) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single outcome-wide UK Biobank cohort study (Li 2026, n=281,282, >13.4-year follow-up) testing daily outdoor-time duration against 134 diseases. A genuinely new lever, distinct from vitamin-d: that map is specifically about pill-form supplementation in replete adults (and is contested on a strong RCT null); this map is about a behavior — time spent outdoors — that carries its own bundle of correlated effects (UV/vitamin-D synthesis, physical activity, temperature, socioeconomic patterning) that cannot be reduced to "vitamin D levels" alone, confirmed by the founding source's own mediation analysis (vitamin D is only one of several statistical mediators).

What we believe now

One very large, carefully-run, honestly-limited cohort found small, real, bidirectional associations between daily outdoor time and a wide range of diseases — too small and too confounded to act on individually, but too well-corrected-for-multiple-comparisons to dismiss. More outdoor time tracked with higher risk of skin cancer (expected), obesity, cerebrovascular disease, and osteoarthritis, and with lower risk of osteoporosis and mineral-metabolism disorders — all surviving FDR correction across 402 tests. Thirty further diseases (depression, anxiety, sleep disorders, arrhythmias, iron-deficiency anemia, kidney failure among them) showed U-shaped curves, with the lowest-risk point around 1 hour (summer) to 1–2 hours (winter) of daily outdoor time in this cohort. Effect sizes throughout sit in the same small range (HR 0.91–1.09 per hour) this vault's own vitamin-D map already treats as too modest to act on individually.

The study's own framing is deliberately conservative: the authors explicitly warn against using "~1 hour summer, 1–2 hours winter" as an individualized recommendation, calling it a preliminary population-level reference range pending validation. This map holds that same caution. No dose, duration, or exposure prescription is stated or implied here (shared/safety.md).

⚠ For David (GOAL.md): outdoor time is relevant to his pickleball goal and general activity pattern, but the effect sizes and confounding here are too small and too uncertain to change any specific recommendation — filed as a new, early-stage map, not an action item.
