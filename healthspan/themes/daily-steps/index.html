Daily steps (walking) — exercise

Status: emerging (2026-07-08) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined by a large dose-response meta-analysis of prospective cohorts linking device-measured daily step count to eight health outcomes on hard endpoints. Opened emerging: the evidence is a mortality/morbidity association with an observational ceiling (step count can't be randomized), but the effect is large, dose-responsive, and the intervention is the lowest-burden, most joint-friendly lever in the vault.

What we believe now

Walking more is one of the highest-value, lowest-cost things a healthy adult can do — and ~7,000 steps/day, not 10,000, is where most of the benefit lives. Pooling 24 cohorts (>160,000 adults), ~7,000 vs ~2,000 steps/day tracked with ~47% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.53) and broad reductions in cardiovascular disease, cancer mortality, type 2 diabetes (0.86), dementia (0.62), depressive symptoms (0.78), and falls (0.72). The dose-response is non-linear with an inflection near 5,000–7,000 steps for several outcomes — the culturally-mandated 10,000-step target is a marketing figure, not an evidence threshold. For David (GOAL.md: least work, knee-safe during patellar-contusion recovery), this is the ideal base lever: free, sustainable, near-zero discomfort, zero equipment, joint-friendly.

The tension worth keeping visible (why emerging, not higher): every included study is observational — you cannot randomize how much someone walks — so reverse causation (subclinical illness lowers step count) inflates part of the association, and high vs low steppers differ on many mortality-relevant confounders. The magnitude and dose-response argue against pure confounding, but this is the same observational ceiling the vault applies to zone-2-cardio (CRF→mortality) and resistance-training. A hard-endpoint signal on a free behaviour — strong enough to act on, honest enough to keep capped.

(2) The sleep thread, rewritten 2026-07-13 after three meta-analyses arrived in four weeks and contradicted each other. Walking almost certainly does something for sleep. The magnitude is unknowable from this literature, and the instrument you pick determines the answer you get.

Every paper's strongest result is the one its design is weakest at measuring, and the two claims the vault was most tempted by have both been undercut from the other side. Yesterday it recorded, with visible pleasure, that the machine moved (+34 min TST) where every wind-down supplement only moves the questionnaire — and graded it single-study anyway, because the objective outcomes rested on 2–3 trials each. Zhou's 31-RCT network, in the population most likely to show it, finds no total-sleep-time effect at all (exercise-does-not-increase-total-sleep-time). Meanwhile Kong confirms the subjective direction the vault refused to write off — g = −0.76, landing inside the very interval that made walking-subjective-sleep-quality-unresolved an open question rather than a null (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-large-effect) — and Kong's own GRADE rating of that finding is VERY LOW: not one of its 21 trials is low-risk-of-bias, all are open-label with self-reported outcomes, and its publication-bias tests fire at p < 0.001. Its headline 99.97% posterior probability describes the model, not the world (bayesian-certainty-cannot-repair-biased-inputs).

And the population is the quiet disqualifier. Only 3 of Kong's 21 trials enrolled healthy adults; 8 enrolled diagnosed sleep disorders — and the model's strongest moderator is baseline sleep badness (β = −0.302): the worse your sleep, the more walking helps it (walking-sleep-benefit-scales-with-baseline-sleep-problem). The exercise-and-sleep literature is, almost in its entirety, a treatment literature, and GOAL.md describes David's sleep as a foundation to protect, not a problem to treat.

⚠ Updated 2026-07-19 — the largest analysis in the field arrives, and it confirms the vault's own read rather than the literature's headline. Liang 2026 (Sleep Medicine Reviews, 200 RCTs, n = 23,523, Bayesian multilevel + MetaForest + SEM) asked not does exercise improve sleep but what determines whether it does — and answered: baseline sleep quality and population type, with poor sleepers much more likely to reach a clinically important improvement than good sleepers. The authors call it a "benefit-on-demand pattern" (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers).

On 2026-07-13 this map wrote, from a single moderator coefficient in Kong (β = −0.302), that "the exercise-and-sleep literature is, almost in its entirety, a treatment literature." Twenty times the evidence now reports that as its primary finding. The inference held.

And it cuts against acting, not for it. A benefit concentrated in bad sleepers is, for a person who sleeps adequately, a reason to expect less — so this map's existing conclusion ("small, unmeasured, and free") is now the best-supported reading rather than the cautious one.

Two things genuinely improved. Objective sleep efficiency moved in agreement with the subjective outcomes — the first cross-instrument convergence in this literature, though in only 20 of 200 trials and small (exercise-sleep-objective-and-subjective-converge-weakly). And walking ranked among the top modalities against ACTIVE controls (with qigong and HIIT) — the harder comparison this map has been asking for (walking-among-top-sleep-modalities-against-active-controls), recorded without reading the leaderboard, per the vault's standing SUCRA discipline.

The number that governs everything above: certainty VERY LOW, at n = 23,523. Two hundred open-label trials asking people how they slept produce a very-low-certainty answer for the same reason twenty-one did. Scale does not launder design (exercise-sleep-certainty-very-low-at-200-trials) — the pile got bigger, the instrument did not get better. The ~25-week duration optimum is recorded, not adopted (meta-regression across heterogeneous trials, not a randomised duration comparison; shared/safety.md forbids converting a published range into a prescription).

So: nothing changes. No Status move, no new Foundation, no number in the regimen, and walking is still counted once — on the mortality evidence (HR 0.53), which is what it was ever recommended for. The honest expected value of the sleep bonus for a healthy sleeper who already walks: small, unmeasured, and free. Still logged as a possible synergy, still deliberately not graded as one. Four independent syntheses now agree on the direction and none of them can supply a trustworthy magnitude.

Through-line

  • 2026-07-08 — Ingested Ding 2025 (Lancet Public Health, dose-response meta-analysis, 31 studies / 24 prospective cohorts, >160,000 adults): ~7,000 vs ~2,000 steps/day → all-cause mortality HR 0.53, CVD mortality 0.53, cancer mortality 0.63, CVD incidence 0.75, T2D 0.86, dementia 0.62, depression 0.78, falls 0.72; non-linear inflection ~5,000–7,000 steps (10,000 not required). Auto-coins the map at emerging — hard endpoints, observational ceiling, lowest-burden lever in the vault.

  • 2026-07-12 — Ingested Liu 2026 (Scientific Reports, meta-analysis of 8 RCTs, n=683, PROSPERO CRD420251143771, RoB 2): walking → total sleep time +34.23 min (24.72–43.74) and wake after sleep onset −11.58 min (−18.50 to −4.66), both device-measured; subjective sleep quality null and unstable (PSQI MD −1.41, −3.18 to 0.36, I²=92.7%; the authors' own leave-one-out analysis swings it −0.91 to −2.03 and they call it "unstable"). Opens a sleep sub-thread; Status unchanged. Graded single-study despite the RCT-meta tier because the objective outcomes rest on 2–3 trials each (the authors say so), no publication-bias test was possible (<10 studies), and the pooled populations include Alzheimer's and cancer patients. The read the vault refuses to flatter: it likes this result, and that is exactly why it applied the same underpowered-null lens it used against Shen and Wu — the PSQI interval (−3.18 to 0.36) contains a clinically meaningful improvement and zero, so it is an open question, not a negative finding (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-unresolved).

  • 2026-07-13 — The open question is answered within 24 hours, in the direction the vault protected — and the vault still refuses to bank it; Status unchanged (Kong 2026, Frontiers in Public Health, Bayesian three-level meta-analysis, 21 RCTs, n=1,707, 66 effect sizes, PROSPERO CRD420261351897; no funding, no conflicts). Walking → subjective sleep quality Hedges' g = −0.76 (95% CrI −0.99 to −0.55), 95% prediction interval entirely below zero (−1.37 to −0.16), Bayes factor 8.16 × 10⁵, 99.97% posterior probability of exceeding a pre-specified MCID (walking-subjective-sleep-quality-large-effect). It lands inside Liu's interval, on the improvement side. Yesterday the vault declined to read Liu's PSQI null as a negative finding, on the explicit grounds that its interval "contains a clinically meaningful improvement and zero" — and it was right, within a day. That is the strongest evidence yet that the underpowered-null lens does real work rather than rationalising, because it was applied against a result the vault wanted to be true. Now the same discipline has to run the other way, and that is the harder direction. The paper's own GRADE rating of its own headline is VERY LOW, downgraded on three domains: not one of the 21 trials is low risk of bias (four are high), I² = 73.7%, and publication bias fires on both tests (Egger z = −6.53, p < 0.001; Begg p < 0.001). Every trial is open-label with a self-reported outcome against an inactive control — you cannot blind someone to whether they went for walks and then ask how well they slept — and the authors concede it against their own headline: "the effect sizes reported here may overestimate the true treatment effect." A Bayes factor of 800,000 over a corpus like that measures the consistency of the bias, not the reality of the effect (bayesian-certainty-cannot-repair-biased-inputs) — and this is a new laundering shape, the nastiest yet, precisely because Kong launders nothing. It is scrupulously honest; the inflation happens downstream, in the reading, because "99.97%" travels and "GRADE: very low" does not. And the population is not David's: only 3 of 21 trials in healthy adults, with the model's strongest moderator being baseline sleep badness (β = −0.302) (walking-sleep-benefit-scales-with-baseline-sleep-problem). Direction real; magnitude not bankable. No Status change, no Foundation, no number, still counted once.

  • 2026-07-13 — The +34 minutes fails to replicate in a network four times the size; Status unchanged (global-ripple from exercise-combination-insomnia-nma-zhou-2026 — 31 RCTs, n=2,457 insomnia patients, ten intervention nodes incl. exercise and exercise+CBT). No intervention significantly increased total sleep time (exercise-does-not-increase-total-sleep-time). This is not a refutation — sparser TST evidence, largely subjective instruments, a different population and different comparators — but it is a large, relevant, conspicuous failure to corroborate the vault's most-liked sleep number: Liu's device-measured +34.23 min, which rested on two to three trials. The vault graded that claim single-study when it landed, precisely because it liked it. That caution has now been vindicated from the second direction in a single night. The sleep sub-thread stays thin, ungraded, and un-promoted.

  • 2026-07-18 — Ingested Zhu 2026 (PLoS One, randomized crossover, n=60 sedentary college students, registered ChiCTR2500097728, no competing interests, OA) as a cross-intervention interaction, not a walking finding. Walking with a blood-flow-restriction cuff at rising occlusion pressure (0/40/60/80% LOP) produced significantly more perceived exertion and discomfort and significantly FEWER steps (both p<0.05) (bfr-occlusion-reduces-walking-volume-and-comfort, safety_flags: [interaction]). This map's entire mortality claim is denominated in steps — ~7,000 vs ~2,000/day → HR 0.53 — so a technique that measurably lowers step count is not neutral toward it. First graded interaction between blood-flow-restriction and this map, noted on both per shared/safety.md. Caveats: 5-minute acute bouts, young sedentary students, significance reported without magnitudes, so it does not establish that habitual BFR walking lowers daily step totals. Appraised mixed, graded suggestive; extends; Status unchanged (emerging) — nothing here touches the mortality dose-response. Practical rule generated: if BFR ever enters David's knee rehab, it belongs on the lifting, not on the walk. OA PDF archived.

  • 2026-07-19 — The field's largest synthesis lands, confirms this map's own inference, and changes nothing; Status held emerging (Liang 2026, Sleep Medicine Reviews 86, systematic review + Bayesian multimethod meta-analysis, 200 RCTs, n = 23,523 adults ≥18; six databases to Aug 2025; RoB 2 in duplicate; Bayesian multilevel models + MetaForest + structural equation modelling). Four findings. (1) Baseline sleep quality and population type are the main determinants of response — poor sleepers much more likely to reach a clinically important improvement than good sleepers; the authors name it "benefit-on-demand" (exercise-sleep-benefit-concentrated-in-poor-sleepers). This map inferred exactly that on 2026-07-13 from Kong's single β = −0.302 and called the field "a treatment literature"; 200 trials now say so directly. (2) In 20 trials reporting objective sleep efficiency, exercise produced a small but significant improvement consistent with the subjective outcomes — the first cross-instrument agreement in this literature, on 10% of the corpus (exercise-sleep-objective-and-subjective-converge-weakly). (3) Against active controls, qigong, walking and HIIT showed the largest benefits — the isolating comparison this map requested (walking-among-top-sleep-modalities-against-active-controls); ranking recorded, leaderboard declined per the vault's five-case SUCRA discipline. (4) Certainty of evidence: VERY LOW, stated by the authors, who explicitly caution against strong conclusions (exercise-sleep-certainty-very-low-at-200-trials) — the cleanest demonstration yet that scale does not launder design. The ~25-week U-shaped duration optimum is recorded, not adopted (meta-regression, not a randomised duration comparison). The confirmation argues for expecting less, not more: David sleeps adequately, so he sits in the subgroup where this literature's effect is smallest. Appraised mixed; capped suggestive. Confirmed non-OA (Elsevier, no PMCID, Europe PMC "Subscription required"); pdf_unavailable logged after the ladder — the same wall that defeated Fiori 2026 in this journal.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ 2026-07-24 — an exercise-timing × appetite finding that does NOT transfer to walking, recorded so a future run does not assume it does. Höchsmann 2026 (intermittent-fasting, zone-2-cardio) found evening aerobic exercise at the end of a 12-hour fast was followed by +547 kcal over 24 hours versus the morning (evening-fasted-exercise-followed-by-higher-24h-energy-intake). The tested modality was 30 minutes of treadmill running at 65% VO₂peak — a considerably harder stimulus than this map's behaviour, and nothing in the trial speaks to walking at any time of day. Logged as an untested extension rather than a flag: no safety_flags are set on this map, and the step target is unchanged. Relevant because walking is the modality David actually uses and the one this map recommends on mortality grounds.
  • ⚠ GRADED INTERACTION — blood-flow-restriction costs you steps (new 2026-07-18, safety_flags: [interaction]). Zhu 2026 (randomized crossover, n=60) measured what nobody had asked across four prior BFR syntheses and 51 RCTs: what the cuff costs the activity underneath it. As limb occlusion pressure rose, perceived exertion and discomfort rose and step count fell (both p<0.05) (bfr-occlusion-reduces-walking-volume-and-comfort). This map's mortality evidence is denominated in steps, so occlusion applied to walking trades a well-evidenced free lever (daily-steps-mortality-dose-response, HR 0.53) against a weakly-evidenced strength adjunct that is still capped at proposed — review with your doctor/physio. The rule this generates is specific: put the cuff on the lifting, not on the walk. Per shared/safety.md interaction evidence is asymmetric — and this one is measured, not mechanistic, which is stronger than the bar requires. Carried on both maps. Bounded honestly: 5-minute acute bouts in young sedentary adults; adaptation could shrink the discomfort, and no daily-step-total data exist.
  • ⚠ NOT the same lever as sedentary-behavior — count them separately (new 2026-07-10). An umbrella review of 30 systematic reviews (83 studies, n=2,778,595) finds sedentary time and TV viewing predict all-cause mortality even after adjusting for physical activity, consistently across device-based and self-reported measurement (sedentary-time-independent-of-exercise-mortality-risk). Steps measure what you do; sitting measures what you don't. A person can hit ~7,000 steps and still spend eleven waking hours seated, and the evidence says those hours carry their own risk. This is the opposite of the sleep-duration ↔ sleep-regularity ruling (same behaviour measured twice → count once); here two distinct behaviours compete for the same clock → count twice. Cross-linked on both maps. Caveat carried honestly: the sedentary umbrella publishes no pooled effect size, so its magnitude — unlike this map's HR 0.53 — is unknown, and reverse causation (illness causes sitting) is unexcluded.
  • The low-intensity floor beneath the aerobic and strength levers — complementary, not redundant. Walking is the base of the same activity→mortality story as zone-2-cardio (CRF→mortality) and resistance-training (direct mortality). Steps and Zone-2 overlap on aerobic dose but sit at different intensities — steps is the everyday floor, Zone-2 the structured session; they stack, they don't double-count (both are the same underlying "move more" lever expressed at different intensities). For the regimen, count aerobic activity as one axis (steps + Zone-2), resistance training as another. Reinforced 2026-07-15: zone-2-cardio now carries its own aerobic-volume dose-response (Garcia 2023, minimum ~75 min/wk) with the same front-loaded "less is enough" shape as the ~7,000-step floor here — two instruments, one story, still one axis.
  • David-specific (GOAL.md): the cleanest knee-safe base. Unlike deep-knee-flexion loading, walking is low-impact and compatible with patellar-contusion recovery — the first-line way to stay active while the knee heals, and a fall-risk reducer once mobile.
  • ⚠ Possible SYNERGY with the sleep Foundation — logged, still deliberately NOT graded (rewritten 2026-07-13). Three meta-analyses in four weeks, and they disagree with each other: Liu (objective TST up, subjective null), Kong (subjective up hugely, GRADE very low, no objective outcome), Zhou (subjective up, TST null across 31 RCTs). If a synergy is real, the step target feeds sleep-regularity (the vault's strongest sleep Foundation) and helps defend the ~7 h floor that sleep-duration sets (short-sleep-under-7h-mortality-risk). But shared/safety.md's asymmetry rule cuts one way only — a missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious flag, and that logic does not license inflating a benefit on evidence this contradictory. Not counted twice in the regimen, logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md, and the gap is now specific: a blinded-as-possible walking RCT in healthy sleepers with both objective and subjective endpoints — because every existing trial has an unblindable intervention, a self-reported outcome, and a population that already sleeps badly.
  • ⚠ The wind-down cluster now has a free competitor it cannot beat — and the vault will not promote it anyway (new 2026-07-13). On the same subjective instrument the entire supplement shelf is scored on, walking's pooled effect (g = −0.76) and exercise+CBT's (PSQI −2.80, moderate certainty — cbt-insomnia) are larger than anything l-theanine (whose highest-tier synthesis pools no sleep endpoint at all), chamomile (I² = 88%), melatonin (+12 min, in cognitively impaired elders) or magnesium-glycinate (d = 0.2) can show. The cheapest thing in the vault outperforms every pill in the cluster on the pills' own outcome. And the vault still refuses to bank it, because the walking evidence is GRADE very low with publication bias at p < 0.001 — the free thing winning is not a reason to relax the standard. What this does justify is a negative conclusion, which is cheap and safe: it is one more reason not to buy a sleep supplement, and no reason to add anything at all.
  • ⚠ The wind-down cluster now has a MEASURED ceiling, and the gap is four-to-one (new 2026-07-19). The same night Liang landed, dietary-supplements-sleep-quality-meta-mei-2025 pooled 28 RCTs of the entire supplement shelf — tryptophan, L-theanine, GABA, magnesium, zinc, vitamin D, omega-3, ashwagandha and more — on the same instrument: PSQI MD −0.70 (−1.37 to −0.03), against a commonly cited MID of ~3 (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold). On 2026-07-13 this map estimated exercise+CBT at "roughly twice the size of anything on the supplement shelf." Measured: −2.80 versus −0.70. It is four times, and the vault had been under-stating it. The comparison spans different trials, populations and comparators, so it is an orientation, not a head-to-head — but the ordering is not close. Neither is promoted; the negative conclusion is what gets cheaper: still nothing to buy.
  • Timing note (unsourced, so it stays a note): the same paper's subgroup hint favoured ≥30-minute sessions. Included trials ran 30–75 min, 3–5×/week — that range is quoted, and nothing outside it is invented (shared/safety.md).
  • Global-ripple / safety: no interaction with any vault intervention. Walking is universally compatible; nothing to flag. Its value is as a foundation behaviour that amplifies (never conflicts with) every other lever.
