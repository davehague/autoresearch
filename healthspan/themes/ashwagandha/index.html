Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) — stress & mental

Status: emerging (2026-07-10) — the vault's first Stress & mental lever, now carrying two threads from two independent syntheses that reach the same verdict. A meta-analysis of RCTs shows ashwagandha lowers cortisol and subjective stress/anxiety over ~8 weeks — a semi-objective signal (the cortisol drop) that most sleep/stress botanicals lack. A second, conflict-free meta-analysis shows a modest exercise-performance benefit concentrated in aerobic endurance — whose own 95% prediction interval crosses zero. Direction promising on both axes; magnitude unsettled on both. Held at emerging on those honesty gates.

What we believe now

Two threads, enumerated because they point at different outcomes, different populations, and different times of day:

(1) Stress — ashwagandha modestly reduces the stress axis, on mostly-subjective evidence. In 15 placebo-controlled RCTs (873 adults, ~125–600 mg/day for 30–90 days), it lowered serum cortisol (−2.36, 95% CI −3.26 to −1.46), perceived stress (PSS −4.88), and anxiety (HAM-A −3.52) at ~8 weeks — but did not improve quality of life. The biochemical cortisol reduction is the most objective finding and is why this earns a map rather than a footnote — but the stress/anxiety benefits are questionnaire-based, and a live tension runs through the 2025–2026 literature: some syntheses reproduce the cortisol drop yet find no reliable effect on perceived stress, and a 2026 dose-response analysis suggests benefit concentrates at lower doses (≤500 mg/day), longer courses (>8 weeks), and in people with pre-existing symptoms.

(2) Exercise performance — a real average effect that does not generalize (new 2026-07-10). A three-level meta-analysis of 13 RCTs (n=599, 79 effect sizes) found improved overall performance (Hedges' g = 0.47, 0.25–0.69, I²=60%), with aerobic endurance the most consistent domain (g = 0.54, 0.22–0.85) (ashwagandha-aerobic-endurance-performance). But its 95% prediction interval is [−0.40, 1.33] — the effect expected in a new trial spans mild harm to large benefit (ashwagandha-performance-effect-does-not-generalize). The authors report this themselves, which is rare and to their credit. Source's reported dose range: 500–600 mg/day of extract, labelled hypothesis-generating. Population: mainly young adults and athletes — one cohort averaged 17.4 years old.

(3) Two independent syntheses, two outcome families, one verdict. The stress thread's recorded weaknesses were subjective endpoints and an industry-adjacent trial base. The performance meta-analysis repairs both — objective outcomes, and no declared conflicts of interest — and lands in exactly the same place: direction promising, magnitude unsettled. That convergence is more informative than either paper alone. It is also the independence the map's logged gap asked for, arriving on an axis nobody was watching.

(4) It is still an industry-adjacent botanical — the same gate as l-theanine and chamomile. Branded standardized extracts of differing withanolide content, manufacturer-sponsored primary trials, small heterogeneous samples, weak blinding. Watch, don't adopt; any product must be third-party tested.

Why emerging, not higher: high evidence tier on both threads (meta-analyses of RCTs), undercut on the stress axis by subjective-dominant endpoints, no QoL benefit, and pervasive conflict of interest; and on the performance axis by a null-crossing prediction interval, a young athletic population irrelevant to GOAL.md, and unstandardized extracts. Population match, not evidence tier, is the binding constraint on thread (2) — mirroring l-theanine and chamomile.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠ Redundant sedation with the sleep-aid stack — flag, don't co-recommend silently. Ashwagandha is sedating/anxiolytic and overlaps in mechanism and use-case with l-theanine, chamomile, magnesium-glycinate, and melatonin. Stacking several wind-down agents is redundancy, not additive benefit, and compounds sedation — the concept carries safety_flags: [interaction] so the regimen treats these as one wind-down lever, not four independent wins.
  • ⚠ A timing tension inside the sedation flag (new 2026-07-10). The wind-down cluster's premise is that ashwagandha is a sedating agent taken before bed. Its better-evidenced effect is now an ergogenic, pre-exercise, daytime one (ashwagandha-aerobic-endurance-performance). Both cannot be the use case at once. The sedation interaction flag holds (it gates co-use with the cluster regardless), but the map records that the two threads would be dosed hours apart, and that no source tests taking it for both.
  • Ergogenic overlap with zone-2-cardio and resistance-training — a redundancy note, not a co-recommendation (new 2026-07-10). The aerobic-endurance signal notionally serves the aerobic axis, but on a young-athlete performance surrogate, not a healthspan endpoint, and with a prediction interval crossing zero. Meanwhile creatine-monohydrate holds the vault's only bankable ergogenic evidence (creatine-dose-response-strength — a small effect, but flat and consistent across 61 trials). Do not stack a second ergogenic supplement on thin evidence to serve a goal the first one already serves better. No safety edge; pure redundancy management.
  • A shared oddity with l-theanine (new 2026-07-10). Both wind-down-cluster botanicals have their strongest evidence for a daytime effect — ashwagandha on aerobic endurance, L-theanine on acute attention (l-theanine-acute-attention-benefit) — and softer evidence for the evening property the cluster is named for. Ashwagandha at least retains a semi-objective cortisol anchor that L-theanine now conspicuously lacks (l-theanine-stress-benefit-driven-by-biased-trials), so within the cluster it ranks ahead of L-theanine on the sedating property. A coherence check, not a safety edge.
  • Thyroid + hepatotoxicity pharmacology edge — logged as an open gap, not fabricated. Ashwagandha may raise thyroid hormone (caution with thyroid conditions / levothyroxine) and rare hepatotoxicity case reports exist; real pharmacology, but no vault-quality source is yet ingested, so it is logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md rather than graded into a flag. Note (2026-07-10): Li 2026 assessed performance, not safety, and reports no adverse-event synthesis — so it does not close this gap. A missed interaction is worse than an over-cautious one — the sedation flag above already gates co-recommendation.
  • Global-ripple: no confirm/refute safety interaction with the metabolic or vitamin/omega maps. The new exercise-performance thread creates a redundancy edge with the exercise maps (above), not a harm edge. Nearest methodological neighbor remains the subjective-endpoint + seller-conflict pattern shared with l-theanine and chamomile — though Li 2026 is notably free of that conflict.
