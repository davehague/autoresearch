Weighted vest (worn load, "rucking") — exercise

Status: emerging (2026-08-15) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined from a single systematic review + meta-analysis of nine RCTs, 368 participants, and coined on a split verdict: the comparison that isolates the vest from the exercise it is worn during finds no bone benefit (from one trial) and 0.73 kg of body fat (at GRADE high certainty). Opened at emerging because that is what nine ~41-person trials with an unstandardised load support. git is the undo.

What we believe now

A weighted vest is not a bone intervention. It is a way of making a walk cost more, and the best-evidenced thing it buys is about three quarters of a kilogram of fat.

Wang X 2026 ran two comparisons and they answer different questions. Against usual activity, vest-plus-exercise raised femoral-neck BMD (SMD 0.59, 95% CI 0.05–1.13), lumbar-spine BMD (SMD 0.73, 0.29–1.16, I² = 0%) and cut body fat by 2.67 kg (−4.08 to −1.27) (weighted-vest-plus-exercise-beats-usual-activity-on-bone-and-fat). Every one of those numbers is compatible with the exercise doing all of the work — and this vault already holds the evidence that it might, in moderate-intensity-resistance-training-raises-hip-bone-density-against-control.

Against exercise alone — the same training, without the vest — the bone benefit disappears. The authors state it themselves: "Adding a vest to exercise alone did not confer a significant BMD benefit compared with exercise alone, although this finding is derived from a single study" (weighted-vest-adds-no-bone-density-once-the-exercise-is-controlled-for). ⚠ One trial is a failure to demonstrate, not a demonstration of failure, and the paper says so rather than selling the null as equivalence — which is why shared/study-appraisal.md's fatal flag #3 was weighed and did not fire.

What does survive the isolating comparison is fat: −0.73 kg (−1.20 to −0.27), and the body-fat evidence is the only outcome in the review GRADE rated high certainty (weighted-vest-adds-three-quarters-of-a-kilo-of-fat-loss-to-exercise-you-already-do). Run the arithmetic against the 2.67 kg headline: roughly three quarters of the advertised fat loss belongs to the exercise and about a quarter belongs to the vest. The mechanism needs nothing exotic — the review's own reference list carries two studies of the metabolic cost of walking with a loaded vest (Gerhart 2020; Payne 2008).

⚑ Why this map exists when fifteen other candidates have been refused — the bar, scored. This vault set a four-part coining bar on beetroot-juice (2026-08-02), reused it on anthocyanins (2026-08-05) and applied it against lycopene (2026-08-14) and ginger (tonight): an isolating contrast · a machine-measured outcome · low risk of bias · a pre-specified dose. Weighted vest scores three of four, and it clears the hardest leg more cleanly than anything the vault has ingested. Beetroot cleared the isolation leg with nitrate-depleted juice; here the comparator is the identical exercise programme without the vest — the contrast was pre-planned, was run, and was published even though it undercuts the paper's own headline. Outcomes are DXA, not questionnaires. RoB 2.0 and GRADE were both applied, and there is no commercial funding anywhere in a category that is a retail product. ⚠ The failing leg is dose: the vest load is behind the Springer paywall and no dose-response was reported — the same leg stretching fails after 79 trials.

What this map does NOT claim. It does not claim vests do nothing for bone. Nine trials, ~41 participants each, one of which carries the entire isolating bone comparison, with fixed-effect pooling that narrows every interval it produces (the femoral-neck lower bound is 0.05; under random effects it would very likely cross zero). It does not claim anything about fracture, which nobody measured. And it says nothing about people with restricted exercise capacity — the authors flag that gap themselves.

⚠ For David (GOAL.md: least work for maximum benefit, adherence-first, knee/joint-safe, cost a tiebreaker): this one is gated by his knee, not by its evidence, and nothing enters the regimen. A vest adds axial load to every step, and the joint that absorbs it on level ground and especially on descent is the patellofemoral joint — the joint GOAL.md records as recovering from a March 2026 contusion, and the joint blood-flow-restriction exists in this vault to work around. The offer on the table is 0.73 kg of fat, against exercise he would have to already be doing, with an unproven skeletal return and a live joint constraint. ⚑ If the knee fully resolves this becomes a cheap, zero-time way to make existing walking harder — and at that point the missing load is a question for a clinician, not a number to guess.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • 2026-08-15 — global ripple across all other maps: one safety-relevant composition, no interaction, no safety_flags set. Walked every map against Wang X 2026. No pharmacological or physiological interaction is possible — a vest is a mechanical load, with no absorption, no metabolism and no pathway to interact with any supplement or medication in this vault. ⚠ What it does compose with is a joint constraint, and that composition is the reason this map opens with a caution rather than a recommendation. blood-flow-restriction exists in this vault precisely because GOAL.md records a patellar contusion and BFR is the low-joint-load route to strength; a weighted vest is the opposite trade — it raises joint load at every step to buy metabolic cost. No safety_flags: [interaction] is set, because a joint-loading caution against a personal constraint is a regimen-layer judgement (stage 05), not a cross-intervention interaction. Recorded here so stage 05 sees it.
  • ⚑ The bone shelf now holds four independent nulls that rhyme, and this is the fourth. Every time this vault controls for the training and asks what the add-on did to bone, the answer is nothing: creatine-bone-bmd-null (creatine + resistance training vs training alone, 5 RCTs, all sites p > 0.25), rt-intensity-does-not-change-bmd (raising training intensity does not move BMD), bone-density-network-ranks-sites-where-no-intervention-differed (a network ranking sites that did not differ), potassium-citrate-bone-nulls-honestly-underpowered (a supplement null, honestly labelled) — and now the vest. The reading across all five: loading the skeleton is what moves bone, and the accessories sold alongside the loading do not add to it. creatine-monohydrate's bone sub-thread and potassium-citrate both now have a mechanical-loading sibling that fails the same test.
  • The fat result composes with the pharmacology shelf, and the comparison is unflattering to neither. glp1-lean-mass-loss-magnitude and tirzepatide-largest-fat-and-lean-mass-loss record fat losses an order of magnitude larger, and record what they cost in lean tissue. 0.73 kg from a vest costs no lean mass, no prescription and no money beyond the vest — which is the relevant axis under GOAL.md rather than the size of the number. Nothing is double-counted at regimen time; neither enters the daily protocol.
  • Redundancy watch, not interaction. daily-steps owns incidental ambulatory activity and is the vault's base lever; a weighted vest is a modifier of that lever, not a separate one. If vest evidence accumulates, the risk is double-counting walking's benefit under two maps. Recorded now so a future run reads them together.
