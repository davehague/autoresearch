Intermittent fasting — nutrition

Status: emerging (2026-07-06) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. A network meta-analysis of RCTs shows intermittent fasting improves cardiometabolic risk factors (weight, blood pressure, glucose, lipids), modality-dependent — but every endpoint is a surrogate, the trials are mostly short, and a third are at high risk of bias. Opened at the bottom of the ladder: randomized signal on markers, no hard-outcome evidence, real adherence cost.

What we believe now

Intermittent fasting moves cardiometabolic risk markers, and which marker depends on the variant. In a network meta-analysis of 56 RCTs (3,965 participants): modified alternate-day fasting (mADF) ranked best for weight (−5.18 kg), blood pressure (−7.24 / −4.70 mmHg), and waist; time-restricted eating (TRE) ranked best for fasting glucose (−3.74 mg/dL); 5:2 periodic fasting best for LDL (−6.80 mg/dL).

Read honestly and low. These are surrogate endpoints — risk factors, not events or mortality — in mostly overweight/obese cohorts, from short trials, with 37.5% at high risk of bias. So the claim is "intermittent fasting improves risk markers," not "prevents cardiovascular disease" or "extends healthspan." The most decision-relevant nuance for GOAL.md's least-discomfort / adherence-first philosophy: TRE is the lowest-friction variant (an eating window, no calorie counting), while the more effective mADF/ADF protocols carry a larger hunger and social cost. There is no invented protocol here — the studied windows/schedules are the evidence, not a recommendation (shared/safety.md).

⚠ Updated 2026-07-21 — the field's largest synthesis lands, and it says the fasting is just the calorie restriction. Semnani-Azad 2025 (BMJ, network meta-analysis, 99 RCTs, n=6,582, >80% low risk of bias, GRADE-assessed) compared ADF, TRE, WDF, continuous energy restriction (CER), and ad libitum. Against ad libitum, weight loss sorted by restrictiveness (ADF −3.40 kg > WDF −2.36 > CER −2.11 > TRE −1.72). Against CER — the contrast that decides whether fasting adds anything — only ADF beat calorie restriction (−1.29 kg), and that edge vanished in trials ≥24 weeks; TRE and WDF were no different from CER (intermittent-fasting-matches-caloric-restriction-for-weight). The cardiometabolic markers refused to distinguish the strategies — HbA1c flat everywhere, glucose/lipids/BP/CRP largely indistinguishable from CER (intermittent-fasting-cardiometabolic-markers-match-caloric-restriction). The honest read: intermittent fasting is a delivery format for eating less; the eating-less is the effect. This lowers the stakes of the whole question — the format is a personal adherence choice, not a physiological one — and it reinforces the map's standing point that TRE gives up nothing meaningful to the harsher schedules. Kibret 2025's between-variant marker rankings (mADF best for weight, TRE for glucose) are mostly noise at this larger scale. Appraised reliable; the weight-equivalence concept graded strong, the markers suggestive. Still surrogate, still short, still emerging — a bigger, better synthesis of markers is still markers.

⚑ Updated 2026-07-24 — if the format is an adherence choice, then WHEN YOU TRAIN INSIDE IT is part of the format, and it has now been randomized. Höchsmann 2026 (registered, conflict-free crossover, n=35 healthy young adults, 30 min treadmill running at 65% VO₂peak) found the same session in the evening at the end of a 12-hour fast was followed by +400 kcal at the next meal (286–515) and +547 kcal over 24 hours (335–759) versus the morning (evening-fasted-exercise-followed-by-higher-24h-energy-intake, safety_flags: [interaction]). A 547-kcal day is larger than a compressed eating window plausibly removes — so a fasting schedule and aerobic training, two levers this vault scores separately, can cancel each other depending only on their order in the day. The obvious fix fails: shortening the fast to 6 h fixed the meal and not the day, and eating beforehand suppressed fat oxidation while leaving intake and appetite untouched (shortening-the-pre-exercise-fast-does-not-restore-24h-intake). And the arithmetic retires fasted cardio's rationale: the extra fat oxidised is 0.77 kcal/min ≈ 23 kcal across the bout, against 547 kcal of intake — about 1 to 24, the wrong way (fat-oxidation-gain-is-dwarfed-by-compensatory-intake). The load-bearing caveat, stated up front: the trial describes no non-exercise control arm, and people eat more in the evening regardless of exercise — so the vault carries the number and declines the word "compensatory." Acute, one day, one modality, 35 young adults. Appraised mixed, capped suggestive. Status held emerging.

  • 2026-07-21 — The field's largest synthesis reframes the map: fasting works by energy restriction, not timing; Status held emerging. Against continuous energy restriction, only ADF beat CER (−1.29 kg) and only in trials <24 weeks; TRE and WDF matched CER, and at ≥24 weeks no IF strategy differed from CER (intermittent-fasting-matches-caloric-restriction-for-weight). Intermittent fasting is a delivery format for eating less — which makes the choice among formats an adherence question and reinforces TRE as the lowest-friction option. Appraised reliable; weight-equivalence strong, markers suggestive. Surrogate endpoints, short trials → Status held emerging.

  • 2026-07-24 — The map's first graded INTERACTION, and it is with exercise timing; Status held emerging. Ingested Höchsmann 2026 (Am J Clin Nutr, randomized crossover, n=35 healthy young adults, fed subsample n=17; prospectively registered NCT06947824; Technical University of Munich; authors declare no commercial or financial relationships; Elsevier paywalled, pdf_unavailable after the full ladder). Three fasted conditions — morning after a 12 h fast, evening after a 12 h fast, evening after a 6 h fast — each 30 min treadmill running at 65% VO₂peak; intake by ad libitum test meal + weighed 24 h food records; substrate use by indirect calorimetry. Evening fasted vs morning fasted: acute intake +400 kcal (286–515), 24-hour intake +547 kcal (335–759), both p<0.001, with hunger, desire to eat and prospective consumption up and fullness down (evening-fasted-exercise-followed-by-higher-24h-energy-intake, safety_flags: [interaction], noted in both this map and zone-2-cardio per §6.5). The mitigation half-works and then fails: 12 h → 6 h attenuated acute intake but not 24-hour intake, and full pre-exercise feeding suppressed fat oxidation without touching intake or appetite (shortening-the-pre-exercise-fast-does-not-restore-24h-intake) — a study that stopped at the test meal would have reported a working fix. The surrogate arithmetic is the finding's sharpest edge: fat oxidation rose 0.77 kcal/min (0.37–1.17) — about 23 kcal across a 30-minute bout (vault-derived from the paper's rate × duration, not a stated figure) — against 547 kcal of intake (fat-oxidation-gain-is-dwarfed-by-compensatory-intake). The vault's fourth verified surrogate-versus-outcome dissociation this month and the first from a behaviour rather than a molecule, after creatine's brain loading, NR's doubled NAD⁺ and DHA's CSF entry. Appraised mixed — green: randomized crossover (each participant their own control), prospectively registered, conflict-free, objective calorimetry and weighed records rather than recall, large precise intervals, and the authors' own candidate mechanism tested and reported null; red: no non-exercise control condition described, so a circadian appetite gradient cannot be separated from exercise-induced compensation and the word "compensatory" is declined; one acute bout, a 24-hour horizon, no weight or body-composition outcome, 35 healthy young adults, one modality (running — the modality GOAL.md's knee constraint most disfavours), self-reported appetite in an unblindable protocol. Capped suggestive. Status emerging HELD. For David: a free scheduling observation, not a rule — if aerobic work and a compressed window are both in play, the earlier session is the one less likely to be followed by a large evening intake. No window, schedule or dose is invented here (shared/safety.md).

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠ INTERACTION (new 2026-07-25) — a second daily-schedule variable that can cancel the format, and this one is free to fix. Duan 2026 (Obesity Reviews, 30 studies, n=10,944; the two RCTs that randomised sleep restriction alongside calorie restriction) found restricted sleep produced consistently reduced fat-mass loss, one trial also with significantly lower total percentage weight loss (sleep-restriction-reduces-fat-loss-during-calorie-restriction, safety_flags: [interaction], noted here and in sleep-duration and glp-1). This map's own 2026-07-21 reframing makes the point sharp: if intermittent fasting is a delivery format for eating less (intermittent-fasting-matches-caloric-restriction-for-weight), then a sleep deficit running alongside the format changes which tissue the deficit removes. Efficacy-defeating rather than hazardous, it enters no benefit score, and it is raised on two trials because shared/safety.md makes interaction evidence asymmetric. It is the SECOND such interaction on this map in five days, after evening-fasted-exercise-followed-by-higher-24h-energy-intake (+547 kcal/24 h from evening fasted exercise) — and neither is yet carried in the regimen, because stage 05 triggers on a Status move and none has occurred. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md for the next refresh's across-protocol check. The practical shape of both: the fasting window is not the only schedule that matters — when you train and how much you sleep can undo it.
  • ⚠ INTERACTION (new 2026-07-24) — a fasting window × aerobic-training-timing interaction, graded and noted in both maps. Evening aerobic exercise performed at the end of a 12-hour fast was followed by +547 kcal over 24 hours versus the same session in the morning (Höchsmann 2026, randomized crossover) (evening-fasted-exercise-followed-by-higher-24h-energy-intake, safety_flags: [interaction]). This map's whole current claim is that fasting is a delivery format for eating less — so a schedule that is reliably followed by several hundred extra kilocalories is an efficacy-defeating combination of two interventions the vault otherwise scores independently. Same class as cwi-post-workout-blunts-hypertrophy (cold after lifting) and bfr-occlusion-reduces-walking-volume-and-comfort (cuffs on the walk): it gates naive co-recommendation and enters no benefit score, per shared/safety.md. Cross-noted on zone-2-cardio; referenced on daily-steps (untested modality) and glp-1 (the pharmacological route to the same appetite target). Held loosely on one axis: with no resting arm in the trial, how much of the effect is exercise rather than evening is undetermined — which argues for the flag, not against it, since shared/safety.md makes interaction evidence deliberately asymmetric.

  • ⚠ Timing tension — protein-target and resistance-training (eased 2026-07-07). A compressed eating window can crowd out the ~1.6 g/kg/day protein target. But the protein map's new timing/distribution evidence reframes this as a total-intake problem, not a meal-spacing one: a large single protein bolus is used, not wasted (Trommelen 2023), and protein timing/distribution is null for lean mass (Casuso 2025). So the fix is simply hitting the daily total inside the window — you don't also need to spread it across many meals. Fasted training + very delayed post-workout protein remains a minor consideration, but overall this is a manageable timing trade-off, not a safety flag — logged in both directions, not fabricated into a graded safety_flags concept.
  • ⚠ Clinician-only intake-suppression overlap — glp-1 (gap now closed 2026-07-07). Intermittent fasting and the compounded GLP-1 David is weighing (GOAL.md) both suppress food intake; combining an appetite-suppressing prescription with a fasting schedule compounds under-eating / nutrient-adequacy risk. The vault has now ingested GLP-1 evidence (glp-1, Tang 2025) — but GLP-1 is a prescription, so this stays a clinician-conversation item, flagged in both maps, never a self-stacked protocol.
