Homocysteine-lowering with B vitamins (B6 · B9 folic acid · B12) — supplements

Status: consolidating (2026-08-25) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review, and coined on a NULL, which this vault has refused to do before. The refusal precedent is real and the argument for overriding it is set out below. Coined from a Cochrane systematic review of 15 randomised trials, 71,422 participants, GRADE high-quality on every primary outcome, with trial sequential analyses that reached the futility boundary — the rarest thing in this vault, a question that has been closed rather than merely left open. ⚑ It was ingested because docs/open-gaps-log.md asked for it by name on 2026-08-23, after the folic-acid candidate was refused and the log noted that the literature which would actually decide the folate question had never been searched. git is the undo.

Sub-threads: none — one claim (does lowering homocysteine prevent cardiovascular events?) whose coronary and mortality arms are settled null and whose stroke arm is a live scope condition on that same claim, not a separate strand. [reviewed 2026-08-25 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

Elevated homocysteine predicts cardiovascular disease. B vitamins lower homocysteine. Lowering homocysteine does not prevent heart attacks and does not prevent death — and the sequential analysis says no further trial is going to change that.

Across 15 randomised trials and 71,422 participants published 2002–2015, with nine trials (60%) at low risk of bias and follow-up from one to 7.3 years, vitamins B6, B9 (folic acid) and B12 — alone or in combination — versus placebo:

OutcomeResultTrials / NGRADE
Myocardial infarction7.14% vs 5.96%, RR 1.02 (0.95–1.10), I²=0%12 / 46,699High
Death from any cause11.70% vs 12.29%, RR 1.01 (0.96–1.06), I²=0%11 / 44,817High
Serious adverse events (cancer)8.3% vs 8.5%, RR 1.07 (1.00–1.14), I²=0%8 / 35,788High
Stroke4.26% vs 5.06%, RR 0.90 (0.82–0.99), I²=8%10 / 44,224High

What makes the nulls unusually strong. This ledger has refused nine underpowered nulls sold as equivalence, so a null earns its weight here or it does not get it. This one earns it three ways. (1) The trial sequential analyses reached FUTILITY on myocardial infarction and on all-cause death — "the cumulative Z-curve reached futility area which means that no more trials are needed." A 10% relative risk reduction has been ruled out, not merely unobserved. (2) I² = 0% across twelve and eleven trials spanning two decades, three continents, five B-vitamin regimens and both fortified and unfortified food supplies — and the review used random-effects models anyway, borrowing nothing from the heterogeneity statistic. (3) Publication bias was formally tested and absent (Harbord P=0.88 / Peters P=0.86 for myocardial infarction; 0.95 / 0.82 for death). See b-vitamins-do-not-prevent-heart-attack-or-death-and-the-sequential-analysis-says-stop.

⚑ The one exception, stated with every clause it needs. Stroke is the only outcome that moved: RR 0.90 (0.82–0.99), Bayes factor 5.84. The review then argues against its own positive finding, and the argument is good: the cumulative Z-curve "crossed the upper conventional alpha of 5%, but … crossed no the trial sequential alpha-spending monitoring boundaries." It clears the ordinary bar and not the stricter sequential bar the same review used to close the nulls. In absolute terms the effect is 51 strokes per 1,000 becoming 46 per 1,000 (95% CI 42 to 50) over one to 7.3 years, with a relative reduction whose interval runs from 1% to 18% (the-homocysteine-stroke-benefit-clears-the-p-value-and-not-the-sequential-boundary).

And the trial carrying that signal is a different question in a different population. CSPPT — 20,702 Chinese hypertensive adults without prior cardiovascular disease, in a country with no mandatory folate fortification — compared enalapril 10 mg + folic acid 0.8 mg against enalapril 10 mg alone. Stroke RR 0.79 (0.68–0.93), absolute risk reduction 0.7%, NNTB 143 (85 to 428) over 5.4 years, fragility index 23. Both arms received an ACE inhibitor, so the design cannot separate a folate effect from a drug interaction; the concept is flagged safety_flags: [interaction] accordingly, per shared/safety.md's asymmetry rule. The review states the generalisation problem itself: "it is unknown whether such treatment would benefit a non-Chinese population." (the-one-large-folic-acid-stroke-win-is-an-unfortified-population-on-an-ace-inhibitor.)

⚠ The number that contradicts the conclusion, and it is in the safety row. Serious adverse events — predominantly cancer — RR 1.07, 95% CI 1.00 to 1.14, in 35,788 people, with the lower bound sitting exactly on the null, reported in the abstract with the same phrase used for RR 1.02 (0.95–1.10): "there were no differences." The trial sequential analysis for cancer also reached futility, so this borderline estimate is as settled as it will get. It is not a demonstration of harm — the raw percentages run the other way (8.3% vs 8.5%) — but a supplement with demonstrated zero benefit and a harm interval anchored at unity is a different object from an inert one (a-serious-adverse-event-estimate-whose-lower-bound-is-exactly-one).

⚑ Why this map exists, against the vault's own refusal precedent. On 2026-08-24 the exercise-timing candidate was refused with the reasoning that "a map coined on 'timing does not appear to matter much' would have one bullet and no trajectory." That precedent is correct and it does not apply here, for three reasons worth stating so a future run does not have to re-derive them. (a) That null was mixed-appraised, at suggestive strength, with no risk-of-bias table. This one is reliable, GRADE high on four outcomes, with sequential analyses that close the question. (b) That null had no trajectory. This one is a trajectory: a major hypothesis, fifteen trials across thirteen years, three Cochrane updates, and a formal demonstration that the accumulation is complete. (c) That null had no scope condition. This one has a live one — stroke, in unfortified populations, alongside antihypertensive therapy — which is a claim a future source can confirm or refute. Against the four-part coining bar the candidate scores 4/4: isolating contrast (placebo-controlled RCTs of the vitamins alone), hard machine-adjudicated outcome (myocardial infarction, stroke, death), low risk of bias (9/15 low, GRADE high, publication bias tested and absent), and pre-specified dose (stated per trial; CSPPT's folic acid 0.8 mg). It is the highest-scoring candidate this vault has assessed.

Status is consolidating and not consensus. Per shared/evidence-model.md, consolidating requires ≥2 independent groups, high-tier evidence, a real effect, replication, and no live rebuttal — all satisfied within the review's fifteen independent trials, where the "real effect" is a real, well-bounded answer. consensus is a higher bar and a map does not open there on its first source. It is not fading, because the stroke exception is alive and the mechanism has not been refuted.

For David (GOAL.md). He is 43, has no cardiovascular disease, is not on an ACE inhibitor, and eats from a folate-fortified food supply (mandatory US fortification since 1998). Every clause of the surviving exception fails to apply to him. A B-complex or folic acid supplement taken for cardiovascular or longevity reasons has GRADE-high randomised evidence of no benefit on heart attack or death, plus a serious-adverse-event estimate of RR 1.07 with a lower bound at 1.00. This belongs in Considered & NOT doing, and it is among the better-evidenced entries there. ⚠ This says nothing about folate for any other indication, and nothing about diagnosed deficiency, malabsorption, pregnancy, or methotrexate co-therapy — different questions, none of them answered here. No dose is set; the 0.8 mg figure is CSPPT's, reported because CSPPT used it. Not medical advice.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚑ The completed surrogate test that biological-age-clocks is still waiting for. That map holds the rule that a biomarker qualifies as a surrogate only if it (1) predicts the outcome, (2) responds to intervention, and (3) moving it moves the outcome — and that legs 1 and 2 are jointly insufficient. The epigenetic-clock literature has now done leg 1 (Vetter 2026, badly) and leg 2 (Sehgal 2026, barely), and nobody has done leg 3. Homocysteine did all three. It passed the first two and failed the third, in 71,422 people, at GRADE high. Any reader tempted to treat a clock as a target should read this map first.
  • The same shape as multivitamins's central finding, one tier harder. That map holds "a daily multivitamin does not extend life" from a 19-meta-analysis umbrella review plus COSMOS (multivitamin-no-cvd-cancer-mortality-benefit-cosmos, n=21,442, null on CVD, cancer and mortality). This is the mechanism-targeted version of the same negative, with a sequential analysis closing it. ⚑ Note the overlap the reader should be told about: B6, B9 and B12 are constituents of most multivitamins. The two maps are not independent evidence — they are the same nutrients tested with and without a rationale.
  • A shared trial with omega-3. SU.FOL.OM3 appears in this review's fifteen and was a 2×2 factorial testing B vitamins AND omega-3 in people with prior cardiovascular events. Both arms were null on their primary. Two of this vault's supplement maps therefore rest partly on the same participants — worth knowing before treating them as independent corroboration of a general "supplements don't prevent events" claim.
  • ⚠ Interaction, flagged not resolved — folic acid alongside antihypertensive therapy. CSPPT's entire stroke benefit exists only in co-administration with enalapril, and no trial isolated folic acid in that population. Per shared/safety.md a credible signal is enough to flag and interaction evidence is deliberately asymmetric. No vault map holds antihypertensive drugs, so the flag is carried on the concept and recorded here rather than mirrored into a map that does not exist. The adjacent primary-prevention maps are statins-primary-prevention (the other pharmacological one), exercise-for-blood-pressure (the behavioural blood-pressure lever) and aspirin (the other cheap over-the-counter cardiovascular-prevention agent whose benefit narrowed under scrutiny). A future run ingesting an antihypertensive source should re-read this.
  • The precedent that sent the vault here. protein-target's 2026-08-23 run refused a folic-acid candidate on the grounds that its founding source was appraised unreliable, and docs/open-gaps-log.md noted that folate lowering homocysteine is settled biochemistry while "whether lowering homocysteine does anything for a person is a separate question." That question is now answered and this map holds the answer. The folic-acid candidate remains refused — this map supersedes the need for it.
