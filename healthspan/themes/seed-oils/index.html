Seed oils / dietary linoleic acid (omega-6) — nutrition

Status: emerging (2026-11-26) — ⚑ auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. The vault's first map on dietary omega-6/linoleic-acid intake and named seed oils, distinct from omega-3's fish-oil (EPA/DHA) supplementation evidence. Founded on the largest umbrella review this specific, highly-online-contested topic has yet produced (33 appraised syntheses). Randomized evidence shows no uniform harmful or beneficial effect on mortality or cardiovascular events; a small subgroup split hints at a possible harm signal for pure linoleic acid specifically, but on far too few trials to call anything.

Sub-threads: none — one map, one question so far: does omega-6/linoleic-acid intake from seed oils affect cardiometabolic and mortality outcomes. [reviewed 2026-11-26 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

Seed oils / dietary omega-6 (linoleic acid) show no uniform harmful or beneficial effect on hard outcomes, at low certainty. An umbrella review of 33 systematic reviews/meta-analyses (AMSTAR 2 + ROBIS appraised) finds randomized evidence increasing omega-6 intake has little or no effect on all-cause mortality (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.88–1.12) or cardiovascular events (RR 0.97, 0.81–1.15), both low certainty (seed-oils-no-uniform-effect-on-mortality-or-cardiovascular-events-clementesuarez-2026) — directly undercutting the strong "seed oils cause chronic disease" claim this topic is known for. A hypothesis-generating subgroup split within that same randomized evidence found pure linoleic-acid trials show more cardiovascular events than mixed omega-6/omega-3 trials (RR 1.45 vs. 0.80) — but on only two trials per arm, explicitly flagged by the review's own authors as hypothesis-generating, not a finding (seed-oils-la-specific-trials-vs-mixed-trials-hypothesis-generating-split-clementesuarez-2026). Prospectively, dietary/circulating omega-6 associations generally run inverse (protective) — mostly from overlapping cohorts, so not independent confirmation. LA-rich oils reliably lower LDL cholesterol when replacing butter (sunflower −0.34 mmol/L per 10% of energy) without raising inflammatory markers — uncontroversial mainstream lipidology, not in tension with anything above.

Why emerging, not higher or lower. The founding source is a single (if large and rigorously appraised) umbrella review, not prospectively registered, whose own highest-tier randomized evidence is not independent (4 of 6 "higher confidence" syntheses share trials from one WHO-commissioned program). The headline finding is a real, low-certainty null on hard outcomes — genuinely useful (it rules out a uniform harm claim), but a null from a thin evidence base doesn't earn consolidating any more than a positive finding from one would.

Not yet actionable for David. No specific oil, dose, or replacement rule is established well enough to change a cooking-oil or dietary choice; this map exists to track the question honestly, not to settle it.
