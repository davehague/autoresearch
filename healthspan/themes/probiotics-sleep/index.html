Probiotics for sleep quality — sleep

Status: emerging (2026-10-16) — auto-coined this run, flagged for human review. Coined by a single meta-analysis (Ren 2026, Frontiers in Nutrition, 13 RCTs, n=890) finding probiotic supplementation modestly improves Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index and Insomnia Severity Index scores, with no effect on daytime sleepiness or cortisol. The vault's first source on the gut-brain axis as a sleep lever — a candidate addition to the wind-down supplement shelf alongside melatonin, l-theanine, chamomile, and magnesium-glycinate.

Sub-threads: none — one question (does probiotic supplementation improve sleep, and by how much) asked once, from one meta-analysis. No separable strands exist yet. [reviewed 2026-10-16 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

Probiotics modestly improve self-reported sleep quality and insomnia severity — a small effect, on self-report instruments only, from a mechanism independent of every other substance already on this vault's sleep shelf. Pooling 13 RCTs (n=890), probiotic supplementation lowered PSQI by MD −0.59 (95% CI −0.83 to −0.35, p<0.001, I²=46%) and ISI by MD −0.86 (−1.60 to −0.12, p=0.02, I²=26%). Epworth Sleepiness Scale and serum cortisol did not move.

Read against the vault's existing wind-down cluster, this does not clear the bar that cluster has already failed: cbt-insomnia records that the entire supplement shelf (tryptophan, L-theanine, GABA, magnesium, zinc, vitamin D, omega-3, ashwagandha — 28 RCTs) pools to PSQI −0.70, entirely below the ~3-point minimal important difference (supplement-sleep-effect-small-and-below-clinical-threshold). Probiotics' −0.59 sits inside that same below-threshold range. The vault's best-evidenced sleep intervention remains free: exercise + CBT-I at PSQI −2.80, at moderate certainty — roughly five times this effect.

Why emerging, and why this map opened at the bottom of the shelf rather than higher. One meta-analysis, no objective sleep measure anywhere in the pooled literature (no actigraphy, no polysomnography), a population pooling healthy adults and poor sleepers without a reported subgroup split, and no strain/dose/duration specified. The effect is real and statistically significant, but thin by the standards this vault already applies to the rest of the sleep shelf.

What this costs: whatever a probiotic supplement costs, for an effect below the clinical threshold. No strain, dose, or duration is recommended (shared/safety.md) — none is specified in the source, and none is invented here.
