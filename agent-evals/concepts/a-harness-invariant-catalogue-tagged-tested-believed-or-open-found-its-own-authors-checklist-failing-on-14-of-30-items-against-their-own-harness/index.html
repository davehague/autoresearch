A harness-invariant catalogue tagged Tested/Believed/Open found its own author's checklist failing on 14 of 30 items against their own harness

Grounded in anatomy-of-an-agent-harness-decisions-invariants-corner-cases; belongs to design-for-verifiability and build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

An agent harness makes dozens of decisions in code that are rarely written down: how it builds each request, what the model may do without asking, how it stores conversation, how it recovers from failure, what it shows a person afterward. This catalogue writes 30 of those decisions down as cards across six groups, each card carrying an invariant, the corner cases that break it, a test, and an evidence tag of Tested, Believed, or Open — 56 of 83 total invariant clauses Tested, 12 Believed, 15 Open. Applying the resulting checklist to the author's own harness, scored by its own builder with no second rater, found 11 of 30 items holding, 14 not holding, and 5 untested.

The evidence-tag vocabulary is this vault's own strength field — strong / suggestive / single-study — reinvented independently by a harness builder auditing their own invariants rather than by this vault grading someone else's claims, and 15 of 83 clauses admitted as genuinely open (not quietly assumed Tested) is more candor about a harness's own edges than most build-vs-adopt entries in this theme offer. The self-audit result carries weight for the reason this vault treats self-reported negative findings as credible: the author's own checklist, scored by the author, found fewer than half of the author's own 30 items pass, which damages the source's own case rather than flattering it. That null-finding instinct pairs with letting-the-agent-prove-the-specification-unsatisfiable-or-the-reference-wrong-is-grader-validation-that-runs-during-curation's adversarial-against-oneself move, here applied to a harness-design checklist instead of a grading specification. The paper's explicit refusal to rank or compare against other harnesses' cost or quality marks it as a different artifact than most of this theme's entries: a checklist for auditing a harness against its own stated invariants, not a leaderboard entry to weigh against HarnessFlow or Co-Sight.

Bounds. Abstract-level read; single author, single harness, no second rater on the self-audit, and the author states the catalogue is not independent of the ten other harnesses it drew patterns from while building it. The full 30-card contents and the six failure cause classes are not given at this level.
