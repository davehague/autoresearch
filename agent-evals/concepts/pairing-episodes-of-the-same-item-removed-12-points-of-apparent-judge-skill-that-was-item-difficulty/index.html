Pairing episodes of the same item removed 12 points of apparent judge skill that was item difficulty

Grounded in competence-not-accuracy-judge-gate-diagnostic; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

Judge agreement computed by pooling across items is inflated by a channel that has nothing to do with grading: a judge that reads only how hard a question is, and never looks at the answer, scores above chance. Hard items produce more failures and lower scores; easy items produce the reverse; the pooled statistic rewards a judge for sensing difficulty. The identifying quantity is the within-question estimate, which pairs a passing and a failing episode of the same item and asks which the judge scored higher.

Measured, same judge and same episodes:

Factual QA0.8550.735+0.12
Research math0.4570.489−0.03

Twelve points of a judge's apparent skill on factual QA was difficulty sensing. On mathematics the two agree, so the confound is not a constant — it is large where the item pool spans a wide difficulty range and small where it does not, which means it cannot be corrected for by a rule of thumb.

This is the second correction this vault has had to make to its own reasoning about judge agreement in a month. The first was class balance: raw-agreement-is-only-admissible-near-balanced-classes-so-an-imbalanced-eval-has-to-report-judge-precision-and-recall-separately established that at a 3% defect rate a judge that passes everything scores 97% agreement. This one is independent of that and has the same consequence — a single pooled number over a heterogeneous item set can be high for reasons unrelated to the judge doing its job. Between them, the requirements for an admissible judge-acceptance number are now three: balanced classes or precision and recall reported separately, within-item pairing, and a stated competence floor.

The procedure is mechanical and it is nearly free for anyone already fixing the noise floor. Within-item pairing needs multiple episodes per item — which is exactly what running your suite three times produces. A team adopting the repeat-run discipline this vault has been asking for since 2026-08-20 gets the un-confounded judge estimator as a by-product of the same runs.

Bounds. One judge, two tasks with within-question strata, low-hundreds episode counts; GPQA had a single pass per item and so admits no within-question estimate at all, which is a reminder that most published judge-agreement numbers cannot be recomputed this way after the fact. The confound share is reported as +0.12 to +0.19 on free-text factual QA and ≈0 on mathematics in one study — two points on a curve, not a calibration.
