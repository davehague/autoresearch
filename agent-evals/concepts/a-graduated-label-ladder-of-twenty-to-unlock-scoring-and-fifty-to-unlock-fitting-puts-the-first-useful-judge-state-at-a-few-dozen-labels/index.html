A graduated label ladder of twenty to unlock scoring and fifty to unlock fitting puts the first useful judge state at a few dozen labels

Grounded in eugeneyan-aligneval-building-an-eval-app; belongs to what-an-eval-costs-to-run.

20 labelled rows unlock evaluation mode; 50–100 is the recommendation "before writing criteria and running evals"; 50 labelled samples minimum before optimisation mode unlocks. Labels are binary throughout, "it leads to more accurate data, takes less time."

It is the first sizing figure here expressed as a gate rather than a target, and the ordering is the finding. Every other number in this theme is a threshold you should reach. These say what each increment buys: 20 to score a judge at all, 50 to fit one, 50–100 before the criteria are worth writing down. That last clause inverts the sequence a team would guess — criteria come after the first fifty labels, because labelling is how you discover what the criteria are. It agrees with the sequencing already recorded in four-weeks-to-a-working-eval-harness-is-the-first-build-figure-covering-criteria-labels-judge-alignment-and-runner-as-one-number, where the time went into labels and judge alignment and the runner was the last and smallest piece.

Together with tonight's other practitioner it puts the first useful judge state an order of magnitude below the figure this vault has been quoting. thirty-examples-with-a-saturation-rule-is-the-smallest-judge-dataset-here-and-it-sorts-four-conflicting-label-counts-into-three-jobs starts at ~30 and stops on saturation; this gates a working evaluator at 20. Against the 100+ entry fee in an-llm-judge-costs-a-hundred-labels-plus-weekly-maintenance-so-it-is-a-dependency-rather-than-an-artifact, the two smaller numbers are not in conflict with it — they price discovery, and 100 prices a judge you would ship. Two independent practitioners now agree that a few dozen labels is enough to learn something, which matters for this theme because the reason teams never start is that the stated entry fee sounds like a week.

The binary-label justification is a cost argument rather than a rigour argument here. Elsewhere in this vault binary labels are chosen because they are the only labels two people reproduce (human-human-agreement-runs-from-kappa-0-2-to-0-97-depending-on-the-task-so-the-agreement-floor-is-a-property-of-the-task-not-of-people). This adds throughput and cognitive load to the case, which is the reason a labeller actually gives, and it is the reason that predicts whether the procedure survives contact with a busy expert.

Bounds. These are one person's product decisions in his own app, not measurements — no experiment shows that 20 is sufficient or that 50 is necessary, and the author's own optimisation run at 25 per split is reported as too small to trust. Two-year-old model generation. What transfers is the ladder's shape; the specific integers are a design choice.
