Structural scanning and LLM review of the same skill correlate at Spearman 0.14 so the two gates are not substitutes

Grounded in aces-skill-lift-continuous-skill-evaluation; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

Across 145 real enterprise skills, ACES found that "structural scanning and LLM-based assessment measure distinct facets (Spearman correlation of 0.14)."

This is a measured redundancy check between two gates that are already in production, and it comes back negative. Organisations running skill or plugin catalogues typically pass a candidate through a structural/security scan and an LLM review of its definition, then treat a pass on either as evidence of quality. At ρ = 0.14 those two rankings are close to independent. Three consequences follow immediately, and none of them requires believing anything else in the paper: a skill that clears one gate carries almost no information about the other; the routine cost-saving move of deleting the LLM review because the scanner already ran deletes an uncorrelated signal, not a duplicate one; and any single-gate "approved" label in a catalogue is a much weaker claim than its readers assume.

The deeper point is that neither gate is measuring the deployment question. Both inspect the artifact — its shape, its wording, its declared permissions. Lift is a property of executed behaviour, and this vault's recurring finding is that an instrument which never observes the behaviour cannot be assumed to predict it. The same pattern has now landed on graders (nine-hundred-thirty-one-human-annotated-trajectories-scoring-six-published-judges-is-the-first-population-level-grader-validation-here), on deterministic gates (a-shipped-similarity-gate-caught-zero-of-fifty-six-meaning-breaking-edits-and-never-exceeded-0-700-balanced-accuracy-across-ninety-cells), and on trajectory-derived diagnostics (a-read-derived-coverage-score-returned-a-perfect-1-000-in-the-condition-where-nothing-was-available-because-the-agent-filled-the-hole-itself). Skill-admission gates are the fourth instrument class to be audited by an outsider and found not to measure what its users think.

The cheap generalisation for anyone running more than one gate: correlate them before you trust either. Two checks over the same population produce two rankings. Computing the rank correlation between them costs nothing beyond data you already have, and it answers a question every pipeline owner has implicitly assumed the answer to — are these two gates the same gate. A high correlation means you can drop one and save the money. A low one, like this, means your pipeline has two partial orderings and no total one, and the honest presentation is both scores side by side rather than a merged verdict.

What it does not establish. ρ = 0.14 says the gates disagree; it does not say which is better, and the paper's own framing is that neither tracks lift. A third axis exists that neither covers at all — the security probe categories recorded from skilltester-paired-utility-and-security-probes — so the correct reading is not "replace both with lift" but that skill admission is a multi-axis decision currently being reported as one label.
