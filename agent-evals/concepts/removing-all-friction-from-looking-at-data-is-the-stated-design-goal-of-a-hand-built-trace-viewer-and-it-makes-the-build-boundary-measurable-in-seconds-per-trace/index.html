Removing all friction from looking at data is the stated design goal of a hand-built trace viewer, and it makes the build boundary measurable in seconds per trace

Grounded in hamel-your-ai-product-needs-evals; belongs to build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

Level 2 splits into logging traces, viewing traces, and automated critique. Logging is bought — LangSmith named, with arize, humanloop, openllmetry and honeyhive in footnotes. Viewing is built: Rechat's own tool in Shiny for Python, with CRM links, feature categorization, filters, and outputs made editable so a reviewer can curate while reading. Samples are worked "25-50 examples at a time." The stated principle: "You must remove all friction from the process of looking at data," alongside "you can never stop looking at data — no free lunch exists."

This is the store-versus-viewer boundary the vault already holds, with a better justification than the one it has been carrying. The ledger has treated custom annotation tooling as a preference backed by a claimed ~10x iteration speed and a crossover at annotator coordination (custom-annotation-tooling-is-claimed-to-iterate-ten-times-faster-and-the-crossover-to-off-the-shelf-is-annotator-coordination). Those are outcome claims. This is a design goal with an implied measurement: every click, filter, tab-switch and missing bit of context between a reviewer and the next trace is a tax on the one activity that never stops.

That reframes the boundary from "which tool" to a number nobody here has collected. Seconds per trace, on your own tooling, on your own domain. It is trivially measurable — time a reviewer working a batch of 25 to 50 — and it converts the build-vs-buy question into arithmetic against the ~10x claim rather than into a preference. It also explains why the boundary sits where it does: the routing surface (who reviews what, across how many people) has no per-trace friction, and the viewing surface is nothing but per-trace friction.

The domain-specific features are the friction, itemized. CRM links, feature categorization, filters, editable outputs. None of these is hard to build and none can be supplied generically, because each encodes what a reviewer of this product needs to see next to a trace. That is the same structure as the generic-versus-specific boundary the vault drew for eval skills — the generic layer knows the procedure, not the criterion — applied to a UI instead of a prompt.

Against it, from the same author, later. an-agent-generated-annotation-interface-attacks-the-reason-teams-build-their-own-rather-than-the-boundary ships a skill that generates the annotation UI. If a generated viewer can carry the domain-specific affordances, the friction argument survives and the build it yourself conclusion does not.

Bounds. One product, one team, 2024. No friction measurement is actually reported — the principle is stated, not quantified, and this atom's contribution is that it could be. The 25–50 batch size is an example rather than a calibrated figure. Author now sells an evals course, and the post predates most of the annotation tooling now on the adopt side, so the build recommendation is made against a weaker buy option than exists today.
