Seventy-three percent of enterprise skills showed positive lift so the mean is averaging over a quarter that did not help

Grounded in aces-skill-lift-continuous-skill-evaluation; belongs to what-the-headline-number-averages-away.

"Across 947 paired evaluations, mean composite lift reached 0.2134, with positive outcomes in 72.8% of cases."

The complement is the actionable number and it is not the one in the abstract: roughly one skill in four in a live enterprise catalogue did not help. A mean of 0.2134 is computed over both populations, so it simultaneously describes skills that earned their place and skills that cost tokens and latency for nothing — or worse, since a composite scale reporting only "positive / not positive" does not say how far below zero the failures went. No catalogue owner wants the mean. They want the list of the 27% and a keep-or-drop decision on each, and the framework has already computed exactly that per skill.

This is the vault's headline-averaging argument in its cheapest possible setting. Elsewhere the fix costs a rerun or a new grader: pass^20 beside pass@1 needs twenty runs, the soft/hard split needs a second scoring pass (soft-f1-of-0-363-against-hard-f1-of-0-133-is-one-run-scored-for-record-quality-and-for-hierarchical-completeness), the retrieval-depth reversal needs two task families. Here the per-skill lift already exists as a computed intermediate. Publishing the distribution instead of the mean costs one chart and zero additional compute, which makes this the least defensible instance of averaging recorded in this vault.

And a mean is the wrong summary for this quantity on its own terms, because lift is signed and bimodal by construction. A catalogue contains skills that reliably help and skills that were a good idea nobody validated; those are two populations, not one distribution with a centre. Reporting their average is the same error as reporting a single accuracy across a benchmark whose subsets move in opposite directions — which this vault holds as the strongest form of the theme, where retrieval depth helps QA and hurts embodied tasks. The remedy is identical in both cases: report the split, not the centre.

The sharper version of the complaint is that the sign convention may be hiding the worst cases entirely. A comparably designed scoring rule from another team floors at zero, so a skill that broke a task the baseline had solved is indistinguishable from a skill that failed a hard task (a-comparative-utility-rule-that-floors-at-zero-cannot-distinguish-a-useless-skill-from-a-harmful-one). ACES reports a signed composite and is therefore in a position to name the harmful tail; it just reports the average instead.
