This is the first localisation result in this vault that wins on both axes at once. Every previous one bought accuracy with tokens: decomposed judging, panels, step-level credit signals, judge-with-run-directory — each traded spend for quality, and this vault's standing complaint has been that none of them quoted the price. Here the price is negative. Suspicious-Node spends more calls in absolute terms (419–466 against 254–400) and returns them 1.9× to 3.6× over in repairs per thousand calls, because a targeted intervention replays a validated prefix instead of regenerating the whole trajectory.

The reframing matters more than the numbers. Step-level diagnosis has been filed here as a diagnostic luxury — the thing you do when you need to know why, after you have already tried the cheap thing. On this corpus it is the cheap thing. If your failures are late in the trajectory, re-running from the top is the expensive option, because you pay to regenerate everything that already worked.

The controls are what make it readable, and they are the part to copy. Random-Node and Last-Node get the same single selective-replay budget with no symptom evidence and reach 3.73% and 1.31%. So selective replay by itself explains almost none of the gain — the symptom signal does. Suspicious-Node beats unguided rerun on all three frameworks after within-MAS Holm correction, so it is not one architecture carrying the average. The authors are explicit about the residual ambiguity: target selection and repair guidance were evaluated jointly, so the improvement is not attributed to either in isolation.

It sits directly against the vault's most sceptical entry on this thread. step-level-credit-signals-did-not-beat-their-own-shuffled-controls-when-audited-against-executed-replay found step-attribution signals failing against shuffled controls under causal audit. This does not overturn that, and the authors do not claim it does — their conclusion says localised symptoms guide effective intervention without uniquely identifying a root cause. The two are compatible and the combination is the actual finding: a step signal can be worth acting on while being wrong about which step caused the failure. Utility and attribution are separate properties, and this vault has been conflating them.

Single study, one model family at temperature 0.00, benchmark-derived Web tasks. The cost figures are API calls, not tokens or dollars — a call is not a fixed price, and a replayed prefix consumes no model calls but does consume the recording infrastructure that produced it, which is never costed. The comparison is deliberately conservative in the proposed method's disfavour on budget (1 attempt vs 3) and silently in its favour on infrastructure (a recorded trace must already exist).
