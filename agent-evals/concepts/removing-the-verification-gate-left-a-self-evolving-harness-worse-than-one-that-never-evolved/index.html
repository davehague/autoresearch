Removing the verification gate left a self-evolving harness at 0.33 against 0.43 for one that never evolved, so an ungated self-improving harness does not plateau — it decays

Grounded in opsharness-self-evolving-rca-harness; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

Three variants of one self-evolving RCA harness ran in continuous-diagnosis mode over the training split of two benchmarks, split into 12 windows with one evolution per window, mean top-1 accuracy per window, repeated across four backbones. Final-window A@1, averaged over backbones:

VariantFinal-window A@1
Full (evolve + dual gate)0.83
No-evolve (static substrate)0.43
No-verify (evolve, gate removed)0.33

An ungated self-improving harness ends up worse than one that never learned anything. It does not plateau, does not merely fail to improve — it climbs early as the first proposals help, then stalls and regresses below the static baseline. This vault has been asking every harness-search entrant since 2026-08-25 for its overfitting control. Here is the counterfactual, run: the gate is worth 50 points of final accuracy, which is more than the evolution it gates.

The mechanism is a single worked example and it generalises immediately. In one cycle the evolver saw repeated failures on network-type cases and proposed up-weighting network anomalies during reasoning. That helped the source cases. In the next window it mislabelled CPU- and memory-type cases that had previously been correct, because the change now surfaced network noise as the cause. Every accepted proposal is fitted to the window that motivated it, and pays for it in the next window on a class of cases that was previously fine. Accuracy measured only on the cases that prompted the change cannot see that; a held-out set can.

What the gate is, concretely — two conditions, and the outer one is where the 50 points live.

  • Inner gate, on the mined source cases: no accuracy regression, no material cost increase (ΔC ≤ max(0.05·C, 1)), and a strict improvement in at least one objective. The strict clause keeps out no-ops; the cost band tolerates a small token increase but rejects accuracy bought with runaway exploration. That cost term is the first acceptance rule in this vault that prices the change as well as scoring it.
  • Outer gate, on a held-out testbed: non-regression only. Not improvement — just "does not break anything else."

Promotion requires both, and is atomic. A failed proposal returns to the evolver with the failing cases as fresh negatives, up to three rounds; still-failing proposals are discarded and their stage environment torn down. 37% of proposals are rejected on average, and accepted ones take 1.7 attempts.

The asymmetry between the gates is the design insight. The gate that improves you is the inner one; the gate that keeps you is the outer one, and it asks for nothing but non-regression. A change has to earn its way in on the cases that motivated it and merely not hurt everywhere else — which is exactly the shape of a regression suite, and exactly what committing-a-harness-change-only-after-current-improvement-historical-retention-and-validity-is-a-regression-gate-for-the-scaffolding proposed without an ablation. This is the ablation.

And it reframes the harness-search results this vault has been holding at arm's length. evolving-a-harness-with-a-proposer-hidden-selection-set-moved-three-enterprise-benchmarks-20-to-35-points-and-the-gains-held reported a large gain with a proposer-hidden selection set. The obvious sceptical read was that the split was a formality. This says the split is doing most of the work: without it, the same machinery produces a harness that is actively worse than the one you started with. The reason to ask a harness-search paper for its overfitting control is not tidiness. It is that the ungated version has a negative expected value.

Single study. The comparison is across 12 windows on training splits with four backbones aggregated rather than repeat runs, so no interval on any cell. The gate's own accuracy is never reported — nobody measured how many of the rejected 37% were actually good proposals, which is the unchecked-grader problem one layer down (an-acceptance-gate-that-rejects-37-percent-of-proposals-never-reports-its-own-accuracy). And the no-verify variant is a fairly aggressive strawman: it promotes any proposal that helps its source cases, with no inner gate either, so the 0.33 figure bounds "no checking at all" rather than isolating the outer gate's marginal contribution.
