Changing only the observation and action space inside one harness moved success 34.2 points

Grounded in componentbench-component-level-failures-in-computer-use-agents; belongs to build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

2,910 tasks, seven models, one shared harness, four observation/action spaces. GPT-5 mini: 83.1% with accessibility-tree observations, 48.9% with coordinate-only Pixel control. Qwen3-VL-235B: 77.0 → 50.5 between AX-tree and Pixel, and 77.0 → 54.4 between AX-tree and Set-of-Marks. The paper's own summary: "changing only the observation and action space shifts task success by more than 30% for the same model."

Every harness comparison in this vault swaps a whole named system. This one swaps a part. Nanobot against Hermes against Claude Code; four harnesses by five backends; three agent frameworks in a robustness check. All of them report a delta between bundles and none says which component of the bundle produced it. Holding the harness fixed and varying one channel inside it is a different experiment, and it returns a larger number than most of the whole-harness swaps this vault holds. The straightforward reading is that a substantial share of what those studies attributed to "the harness" was the interface between the model and the thing it acts on.

The channels are not ordered, which is the part that blocks adoption. The intuition — accessibility tree beats marks beats pixels — is violated in the table: GPT-5.4 scores 83.8 in Pixel and 77.0 in SoM. Gemini 3 Flash barely moves across all four (95.2 / 89.6 / 87.1 / 85.4) while GPT-5 mini falls off a cliff. So there is a real model × channel interaction, and no published ranking transfers to your model. What a team can take from this is not a recommendation but an experiment.

The experiment is two cells. Fix the model, the tasks, the runner and the budget; run your suite with the richest observation channel your stack supports and with the poorest. The gap is your harness effect, localized to the term you can actually change. It costs one extra run of an existing suite, and it is the cheapest form of the question build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling has been contesting since the 15.36-point and 1.79-point measurements arrived.

One consistent direction does survive. Browser-Use — screenshot plus "serialized DOM information and grounded elements," up to 4 primitive actions per step — is the best configuration for all six models that ran it, by 5.6 to 8.2 points. Richer tool surface wins; which of the three narrower channels comes second does not.

Bounds. Web-only, Chromium, one component-interaction task family, and the paper concedes it "measures component-level competence, not long-horizon planning." Model access is non-native by disclosure — chat completions rather than Operator or Computer Use APIs, and "BrowserGym renders no visible cursor" — so the Pixel numbers are a floor and 34.2 points may be an overstatement of the gap under native tooling. UI-TARS-1.5-7B ran in Pixel only. No run-to-run variance is reported anywhere in the paper, so the 34.2-point figure has no error bar attached; read it against the 74.7% three-run verdict agreement in three-run-verdict-agreement-of-74-7-percent-published-beside-a-harness-comparison-is-the-noise-floor-this-vault-has-been-asking-for and it clears comfortably, but that is a different benchmark.
