A harness tuned to one model scored differently under another, so harness work is a per-model artifact with a recurring bill

Grounded in langchain-harness-engineering-terminal-bench; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

One sentence in the post: the harness that reached 66.5% with GPT-5.2-Codex scored differently under Claude Opus 4.6 and required separate optimization.

Why this confirms. harness-gains-do-not-transfer-across-tasks-or-seed-quality established non-transfer across two axes — which downstream task, and how good the starting harness was. This adds the third axis and the one that costs the most money: across models. It is independent evidence, from a practitioner rather than a benchmark, and it arrives with the reporter's incentives pointing the other way. A framework vendor benefits from claiming its scaffold is a portable asset; this says the opposite about their own work, in passing, which is the kind of admission worth more than a headline.

What it does to the opposing atom. a-single-harness-carried-across-five-backends-from-three-model-families-without-per-model-tuning is the only thing on the durability side of build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling, and it was already weak — a system paper's secondary claim with no per-backend score table, where "carried across" meant the harness ran, not that it performed. This is a direct case of a harness running fine on a second model and performing differently. The two are not strictly contradictory, because untuned portability and tuned-gain transfer are different claims, and that distinction is the useful takeaway: a harness ports; a harness's tuning does not.

The decision it changes. Price harness engineering as recurring rather than capital. If a tuned scaffold is worth double digits and has to be re-tuned per model, then every model upgrade carries a re-optimization cost that nobody currently budgets — and the upgrade decision is not "is the new model better" but "is the new model better after I redo the harness work I already paid for." That reframes model migration as a joint decision, which is the same conclusion evals-that-localize-the-fix reached from the rank-instability result, arriving now from the cost side.

Why suggestive rather than single-study. It is one line of a vendor blog with no numbers — "scored differently" and "requires separate optimization," no delta, no direction stated. On its own that is barely evidence. It reaches suggestive because it is the third independent axis of non-transfer and because the reporting incentive runs against the claim, not because the observation is well characterized.

What would move this. The Opus 4.6 number. A single score for the same harness under a second model, published beside the first, converts this from an anecdote into the cheapest transfer measurement anyone could make.
