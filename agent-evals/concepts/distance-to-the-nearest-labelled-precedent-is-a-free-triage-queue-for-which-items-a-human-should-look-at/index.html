Distance to the nearest labelled precedent is a free triage queue for which items a human should look at

Grounded in da-rac-distance-aware-judge-calibration; belongs to what-an-eval-costs-to-run.

Items are partitioned by r = d_hard / (d_easy + d_hard) — how far the item sits from the pool's hard precedents relative to its easy ones — into confident-easy (r > 0.60, n=231), ambiguous (0.40 <= r <= 0.60, n=50) and confident-hard (r < 0.40, n=199). In the ambiguous zone, "where nearest precedents are split between easy and hard exemplars, DA-RAC reaches 92.0% judge accuracy versus static-centroid's 52.0% (near chance)". Overall calibration: ECE 0.084 and Brier 0.173, against static-centroid at ECE 0.666 in the easy zone. The stated use: "the ambiguous zone is the use-case that should be flagged for human review: the neighbourhood-difficulty score makes such cases inspectable rather than silently judged."

This is the missing half of the judge's standing label budget. an-llm-judge-costs-a-hundred-labels-plus-weekly-maintenance-so-it-is-a-dependency-rather-than-an-artifact prices a judge as a recurring obligation — a hundred labels up front and maintenance forever. What that account never says is which items to spend the recurring labels on. Sampling uniformly spends most of the budget confirming the judge on items it was never going to get wrong. This gives an ordering computed from the retrieval you already did, at no extra model call: rank by how mixed the item's labelled neighbourhood is, review the top of the list.

It is a fifth entry in the vault's set of checks that need no new ground truth, alongside difficulty residuals over a corpus (the-residual-between-observed-and-predicted-difficulty-audits-a-whole-corpus-for-broken-items-without-reading-any-of-them), position and verbosity bias measurable without labels, label-count-versus-input-length correlation, and the agent arguing its own item is broken. The difference is what it addresses: those audit the instrument or the corpus, and this routes individual live judgments. Same family, different job — it is a runtime signal rather than a validation pass.

The 92.0% against 52.0% in the ambiguous zone is the number that makes the triage worth doing, and it reads two ways. Read one: retrieval helps most exactly where the item is hardest, so anchoring is not merely lifting the average. Read two, and the more useful one for a team: a badly anchored judge is at chance on the ambiguous fifth of the corpus while looking fine overall — static-centroid's headline was 66.7%, which does not advertise the coin flip underneath. That is what-the-headline-number-averages-away applied to a judge's own accuracy figure, and it says a single agreement number for a judge is worth less than the same number split by neighbourhood difficulty.

Bounds. One dataset, one judge family. The zone boundaries (0.40 and 0.60) are asserted, not derived, and the ambiguous zone is n=50 out of 480 — the 92.0-versus-52.0 contrast is 46 items against 26. The signal requires a labelled precedent pool of about 800 items to compute against, so it is not free in the sense of free: it is free given a substantial labelling investment already made. And nobody has reported running the triage in production, so there is no figure for how many human reviews it saves or what it misses in the confident zones.
