A score gain reported alongside a 24% token reduction is the second source here to price a configuration change

Grounded in longhorizon-harness-loop-engineering; belongs to what-an-eval-costs-to-run.

Terminal-Bench 2.1, model and execution backend held fixed, harness swapped: success rate 69.7 → 77.2, at 24% fewer tokens. Both numbers on the same row of the same table.

Why this extends. This theme's longest-standing open question is why almost nothing reports what a run cost alongside what it scored, which is the reason a 15-point harness gap could not be priced against the harness that produced it. drawai-image-to-editable-reconstruction has been the lone exception, reporting cost variation across thirteen models and five harnesses. This is the second, and it is the more useful kind: DrawAI prices a grid, which tells a reader what the spread looks like; this prices a change, which is what a team actually decides about.

The sign is what makes it interesting. Every cost result in this theme so far is a trade — item selection buys cheapness and gives up coverage, more trials buy consistency and cost linearly, a judge panel buys agreement and multiplies the bill. This one claims both directions at once: more score, fewer tokens. If it holds, the mechanism is not efficiency in any ordinary sense but avoided flailing — a bounded step that gets verified and checkpointed does not get retried blindly, and blind-retry-reproduces-latent-faults-and-delays-detection-so-recovery-machinery-is-negative-value-without-attribution already established that undirected retry is where long runs burn budget. Tokens spent on a failing trajectory are the waste, and structure removes them.

The measurement is weak in a specific way worth naming. A Manager/Executor/Auditor loop runs three agents where there was one, so a 24% token reduction is a net figure over a strictly larger amount of orchestration. That is a stronger claim than it first appears and also a harder one to check: no per-role breakdown, no wall-clock, no dollar figure, no statement of whether the baseline was allowed the same round budget. It is a bare README number from the harness's own authors.

What it changes about this theme's request. The standing complaint has been that cost is unreported. Two exceptions in thirty-eight sources is still an indictment, but it now has a shape: both exceptions report tokens, neither reports wall-clock or dollars, and neither reports the cost of the eval — they report the cost of the system under test. The thing this theme actually wants to price, what it costs to run the measurement, remains unreported by everyone.

Weakest evidence class. Self-reported, one week old, no methodology.
