An eval runner now reads traces from three observability backends it did not produce, which moves the adopt case from producing the record to consuming yours

Grounded in promptfoo-0-122-1-tracing-and-token-accounting; belongs to build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

One release shipped eleven tracing features. Four of them ingest traces the runner did not generate:

  • fetch external spans from Grafana Tempo (#10327)
  • fetch external traces from Braintrust (#10329)
  • fetch external traces from Langfuse (#10372)
  • integrate OpenAI Agents and Vercel SDK telemetry (#10364), plus support Claude and Codex subprocess telemetry (#10365)

And the runner's own emission was realigned to a public schema: align spans with OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions (#10361), with add test-case roots and universal target spans (#10328) and standardize tool execution across providers (#10363) making the shape uniform regardless of provider.

Every tracing feature this vault has previously recorded — from this runner and from Inspect — instrumented the runner's own execution. The adopt case rested on things the runner does during an eval: run the items, apply the assertions, aggregate the scores. This is the first move in the other direction. The claim is no longer "run your evals in our harness and get traces"; it is "keep your traces where they are and point the assertions at them."

What that buys is concrete and this vault has already priced it. giving-the-judge-the-run-directory-and-code-instead-of-the-transcript-is-worth-17-2-recall-and-kappa-0-53-to-0-75 measured what better evidence for the grader is worth: 17.2 recall points and kappa from 0.53 to 0.75. The obstacle to that has always been that the eval harness must have produced the richer evidence, which means running your system inside the harness. If the harness can read a Langfuse span from a production run, the evidence and the grading decouple. The eval stops being a separate instrumented universe and becomes a query over the one you already have.

It also changes what "the eval harness" means for the build-vs-adopt boundary. The boundary this vault settled on was: build the viewing surface, buy the routing surface, and the crossover is annotator coordination. This adds a third surface — the record — and puts it firmly on the adopt side, but adopted from your observability vendor rather than your eval vendor. A team that already runs Langfuse or Tempo has paid for the record; the eval runner is now a consumer of it rather than a competitor to it.

The caveat is the one this vault applied to every previous shipped norm and it applies unchanged. A feature being available is not a feature being used. Reading a production span only helps if your assertions are written against span content rather than final output, and nothing here makes that easy — it makes it possible. The same release's assertion list is still dominated by output-shaped checks.

The OTel GenAI alignment is the third convergence on a published interchange format in a week, after Inspect on 2026-08-24 and the scaffold-comparison limit in frontierchallenge-scientific-workflow-completion, where cross-model trajectory analysis was restricted to a single scaffold because it was the only one with a common event schema. That is the argument for interchange formats stated as a research limitation rather than a product feature, in the same week two runners shipped the fix. A paired trial is undefined until both arms are represented identically, and OTel GenAI conventions define that identity from outside either vendor.

Single release, single vendor, and a changelog is a vendor's account of its own work. Ingestion depth is unknown from the changelog alone — reading spans from Tempo is not the same as being able to assert over arbitrary span attributes, and nothing here says which. What is verifiable is the direction: five features in one release whose purpose is to consume records produced elsewhere.
