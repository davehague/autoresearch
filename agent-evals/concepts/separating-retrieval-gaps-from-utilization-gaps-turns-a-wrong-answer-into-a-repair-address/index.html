Scoring what evidence an agent retrieved separately from whether it used it splits a wrong answer into two repairs that go to different places

Grounded in diagnosing-search-behavior-in-long-horizon-search-agents; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

With the retrieval model and evaluation harness held fixed, six deep-search agents were scored at every search step against human document-level relevance judgments. That instrument splits a failure in two: a retrieval gap, where the necessary evidence never enters the trajectory, and a utilization gap, where it is retrieved and then not used correctly. A final-answer score fuses both into one number.

Why this confirms. It is the theme's founding argument executed on a different system class. assigning-each-failure-mode-to-a-component-edge-and-a-fault-side-turns-an-eval-score-into-a-repair-owner made the case from a taxonomy of 41 modes; this makes it from two, and the two-way split is more usable for exactly that reason. Retrieval gaps route to query formulation and search; utilization gaps route to context management and reasoning. Different fixes, different owners, one score that names which.

The decision it changes. It tells a team where a deep-research quality problem lives before they start guessing. It also sets the cost of finding out: the split requires human relevance labels at the document level, per step. That is the expense standing between most teams and this diagnostic, and it is why nearly everyone is measuring step counts instead.

Why single-study. One paper, six agents, two benchmarks, a single retrieval model held fixed throughout. The gap taxonomy will travel further than any measurement attached to it here.

What would move this. A cheaper proxy for the relevance labels — if an LLM judge on document relevance reproduces the retrieval-versus-utilization split that human annotators produce, the diagnostic becomes affordable and the finding becomes operational rather than academic.
