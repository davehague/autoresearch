A confidence-interval metric in the runner turns the noise floor from a reporting norm into a one-line call

Grounded in inspect-ai-changelog-confidence-intervals-in-the-runner; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix and build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

Inspect AI's unreleased block adds ci(), "a metric reporting a confidence interval for the mean (as {"lower", "upper"})". The default is mean ± t · stderr with a Student-t critical value at n - 1 degrees of freedom, "so small samples get honest widths"; setting cluster= moves the degrees of freedom to clusters - 1; method="bootstrap" gives a percentile (cluster) bootstrap interval instead.

This vault's largest open gap is a reporting norm nobody adopts, and this is the third time in two weeks that one of its norms has arrived as an API instead. The gap says: run your suite three times, publish the share of items whose verdict is stable, and read every delta against it. Eleven runs of comparing harnesses and interventions have gone by without a single source publishing the variance beside the effect. Norms have a demonstrated adoption rate of roughly zero here — the vault's own repeated finding is that only a changed default moved anyone's behaviour. Framework features are the next best thing, and this is the same move as a-reducer-that-preserves-every-scorers-value-instead-of-combining-them-is-un-averaging-shipped-as-a-framework-option and an-inter-rater-agreement-metric-in-the-runner-turns-judge-agreement-from-a-reporting-norm-into-a-one-line-call: un-averaging, then agreement, now the interval.

The cluster= argument is the part that makes this more than a convenience. Eval samples are routinely non-independent — several items drawn from one document, one task family, one generated seed, or one repeated trial of the same task. The unclustered interval on clustered data is too narrow, which means it reports more precision than the run has. Shipping clustered degrees of freedom and a cluster bootstrap in the same entry takes a position on a mistake eval suites make by construction, and it is precisely the part a team computing intervals in a spreadsheet off a results CSV will not have.

For the build-vs-adopt ledger, it belongs in the same column as the last two. The adoption cost is one line. The build cost is a t-interval (trivial), a clustered t-interval (not trivial to get right), and a cluster bootstrap (a genuine piece of work) — and the reason it matters is that the naive version of this is the one that silently overstates.

Bounds. A changelog entry in an unreleased section on main, not a tagged release, and no evidence anyone has used it. A metric being available is not a metric being reported: the standing gap is about practitioners publishing the noise floor, and a runner shipping a function does not move that until someone calls it. Nothing here measures variance on any actual suite. What the entry supports is narrow — the capability exists, priced at one line — and this vault should not read a feature as a practice.
