A composite over non-substitutable release evidence describes no real state, so the dimensions have to stay un-averaged

Grounded in proofagent-index-capability-is-not-readiness; belongs to what-the-headline-number-averages-away.

The ProofAgent Index spans four dimensions of deployment evidence — Evaluation, Context, Compliance, Governance — and the paper's third stated finding is that governance evidence must remain visible rather than being averaged away.

The argument, which does not depend on the source. An agent that behaves well in testing but cannot be authorized, monitored, or shut down is not seventy-five percent ready. It is not shippable. Averaging over dimensions that are not substitutes produces a number describing no configuration that exists: strong-Evaluation-weak-Governance and weak-Evaluation-strong-Governance land on the same score, and one of them is a release and the other is a lawsuit.

Why this extends. It is the same structure this theme keeps finding, applied to a release gate rather than a task score, and it lands on the constraint end of the spectrum the theme is mapping out. reporting-two-axes-that-trade-off-leaves-the-trade-to-the-reader-where-a-single-quality-number-picks-it-silently covers dimensions that are genuinely different goods, where a reader should pick the mix. This is the other case: Compliance and Governance are not goods to be traded against Evaluation, they are gates. The correct aggregation over a gate is not a mean or even a minimum over normalized scores — it is a conjunction. Each gate passes or the release does not happen, and the Evaluation score only becomes interesting afterwards.

That gives the theme three aggregation regimes rather than two, distinguished by what the components are to each other: substitutes take a mean, opposing groups that all must hold take a minimum, gates take a conjunction and never enter the arithmetic at all.

Why single-study, and it is generous. Single author, a framework paper introducing both an index and the tool that computes it, validated by that author using that tool, with the held-out readiness signal asserted and no figure given. The four-dimension decomposition is a reasonable checklist and the anti-averaging argument is sound on its own terms — but the claim is carried here by its logic, not by this source's evidence, and nothing external has applied the index. If the reasoning above fails, this source will not catch it.
