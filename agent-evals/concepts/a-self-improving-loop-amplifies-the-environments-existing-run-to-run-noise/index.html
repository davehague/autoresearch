A self-improving loop amplifies the environment's existing run-to-run noise

Grounded in fragility-of-self-improving-agents-variance-task-order-underspecification; belongs to what-the-headline-number-averages-away.

"Agent evaluation is inherently noisy in complex environments and on multi-step tasks, and stacking a self-improving loop on top can further amplify this noise."

The mechanism is the contribution, not the observation. That agent evals are noisy is now well established here — three-run verdict agreement at 74.7%, a repeat-run spread of 0.33 Bradley-Terry units against a protocol effect of 1.61, pass@1 of 65.36% against 25.25% pass-all-of-twenty. All of those describe a fixed system sampled repeatedly. A memory loop is not a fixed system: the outcome of task i is written into the state that executes task i+1, so a lucky or unlucky early run does not average out, it propagates. Variance is not merely present, it compounds along the stream, which means the noise floor of such a system grows with run length rather than staying constant.

That changes what a single number from these systems is averaging away. For a stateless eval, the headline hides a distribution over independent draws, and more draws fix it. For a loop, the headline hides a distribution over trajectories, and the spread between trajectories is generated by the method under test. Reporting a mean over three runs is a weaker act here than elsewhere, because the three runs are three samples from a process whose divergence is the interesting property.

It also puts a floor under the harness-evolution results this vault has been accumulating. A meta-optimizer that rewrites its own harness, a self-evolving skill compiler, a continual harness optimizer — every one of them is a loop of this shape, and every one reported a headline gain. harness-level-forgetting-makes-the-harness-a-variable-across-time-rather-than-only-across-configurations arrives on the same night with a 10%-relative gain from exactly this class of system. None of that is refuted by this finding, but all of it now carries a question the papers do not answer: how much of the reported gain survives a second run and a different ordering.

Bounds. No magnitudes. "Further amplify" is a qualitative claim over two methods with no variance figure attached, which is the weakest form the claim could take while still being useful. What makes it worth filing is that the amplification argument is structural — it follows from the loop's topology, not from these two systems.
