Ground-truth exposure reduced alignment for the two strongest judges by 1.5 and 3.9 points, consistent with over-anchoring

Grounded in agentjudgebench-llm-judge-reliability-dag-workflows; belongs to design-for-verifiability and the-judge-as-a-maintained-dependency.

"Ground-truth exposure is not uniformly beneficial: it reduces alignment for GPT-5.4 (1.5 pp) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (3.9 pp), consistent with over-anchoring." Showing the strongest two judges the programmatic answer key made them agree with humans less, not more.

Pushes back on the default assumption running through every judge design catalogued here — that more grounding is strictly better. Tool-grounded judges pinned to a live commit (giving-a-judge-tool-access-to-the-commit-under-review-pins-the-live-dependency-a-refetching-judge-leaves-open), state-view judges shown the environment instead of the transcript (showing-the-judge-the-final-state-instead-of-the-transcript-moves-the-reported-rate-about-ten-points-and-flips-the-label-on-up-to-a-fifth-of-cases), decomposed rubrics — every one of these treats "give the judge more to look at" as a one-directional lever. Ground truth is the most authoritative thing a judge could be shown, and here it moves two frontier judges backward.

The mechanism matches a component this vault has already caught doing the same thing. random-few-shot-examples-put-a-judge-eighteen-points-below-its-own-zero-shot-baseline-so-the-example-block-is-a-component-with-a-sign found that a judge's few-shot example block is not a neutral addition — badly chosen precedents cost 18 points against giving the judge nothing at all. Ground truth here plays the same role: a fixed reference the judge is meant to lean on, and leaning on it apparently narrows the judge's own reasoning toward matching the reference's surface form rather than assessing the trajectory independently — "over-anchoring" is the paper's word for exactly that failure mode.

Practice. Before wiring ground truth into a judge prompt as an assumed improvement, run the ablation both ways on a held-out slice, the same way this vault has recommended for few-shot blocks and moderator components. The result here is two judges out of six; whether it generalizes to the other four, or to judges with different training emphasis, is unknown from this abstract alone.

Bounds. Single study, abstract-level detail only, exactly two judges named. No mechanism beyond the label "over-anchoring" is given; whether it comes from prompt structure, calibration, or something else is unstated.
