A matched score gap of 3.6 points conceals 64.3 points of damage and 60.7 points of compensation

Grounded in quotebench-execution-transport-and-matched-scores; belongs to what-the-headline-number-averages-away.

"GPT-5.6-sol's matched gap of -3.6 points hides -64.3 points of damage and +60.7 points of compensation." Two effects an order of magnitude larger than the reported difference, with opposite signs, inside one number.

This is a new way for a headline number to lie, and it is not aggregation. Everything else this theme collects is a combination step: a mean over items, a mean over axes, a majority over judges, a weakest-link over trials. Here nothing is being averaged. A single configuration takes a large hit from the execution transport and recovers almost all of it by adapting its generation, and the residual is what gets printed. A reader seeing −3.6 concludes the factor did not matter. The true reading is that it mattered enormously, twice, and this particular model happens to be good at compensating — which is a different fact about the system and the one that predicts what happens when the transport changes again.

The practical instruction is to report the components, not the net. The paper's own recommendation is a five-item disclosure, but the narrower rule generalises to any A/B where the two arms differ in more than one respect: a small delta between two configurations is evidence of no effect only if you know no offsetting effects were bundled into it. This is cheap to check whenever the intermediate condition can be run — here it required replaying stored replies through both paths, which costs no new model calls.

It also connects to the theme's stability question from the other direction. re-running-with-the-rule-withheld-separates-compliance-from-coincidence-and-aggregate-scores-overstate-by-3-6-to-7-4-points-on-every-model found a correction that moved every number and barely moved the ranking. This is the opposite geometry — a number that barely moves while everything underneath it moves a great deal — and the pair sharpens the theme's rule: what a reported figure is safe to be read as depends on what could have cancelled inside it, and nobody reports that.

Limits. One configuration's numbers, quoted from an abstract, in a study with 56 tasks and no repeated runs. Whether the cancellation is typical or whether GPT-5.6-sol is unusual in its recovery ability is exactly what eight configurations cannot settle — though the reported disclosure-recovery range (30.4 to 60.7 points for six of eight, zero or slightly negative for two) says the compensation term is large and highly variable across models, which is the part that makes the net figure untrustworthy in general.
