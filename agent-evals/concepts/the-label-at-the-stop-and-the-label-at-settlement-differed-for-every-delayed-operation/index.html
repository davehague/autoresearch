The label at the stop and the label at settlement differed for every delayed operation

Grounded in when-is-an-agent-evaluation-over; belongs to evaluating-over-a-horizon.

Agent evals "score models on the state visible at the end of a stopped run which they count as one trial." In controlled runs holding the agent's actions fixed and varying only whether operations had settled, the endpoint label and the terminal label differed for every delayed operation tested.

This theme has been asking what happens inside the run. This is the first result about the edge of it. Every horizon finding recorded here concerns structure along the trajectory — order effects, regressions, decay, partial credit, inert steps. All of them still assume the run's end is the outcome's end. It is not, whenever the agent's last actions initiated something that completes elsewhere: a queued job, an uncommitted write, an un-delivered message, an un-finished build, an API call whose effect lands after the process exits. The score is then a snapshot of a system mid-flight, and calling it a result is a category error rather than a measurement error.

The design is the reason this is worth keeping despite being one small experiment. Holding actions fixed makes the endpoint the only thing that varies, so the divergence cannot be attributed to the policy. That is the same isolation move that made the execution-transport result credible — replaying-the-same-model-reply-through-an-added-parser-lowers-success-by-55-to-73-points-so-the-execution-transport-is-a-variable-nobody-declares replayed identical model output through a different transport and watched the score move. Two variables now have that treatment: what carries the action, and when you look.

Where it bites hardest is the corner of this vault that is currently most confident. Partial credit, prefix progress, per-check scoring, longest-satisfied-prefix — every one of them evaluates predicates against a final state. longest-satisfied-prefix-rather-than-fraction-of-criteria-refuses-to-credit-out-of-order-progress explicitly notes that all criteria share one environment state and that later actions can invalidate earlier ones; the natural extension is that pending actions can too, after scoring. A prefix measured at the stop can be wrong in both directions.

Bounds. Single author, preprint, and "every delayed operation" is reported over a denominator the abstract does not give. Take it as an existence proof that endpoint and terminal labels come apart under controlled conditions, not as a rate at which they do so in the wild.
