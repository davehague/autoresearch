Step-level credit signals did not beat their own shuffled controls when audited against executed replay

Grounded in credit-without-ground-truth-executed-replay-audit; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

Three signals used to attribute credit to individual steps of an agent trajectory — LLM-judge turn scores, outcome-conditioned log-probability ratios, and the policy's own confidence — audited against a causal ground truth built by re-sampling the policy's own alternatives at each decision point and executing them forward. Rank fidelity (median within-trajectory Spearman against the replay advantage), each compared to its own marginal-matched shuffled control:

SignalFidelityOwn shuffled control
Qwen2.5-7B implicit0.0193 [−0.109, 0.081][0.005, 0.114]
Qwen2.5-7B judge0.1142 [0.027, 0.168][−0.049, 0.117]
Llama-3.1-8B implicit (corrected instrument)−0.043 [−0.125, −0.016][−0.102, +0.024]

"Every family–control pair overlaps under the controls-first order." None of them "identifies which steps causally matter better than chance."

The distinction the paper insists on is the one this vault has been missing. Existing step-level benchmarks grade credit signals against annotated step correctness. This grades them against step contribution — what re-sampling and rolling forward actually changes about the outcome — "and the two come apart." A correct step can contribute nothing. Every failure taxonomy, per-step judge and error-attribution pipeline in this vault has been validated, where it was validated at all, against correctness or against human labels of correctness. None has been validated against consequence.

This is the third and strongest arrival at the same place. The machine transcript audit ran at 34% accuracy against human attribution. Two automated labelings of the same failed traces put the leading cause 20 points apart. Both of those compared machine attribution to another labeling. This one compares it to an executed causal ground truth, and the verdict is not "less accurate than a human" but indistinguishable from a shuffle. evals-that-localize-the-fix is contested precisely because its instruments are unaudited; here one was audited properly and returned nothing.

What a practitioner should do with it. Not "stop localizing" — the theme's whole premise is that a score you cannot act on is worth less than a diagnostic you can. What it forbids is a specific and popular shortcut: ranking the steps of a failed trajectory by asking a model which one was to blame. If your pipeline produces a per-step blame score and you have never tested it against anything but its own plausibility, this is the study that says the null is live.

Bounds. One environment (ALFWorld), single-agent tool use, two 7–8B open-weight policies, 37 trajectories retained of 50 in one family and 21 of 28 in the other. Wide intervals: "indistinguishable from control" is partly a statement about power, not a demonstration that the signal is absent. The judge signal has the best point estimate and its interval excludes zero — the verdict rests on it overlapping its own shuffle, which is the right comparison but a demanding one. Untested on frontier-scale policies, on multi-agent settings, and on coding agents, where a stronger policy might produce credit that does track contribution.
