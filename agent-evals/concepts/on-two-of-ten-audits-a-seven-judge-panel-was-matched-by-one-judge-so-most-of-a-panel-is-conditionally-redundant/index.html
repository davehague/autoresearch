On two of ten audits a seven-judge panel was matched by one judge, so most of a panel is conditionally redundant

Grounded in stopping-and-routing-llm-judge-panels; belongs to what-an-eval-costs-to-run and design-for-verifiability.

Ten audits, panels of five to seven candidate judges, risk reported against average judge calls per item:

  • Arena100K-7: full seven-call panel 0.2286, one call 0.2321. Six extra judges bought 0.0035.
  • SummEval-7: full panel 0.0446, one call 0.0450. Six extra judges bought 0.0004.
  • JBB-7 (safety): full panel 0.1069 at 7.00 calls, matched to within 0.0025 at 2.29 calls.
  • LLMBar-7 (Qwen3 backbone): full panel 0.2034 at 7.00, matched at 0.2033 at 3.28 calls.
  • RewardBench-7 and MATH-500: full-call aggregation genuinely wins — "extra broad signals can be worth their cost when the researcher wants the lowest risk."

Everything in this vault's judge thread pushes toward more judges. Cross-family panels on the axes where ground truth is contested; a panel of small judges at a seventh the cost of a large one; an item-response panel estimator that neither votes nor averages. Not one of those results was accompanied by the null — the check that the panel beats its own first member. Here that check is run ten times and on two of ten audits the panel is buying nothing, at seven times the price.

The mechanism has a name and it is checkable before you build. The additional judges on those audits are copies: conditionally redundant given the panel already selected. A copy can have excellent standalone accuracy — it is redundant not because it is bad but because it is right exactly where the existing panel is already right. The test is conditional, not marginal: on the items your current panel gets wrong, does the candidate get them right? That is answerable on a small labelled audit split, and it is a different question from "is this judge accurate," which is the question everyone actually asks.

Where this leaves the cheap-panel result. a-panel-of-small-judges-beat-a-single-large-one-at-one-seventh-the-cost-which-is-the-first-price-on-a-judge-configuration priced a configuration and remains the only such price in this vault. It is not contradicted here — a cheap panel that beats a large single judge is a real finding — but it is now flanked. On one side, small judges may sit below the competence floor for the material (a-judges-discriminability-collapses-to-chance-as-its-competence-approaches-one-over-k-which-is-the-first-stated-admissibility-threshold-for-a-judge). On the other, the members of a cheap panel are the most likely candidates to be copies of each other, since judges of similar size and training tend to be wrong in similar places. Neither the cheap-panel study nor any other panel result in this vault reports conditional redundancy among its members.

Bounds. Ten public audits, not production traffic. Cost is normalized calls with every LLM judge priced identically at 1.0 — real panels mix a frontier judge with small ones at a large price ratio, which would reorder several rows. Single held-out split as reported. The two "one call is enough" audits are preference and summarization, the two task families where a single strong judge is already known to do well, so the finding may be about those domains rather than about panels in general.
