A corpus not balanced against the nuisance variable returned AUROC of exactly zero where a matched-pair design returned 0.44 to 0.82

Grounded in similarity-gates-approve-reversals; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

"A naively built corpus inherits this confounder and can return an inverted verdict, with a decision AUROC exactly 0.000 in 13 of 18 configuration-task cells (at most 0.040 in all 18) against 0.440-0.815 for the same nine configurations under a balanced 2x2 design." And, against the author's own interest: "Twice in the effort it captured our own headline claims."

AUROC of exactly 0.000 is not a failure to answer, it is a confident wrong answer, and the corpus caused it rather than the grader. Same nine configurations, same task, two item sets: one says the instrument ranks correctly, the other says it ranks perfectly backwards. Every construction skeleton in this vault — from a-transferable-eval-skeleton-is-a-sandboxed-target-a-difficulty-dial-a-toolset-and-a-deterministic-grader onward — specifies target, difficulty dial, toolset and grader, and none of them specifies balance against the variable the grader is actually sensitive to. This is the first measurement of what that omission costs, and the cost is the sign of the result.

The general rule is portable and the nuisance variable changes per instrument. Here it is surface overlap: the naive corpus let reversals be short edits and paraphrases be long ones, so the gate's real sensitivity and the label were correlated. Elsewhere in this vault the same structure appears with a different nuisance — response length for a judge (position-and-verbosity-bias-are-judge-defects-measurable-with-no-ground-truth-at-50-to-70-and-over-90-percent), input length for a labelling agent (label-count-correlating-with-input-length-at-rho-0-81-is-a-free-detector-for-an-agent-paraphrasing-instead-of-analysing), answer position for a pairwise comparison. So: hold the thing your grader responds to constant across the two classes, or the corpus will confirm whatever the grader already believes. A 2×2 is the cheapest form and it needs no new items, only a different pairing of the ones you have.

It composes with the grader-validation procedures already on file by attacking a case none of them reaches. Scoring a known-good output catches a grader that rejects correct answers. A 25% holdout with precision, recall and κ catches a judge that disagrees with a person (holding-out-25-percent-and-reporting-precision-recall-and-kappa-is-the-first-stated-acceptance-procedure-for-a-judge). Letting the agent prove the specification unsatisfiable catches an error in the definition of correctness (letting-the-agent-prove-the-specification-unsatisfiable-or-the-reference-wrong-is-grader-validation-that-runs-during-curation). None of them catches a validation corpus whose class balance is entangled with the grader's real sensitivity, because the grader and the corpus agree — which is exactly why the verdict comes out clean and inverted. A fifth check, and it validates the validation set rather than the grader.

The self-disclosure is what makes it credible rather than rhetorical. "Twice in the effort it captured our own headline claims" says the author's own results were wrong twice before the design was fixed. That is the first admission of this kind in this vault, and it is the behaviour letting-the-agent-prove-the-specification-unsatisfiable-or-the-reference-wrong-is-grader-validation-that-runs-during-curation argued for: treat the measuring apparatus as the thing under test, and count a successful attack on it as a curation bug rather than a failed run.

Bounds. Single author, single paper, one instrument class. 18 cells and 90 cells are grids over configurations and thresholds rather than independent replications. "Naively built" is the author's characterisation of a corpus he built, so the comparison is between two of his own designs rather than between his design and a published one, and no third party has reproduced the inversion.
