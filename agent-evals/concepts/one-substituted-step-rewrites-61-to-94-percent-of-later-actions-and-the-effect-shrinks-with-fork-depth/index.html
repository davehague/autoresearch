One substituted step rewrites 61 to 94 percent of later actions and the effect shrinks with fork depth

Grounded in the-replay-gap-static-evaluation-of-model-switching; belongs to evaluating-over-a-horizon.

Forking live SWE-bench trajectories and continuing each fork with a different model, swaps "exceed their matched control floors by +0.25 to +0.66 normalized edit distance (multiplicity-corrected CIs exclude zero), rewriting 61-94% of post-fork actions." And: "Divergence decreases with fork depth in both directions."

A per-step decision is a whole-run decision, and how much of one depends on when it is made. This theme has collected the ways a long run stops being a sequence of independent scoreable moments — order effects (the-order-of-accumulated-experience-changes-a-lifelong-agents-score-so-one-run-sequence-measures-a-path-rather-than-an-agent), cross-session carry (a-failure-that-enters-in-one-session-and-fires-in-a-later-one-is-invisible-to-any-eval-whose-unit-is-a-single-run), history with an uncontrolled sign (experience-reuse-can-help-or-mislead-later-decisions-so-carrying-history-between-tasks-is-a-parameter-with-an-uncontrolled-sign). Those describe state accumulating. This describes the opposite direction: a change at step k propagating forward and destroying the rest of the run as a comparable object. The two together say a long-horizon run has no interior points that can be treated as interchangeable with the same point in another run.

The depth gradient is the actionable half, and it is new. Divergence falls as the fork moves later, in both arms. That gives an eval designer a usable rule: an intervention placed early in a horizon has to be measured by running the whole thing again, while one placed late can be approximated locally, and the crossover is measurable per task family by forking at a few depths and reading the control-relative divergence. Nobody in this vault has stated where in a run they intervened, because until now there was no reason to think it mattered.

It also prices the honest version of the measurement. Six paired runs and ~900 rollouts to characterise one substitution policy on one benchmark. That is the real cost of not being allowed to splice, and it belongs beside what-an-eval-costs-to-run's open complaint that nobody prices the methods they recommend.

Bounds. SWE-bench only; software-repair trajectories may be unusually path-sensitive because early file and test choices constrain everything after. Normalized edit distance over actions is the divergence measure, so "rewriting 61-94%" is textual difference in the action sequence rather than a claim about outcome. The outcome claim is separately reported and rests on five flips.
