One tuned judge served as both the gate and the critic under a retry budget of three

Grounded in netflix-judge-lifecycle-recommendation-explanations; belongs to the-judge-as-a-maintained-dependency and design-for-verifiability.

At deployment the same tuned judge occupies two positions in the pipeline:

  • Gate. An explanation is served only if it passes every must-have criterion. Conjunctive, not scored — no weighted average, no threshold on a composite.
  • Critic. Inside a generate → judge → revise loop with a retry budget of K=3. The judge's rationale is the revision instruction.

At K=3 the system clears over 75% pass rate; stronger generators reach ≥80% by k=3–4. Per-item cost is bounded by the retry cap, and total weekly inference — hundreds of thousands of explanations — runs at a few thousand USD per week.

Why the dual role matters more than it looks. The authors' second stated lesson is that one tuned judge can serve many roles, and read carelessly that is a cost argument: build one instrument, amortize it. Read carefully it is a constraint on how the instrument has to be validated. A gate consumes one bit; a critic consumes the prose. The same judge passing an acceptance test on verdicts is qualified for the first job and unqualified for the second, which is exactly why this system had to invent reasoning-agreement tuning to go with it. The dual role is what makes the extra validation non-optional, not what makes it unnecessary.

The conjunctive gate is the right shape and it is not free. Requiring every must-have criterion means no criterion can be traded off against another — a common failure mode this vault records under aggregation, where a strong score on four axes buries a disqualifying score on the fifth. It also means the pass rate falls multiplicatively in the number of criteria, which is what the retry budget is paying for. 75% at K=3 is the operating point where those two forces balance; a team adding a sixth must-have criterion should expect to pay for it in retries, not in quality.

The retry budget is the cost control, and it is a quality control too. K=3 caps spend per item. It also caps how far the loop can drift: an item that cannot be fixed in three attempts is discarded rather than ground on. The alternative — unbounded revision until the judge is satisfied — optimizes the artifact against the judge's idiosyncrasies, which is the closed-loop failure this vault has recorded elsewhere as a judge gate steering worse than a random one.

Practice. If you already run a judge as a gate, the marginal cost of running it as a critic is one prompt and the retries. Before you do, check that its rationales are right and not just its verdicts. And publish the pass-rate-at-K curve — it is the number that says whether your criteria are achievable or merely aspirational, and it comes free out of a loop you are already running.

Bounds. One system, one task family, short text rather than agent trajectories. "Over 75%" and "≥80% by k=3–4" are read off a cumulative figure with no per-criterion breakdown, so which criteria are the binding ones is unknown. The cost figure is an aggregate weekly number with no per-item decomposition and no separation of generation spend from judging spend.
