Historical-credit partial scoring inflated scores by a mean of .252 and missed every one of 14 staged rollbacks

Grounded in parthackbench-certified-equal-progress-stress-tests-partial-credit-tool-agent-evaluation; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

PartHackBench's certifier pairs an honest trajectory with an adversarial one only when both match componentwise on current-state predicate satisfaction and standardized agent attribution — removing the "the adversarial run just made more genuine progress" confound before any score comparison happens. Against a historical-credit scorer (one that credits any milestone ever reached, including later-reversed ones), certified matched pairs existed for 15 of 18 sealed held-out tasks. On those 15: mean score inflation of .252, conditional attack success in 10/15, and — separately — zero of 14 staged "reach the milestone, then explicitly undo it" rollbacks were detected. A certified current-state control, defined as an exact function of the same certified components, scored zero inflation by construction on the identical pairs.

This is the attack-and-verify move applied to a scorer rather than to a harness or a verifier's tolerance rule. The theme's standing pattern — synthesize or manufacture the one case your instrument cannot safely handle, then check whether it handles it — here targets partial-credit milestone scoring specifically, and finds it fails on the case that defines "partial credit" in the first place: crediting progress that no longer exists at the state actually being scored.

Bounds. Single paper, read at abstract/release level. 15 of 18 tasks yielded a certified pair; the other 3 are unaccounted for. No claim about generalization to non-milestone partial-credit schemes (e.g. rubric-weighted scoring).
