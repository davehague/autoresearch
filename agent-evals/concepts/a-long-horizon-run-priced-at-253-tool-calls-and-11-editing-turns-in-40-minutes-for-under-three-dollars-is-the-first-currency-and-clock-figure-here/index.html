A long horizon run priced at 253 tool calls and 11 editing turns in 40 minutes for under three dollars is the first currency and clock figure here

Grounded in autodesign-meta-harness-optimization; belongs to what-an-eval-costs-to-run.

"In a fully autonomous long-horizon loop, it executes 253 tool calls and 11 editing turns within 40 minutes for under $3, reaching average conference-poster quality in human evaluation."

It closes half of a complaint this theme has carried since 2026-08-12. The finding then was that two sources in thirty-eight had priced anything at all, that both reported tokens, that neither reported dollars or wall-clock, and that both priced the system under test. Forty-eight sources in, this is the first dollar figure and the first wall-clock figure in the vault, and the first decomposition of a long-horizon run into countable actions.

A dollar figure is usable in a way a token count is not. Every token figure here requires the reader to supply a price list that changes monthly and differs per provider; $3 per long-horizon multimodal run against a 100-item suite is $300 a pass, which is a budget line a team can hold a decision against. The action decomposition is the more transferable half — 253 tool calls and 11 editing turns says where the money went, and a team can compare its own loop against that shape without knowing anything about posters.

It is still the wrong side of the ledger, and that is the point this theme is named for. This prices the system under test: one agent producing one poster. What a graded pass of the 100-paper benchmark costs, what the system-blind human study cost, and — most interesting — what the meta-harness optimizer's rollout loop cost to learn the harness are all unreported. That last one is a genuinely new cost category: a harness improved from rollout feedback is paid for in evaluation runs, so the harness has an eval bill attached to its construction, and this is the first source that could have reported one.

Limits. One loop, one task, one system, no repeated runs — a data point, not a rate. "Under $3" is a bound with unstated model pricing and unstated retries. Human evaluation cost, the largest line item in most real eval budgets, is not priced at all. Reported by the system's own authors in an abstract, in the sentence advertising the system's efficiency, which is the weakest evidence class this vault accepts for a cost figure.
