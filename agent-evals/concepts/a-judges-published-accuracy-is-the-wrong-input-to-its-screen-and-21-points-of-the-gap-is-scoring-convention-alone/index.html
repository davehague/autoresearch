A judge's published accuracy is the wrong input to its screen, and 21 points of the gap is scoring convention alone

Grounded in competence-not-accuracy-judge-gate-diagnostic; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

The judge screen needs a competence estimate. The tempting input is the model's published accuracy on a relevant benchmark. Measured on GPQA-Diamond for one judge: answered-only 0.840, empty-inclusive 0.626, domain-matched proxy 0.547, grading-behaviour inversion 0.602 — three estimators with different error sources landing in 0.55–0.63 against a headline of 0.84.

The largest single component is not exotic. It is the denominator. 0.840 → 0.626, 21.4 points, is the same judge on the same items, differing only in whether episodes that never produced a parseable answer within the generation budget are counted. "Reasoning judges fail to converge precisely on items they cannot solve, so discarding them selects for solvable items." The same effect appears in the replication: DeepSeek-V4-flash scores 94.8% over answered GPQA items and 73.0% once non-convergence is included, and on mathematics it fails to answer 75% of the time.

The practice rule is one sentence: measure the competence yourself, on your own items, empty-inclusive. A number taken off a model card or a leaderboard is optimistic in a direction that pushes a judge over the admissibility floor it should have failed. This is not a small correction applied to a safe margin — on mathematics the measured competence was 0.267 against a floor of 0.2, and a 21-point survivorship inflation would have put it comfortably clear.

It also generalizes past judges, to every capability number this vault reads. Non-convergence is a scoring convention, and the convention that discards it is the common one. Any agent benchmark that drops runs which timed out, crashed, or produced no parseable final answer is reporting performance conditional on the agent having got far enough to answer — which is the same survivorship selection. This vault has spent two weeks on the observation that infrastructure failures get scored as capability failures; here is the mirror image, where the hardest items quietly leave the denominator.

Bounds. One judge family measured with all four estimators; on the second (weaker) judge the proxy and inversion estimates differ by 0.34 and the paper explicitly declines to claim agreement, so the "three estimators converge" result is a single-judge result. The proxy estimator depends on a domain- and difficulty-matched benchmark being genuinely comparable, and part of its gap may be dataset mismatch. The inversion estimator depends on the paper's own model assumptions. What is robust across all of them is the direction and the censoring component, which needs no modelling at all.
