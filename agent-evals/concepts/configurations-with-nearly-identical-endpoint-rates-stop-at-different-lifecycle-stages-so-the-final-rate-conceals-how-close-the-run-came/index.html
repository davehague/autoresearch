Configurations with nearly identical endpoint rates stop at different lifecycle stages, so the final rate conceals how close the run came

Grounded in harnesssafe-persistent-carriers-across-agent-harnesses; belongs to what-the-headline-number-averages-away.

Each run is assigned the furthest stage supported by observable trace evidence — from N0, no contact with the poisoned surface, to N5b, an oracle-verified full-chain violation — and the distribution is summarized as a Chain-Stage Score. The finding: configurations with nearly identical attack-success rates stopped at different stages. A second one from the same table: the configuration with the highest overall CSS scored 47.0 on reusable skills against a leading 70.0, so the best-on-average setup is the worst on a family.

Why this extends. This theme's table has been about combining components that coexist — items, axes, judges, gates. A stage distribution is a different object: the components are ordered, and a single endpoint rate is not an average over them, it is a projection that keeps only whether the last one was reached. Two systems with the same endpoint rate can be one step from failure and five steps from failure, and the rate is identical in both cases. That is information loss of a kind a mean does not produce and a minimum does not fix.

The transferable form has nothing to do with security. Any eval where a run can fail at more than one point has a stage distribution sitting in its traces already: the deepest checkpoint reached is free to compute and nobody reports it. It is the same argument as subtask-partial-credit-recovers-the-signal-binary-grading-throws-away-and-matters-most-when-every-model-scores-near-zero pointed at failure chains rather than task progress, and it inherits that argument's amendment from strict-pass-and-process-reward-diverge-in-every-configuration-so-partial-credit-is-a-second-question-rather-than-a-rescue-for-hard-benchmarks: the staged view is a second question, not a rescue for a low endpoint rate.

The second finding is this theme's oldest pattern in a new place. An overall winner that is worst on one family is exactly what an average over non-substitutable groups produces, and the 47.0-against-70.0 gap is the size of what the overall score absorbed. It joins aggregating-as-the-worse-of-two-opposing-groups-removes-the-advantage-a-mean-hands-to-a-one-sided-agent as a second measured instance rather than a design argument.

The decision it changes. Two cheap reporting obligations, both computable from traces you already store. Report the stage distribution beside the endpoint rate. And when you quote a composite over families, quote the worst family with it — the gap between the two is what the composite is hiding, and it costs one line.

Why single-study. One team, one paper, one metric of the authors' own design. "Nearly identical" is not quantified, the stage taxonomy is domain-specific to persistent-risk chains, and no other benchmark here reports a stage distribution to compare against. The claim that this generalizes past security is an argument from structure, not a demonstration.

What would move this. Any eval outside safety reporting a per-stage distribution and showing two systems that tie on the endpoint and separate on the stages. That is a re-analysis of stored traces rather than a new experiment, which makes its absence the more interesting fact.
