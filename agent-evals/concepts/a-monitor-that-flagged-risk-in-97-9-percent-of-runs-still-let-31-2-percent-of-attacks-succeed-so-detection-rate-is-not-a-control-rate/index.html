A monitor that flagged risk in 97.9% of runs still let 31.2% of attacks succeed, so detection rate is not a control rate

Grounded in harnessrisk-lifecycle-agent-harness-safety; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

MiniMax M3 running on the OpenClaw harness "detects risks in 97.9% of runs but retains a 31.2% ASR." Across all 14 configurations, Detection and attack success are strongly negatively correlated — Pearson r = −0.71 — and the authors still conclude that "detection alone does not ensure safe execution" and that "effective detection must therefore be coupled with controls that block unsafe actions, protect persistent state, and complete remediation."

Two numbers, and every eval in this vault reports only the first. The noticing rate is what an instrument produces natively: a judge emits a verdict, a monitor emits a flag, a gate emits a decision. The acting rate — what fraction of the noticed cases were actually stopped — is downstream of the instrument, usually implemented somewhere else, and almost never scored. The gap here is 66.7 percentage points on one configuration.

The correlation is the trap. r = −0.71 is a strong relationship, and a paper that stopped there would have licensed "improve detection and safety follows." The per-configuration number kills that reading: the single best detector in the study is not close to the safest configuration. A correlation across configurations tells you detection tends to accompany safety and tells you nothing about whether it causes it in yours.

This is the same failure as the similarity gate, approached from the opposite side. a-shipped-similarity-gate-caught-zero-of-fifty-six-meaning-breaking-edits-and-never-exceeded-0-700-balanced-accuracy-across-ninety-cells is an instrument that never noticed. This one notices at 0.979 and is still worth 31.2 points of realized damage. Both produce the same operational outcome — the bad thing happens — from opposite instrument qualities, which means instrument quality does not predict system outcome and has to be measured separately from it.

The concrete practice. If your eval scores a detector, a judge-as-gate, or a monitor, score the pass-through rate beside it: of the cases the instrument flagged, what fraction were actually blocked, and of the ones it blocked, what fraction were then correctly remediated. The paper supplies the failure mode for the third: during Incident Recovery "agents may identify contaminated state but fail to remove unsafe tokens, skills, or policies." Recognition, containment and repair are three measurements and this vault has been treating them as one.

Bounds. One paper, 14 configurations, one grader (GPT-5.4) whose Detection label is the least reliable of the four it assigns (85.7% agreement, κ=0.69) — so the 97.9% figure has the widest error of anything in the study. The authors note detection assessment "depends on harness transparency," meaning a harness that logs less may score lower on Detection for reasons unrelated to whether the agent noticed. Correlation work covers only 12 configurations and is called "exploratory."
