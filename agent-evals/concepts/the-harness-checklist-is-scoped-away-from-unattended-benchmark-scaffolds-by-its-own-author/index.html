A harness-design checklist is scoped away from unattended benchmark scaffolds by its own author, which draws a line between the harness you ship and the harness you evaluate with

Grounded in harness-architectural-convergence-three-coding-agents; belongs to build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

Having derived five architectural elements that three independently built coding-agent harnesses converged on, and having said the engineering move is to adopt the settled shapes rather than defend your own, the author immediately restricts who that advice is for:

We scope that advice to the audience all three serve, a developer at a terminal in an attended, long-horizon session: a scaffold that runs unattended to a benchmark score pays for durability and disclosure it may never draw on, and we would not press the same checklist on it.

This is the first source in this vault to say that the harness you ship and the harness you evaluate with are different objects with different requirements, and it says it from the harness-design side rather than the eval side.

It pushes back on the direction the harness-search thread has been travelling. starharness-evolving-harnesses-stratified-search evolves an enterprise agent harness and scores it on enterprise benchmarks. opsharness-self-evolving-rca-harness evolves a production RCA harness and scores it on public RCA benchmarks plus an industrial deployment. videoharness-rsi-context-construction-as-a-search-space searches executable context constructors against a held-out benchmark split. All three treat one artefact as both the deployed system and the thing under measurement. This author says the two have divergent cost structures — durability and disclosure are load-bearing for the attended developer session and dead weight for the unattended benchmark run — so an artefact optimised against a benchmark score is being optimised without pressure on the parts that matter in production.

The direction of the bias is the useful bit and it is not the obvious one. The naive worry is that a benchmark-tuned harness overfits the tasks; this vault already has controls for that (proposer-hidden selection sets, resampled held-out testbeds). The worry here is different: a benchmark-tuned harness underbuilds the dimensions the benchmark cannot see. Session-record strength, recovery semantics and quirk coverage cost tokens and complexity, and pay off only across a long attended run with a human who will be angry when history is destroyed. A search that accepts on selection-set score has no term for any of them.

And it inverts cleanly. If the two are different objects, then a harness delta measured on benchmarks does not transfer to production for reasons that have nothing to do with task distribution, and a harness chosen for production is not the right baseline for a benchmark comparison. Which harness a paper evolved — the shipping one or the scoring one — is a variable no harness-search result in this vault reports.

The same paper hands over the mechanism this cuts both ways on. Its evaluation implication is that as harnesses converge in form, residual performance differences increasingly live in the un-converged dimensions — recovery semantics, quirk coverage, verifiability — rather than in the loop everyone now shares. If that holds, a benchmark harness comparison is measuring exactly the dimensions its own selection pressure ignores.

Single study, N = 3, all coding-agent harnesses read at one point in time, and the scoping clause is an argued judgement rather than a measurement — no unattended scaffold was read, and no cost of the "dead weight" was quantified. It is a boundary claim from someone who read three harnesses line by line and has no product to sell on either side of it, which is why it belongs here at pushes-back rather than as a stray caveat.
