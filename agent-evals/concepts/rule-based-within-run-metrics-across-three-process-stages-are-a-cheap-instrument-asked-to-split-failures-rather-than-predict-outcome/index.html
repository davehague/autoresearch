Rule based within run metrics across three process stages are a cheap instrument asked to split failures rather than predict outcome

Grounded in beyond-final-scores-long-horizon-rd-agents; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

Seven frontier models on 36 long-horizon tasks, evaluated with "rule-based metrics to characterize within-run behavior through Solution Framing, Execution, and Feedback Control." No judge, no labels, no reference trajectory — rules over what the run already emitted.

It is the second instrument to survive this vault's free-metric rule, and it survives for the same reason as the first. The standing finding is that no free metric has survived a test: step counts, tool calls, tokens, context volume, the agent's recorded plan and tool-call count all have a direct finding against them. The refinement recorded 2026-08-12 was that the failures share a shape — every one of them was being asked to stand in for outcome. The constraint-statement signal escaped by partitioning failures instead (nearly-one-in-five-confirmed-violations-happen-after-the-agent-states-the-constraint-and-an-instruction-level-fix-then-removes-74-points). A three-stage characterisation of within-run behaviour is the same move at a larger scale: it makes no claim to track whether the agent got better, only to say where in the run progress was gained or lost. Cheap transcript-derived signals are useless as scores and may be good as splits now has two instances and no counterexample.

The three stages are the transferable part. Solution Framing, Execution, Feedback Control is a decomposition of an agentic run into phases that fail for different reasons and belong to different owners — roughly, did it plan the right thing, did it carry the plan out, did it respond to what came back. That is assigning-each-failure-mode-to-a-component-edge-and-a-fault-side-turns-an-eval-score-into-a-repair-owner applied along the time axis rather than the component axis, and unlike that taxonomy it is computed by rules rather than assigned by a reader.

Limits, and one is load-bearing. The abstract does not say what the rules compute or how the stage boundaries are drawn, and for a rule-based instrument the value is entirely in the rules — a reader cannot adopt this from the abstract, only the shape of it. Feedback Control in particular is the hardest of the three to detect mechanically. 36 tasks, one team, no replication, and the rules were presumably authored knowing the tasks.
