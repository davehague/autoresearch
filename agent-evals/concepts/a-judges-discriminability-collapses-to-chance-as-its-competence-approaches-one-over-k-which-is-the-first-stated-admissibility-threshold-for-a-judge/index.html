A judge's discriminability collapses to chance as its competence approaches 1/k, which is the first stated admissibility threshold for a judge

Grounded in competence-not-accuracy-judge-gate-diagnostic; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

A reference-free judge is modelled as a latent solver: to grade an answer it has to work out what it would itself conclude, so "its capacity to evaluate is bounded by its capacity to solve." With competence c (the probability its own answer is correct) over an answer space of size k, discriminability has a closed form —

AUC = ½ + (ck − 1) / (2(k − 1))

— giving the necessary condition c > 1/k. As k → ∞ (free text) the bound relaxes to AUC = (1 + c)/2, because "two independent errors almost never collude"; at small k they collude often and a unit of competence buys much less.

Measured: on research mathematics with k = 5, the judge's closed-book self-solve competence was 0.267 against a floor of 1/k = 0.2, and its within-question discriminability was 0.489 — at or marginally below chance, exactly where the model says it should be. Four judges from independent model families reproduce the pattern: at competence 8–21% discriminability runs 0.31–0.57; at competence 33–80% it runs 0.67–0.80.

This closes a gap this vault has held open since 2026-08-17 with a direction and no number. a-judge-no-stronger-than-the-agent-it-scores-yields-no-usable-signal-which-is-an-admissibility-condition-six-configurations-here-never-state recorded a practitioner claim that a similarly powered judge returns nothing, noted it had no threshold attached, and named the unrun experiment. Here is a threshold, a derivation, and a measurement procedure — and it is arrived at from the opposite direction (hold the judge fixed, vary the task) which makes the agreement more interesting rather than less.

The procedure costs one pass and no labels. Have the judge answer your own eval items closed-book, with no gold answer, counting any episode that fails to produce a parseable answer as unsolved. That is ĉ. Count your answer space: for multiple choice it is the option count, for free text treat k as large. If ĉ sits near 1/k, the judge is not admissible on those items and nothing downstream of it is a measurement. This is the same shape as the vault's existing first gate — score a known-good output and check the grader passes it — moved one level up, to the grader's capacity rather than its calibration.

Read it as necessary, not sufficient. The paper states this repeatedly and it matters: above-floor competence is required for a judge gate to discriminate at all and does not guarantee that it does. Noise shrinks any real AUC toward ½, and correlated errors between judge and target ("collusion") can put discriminability at chance even when c > 1/k. A pass is a licence to look.

Bounds. One optimizer, one target model, three tasks, one primary judge plus a four-judge replication that reports only the confounded marginal estimate. Episode counts in the low hundreds. Competence measured by closed-book self-solve is a lower bound on grading-context competence, since a candidate answer and its reasoning scaffold re-solving — so an observed AUC above the predicted bound falsifies nothing. The two independent competence estimators diverge by 0.34 on the weaker of the two judges, which is exactly the regime the screen is meant to adjudicate.
