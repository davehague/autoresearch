One oracle per data surface — executed queries, verified spans, traced annotations — is what auditable ground truth costs in a heterogeneous domain

Grounded in worksurface-bench-multi-surface-knowledge-routing; belongs to design-for-verifiability and build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

WorkSurface-Bench's 1,151 reference answers are auditable by three different mechanisms, one per surface: table answers reproduced through executed DuckDB queries, document answers grounded in verified text spans, graph answers traced to source dependency annotations. The three are not interchangeable and none covers another's surface.

Why this extends the substrate fork. build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling had the build decision forking on a domain property: if the domain computes its own answers, generate from execution (building-the-executable-tool-layer-first-lets-a-generator-produce-ten-thousand-tasks-whose-ground-truth-comes-from-execution); if it does not, fit a modelled counterpart and panel the soft axes (where-ground-truth-is-contested-the-construction-move-is-a-simulated-counterpart-fit-to-real-data-plus-a-cross-family-judge-panel-on-the-soft-axes). This is the case the fork does not cover: a domain that is on both sides at once. One surface executes (SQL over tables), one can only be pinned to evidence rather than computed (text spans in documents), one has pre-existing structural annotations to trace to (dependency graphs). The answer is not to pick a side — it is one oracle per surface, and the build cost multiplies by the number of surfaces rather than by the number of tasks.

Why the third mechanism is the interesting one. Executed queries and verified spans are familiar. Tracing to source dependency annotations is neither computed nor human-labelled — it reuses metadata the workspace already contained. That is the third instance in this vault of harvesting eval structure from artifacts that already exist rather than authoring it, after prerequisite edges recovered from build history (a-dependency-dag-of-separately-testable-units-with-completed-nodes-held-as-regression-obligations-scores-a-long-run-continuously) and difficulty spread inherited from active project work (taking-tasks-from-active-project-work-supplies-a-difficulty-dial-nobody-had-to-author). Three sources, three different artifacts harvested, same underlying move: the cheapest ground truth is the ground truth somebody already wrote down for another reason.

What it says about the exact-matching line. the-line-between-a-deterministic-check-and-a-rubric-is-whether-exact-matching-misleads-and-three-unrelated-domains-now-draw-it-there gave a criterion for splitting deterministic checks from rubrics. This adds a middle tier the criterion did not anticipate: groundable but not computable. A verified text span is not an exact match and not a rubric — it is a provenance check, and it needs no judge. Any grader design that offers only the two ends will push document answers to a rubric that a span check would have handled deterministically.

The decision it changes. A team estimating grader cost for a heterogeneous product should count surfaces, not tasks. Three surfaces is three oracles, three sets of tolerance rules, three failure modes in the grader itself — and the alternative that looks cheaper, one LLM judge over everything, gives up the auditability that made the corpus reusable.

Why single-study. One benchmark, three surfaces, no reported cost for building any of the three oracles. The claim that the mechanisms are non-substitutable is structural and obvious on inspection; the claim that three is the right decomposition for enterprise workspaces is this team's judgment.

What would sharpen it. Hours or lines of code per oracle, which nobody reports for any grader in this vault, and whether a fourth surface (email, chat, code) needs a fourth mechanism or reduces to one of these three.
