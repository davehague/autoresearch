Nearly one in five confirmed violations happen after the agent states the constraint, and an instruction-level fix then removes 74 points

Grounded in redagentbench-executable-red-teaming-faithful-measurement; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

Across 2,846 state-confirmed violations with resolved action anchors, 17.92% (510 cases) occurred after the agent had stated the relevant constraint or risk in its own output; 5.48% (156) after it explicitly acknowledged the prohibition. Counting every unresolved case as recognition-absent still leaves lower bounds of 16.05% and 4.91%. On the 510-case confirmatory cohort, a policy reminder then took attack success from a baseline of 88.25% to 14.06% — a 74.19-point reduction, preventing 368 of 434 harmful executions in complete pairs. A self-reminder reached 51.76%; a neutral control changed nothing (85.51%).

Why this extends. This theme has been collecting instruments that assign a repair owner rather than a rank — failure-mode-to-component-edge taxonomies, retrieval gaps separated from utilization gaps, per-mode recovery rates. All of them classify a failure after the fact using a scheme the analyst brought. This one splits on a variable the run produced by itself: did the agent say the constraint before violating it. That partitions failures into did not know and knew and did it anyway, which are owned by different repairs — the first by the model or the context supplied to it, the second by whatever sits between deciding and acting.

The intervention is the confirmation, and this is the first complete loop in the vault. A diagnosis that assigns a repair address is only worth something if the repair works. Here it does: nearly one in five violations were on the execution side of the recognition line, and an instruction-layer change removed 74 points on exactly that cohort. If the fault had been in the model's knowledge, a reminder would have done nothing — and the neutral control shows the effect is not just extra tokens in the prompt. Localize, repair, measure the repair. Every other localizing instrument here stops at the first step.

The self-reminder gap is the sharper practical result. Asking the agent to remind itself reached 51.76%; supplying the policy reached 14.06%. Same intervention point, 38 points apart, and the difference is whether the constraint came from the agent or from the harness. That is a directly usable finding for anyone building agent guardrails and it is an argument about where content lives, not about model capability.

What it does not do is apportion. The paper has a full 6-model × 3-harness grid and reports a ~10-point harness spread on one model's row without the model spread in matched units. Sixth family, same shape as the five before it: qualitative agreement that the harness matters, no variance decomposition. The 08-10 hypothesis — that the model-or-harness answer is conditional on the axis being measured — gains a consistent data point and no test.

Single-study, one week old. The recognition split depends on the paper's own action-anchor resolution, and 40% of the confirmed cohort had no resolvable anchor, which is why the lower bounds matter. The intervention result is on a cohort selected for confirmed baseline violations, so the 74-point figure is not a general defence rate.
