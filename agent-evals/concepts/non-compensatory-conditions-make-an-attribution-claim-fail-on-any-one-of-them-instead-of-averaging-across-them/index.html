Non-compensatory conditions make an attribution claim fail on any one of them instead of averaging across them

Grounded in cmb-criterion-revision-trace-anchored-protocol; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

CMB requires "five non-compensatory conditions" before an observation may be read as an agent having formed and reused a revised success criterion: criterion-failure detection, a model-emitted proposal, new-episode transfer, intervention sensitivity on the claimed carrier, and preservation.

Almost every instrument in this vault reports a composite, and a composite lets a strong dimension pay for a broken one. Weighted scores, means over rubric dimensions, pass rates across subsets, the six-metric Skill Lift arriving the same night. That is the right aggregation when the dimensions are desirable and substitutable. It is the wrong one when they are logically necessary — when the claim is "the agent did X," and each condition rules out a different way the observation could be true without X having happened. Conjunction is what turns a set of checks into an argument.

The design pays off in a way this vault should notice, because the payoff is legibility of failure rather than a better score. CMB returned zero — no trial satisfied all five — and because the conditions are conjunctive and separately named, the zero is diagnosable. The author could point at which condition each arm failed and conclude the instrument does not discriminate, rather than reporting a middling composite that would have looked like a weak positive. A compensatory version of the same benchmark would have returned something like 0.6 and been quoted as evidence about models.

The same mechanism is visible in the two conjunctive scores already on file, and they behave identically. Hard F1 collapsed to 0.133 against a soft 0.363 for the same verdicts because hierarchical completeness is conjunctive across levels (soft-f1-of-0-363-against-hard-f1-of-0-133-is-one-run-scored-for-record-quality-and-for-hierarchical-completeness), and pass-of-all-twenty fell to 25.25% against a 65.36% pass@1 for the same reason. In every case the conjunctive number is the smaller one, the more alarming one, and the one that corresponds to what somebody actually needs to be true. Conjunction is what makes a failure visible instead of averaged, and the cost of adopting it is that your headline gets worse.

The practical instruction: for any claim of the form "the system does X," write down the conditions that must hold and refuse to weight them. Then report which condition failed. This is cheap, it is a design-time decision rather than an experimental one, and it is the difference between an instrument that produces a number and one that produces an argument.
