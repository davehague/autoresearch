A single agent configuration run three times has a standard error of 6 to 12 points which is the size of most harness effects recorded here

Grounded in infrabench-durability-and-per-check-scoring; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

InfraBench runs every task three times across 15 agent-model configurations and reports mean effective scores from roughly 40% to 88% with per-configuration standard errors of 6–12 points. It also reports that "top configurations still pass only a fraction of their attempts."

It pushes back on this vault's own accumulated comparison table. The harness-effect magnitudes collected here — 13.7 points on Terminal-Bench, 15 points on tabular analytics, 11 points across nine harnesses, 7.5 points from a released loop harness — are single-run point estimates. Not one of the sources they came from reports repeated runs or an interval. Here is a benchmark saying that one configuration, held completely fixed and run three times, wobbles by 6 to 12 points. Several of the deltas this vault has been treating as findings are the size of one configuration's run-to-run noise.

This does not falsify them, and overstating it would be its own error. Different benchmarks have different variance; a task suite with deterministic graders and short horizons will be tighter than infrastructure tasks with long tool chains. What it establishes is that the band exists, that it can be large, and that nobody in this vault measured theirs. The disputed atoms are marked rebutted-by: harness-only-changes-moved-a-fixed-model-13-7-points-on-a-public-coding-benchmark-which-is-an-upper-bound-on-tuning-rather-than-an-apportionment and harness-choice-moved-agent-accuracy-15-points-with-the-model-backbone-held-fixed are the two most exposed, both being single-run deltas within a factor of the reported error.

The practice claim, which is the part that belongs in this vault. A single run does not support a ranking, and finding out costs three runs instead of one — the cheapest structural check on this list and the one with the widest consequences, because it applies retroactively to every comparison a team has already made. It also gives the theme's central question an uncomfortable prior: if run-to-run variance on a fixed configuration is 6–12 points, the model-versus-harness apportionment this vault has been chasing needs repeated runs before any of its inputs mean anything, and none of the five families that reported qualitative agreement ran them.

It is also the first source here to actually run the trial-count question. pass-at-k-and-pass-to-the-k-are-the-optimistic-and-weakest-link-endpoints-of-one-operator-family-applied-across-trials-of-a-single-task opened that gap on 2026-08-10 and noted almost nobody runs more than one trial. Three trials per task, with top configurations passing only a fraction of attempts, is that gap observed rather than argued.

Single study, and three runs is a small n for an error estimate. A standard error computed from three repetitions is itself noisy, the "effective score" construct is not explained in the abstract, and infrastructure tasks are plausibly among the most variance-prone domains in this vault. Whether an observed delta is statistically real is an epistemics question and is left to the separate vault; what is taken here is the operational instruction — repeat the run, report the spread.
