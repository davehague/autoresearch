A judge reached over 90 percent agreement with its domain expert in three prompt iterations which is the first convergence figure here

Grounded in hamel-using-llm-as-a-judge-complete-guide; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

"> 90% agreement between the LLM and Phillip" — the product's domain expert — reached in three iterations of prompt refinement, on a Honeycomb natural-language-to-query product.

The iteration count is worth more than the agreement rate. Six judge configurations are catalogued in this vault and not one says how long it took to reach the number it publishes. Three cycles of read-the-disagreements-and-edit-the-instruction turns judge alignment from an open-ended activity into a bounded task, which is what a team budgeting the work needs and what an-llm-judge-costs-a-hundred-labels-plus-weekly-maintenance-so-it-is-a-dependency-rather-than-an-artifact leaves unstated — 100 labels plus weekly maintenance prices the inputs and says nothing about the loop.

It is also the strongest available argument against a judge you cannot inspect. The loop only converges because a person can see which cases the judge got wrong and change the specific instruction that caused it. That is the mechanism behind the warning in a-tool-that-writes-the-rubric-and-then-scores-against-it-removes-the-only-step-where-a-human-would-notice-the-rubric-was-wrong stated positively: a judge whose criteria are visible and editable can be driven to agreement in three passes, and one whose criteria were generated and never read cannot be driven anywhere.

Read the metric with the condition attached, because the source attaches it. Agreement was used "because our dataset was roughly balanced (about 50% of instances were failures)," and with imbalanced classes the same source says to report precision and recall separately (raw-agreement-is-only-admissible-near-balanced-classes-so-an-imbalanced-eval-has-to-report-judge-precision-and-recall-separately). Most product evals are nowhere near balanced — two-hundred-samples-buys-plus-or-minus-2-4-points-on-a-3-percent-defect-rate-and-halving-that-error-costs-four-times-the-samples works a 3% defect rate — so ">90% agreement" is not a target another team should copy without first checking its own base rate.

Bounds. One product, one expert, one company, reported by a consultant who worked on it. No confusion matrix, no holdout, no statement of dataset size at the point the figure was taken. It measures agreement with one person's labels and therefore inherits everything appointing-one-benevolent-dictator-annotator-is-the-coherent-response-to-kappa-0-3-and-it-trades-correctness-for-a-stable-target says about that trade: a judge aligned to a dictator is reproducible, and nothing in the procedure detects the dictator being wrong.
