Two complete harnesses under matched backends differed by 15 to 17 tasks out of 232 and by four fifths of the token total, so the efficiency gap dwarfed the accuracy gap

Grounded in pufibara-modelica-agent-workflow-benchmark; belongs to build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

Pufibara against Claude Code on 232 Modelica engineering tasks, backend held fixed. With DeepSeek v4 Flash: 202 versus 185. With Claude Sonnet 5: 202 versus 187. Roughly 6.5 percentage points of task success — and 76.4% to 82.5% lower logical-token totals, with sequential runtime 6.1% to 58.4% lower.

The accuracy delta sits near the bottom of this vault's harness range and the efficiency delta sits far above anything recorded here. The ledger's capability figures run 1.79, 4.3× on a behavioural axis, 8.6, 15.36, and tonight's 20–35 points. At 6.5 points this is unremarkable. A four-to-five-fold reduction in model-facing context while scoring higher is not: it is the largest quality-per-token gap on file, and it is on an axis the theme has been reading only as a cost consequence rather than as a harness property in its own right.

Which suggests the wrong question is being asked at adoption time. Teams compare harnesses on benchmark accuracy. On this evidence the accuracy spread between two competent harnesses on the same backend can be small while the token spread is nearly an order of magnitude — and token spend is the term that decides whether the system is affordable in production and whether an eval suite over it is affordable to run repeatedly. The vault already concluded that an adoption decision made on benchmark accuracy is made on the axis where the harness matters least, on the safety-versus-capability split; this adds a third axis with the same conclusion for a different reason.

Attribution is unresolved and the authors say so. No ablation isolates which mechanism — persistent engineering state, evidence-to-candidate binding, explicit submission — produces the gap, and no repeated runs means no variance on either arm. The comparison is also the authors' own system against a commercial competitor on a benchmark they built, in a language where their design assumptions were the design assumptions of the benchmark. Read the mechanisms as the transferable content and the margin as a single unreplicated observation.

One structural caution about the token figure. "Logical tokens" is defined as uncached input plus cache-creation plus cache-read plus output (logical-tokens-summing-uncached-cache-creation-cache-read-and-output-is-the-cost-figure-that-survives-a-pricing-change), which counts a cache read at the same weight as a fresh input token. A harness that leans hard on prompt caching will look expensive on this metric and cheap on a bill. So the 76–82% is a claim about context volume, not about money, and the direction of the discrepancy favours whichever harness caches less.
