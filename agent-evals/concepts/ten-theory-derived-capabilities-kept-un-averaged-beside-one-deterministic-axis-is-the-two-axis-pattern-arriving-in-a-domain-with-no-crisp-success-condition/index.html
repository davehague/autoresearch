Ten theory-derived capabilities kept un-averaged beside one deterministic axis is the two-axis pattern arriving in a domain with no crisp success condition

Grounded in companionbench-judge-severity-separated-from-agent-quality; belongs to what-the-headline-number-averages-away.

CompanionBench's stated complaint about its predecessors is that they "aggregate empathy into one score." Its replacement: ten capabilities derived from 25 theories across psychology and counselling, reported separately, and assessed on two complementary axes — the subjective ten-capability rubric, and a deterministic measure of whether deeper disclosure was earned. Four of the ten are graded explicitly for the first time in the domain: holding ambiguity, selfobject responsiveness, positive resonance, calibrated challenge.

Why this confirms. The two-axis pattern is now on its fourth unrelated task family. DrawAI reported fidelity and editability separately because they trade off (reporting-two-axes-that-trade-off-leaves-the-trade-to-the-reader-where-a-single-quality-number-picks-it-silently); AgentS4D and StealthBench each reported a constraint rate beside a success rate (scoring-a-reckless-solve-rate-beside-success-catches-the-runs-an-outcome-only-grader-certifies); this reports a soft rubric beside a hard behavioural check. Four teams, four domains, none citing the others' framing, all declining to produce the single number.

What it adds beyond a fourth instance is where it happened. Every prior instance came from a domain with something deterministic to hold on to — pixels, a security constraint, a workspace side effect. Companionship has none, which is the domain type this vault flagged as the one where the discipline would break down and the pressure to emit one empathy score would win. It did not.

The decision it changes. It removes the last excuse. "Our domain is too soft to decompose" is the standard reason for a single quality score, and a benchmark in the softest domain here decomposed to ten and then found a deterministic eleventh axis anyway. The transferable move is the search itself: look for the one thing in a soft domain that is mechanically checkable — here, whether a state the agent had to earn was reached — and report it beside the rubric rather than inside it.

Why single-study despite confirming. One paper, and the ten capabilities rest on a theoretical literature this vault is not equipped to assess. Ten un-averaged axes is also not free of the problem it avoids: a reader facing ten numbers will construct their own composite, and the paper does not say what the recommended reading is. Un-averaged is the right default and it is not a complete answer.

What would move this. Somebody reporting that two of the ten capabilities anti-correlate, the way fidelity and editability do. That would turn "we kept them separate" from a reporting preference into a necessity.
