Half of a reasoning baseline's items came back empty on budget exhaustion, so its headline accuracy was reporting truncation as much as reasoning

Grounded in recursive-agentic-reasoning-paired-scoring; belongs to what-the-headline-number-averages-away.

Under a 16,000-token budget, the single-pass chain-of-thought baseline returned empty, budget-exhausted content on 51.2% of HLE items, 34.5% of BBEH items, and 36.1% of Omni-MATH items. The model burned its budget inside a hidden deliberation stream and emitted nothing.

A published accuracy over those items is a blend of two quantities with nothing in common. "Got it wrong" and "never spoke" are averaged into one number, and on HLE the second is the majority of the failures. Anyone reading that cell's score as a measure of reasoning quality is reading a measure of budget fit. Change the budget and the number moves without the model changing at all — which makes token budget a first-class configuration variable of the benchmark, on the same list as the harness and the prompt.

The theme's usual shape is a split that reveals a hidden distribution; this one reveals a hidden failure mode. Partial credit masking instance failures, category-level breakdowns partitioning the least informative dimension, a 95.2% pass rate that is 56.1% inside the human step count — each of those averages over items that differ in difficulty or in kind. Here the average is over items where the system produced no output at all, which is worse: it is a category error rather than a coarse summary, and it is invisible in every results table that reports a single accuracy.

The reporting fix is one counter and nobody prints it. Empty-output rate per arm, per benchmark. It costs nothing, it is already in the logs, and its absence is why the sign reversal in scoring-only-the-items-every-arm-resolved-turned-a-three-point-loss-into-a-gain went unnoticed until someone went looking. A benchmark result that does not state what fraction of its items produced a parseable answer is not reporting its denominator.

One caveat on generality. The authors scope their conclusions to models with hidden deliberation streams, which is where this failure lives — a model that streams its reasoning into the visible output does not silently return blank when the budget runs out, it returns a truncated argument, which is wrong in a different and more legible way. The 51.2% is a property of one budget, one model family and one hard benchmark; the practice — count your empties and print them — is general.
