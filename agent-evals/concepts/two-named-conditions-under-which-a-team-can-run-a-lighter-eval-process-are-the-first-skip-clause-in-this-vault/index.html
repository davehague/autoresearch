Two named conditions under which a team can run a lighter eval process are the first skip clause in this vault

Grounded in shankar-in-defense-of-ai-evals; belongs to build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

"The more interesting question, then, is not whether you do evals, but when you can afford to be less rigorous and when you cannot. In practice, there are two main situations where you can get away with lighter processes. The first is when your task is already well represented in posttraining, as in the aforementioned coding agent example. The second is when you and your team have enough domain expertise and taste to rely on your own dogfooding — and are religious about continuously dogfooding."

Everything else on the build side of this vault is a cost with no exemption attached. Four weeks to a working harness. A hundred labels plus weekly maintenance. Thirty examples with a saturation rule. Three annotators over 200 sampled tasks. An annotation layer built in-house because the off-the-shelf one coordinates badly. Each entry prices a piece of the work and none of them says when the right amount of that work is close to none. A ledger with no skip clause is an argument that every team owes the full programme, which is not what anyone with production experience believes.

The first condition is the interesting one because it is measurable and it connects to a mechanism the vault already holds. "Well represented in posttraining" is the same quantity as parametric coverage — how much the model already knows about your domain without being told. harness-gains-track-recoverable-execution-defect-mass-so-where-there-is-no-defect-there-is-no-gain found harness work paying in proportion to recoverable defect mass, and a-read-derived-coverage-score-returned-a-perfect-1-000-in-the-condition-where-nothing-was-available-because-the-agent-filled-the-hole-itself comes from a study that manipulated exactly this channel by renaming a real library's API and watching seven models fail identically. So the same variable governs how much harness work pays and how much eval work you owe, and a team can probe its own position on it cheaply: strip the domain-specific names out of a task and see whether performance moves.

The second condition has a trap in it, stated by the author. "Religious about continuously dogfooding" is doing the work, not "domain expertise". Expertise without the continuous looking is the condition under which a team believes it is exempt and is not — which is the same failure the definition addresses when it says the teams claiming to do no evals "are usually lying to themselves". The observable form of the condition is a habit, and nobody has proposed a way to check whether a team actually has it.

What the clause does not cover, and this is where it is weakest for this vault's question. Posttraining rigor is rigor about the model's capability. Coding agents are the named beneficiary, and coding agents are where harness choice moves accuracy 15 points (harness-choice-moved-agent-accuracy-15-points-with-the-model-backbone-held-fixed) and where configurations at identical outcomes differ 12.8x in spend (harness-configurations-that-all-pass-every-test-differ-12-8-times-in-tokens-so-at-ceiling-the-only-remaining-question-is-the-bill). None of that variance is covered by anyone else's evals, because nobody else assembled your system. The skip clause is sound about capability and silent about the harness, and the corrected version of it would read: if your task is well represented in posttraining you may skip building capability evals, and you still owe an eval of the thing you built around the model.

Bounds. Asserted from experience, no data, no thresholds. "Well represented in posttraining" has no test attached and "religious about dogfooding" has no observable form, so both conditions are currently self-assessed — which is exactly the situation in which a team gets them wrong. The author runs a paid evals course, and the piece is written in reply to an argument, so it is positioned rather than neutral. Published 2025-09-05, describing a model generation now nearly a year old, and what is well represented in posttraining moves every release.
