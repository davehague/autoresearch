Written critiques make one annotator hour buy the ground truth and the judge prompt at once

Grounded in hamel-using-llm-as-a-judge-complete-guide; belongs to build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling.

The domain expert produces "pass/fail judgments with critiques," and the critiques must be "detailed enough so that you can use it in a few-shot prompt" and detailed "enough that a new employee could understand it." Those critiques then become the judge prompt's few-shot examples.

Every other labelling procedure in this vault produces a label; this produces a label plus its reason, and the reason is the deliverable. That changes the economics of the one step nobody can automate. The expensive input is a domain expert's attention, and here a single pass over the data yields both the ground truth and the instruction the judge will follow — so the marginal cost of the judge prompt is the difference between clicking a button and writing a sentence, rather than a separate authoring project. Against the ladder in an-llm-judge-costs-a-hundred-labels-plus-weekly-maintenance-so-it-is-a-dependency-rather-than-an-artifact, this is the cheapest structural improvement to the expensive tier recorded here.

It also partly answers the worst objection to the position this vault has recorded on annotator configuration. appointing-one-benevolent-dictator-annotator-is-the-coherent-response-to-kappa-0-3-and-it-trades-correctness-for-a-stable-target chooses a single labeller for reproducibility and has no mechanism that detects the labeller being wrong. Written critiques are that mechanism's cheapest form: taste recorded only as pass/fail is unauditable, and taste recorded as "failed because it silently dropped the time filter" can be disagreed with specifically by a second person, a new hire, or the judge itself. The dictator's decisions stop being opaque without a panel being convened.

For this theme's boundary the critique is a build-side artifact of the kind the boundary predicts. The line here runs at the human interface — build wherever a person looks at a failure and decides something. A critique is the output of that surface, and it is domain-specific in exactly the way a rubric is: the "new employee could understand it" bar is a legibility requirement no framework can supply, and the reuse as few-shot content is generic plumbing any framework could. That is the same procedure-versus-criterion split a-packaged-eval-skill-is-labelled-a-floor-by-its-own-author-and-names-six-things-that-generalize-across-projects identified, applied one level down.

Bounds. Practitioner recommendation, no measurement of what the critiques bought — no comparison of a judge built from labels-plus-critiques against one built from labels alone, and no statement of how much longer critiquing takes per item. The claimed benefit is plausible and unquantified, and the ">90% agreement in three iterations" figure from the same source cannot be attributed to this mechanism specifically.
