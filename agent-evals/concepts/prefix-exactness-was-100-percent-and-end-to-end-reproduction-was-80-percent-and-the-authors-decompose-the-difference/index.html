Prefix exactness was 100% while end-to-end reproduction was 80.78%, and the authors decompose the difference into three named sources rather than reporting the flattering number

Grounded in symtrace-repair-or-resample-replay-controlled-debugging; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

A replay instrument has two numbers and they are not the same number. This paper reports both.

The mechanism number: attempt-level prefix exactness of 1,608/1,608, with every attempt preserving the parent/edge topology of the reused prefix and all 6,159 materialised reused nodes matching their source content hashes (6,159/6,159). The replay reconstructs what it claims to reconstruct, verified by hash rather than asserted.

The outcome number: 80.78% same-failure reproduction in one attempt, 52.43% across three.

The 19-point gap is decomposed into three parts, and the decomposition is the contribution:

  1. There is often barely any prefix to hold fixed. 381 of 536 cases (71.08%) reuse only 2–3 nodes; 738 of 1,608 attempts (45.90%) reuse exactly two. The benefit over plain rerun scales accordingly — +9.80/+6.82 points at 2–3 nodes, +17.76/+18.69 at 4–8, +25.69/+27.08 at nine or more.
  2. Everything from the anchor onward is genuinely live, which is the point of the design and also a source of divergence. One case reconstructed identical prefixes three times and produced three different unsupported answers with no intervening tool calls. Another received HTTP 200 from the Wikipedia API in the source run and HTTP 429 on replay — the world changed between the two, and no replay technique fixes that.
  3. The judge deciding "same failure" has its own error rate, measured rather than assumed. A stratified secondary audit of 72 judgments found 64 agreeing with the original labels, 5 confirmed disagreements and 3 uncertain. Weighted by stratum: 3.30% confirmed-disagreement rate, 3.67% uncertainty rate, 6.96% combined sensitivity upper bound. All five confirmed disagreements were false negatives — genuine reproductions labelled different_failure — so the direction is known and the reported 80.78% is, if anything, low.

This is the reporting shape this vault has been asking for, arriving without being asked. The pattern here is normally the reverse: a method reports one figure, and the gap between what the instrument guarantees and what the outcome shows is left for a reader to notice. Prefix exactness alone would have been a 100% headline. Reproduction alone would have been 80.78% with no account of where the other 19 points went. Publishing both, plus a measured error rate on the judge that produced the second, is what makes the instrument usable by someone who did not build it — because a reader can now tell which failures the technique will help with (late ones, deep prefixes) and which it cannot touch (early failures, live external state).

The content-hash audit is a first for this vault. no-agent-harness-produces-a-record-an-outside-party-can-check-without-trusting-the-runtime records that no shipped harness produces a record an outsider can verify. Hashing every reused node and reporting 6,159/6,159 is not tamper-evidence — the same process computed the hashes — but it is the first integrity check on an eval record in this vault, and it is the kind of number a third party could re-derive if the artefacts are released. They are: the release includes the task manifest, linked traces and graphs, initial and final annotations, rationales and supporting evidence.

And the third bullet is the standing warning restated. The instrument that decides whether the replay worked is an LLM judge, so an evaluation framework built to remove one source of uncontrolled variance introduces another at the measurement layer, with a 6.96% sensitivity bound. The authors say so, propose expert verification for disagreements and low-confidence cases as future work, and recommend reporting human-LLM agreement alongside automated results — which is the same recommendation an-inter-rater-agreement-metric-in-the-runner-turns-judge-agreement-from-a-reporting-norm-into-a-one-line-call made available as a one-line call.

Single study, one model family, one temperature, benchmark Web tasks. The 72-judgment audit is a small sample for a rate estimate, and it is stratified rather than random. Against that: it exists, and almost nothing else in this vault that leans on an LLM judge reports one at all.
