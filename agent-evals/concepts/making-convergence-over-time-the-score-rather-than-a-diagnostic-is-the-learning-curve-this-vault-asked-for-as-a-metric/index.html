Making convergence over time the score rather than a diagnostic is the learning curve this vault asked for as a metric

Grounded in label-free-harness-evaluation-teacher-relative-lift; belongs to evaluating-over-a-horizon.

"We score a harness by how much its student converges toward the teacher over time," where the student runs a "continual learning harness" — an LLM paired with retrieval or memory that "improve[s] from feedback without retraining."

The learning curve stops being an observation about a run and becomes the unit of measurement. a-harness-that-rewrites-itself-from-rollout-feedback-removes-harness-held-fixed-as-an-available-control concluded on 2026-08-14 that a self-improving harness destroys the fixed-harness assumption every comparison in this vault relies on, and had only "report a learning curve" to offer. early-saturation-in-a-self-evolving-harness-is-the-learning-curve-the-vault-asked-for-and-it-caps-the-rollout-bill then found a curve, as an anomaly a benchmark happened to notice. Here the curve is the score by construction, which is the first method in this vault whose output is a trajectory rather than a point.

That makes this theme's founding claim apply to the thing doing the evaluating. The claim is that a terminal number on a sustained run is an average over a structured trajectory and the structure is where the information is. A harness whose whole purpose is to improve from feedback has no meaningful terminal number at all — its score depends on where you stopped — so the horizon problem is not an artifact of long tasks here, it is intrinsic to the object.

The open question it inherits is this theme's oldest one, in a new place. The standing ask is for the shortest horizon that still surfaces the effect. Here: how many correction rounds before the convergence ranking of two harnesses stabilises, and can a short run invert it? That matters more than usual because "sparsely sampled" corrections are what make the method cheap and also what make an early ranking noisy — and experience-reuse-can-help-or-mislead-later-decisions-so-carrying-history-between-tasks-is-a-parameter-with-an-uncontrolled-sign establishes that a truncated horizon can see a different sign, not merely a smaller effect. Nobody reports the trajectory of the ranking, only that the endpoint correlates with a gold standard.

Bounds. One domain (cybersecurity), no coefficient on the correlation, no statement of how many rounds were run or how sparse the corrections were, and no report of ranking stability at any point before the end. The comparison to a held-out gold standard is what validates the endpoint; nothing validates an intermediate reading, which is the one a team on a schedule would actually take.
