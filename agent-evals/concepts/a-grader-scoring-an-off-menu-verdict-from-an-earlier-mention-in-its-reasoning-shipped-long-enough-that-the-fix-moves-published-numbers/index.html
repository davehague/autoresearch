A grader scoring an off-menu verdict from an earlier mention in its reasoning shipped long enough that the fix moves published numbers

Grounded in inspect-ai-changelog-0-3-258-to-0-3-259; belongs to design-for-verifiability.

"model_graded_qa/model_graded_fact now leave a sample unscored when the default grader's final GRADE: verdict is a letter the instructions never offered, instead of scoring a grade mentioned earlier in its reasoning. Note: re-scoring existing logs may shift metrics and sample counts — samples whose grader verdict was off-menu (including P grades when partial_credit is disabled) were previously scored from an earlier on-menu mention and are now unscored with grade_parse_failure recorded."

The defect is in the extraction step, not in the judge. The grader model produced a verdict the rubric did not offer — the honest reading of which is that the judge failed to answer the question. The scorer instead scanned backwards and found a letter it recognised, and scored that. So a sample where the grading run broke was recorded as a graded sample, with a value taken from a sentence in the reasoning that was never the verdict. This is the same class of defect as fixing-grading-bugs-moved-one-model-from-42-to-95-percent-so-the-measuring-instrument-can-own-more-of-a-score-than-anything-it-measures, and the same class as the math() scorer's answer parser (the-math-scorer-was-executing-model-output-on-the-evaluator-host-so-the-grader-is-an-untrusted-input-surface): the failure lives in the few lines between what the model emitted and what the harness recorded.

The failure direction is the one this vault keeps finding invisible. A parse failure that produced no score would show up as a hole in the results. This one produced a score, from a plausible-looking letter, on a run that had actually gone wrong — so the eval stayed complete and the dashboard stayed populated. The P case named in the entry makes it concrete: with partial credit disabled, a grader saying P is out of range, and the previous behaviour would score it from something earlier in the transcript rather than flag it.

"Re-scoring existing logs may shift metrics and sample counts" is the part worth carrying. Not just values moving, but the denominator moving — samples that used to count now do not. Any team holding an Inspect-scored baseline from before 0.3.259 is holding a number that is not comparable to what the same logs produce today, and the changelog says so rather than leaving it to be discovered. That disclosure is itself the argument for the adopt column: a hand-rolled grader with the same bug has nobody to publish the note.

The generalisable check is cheap and nobody runs it. Count how many grading calls in your last run produced a verdict outside the menu you offered, and look at what your scorer did with them. If the answer is "none, ever", the parser is probably lenient rather than the grader perfect.

Bounds. One changelog entry from one project. No count of affected samples, no example, no statement of how long the behaviour shipped or how much any published number moved — "may shift" is as specific as it gets. Applies to the default grader template; a custom grade pattern may never have had the problem.
