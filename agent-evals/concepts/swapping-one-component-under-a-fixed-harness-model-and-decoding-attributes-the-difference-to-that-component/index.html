Swapping one component under a fixed harness, model, and decoding attributes the difference to that component, and for memory the difference is total

Grounded in agentmembench-memory-strategy-comparison; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

Five memory-management strategies — in-context windowing, external key-value store, graph-based episodic memory, compression-based summarisation, web-augmented memory — were run under one harness, one model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 4-bit), greedy decoding, and the same three datasets across 491 annotated question turns. On the long-range dataset the external key-value store reached Recall@5 0.573 while the other four reached at most 0.005.

Why this confirms. It is the localizing argument executed as a protocol rather than argued for. assigning-each-failure-mode-to-a-component-edge-and-a-fault-side-turns-an-eval-score-into-a-repair-owner proposed labelling failures by component; this holds every component fixed but one and reads the difference off directly. Greedy decoding is the underrated part — it removes the sampling variance that makes most agent comparisons unfalsifiable. The cost is that only modal behaviour is measured, which is the right trade for isolating an effect and the wrong one for estimating a distribution.

How to read a 100× gap. A ratio that large usually indicates a construction artifact rather than a technology difference, and the paper half-says so. On the long-range dataset the retrieval target sits many sessions back, and four of the five strategies structurally cannot reach that far — recency windows expire, summaries compress it away, entity graphs never indexed it. The paper's own fourth finding concedes the pattern explicitly for web-augmented memory, which ties in-context windowing "by construction" because external results carry no in-corpus provenance. So the honest claim is narrower than the headline: this measures long-range in-corpus retrieval, and anything optimizing for something else lands at the floor.

The transferable move for a team. Before attributing an agent's behaviour to the model, hold everything and swap the one component you suspect. This is the cheap local version of the variance question evals-that-localize-the-fix is stuck on — it will not tell you how variance is apportioned in general, but it will tell you whether your memory layer is the binding constraint, which is the decision actually in front of you.

Why single-study. One team, three public datasets, and a 4-bit 7B model doing both generation and judging. The absolute numbers are low across the board — F1 0.156, Faithfulness 0.354 — low enough that the model is plausibly the binding constraint on everything measured, and rankings established under a small quantized model are not guaranteed to hold at frontier scale.

What would move this. The same swap protocol re-run at frontier scale. Or a dataset where the retrieval target is near rather than many sessions back, which would show whether the ordering is a property of the strategies or of the distance being probed.
