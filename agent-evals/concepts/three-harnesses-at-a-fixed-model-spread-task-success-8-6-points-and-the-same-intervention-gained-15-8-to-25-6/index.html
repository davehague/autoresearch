Baseline spread: 8.6 points. That is this vault's third same-shape measurement of harness choice as a capability variable, after 15.36 points and 1.79 points, and it lands between them — which is what a real effect with genuine task-family dependence looks like, rather than a contradiction.

What makes this one different is that the ruler ships with it. The theme's standing open question has been whether any of these deltas exceed run-to-run variance, and no source has answered it because nobody publishes the variance. This paper does, in the same study, on the same benchmark: three independent runs of all 656 (task, strategy, model) triples agree on the verdict 74.7% of the time (three-run-verdict-agreement-of-74-7-percent-published-beside-a-harness-comparison-is-the-noise-floor-this-vault-has-been-asking-for). Read against that scale, 8.6 points across three harnesses on 82 tasks is a modest effect sitting uncomfortably close to the noise, and the 1.79-point measurement is plainly beneath it. The contested reconciliation this theme carries — the harness as a small capability variable — survives, and now looks less like a finding and more like a resolution limit.

The finding nobody asked for is the second column. The recovery layer gains +25.6 on mini-SWE-agent, +23.2 on FnCallAgent and +15.8 on CodeAgent — a 9.8-point spread in the effect size of an intervention, larger than the 8.6-point spread in the baselines it was measured against. And the ordering inverts: the strongest baseline harness gains least. Checklist progress shows the same shape (+12.9 / +13.5 / +6.7).

That is a harder constraint on adoption than the baseline spread is. A team reading "this technique is worth 25 points" is reading a number measured in one cell. Here the same technique, same model, same tasks, is worth 15.8 in another. So a published improvement carries its sign across harnesses and does not carry its magnitude — which means the harness is not only a term in the score, it is a multiplier on everything you try to add. It is evals-that-localize-the-fix's "a layer matters where it was binding" reached from a third direction, and it says the practice of quoting an intervention's headline delta is unsound without naming the harness it was measured on.

Practice. When adopting a technique from a paper, expect roughly its direction and not its size, and re-measure on your own harness before budgeting against the number. When publishing one, report it on more than one harness or state which one it was.

Bounds. Single study, single model (GPT-5.4), single benchmark of 82 tasks, three harnesses. No per-harness error bars are given for the harness comparison itself — the 74.7% agreement figure comes from the strategy comparison, which used mini-SWE-agent only, so applying it as a scale to the cross-harness numbers is an inference rather than a reported result. The three harnesses differ in control loop and tool interface but all are code-agent scaffolds, so this is a narrow slice of harness space. Experiments "impose no preset limits on wall-clock time, execution steps, or total token usage," so none of these gains is cost-normalized and a harness that gains more may simply be spending more.
