The same model was 4.3 times less safe under a different harness, so a safety number names a configuration rather than a model

Grounded in harnessrisk-lifecycle-agent-harness-safety; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

GLM-5.2 completed the adversarial objective in 54.7% of runs on OpenClaw and 12.6% on Nanobot — a 4.3x spread with the model held fixed. Across the study, ASR bands by harness were OpenClaw 31.2–80.9%, Nanobot 12.6–55.2%, Hermes 14.8–65.4%. The authors: "the same model can be over four times less safe under a different harness," and "safety must be evaluated for each model–harness configuration rather than attributed to the model alone." The spread reorders the safety ranking of models, not just their levels.

The axis is what makes this new. This vault already holds harness-swap effects on capability: 15.36 points on tabular analytics, reordered model rankings on skill use, "significant" but unquantified on graph analysis. Every one of those is an accuracy axis. This is the first clean per-model magnitude on a behavioural axis, and it is roughly an order of magnitude larger in relative terms than anything measured on accuracy — including the 1.79-point framework spread that swapping-among-three-agent-frameworks-moved-the-score-by-1-79-points-so-harness-choice-is-a-small-capability-variable-on-rubric-graded-work recorded the same night on two of the same three harnesses.

Read together, the two results give the model-versus-harness question its first honest answer. The harness is a small term in what the agent can do and a dominant term in what the agent will do to your system. That is not a contradiction between two studies; it is a statement about which axis you scored, and it means any single number quoted for "what the harness is worth" is incomplete without the axis attached.

Where the localization actually lands. Mean ASR was highest during the Harness Configuration phase on all three harnesses — "the only consistently dominant vulnerability" — with attacks succeeding "by altering security-sensitive parameters within otherwise authorized workflows." So the harness is not diffusely riskier; the risk concentrates in the phase where the harness is being set up, which is the phase that runs once, is usually not in the eval at all, and is the one part of the system a team configures by hand.

Practice. If you publish or consume a safety, containment or refusal number for a model, it is a number about a configuration. Re-measure on your own harness before relying on it, and expect the ranking to change rather than the level to shift uniformly.

Bounds. Cross-harness comparisons "reflect deployed configurations" — different system prompts, tool surfaces and state management — so the 4.3x is not an isolated harness effect and cannot be decomposed. Detection and Persistence measurement "depend on harness transparency," so part of any cross-harness difference may be visibility rather than behaviour. Low ASR "may result from refusal, tool unavailability, or general failure," which means the safer harness may be safer partly by being less capable — and utility stayed at 75.0–97.6% everywhere, which argues against that reading without settling it. Four shared models across three harnesses; 128 cases × 3 seeds per configuration.
