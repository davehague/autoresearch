A learned harness transferred across seven code agent model configurations on one fixed task for 12.4 points

Grounded in autodesign-meta-harness-optimization; belongs to evals-that-localize-the-fix.

"Across seven controlled code-agent-model configurations, integrating the learned DesignHarness consistently improves performance, increasing the average PosterBench Score from 54.99 to 67.39 (+12.4%)."

On its face it contradicts two claims held here, and on a close read it does not. harness-gains-do-not-transfer-across-tasks-or-seed-quality and a-harness-tuned-to-one-model-scored-differently-under-another-so-harness-work-is-a-per-model-artifact-with-a-recurring-bill are the vault's two arguments that harness work does not amortize. This reports a harness improving performance consistently across seven model configurations. The distinction that reconciles them is the axis of transfer: the earlier results concern transfer across tasks and across model families under tuning, and this is transfer across model configurations on one fixed task. A harness learned for poster generation working under seven backbones all doing poster generation is the weaker and more plausible claim, and it is the one a team actually needs — nobody swaps task domains, everybody swaps models.

It is the second piece of evidence that the per-model bill is smaller than feared when the task is held still. The first is a-single-harness-carried-across-five-backends-from-three-model-families-without-per-model-tuning, a system paper's secondary claim with no per-backend numbers. This one has a controlled grid and an average delta. Two sources is not a finding, but the shape of the open question sharpens: harness work amortizes across models and does not amortize across tasks, which if it holds means the harness is a per-domain asset rather than a per-deployment one, and that is a different purchase decision from the one build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling has been framing.

Magnitude, in the vault's running comparison. 12.4 points joins 13.7 (Terminal-Bench), 15.36 (tabular analytics), ~11 (nine harnesses), 7.5 and 28.9 (loop harness), and the 22.9/36.0 harness-versus-backend margins. It sits mid-range, and unlike most of them it is an average over seven configurations rather than a single pair, which makes it more robust against the 6–12 point noise floor from a-single-agent-configuration-run-three-times-has-a-standard-error-of-6-to-12-points-which-is-the-size-of-most-harness-effects-recorded-here — though no interval is reported and the per-configuration deltas are not given, so "consistently" is the authors' word and not a visible distribution.

Limits. PosterBench Score is the authors' own metric on the authors' own benchmark, scoring a soft output with no axis breakdown in the abstract. Single-run, no interval, no per-configuration numbers. The harness was learned on this task, so cross-model transfer here is transfer of an artifact optimized under conditions that include the models — the abstract does not say whether the seven evaluation configurations overlap the ones used during optimization, and if they do this is closer to a fit statistic than a transfer result.
