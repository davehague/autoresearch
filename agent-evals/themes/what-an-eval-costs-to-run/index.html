What an eval costs to run

Status: emerging

Sub-threads: cutting the item count · the dials that push the bill the other way · levers that turn out not to be levers · what teams actually reach for when the bill lands · who can afford the method that cuts the bill [reviewed 2026-08-09 @ 13 bullets]

Scope

Not which eval to run or how to build it, but what it costs every time it fires — and how much of that cost buys nothing. This theme collects evidence that eval set size is a design parameter rather than a fixed price, and the methods for setting it.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-07 — opened at emerging. Running a modern safety benchmark suite in full takes on the order of 10^5 model responses, most of which carry no ranking signal because every model in the pool passes or fails those items alike. Selecting items by how much they discriminate reproduced full-benchmark rankings at at least 80% lower cost, and up to 99.9% on one benchmark (adaptive-item-selection-reproduces-full-benchmark-rankings-at-80-to-99-percent-lower-cost-so-eval-set-size-is-a-dial, efficient-safety-benchmarking-via-item-response-theory).

  • 2026-08-07 — and the version worth adopting is the boring one: extracting a fixed informative subset once and reusing it captured up to 99.8%, against 99.9% for live adaptive selection — nearly all the benefit with no machinery left in the run loop (a-fixed-informative-item-subset-extracted-once-captures-most-of-the-savings-without-adaptive-machinery-in-the-loop). The generalizable shape: when a technique's value comes from an analysis rather than a runtime behaviour, ship the analysis output, not the analyzer.

  • 2026-08-07 — the bill acquires a second dial, and this one multiplies rather than adds. Judge count and judge family are set by fiat in every benchmark here: one source reuses its own generator model as its Faithfulness judge, another runs a cross-family panel of three on its taste axes, and neither reports what the choice bought (judge-configuration-is-an-unpriced-construction-parameter-with-two-benchmarks-choosing-opposite-defaults). Three judges is three times the judging spend on every item, forever. If a single cross-family judge captures most of what a panel does, that is the same shape of result as the fixed-subset finding above and worth as much.

  • 2026-08-07 — and the counter-pressure, stated plainly so this theme does not read as one-directional. The eval that answered the vault's contested-ground-truth gap costs 30 simulated days × 55 scenarios × 10 adapters × 3 model tiers, with a three-judge panel on three of five axes (educlaw-bench-pedagogical-agents-simulated-learners). The horizon-based designs arriving in evaluating-over-a-horizon are all like this. Item selection cuts the item count; measuring a trajectory instead of a point multiplies the run length, and nobody has looked for the shortest horizon that still surfaces the effect.

  • 2026-08-07 — a lever that looked free and is not. Sweep an explicitly stated, explicitly priced budget across the threshold where the right answer changes, and GPT-5.4's escalation rate moves from 0% to 3% (a-stated-budget-barely-reaches-agent-behaviour-so-eval-spend-has-to-be-cut-structurally-rather-than-by-instruction, ecoagent-bench-budget-constrained-agents). Prompt-level thrift — tell it the budget, tell it to prefer the cheap tool — is a request, not a control. Everything with evidence behind it in this theme changes what the harness executes rather than what the model is told, and that is now a distinction with a result behind it rather than a preference. The sharper version: to make an agent spend less, take the expensive action away rather than pricing it.

  • 2026-08-08 — the counterfactual to this theme's opening finding, observed in the wild. A team hit the closed-model cost wall against a full suite and cut it by declaring a random 300-item subset (declaring-a-random-300-item-subset-for-expensive-models-is-the-ad-hoc-version-of-informative-item-selection, simplewikisearch-specified-offline-search-environment). Random-n buys the cost saving with an unbiased estimate of the mean and no guarantee about ranking preservation, which is precisely the property the item-response-theory result optimizes for and the property a comparison needs. Two subsetting strategies are now visible in this vault and nobody has run them against each other on one suite. The cheap intermediate nobody does: run the full suite once with open models, report the subset-to-full rank correlation, and turn an assumption into a stated property.

  • 2026-08-08 — the judge dial picks up a third setting, so the spread on the judging line of every run is now a factor of several with no guidance anywhere. Self-judging at 1×, a cross-family panel of three restricted to taste axes, and three judges with majority voting across the scored axes (a-third-judge-configuration-in-one-month-three-judges-with-majority-voting-on-every-axis-widens-an-already-unjustified-range, stealthbench-operational-stealth-in-offensive-security-agents). Three papers, one month, three answers, no agreement rates reported by any of them.

  • 2026-08-08 — and a cost this theme had not been counting, from the human side. Validating a 1,151-task corpus took three annotators over a 17% sample against six criteria (a-three-annotator-audit-of-200-sampled-tasks-against-six-criteria-is-the-first-countable-human-labour-line-item-on-the-build-side, worksurface-bench-multi-surface-knowledge-routing). It is a one-time construction cost rather than a per-run one, so it sits at the edge of this theme's scope — recorded here because it is the only human-labour figure anywhere in this vault, and because the 17% sample rate makes the obligation bounded rather than open-ended.

  • 2026-08-09 — the theme's opening finding gets replicated, and the comparison it asked for arrives half-answered. A second, independent team fits IRT to eight safety benchmarks across 192 models and reports 97–99% cost cuts from roughly ten adaptively chosen items (a-second-independent-team-cuts-safety-evaluation-cost-97-to-99-percent-with-about-ten-items-so-the-item-selection-result-is-no-longer-single-source, item-response-theory-for-ai-safety). The same paper runs selected against random at equal size, and selected wins on score-recovery error (discrimination-selected-items-beat-random-subsets-of-the-same-size-which-settles-half-of-the-comparison-between-the-two-cheap-subsetting-rules). Score error is not rank preservation, which is the property this theme's open question named and the property random-n was suspected on, so the question narrows rather than closes. Worth sitting with the ten-item figure separately: a benchmark recoverable from ten items was measuring roughly one thing several hundred times, and a suite that compresses that far cannot see a regression outside what its ten items touch. Cheapness and blind spots are the same property.

  • 2026-08-09 — and the counter-bill, from a paper motivating its own method. Item-response selection has a cold start: the item parameters are fitted from a response matrix over many models and many items, which a leaderboard maintainer has as a byproduct and a single-product team does not (item-response-theory-subsetting-has-a-cold-start-cost-in-historical-response-logs-that-a-single-team-cannot-pay, cot-core-coreset-selection-for-eval-cost). That lands on the exact recommendation this theme opened with — extract a fixed subset once, offline — and splits it by reader. To extract the subset you must already have run the full suite against a fleet, which is the expense the subset exists to avoid. The theme's cheapest advice turns out to be unavailable to the team it was written for, and the disputed atom is now marked rebutted-by.

  • 2026-08-09 — a third subsetting rule, selecting on a third thing, and the only one available on day one. Roll out a zero-shot chain of thought per candidate item, embed the reasoning trajectory, cluster by logical equivalence, and take representatives — training-free, no history required (clustering-questions-by-reasoning-trajectory-rather-than-surface-text-is-a-third-subsetting-rule-whose-payoff-is-gated-by-task-complexity, cot-core-coreset-selection-for-eval-cost). Discrimination selection asks which items separate models; random-n asks nothing; this asks which items are redundant with each other. Carry the warning attached to it even if the method never pans out: deduplicating a suite by embedding question text has a surface lexical bias, so two cases with identical reasoning and different wording both survive and the suite looks more diverse than it is. And note what this bullet does not have — it is the only cost claim in this theme with no number against it, which is why it is a lead.

  • 2026-08-09 — the judge dial gets a fourth setting, and this one is an analysis rather than a spend. An IRT model that estimates judge severity as a separate latent from agent quality, over 28 agents scored by a cross-family panel (an-item-response-model-separating-agent-quality-from-judge-severity-is-a-fourth-panel-estimator-that-neither-votes-nor-averages, companionbench-judge-severity-separated-from-agent-quality). It does not cut the bill — the panel still runs on every item — which puts it alongside fixed-subset extraction in the same category: value that comes from an analysis after the fact rather than from a runtime behaviour. What it does supply is the first stated reason for a cross-family panel anywhere in this vault: same-family favouritism, named as a bias rather than assumed as good taste.

  • 2026-08-09 — and a use for the cheap subset that is not thrift. A ten-item probe run on a schedule against a hosted endpoint detects the model behind it changing (a-ten-item-psychometric-probe-detects-a-model-swapped-behind-an-api-which-turns-a-cheap-eval-subset-into-a-monitoring-instrument). Every team building on a vendor API has silent-swap exposure and almost none monitor it, because a full suite is too expensive to schedule and nothing smaller was thought to carry signal. No false-positive rate is reported, so this is a lead rather than a monitor.

Status note, 2026-08-09 — held at emerging, with the theme's own question answered on the wrong axis

Three arrivals on cost in one night, and the status does not move. The reason is the same discipline this vault applied when it walked build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling back from consolidating: the named object, or nothing.

The object this theme named was "Does a random subset preserve rankings as well as a discrimination-selected one, on a suite of a few hundred items?" What arrived answers a nearby question — selected beats random on score-recovery error, on eight large public safety benchmarks. Two of three qualifiers are unmet. Ranking is the property random-n was always suspected on, since its known virtue is an unbiased mean and its known silence is about order. And the scale is again the benchmark-maintainer scale, not the application scale where the theme claims its value.

What did genuinely change is the evidence base under the opening finding. It was one paper, one team, one benchmark family. It is now two independent teams at the same order of magnitude — still inside the safety-benchmark family, whose heterogeneous adversarial item pools are the condition that makes selection pay. Two studies of the easy case.

Against that, the night also produced the first real cost of the cost method. The cold-start requirement is not a caveat on a finding, it is an eligibility rule, and it excludes the reader this theme is written for. A theme cannot firm up on the same night its central recommendation is shown to be unavailable to most of its audience.

The useful reframing to carry forward: there are now three subsetting rules and they are not competitors on one axis. Discrimination selection needs a response matrix and buys ranking fidelity. Random-n needs nothing and buys an unbiased mean. CoT clustering needs one pass over the item pool and buys redundancy removal. The question a team should ask is not which is best but which one it is eligible for, and that is decided by what data it already has rather than by what it wants.

Why this is its own theme

It is orthogonal to the other three rather than nested under any of them. build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling asks who writes the runner; this asks how many items the runner should execute, and the answer is the same whoever wrote it. evals-that-localize-the-fix asks what a score points at; this asks what the score cost to produce. And design-for-verifiability is about the artifact being measured, not the size of the measurement.

It also earns a theme because it is the first thread here with a lever big enough to change behaviour immediately. An 80% cost reduction that preserves rankings is not a refinement — it is the difference between an eval suite that runs on every change and one that runs when someone remembers.

The evidence is thin and one-sided so far: a single paper, demonstrated entirely on safety benchmarks, whose heterogeneous adversarial item pools are precisely the condition that makes item selection pay. Nothing here has tested a hand-built task-specific suite, which is what an application team actually maintains.

Open questions

  • Does a hand-built application eval of a few hundred cases also carry 80% dead weight, or is that a property of large scraped benchmark suites?
  • How long does a fixed informative subset stay informative? As models improve past the items that discriminated, the subset quietly stops separating them, and nothing here says how often to re-extract.
  • Does cheapening the rerun change what teams measure, or only what they spend? The interesting version of this theme is whether a cheap suite gets run on changes that currently ship unevaluated.
  • Where does this collide with the expensive-metric problem — cumulative retrieval recall, human relevance labels, transcript audits — where the cost is in labelling rather than in inference?
  • What is the marginal value of the second and third judge? This is the item-selection question asked about the judging axis instead of the item axis, and it has the same shape: most of the spend may be buying agreement that was never in doubt. Four published configurations now (an-item-response-model-separating-agent-quality-from-judge-severity-is-a-fourth-panel-estimator-that-neither-votes-nor-averages), none reporting an agreement rate.
  • Can judge severity be corrected for after the fact cheaply enough to justify a smaller panel? If a severity model recovers what a three-judge panel buys from a two-judge one, the correction is the cheapest thing in this theme — it moves spend from inference into arithmetic. Nobody has tried it, and the method as published needs many agents scored by the same judges to identify the parameters, which a single team does not have.
  • Does a random subset preserve rankings as well as a discrimination-selected one — answered on score error, 2026-08-09, and still open on ranking. Selected recovers full-benchmark scores with lower error than random at the same size, across eight safety benchmarks and 192 models (discrimination-selected-items-beat-random-subsets-of-the-same-size-which-settles-half-of-the-comparison-between-the-two-cheap-subsetting-rules). Nobody has reported the same head-to-head as a rank correlation, and nobody has run it on a suite of a few hundred items. Both remain open, and the second is the one that decides whether any of this reaches an application team.
  • What is the smallest response matrix — how many models × how many items — from which item parameters recover the full-suite ranking? "Massive historical logs" is the stated blocker and the word is doing all the work. If the honest answer is twenty models, the cold-start objection mostly dissolves; if it is two hundred, item selection is permanently a benchmark-maintainer's tool.
  • Which of the three subsetting rules is a given team eligible for? This is the version of the comparison question that a team can act on, and it turns on what data they already have rather than on which method is best.
  • Does clustering by reasoning trajectory find redundancy in a hand-built product suite, where items differ by domain fact, input format, or customer segment rather than by reasoning structure? The method's own authors gate its efficacy on task complexity, and most product suites are on the wrong side of that gate.
  • What is the shortest horizon that still surfaces a regression or a forgetting event? Every long-horizon design so far picked a length by intuition.
  • Does an enforced budget — a hard cap at the harness, with running totals visible to the agent — move behaviour where a stated one does not? The negative result here is about instruction, and the obvious repair has not been tested.
  • Almost nothing in this vault reports what a run cost alongside what it scored, which is why a 15-point harness gap cannot be priced. drawai-image-to-editable-reconstruction reports cost variation across thirteen models and five harnesses and is the exception; why is that not standard, and what would it take to make comparisons cost-normalized by default?
