What the headline number averages away

Status: consolidating

Sub-threads: metrics a constant policy can win · choosing the aggregation to match what the components are to each other · the operator as a dial rather than a taxonomy · objective and constraint axes that decouple · combining judges, who are instruments rather than measurements · what the harness already decided before the operator ran · compounding across trials rather than averaging across items · which axis got printed at all · the stages a rate collapses before any operator runs · corrections that move the level without moving the ranking · large effects that cancel inside a single reported figure · an axis that is absent rather than weak · the class balance underneath an agreement figure [reviewed 2026-08-17 @ 24 bullets]

Scope

How an eval combines its parts into the number people quote. Not which parts to measure — that is evals-that-localize-the-fix — but what happens at the last step, where a set of per-item or per-axis results becomes one score, and where a mean can invert the ranking that the underlying measurements support.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-07 — opened at emerging. The cleanest demonstration: a scripted always-escalate policy with no decision-making in it achieves high micro-averaged success on a benchmark built to measure decision-making, while failing every save-oriented task (scripting-the-degenerate-policies-as-controls-exposes-a-metric-a-constant-policy-can-win, ecoagent-bench-budget-constrained-agents). Writing the degenerate policies and scoring them is the cheapest instrument in this vault — no labels, no judges, no model calls.

  • 2026-08-07 — and the repair the same source ships: aggregate as the worse of the two opposing family groups rather than as a mean over all items. Micro strict success runs 3.9–24.0%; economic consistency tops out at 7.3%, and the difference is one-sidedness the mean was absorbing (aggregating-as-the-worse-of-two-opposing-groups-removes-the-advantage-a-mean-hands-to-a-one-sided-agent). A minimum is insensitive to task mix and degrades to the mean when the groups agree, so it costs nothing when the problem is absent.

  • 2026-08-07 — the other answer, from a domain where the trade is legitimate. Fidelity and Editability are reported as separate axes because they pull against each other and different readers want different mixes; a combined quality score would be a weighting nobody can argue with because nobody can see it (reporting-two-axes-that-trade-off-leaves-the-trade-to-the-reader-where-a-single-quality-number-picks-it-silently, drawai-image-to-editable-reconstruction).

  • 2026-08-07 — and the third regime, from the release-gate end. Evaluation, Context, Compliance and Governance are not substitutes: an agent that behaves well and cannot be authorized or shut down is not 75% ready, it is unshippable, and averaging puts it level with a well-governed agent that fails testing (a-composite-over-non-substitutable-release-evidence-describes-no-real-state-so-the-dimensions-have-to-stay-un-averaged, proofagent-index-capability-is-not-readiness). Weak source — single author, own index, own tool — and the argument stands on its logic rather than on its validation.

  • 2026-08-08 — moved emerging → consolidating. The theme named its own bar when it opened: not "the numbers change" but a ranking inversion. Across 54 frontier models on ten HELM scenarios, the top five by mean score and the top five by weakest-link score are completely disjoint (mean-and-weakest-link-aggregation-produce-completely-disjoint-top-fives-on-a-54-model-public-leaderboard, evaluation-scores-are-perishable-knowledge-claims). The same source supplies a reframing worth more than the demonstration: mean and minimum are endpoints of one operator family governed by a single pessimism parameter, so the practical question is not which of three cases you are in but how pessimistic the composite should be — a config value and a line in a methods section rather than a taxonomy self-diagnosis. See the status note below for what this does and does not settle.

  • 2026-08-08 — and the same source opens a mixing this theme had not considered: aggregation across instrument types rather than across items or axes. A dashboard number blending a deterministic pass rate, a judge score, and a small human sample inherits its confidence from the strongest-looking input and its reliability from the weakest, which the source calls trust inflation (averaging-signals-of-unequal-reliability-inflates-confidence-above-the-weakest-input-so-mixing-human-judge-and-automated-scores-is-itself-an-aggregation-decision). It makes the deterministic-where-you-can, panel-where-you-cannot construction rule conditional on not averaging the two halves afterwards.

  • 2026-08-08 — the gate regime stops being an argument. 6,560 runs across a complete harness-by-backend grid, and 4,344 of them (66.22%) were unsafe yet completed the task (two-thirds-of-runs-were-unsafe-yet-complete-so-a-task-completion-score-cannot-carry-a-safety-claim, agents4d-lifecycle-runtime-safety-harness-grid). Two thirds of that dataset would read as wins on a completion dashboard. The analysis producing the theme's best number so far is a 2×2 contingency table.

  • 2026-08-08 — and a second measurement of the same decoupling, independent task family, independent team, days apart. Reporting a reckless solve rate beside success catches runs that achieved the objective at a cost the objective did not price — no model exceeds 54% safe success (scoring-a-reckless-solve-rate-beside-success-catches-the-runs-an-outcome-only-grader-certifies, stealthbench-operational-stealth-in-offensive-security-agents). The transferable form has nothing to do with security: for any agent that acts on the world, name the constraint and score violations as their own rate, because two named rates cost nothing over one and cannot be averaged into each other by accident.

  • 2026-08-09 — the judge line of the table gets an operator that is not on the table. Twenty-eight agents, a cross-family panel, and an Item Response Theory model that estimates judge severity as a latent separate from agent quality (an-item-response-model-separating-agent-quality-from-judge-severity-is-a-fourth-panel-estimator-that-neither-votes-nor-averages, companionbench-judge-severity-separated-from-agent-quality). Voting and averaging both pool judges as interchangeable instruments; this models each judge's calibration and estimates the agent net of it. That is a different move from picking a pessimism setting, and it exposes an assumption the three-regime table was making silently: the table assumes the components being combined are measurements of the thing. A judge's score is the thing plus a property of the judge, and the right response to a nuisance term is to model it rather than to aggregate over it. The panel is cross-family for a stated reason, which is also new here — same-family favouritism, named as a bias rather than asserted as taste.

  • 2026-08-09 — the fourth unrelated task family declining to emit one number, and from the softest domain yet. Ten theory-derived capabilities reported un-averaged, beside a deterministic measure of whether deeper disclosure was earned (ten-theory-derived-capabilities-kept-un-averaged-beside-one-deterministic-axis-is-the-two-axis-pattern-arriving-in-a-domain-with-no-crisp-success-condition). Every prior instance of the two-axis pattern had something mechanical to anchor on — pixels, a security constraint, a workspace side effect. Companionship has none, and the team found a mechanical axis anyway by scoring a state the agent had to earn. That removes the standing excuse: "our domain is too soft to decompose" is now contradicted from inside the softest domain here. The transferable move is the search — look for the one thing in a soft domain that is mechanically checkable, and report it beside the rubric rather than inside it.

  • 2026-08-09 — and one level below the operator, a decision the harness was making for you. Inspect AI now preserves NaN scores rather than dropping or coercing them (preserving-a-nan-score-instead-of-coercing-it-is-an-aggregation-decision-the-harness-was-making-silently, inspect-ai-changelog-0-3-253-to-0-3-254). A sample the scorer could not score has three fates and each is a different claim: coerce to zero and "not measured" becomes "measured and failed"; drop it and the denominator changes silently; preserve it and the decision goes to whoever computes the summary. The bias is not symmetric — coercion punishes flaky infrastructure, dropping rewards it, and the samples a grader fails on correlate with the samples the agent struggles on. The cheapest integrity check available on any eval run follows directly: compare scored n against submitted n. Nothing in this vault reports it.

  • 2026-08-10 — the pessimism parameter turns out to have a second axis, and the arithmetic on it is much harsher. pass@k — succeed at least once in k attempts — and pass^k — succeed every time — are the optimistic and weakest-link endpoints of one operator family applied across trials of a single task rather than across items (pass-at-k-and-pass-to-the-k-are-the-optimistic-and-weakest-link-endpoints-of-one-operator-family-applied-across-trials-of-a-single-task, anthropic-demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). Averaging over items degrades gracefully; compounding over trials is exponential and indifferent to how good you are — 75% per trial is 42% at three trials, 90% is 73%, 99% is 97%. This is the cheapest correction in the theme: no new runs, no grader, one exponentiation on data already collected, and it applies to every eval here since almost none of them repeats a trial at all. The assumption doing all the work is independence between trials, which is plausibly false through caching, a deterministic environment fault, or a prompt the model reliably misreads — and nobody reports trial-to-trial correlation.

  • 2026-08-10 — a third kind of information loss, and it is not an average. Assign each run to the furthest lifecycle stage its trace supports and the distribution shows configurations with nearly identical endpoint rates stopping at different stages (configurations-with-nearly-identical-endpoint-rates-stop-at-different-lifecycle-stages-so-the-final-rate-conceals-how-close-the-run-came, harnesssafe-persistent-carriers-across-agent-harnesses). An endpoint rate is not a mean over ordered components, it is a projection keeping only whether the last one was reached, so two systems that tie on it can be one step and five steps from failure. The same table also supplies this theme's oldest pattern in a new place: the configuration with the best overall score is worst on one family, 47.0 against a leading 70.0. Both obligations are computable from traces already stored — report the stage distribution beside the endpoint rate, and quote the worst family beside any composite.

  • 2026-08-10 — and the decision that happens before any operator runs: which axis got printed. Across nine harnesses and 23 benchmarks, final-answer correctness spans 0.57–0.68 while planning, tool use and efficiency vary much more widely (final-answer-correctness-spans-eleven-points-across-nine-harnesses-while-planning-tool-use-and-efficiency-vary-much-more-so-the-headline-axis-is-where-the-harness-effect-hides, a2e-agent-auditing-engine). Every other bullet here is about combining components within an axis; this is a level up. The outcome axis is a low-variance summary of a high-variance system, which makes a comparison run on it not merely less informative but statistically underpowered for the difference it exists to detect. Thin sampling — five tasks per harness-benchmark pair — so treat the band as a shape rather than a measurement.

  • 2026-08-12 — the decomposition that sits above every other bullet here, and it is of the pipeline rather than the results. A single reported rate collapses four stages: exposure (did the intervention reach the agent), execution (what the agent did), observation (which evidence you looked at), adjudication (how you labelled it) — and only one of the four is a property of the system under test (a-reported-rate-is-a-composite-of-exposure-execution-observation-and-adjudication-and-two-of-the-four-have-nothing-to-do-with-the-agent, redagentbench-executable-red-teaming-faithful-measurement). Everything else in this theme aggregates over things the agent produced. This says the number can move for three reasons that have nothing to do with the agent, and it is not safety-specific: a task success rate has the same four stages. Read that way it is a superset of most of this vault's standing complaints — the seven unreported environment parameters live in exposure, transcript-versus-state in observation, judge severity and rubric choice in adjudication, and the entire model-or-harness argument sits inside execution. First framing here that gives them one address. Single study, one week old, and the framework is an analytical claim rather than a measured one — but the same paper then prices two of the four stages, below.

  • 2026-08-12 — and observation turns out to cost about ten points on its own. Same runs, same rubric, judge shown the trajectory versus judge shown the final state: state-judge rate runs 7.73–11.72 points higher, and the two disagree on the label for 12.97–21.20% of paired cases (showing-the-judge-the-final-state-instead-of-the-transcript-moves-the-reported-rate-about-ten-points-and-flips-the-label-on-up-to-a-fifth-of-cases). The disagreement rate is the load-bearing half — a uniform 10-point shift would be a sensitivity difference, but a fifth of cases flipping in both directions means the two views are not measuring the same thing. Any failure vocabulary, error-analysis pass or repair-owner assignment built on transcript labels is one-fifth arbitrary with respect to a choice nobody reports making. The direction is consistent and uncomfortable: transcript grading under-counts, because the agent's account of its run is not its run.

  • 2026-08-12 — the un-averaging argument ships as a framework option, which is the first time this theme's recommendation exists as code. Inspect AI adds collect, a score reducer whose job is to not reduce — keep each scorer's value as a list — plus aggregate(key, agg=...) to summarize one key of a structured score without dragging the others through the same operator (a-reducer-that-preserves-every-scorers-value-instead-of-combining-them-is-un-averaging-shipped-as-a-framework-option, inspect-ai-changelog-0-3-256-to-0-3-257). That is the plumbing for the two-axis pattern this vault has recorded in six benchmarks that each wrote it by hand — strict-pass beside process reward, route F1 beside answer accuracy, completion beside safety. The honest limit: it is an option and not a default, and the whole reason the mean is everywhere is that it is what happens when nobody chooses. The same vendor flipped a default to force a disclosure two releases earlier, which is the stronger instrument and was available here too.

  • 2026-08-12 — and the judge panel's premise gets undercut from the human side. Practitioner-stated calibration figures: human annotators "can miss as many as 50% of the defects," and human inter-rater reliability is often as low as kappa 0.2–0.3 (human-raters-agree-at-kappa-0-2-to-0-3-so-a-majority-vote-of-judges-converges-on-a-majority-rather-than-on-truth, eugeneyan-product-evals-in-three-steps). Five judge configurations are catalogued in this vault and every argument between them assumed disagreement is noise around a real label. At kappa 0.3 there is no agreed label, so a majority of three reports what a majority of three thought. It is also the strongest available argument for the binary pass/fail rule the same source insists on — binary labels are not chosen for simplicity, they are chosen because they are the only labels two people reproduce. Round numbers asserted from experience, no study cited; recorded because the alternative is continuing to reason about panels with no estimate of the human floor at all.

  • 2026-08-13 — the first correction recorded here that moves every number and barely moves the ranking, which is a useful negative result for this theme. Harness-IF withholds each rule to establish what the agent would do unprompted, scores only the rules it violates by default, and finds all 12 frontier models overstated by 3.6–7.4 points, mean 5.81 — then reports that prior control "leaves the top build unchanged and exchanges three adjacent rank pairs" (re-running-with-the-rule-withheld-separates-compliance-from-coincidence-and-aggregate-scores-overstate-by-3-6-to-7-4-points-on-every-model, harness-if-against-prior-accuracy). Set that beside mean-and-weakest-link-aggregation-produce-completely-disjoint-top-fives-on-a-54-model-public-leaderboard, where the operator choice reordered completely, and the theme's claim sharpens: un-averaging bites hardest when the parts trade off against each other, and least when the correction shifts everyone similarly. This one is a bias rather than a dispersion — known sign, every model, always inflating — which is a worse thing to live with than noise and a different thing from what this theme usually collects. The precondition is also this theme's own recommendation: you cannot withhold "the task," but with 256 rules receiving individual verdicts across 60 items you can withhold one rule, so per-item un-averaging is what makes the control affordable at all.

  • 2026-08-13 — a second independent arrival at task completion cannot carry a quality claim, and this one brings a checkable vocabulary. Per-check scoring across 15 agent-model configurations exposes agents that "satisfy short-term objectives while leaving non-durable changes, broken distributed invariants, unsafe side effects, and uncleaned state behind" (per-check-scoring-names-four-deterministically-checkable-failure-classes-a-terminal-success-check-cannot-see, infrabench-durability-and-per-check-scoring). two-thirds-of-runs-were-unsafe-yet-complete-so-a-task-completion-score-cannot-carry-a-safety-claim said this from a 6,560-run safety grid; unrelated domain, unrelated team, same conclusion. What is new is the cost profile: most un-averaging arguments here add a soft axis and multiply the judging bill, and these four are additional deterministic assertions against the same end state. A team with a passing task check can add "does it survive a restart" and "did it leave anything behind" without introducing a judge. Limited by domain — infrastructure has an exhaustively interrogable end state and most LLM applications emit prose — so what transfers is the question rather than the checklist: what does my success check not look at, and is any of it cheap to assert?

  • 2026-08-13 — a fifth domain keeps two axes apart, and it is the first where both axes are soft. Faithfulness and helpfulness are reported separately on an explicit decoupling argument — "a faithful answer isn't always a helpful answer" (two-soft-axes-are-kept-un-averaged-on-a-stated-decoupling-argument-rather-than-because-one-of-them-is-computable, eugeneyan-evaluating-long-context-qa-systems). Every previous instance in this theme had one crisp axis anchoring the pair, which allowed the un-averaging to be dismissed as a technical convenience. Here neither is computable and they are still held apart, on how they behave rather than how they are measured — which is the stronger and more portable form of the claim. The trade is directionally specific and familiar: refusing more often raises faithfulness and lowers helpfulness, so one quality number silently picks a point on that curve. Also worth copying: faithfulness is scored on a spectrum with three named labels for the no-answer case — hallucination, incorrect refusal, correct refusal — which separates two failures with opposite repairs.

  • 2026-08-13 — and a position on judge combination that contradicts every configuration catalogued here, taken from the human side: appoint one "benevolent dictator" domain expert, binary labels, no panel unless you are large or multi-domain (appointing-one-benevolent-dictator-annotator-is-the-coherent-response-to-kappa-0-3-and-it-trades-correctness-for-a-stable-target, hamel-llm-evals-faq). Read against last night's kappa 0.2–0.3 finding it is the coherent conclusion rather than a lazy one: if there is no latent label, a panel's average is not closer to truth, and a single consistent labeller at least yields a reproducible target. Consistency chosen over correctness, deliberately. Two consequences nobody states. The eval then encodes one person's taste with no mechanism that can detect it being wrong, and an-inter-rater-agreement-metric-in-the-runner-turns-judge-agreement-from-a-reporting-norm-into-a-one-line-call becomes unavailable — you cannot compute alpha on one rater. The reconciliation worth holding: the panel literature here is written by benchmark operators scoring many systems, and this advice is written for teams scoring one product, so it may be a fact about eligibility rather than a disagreement about method.

  • 2026-08-14 — a new way for a headline number to lie, and for once it is not aggregation. "GPT-5.6-sol's matched gap of -3.6 points hides -64.3 points of damage and +60.7 points of compensation" (a-matched-score-gap-of-3-6-points-conceals-64-3-points-of-damage-and-60-7-points-of-compensation, quotebench-execution-transport-and-matched-scores). Nothing is being combined here — no mean over items, no mean over axes, no majority over judges, no weakest-link over trials. One configuration takes a very large hit from the execution transport, recovers almost all of it by adapting its generation, and the residual is what gets printed. The reader sees −3.6 and concludes the factor did not matter; the true reading is that it mattered enormously twice and this particular model happens to be good at compensating, which is the fact that predicts what happens when the transport changes again. Note the geometry against re-running-with-the-rule-withheld-separates-compliance-from-coincidence-and-aggregate-scores-overstate-by-3-6-to-7-4-points-on-every-model, which moved every number and barely moved the ranking: this is the mirror image — a number that barely moves while everything underneath it moves a great deal. Together they sharpen the theme's rule past aggregation: what a reported figure is safe to be read as depends on what could have cancelled inside it, and nobody reports that. The check is cheap wherever the intermediate condition can be run — here it needed only replaying stored replies through both paths, at no new model cost. The same source also reports the deployment configuration reordering models, one unambiguous reversal in 26 comparable pairs plus four on single-task margins, which is the first rank reversal here caused by neither the aggregation operator nor a correction. Limits: one configuration's numbers from an abstract, 56 tasks, no repeated runs — though the reported spread of the compensation term across models (30.4 to 60.7 points for six of eight configurations, zero or slightly negative for two) is what makes the net figure untrustworthy in general rather than in this case.

  • 2026-08-15 — the widest gap between two axes this theme has recorded, and one of them is a capability that is simply absent. Best model: 70.4% on single-hop endpoint tasks, 50–51% on compositional APIs, and 2.4% on unanswerable queries under stated tool-use policies (an-unanswerable-query-rate-of-2-4-percent-sits-under-a-headline-in-the-fifties-so-the-refusal-axis-has-to-be-printed-separately, vakra-multi-hop-apis-under-tool-use-policies). Pooled, the benchmark reads as middling competence in the fifties; split, one axis is near zero. Knowing you cannot answer is not a harder version of answering — it is a different axis — so this is the non-substitutability rule, not a lossiness complaint: a system at 60/2.4 and one at 45/45 report alike and behave nothing alike, and the first one fails confidently. Credit where due, VAKRA prints the axis rather than pooling it, which is the only reason the number is visible; the small generalisable request is that any tool-use eval containing questions its tools cannot answer report that subset on its own line, and most do not contain such questions at all.

  • 2026-08-16 — the panel arithmetic that five of this theme's six judge configurations rely on assumes the errors disperse, and two backbones here have opposite signs. "Failure analysis across two LLM backends uncovers qualitatively opposite failure profiles, one conservative and the other permissive, linked to the backbone's precision-recall characteristics" (two-judge-backbones-produce-opposite-conservative-and-permissive-failure-profiles-so-a-mixed-panel-averages-to-a-rate-matching-neither, mobilejudgebench-benchmarking-llm-judges). Majority voting, cross-family panels, severity separation and per-axis reporting all treat rater disagreement as noise around a common target. Opposed bias does not disperse — it cancels into a middling rate describing no judge's actual behaviour, with the residual set by which models happened to be in the panel. Same failure as averaging-signals-of-unequal-reliability-inflates-confidence-above-the-weakest-input-so-mixing-human-judge-and-automated-scores-is-itself-an-aggregation-decision, one level in: not unequal reliability but opposite direction. It also finally supplies a mechanism for judge severity, which an-item-response-model-separating-agent-quality-from-judge-severity-is-a-fourth-panel-estimator-that-neither-votes-nor-averages could only model as a latent — it is the backbone's precision-recall operating point, so you can predict which kind of judge you have by looking at the model instead of fitting one. And it makes judge selection a product decision (which error costs you more, a missed defect or a false alarm) currently made by whoever picked the cheapest model. The disclosure is this theme's own instruction applied to the grader for the first time: report the judge's false-pass and false-fail rates separately instead of one agreement figure. Two backends, one domain, described qualitatively with no rates attached; the panel consequence is this vault's inference and nobody has run the mixed panel to check it.

  • 2026-08-16 — the agreement floor this theme has been generalising from turns out to be a property of the task. Human-human Cohen's κ = 0.97 on TriviaQA and human-human agreement 81% on MT-Bench, against the κ 0.2–0.3 among product annotators that has been load-bearing here since 2026-08-12 (human-human-agreement-runs-from-kappa-0-2-to-0-97-depending-on-the-task-so-the-agreement-floor-is-a-property-of-the-task-not-of-people, eugeneyan-effectiveness-of-llm-evaluators). The low figure is the reason the benevolent-dictator position reads as coherent rather than lazy — with no latent label a panel average is not closer to truth — and that argument is sound in the regime it was measured in and does not extend. The disputed atom is marked rebutted-by. What replaces it is more useful than either number: measure your own annotators before you measure your judge. κ 0.9 among your reviewers puts you in the regime where a latent label exists, a panel converges and a judge can be validated normally; κ 0.3 puts you in the dictator regime where any agreement metric measures agreement with one person's taste. Two annotators and an afternoon, and it decides which of the six catalogued configurations you are even eligible for. It also supplies the variable behind the standoff — the panel literature is written by benchmark operators on crisp tasks and the dictator advice by practitioners on soft ones, so neither camp is wrong about its own setting. Suggestive only: every figure is a secondhand quotation, and "0.2 to 0.97" spans incomparable studies, tasks, rubrics and years rather than a measured spread.

  • 2026-08-17 — the instruction this theme issued nine days ago, arriving independently from the human side, with the condition that makes it enforceable. "I used agreement between the model and human evaluator because our dataset was roughly balanced (about 50% of instances were failures)," and with imbalanced classes raw agreement is "generally not recommended" — measure "precision and recall separately" (raw-agreement-is-only-admissible-near-balanced-classes-so-an-imbalanced-eval-has-to-report-judge-precision-and-recall-separately, hamel-using-llm-as-a-judge-complete-guide). two-judge-backbones-produce-opposite-conservative-and-permissive-failure-profiles-so-a-mixed-panel-averages-to-a-rate-matching-neither reached report false-pass and false-fail separately from two backbones with measured opposite biases; this reaches it from class arithmetic, and the two routes are genuinely different — one says a single number hides which direction the judge errs, the other says a single number is dominated by the majority class regardless of direction. The regime matters more than the rule, because this vault has been in the wrong one. two-hundred-samples-buys-plus-or-minus-2-4-points-on-a-3-percent-defect-rate-and-halving-that-error-costs-four-times-the-samples sizes a product eval around a 3% defect rate, at which a judge that passes everything scores 97% agreement — and this theme has been reasoning about panels, κ and Krippendorff's α as though agreement were the acceptance metric. The cheap fix is not to abandon agreement but to print the base rate beside it: an agreement figure with no class balance next to it is uninterpretable. One further consequence worth noticing: holding-out-25-percent-and-reporting-precision-recall-and-kappa-is-the-first-stated-acceptance-procedure-for-a-judge deliberately seeds 50–100 failures into a 200+ set, which is that procedure engineering itself into the balanced regime — so the numbers it produces describe judge behaviour on a constructed balance and not on the traffic the judge will see. A practitioner's methodological aside, no study, no worked comparison; standard classification practice, recorded because six judge configurations here report an agreement-shaped number and none states its balance.

The rule the bullets are converging on

Decide what the components are to each other before choosing how to combine them. Three regimes, three aggregations:

Components are…AggregationFailure if you use a mean instead
substitutes — more of one really does compensatemeannone; this is the case a mean is for
opposing groups that must all holdminimuma one-sided policy banks its favoured group and wins
gates — pass or the thing does not shipconjunction, kept outside the arithmetica failed gate is offset by a strong score elsewhere

The mean is the default in every eval framework and the right answer in one of three cases. Nothing in this vault reports checking which case it is in.

A row the table was missing, added 2026-08-09. All three rows assume the components are measurements of the thing being scored. Judges are not: a judge's score is the thing plus that judge's severity, and combining several of them with any operator on the table propagates the nuisance term instead of removing it. The fourth regime is therefore instruments with their own calibration, and its handling is a severity model rather than an aggregation — estimate the agent net of the judge. Available in practice only to whoever scores many agents with the same judges, which is a leaderboard operator and not a team scoring one system.

Two more rows the table does not cover, added 2026-08-10. Both break the table's other silent assumption — that the components sit side by side. Trials of one task compound rather than combine, so the operator family is the same and the arithmetic is exponential (pass-at-k-and-pass-to-the-k-are-the-optimistic-and-weakest-link-endpoints-of-one-operator-family-applied-across-trials-of-a-single-task). Stages of one run are ordered, so the endpoint is a projection rather than an aggregate, and the distribution over stages is the thing being discarded (configurations-with-nearly-identical-endpoint-rates-stop-at-different-lifecycle-stages-so-the-final-rate-conceals-how-close-the-run-came). The table is about which operator to use on a set; these are about components that were never a set.

Two amendments from 2026-08-08. First, the top two rows are not a dichotomy — they are the endpoints of one operator family on a pessimism parameter, so the middle is reachable and reportable rather than a compromise nobody has a name for. Second, the table now applies to judges as well as to axes: stealthbench-operational-stealth-in-offensive-security-agents resolves judge disagreement by majority vote, which is a gate, while the two prior sources using judges never state whether their reported score is a vote or a mean over judges. A mean over judges reintroduces this theme's whole problem one level down.

Why this is its own theme

It is the last step of every eval here, and none of the existing four themes owns it. evals-that-localize-the-fix is about decomposing a score so a failure routes to a repair — it argues for splitting, and stops before saying what single number goes on the dashboard afterwards. If that number is a mean over the components the split just separated, the decomposition has been undone downstream of the analysis that produced it, and the split bought a diagnostic while leaving the gate broken. what-an-eval-costs-to-run asks what the score cost; this asks whether it survives contact with a policy trying to game it. design-for-verifiability is about the artifact, not the arithmetic.

What made it worth a theme rather than a bullet is that the founding sources reached the same structural problem from budget economics, image reconstruction, governance readiness, and scientific coding, none citing the others' framing, and that the corrective is cheap enough to apply tomorrow.

Status note, 2026-08-08 — moved emerging → consolidating

The theme opened the previous day at emerging with four bullets, three of which were design decisions rather than measured results. It named the object that would move it: "Does the min-versus-mean choice change any published ranking, or only the absolute numbers? A ranking inversion is what would move this theme off emerging." That object arrived, on a public leaderboard, at a scale nobody can wave off — 54 frontier models, ten scenarios, and the two top-fives share no models at all.

Moving on it is the consistent call, not a generous one. This vault walked build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling back from consolidating for accepting a weaker substitute for a stated bar, and held evals-that-localize-the-fix at contested through four agreeing sources because none delivered the measurement it asked for. The discipline in both cases was the named object or nothing. Here the named object arrived. Refusing it would be moving the goalposts, which is the same failure in the opposite direction.

Two discounts, on the record. The HELM demonstration comes from a position paper making its own case, and weakest-link is the maximally pessimistic endpoint of the operator family — showing the two extremes disagree is weaker than showing a defensible middle setting reorders. And it is one leaderboard. Neither discount touches the direction of the finding, and consolidating is not consensus.

What earns the move beyond that one result is that the theme now has measured evidence on two of its three regimes rather than one. The gate regime was pure argument yesterday, resting on a single-author governance index; it now has 66.22% unsafe-yet-complete on 6,560 runs and a 54% safe-success ceiling from an unrelated team on an unrelated task family, arriving within a fortnight of each other and neither citing the other. Three independent routes to "these components are not substitutes," two of them with numbers.

What would move it to consensus: the same re-ranking on two or three more public leaderboards under a mid-range pessimism parameter, and one report of a team's own aggregation choice changing a decision they went on to make.

Open questions

  • What is the class balance under every agreement figure in this vault? Six judge configurations report an agreement-shaped number and none states its base rate, which at a 3% defect rate makes 97% agreement the score of a judge that does nothing.
  • Are trials of one task independent? pass^k assumes they are, and caching, deterministic environment faults, and prompts a model reliably misreads all argue otherwise. The gap between an empirical pass^k and p^k is the correlation, and it is measurable from any suite already running repeats.
  • Does anyone publish a ranking under pass@k beside the same ranking under pass^k? That is the trial-axis version of the HELM disjoint-top-five demonstration, it needs no new runs on a suite with repeats, and it would say whether the trial operator reorders as thoroughly as the item operator does.
  • How many published agent benchmarks fail the degenerate-policy test? Nobody has run that survey, and it is a weekend of work against public leaderboards.
  • Does the min-versus-mean choice change any published ranking, or only the absolute numbers? Answered 2026-08-08. It changes the ranking, completely, on HELM across 54 models (mean-and-weakest-link-aggregation-produce-completely-disjoint-top-fives-on-a-54-model-public-leaderboard). The follow-on: does a mid-range pessimism parameter also reorder, or only the extreme endpoint? That is what decides whether this is a real reporting obligation or an artifact of picking the most conservative operator available.
  • A minimum is high-variance by construction — it is determined by whichever group is worst and discards information about the others. What is the right pairing of a gate metric with a reported measurement?
  • Where does the substitutes-versus-gates line fall for judge axes specifically? A three-judge panel scoring five axes has this problem five times. One source states a resolution rule — majority vote, which is a gate (a-third-judge-configuration-in-one-month-three-judges-with-majority-voting-on-every-axis-widens-an-already-unjustified-range); a second models severity instead of pooling at all; the rest do not say whether they vote or average. Four configurations, three named estimators, one convention: none.
  • How far does a severity correction move a published ranking? Publishing one leaderboard twice — pooled, then severity-corrected, with the rank changes reported — is the judge-axis version of the mean-versus-weakest-link demonstration, and it would be worth as much. Nobody has done it, and until somebody does, severity separation is a technique rather than a finding.
  • What does a harness do with a sample its scorer could not score, and does the reported n match the number submitted? A cheap check nobody runs, and the two silent options bias in opposite directions.
  • What does the constraint-rate practice cost against transcript auditing, and how much do they overlap? A named constraint rate catches what you prespecified; an audit catches what you did not. Nobody has run both on the same runs and reported what only the audit found.
  • Is the gap between an objective rate and a constraint rate itself worth reporting as a third number, the way the strict-versus-process gap may be? Two sources now report both axes and neither reports the spread.
  • strict-pass-and-process-reward-diverge-in-every-configuration-so-partial-credit-is-a-second-question-rather-than-a-rescue-for-hard-benchmarks is a fifth case: two gradings of the same run that disagree in every configuration. Is the gap between them a third number worth reporting, or just the shadow of the two?
