The judge as a maintained dependency

Status: emerging

Sub-threads: the lifecycle a judge has after it is accepted · what monitoring costs and what triggers a re-tune · the panel as a configuration with named components · what a panel actually recovers and from what · tuning the rubric rather than the model · the roles one judge is asked to play at once · what the judge programme is really buying [reviewed 2026-08-22 @ 8 bullets]

Scope

Split from design-for-verifiability on 2026-08-22. That theme was carrying two questions that answer to different evidence, and the judge sub-thread had grown to roughly forty concept notes inside it — flagged as overdue for a split on 2026-08-20 and again on 2026-08-21.

The boundary:

  • design-for-verifiability keeps the design question. Can this artifact be checked at all? Where does the check go? Is this instrument admissible — the competence floor, within-item pairing, the random-gate null, what the judge is allowed to look at, whose labels the ground truth is. These are questions you answer once, before deployment, and the answer is a property of the instrument and the task.
  • This theme takes the operations question. A judge that passed its screen is now a component in a running system. What does it cost to keep it aligned? What tells you it has drifted? Who tunes it and against what signal? What does a panel configuration consist of, and which parts of it are load-bearing? These are questions you answer repeatedly, forever, and the answers are properties of an operating programme.

Nothing has been moved retroactively. Existing judge concepts stay where they were filed; the split takes effect going forward, and this map cross-references the design-side material rather than claiming it.

The seed for the theme predates the split. On 2026-08-13 a practitioner's assertion was recorded here that an LLM judge costs a hundred labels plus weekly maintenance and is therefore a dependency rather than an artifact (an-llm-judge-costs-a-hundred-labels-plus-weekly-maintenance-so-it-is-a-dependency-rather-than-an-artifact). That claim had nowhere to live. It does now.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-22 — opened at emerging on the first production judge with an operating history in this vault (netflix-judge-lifecycle-recommendation-explanations). Four named phases — birth, training, deployment, monitoring — and each one supplies a number the vault did not have. Birth: ~900 human-labelled items with written rationales, ~54% failures by deliberate over-sampling, mixed from expert-crafted, synthesized-boundary and production streams (nine-hundred-labelled-items-with-written-rationales-is-what-a-production-judge-benchmark-cost). That is the top of a ladder this vault built from below — 30 items with a saturation rule, a 20/50 graduated ladder, a practitioner's hundred, 200 samples for ±2.4 points — and the gap is not a contradiction: it is the difference between a judge that steers your iteration and a judge that gates what ships.
  • 2026-08-22 — monitoring gets a computable trigger, and it is anchored to human disagreement rather than to a number someone picked: M(J) ≥ mean(M(H)) − 2·sd(M(H)), evaluated on a weekly sample of ~300 explanations stratified across judge outcomes (a-judge-drift-alarm-set-at-two-standard-deviations-of-human-disagreement-has-never-fired-in-production). It degenerates correctly — where raters agree tightly the judge is held tightly, and where human κ sits at 0.2–0.3 the band is wide because there is no tighter target available. It also prices maintenance for the first time: ~300 labels a week, indefinitely, which is roughly three times the build-time label count per year. And it comes with the warning this vault applies to every gate: the alarm has never fired since launch, and a monitor with zero recorded positives is indistinguishable from a logging feature until someone runs a degraded judge through it.
  • 2026-08-22 — the tuning signal is the novel part, and it exposes a hole in every acceptance procedure recorded here. RART tunes the rubric, not the model, on a focus set built from two error classes: label mismatches, and items where judge and human agree the item fails and disagree about why (a-production-judge-was-tuned-on-why-it-failed-an-item-not-just-whether-it-failed). Precision/recall/κ on a holdout, TPR/TNR with bias correction, within-item paired AUC — all of them grade the verdict, so all of them pass a judge that is right for the wrong reason. That judge is fine as a gate and useless as a critic. Scoring the second class needs a reasoning meta-judge, and this one was validated first at 98.6% on 300 pairs, which is the discipline this vault has repeatedly found missing when a source adds a judge to fix a judge.
  • 2026-08-22 — one judge, two roles, and the second role is what forces the extra validation. The same tuned judge is the conjunctive gate — pass every must-have criterion or do not ship — and the critic inside a generate → judge → revise loop capped at K=3, clearing over 75% pass rate at that budget (one-tuned-judge-served-as-both-the-gate-and-the-critic-under-a-retry-budget-of-three). Read carelessly, "one judge serves many roles" is a cost argument. Read carefully it is a constraint: a gate consumes one bit, a critic consumes the prose, and an acceptance test on verdicts qualifies the judge for exactly one of those jobs.
  • 2026-08-22 — the panel is a configuration with named components, and two of them turn out to matter more than the thing everyone reports. From a moderated-consensus jury on step-level defect detection (reasoning-jury-moderated-consensus): a three-model open-weight jury reaches 80.8 balanced F1 against 83.9 for the best single frontier judge (a-three-model-open-weight-jury-reached-within-three-points-of-the-best-frontier-judge) — but the frontier jury gains only half a point over its strongest member. Panels do not add capability, they recover it, and the recoverable amount is roughly your gap to the frontier. Which gives the panel decision a shape it has not had here: enormous value to a team constrained by spend, near-zero to a team that already has the best judge.
  • 2026-08-22 — and the mechanism is not the one this vault has been assuming. A homogeneous jury — three independent samples of one model — gains +13.0 points, against +15.4 for a five-model cross-family jury (three-samples-of-one-model-gained-13-points-so-most-of-the-panel-effect-is-sampling-not-diversity). About 85% of the panel effect survives with the vendor variable removed. Several sources here have been read as evidence that panels work because different families fail differently; that systematic component is real and it is worth about 2.4 points, and it is the expensive part to procure and to keep running. Before buying a multi-vendor panel, resample the judge you have. Pushes back on the cross-family-panel reading this vault has been accumulating since 2026-08-07.
  • 2026-08-22 — the moderator is a component with a 6-point swing and nobody reports which one they used (the-moderator-of-a-judge-panel-is-a-component-with-a-six-point-swing-that-nobody-reports): 2.6–3.7 points under deliberation, 6.1–6.4 under consolidation. Larger than most effects in this vault. The asymmetry is design guidance rather than trivia — under consolidation the moderator is the sole integrator, so its priors become the aggregation function, while deliberation makes jurors argue and revise first with a content-blind moderator running the discussion. The debate does work that would otherwise fall entirely on one undocumented component choice. Add moderator and consensus mode to the judge reporting norm, which this vault has now extended six times.
  • 2026-08-22 — what the whole programme buys, stated honestly by the only source here in a position to know. Four phases, 900 rationale-annotated labels, weekly human sampling, a few thousand USD a week — and a five-week A/B across tens of millions of members returning +0.2% and +0.3% relative, p<0.05, plus zero quality-related takedowns (the-payoff-of-a-four-phase-judge-programme-was-zero-takedowns-not-a-lift-the-a-b-moved-0-2-percent). The third result is the one that justifies the spend, and it is a risk argument, not an improvement argument. The judge did not make the explanations better in any way a member-facing metric could detect at meaningful magnitude; it made them safe enough to ship at all. Those two justifications compete against completely different alternatives and this vault has been conflating them.

Open questions

  • Does anyone's drift alarm fire? Two standard deviations of a noisy quantity estimated from ~300 weekly items is a generous band, and the one production system reporting an operating history has never tripped it. Running a deliberately degraded judge through an existing monitor is a half-day experiment and it is the only thing that distinguishes a well-built judge from a decorative threshold.
  • How much of the panel literature in this vault survives the homogeneous-jury control? Cross-family panels on the soft axes, the conservative/permissive backbone split, the copy/complement/specialist taxonomy — none of them ran the three-samples-of-one-model arm. It costs one rerun each and it would resize a claim this vault has repeated for two weeks.
  • What is the re-validation trigger when the judge's own model changes? Every mechanism here monitors the judge against humans on a fixed cadence. None of them treats a vendor model update as an event. A judge whose backbone silently changes has changed instrument mid-experiment, and the weekly sample would take a week to notice — if the drift exceeds a band built to tolerate human disagreement.
  • Is a judge validated as a gate ever re-validated before being promoted to a critic? One production system needed a new tuning method to make that promotion safe. Everyone else appears to promote silently, and the failure — a correct verdict with a confabulated reason sending the generator to fix the wrong thing — is invisible in every acceptance metric currently in use.
  • What does a judge programme cost per year, all in? The pieces now exist: ~900 labels to build, ~300 a week to monitor, judge inference separable from system inference in two runners, and a panel priced at 0.08–0.16× a frontier judge. Nobody has added them up, and the total is the number that decides whether a judge or a deterministic verifier is the cheaper instrument for a given check.
