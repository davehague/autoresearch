Design for verifiability

Status: emerging

Sub-threads: redesign the artifact so it can be checked · where the check is placed · which checks can be deterministic at all · harvesting the check from something already written down · the same argument reached from the grader's side · what the choice of evidence is worth in points · restructuring the judgment when you cannot restructure the artifact · making the check itself falsifiable · when the oracle is external and mutable · validating the validation set · what a judge has to be stronger than · what a judge has to be stronger than · the examples in the judge prompt as a component with a sign · whose labels the ground truth is · how many criteria to put in one call · noticing versus acting · what the judge is allowed to look at · re-running the instrument to find its own resolution · auditing the grader's source rather than its verdicts · keeping the criterion out of the observation channel · the competence floor a judge has to clear · within-item pairing rather than pooled agreement · the random gate as the null for a gate what the deterministic instrument structurally cannot see · the grader defect that arrives from the vendor [reviewed 2026-08-22 @ 46 bullets]

Scope

The claim that some evals are expensive because the product made them expensive, and that changing the artifact is cheaper than measuring it. Distinct from how to build or choose an eval — this is the step before either.

Through-line

  • 2026-08-07 — opened at emerging. Difficulty evaluating an AI product is read as a design signal rather than a measurement problem: artifacts hard for you to verify are hard for users too, and the move is to decompose them into units a user can accept or reject (eval-difficulty-is-a-product-design-signal-so-the-first-move-is-restructuring-outputs-into-units-a-user-can-accept-or-reject, hard-to-eval-is-a-product-smell).

  • 2026-08-07 — the mechanism underneath all three of the source's cases is the same: anchor the output to something already trusted and show only the delta, which makes the check local and deterministic with no judge in the path (anchoring-generated-output-to-a-vetted-reference-and-showing-only-the-diff-converts-whole-artifact-judgment-into-cheap-local-checks).

  • 2026-08-07 — the first numbers arrive, and they price the mechanism above. A 410-task benchmark grades complete tables deterministically, and its "deterministic evaluator" turns out to be three named tolerance rules — header-invariant column alignment, type- and precision-aware value normalization, order-aware row comparison. The diff is only cheap once you have decided which differences are not differences, and that decision is domain-specific and cannot be adopted from anyone (a-deterministic-grader-for-tabular-answers-is-three-named-normalizations-not-an-equality-check, dataspace-verifiable-analytics-benchmark). It also buys its determinism by requiring the whole artifact, giving up the partial credit subtask-partial-credit-recovers-the-signal-binary-grading-throws-away-and-matters-most-when-every-model-scores-near-zero argues for — a real trade, made on purpose.

  • 2026-08-07 — an unresolved variant that runs against the grain of the theme: a long-horizon harness makes verify-and-repair one of its own stages, so the agent fixes its deliverable before anything scores it. Good for the product, bad for measurement — the eval never sees what was caught internally, and the score becomes partly a measurement of the repair loop (verifying-and-repairing-the-deliverable-inside-the-harness-raises-the-floor-and-hides-the-variance-from-the-eval, onedayagent-long-horizon-harness).

  • 2026-08-07 — the theme's core move gets its first price tag at corpus scale, applied to work-in-progress rather than to a finished output. 112 long-horizon coding tasks restructured as dependency DAGs over separately testable development units — more than 5,300 units with executable tests, prerequisite edges recovered from the projects' own build history rather than authored (a-dependency-dag-of-separately-testable-units-with-completed-nodes-held-as-regression-obligations-scores-a-long-run-continuously, loopsbench-loop-engineering-benchmark). "Decompose it into units something can accept or reject" is what this theme has been arguing for; 5,300 tested units is what it costs when a team means it, and recovering the decomposition from history rather than writing it is the reason it was affordable. The half this theme does not cover — keeping those checks live afterwards — is why evaluating-over-a-horizon opened tonight.

  • 2026-08-07 — the theme's move shows up as somebody's product, and it comes with a rule for where the deterministic checks can go. Image-to-editable reconstruction is defined as recovering a manipulable artifact from a flat one, on the grounds that pixels prevent users from inspecting, modifying, rearranging, or reusing individual components — this theme's argument, stated as a task specification. Its grader is 39 criteria split by whether exact matching would mislead: deterministic rules where properties have direct correspondences, vision-language rubrics where they do not (the-line-between-a-deterministic-check-and-a-rubric-is-whether-exact-matching-misleads-and-three-unrelated-domains-now-draw-it-there, drawai-image-to-editable-reconstruction). Three unrelated domains — security, tutoring, visual reconstruction — now draw the line in the same place, and this is the first to give a criterion you can apply to a single property in isolation rather than a post-hoc description of where the line ended up.

  • 2026-08-07 — and the cheapest construction result yet, on the input side rather than the grading side. 50 molecular-dynamics tasks taken from active simulation projects, and the difficulty spread came with them — routine trajectory analysis through membrane-protein preparation that nothing solves (taking-tasks-from-active-project-work-supplies-a-difficulty-dial-nobody-had-to-author, mdarena-molecular-dynamics-coding-agents). Second source to harvest eval structure from work people were already doing rather than authoring it, after LoopsBench's recovered DAG edges. Real work is pre-sorted by difficulty for free, because some of it is routine and some of it is what the team has been stuck on.

  • 2026-08-08 — a middle tier the deterministic-versus-rubric rule did not anticipate, plus a third instance of harvesting. One benchmark's 1,151 reference answers are auditable by three different mechanisms, one per data surface: executed DuckDB queries for tables, verified text spans for documents, traced dependency annotations for graphs (one-oracle-per-data-surface-is-what-auditable-ground-truth-costs-in-a-heterogeneous-domain, worksurface-bench-multi-surface-knowledge-routing). The span check is the interesting one: it is neither an exact match nor a rubric but a provenance check — groundable without being computable, and needing no judge. Any grader design offering only the two ends will push document answers to a rubric that a span check would have handled deterministically. And the third mechanism reuses metadata the workspace already contained, which makes three sources now harvesting eval structure from artifacts written for other reasons: prerequisite edges from build history, difficulty spread from active project work, and now ground truth from dependency annotations. The cheapest check is the one somebody already wrote down.

  • 2026-08-10 — the theme's argument arrives from the opposite end for the first time. Every source here so far reasoned product-first: the artifact is hard to check, so change the artifact. This reasons grader-first — prefer outcome and state checks wherever they exist (the tests pass, the record changed, the reservation exists, the browser is in the right state), and grade what the agent produced rather than the path it took (checking-the-end-state-rather-than-the-path-is-the-verifiability-argument-arriving-from-the-eval-side-instead-of-the-product-side, anthropic-demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). The two meet at a design question rather than a measurement one: "does the reservation exist" is available as a check only because booking writes a row, and a product whose output is a paragraph has no equivalent that grader engineering can manufacture. The check you wish you could write is a feature request, not a grader task — which is this theme's whole claim, restated by someone who was not making it. The accompanying example is the sharper half: an agent found a flight-booking loophole that failed the written eval while delivering more user value, so the eval was wrong and the agent was right, and only an outcome check can tell that apart. That bounds the transcript-audit obligation this vault carries — an audit flags the run that cheated and the run that found a better route, and separating them is a judgment about what the product is for. Vendor guide, prescriptive, no data; recorded because it is the first independent route to this theme's conclusion.

  • 2026-08-12 — the prescription from two days ago gets a magnitude, and it is about ten points. Same runs, same rubric, two evidence views: a judge shown the trajectory versus a judge shown the environment's final state — service receipts and baseline-to-final diffs. The state judge reports 7.73–11.72 points higher, and the two disagree on the label for 12.97–21.20% of paired cases (showing-the-judge-the-final-state-instead-of-the-transcript-moves-the-reported-rate-about-ten-points-and-flips-the-label-on-up-to-a-fifth-of-cases, redagentbench-executable-red-teaming-faithful-measurement). checking-the-end-state-rather-than-the-path-is-the-verifiability-argument-arriving-from-the-eval-side-instead-of-the-product-side made this recommendation with no data at all; this is the first measurement of what the choice is worth, and it is the size of a frontier-model gap produced entirely by which artifact the grader was handed. The direction matters as much: the state judge finds more, so transcript grading systematically under-counts — the failure is not "the agent narrated something it did not do" but "the agent did something it did not narrate." Second finding here where the agent's own account is the weaker instrument, after an-agents-recorded-plan-recovers-only-part-of-the-true-prerequisite-structure-so-grading-against-the-plan-grades-the-narration. And the precondition is this theme's whole claim in measurable form: the state view existed only because those five services write receipts, and a product whose output is a paragraph has no receipt for a grader to read.

  • 2026-08-12 — the verify-inside-the-harness problem gets a number on it, and the number is large. A released loop harness makes an independent Auditor a named architectural role — it checks files, UI, logs and tests in the real environment, and "a rejected result remains evidence, not progress" — and the reported gain over the bare agent runs to +28.9 points on one benchmark with model and execution backend fixed (a-second-harness-only-delta-on-a-fixed-model-and-backend-runs-from-7-5-to-28-9-points-depending-on-the-benchmark, longhorizon-harness-loop-engineering). That is verifying-and-repairing-the-deliverable-inside-the-harness-raises-the-floor-and-hides-the-variance-from-the-eval with a magnitude attached: anyone comparing a bare agent's number against a harnessed one is comparing two different measurements, and the difference exceeds the frontier-model spread on most suites here. It also gives that concept's open question a concrete target — how often the Auditor rejected, and what it never caught — which the harness could report and does not.

  • 2026-08-12 — and a complete, cheap acceptance procedure for the judge, which this theme has argued for indirectly and never had written down. Label 200+ samples binary with 50–100 failures among them; iterate the judge on 75% and hold 25% out; report precision, recall and Cohen's kappa on the holdout against bands of 0.4–0.6 substantial, above 0.7 excellent; one judge per dimension combined by an explicit AND rather than one judge asked for a verdict; run every pairwise comparison twice with the order swapped (holding-out-25-percent-and-reporting-precision-recall-and-kappa-is-the-first-stated-acceptance-procedure-for-a-judge, eugeneyan-product-evals-in-three-steps). Note precisely what it checks: whether the judge agrees with a person. It does not check whether the scoring code works — CORE-Bench's 53-point swing came from ordinary defects that would be perfectly consistent across both splits — so the two checks stack rather than substitute, and the cheap order is known-good output first, agreement second.

  • 2026-08-13 — the first mechanism here that gets the theme's benefit without touching the product. Break the generated answer into atomic claims and verify each against the source document (decomposing-an-answer-into-atomic-claims-checked-against-the-source-turns-a-rating-into-a-count-without-changing-the-product, eugeneyan-evaluating-long-context-qa-systems). This theme's standing advice — eval-difficulty-is-a-product-design-signal-so-the-first-move-is-restructuring-outputs-into-units-a-user-can-accept-or-reject — asks teams to change what their system emits, which is correct and unavailable to anyone who cannot change the output format. Claim decomposition restructures the judgment instead: the answer stays prose, the grading is what gets split. It is also cheaper than anchoring-generated-output-to-a-vetted-reference-and-showing-only-the-diff-converts-whole-artifact-judgment-into-cheap-local-checks, because the anchor is the source document the system was already handed rather than a reference someone had to author. What it buys is a count — "three unsupported claims out of eleven" is a repair address, "faithfulness 6/10" is a number about which nothing further can be asked. Two unpriced costs: the decomposition is a model call per answer, and two decompositions of the same answer need not produce the same claims, so the denominator is itself a model output and nobody here has checked its stability.

  • 2026-08-13 — the argument arrives once more from the grader's side, in a domain that can be interrogated exhaustively. InfraBench's per-check scoring names four failure classes a terminal success check cannot see — non-durable changes, broken distributed invariants, unsafe side effects, uncleaned state (per-check-scoring-names-four-deterministically-checkable-failure-classes-a-terminal-success-check-cannot-see, infrabench-durability-and-per-check-scoring). The point for this theme is the one the source makes implicitly: in infrastructure, running one check instead of forty is a choice, not a constraint. Most sources here present their grader as determined by the domain, and this is the clearest case of a domain where verifiability is abundant and mostly unspent. It is the mirror image of the product-side argument — there, you redesign the artifact so a check exists; here, the checks already exist and nobody wrote them.

  • 2026-08-14 — the check itself is made falsifiable, which is a direction this theme has never pointed in. Vero lets agents "formally prove unsatisfiability of provided specification or incorrectness of reference code, which surfaces and corrects latent code and specification errors during curation" (letting-the-agent-prove-the-specification-unsatisfiable-or-the-reference-wrong-is-grader-validation-that-runs-during-curation, vero-repository-scale-verified-code-synthesis). It answers the vault's largest open gap — nobody validates the grader, and fixing-grading-bugs-moved-one-model-from-42-to-95-percent-so-the-measuring-instrument-can-own-more-of-a-score-than-anything-it-measures prices a broken one at 53 invisible points — in the one place the answer can be complete. Note what the two existing procedures cannot do: scoring a known-good output catches a grader that rejects correct answers, and the 25% holdout with precision/recall/kappa catches a judge that disagrees with a person, and neither can find an error in the definition of correctness itself, because a specification no correct implementation satisfies is perfectly self-consistent and passes both. The generalisable move is adversarial and survives leaving Lean: let the system under test attack the ground truth, and count a successful attack as a curation bug rather than a failed run. Its weakest form costs nothing and is run nowhere here — when an agent fails an item and its trace argues the item is broken, read the argument. Every source in this vault treats a failed item as a fact about the agent, which is exactly the blindness that kept the CORE-Bench grading bugs invisible. This is also the first mechanism here that treats the measuring instrument as the thing under test. The number that would tell a reader whether to bother — how many latent errors the audit actually surfaced — is not reported.

  • 2026-08-14 — and the same source is the honest counterweight to this theme's own advice, because it shows the limit case and the limit case is expensive. Machine-checked proof is the strongest verdict available anywhere, and it buys 43 instances (the-crispest-available-grader-buys-the-smallest-corpus-here-at-43-hand-specified-instances) — against 50 to 410 for the curated corpora here and five figures for the generated ones — with the strongest agent fully solving 27 of them and closing no specifications on the hardest repositories. Two consequences. The theme's bullets could be read as "more verifiability is always better value," and at the limit that is false: full verification relocates the cost from grading to specification, and specification is human-authored per item, harvesting nothing. Every cheaper move collected here works by harvesting a check from something that already exists — claim decomposition uses the source document, diff-against-reference uses a vetted artifact, per-check scoring uses a state you can already interrogate. And a 43-item suite has coarse resolution: a few instances either way moves the headline more than most model differences would, and none of what-an-eval-costs-to-run's subsetting techniques apply to a suite with no items to spare.

  • 2026-08-15 — a fourth grader shape, and it settles the thing that has made tool-use grading bad: there are many right answers. "Correctness is verified by re-executing predicted tool calls against live APIs, accommodating multiple valid paths," over 8,000+ executable APIs in 62 domains (re-executing-predicted-tool-calls-against-live-apis-grades-effect-rather-than-path-and-accepts-multiple-valid-routes, vakra-multi-hop-apis-under-tool-use-policies). Trajectory matching punishes correct-but-different runs; outcome-only grading certifies lucky ones, which is why outcome-based-grading-requires-transcript-auditing-as-its-counterweight-or-it-certifies-shortcuts-as-successes exists. Re-execution ignores the path, runs what the agent actually asked for, and checks what came back — checking-the-end-state-rather-than-the-path-is-the-verifiability-argument-arriving-from-the-eval-side-instead-of-the-product-side applied per call rather than per run, which is where the localization comes from. The cost is where this theme has not had to look before: the oracle is external and mutable. Reproducing any number here requires 62 domains of third-party services to be up and unchanged, so a benchmark graded this way silently expires. No other grader shape collected here carries a maintenance obligation, and nobody prices this one.

  • 2026-08-16 — the grader gap stops being a per-team afternoon and becomes a population study, and the population had never been checked. MobileJudgeBench scores six judge methods — five of them the judges published mobile-agent benchmarks currently use for their headline numbers — against 931 human-annotated trajectories across 6 benchmarks, 4 agent models and 68 apps (nine-hundred-thirty-one-human-annotated-trajectories-scoring-six-published-judges-is-the-first-population-level-grader-validation-here, mobilejudgebench-benchmarking-llm-judges). This theme has held a procedure for validating your grader since 2026-08-12 and nothing about whether the graders already in circulation work. The finding that matters most for adoption is the acceptance criterion: judge-agreement metrics were shown to predict agent ranking fidelity and downstream utility as an RL reward. That is the argument the 25%-holdout procedure needed and did not have — compute agreement not because agreement is intrinsically good but because it predicts the property the eval exists to deliver. Uncomfortable omission in a paper about agreement: no inter-annotator reliability is reported on the 931 labels, so the ground truth carries an error bar nobody printed.

  • 2026-08-16 — a fourth way to check the instrument, and the only one that reads none of the corpus. Fit item difficulty from outcomes you already have, predict it from the task text, and inspect the items where the two disagree most: a large positive residual on SWE-bench django-14725 traced to critical information missing from the task description (the-residual-between-observed-and-predicted-difficulty-audits-a-whole-corpus-for-broken-items-without-reading-any-of-them, predicting-task-difficulty-without-rollouts). The three procedures already here each read something — a known-good output, a labelled holdout, an agent's argument that the item is broken. This one ranks a corpus of any size and hands back a shortlist. It aims at the failure mode that is invisible by construction: an item no correct answer satisfies looks exactly like a hard item, which is the same blindness that hid 53 points of CORE-Bench grading bugs. The prediction does not have to be good for the audit to work — a weak predictor still ranks items by how surprising their difficulty is, and only the tail matters. What is missing is the yield: two named examples, no count across 17 benchmarks, so nobody knows whether an hour spent on this finds one broken item or forty.

  • 2026-08-16 — two judge defects that need no ground truth at all, which is a first here. Position bias — GPT-3.5 preferring the first-listed answer 50% of the time, Claude-v1 70% — and verbosity bias, both models preferring longer responses over 90% of the time (position-and-verbosity-bias-are-judge-defects-measurable-with-no-ground-truth-at-50-to-70-and-over-90-percent, eugeneyan-effectiveness-of-llm-evaluators). Every validation procedure this theme holds costs labels: 100+ to build a judge, a 25% holdout to accept one, 931 annotations to study a population. These two are computed from the judge's own outputs — swap the order and count disagreements; correlate the verdict with response length — and at these magnitudes they surface on a few dozen items before any annotation budget is spent. The order-swap was already one line inside the acceptance procedure and nobody knew whether the second run was worth it; at 70% it is the highest-yield line in that procedure and the only one runnable before labels exist. Verbosity bias is also the judge-side twin of label-count-correlating-with-input-length-at-rho-0-81-is-a-free-detector-for-an-agent-paraphrasing-instead-of-analysing — an instrument responding to input length instead of input content — which makes "does the output volume track the input volume" a general integrity check rather than a one-off. Two-year-old models, NLP pairwise evaluation rather than agent trajectories, secondhand figures: the practice transfers more confidently than the magnitudes.

  • 2026-08-17 — the cheap tier of the grader ladder gets audited for the first time and it is at chance. A production drift guard caught 0 of 56 meaning-breaking mutations; "withhold the study drug" -> "administer the study drug" scored cosine 0.9608; across 90 configuration-threshold-task cells balanced accuracy "never exceeded 0.700 (median 0.525)" (a-shipped-similarity-gate-caught-zero-of-fifty-six-meaning-breaking-edits-and-never-exceeded-0-700-balanced-accuracy-across-ninety-cells, similarity-gates-approve-reversals). Second population-scale grader validation in two runs after nine-hundred-thirty-one-human-annotated-trajectories-scoring-six-published-judges-is-the-first-population-level-grader-validation-here, and the first on a grader with no model in it. The result is structurally worse than a bad judge: a judge that disagrees with a person is miscalibrated and can be moved at the threshold, while a score monotone in the wrong quantity is wrong at every threshold. It also breaks an assumption this theme has been carrying since the ladder arrived — cheap checks were treated as trustworthy but limited, and embedding cosine sits in the cheap tier and is the least reliable instrument measured anywhere in this vault. The failure direction is why nobody caught it: fixing-grading-bugs-moved-one-model-from-42-to-95-percent-so-the-measuring-instrument-can-own-more-of-a-score-than-anything-it-measures noted a broken grader producing a low score eventually gets doubted, and this one scores 0.96 on a reversal, so the dashboard stays green. The action is an afternoon and belongs beside the known-good-output check: run a matched-pair audit — a handful of single-word reversals against a handful of full rephrasings — and look at whether the scores order the way you assumed. Not a ban: the strongest two of nine configurations separated reversal from paraphrase at matched overlap at AUROC 0.79–0.90. Single author, no replication, one production system behind the 0-of-56.

  • 2026-08-17 — a fifth way to check the instrument, and it checks the set you validate against rather than the instrument. Same nine configurations, same task, two item sets: a naively built corpus gave "decision AUROC exactly 0.000 in 13 of 18 configuration-task cells (at most 0.040 in all 18) against 0.440-0.815" under a balanced 2×2 design (a-corpus-not-balanced-against-the-nuisance-variable-returned-auroc-of-exactly-zero-where-a-matched-pair-design-returned-0-44-to-0-82). Zero is not a failure to answer, it is a confident inversion, and the corpus caused it. The four procedures already here each catch something the others cannot — a known-good output catches a grader rejecting correct answers, a 25% holdout catches a judge disagreeing with a person, an agent's own argument catches a broken item, a difficulty residual ranks a corpus without reading it — and none of them catches a validation set whose class balance is entangled with the grader's real sensitivity, because the grader and the corpus agree and the verdict comes out clean. The portable rule: hold the thing your grader responds to constant across the two classes. The nuisance variable is per instrument and this vault has already found three others — response length (position-and-verbosity-bias-are-judge-defects-measurable-with-no-ground-truth-at-50-to-70-and-over-90-percent), input length (label-count-correlating-with-input-length-at-rho-0-81-is-a-free-detector-for-an-agent-paraphrasing-instead-of-analysing), answer position. What makes the report credible is the author disclosing it bit his own headline claims twice, which is letting-the-agent-prove-the-specification-unsatisfiable-or-the-reference-wrong-is-grader-validation-that-runs-during-curation practised on himself.

  • 2026-08-17 — an admissibility condition on the judge, and six configurations here never state it. "LLM-as-a-judge between similarly powered models yields no usable signal," because the judge "is no stronger than the agent it evaluates" (a-judge-no-stronger-than-the-agent-it-scores-yields-no-usable-signal-which-is-an-admissibility-condition-six-configurations-here-never-state, label-free-harness-evaluation-teacher-relative-lift). Every judge configuration catalogued here is argued on correctness or cost and none names a required capability gap. Three consequences. Self-judging becomes the limit case of a measured null rather than a stylistic objection — a gap of zero. It cuts against a-panel-of-small-judges-beat-a-single-large-one-at-one-seventh-the-cost-which-is-the-first-price-on-a-judge-configuration rather than agreeing with it, since a panel of small judges rating a frontier agent is precisely the configuration said to return nothing, and the unrun test is cheap: hold the judge fixed, vary the agent's tier, find where correlation with human labels collapses. And it puts a structural floor under the-best-automated-critical-error-attribution-reaches-34-percent-accuracy-so-a-machine-audit-is-triage-rather-than-measurement — an attributor built on the agent's own model family is in the similarly-powered regime by construction. Direction only: no rates, no models named, no threshold for "sufficient gap," and asserted in an abstract as support for the paper's actual method.

  • 2026-08-17 — and the judge-validation loop gets its first convergence figure, from the practitioner side. ">90% agreement" with the domain expert in three prompt iterations at one named company (a-judge-reached-over-90-percent-agreement-with-its-domain-expert-in-three-prompt-iterations-which-is-the-first-convergence-figure-here, hamel-using-llm-as-a-judge-complete-guide). Six configurations here and not one says how long it took to reach the number it publishes; three read-the-disagreements-and-edit cycles makes alignment a bounded task rather than an open-ended activity, and it is the strongest argument on file against a judge whose criteria you cannot see, because the loop converges only because a person can find the specific instruction that caused a specific error. Read with the condition the source attaches: agreement was admissible there because the set was ~50/50, and raw-agreement-is-only-admissible-near-balanced-classes-so-an-imbalanced-eval-has-to-report-judge-precision-and-recall-separately says otherwise report precision and recall — which is the regime most product evals are in. One product, one expert, one consultant reporting it, no confusion matrix.

  • 2026-08-18 — the admissibility condition recorded last night as the thing nobody states arrives as a flag in a shipped runner. "Model-graded scorers can now require their grader model role, preventing unintended fallback to the model being evaluated" (a-runner-can-now-require-the-grader-role-so-the-judge-cannot-silently-resolve-to-the-model-under-test, inspect-ai-changelog-0-3-258-to-0-3-259). What makes it different in kind from the five instrument checks already here is that it is enforceable rather than remembered. A known-good output, a 25% holdout, the agent arguing its item is broken, a difficulty residual, a matched-pair corpus — all five are things a team has to decide to do. This one fails loudly whether or not anyone was thinking about judge admissibility that day, and the failure it prevents is the one nobody chooses: a default resolving, so the eval self-judges and no person ever made that call. It sets no threshold. "Not the model under test" is a floor, and pointing the grader at a same-generation sibling satisfies the flag and not the condition.

  • 2026-08-18 — and the same release discloses a grader defect in the parser rather than the judge, which is where this vault keeps finding them. model_graded_qa was scoring an off-menu final GRADE: verdict from a letter mentioned earlier in the grader's reasoning; it now leaves the sample unscored with grade_parse_failure, and "re-scoring existing logs may shift metrics and sample counts" (a-grader-scoring-an-off-menu-verdict-from-an-earlier-mention-in-its-reasoning-shipped-long-enough-that-the-fix-moves-published-numbers). Same failure direction as everything else this theme has collected: a parse failure that produced no score would have shown up as a hole, and this produced a plausible score on a run that had gone wrong, so the suite stayed complete. The check it implies costs one query — count how many grading calls in your last run returned a verdict outside the menu you offered, and look at what your scorer did with them. If the answer is none ever, the parser is lenient rather than the grader perfect. Related and cheap: the control channel now returns metadata only by default so a monitor reading agent output cannot be prompt-injected (making-transcript-reads-metadata-only-by-default-treats-the-monitor-as-a-prompt-injection-target-not-just-the-grader), which extends "the grader is an untrusted input surface" to everything downstream of the agent, the operator's terminal included.

  • 2026-08-20 — and a leak this theme has never named, because everything here grades text. In an interactive environment the agent observes the thing it is scored on, so the criterion and the artifact are separate only if someone keeps them separate. A 2,910-task component benchmark does: "target values and success predicates reside in React component closures and are never exposed as DOM attributes, accessible-tree labels, or page text," and success requires an explicit commit control rather than an inferred state change (hiding-success-predicates-in-component-closures-stops-an-agent-reading-the-answer-off-the-page, componentbench-component-level-failures-in-computer-use-agents). The general rule is one line — the criterion must live somewhere the observation channel does not reach — and the check is mechanical: enumerate what your observation channel returns and grep it for your criterion. It applies to a test file in a repository the coding agent can read, a fixture in a mocked API it can list, an oracle row in a database it can query. Costs nothing at build time and cannot be retrofitted: once the predicate is in the channel, every score collected under it is suspect and there is no post-hoc correction. Asserted as a design property with no ablation, so the size of the leak it prevents is unmeasured.

  • 2026-08-21 — the grader-validation sub-thread gets the thing it has never had: a threshold you can compute before the judge touches a decision. A reference-free judge is modelled as a latent solver — to grade an answer it must work out what it would itself conclude — which yields AUC = ½ + (ck − 1)/(2(k − 1)) in the judge's competence c and answer-space size k, and the necessary condition c > 1/k (a-judges-discriminability-collapses-to-chance-as-its-competence-approaches-one-over-k-which-is-the-first-stated-admissibility-threshold-for-a-judge, competence-not-accuracy-judge-gate-diagnostic). Measured: on research mathematics, competence 0.267 against a floor of 0.2 and discriminability 0.489 — chance. Four judges from independent families reproduce it: at 8–21% competence, AUC 0.31–0.57; at 33–80%, AUC 0.67–0.80. This closes the vault's oldest open judge question, which since 2026-08-17 has been a direction with no number — a-judge-no-stronger-than-the-agent-it-scores-yields-no-usable-signal-which-is-an-admissibility-condition-six-configurations-here-never-state recorded the claim and named the unrun experiment; this runs its dual (hold the judge, vary the task) and returns a computable screen. The procedure costs one pass and no labels: have the judge answer your own items closed-book, count non-answers as wrong, compare to 1/k. Read it as the paper states it — necessary, not sufficient. Noise and correlated judge/target errors can leave discriminability at chance even above the floor, so a pass is a licence to look.

  • 2026-08-21 — and the same source shows the screen is a property of the (judge, task) pair, not of the judge. One judge, one prompt, three tasks: 0.489 on mathematics, 0.735 on factual QA (the-same-judge-scored-0-489-on-mathematics-and-0-735-on-factual-qa-so-judge-admissibility-is-a-per-task-property). Within a single fixed model: DeepSeek-V4-flash at 73% competence and 0.76 on GPQA, 8% and 0.31 on mathematics. Every judge configuration this vault holds is stated as a fact about the grader and was measured on one task family. That is the judge-side instance of what-the-headline-number-averages-away, and the second reason after class imbalance that one agreement number cannot accept a judge.

  • 2026-08-21 — a second correction owed to this vault's own agreement arithmetic, independent of the class-balance one. Pooling judge scores across items rewards a judge for sensing item difficulty: "a judge reading only difficulty and never the answer can score above chance." The identifying quantity pairs a passing and a failing episode of the same item. Measured on factual QA, that stratification removed 12 points — 0.855 → 0.735 — while on mathematics it changed nothing (pairing-episodes-of-the-same-item-removed-12-points-of-apparent-judge-skill-that-was-item-difficulty). So the inflation is not a constant and cannot be discounted by rule of thumb. It is also nearly free for anyone fixing the noise floor: within-item pairing needs several episodes per item, which is exactly what running your suite three times produces. Requirements for an admissible acceptance number are now three — balanced classes or precision and recall separately, within-item pairing, and a stated competence floor.

  • 2026-08-21 — and the input everyone would reach for is the wrong one, by 21 points of pure bookkeeping. For one judge on GPQA-Diamond: answered-only 0.840, empty-inclusive 0.626, two further independent estimators at 0.547 and 0.602 (a-judges-published-accuracy-is-the-wrong-input-to-its-screen-and-21-points-of-the-gap-is-scoring-convention-alone). Same effect in the replication: 94.8% over answered GPQA items, 73.0% once non-convergence counts. Measure the competence yourself, empty-inclusive. The generalization is uncomfortable and belongs on the record: any agent benchmark that drops timed-out, crashed or unparseable runs is reporting performance conditional on having got far enough to answer — the mirror image of this vault's finding that infrastructure failures get scored as capability failures.

  • 2026-08-21 — the counterweight, and it is the first closed-loop test of a judge in this vault. Hold the optimizer, target and task fixed; vary only the signal driving accept/reject. A systematically wrong acceptance signal steers worse than no signal. The 2026-08-17 addendum noted five instrument checks against zero product comparisons and concluded "neither the status nor the open questions move on that; the emphasis should." It is now nine instrument checks against zero, plus a two-source decomposition result with a 39.8-point effect that is the most actionable finding anywhere in this vault. Two distinct claims are living under one title: restructure the artifact so it can be checked at all, which remains untested and is the founding claim, and restructure and validate the judgment, which is well evidenced, has numbers, and is what practitioners actually come here for. They share a verb and not much else — one is a product decision made before any eval exists, the other is eval engineering that presumes the artifact as given. Splitting them is the obvious move and it is not one to make silently inside a nightly run; flagged here as the standing structural question for this vault, ahead of any further status decision on either half.

Why this is its own theme

It does not fit build-vs-adopt-eval-tooling, because its whole point is that the build-vs-adopt question is sometimes premature — a product change can remove the need for the measurement either path would deliver. Filing it there would bury the one move that dissolves the choice.

Thin so far: one practitioner essay, three engagements, no numbers on any of them. It stays here as a real thread rather than a bullet because the mechanism is legible and cheap to test, not because the evidence is good.

Open questions

  • Is the validation corpus balanced against the variable your grader actually responds to? A 2×2 needs no new items, only a different pairing of the ones you have, and it changed a verdict from AUROC 0.000 to 0.44–0.82 the one time anyone checked.
  • At what capability gap does a judge start carrying signal? "Similarly powered yields nothing" is a direction with no threshold, and the experiment is one judge held fixed against agents of ascending tier.
  • Which of this theme's five instrument checks has anyone actually run on a shipped eval, and what did it find? All five are afternoons; the vault has no report of any team running even one.
  • Has anyone instrumented one of these redesigns and reported what it saved in review time or eval cost?
  • What share of eval failures turn out to be the eval's fault rather than the agent's? Teams learn this every time they triage a failure and nobody publishes it, and it is the direct measurement of whether path grading is as brittle as everyone says.
  • Does the tolerance-rule count stay small for artifacts other than tables — code, structured documents, plans? DrawAI's 39 criteria is the second data point and it is an order of magnitude larger than DataSpace's three, though the two are counting different things — criteria versus tolerance rules. Somebody needs to count the same object twice before this is answerable.
  • Does the provenance tier generalize beyond document QA? A verified span works because the answer exists verbatim somewhere. For a generated summary, a plan, or a rewrite, there is no span to point at — is there a provenance check for outputs that are not extractive?
  • What share of a grader ends up on each side of the exact-matching line, and does that share predict what the grader costs to run? DrawAI is the first source that enumerates enough criteria to make the ratio visible; nobody has reported it as a ratio.
  • Where does the difficulty dial come from when the domain has no active project work to harvest — a greenfield product, or a capability nobody is doing by hand yet?
  • How complete is a decomposition recovered from build history? The one source reporting the technique says agent-authored plans recover "only part" of it, but never states how much of the true structure its own recovery captured.
  • When a harness repairs its own deliverable, what should the eval measure instead — how often the repair fired, and what it could not fix?
  • What happens in greenfield generation, where no vetted reference exists to diff against?
  • Does this survive when you do not control the product surface — model selection, or evaluating a system someone else ships?
  • Six ways to check the instrument and still zero reports of a team validating its own. The count keeps rising and the denominator does not move.
  • Does the grader-role flag change any published score? A runner shipped the enforcement and nobody has reported running an eval both ways to see what silent self-judging was worth.
  • The two cosine failures have different repairs — a second kind of signal worked, a better encoder did not. Is that a rule or a coincidence of two studies? The cheap test is running a structural distance against the similarity-gate audit's own matched-pair corpus.
  • Where does the rubric-wise gain saturate? 25.3 criteria in one call costs 9.8 points of agreement; two in one call presumably costs nothing. Somebody sweeping criteria-per-call from 1 to 25 on a fixed rubric set would turn a binary preference into a curve, and it is one afternoon on labels that already exist.
  • Does rubric-wise judging compose with anchoring, panels and bias correction, or do they overlap? All four are now on file, all four claim agreement gains, and no source has run more than one at a time.
  • Of the cases a judge-as-gate flags, what fraction are actually blocked, and of those, what fraction are correctly repaired? Three numbers, this vault has none of them, and the one study that separates recognition from containment found a 66.7-point gap on a single configuration.
  • What is your judge's tie rate? A fixed rubric drew on 52.2% of comparisons where a decomposed one drew on 1.8%, which means non-resolution rather than error was most of what was wrong. Nobody has to run anything to answer this — it is a count over judgments already logged — and it predicts whether decomposition will pay before you build it.
  • Does the decomposition gain come from resolution or from format? Two studies, two different mechanisms proposed, and they make opposite predictions about a judge whose outputs are already valid and already discriminating. The cheap discriminator is the tie rate above, measured before and after.
  • What does rubric-wise, sub-agent decomposition cost? Two independent sources now report large gains from it and neither quotes a token count, a latency or a dollar figure. This is the second-largest unpriced recommendation in the vault after item selection, and pricing it needs one instrumented run.
  • Nine ways to check the instrument, and the count of teams reported validating their own remains zero. The denominator has not moved since 2026-08-14.
  • Run your grader suite through the four-item determinism grep — randomness, network, UUID, wall clock. The one team that did it found a real hit in 82 files. Nobody else has published having looked, and it is minutes.
  • If self-review is a check that notices and does not act at 82.5%, what fraction of the harness-level checks in this vault are the same shape? A gate with no measured pass-through rate is indistinguishable from a logging feature, and three separate sources here have now declined to report one.
