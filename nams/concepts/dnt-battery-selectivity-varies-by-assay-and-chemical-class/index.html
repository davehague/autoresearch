How well the brain-development battery works depends on what you put in it

A selective hit is the distinction that decides whether a screening assay is worth anything. If a chemical makes neurons stop growing at a concentration that also kills every cell in the well, the assay has learned nothing about brain development — it has learned that the chemical is poisonous. A selective hit fires below the concentration that causes general damage, which is the only kind that supports a claim about developmental neurotoxicity specifically.

Across 95 chemicals, this fraction was not stable:

"selectivity varies significantly by assay and chemical class with neurite outgrowth assays and fungicides exhibiting the highest percentage of selective hits."

Read that carefully. It is not "the battery has selectivity X." It is: how much signal you get depends jointly on which of the 17 assays you look at and which kind of chemical you tested. Neurite outgrowth assays discriminate better than the rest; fungicides are better discriminated than the rest.

Why this is graded pushes-back rather than a footnote

Two reasons, and the second is the one with consequences.

First, it interacts with the applicability-domain problem the vault already carries. developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb holds a domain_gate recorded on 2026-07-20 that is biological rather than chemical: the battery is blind to STAT3- and TrkB-mediated mechanisms, omits astrogliogenesis, myelination and the peripheral nervous system entirely, and its neuronal-migration readout responded to 0 of 18 probe pathways. The vault's note on that gate was that a loop cannot measure which pathway a chemical acts through before deciding whether to trust the result — you run the assay to find that out. Tonight adds a second, correlated dimension of the same problem: even for the mechanisms the battery does cover, discriminating power varies by chemical class, and chemical class is likewise not knowable in advance in any way the pipeline consumes.

Second, the integration pipeline published alongside it does not correct for this. Benchmark concentration → Pareto → ToxPi treats the assays as slices to be weighted and ranked. If the fraction of selective hits differs systematically by assay, then the ranking's meaning differs systematically by which chemicals happen to be in the set being ranked. Rank the same chemical against a fungicide-heavy list and against a flame-retardant-heavy one and its position moves for reasons that are about the comparison set, not about the chemical.

That is a specific, named version of the vault's oldest rail. The correlated blind spot was formulated for assays that fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, casting one vote instead of two. This is its arithmetic cousin: assays whose discriminating power co-varies with chemical class, feeding a scoring scheme that assumes it does not. An automated system iterating on that ranking would concentrate its next experiments on the classes the battery happens to see best — and would read the resulting high hit rate as confirmation that it was looking in the right place.

Rebuts

This pushes back on the vault's reading of the battery's screening utility as a stable property. Specifically it qualifies dnt-battery-integration-pipeline-produces-a-ranking-not-a-call — that note records that the pipeline is fully computable; this one records that what it computes is not comparable across chemical sets. Both are from the same source and neither cancels the other.

The brakes

  • The magnitude is unread. "Varies significantly" is the abstract's word; the per-assay percentages are behind Elsevier's paywall and are logged as unread. The vault will not turn a qualitative claim into a number it has not seen.
  • How "selective" was operationalised is unread — where the cytotoxicity filter was set determines the entire result, and it is not in the abstract.
  • funding_flag: [method-developer] — two authors hold equity in the company selling the human-cell assay platform, one is at the company selling the zebrafish screening. See hall-2026-dnt-battery-prioritization-95-chemicals.
  • One study. For calibration, the vault's existing specificity figure for this battery is about 60% — around two in five genuinely clean chemicals wrongly flagged (recorded 2026-07-30). Tonight's finding is consistent with that and does not independently confirm it.
