Six layers between the plate and the call — and the vault has been grading one of them

The claim

Blum et al. lay out the anatomy of a test result. It is not one step:

LevelWhat it isThe choice that produces it
zeromachine configurationfilter width, camera gain, electrode impedance, incubator conditions
Imachine output(acquisition)
IIraw dataconversion to readable numbers; quality control; filtering of erroneous data
IIIaveraged / relativewhich samples are replicates; arithmetic, geometric or median mean; normalisation to which control
IVconcentration-response curvewhich regression model; whether non-monotonic shapes are allowed; how many points define the baseline
Vintegrated curve info (PoD, benchmark concentration)what benchmark response is set; how uncertainty is computed
VIhit definitionthe prediction model / data-interpretation procedure

"for some NAMs, a dozen or more processing steps can be required."

Why it matters to this vault, specifically

shared/evidence-model.md separates automatable-open-loop from closed-loop-ready on one question: does the guideline publish a fixed, deterministic data-interpretation procedure? The data-interpretation procedure is the level V → VI transition. It is the last of seven.

The vault has implicitly been treating levels zero through V as "the readout" — as though the property "instrument-readable" meant the instrument emits a point of departure. It does not. It emits level I. Getting from there to level V is arithmetic and statistics, performed by software, configured by choices, and — per this paper's central claim — outcome-changing.

Nothing in the vault demotes on this. Two reasons, both real:

  1. For most of the 24 closed-loop-ready maps, the lower levels are genuinely simple. OECD TG 439's chain is absorbance → percent of control → compare to 50%. There is a normalisation step and a threshold, and both are written in the guideline. The paper's warning is aimed at assays with curve fitting, benchmark concentrations and multi-endpoint feedback, which is a different population.
  2. Adopted Test Guidelines specify more of the chain than a research paper does. That is much of what a Test Guideline is for.

What changes is what the tier means. closed-loop-ready says the last step is written down. It has never said the whole chain is written down, and the vault has occasionally been reading it that way. The honest reading, after this source: the tier certifies the decision rule, not the pipeline that feeds it.

The transition the vault had not modelled at all

Level VI can invalidate level V. In a multi-endpoint assay, a cytotoxicity call flags a functional readout as meaningless — dead cells cannot grow neurites, so a neurite measurement taken in a cytotoxic well is not a low number, it is not a number. The paper: "Re-analysis by alternative pipelines (e.g., not using such a feedback connection) may not consider such rules in the same way and thus lead to an altered overall output."

That is a cycle in what the vault has been drawing as a straight line, and it is the same architectural trick automation-tox21-qhts-platform uses at scale (multiplexing a viability measurement into the same well so a dying cell is not read as an active one). The vault called that a counter-design against correlated blind spots on 2026-07-13. It is also a data-pipeline feature, and if a loop's analysis code omits it the loop gets confident false positives.
