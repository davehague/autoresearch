The loop was made safe by a type schema

What the model was and was not allowed to do

LayerWho did it
Choose the next reaction compositionsGPT-5
Analyse returned data, generate the next hypothesisGPT-5
Validate that a proposed design is physically executablea Pydantic schema, hand-written by Ginkgo engineers
Write the laboratory protocols the robot runshumans, by explicit decision
Execute: liquid handling, incubation, plate readingrobotic carts
Prepare, load and unload reagents and consumableshumans

The authors state the design decision outright:

"We chose not to use GPT-5 to write the Catalyst protocols responsible for executing the RAC-based workflows. Indeed, all Catalyst protocols were written by Ginkgo personnel. Instead, we constrained GPT-5, via the Pydantic schema, to produce experimental designs within a predefined operational envelope."

Their stated reason is efficiency — keep the model "exploring relevant regions of biological parameter space rather than … writing, attempting to run, and debugging RAC-compatible protocols." Whatever the motive, the architecture is the finding: the model chooses points inside a space that humans defined and encoded.

Why this is the actionable engineering lesson in the paper

The headline is "AI ran 36,000 experiments." The architecture says something more useful: autonomy was purchased with specification. Somebody had to write down, in machine-checkable form, what a valid experiment is — volumes, reagent identities, ranges, totals. That artefact is what made it safe to let a model propose 36,000 of them.

And it worked as a control loop in the ordinary software sense. One of the two observed failures was the model overriding a fixed buffer volume to fit more reagents in; the fix was not a better prompt or a better model. It was: "We updated the validation in the Pydantic schema to prevent this error from recurring." A constraint was discovered, encoded, and never violated again. That is a regression test.

What this says about a toxicology loop, and it is not encouraging

The schema encodes what is physically executable. It cannot encode what is scientifically meaningful.

The second failure proves it — the unit-conversion bug produced a plate of glucose and ribose that was perfectly valid under the schema: real reagents, correct volumes, correct total. Every well was executable and every well was pointless. A type checker cannot tell you the experiment is stupid.

Now map that onto the vault's territory. The properties a hazard-testing loop would need to encode are exactly the ones the vault spends its time recording, and none of them are volume constraints:

  • Applicability domain. TG 435's pH carve-out; TG 249's log KOW −4 to 7 bound; TGx-DDI's requirement for functional p53. Some of these a robot can measure — eye-irritation-vitrigel-tg494 is the vault's only method that pre-tests its own domain — but most must be told.
  • Correlated blind spots. Two assays in a battery that fail on the same chemicals for the same reason. Nothing in a schema knows that FET and RTgill-W1 share a missing nervous system.
  • Metabolic competence. A pro-toxicant needing a conversion the test system cannot perform reads negative, legally and reproducibly.

The schema is the right shape of answer and the wrong contents. What a tox loop needs is the same artefact — machine-checkable, versioned, enforced before execution — carrying the domain gates and blind-spot rules the vault has been accumulating by hand. Nobody has written that, and this vault is, among other things, a handwritten draft of what would go in it.
