The loop received its feedback and turned a knob

A closed loop is defined by one edge: a physical result comes back and changes what the system does next. Everything else — the robot, the scheduler, the protocol compiler, the provenance ledger — is plumbing that exists to make that edge possible.

This study ran that edge five times and reports what came out of it: "experimental feedback prompted local adjustments but no workflow-level replanning or analytical-method redesign."

Translated: the agents changed the numbers, not the plan. Concentration up, time longer, temperature different. None of them concluded that the workflow was the wrong workflow, or — the harder and more important one — that the measurement was the wrong measurement.

Why this is the sharpest loop-layer finding the vault has taken in

This map's running complaint has been that nothing in the field measures the update leg. single-turn-benchmarks-cannot-evaluate-a-closed-loop recorded that benchmarks score one-shot prediction with no path from a physical result back to the model. The measurements that did exist scored either the planning half (LabBench2, SCOPE) or the execution half (Pipette, AEGIS, LabRobFail).

Here the edge exists, is exercised five times on real results, and fires only at the level where firing is cheapest. That is a different failure from "the agent could not act on the feedback." It acted. It acted shallowly.

Local adjustment is the move that a system will always be able to make and that always looks like learning. A dose-response curve that came back flat has an obvious parameter reading — try higher concentrations — and a much less obvious structural reading: this cell line does not express the receptor, this readout saturates before the effect appears, this chemical never reached the cells because it stuck to the plastic. Only the second class of conclusion requires abandoning the design.

What it means for a toxicology loop, and it is specific

Regulatory toxicology's characteristic failure is exactly the one that parameter tuning cannot reach.

The vault's correlated-blind-spot rail says: when two assays in a battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, the battery casts one vote, not two — and a bioactivation-dependent compound walks through a metabolically incompetent battery clean. The correct response to that result is "this battery cannot see this chemical class; run something with metabolic competence" — an analytical-method redesign, precisely the category this study observed zero instances of. The parameter-level response is to re-run at higher concentrations, which produces a cleaner, more confident, equally wrong negative.

So the rail's mechanism now has an empirical foot under it. The vault has been asserting that an automated system trusting a decision rule "will compound this error rather than catch it." This is the first source that watched a system receive contrary physical evidence and observed what kind of change it made. It made the kind that cannot catch a structural error.

Set against the map's most successful loop: the GPT-5/Ginkgo cell-free-protein campaign (the-loop-closes-where-the-objective-is-a-number) converged over 580+ plates on dollars per gram — a scalar the instrument reports, where parameter search is genuinely the right move because the objective is a continuous surface. Read together, the two results say something narrower and more useful than "agents cannot replan": the update leg has been demonstrated on problems where turning knobs is the correct behaviour, and has not been demonstrated on problems where it is not. Hazard identification is the second kind.

Honest limits

Qualitative, not quantified — "no workflow-level replanning" is an observation over five rounds, with no denominator, no rate, and no comparison to what a human would have done with the same five results. It is a preprint, the authors own the laboratory, and no toxicological endpoint appears anywhere in it. Read it as the first datum on an unmeasured leg, not as a measured rate.
