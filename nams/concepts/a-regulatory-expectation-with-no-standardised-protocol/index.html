A non-animal method a regulator requires and nobody has written down

The situation. In 2006 six healthy volunteers received TGN1412, an antibody designed to switch T cells on. It had looked safe in animals. Within hours they were in intensive care with cytokine release syndrome — the immune system's signalling molecules flooding the body. Since then, sponsors are expected to test immune-stimulating antibodies for that hazard in human cells, in a dish, before the first human dose.

And that is the whole regulatory instrument. The source's phrasing is "there is now a regulatory expectation" — not a Test Guideline, not an ICH guideline naming a method, not a validated reference protocol. The consequence follows immediately in the same paragraph: "methodology is not standardized, and differences in methods used throughout the pharmaceutical / biotechnology industry could influence assay outcomes." Every company runs its own version, and the same study demonstrates that the versions do not agree (the-plate-is-a-method-parameter-and-nobody-reports-it).

The assay is assigned proposed as the least-wrong value, and that is recorded as strain rather than resolved. none would be the plain reading of "no guideline" and it would be misleading, because a sponsor that skips this assay has a problem with a regulator. The vault's own evidence-model already warns about the mirror-image error — that regulatory_status: none means "no guideline," not "unusable," since FDA's CDER will accept a fit-for-purpose method with no Test Guideline at all. This case is the completion of that thought: acceptance and specification are two different things, and a method can have the first without the second.

What it means for the loop

Loop-readiness asks whether a fixed, deterministic data-interpretation procedure exists. Here the answer is worse than "no rule": there is no agreed protocol either, so two laboratories' numbers are not comparable before you even get to the threshold question. The readouts are plate-reader cytokine measurements — thoroughly instrument-readable — and the assay is run at scale across the industry. It sits squarely at automatable-open-loop, and the blocker is not one missing committee decision but an entire unwritten method definition.

The asymmetry worth carrying: the field's usual complaint is a validated method that no regulator will accept. This is the inverse — a required method that nobody has validated. Both produce the same practical outcome, which is that the number a laboratory produces cannot be trusted to mean the same thing somewhere else, but they need opposite fixes.
