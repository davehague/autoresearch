A six-level autonomy ladder, and a call for auditable agents — zero toxicology, read from the abstract only

A perspective on agentic AI across the drug-discovery pipeline proposes a 6-level "Spectrum of Agentic Autonomy" maturity framework and argues that evaluating these systems should center on full-chain traceability — whether every step an autonomous agent took can be reconstructed and checked after the fact — rather than only on whether the final output looks correct. The stated conclusion frames agents as "scientist-in-the-loop co-pilots," not unsupervised replacements.

Why this touches automation-self-driving-labs's declared "verification" sub-thread (one of the map's two strands: "is the measuring stick sound"): full-chain traceability is exactly the property that strand already asks about — can a human or a regulator tell what an autonomous system actually did and whether its call can be trusted. This paper is a general, drug-discovery-pipeline-scoped argument for that property, with no toxicology, hazard assay, or physical closed-loop wet-lab example confirmed from the retrievable text.
