The validation pipeline becomes the second bottleneck

This vault's founding question treats validation as a gate a method passes once, on its way from single-lab-exploratory to formally-validated to regulatory-adopted. This essay's sharpest claim is that the gate itself does not scale — and names the mechanism precisely: ring-trial validation was designed to process a handful of candidate methods a year, built in an era when that was the actual submission rate. Tox21, RASAR-style read-across, microphysiological systems, and now LLM/agentic tooling each independently accelerate method generation. None of them accelerate method evaluation, because evaluation still runs on the same committee-and-ring-trial machinery built for the pre-exponential era.

Why this is a loop-relevant claim even though the map it sits on is scored not-applicable. This vault's three axes (evidence_tier, regulatory_status, loop_readiness) are themselves outputs of the validation process this essay is worried about. If validation throughput cannot keep pace with NAM proliferation, the practical effect is a growing backlog of methods stuck at single-lab-exploratory or peer-reviewed-method — not because the science is weak, but because nobody has gotten to grading it. That is a structural explanation for a pattern this vault has observed empirically but never had a field-level account for: many method maps in vault/maps/ sit at emerging for sources that are individually solid but never accumulate the independent replication consolidating requires.

The proposed fix is named, not built. An "AI-supported e-validation framework" that keeps human judgement on individual calls while raising overall throughput is asserted as the answer, citing the author's own evidence-to-decision agentic-AI proposal (hartung-2026-evidence-to-decision-agentic-ai) as the mechanism. No implementation, benchmark, or pilot validation run is reported here or in the cited prior work.

The honest limit

Nothing here is measured. The claim that validation specifically — as opposed to method generation, which the essay does quantify with illustrative curves — will become the binding constraint is argued from structural reasoning (committees are slow; methods are many) rather than from a backlog count, a queue-length figure, or a time-to-adoption trend across the actual OECD Test Guidelines pipeline. This vault could in principle check that claim against its own data — the lag between single-lab-exploratory and regulatory-adopted across its 473 ingested sources — but that analysis has not been run and is recorded here as an open question, not performed as a finding.
