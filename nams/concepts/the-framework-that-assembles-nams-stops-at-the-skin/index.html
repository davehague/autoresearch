The framework that assembles NAMs into a decision stops where the chemical stops being local

This vault grades methods one at a time. Next Generation Risk Assessment (NGRA) is the layer above them — the framework that takes a set of non-animal methods and produces an actual answer about an actual product: at the amount a person is exposed to, is there a margin of safety?

A critical review of the applied literature audited nine published NGRA case studies across seven chemicals and reported two things in one sentence:

"There are currently no apparent validated NGRA frameworks for systemic toxicity endpoints where significant gaps remain for complex toxicological endpoints, even though defined approaches for skin sensitization and eye irritation have gained regulatory acceptance."

Why this matters more than a coverage statistic

Systemic toxicity is harm to the body after a chemical is absorbed and carried around — liver, kidney, heart, the developing nervous system, fertility — as against damage where the chemical touches. The endpoints with an accepted integrated framework are skin allergy and eye irritation: local, fast, and reached by direct contact.

That is the same shape this vault's own coverage map has, derived independently and from the other direction. The vault's monthly connect pass on 2026-08-01 found that every endpoint with a method past emerging is one where the chemical acts locally and fast, and every endpoint needing an organ to keep working over time has nothing past emerging. The vault reached that by counting method maps. This review reaches it by auditing whether anyone has assembled those methods into a decision anybody accepted. Two different measurements of the field, same boundary.

What it does not establish

  • Nine case studies and seven chemicals is a very small base for any claim about a framework, and the review says so by calling itself critical.
  • "No apparent validated" is not "invalid." The review does not define what validation of a framework would consist of — a ring trial, agreement with human data, or regulatory acceptance are three different bars.
  • The full text is gated (subscription; browser rung returned HTML, not a PDF). Which chemicals, which case studies, and what the margins actually were are unread.

What it means for the loop

An autonomous laboratory does not produce a risk assessment; it produces measurements. Somebody has to assemble them. On this evidence the assembly procedure exists and is agreed for exactly the endpoints where the assays were already the easiest to automate, and does not exist for the rest — so the two axes this vault insists on keeping apart, automatability and acceptance, are for once pointing the same way, and both point at the skin.

The honest reading is not that this is encouraging. It is that the field's integration layer and its automation layer are both stuck at the same boundary, for related but distinct reasons: local endpoints are easy to model in a dish and easy to write a rule for. Nothing about solving the second problem solves the first.
