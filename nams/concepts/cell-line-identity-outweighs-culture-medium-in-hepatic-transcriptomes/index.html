Taking the cow out of the dish matters. Choosing the dish matters more.

On 2026-07-20 this vault opened nam-reagent-provenance-animal-derived-components on a review that found every one of the seven human-cell assays in the developmental-neurotoxicity battery still full of animal-derived material, and flagged an open question it could not answer: replacing those components may change the result, and nobody has systematically measured by how much.

For hepatic cell models, this is the measurement.

Fetal bovine serum is cow blood serum — an undefined mixture whose composition varies lot to lot. A chemically defined medium replaces it with named molecules at stated concentrations. Swapping one for the other in two widely used human liver cell lines moved:

ContrastGenes changed
HepaRG, defined vs serum medium2,272
HuH7, defined vs serum medium1,017
Cell line vs cell line, and either line vs real human liver cells≈12,600–14,100

Both effects are real. One is six to fourteen times the other. The authors call the resulting picture "identity-first, medium-modulated," and land on a conclusion that is deliberately undramatic:

"adopting CDM should not be viewed as a disruptive break from serum-based literature, but rather as a controlled refinement that clarifies which programs reflect model-intrinsic biology and which are modulated by serum."

What this does to the reagent-provenance worry — it survives, resized

This is good news for the field and it is not a retraction of the 2026-07-20 finding. The variability channel is still there; a thousand-to-two-thousand-gene shift is not noise. What has changed is its rank. Anyone worried that the serum question invalidates the existing serum-based literature now has an answer: it does not, because the bigger decision — which cell line — was made first and moved far more.

The loop reading, and it is the reason this note is graded limits

A closed loop's founding assumption is that a change in the readout means a change in the chemistry. The 2026-07-20 concern was that an undeclared serum lot change violates that assumption silently, and that an active-learning loop, having no representation for reagent lot, would treat the shift as information and update on it.

That concern holds and gets sharper here. The medium effect is directional and model-dependent, not random noise: in HepaRG it lifted xenobiotic-metabolism and transport programmes; in HuH7 it lifted sterol biosynthesis and stress responses. A systematic, model-specific shift is worse for a loop than a random one — random error averages out across a campaign, a directional one does not, and this one changes exactly the machinery that decides whether a chemical is activated or detoxified.

And the dominant term is worse still, because it is invisible by construction. A loop never varies its cell line. Model identity is fixed on day one, contributes the largest share of the variance, and therefore never appears in the loop's data at all. The loop cannot learn about the input that matters most, because it holds it constant. That is not a defect of any particular platform; it is what one laboratory running fast looks like, and it is the same structural point the vault recorded on 2026-07-30 about generalisability — a property of a body of evidence that a single fast laboratory produces structurally zero evidence about.

The brakes

  • The comparison is not clean serum-removal. The authors are explicit: the defined media were each optimised for their own cell line, so "medium-associated effects cannot be attributed solely to the absence of serum, but reflect the combined influence of serum removal and the defined formulation used for each cell line." The 2,272 figure is a combined effect. Do not let a later run cite it as the cost of removing serum.
  • "CDM" is not one thing. "'CDM' formulations are not interchangeable." Another laboratory's defined medium is a different experiment.
  • n = 3, one laboratory, one run. strength: single-study, and it will stay there until somebody reproduces it.
  • Two cell lines, one tissue. Nothing here licenses the same conclusion for neural, renal or immune models — including the developmental-neurotoxicity assays that raised the question in the first place.
  • funding_flag: none — Technische Universität Berlin plus the German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment, neither of which sells HepaRG, HuH7 or any medium compared. The clean-outsider posture, same as the source that opened the map.
