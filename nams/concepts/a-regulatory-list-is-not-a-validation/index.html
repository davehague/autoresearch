A fourth shape of regulatory acceptance: the list

The three shapes the vault already had

Before tonight, regulatory_status recognised three ways a non-animal method acquires standing:

ShapeWhat it isVault example
A Test GuidelineOECD writes down the method and the rule; member countries mutually accept the dataOECD TG 439, the lab-grown human skin irritation test
A validation pipelineThe method is inside an ECVAM/ICCVAM/OECD process, not out the other endthe human thyroid microtissue assay
Fit-for-purpose submissionA regulator accepts a method with no guideline at all, judged against the specific decision it supportsUS FDA's drug-review centre, per its 2026 draft framework

The fourth

The list. The Toxic Substances Control Act — the US law governing industrial chemicals — obliges EPA to "maintain and regularly update a list" of non-animal methods. Being on it is a real regulatory fact: it tells a company which methods the agency will take instead of an animal study.

But EPA performs no validation to put a method there. Its own sentence:

"Many of the NAMs have been reviewed and established by different organizations (e.g., OECD, ICCVAM, and ICATM) and meet the Section 4(h)(2)(C) criteria for scientific relevance (i.e. accuracy) and reliability (i.e., repeatability/reproducibility)."

Three things follow, and they matter more than the list itself.

1. The determination is inherited, not made. OECD, ICCVAM and ICATM did the assessing. EPA is aggregating and conferring statutory standing on other people's conclusions. This is not a criticism — it is efficient and exactly what an aggregator should do — but it means a listing is not independent evidence. A method on the list and the OECD Test Guideline behind it are one fact, not two, and the vault must not count them twice. That is the correlated-blind-spot rail's logic applied to acceptance rather than to assay biology.

2. The word is "many." The relevance-and-reliability sentence covers the list in aggregate. It is not a per-entry certification, and the page publishes no entry-level statement of what each method's evidence is.

3. There is no disclaimer. The page carries nothing saying listing is not endorsement, or that a listed method is still judged case-by-case for a given decision. The distinction is real — EPA still evaluates fitness for purpose — but the source does not draw it, so a reader is left to.

Where this bites: the DNT battery

The clean instance is the developmental-neurotoxicity in-vitro battery, which appears as NEW on EPA's list under "Data Integration Tools, Predictive Models, and Assessment Frameworks."

On 2026-07-20 this vault moved developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb to contested on the OECD Secretariat's own words, written with NICEATM and EFSA:

"no formal validation procedure has yet been completed for the DNT-IVB as an integrated battery"

Both statements are current, public, and from government bodies. They can be reconciled — EPA filed the battery among frameworks and tools rather than among validated test methods, and the relevance sentence is a blanket one. They cannot be reconciled by the reader of the list, who is given a category heading and a NEW tag. (the-model-layer-is-now-on-the-same-list-as-the-assays covers the same category's other occupants.)

Why an autonomous loop should care

An AI system reasoning about which methods it may use will find regulatory_status far easier to read off a government list than off a validation report — the list is a table, machine-readable, authoritative-looking, and free of qualifiers. The qualifier is the whole content. A loop that treats list membership as validation will conclude the developmental-neurotoxicity battery is reliable, on the same night the vault's sources show its own guideline body saying it has never been formally validated as a battery.

Status effect: none. Listing is a regulatory_status fact and the vault does not move evidence_tier or status on it. developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb stays contested. A list does not un-contest a validation gap.
