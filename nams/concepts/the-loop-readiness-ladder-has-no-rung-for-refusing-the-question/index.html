The axis grades the assay and gets read as grading the loop

The vault's third axis assigns a method one of five tiers by a deterministic ladder. The two decisive questions are:

  1. Readout — is what is measured instrument-readable, or scored by a trained human?
  2. Decision rule — does the guideline publish a fixed, deterministic data-interpretation procedure?

Both are questions about the method. Answer them and you know whether an assay can be executed and scored without a person. That is a real and useful property, and it is what closed-loop-ready means.

It is not what closed-loop-ready gets read as meaning. The phrase invites the reading "an AI could run this safely," and the vault has guarded that with a standing caveat — loop-ready is not endorsement — which has always been a claim about the method's scientific validity: the assay might be automatable and still be poor science, or automatable and carry a blind spot. That caveat is about the assay.

Liao et al. name the other half, and the vault had no field for it. Nothing in the ladder asks whether the agent would decline. An assay can have a perfect instrument readout and a perfectly fixed rule, and be pointed at:

  • a mis-specified reference chemical;
  • a substance outside the applicability domain the rule is only valid inside;
  • a hypothesis the assay structurally cannot address (an immune-mediated mechanism handed to a battery with no immune cells);
  • a premise that is simply false.

In every one of those cases the machinery runs correctly and the answer is meaningless. The loop-readiness tier is unchanged, because the tier is not about that. And the measured ability of current agentic research systems to catch it is near zero, with the best of seven systems at 27.4% on 200 adversarial items.

Two honest readings, and the vault should hold both.

The narrow one: this is not a defect in the axis. An axis that grades methods should grade methods, and folding agent competence into it would collapse two independent things — the same error the vault refuses to make between scientific validity and regulatory acceptance. A closed-loop-ready rating plus a separate statement about what agents can and cannot be trusted to do is the correct decomposition.

The broader one: the vault's central question is "which NAM endpoints can actually be put in a closed loop." Answering it purely from the assay side answers which assays would not obstruct a loop, which is a strictly weaker claim than the question asks. The agent's judgement is a term in the answer and the vault has no way to record it.

This is logged as an open question rather than a schema change, for two reasons: one unrefereed preprint on a domain-general adversarial set is thin ground on which to restructure a grading model, and the measurement does not exist for the case that matters — nobody has tested whether an agent handed a false chemical safety premise behaves the way these seven did. Recorded in docs/open-gaps-log.md.

What can be said without any schema change at all: the two batteries this vault grades as fully loop-clearing end to end both carry documented shared blind spots (skin-sensitization-2o3-defined-approach, phototoxicity-iata), and this source says the systems that would run them are, on the only measurement available, close to incapable of noticing. Those two facts were already in the vault separately. They had not been put next to each other.
