Cell Painting reaches a metabolically competent cell system

phenotypic-profiling-cell-painting's 2026-08-12 entry flagged a provisional blind-spot note it explicitly declined to formalise as a correlated_blind_spot row: the vault had no source stating which cell line Cell Painting runs in for toxicology applications, so it could not check whether the technique shares the metabolic-incompetence gap that is the shared cause behind four of the rail's seven rows (FET↔RTgill-W1, ROS↔3T3 NRU, TGx-DDI↔micronucleus, the lymphoblastoid trio) — any culture lacking drug-metabolising machinery will read a pro-toxicant (a chemical only dangerous after metabolic activation) as inactive.

This source answers that question for one dataset. Primary human hepatocytes are the liver's own cells, carrying the cytochrome P450 and phase-II metabolising enzymes most immortalised lines lack. Running Cell Painting on them, and finding it more sensitive than standard cytotoxicity assays by 2.5–15.8×, is evidence that the technique's morphological readout works — and works sensitively — in at least one metabolically competent system. This does not close the blind-spot question in general: it is one dataset, and the map's other Cell Painting sources (BBBC021, CPJUMP1, RxRx3 archives; the RTgill-W1 fish-cell application) run in different, mostly non-hepatic, cell systems whose metabolic competence remains unconfirmed. No correlated_blind_spot row is set on this basis — the check still cannot be generalised from a single system.

Why the sensitivity numbers should not be read as "more correct." A lower point of departure means the morphological fingerprint moves before the standard viability assays do — earlier detection of some change, not confirmation that the change is the toxicologically relevant one. The comparison here is against other in-vitro cytotoxicity assays, not against an in-vivo hepatotoxicity reference.
