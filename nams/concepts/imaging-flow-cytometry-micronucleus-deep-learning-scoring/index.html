Deep-learning imaging-flow-cytometry scoring of the in-vitro micronucleus assay → genotoxicity

The in-vitro micronucleus assay (genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus, OECD TG 487) is the vault's sharpest axis-disagreement case: adopted on both the science and regulatory axes (consensus, OECD-TG-adopted) yet stuck at loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop, because the readout is a trained human counting micronuclei by eye down a microscope. This is a loop-move claim about that exact bottleneck.

What Wills et al. (2021) showed. Replace the microscope-and-human with imaging flow cytometry (cells photographed one at a time in flowing suspension, ~2,000–5,000 images/minute) and a convolutional neural network (DeepFlow) that classifies each cell — including the load-bearing "binucleated cell with a micronucleus" category. Trained on two labs' data, it scored an unseen third lab's images with no reconfiguration (85% on the micronucleus-positive class) and, decisively, produced the same benchmark dose as the human scorer — the number that actually drives the safety call. The automation changes who counts, not what you conclude.

Why the map does NOT move to loop-ready — three reasons, all recorded.

  1. Not the adopted way to score TG 487. The guideline's data-interpretation procedure is human morphological scoring; this pipeline is a published alternative, not part of the guideline → regulatory_status: none for the automation (the assay itself stays OECD-TG-adopted — never infer one axis from the other).
  2. Inter-lab, but not validated. Three labs and real cross-lab generalisation earns peer-reviewed-method, not formally-validated — there is no ECVAM/ICCVAM ring trial of this scorer.
  3. Instrument-gated, industry-co-authored, imperfect on the key class. It runs only on the Amnis ImageStream (an access_gate); a Luminex employee — the instrument's maker — is a co-author (funding_flag: [industry], the automation-hype variant); and ~25% of the critical micronucleus-positive events are misclassified. Still needs ~19,000 human-labelled cells to train.

What it does change: the reading of the blocker. "Counted by eye" is no longer a permanent architectural fact of the micronucleus assay — it now has a demonstrated, inter-laboratory, benchmark-dose-equivalent machine-vision path. This is a rung stronger than the zebrafish demonstration (zebrafish-machine-vision-malformation-scoring), which was single-lab and slower than a human. The disciplined move — identical to the zebrafish outcome — is to keep loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop (a trained human is still load-bearing in the regulatory call) while annotating the blocker to record that the escape hatch is not only demonstrated but demonstrated across labs, matching the regulatory answer.

The pattern is now two-for-three. Of the vault's three human-in-the-loop maps whose blocker is eye-scoring — micronucleus, zebrafish teratogenicity, and carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay (foci counted by eye) — two now carry a demonstrated machine-vision escape hatch. Whichever gets a validated automated scorer first (an ECVAM/ICCVAM ring trial, or adoption of the automated readout into the guideline) is the vault's next likely human-in-the-loop → automatable-open-loop promotion. The honest brake on all of it: the automation literature is disproportionately written by the people selling the instrument, and this paper is a case in point — graded, not laundered.
