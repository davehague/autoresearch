The battery's numbers now integrate themselves. They still do not decide anything.

The developmental-neurotoxicity in-vitro battery is 17 assays covering 8 stages of brain development. Roughly 650 chemicals have been through it. Not one of those runs produced a stand-alone regulatory conclusion, and developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb has carried the reason since 2026-07-06: there is no published, fixed procedure for turning the battery's numbers into an answer. OECD calls a rule-based Defined Approach for this endpoint an "aspiration" and attaches no date.

This paper builds the closest thing yet, and every component of it is software:

  • Benchmark concentration — fit a curve to each assay's dose–response and find where the response departs from control. Deterministic.
  • Pareto multi-objective optimisation — keep the chemicals that no other chemical beats on both potency and selectivity at once. Deterministic.
  • ToxPi — score each chemical as a weighted pie of assay slices, and rank. Deterministic.

A machine can run all three. Combined with what the vault already knew — that six of seven readouts on the battery's flagship human-cell assay are scored end-to-end by convolutional neural networks (2026-07-20) — the chain from plate in to number out is now automated along its entire length.

And the tier does not move, which is the point

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop holds.

The pipeline's output is a rank order. The authors describe it exactly that way: "prioritization methods … for comparative evaluation of potential developmental neurotoxicity hazard in large groups of chemicals," producing a list "ranking them for further evaluation."

A rank order answers look at this one before that one. A hazard call answers this chemical is a developmental neurotoxicant. The second cannot be derived from the first without a threshold, and no threshold is published — not for ToxPi score, not for Pareto rank, and there is no reported sensitivity or specificity against reference developmental neurotoxicants that would let anyone choose one.

This is the vault's most repeated finding, arriving for the fourth time in eight days, and the repetition is now itself the result:

Four independent groups automated the arithmetic and none of them wrote down the cut. That is a pattern worth naming: the field has a strong incentive to publish a defensible computational pipeline and a strong disincentive to publish the number that would make it a regulatory decision — because publishing the number is what makes you wrong in public.

What would actually move the tier

The gap is now narrow and specific enough to state as a specification: a published rule mapping a ToxPi score (or Pareto rank) to a hazard classification, at a stated threshold, with stated performance against reference chemicals. This paper supplies the score. Nobody has supplied the threshold. When one appears, this map moves to closed-loop-ready — and the vault's warning attaches at the same moment, because a threshold applied fast and confidently to a battery with the documented blind spots below is a worse object than a slow human reading the same numbers.

The brakes

  • funding_flag: [method-developer], and it lands on the headline. Two authors hold equity in DNTOX GmbH, which commercialises the Neurosphere Assay supplying several of the battery's human-cell readouts. One author is at Biobide, which sells zebrafish screening — and the zebrafish assay is the one reported most sensitive. The federal NIEHS funding does not offset this; they are independent facts. The two editor authors recused from their own peer review, which is disclosed and correct.
  • The most sensitive member is an animal assay. The paper labels the zebrafish neurobehaviour readout "an in vivo assay." A battery whose most sensitive leg is a vertebrate embryo is not a fully non-animal battery, and the vault records that rather than smoothing it.
  • 95 chemicals, against ~650 already run and tens of thousands with no data at all.
  • One analysis, one chemical set, no independent replication of the pipeline.
  • The paper is non-open-access; the per-assay tables, the cytotoxicity filter defining "selective," and the ToxPi slice weights are unread and logged.
