The delivery method was generating the response

The measurement

A lab-grown model of the human airway lining sits on a porous membrane with air above it and liquid below — that is what makes it behave like an airway rather than like cells in a puddle. To test an inhaled chemical on it, somebody has to get the chemical onto the tissue.

The usual way is to pipette it on, in a small volume of salt water, repeatedly. Do that with the salt water alone and the barrier degrades and the tissue starts signalling inflammation. No test substance is present. The control is producing the finding.

Mist the same salt water on as an aerosol and "no detectable changes in barrier integrity or inflammatory signalling" occur at all.

Why a vehicle artefact is a loop problem, not a bench problem

A human running this assay by hand has a chance of noticing. They see the PBS-only wells looking as bad as the dosed ones and something feels wrong. That intuition is not a readout, it is not written down anywhere in a protocol, and it does not survive automation.

An automated system sees two numbers and a rule. Barrier resistance fell; inflammatory signal rose; the rule fires; the chemical is called a respiratory irritant. The system has no representation of "the way I put the dose on is what did that." Worse, the artefact is dose-shaped: more applications means more damage, so the artefact produces a clean-looking concentration–response that a curve-fitter will happily fit and a Bayesian optimiser will happily chase.

This is the third distinct instance the vault holds of the thing being controlled not being the thing being delivered:

  • the-dose-the-loop-controls-is-not-the-dose-the-cell-receives — in a perfused liver chip, the concentration dissolved into the medium is not the concentration inside the cell, and the gap is compound-specific and coupled to the response being measured.
  • the-scheduler-is-an-uncontrolled-variable-the-loop-cannot-see — the robot's calendar turned out to be a chemical variable.
  • And now: the act of applying the dose is itself an exposure.

All three have the same shape. The loop's model contains a variable called "dose." The world contains several, and the loop cannot see which one it is holding.

The honest size of it

Small, industry-funded, and the authors say more work is needed. Three donors, three inserts each. The liquid-versus-aerosol contrast is a comparison between two protocols that differ in several ways at once, not a controlled experiment isolating delivery mode, and the authors say so.

It also does not clear the aerosol route. Their own list of what nebulisation makes harder is longer than the problem it solves: droplets stick to the chamber walls, deposition depends on droplet size and viscosity, each new substance needs its own deposition characterisation before use, and the airway-deposition model used to translate applied dose into a human-equivalent dose relies on simplifying assumptions about airway geometry and breathing. Their conclusion on the threshold they found is "model-specific rather than a definitive human exposure limit."

Which lands exactly on this map's standing blocker. The map already records that this endpoint has no classification threshold, and since 2026-08-22 the deeper version: a threshold is a rule applied to a number, and the number is not yet a single standardised quantity — no-decision-rule-without-a-standardised-measurement. Tonight adds that the measurement varies with how the dose was put on, which is one more reason the rule cannot be written yet, and one more thing an automated system would be unable to detect it was missing.

What it does not license

Funded by Unilever, four of seven authors Unilever employees, on a chemical class Unilever sells. The practical conclusion — that meaningful disruption occurs only well above occupational exposure levels — is the conclusion that funder would want, and the vault records it as a claim rather than a finding.

No immune cells in the model, for an endpoint that is about immune sensitisation. The authors name this.

No guideline, no decision rule, no classification. Nothing here moves this map's regulatory axis, which remains none — meaning no Test Guideline exists, not that the method is unusable.
