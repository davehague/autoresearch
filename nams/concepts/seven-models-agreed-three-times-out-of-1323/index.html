Seven models agreed three times out of 1,323

The measurement

Same 1,323 problems, seven different open-weight language models, two ways of scoring. How often do all seven put the correct hypothesis first?

Scoring paradigmUnanimityAggregate quality
Softmax NLL (likelihood)167 / 1,323 = 12.6%stable, 68.8–74.3% pairwise
Raw-logit energy3 / 1,323 = 0.2%best individual results, 45.8–82.2% pairwise

The two paradigms perform comparably in aggregate — the authors note NLL is slightly ahead in seven of twelve disciplines and slightly behind in the rest. So this is not "the good method agrees and the bad one doesn't." The paradigm with the highest ceiling is the one where the models share almost nothing.

Why this is the vault's rail, in a fourth setting

The correlated-blind-spot rail says a battery of components sharing a failure mode casts fewer votes than it has members. The vault has now recorded the arithmetic in assays (tgx-ddi-micronucleus-share-the-s9-metabolism-gap), inside a single cell line (a-battery-inside-one-cell-line-is-not-five-independent-votes), across frontier models (multiple-frontier-models-are-one-vote-not-six) and, measured with Cohen's kappa, across software adjudicators (a-jury-of-three-can-be-two-voices-and-you-can-measure-which).

This note is the opposite pole of the same axis, and it is just as bad for a loop. Chen et al. measured a panel whose members were too correlated to count as independent votes. Here the members are so uncorrelated that the authors cannot tell whether they are tracking the same thing at all.

Both readings sink the same architecture. If your seven models are one voice, an ensemble buys you nothing. If your seven models are seven different voices agreeing 0.2% of the time, an ensemble has no consensus to report, and any aggregation rule you write over them — majority vote, mean score, best-of — is arbitrating between judges you have no reason to believe are measuring the same quantity.

The authors' own sentence is the honest version, and it is a statement about construct validity, not performance:

"the low cross-model agreement under energy scoring leaves open whether the models share a common signal tracking scientific correctness or whether each one is picking up different surface cues that happen to favor the gold hypothesis."

The practical consequence, stated plainly

A loop that reaches for an ensemble as its safety mechanism — "we'll ask several models and go with what they agree on" — needs to know which regime it is in, and the number that tells you is not the accuracy number. It is the agreement number, and the two are close to independent here: the paradigm with the better accuracy has the worse agreement.

The vault's standing ask, now with a second instance behind it: any battery, panel, ensemble or jury whose members are treated as votes should report a measured agreement statistic alongside its performance statistic. Chen et al. showed why raw agreement is the wrong statistic when the base rate is skewed and recommended Cohen's kappa. This paper reports raw unanimity counts, which is better than nothing and is not kappa. Neither statistic appears anywhere in the vault's own correlated-blind-spot rows, which remains an open gap the vault has logged against itself.

What it does not license

  • Preprint, not peer reviewed. One task, one benchmark, one construction of distractors.
  • Low agreement is not proof of independence any more than high agreement is proof of correlation — the authors say only that the question is open, and so does the vault.
  • These are seven open-weight models of differing size, data, tokenizer and alignment, which the authors note makes them "useful for comparison but not for isolating the cause." Some of the disagreement is certainly the models being different rather than the signal being absent.
  • Not toxicology. No chemical, no assay. The transfer is the vault's inference.
