The validated assay whose cells were not what anyone thought they were

From TG 455's own footnote:

"Before June 2016, this cell line was designated as BG1Luc cell line. … Relatively recently, it was discovered that there exist two different variants of BG-1 cells being used by researchers, BG-1 Fr and BG-1 NIEHS. In-depth analysis, including DNA testing, of these two BG-1 variant cell lines carried out by Li and coworkers (2014) showed that the BG-1 Fr was unique and that the BG-1 NIEHS, i.e. the original cell line used to develop the assay, was not the BG1 human ovarian carcinoma cell line, but was instead a variant of the MCF7 human breast cancer cell line. The cell line used in the assay, originally referred to as BG1Luc4E2, will now be designated as VM7Luc4E2 ("V" = variant; "M7" = MCF7 cells)."

And then the OECD's ruling on the consequences:

"While this changes the origin of the cell line upon which the assay is based, it does not affect published validation studies nor the utility and application of this assay."

Both readings are true and the vault should hold them together.

Reading one — the OECD is right, and the reason is instructive. Validation in this field is an empirical performance record, not a derivation from the test system's assumed biology. The VM7Luc assay was never justified by an argument of the form "ovarian carcinoma cells are the correct model for estrogen signalling, therefore this assay predicts ER activity." It was justified by running reference chemicals through it and measuring what came out. Nothing in the ICCVAM validation depended on the cells' organ of origin, so nothing in the validation broke when the organ of origin turned out to be wrong. That is a quietly profound statement about what a validated NAM is: a black box with a measured input–output relationship, whose internals are documented but not load-bearing. It is also, note, the opposite of the argument the FDA's 2026 draft guidance makes for unvalidated NAMs, which leans hard on human biological relevance — "the relevance of the cell types used, species of origin" (fda-nam-validation-framework-four-features). Empirical validation does not care what the cells are; fit-for-purpose credibility assessment cares a great deal. The two routes to acceptance rest on different epistemologies, and this cell line is where you can see the seam.

Reading two — this is a reproducibility failure that reached an adopted international standard. Cell-line misidentification and cross-contamination are among the best-documented systemic failures in biomedical research. Here one passed assay development, formal validation by an independent public body (ICCVAM / NICEATM), and OECD adoption, and was caught only when someone finally ran DNA authentication on the line. The error was not subtle — the cells were from the wrong organ. Whatever one concludes about the validation's robustness, the detection mechanism that caught it was not the validation process; it was an outside group doing genetic authentication a decade later.

What it changes here. Nothing about TG 455's status: consensus, adopted on both axes. It is filed as an open question about the vault's model of evidence, not as a downgrade. The vault grades methods by evidence_tier and regulatory_status, and both of those graded this assay correctly and would have graded it identically on the day the cells were still believed to be ovarian. The provenance of the physical test system is a third thing the vault does not track — the same third thing that, on the same cell line, later became tg455-vm7luc-adopted-but-unrunnable.
