Vitrigel-Eye Irritancy Test (OECD TG 494) → eye irritation / serious eye damage

Human corneal epithelial (hCE) cells are grown on a collagen vitrigel membrane (CVM) into a tissue with a functioning barrier. A chemical is applied for three minutes, and the assay watches Transepithelial Electrical Resistance (TEER) — how strongly the tissue resists a small electric current — fall in real time. Intact barrier, high resistance; damaged barrier, low resistance. The readout is not a single number but the shape of the curve.

The guideline's rationale for measuring barrier function rather than cell death: chemicals irritating to the eye "first destroy tear film and epithelial barrier function of the eye, subsequently induce epithelial cell death, and finally produce stromal degeneration." Barrier loss is upstream of the viability loss that TG 492 measures — the same cascade, read one step earlier.

The decision rule, in full

Three indexes are computed from the mean TEER curve over three tests: time lag (t1), intensity (−[P2 − P1]/[t2 − t1]), and plateau level (100 − P2). Then:

  • No Category — "no further testing with another test method is considered necessary" — if time lag > 180 s AND intensity < 0.05 %/s AND plateau level ≤ 5.0 %.
  • "No Stand-alone Prediction Can Be Made" — routes onward — if any one of those fails.

Three arithmetic quantities, three fixed thresholds, one boolean AND. No expert judgment appears anywhere in the call, which is what puts this method at closed-loop-ready.

The asymmetry is the design, not a defect

TG 494 "is not intended to identify nor differentiate between UN GHS Category 1 and UN GHS Category 2" (nor 2A from 2B). It is a Bottom-Up first step: a negative clears a chemical outright, and anything else is explicitly not a positive call. The guideline concedes "a high percentage of false positive results" and argues they "are not critical" because non-negatives route onward for more testing — the same battery-position argument the vault recorded for TG 492 and TG 495. Specificity of 61–71% is the price: roughly one safe chemical in three is pushed on to further testing.

Read the 83% → 96% move correctly

The sensitivity improvement is not a better assay. It is the same assay answering a smaller question: 51 of 158 chemicals (32%) were removed by a pre-test before the numbers were computed. Excluded are acidic preparations (pH ≤ 5) — five of the ten original false negatives were acidic, and their TEER values increased, so the barrier appeared to improve — and rapid phase separation (water-insoluble solids separating from the medium within the test window), plus any chemical whose 2.5% w/v preparation shifts absorbance by more than 0.1 between 0 and 3 minutes.

The exclusions are pre-specified and mechanistically justified, not post-hoc. But 96% sensitivity is a conditional number and the condition covers a third of the chemicals tested. Both surviving false negatives were Cat. 2B (mild irritants) — the least costly class to miss.

What is genuinely new here

Every exclusion criterion is itself a measurement. A pH reading, an absorbance delta at two timepoints, an observed phase separation — all instrument-obtainable, all pre-specified with numbers. This is the vault's first domain_gate a machine can execute rather than merely be told. See machine-checkable-applicability-domain-gate.
