The phototoxicity column's oldest blank gets a candidate, and it is one chemical wide

The blank. Sunlight plus a chemical injures skin two ways, and they are different diseases. Photoirritation is direct chemical damage — it happens to anyone, on first exposure, in proportion to dose. Photoallergy is an immune reaction — it needs a prior sensitising exposure, affects only sensitised people, and does not scale the same way. Every adopted non-animal phototoxicity method in this vault measures the first and, by its own text, disclaims the second. phototoxicity-3t3-nru records that OECD Test Guideline 432 — the internationally adopted mouse-cell test that reads how much more cells die in the light — excludes "photogenotoxicity, photoallergy, photocarcinogenicity" explicitly. The tiered strategy above it, phototoxicity-iata, orders four adopted methods and not one of them touches the immune mechanism. The source's own statement of the gap: the ability to discriminate "remains an unmet need, as no validated in vitro assay is currently available for this purpose."

The candidate. Take TG 432's structure — chemical on two plates, one irradiated with ultraviolet-A, one dark — swap the guideline's mouse fibroblasts for human keratinocytes (HaCaT), and read interleukin-18 release instead of cell death. IL-18 is a signalling protein keratinocytes put out when they are provoked in a way that engages the immune system; it has been proposed for years as an allergy-vs-irritation marker.

What separated. Benzophenone — a sunscreen UV filter and a known photoallergen — raised IL-18 by roughly two-fold (total stimulation index 1.9–2.1) under 4 J/cm² UVA, significantly at two of three concentrations. The two photoirritants barely moved it. An exploratory antibody array added two more directional candidates pointing opposite ways: MMP-1 up with the photoallergen, IL-6 up with a photoirritant.

What this is, at its actual size

One photoallergen. The entire discrimination claim is benzophenone behaving differently from 8-methoxypsoralen and chlorpromazine. With one compound in the class of interest, no sensitivity, specificity or accuracy exists to report — not because the authors withheld them but because they cannot be computed. This is a demonstration that the marker can separate a case, not an estimate of how often it would.

A two-fold effect on a baseline the authors call noisy. "A relatively high inter-experimental variability in basal intracellular IL-18 levels among control cells was observed throughout the study." A doubling is a real effect; a doubling sitting on unstable controls is a thin margin, and it is the first thing a validation would have to nail down.

The mechanism is not in the dish, and the authors say so. "IL-18 alone may not be sufficient to unequivocally discriminate photoallergens from photoirritants in all cases, as keratinocyte monocultures cannot fully reproduce the complex immune mechanisms involved in photoallergic reactions." A photoallergic reaction requires dendritic cells presenting to T cells and a second exposure days later. A well of keratinocytes has none of that. What is measured is an early keratinocyte signal that correlates with an immune outcome, which is the same relationship the skin-sensitisation key-event assays have to skin allergy — and those took two decades and a defined approach to become usable.

The marker is cell-line-dependent, which is a problem for automation specifically. The results disagree with earlier IL-18 work in the NCTC2544 line, and the authors attribute the difference to "protocols used, such as contact time between the chemicals as well as the different cell profiles." That is an unresolved discrepancy, and it means the readout's behaviour depends on which immortalised keratinocyte you bought.

Why it matters to the loop, and what it does not mean

The measurement is machine-ready and the decision is not. IL-18 by immunoassay and cytokines by antibody array are plate-reader outputs. Nothing here is scored down a microscope. A robot could run this assay tomorrow. And there is no decision rule of any kind — no threshold on the stimulation index, no rule for combining IL-18 with MMP-1 and IL-6, no reference chemical set to calibrate one against. That is the vault's most common blocker, arriving at the earliest possible moment in a method's life: the instrument exists before the rule does.

Do not read this as "photoallergy is now testable." regulatory_status: none, evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, four chemicals, one laboratory, one cell line. The honest statement is that a named coverage blank in the phototoxicity column now has a first candidate marker with a plausible mechanism and one supporting compound.
