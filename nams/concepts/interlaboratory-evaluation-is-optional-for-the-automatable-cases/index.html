The exemption, verbatim, and what it is and is not

The framework. ICCVAM's Validation Workgroup — the US interagency body that decides how alternative test methods get validated for federal regulatory use, spanning 17 agencies — replaces the one-size-fits-all validation model with five steps, each able to send you back to an earlier one:

"1) initial vetting of the scientific relevance of the NAM, 2) conceptual evaluation, 3) within-laboratory evaluation, 4) statistical data analysis and reporting, and 5) interlaboratory evaluation (if needed¹)."

Footnote 1:

"There could be examples of methods for which interlaboratory evaluation is not needed or not feasible, such as large scale roboticized high-throughput screening assays and machine learning-based in silico approaches."

The vault holds a map for each named exemption.

  • Large-scale roboticised high-throughput screening is automation-tox21-qhts-platform — a Stäubli arm running ~10,000 chemicals × 15 doses × triplicate, weekly, since 2013.
  • Machine-learning-based in silico approaches is in-silico-qsar-read-across and the model layer's own quality record, ml-model-reporting-reproducibility.

Read the sentence carefully, because it does two different things at once.

"Not feasible" is the strong and unobjectionable half. You cannot re-run a 10,000-chemical robotic screen in a second laboratory at reasonable cost, and a trained model has no second laboratory — it has a second machine, which is not the same test of anything. This is a genuine practical limit, and pretending otherwise would just freeze the field.

"Not needed" is the load-bearing half, and it is asserted rather than argued. The report gives no reasoning for why these method classes require less confidence in transferability, and no substitute requirement in place of it. Two of ICCVAM's five key concepts — data integrity and information transparency — are plausibly the intended replacements, and the report does say "Use of computational algorithms, e.g., machine learning models, and software (including version number) should be well-documented to ensure reproducibility." But documenting a model is not the same evidence as a second laboratory reproducing a result. It is a claim about the record, not about the world.

What the exemption is NOT. It is not the abolition of the ring trial, and the vault must not report it that way. Three checks:

  1. The report still commissions ring trials. Its own international-harmonisation chapter cites "ICCVAM's support in coordinating an interlaboratory prevalidation study for a NAM developed by EPA based on an in vitro human thyroid microtissue assay" — which is deisenroth-2026-thyroid-microtissue-interlab-validation, the three-lab study the vault ingested on 2026-07-18. The exemption is targeted at two method classes, not a general retreat.
  2. It is non-binding and US-only. "This report will be updated on a regular basis and as needed"; OECD's Mutual Acceptance of Data still runs on Test Guidelines, and an OECD TG still requires what an OECD TG requires.
  3. It says "could be examples," not "shall be exempt." The permission is conditional and unexercised on any specific method in the document.

Why it still matters enormously. The direction is what counts. This vault's evidence_tier ladder puts formally-validated above peer-reviewed-method on exactly one distinguishing property — that a method has been shown to work on somebody else's bench, with somebody else's hands. It is the only rung in the ladder that is genuinely hard to fake. Footnote 1 says that rung is skippable for the automatable cases. See automatability-buys-an-exemption-from-the-hardest-to-fake-evidence.

The vault's own open question, answered — negatively. On 2026-07-22 the vault logged: "the ring trial is being demoted by four actors with four motives, and nobody has said what replaces it." This is a fifth actor, and it is the one with the authority, and it names the mechanism: nothing replaces it. It becomes conditional, and the condition is the context of use — "The COU determines the detailed criteria and implementation of the key concepts." There is no fixed bar. What counts as enough depends on what you claim you will use it for, and the agency decides case by case.
