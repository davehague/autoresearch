Everything in the vault is run in room air, and nobody has asked what that costs

A standard cell-culture incubator is room air plus 5% carbon dioxide. The oxygen the cells see is therefore roughly 18–19 kPa. Human tissue in a living body is nowhere near that: skin, gut, marrow and most solid tissue sit somewhere around 1–6 kPa, because oxygen is consumed on the way in from the blood. Cells in a dish are, by tissue standards, permanently in excess oxygen.

This is not obscure. Physiological-oxygen culture is an established methodological argument in cell biology. What makes it worth a vault note is the scope of what shares the condition. Look at the wet methods the vault maps — the reconstructed-skin irritation and corrosion tests, the eye tests, the five ARE-Nrf2 sensitisation methods, the micronucleus and comet genotoxicity assays, HepaRG and liver-chip work, the receptor-transactivation and steroidogenesis assays, the neurosphere and zebrafish batteries, Tox21's entire 1536-well platform. All of them, without exception, are performed in room air. It is not a variable in any of their guidelines. It is the unexamined constant underneath the entire column.

And oxygen tension is not a neutral background for the thing several of these assays measure. The source states the mismatch "can alter redox tone, stress-response signaling, and interpretation of chemical effects." The assays most exposed to that are the ones whose readout is a stress response — which is exactly the ARE-Nrf2 slot at the centre of OECD Test Guideline 442D, the reactive-oxygen-species photoreactivity assay, and every viability endpoint that can be pushed by oxidative damage.

Why this is filed as a question and not as a rail flag

The vault's correlated-blind-spot rail fires when two assays in a battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, and it is tempting to fire it here at maximum scope: forty maps, one shared condition, therefore one vote. That would be wrong, and it is worth being explicit about why, because the temptation is the failure mode the rail itself warns about — reasoning fast from a plausible structure to a confident conclusion.

Three things are missing:

  1. No demonstrated effect on any hazard call. The source profiled four materials at 5 kPa. It does not report a side-by-side room-air arm, so there is no internal comparator and no measurement of how much any answer moved. "This condition could matter" is not "this condition changed a classification."
  2. A shared condition is not a shared failure mode. The rail's existing entries name a specific chemical class that walks through: the fish embryo test and the RTgill-W1 fish cell assay both miss neurotoxicants and bioactivation-dependent compounds because neither has a nervous system or full metabolism. Nobody has named the chemical class that ambient oxygen causes assays to miss, or to over-call. Without that, there is nothing for a loop to check against.
  3. The direction is not even known. Excess oxygen might sensitise a redox readout (more false positives) or might saturate the antioxidant machinery so a real effect no longer registers (more false negatives). These have opposite consequences and the source distinguishes them nowhere.

So no correlated_blind_spot is set on any map from this, and nothing moves. It is logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md as a question that would be cheap to answer and has apparently not been asked: run one adopted assay's reference chemical set at 5 kPa and at 19 kPa and report whether any classification flips.

The loop angle, which is the reason to keep this at all

An automated testing loop inherits its assay's culture conditions from the guideline and never questions them, because the guideline is the specification. A condition that no guideline names is a condition no loop can vary, examine, or report. This is a different shape of limit from the vault's usual one: not a missing decision rule, and not a readout that needs a human — a parameter that has been held fixed for so long it stopped being visible as a parameter.

And it composes with tonight's other finding. feedback-produced-parameter-tweaks-not-replanning observed agents responding to real experimental feedback with local parameter adjustments and never with analytical-method redesign. Asking "is my incubator's oxygen wrong for this tissue?" is firmly on the redesign side of that line.
