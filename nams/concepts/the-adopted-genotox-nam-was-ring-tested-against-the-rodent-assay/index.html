The adopted replacement was certified against the thing it replaces

The vault records OECD Test Guideline 446A (ToxTracker, adopted July 2026) as an adopted method on both the scientific and the regulatory axes: a panel of engineered mouse embryonic stem cells whose green-fluorescent reporters light up along distinct stress pathways, read by flow cytometry, telling you why a genotoxicity result came back positive rather than merely that it did.

What the vault did not have was the reference standard that trial was scored against. Two method developers, writing the framing editorial for a special issue, supply it:

"One example is the interlaboratory ring trial performed to support the OECD guideline creation for ToxTracker, which was accepted by OECD July of this year. A critical component was benchmarking results to in vivo genotoxicity outcomes, specifically, the rodent erythrocyte micronucleus assay."

Why this is a limit and not a scandal

It is not a defect in TG 446A. It is the only option the field has, and the same editorial explains why the alternative is unavailable:

"It is a conceptual catch 22. On one hand, we strive to compare NAM performance to expected human toxicities, yet human data rarely exist as preclinical models have identified mutagenic hazards which limited (or prevented) human exposure."

The reason there is no human data is that the animal test worked. It flagged the chemical and nobody was exposed. Success at the old method destroys the evidence needed to certify the new one — and that is a structural trap, not a funding problem or an effort problem.

What it changes for this vault, precisely

The vault's evidence_tier axis has a top rung, regulatory-adopted, and the vault has been reading that rung as "this has been shown to work." This note narrows what it means to one sentence: it agrees, within stated bounds, with a rodent assay.

That is genuinely worth having and it is not the same claim. And the editorial adds the direction of the error in the reference standard itself:

"LOAELs from rodent studies are not protective of human health unless non-empirically derived fit-for-purpose uncertainty factors are applied. Conversely, points of departure derived from NAMs yield more conservative, protective, hazard estimates."

So the benchmark is known to sit in the wrong direction, and agreement with it is what earns the guideline.

Why this matters more for a loop than for a person

A human toxicologist reading a TG 446A result knows, tacitly, that the validation was against a rodent assay and discounts accordingly. An automated system does not have a tacit anything. Its decision rule inherits the reference standard's error as a constant offset with no flag on it, and the loop's speed converts a known bias into a compounding one — the same structure the vault's correlated-blind-spot rail describes, one level up: not two assays sharing a blind spot, but every assay in the column sharing a comparator.

Nothing demotes. regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted is a fact about the guideline and is untouched; evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted is untouched; the map's loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop and its blocker (too new — no decision rule published yet) are untouched. What changes is what a reader should take the top rung to mean.
