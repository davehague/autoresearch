A domain gate a machine can run — OECD TG 494's pre-test

shared/evidence-model.md defines domain_gate as "an applicability-domain carve-out the loop must check before trusting the decision rule." Every instance the vault holds is a fact the loop has to be told:

TG 494 is different in kind. Its gate is not a sentence about which chemicals are in scope. It is a pre-test — three physical measurements, each with a stated numeric threshold, run on the actual sample before the assay proper:

  1. pH ≤ 5 → out of domain.
  2. Rapid phase separation during the three-minute test window → out of domain.
  3. |absorbance(3 min) − absorbance(0 min)| > 0.1 for the 2.5% w/v preparation → "the chemical should not be tested."

A robot with a pH probe, a plate reader, and a timer can determine, by measurement on the sample in front of it, that this assay is not competent to answer for this chemical. Nothing has to be looked up, inferred from structure, or known in advance.

Why this matters more than another loop-ready assay

The vault holds twenty closed-loop-ready methods. Adding a twenty-first is not a finding. What has been consistently missing is the assay's ability to decline.

shared/evidence-model.md states the risk directly: "a closed-loop-ready tier does not mean 'trust the DIP on any chemical' — it means trust it inside the domain." That sentence describes an obligation on whoever builds the loop. An automated system that runs a validated assay under a fixed decision rule on a chemical outside its domain gets a confident, deterministic, wrong answer — and the blind-spot rail's core worry applies: it will iterate on it.

TG 494 puts the domain check inside the protocol, where the loop cannot skip it by not knowing. That is a structurally better shape for autonomy than a higher-performing assay with a prose caveat.

The honest counterweights

The gate is doing real work, and the headline number hides that. In-domain sensitivity is 96%; across everything tested it is 83%. 32% of chemicals got no answer at all. A loop that reports "96% sensitive" without also reporting its refusal rate is misrepresenting itself — and a refusal rate is the one performance statistic no OECD Test Guideline template currently requires. (Vault observation, flagged as one.)

It is not general. These three criteria protect against this assay's known failure modes — acid-driven TEER increase, and solids separating out of medium. They say nothing about, say, metabolite-mediated hazard, which TG 494 is equally blind to and which no pre-test detects. A machine-checkable gate covers the failures someone anticipated; the dangerous ones are the rest.

And the same idea shows up in the model layer the same night. deep-learning-dart-prediction-recall-gap carries a computed applicability domain (nearest-neighbour distance on molecular fingerprints, threshold mean + 0.5σ) with out-of-domain predictions excluded from the evaluation. Wet assay and model, independently, both converging on: measure whether you are entitled to answer, then answer. That is arguably the single most transferable design pattern the vault has recorded for autonomous systems — and neither source frames it that way. (Vault inference.)
