hrER binding (OECD TG 493): the receptor is touched — that is all it says

TG 493 is the vault's first cell-free receptor method and its first method that reads a molecular interaction rather than a biological response. There are no cells, no transcription, no reporter — just recombinant human ERα protein, a radiolabelled reference ligand, and a competition.

The measurement. In the guideline's words, the assay "measures the ability of a radiolabeled ligand ([3H]17β-estradiol) to bind with the ER in the presence of increasing concentrations of a test chemical (i.e. competitor). Test chemicals that possess a high affinity for the ER compete with the radiolabeled ligand at a lower concentration as compared with those chemicals with lower affinity." Displacement curve → log(IC50) and relative binding affinity (RBA) against estradiol. Two reference methods: Freyberger-Wilson (full-length hrERα) and CERI (ligand-binding domain only).

Binding is not activation, and this is the point of having it. A chemical that binds ERα may:

  • activate it (agonist),
  • block it (antagonist), or
  • occupy it with no transcriptional consequence.

TG 493 cannot tell these apart. It reports that the chemical reaches the receptor's ligand-binding site. Its sibling endocrine-er-transactivation (TG 455) reports what the receptor then does. Neither subsumes the other, and the OECD Conceptual Framework carries both at Level 2 for exactly that reason.

So this is a composition, not a redundancy. The vault's 07-connect layer hunts for two assays covering the same endpoint — and superficially TG 493 and TG 455 both "do the estrogen receptor." They do not do the same thing. They read adjacent nodes of one molecular pathway: ligand→receptor (TG 493), then receptor→DNA→transcription (TG 455). The correct connect verdict is composes-with, and the correct warning is the inverse of a redundancy warning: a TG 493 positive is not an ER-activity finding, and treating it as one would over-call every silent binder as an endocrine active.

A distinct accessibility gate. TG 493 needs [3H]17β-estradiol — a radioisotope. That is neither a commercial licence (HepaRG™, Corrositex®, Derek Nexus) nor an unobtainable cell line (tg455-vm7luc-adopted-but-unrunnable); it is a radiochemistry permit, and it excludes any lab that does not hold one. A third species of the practical-accessibility constraint the vault named on 2026-07-10 and still does not model. Worth noting that this is the one adopted endocrine method here with no commercial licensing gate at all (funding_flag: none) — its barrier is regulatory-physical, not commercial.

Scope. Level 2, screening and prioritisation, weight-of-evidence input. Not apical, not a fertility test (see reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams).
