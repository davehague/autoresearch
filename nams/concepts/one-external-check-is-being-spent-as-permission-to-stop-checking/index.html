One external check is being spent as permission to stop checking

The economics that force the substitution, which are real

A classification society review costs money and months. An autonomous loop generates thousands of candidates. You cannot certify them all, so you build a cheap internal predictor of what the certifier would say, and you run the loop against the predictor. The authors call this a "two-stage validation cascade" and are open that it exists to resolve a "cost–credibility trade-off."

Nothing about that is unreasonable, and every autonomous lab will have to do some version of it. A toxicology loop cannot run an animal study to check itself either. This is the general shape of the problem, and the paper is the clearest statement of it the vault has.

The two places the validation does not reach

First: the audit is one-sided. The single highest-scoring candidate was submitted and it passed. Nothing the internal reviewer rejected was ever shown to the certifier. So the experiment can establish that a design the gate passed also passed CCS. It cannot establish the rate at which the gate passes things the certifier would refuse — the direction that matters, since that is the direction in which an unattended loop ships a bad design — because n = 1 in that cell, and the one trial required three rounds of expert repair before it cleared.

This vault ingested the general form of this failure last night. a-capability-measured-by-the-oracle-it-optimised-against-is-not-a-capability records a self-improving agent that hit 43 of 60 targets under the scorer its own loop was calling and 2 of 43 under a scorer it had never seen. The sentence from that source applies here unchanged: "No statistic computed from the system and its own oracle sees that gap." Chen et al. built a two-sided criterion precisely because a confirming-direction-only check is the thing that cannot see it. This paper ran the confirming direction only.

Second: the "independent" channel is not attributed to an independent party. To show the internal reviewer tracks real regulatory judgement, the authors "ran a parallel regulatory review channel that independently scored the same reference projects against codified classification-society rules" — DNV standards rather than the AI's own reference lines. The result is a Spearman correlation of 0.717 and a mean absolute deviation of 3.9 points across 11 projects.

Two problems. The paper never says who ran that channel. Read plainly, it is the same team applying a second rulebook — which measures whether their scoring function is internally coherent, not whether it predicts a certifier's behaviour. And ρ = 0.717 at n = 11 is reported with no confidence interval; at that sample size the interval is wide enough to include correlations that would not support the word "reliable."

The vault's rail on this is a-jury-of-three-can-be-two-voices-and-you-can-measure-which: adjudicator independence is measurable, is usually lower than its arity suggests, and must be measured rather than asserted. Here it was asserted.

The claim the vault will not carry

"With this calibration in place, the system can subsequently explore thousands of new candidates at negligible marginal cost, relying on its internal Automated Reviewer for routine termination decisions while retaining confidence that the gate is aligned with third-party certification standards."

One passing submission is not a calibration. It is a single point in the confirming direction, on a design that needed three rounds of human-mediated revision to get there. The vault records the architecture as a serious and probably necessary design pattern, and records the licence the authors draw from it as overstated by their own evidence.

Why this matters more in toxicology than in steel

In engineering the internal gate is a scoring function over quantities the physics solvers computed, and the solvers are right. In toxicology the cheap internal predictor of an expensive outcome is the missing artifact — it is the same object as the missing decision rule (no-published-rule-says-how-much-a-nam-result-counts) and the same object the experiment-selection literature needs and does not have (experiment-selection-under-known-cost-is-the-loops-other-half). A tox loop that adopted this pattern would be substituting a surrogate gate for a real one without the surrogate existing yet, and calibrating it against an outcome that takes two years and a rodent to observe.
