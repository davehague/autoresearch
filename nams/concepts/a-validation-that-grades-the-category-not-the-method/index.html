A validation that grades the category, not the method

Every validation instrument this vault holds evaluates one method. An OECD Test Guideline describes one assay. A ring trial takes one protocol to several laboratories. ICCVAM's five-step framework builds confidence in a New Approach Methodology for a context of use.

This study evaluates a class. Eight different commercial liver microphysiological systems — engineered human liver tissue kept alive under flow, from eight competing vendors, built on different architectures — run the same blinded compounds under harmonised principles, and the stated deliverable is "category-level performance expectations rather than rank individual systems."

Why that is a real methodological choice and not a fudge

The commercial reason is obvious: eight competitors will not co-fund a study that names a loser. But the scientific object it produces is genuinely different, and it is one a regulator can use.

A drug sponsor arriving at FDA with liver-chip data does not currently have an answer to "how much should I believe this?" that is independent of which chip they bought. A category-level expectation gives one: this is what a liver MPS, generically, can and cannot resolve. That is the shape of statement a context of use argument needs — and the ICCVAM framework the vault already holds says the context of use is what "determines the detailed criteria."

What it cannot do, and this is the cost

A category-level result cannot tell you whether your platform is the good one. If the eight platforms disagree substantially, the honest category-level summary is a wide band — and a sponsor whose particular system sits at the bad end of that band is credited with the band, not with their own performance. The design buys collective legitimacy by discarding discrimination between products.

For a loop, that matters more than it does for a human. An automated system choosing which assay to run next, or how much to weight a result, needs a per-instrument reliability estimate. A category-level band tells it how much to trust a liver chip; it tells it nothing about how much to trust this liver chip, which is the only one it has.

Held apart from the result, which does not exist yet

Nothing here is a performance number. The paper describes the design. evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method grades the standing of a peer-reviewed methods paper, not a validated method, and hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps does not move on it.
