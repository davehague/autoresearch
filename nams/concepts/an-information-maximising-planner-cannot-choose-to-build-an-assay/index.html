Agenda drift was not a sociology problem. It is a theorem about the scoring function.

This vault has recorded, four times and from four unrelated directions, that automated science drifts toward the questions that are already easy to measure:

  • Hartung's perspective named agenda drift — research agendas "shift from curiosity-driven exploration toward problems that are computationally convenient" (2026-07-13).
  • mcnamara-fallacy-selects-the-endpoints-that-already-have-assays attached a measurement to it: AI adoption shrinks the collective volume of topics studied by 4.63% while its users publish 3.02× more.
  • experiment-selection-under-known-cost-is-the-loops-other-half found that cost-aware Bayesian optimisation needs a likelihood model — how much does this cheap measurement tell me about the real question — and that in toxicology that number does not exist.
  • And the vault's own ledger: 19 maps sit at automatable-open-loop because no agreed decision rule exists, while the endpoints with no method at all are the ones nobody automates.

Every one of those was treated as a behavioural tendency — a thing incentives do to people, or a data gap somebody could fill. This paper says it is a structural property of the acquisition function, and proves it.

The proof, in software terms

Model discovery as a shortest-path problem over belief states. Split actions in two:

  • A measurement returns information now.
  • A constructive action returns nothing now. It buys a capability — an instrument, an assay, a pipeline, an abstraction — whose value is entirely the future actions it unlocks.

The capability-indistinguishability lemma: inside a bounded horizon, acquiring a capability looks exactly like paying for a null action. Both cost money; both yield zero information in the window. So a planner that scores by information-within-horizon cannot see the difference, and any measurement with even epsilon-positive yield beats the construction.

The consequence is not "myopic planning is somewhat suboptimal." For every depth d there is an instance with unbounded approximation ratio, and one the planner never solves. Widening the horizon does not help, because the construction chain can always be made one step longer than the horizon. This is a reachability failure, which the authors separate explicitly from the two difficulty axes the optimisation literature already knows — curvature (submodularity) and information order (adaptivity gaps).

Why this lands hard on this vault, and it is the specific case

Developing a NAM for an endpoint that has none is a constructive action. It is the canonical one. It returns no hazard information on the day you fund it; its entire value is that afterwards you can ask questions you previously could not ask at all.

Now read the vault's two bench-bound maps against the lemma:

  • reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams — bench_bound_reason: no-nam-exists. There is no validated non-animal method for the endpoint. Building one is a multi-year construction returning zero information in any horizon a planner has.
  • immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm — bench_bound_reason: animal-input. The assay is fully quantitative with a fixed decision rule and would otherwise read as loop-ready; it is bench-bound only because its scaling model consumes a prior mouse maximum-tolerated dose. Fixing it means inventing a new prediction model — again, a construction with no immediate readout.

Both are exactly the actions the theorem says an information-maximising loop will never select — while the same loop happily spends its budget re-screening chemicals on the plate-reader endpoints where every action returns a number. An autonomous testing system optimising expected information gain will get better and better at the assays that already exist, and will never build the ones that do not, no matter how long you run it. That is not a funding failure or a taste failure. It is the objective.

And it composes with tonight's other loop finding, unpleasantly. feedback-produced-parameter-tweaks-not-replanning observed agents receiving five rounds of real experimental feedback and responding with parameter adjustments, never with analytical-method redesign. Method redesign is a constructive action. The theory says a myopic scorer cannot value it; the empirical observation is that the systems did not do it. Two sources, ten days apart, different fields, neither citing the other, describing the same missing move from the theoretical and the observed side.

What this does not establish

The remedy is not established, only proposed. CG-Plan — a cost-to-go heuristic split into h_cap + h_exp (cost of capabilities still needed, plus cost of experiments once you have them) — is evaluated on a synthetic testbed the authors built, and its advantage "appears only under gating," which the authors state themselves. No wet lab, no instrument, no real data, not peer reviewed, and no toxicology anywhere in it. The theorem is what this vault is taking; the planner is not.

And a real caution against over-reading: to choose a construction, a planner must first have the construction in its action set. Who writes down "invent a non-animal fertility assay" as an available action, and at what estimated cost, is not addressed by the paper and is not a smaller problem than the one it solves.
