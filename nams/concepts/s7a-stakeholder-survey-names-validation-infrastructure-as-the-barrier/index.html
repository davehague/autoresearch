The people who would run a revised ICH S7A say the obstacle is validation infrastructure, not missing science

This map's standing finding — from the February 2026 Brussels workshop that opened it (the-modernisation-pillars-name-a-better-animal-study-and-a-human-judgement) — is that the two loudest proposed revision pillars are "state-of-the-art functional in vivo approaches" and "weight-of- evidence decision frameworks," a better animal study and a human judgement. This survey is the same finding, reached by polling the people who would actually have to implement a revision rather than summarising a workshop's themes, and it sharpens the "why" in a way the workshop report did not: respondents were not asked whether they wanted NAMs, they were asked what stops them, and the top answer across every respondent group — industry, CRO/CRL, regulator alike — was insufficient qualification/validation standards and limited regulatory precedent. Not cost. Not throughput. Not scientific implausibility. The infrastructure that turns a working assay into an accepted one.

The 69% number is the sharpest single data point. A weight-of-evidence/integrated risk-assessment framework is the clearest majority position in the whole survey — and per shared/evidence-model.md's deterministic loop ladder, a framework that is practised as "resolve disagreements through expert judgement" (the exact phrase this vault's risk-assessment-ngra already recorded from European regulators on 2026-08-25) assigns to human-in-the-loop at step 3, never past it. Two different respondent populations — safety pharmacologists polled about a cardiovascular/respiratory/CNS guideline, and risk assessors polled about systemic cosmetics toxicology — independently converge on wanting the same kind of integration layer, and it is the least loopable kind there is. That is not a coincidence this vault should file as two separate facts.

What this does NOT do. It proposes no method, measures no chemical, and does not move this map's Status or loop_readiness — a survey of priorities is not a validated assay for the CNS or respiratory legs, and the cardiovascular leg's own candidate (cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia) is untouched by it. It is graded here for what it is: the clearest evidence yet of why three organ systems with a 25-year-old guideline and a February 2026 workshop still have, ten months later, zero adopted replacement for any leg.
