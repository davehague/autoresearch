The reference standard is noisy, and the framework says to account for it

The problem this solves. Almost every performance number in this vault is a NAM scored against an animal test — sensitivity and specificity versus the rabbit Draize test, versus the mouse local lymph node assay, versus the rat repeat-dose study. Read naively, a NAM that disagrees with the animal test 15% of the time looks 15% wrong. But the animal test disagrees with itself: OECD TG 492 records that the Draize eye test has about 12% within-test variability, which the vault noted on 2026-07-15 as "the vault's first hard number on reference-test noise." A NAM cannot be more consistent with a noisy reference than the reference is with itself.

ICCVAM states the principle generally:

"When the NAM performance is assessed based on the reference animal test method, the amount of acceptable variance in the NAM must be considered relative to the variance observed in the in vivo data and based on the intended COU."

Two moves in one sentence. First, the reference's own noise sets the achievable ceiling. Second, the context of use sets what counts as acceptable within it — a screening method and a method setting a regulatory point of departure do not owe the same precision.

Where this changes readings in the vault. Several numbers the vault has recorded as disappointing deserve a noise floor beside them:

  • TG 492's false-negative rate sits inside the Draize's 12% variability (already noted; now a general principle rather than a one-off observation).
  • TG 492B's 74.4% balanced accuracy against Draize data (tg492b-three-way-resolution-costs-a-quarter-of-its-accuracy) is measured against a reference whose own three-way category assignments will not fully reproduce on a re-run. How much of the 25.6% is the method and how much is the reference is not determinable from the guideline, and the vault should say so rather than attribute all of it to the assay.
  • The skin-sensitisation potency ITS is "LLNA-equivalent (54–75% vs the LLNA's 60%)" against human data — which is this principle applied by the OECD itself: the honest comparator was the animal test's own score, not 100%.

The uncomfortable corollary, which ICCVAM does not draw. If acceptable NAM variance is set relative to the animal test's variance, then the animal test's noise becomes a budget the NAM is allowed to spend. Two methods with identical error rates would be judged differently depending on how noisy their in-vivo comparator happens to be — and the noisier the animal test, the easier the NAM's target. That is defensible as fairness and indefensible as a safety standard, and nothing in the report distinguishes the two readings. The vault records the tension without resolving it.

The loop consequence. A closed loop comparing its own predictions to reference data has no access to the reference's variance unless somebody supplies it — reference databases ship point classifications, not distributions. A system computing its own accuracy against a curated reference set will systematically under-estimate its performance by the reference's noise, and, worse, will treat irreducible reference noise as residual model error and keep optimising against it. That is a well-known way to overfit, and here the label noise is not random — it is concentrated in exactly the borderline chemicals that matter.
