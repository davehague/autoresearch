The 2-out-of-3 rule was built for one chemical at a time

What the battery is, restated from scratch

Skin sensitisation is allergy: not the burn a chemical causes on first contact, but the immune system learning to react to it, so the next exposure produces a rash. The non-animal replacement for the mouse test is three assays, each reading one step of the documented biological chain:

AssayWhat it measuresInstrument
DPRA (Test Guideline 442C)does the chemical stick to protein? — measured as how much of a test peptide disappearsliquid chromatography
KeratinoSens (Test Guideline 442D)do skin cells switch on their oxidative-stress alarm? — a cell engineered to glow when that pathway firesluminescence
h-CLAT (Test Guideline 442E)do immune-precursor cells put out activation markers?flow cytometry

The rule is the point. OECD Guideline 497 says: if two of the three agree, that is the answer. No expert weighs anything. The guideline calls it "a transparent, rule-based approach requiring no expert judgment" — and that sentence is the basis of this map's closed-loop-ready rating and of six weeks of this vault holding it up as the clearest case of what a machine-runnable regulatory method looks like.

All of that remains true. What follows does not contradict a word of it.

The class of substance the rule was never built for

A botanical is a plant-derived ingredient — an extract, an essential oil, ground plant material. It is not a chemical. It is a variable mixture of hundreds of things whose composition shifts with growing conditions, cultivar, plant part and extraction method. The consortium's own example: bergamot oil sourced in two lots, one cold-pressed (containing the light-activated compounds that cause the classic sun-and-citrus burn) and one steam-distilled (which strips them out). Same name on the label, different hazard in the bottle.

Every one of the three assays hits a mixture-specific wall, and none of them is exotic:

  • DPRA is a stoichiometry problem before it is a biology problem. The method prescribes a 100 millimolar stock, and molarity requires a molecular weight. A botanical does not have one. A gravimetric workaround exists with "preliminary success", and the material must additionally dissolve fully and not precipitate when it meets the aqueous peptide mixture. On top of that, tannins and other plant phenolics bind peptides non-specifically — the assay's positive signal is peptide disappearance, so a non-sensitising plant phenol can produce one. And an extract constituent can leave the chromatography column at the same moment as the peptide being counted; a co-elution control exists for that.
  • KeratinoSens can be attacked at the readout rather than at the biology. Per Test Guideline 442D itself, phytoestrogens at high concentration interfere with the luciferase enzyme — the light-producing protein the whole measurement depends on. That is not the cell responding wrongly; that is the ruler being bent.
  • h-CLAT has a stated greasiness bound. Per Test Guideline 442E, substances above log Kow 3.5 may give false negatives. Log Kow measures how strongly a substance prefers oil to water. Essential oils are exactly that class.

⚠ And the rule itself has no representation of any of this

Here is the consortium's sentence, which is the finding:

"Because these approaches were primarily developed and validated using single chemicals, the extent to which concordant results across key events support hazard identification for complex botanical mixtures remains an important area of investigation."

The 2-out-of-3 rule converts agreement into an answer. It is a function over three binary inputs and nothing else. It cannot ask whether the two agreeing assays agreed for a reason connected to sensitisation, or because they share an interference. For a mixture, that question is live — and the vault's second note from this source, cytotoxic-masking-is-a-second-shared-failure-route-through-the-battery, shows it is not hypothetical.

The published data already runs both ways on one assay. Kolle et al. 2023 saw botanical extracts light up h-CLAT's CD54 marker where five were non-sensitisers in the animal test. Shiraishi et al. 2021 saw Brazilian green propolis come back negative in h-CLAT and a moderate sensitiser in the mouse assay. Over-calling and under-calling, same assay, same substance class.

The loop reading — precisely what moves and what does not

loop_readiness holds at closed-loop-ready. The readouts are still instrument-read. The rule is still published, fixed and deterministic. Nothing here introduces a human judgment step, and it would be wrong to demote the axis for a finding about what the rule was validated on.

domain_gate moves. This map's applicability-domain note has read "chemical applicability domain of GL 497" since it was coined. It now has to carry the specific thing a loop must check first: is the test item a single, defined substance? Because if it is an undefined mixture, the loop will execute perfectly, apply the rule perfectly, and return an answer whose meaning has not been established.

This is the cleanest example the vault has of why closed-loop-ready is not an endorsement. A machine could run this battery on a plant extract today. It has the instruments, it has the rule, and the rule will return a category. The rule does not know what it was validated on, and there is nothing in the procedure that would notice.

Bias watch, in an unusual direction

funding_flag: [industry]. Six authors work for personal-care or dietary-supplement companies; a contract research organisation that sells this testing is a participant; direct funding comes from an industry consortium's corporate sponsors. The commercial interest points toward "the existing non-animal tests work on our ingredients."

The paper argues the harder case instead, in a consortium that includes the US FDA and NIEHS under a formal Memorandum of Understanding, with NIEHS intramural support. That is worth saying plainly, and it does not retire the flag. Also worth saying plainly: no experiment has been run. Eleven reference botanicals have been selected; the literature behind the selection was gathered non-systematically and study quality was not formally assessed, both by the authors' own statement. This is a stated design question, not a measured failure — which is the earliest and most useful moment to record a domain gate, and the weakest kind of evidence for asserting one.
