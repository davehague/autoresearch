The adopted liver test's cell line looks more like a real liver without the cow serum

OECD Test Guideline 445A — adopted 2 July 2026, the test for whether a chemical ramps up the liver enzymes that chew up drugs — runs on HepaRG, an immortalised human liver cell line. It is consensus on the science and acceptance axes and closed-loop-ready on the third (metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg). It also carries an access_gate: HepaRG is patented and available from one licensor.

This note adds a fact about the cells inside it. Grown in a chemically defined medium instead of one supplemented with fetal bovine serum (cow blood serum, undefined composition, varies by lot), HepaRG cells turned up exactly the gene programmes the guideline cares about — xenobiotic metabolism, response to foreign chemicals, and transport. Scored against real pooled human liver cells on hepatic-identity and detoxification gene panels, defined-medium HepaRG sat closer to the real thing than serum-grown HepaRG.

The comparison line, HuH7, did something different under the same swap — sterol and fat biosynthesis, stress responses — and got "limited improvement." Same intervention, two liver lines, two unrelated answers.

What this is and is not

It is not a validation result and it does not touch the guideline. No cytochrome P450 activity was measured. No n-fold induction number was produced. TG 445A's actual readout — catalytic activity of CYP1A2, CYP2B6 and CYP3A4 — was not run in either medium. What was measured is messenger RNA levels, which is a step upstream of the enzyme activity the guideline scores. Gene expression rising is a reason to expect better metabolic competence, not a demonstration of it.

So: Status does not move, regulatory_status does not move, loop_readiness does not move. The guideline specifies its own culture conditions and nothing here supersedes them.

What it is is a directional signal on a question that matters for every liver assay in this vault: the cheapest, most obvious reagent-provenance fix — take out the cow serum — appears, for this cell line, to move the model toward human physiology rather than away from it. On 2026-07-20 the vault recorded the opposite worry, from a review: "Changes in baseline phenotype, sensitivity, or readouts, have been reported even when technical adaptation is successful." For HepaRG's metabolic programmes specifically, the change points the right way.

Why this belongs on the loop axis anyway

An access_gate is a barrier to standing the same loop up somewhere else. HepaRG's is a licence. But there is a second, quieter barrier that this result names: the culture medium is an undeclared part of the protocol. Two laboratories running TG 445A on licensed HepaRG in different serum lots are running the same guideline on measurably different cells — 2,272 genes' worth of difference between the serum and defined conditions alone.

A chemically defined medium makes that input machine-checkable: every component is a named molecule at a stated concentration, so a loop can assert its input contract instead of assuming it. That is the same convergence the vault noted on 2026-07-20 — the 3Rs argument for defined media and the automation argument for defined media are the same argument, and neither literature cites the other.

The brakes

  • Transcriptomic only. No enzyme activity, no induction fold, no guideline performance metric.
  • Neither line reached primary hepatocytes. "Closer" is not "equivalent." The cell-line-to-primary gap stayed roughly 12,600–14,100 genes wide.
  • Not a clean serum-removal test — the defined media were cell-line-optimised formulations, so the effect is serum removal plus formulation, and the authors say so.
  • Single laboratory, n = 3, no replication.
  • Relevant to but not evidence about hepatotoxicity-cholestasis-battery and hepatotoxicity-steatosis-battery, which also run on HepaRG. That those three all depend on one licensed line is a separate finding, recorded on both maps as a correlated blind spot.
