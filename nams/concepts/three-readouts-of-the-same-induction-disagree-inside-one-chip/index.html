The adopted guideline said the gene is not the enzyme. Somebody measured all three in the same well.

OECD Test Guideline 445A — adopted 2 July 2026, the internationally accepted standard test for whether a chemical makes the liver manufacture more of its drug-destroying enzymes — makes a design choice and defends it in one clause: it measures catalytic activity, not messenger RNA, because "measuring mRNA does not necessarily" track induced activity.

That is an assertion. The guideline does not say how far apart the two get, on which enzymes, or in which direction. metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg has carried it since 10 July as a stated rationale with no number behind it.

This paper measures the gene, the protein and the activity, for five enzymes, in the same chips, from the same cells, on the same drug — and reports they disagree in magnitude. For membrane transporters, the mRNA and protein patterns diverged often enough that the authors invoke regulation "beyond PXR-mediated activation" — i.e. the cell is controlling protein levels through machinery the standard mental model does not include.

Why this matters more to a loop than to a bench

mRNA is the readout automation wants. It is cheap, it is multiplexed, it comes off a plate reader or a sequencer without a substrate-turnover assay per enzyme, and it scales to hundreds of targets at once. Every transcriptomic method in this vault — the point-of-departure map, the genotoxicity biomarker, the steatosis battery's key events — is built on that convenience. Activity is the readout the adopted guideline requires, and it is the slow, per-enzyme, non-scalable one.

So the gap between them is not an academic curiosity. It is the exact place where the automatable measurement and the accepted measurement come apart, and it is a place where a loop's incentives point the wrong way: the cheap readout is the one an autonomous system will accumulate thousands of, and the expensive readout is the one a regulator will ask for.

This does not move the map's tier. TG 445A remains closed-loop-ready — instrument-read activity, a fixed four-way prediction model (positive at ≥2-fold at ≥2 concentrations, and so on). What it does is put a mechanism under an existing rule, and warn against the substitution somebody will eventually propose: run the guideline's decision rule on transcriptomic input because it is faster. On this evidence, that would be a different assay wearing the guideline's threshold.

The second thing, which the adopted guideline does not cover at all

TG 445A measures three cytochrome P450 enzymes. This chip also found induction of OATP2B1 and P-glycoprotein — membrane pumps that move drugs into and out of the cell. Induce those and you change the drug's fate without changing any enzyme in the guideline's panel. That is a coverage hole in the adopted method with a name, and the same shape as the vault's standing rail: a battery that reads three enzymes and no transporters casts one vote about metabolic perturbation, not two.

What holds this at single-lab-exploratory and should keep it there

  • One drug, and the easiest one. Rifampicin is the field's reference inducer precisely because everything detects it. The interesting case — a weak or ambiguous inducer near the 2-fold threshold — is untested.
  • funding_flag: [industry, method-developer]. Five of the ten authors work for Javelin Biotech, the company selling the chip. The vault declined to upgrade hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps on a developer-run blinded 27-drug panel; this is a developer-run single-drug study and gets less, not more.
  • No head-to-head against the guideline method. Same compound, HepaRG under TG 445A, side by side, would let anyone size the discrepancy in decision terms. It was not run.
  • No decision rule is proposed for the chip, and no discrepancy is expressed as a number in the retrievable text. "Discrepancies in induction magnitude" is a direction, not a measurement. Whether any of the three readouts would have crossed the guideline's 2-fold cut-off differently is exactly the question, and it is unanswered here.
