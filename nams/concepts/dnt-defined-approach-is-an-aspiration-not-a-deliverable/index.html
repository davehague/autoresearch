The vault's biggest decision-rule gap now has a name, an owner, and no date

Since 2026-07-06 developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb has carried the loop blocker "the battery is integrated by expert IATA judgement rather than a fixed rule," and vault/index.md has called it the highest-leverage decision-rule gap in the vault — 17 plate-based assays, the most automatable battery by volume, with only the interpretation layer missing.

That was the vault's reading of a guidance document. It is now the OECD's own statement about its own project.

The distinction, in the guideline body's words

"In contrast to the assessment process within Integrated Approaches to Testing and Assessment (IATA) that necessarily involves some degree of expert judgment, predictions generated with defined approaches are rule-based and can either be used on their own if they are deemed to be fit-for-purpose or considered together with other sources of information."

This is loop_readiness restated in OECD vocabulary. A Defined Approach is a fixed data-interpretation procedure — the thing that makes GL 497's skin-sensitisation battery closed-loop-ready while this battery, with more assays and more automation, is not. And the OECD places DNT on the expert-judgement side, with the rule-based side as an aspiration:

"The aspiration is to increase standardization and pave the way towards the development of Defined Approaches for DNT."

The scale of what is waiting on it

~650 chemicals. More than 200 at EFSA, about 200 at NIEHS's Division of Translational Toxicology, roughly 250 at the US EPA including ~160 PFAS. Harmonised concentration–response modelling. Centralised databases. And the disposition of every one of them:

"These applications did not treat DNT-IVB outcomes as stand-alone replacements for guideline studies, but rather as complementary evidence streams informing regulatory judgement."

The wet work is done, the data is stored, the analysis pipeline is harmonised, and not one of those 650 results can conclude anything by itself.

Why this is the vault's cleanest instance of its own thesis

Set it beside automation-tox21-qhts-platform. Tox21 automated the hands — 10,000 chemicals a week since 2013 — and its ambiguous curves are still "manually inspected." The vault's standing lesson from that was: automating the wet work did not automate the epistemology.

DNT is the same lesson with the automation pushed one step further. Tonight's companion source (dnt-scoring-is-already-a-neural-network-and-the-blocker-holds) shows that the battery's flagship assay has already automated the interpretation of the images — trained convolutional neural networks identify the cells, a skeletonisation algorithm measures the neurites, a scripted tool fits the curves. Six of seven readouts run end-to-end without a human.

And the blocker does not move a millimetre, because the blocker was never located at the measurement or the scoring. It sits at the point where a set of numbers has to become a regulatory sentence.

The honest read on "aspiration"

Two things are true at once and the vault should hold both.

This is genuinely a move. A named remedy in an official work plan at the body that would have to adopt it is more than the vault had yesterday, when the gap was simply a gap. It is also the cheapest class of fix in the whole vault — writing down a rule for data you already have costs nothing compared with developing an assay or running a ring trial.

And "aspiration" is the operative word. No date. No draft. No published candidate rule. The comparison that keeps this calibrated is metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg: ECVAM-validated in 2014, adopted as a Test Guideline in 2026. Twelve years, for a method that had already cleared the harder scientific bar. The vault has no basis for expecting a DNT Defined Approach on any particular timescale, and should not read "the OECD says it wants one" as "it is coming."
