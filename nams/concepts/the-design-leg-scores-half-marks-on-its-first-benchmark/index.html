The loop's design leg gets its first benchmark, and the score is about half marks

This vault has tracked a closed research loop as four legs: execution (a robot does the physical work), retrieval (finding what is already known), experiment selection (deciding what to run next), and decision (working out what a result means). Since 2026-07-31 the standing complaint has been that the thinking legs had architectures and no measurements. That has now been answered twice in three days: SCHEMA measured the decision leg on 2026-08-06, and SCOPE measures the design leg.

Design is not the same thing as selection, and the distinction is load-bearing here. The vault's existing experiment-selection sources — Bayesian optimal experimental design over a real dose-response assay (experiment-selection-leg-lands-on-a-real-dose-response-assay) and cost-aware pathway optimisation (experiment-selection-under-known-cost-is-the-loops-other-half) — all answer "given this assay, what is the next point to measure?" SCOPE asks the larger question: "given a claim, what is the whole study?" Which experiments. Which controls. Which comparisons. Which measurements. That is the question a laboratory director answers, and it is the one an autonomous lab would have to answer to be autonomous rather than merely automated.

The measurement

300 tasks built from 300 papers accepted at ICML, ICLR and NeurIPS across 19 research domains. The model is handed a paper's task description, related work and methodology — and not its experimental design — and must produce the complete plan. Six sub-dimensions, 0–5 each, total 0–30, judged against what the authors actually did.

Mean across seven frontier models: 14.81 out of 30. Claude 4.5 Sonnet best at 18.62, Grok-4 worst at 12.55. Two of seven clear 18.

Why the redline rule is the part to copy

A sub-dimension is zeroed outright, no matter how good the rest of it is, on three fatal flaws: inventing a dataset or baseline that does not exist, choosing a metric fundamentally misaligned with the task, or violating a stated constraint. The authors' justification is a scoring principle, not a scoring detail:

"preventing averaging from masking critical failures and safeguarding the credibility of evaluation results."

This vault has recorded the same structural complaint about toxicology's own aggregate numbers — a battery accuracy that averages over a chemical class the battery systematically cannot see, a specificity that improves because the applicability domain shrank. A redline is the evaluation-design answer to it: some errors are not allowed to be diluted. For an autonomous testing loop, "fabricated a reference chemical" and "chose an endpoint that does not measure the hazard" are exactly redline-shaped failures.

The honest scope limit

Nothing here happened in a laboratory. Every task comes from a computational-research paper, and "datasets, baselines, metrics" is the computational researcher's material list. The paper makes no claim about wet-lab or toxicological design, and neither does this note. It measures the leg; it does not measure it in this vault's domain — which is the same non-crossover automation-self-driving-labs has recorded since 2026-07-13.
