A formula, not an assay — and its own validation study already discloses which way it is wrong

What this method is, and why it needs its own map

The GHS mixtures equation is not a physical test: it is a published arithmetic formula from the Globally Harmonized System (GHS) of chemical classification that estimates a mixture's acute oral LD50 additively from the already-known LD50s and mass fractions of its individual ingredients — the toxicological equivalent of estimating a recipe's total calories by summing its ingredients' known calorie counts, rather than calorimetering the finished dish. No physical assay, no cell, no tissue, no animal is dosed to generate the prediction itself — only the historical ingredient-level data it draws on required animal testing, in the past, once per ingredient.

This is a distinct method from acute-systemic-toxicity-oral-dt (the AFSA Collaboration's proposed weight-of-evidence decision tree), which this vault already holds for the same endpoint: different mechanism (a fixed additive formula vs. an integrated multi-tier strategy), different provenance (an EPA/NIEHS pilot with real validation data vs. an unvalidated industry-advocacy proposal), and a different loop-readiness profile (below). What we believe now

Hamm et al. 2021 (Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology, PMID 34298086, PMC9623877; EPA/NIEHS/NICEATM contract via ILS Inc.) retrospectively tested the equation against real animal LD50 data for 671 formulations — 620 agrochemical products (BASF, Dow, DuPont, Syngenta, Control Solutions) and 51 antimicrobial cleaning products (Clorox, Ecolab, Procter & Gamble). Overall concordance with the animal-derived EPA toxicity category was 55% (72% under the coarser GHS scheme), with a one-directional error: of 245 misclassified formulations, 163 (66%) were placed in a less toxic category than the animal study assigned — the equation systematically underestimates risk more often than it overestimates it. Restricting evaluation to formulations already expected to be low-toxicity (LD50 > 500 mg/kg) raised concordance to 82%. Only 4 of 671 formulations fell in the most-toxic category, so the equation's behavior on genuinely dangerous mixtures is barely tested.

On 27 August 2026, EPA formally adopted the equation for regulatory use — scoped exactly to the low-toxicity band where the known bias matters least, not as a general-purpose replacement for the animal acute oral study. That is the methodologically defensible response to a documented, characterized, one-directional error: deploy inside the region the validation study says works, and require the animal study everywhere else.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: computational-connective — first-match on the deterministic ladder: this is a pure model, no physical assay exists to grade. It is exactly the kind of "model layer" object this vault scores off the loop ladder rather than on it, alongside (Q)SAR/read-across and PBK/QIVIVE.

The interesting fact for a loop, and it is a cautionary one. The equation itself is a fixed, deterministic formula — nothing about applying it requires human judgement. A naive automated pipeline could run it on every formulation submitted, at any toxicity level, with no code change required to do so — the formula does not know it is only validated for low-toxicity mixtures. The domain restriction that makes this method safe to deploy lives entirely in the regulatory scoping decision (EPA's choice to require it only for formulations "expected to be minimally toxic"), not in the formula's own math or in any published applicability-domain check the formula enforces on itself. domain_gate: low-toxicity mixtures only (LD50 > 500 mg/kg expectation) — a human-set eligibility gate outside the formula, not a property the formula can check about itself. A loop trusting "the formula ran and returned a number" without separately verifying eligibility inherits exactly the underestimation bias this method's own validation study measured, on precisely the formulations (moderate toxicity) where that bias is largest.

Bias watch

funding_flag: [industry] — the 671-formulation validation dataset was supplied by the companies whose products the exemption benefits (BASF, Dow, DuPont, Syngenta, Control Solutions, Clorox, Ecolab, Procter & Gamble), though the statistical analysis was run independently by NIEHS/NICEATM (US government scientists) under an EPA Office of Pesticide Programs contract — a materially different, more independent arrangement than a manufacturer self-validating its own product, but not a fully independent dataset either, since the underlying ingredient LD50 values and formulation compositions all originate with the companies being regulated.
