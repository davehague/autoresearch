A second domain, the same ceiling: nanomaterial "wisdom of crowds" loses to descriptors alone

nanomaterial-nam-landscape named four technology classes with no dedicated performance figure yet: organoid/MPS platforms, label-free imaging, nano-QSAR, and AutoML/generative-AI dosimetry. saarimaki-2025-wisdom-of-crowds-nanomaterial-safety (Saarimäki et al. 2025, Environmental Science & Technology, PMC12312164; a large EU public consortium — NanoSolveIT/INSIGHT/PINK Horizon projects, funding_flag: none) closes part of the nano-QSAR gap directly, with a performance figure.

What it does

Expert-elicited "concern scores" aggregate 21 responding specialists' ratings of 134 ENMs across 18 endpoints into a Bayesian-hierarchical consensus, weighted by estimated expert reliability and per-endpoint difficulty — a statistical answer to ENM data scarcity, not a new assay. The consensus scores "align" with 2,896 real experimental cell-viability assays (no single concordance statistic given in the text read), and then become training labels — alongside ENM physicochemical descriptors and gene-expression profiles — for machine-learning toxicity classifiers.

The finding that connects to a different domain entirely

The descriptor-only classifier reached the highest ROC-AUC for every single endpoint, ahead of the full integrated model that also consumed gene expression and the expert-consensus scores. This is the identical shape the-robot-half-added-one-point-of-auc-over-drawing-the-molecule already found for Tox21's 49-target robotic assay panel against drug-induced liver injury and cardiotoxicity: a cheap, structure/descriptor-only model matching or beating the model built on expensive wet or crowd-sourced inputs. Two independent domains — small-molecule high-throughput screening and engineered-nanomaterial expert elicitation — now show the same pattern, which strengthens the general reading automation-tox21-qhts-platform already carries: the hard-won biological or expert signal buys less predictive power than its cost would suggest, at least by the metric (ROC-AUC) both papers use.

The honest limit

This is an aggregation method, not an assay — it generates training labels and fills gaps; it does not run a wet-lab test. The paper itself names the applicability-domain problem for any ENM model: "given the highly heterogeneous nature of ENMs, it remains difficult to characterize a fixed applicability domain." It also flags an unavoidable circularity: ENM descriptors "are heavily influenced by the primary features that were also reported to the experts," so expert ratings and descriptor-based predictions are not fully independent evidence — "complete blinding would be impossible." No OECD or regulatory body has adopted or evaluated this approach; regulatory_status: none.
