The second species was mostly measuring experimental noise

The two-species requirement in European pesticide law rests on a proposition: a rodent and a non-rodent disagree in ways that reveal human-relevant toxicity, so requiring both buys you information. EFSA tested that proposition against 461 approved substances and found it largely false.

In 97% of consumer-limit cases and 95% of operator-limit cases, the dog's apparent extra sensitivity dissolved on inspection. Most of it — 83 of the consumer cases and all 183 of the operator cases — fell inside a NOAEL ratio of 3, which is the width of ordinary experimental and biological scatter. Most of the remainder was dose spacing (the study happened not to test a dose between the two numbers), study length, or body-size arithmetic that had not been done.

Why this matters beyond dogs

The vault's standing problem with every non-animal method is that its performance is scored against an animal test whose own variability is rarely quantified. That framing has appeared repeatedly — a reconstructed human cornea model's 74.4% accuracy measured against rabbit data carrying ~12% within-test variability, with the split between method error and reference noise undeterminable; OECD guidance conceding that regulatory demand for sensitivity caps achievable specificity at 60–80%; a European validation laboratory having to manufacture a reference chemical list by expert committee because the animal test could not supply one.

This source measures the noise floor directly, on the regulatory record, at scale, and it extends nam-variance-judged-against-animal-test-variance — the framework principle that acceptable non-animal-method variance should be judged relative to the animal test's own variance — from a stated principle into a measured number. A factor-of-three disagreement between two whole-animal studies of the same chemical is expected. Any non-animal method being scored against a single animal study is being scored against a number with that much slop in it — and, crucially, the animal test is never asked to reproduce itself before being used as the answer key.

EFSA's own sentence is the one to keep: "selecting two species for toxicity testing does not necessarily reduce uncertainty regarding potential human health risks." Two measurements of the same uncertain thing are not two votes. That is this vault's correlated-blind-spot rail, arriving from the animal side of the comparison rather than the in-vitro side, and stated by a regulator about its own data requirement.

What this does not say

It does not say the dog study is worthless — a residual 3% and 5% of cases were genuine dog-specific sensitivity, and the strategy built on this analysis keeps a route for exactly those. It does not say a non-animal method could replace either species; the substitute proposed for the dog is the rat. And the denominator is substances that were approved, so any candidate a dog study killed outright is invisible here by construction.
