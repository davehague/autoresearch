The machine put the embryos in four boxes and the boxes turned out to mean nothing

This is the finding, and it is worth stating before any hedge: an AI classifier assigned every embryo to a named biological category, and the categories did not predict the biology they were named after.

The machine's labelSurvivedHatched
fertilized83.3%98.9%
intermediate84.4%87.6%
unfertilized79.2%96.1%
unclassified80.8%100%

All differences non-significant. An unfertilised egg cannot hatch, and 96.1% of the ones called unfertilised hatched. The label was not a weak predictor of fate; it was, on this evidence, unrelated to it.

The same device, asked to do a task with an objectively checkable answer — is this embryo glowing green or not — returned a 50.8% false-negative rate. It threw away half the transgenic embryos it was told to find.

Why this is filed as pushes-back, and against what

On 2026-07-15 the vault recorded zebrafish-machine-vision-malformation-scoring: a Vision Transformer trained on time-lapse zebrafish microscopy that detects developmental malformations at 92% accuracy and classifies fertility at 98%. That was logged as the first concrete evidence that the eye-scoring bottleneck in developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity is learnable — an escape hatch, demonstrated but not walked through.

This does not refute that. It prices it, and the price is higher than the benchmark implies:

  • 98% fertility accuracy on a curated research benchmark; a classifier whose fertility label is uncorrelated with hatching in a working facility. Same organism, same nominal task, two very different numbers. The distance between them is the distance between a held-out test set and a Tuesday.
  • The research system was trained and evaluated by the group that built it; this one was purchased and evaluated by the buyer. The vault's bias-watch rail predicts exactly this gap and this is a clean instance of it — the automation-hype variant, measured.
  • Neither number is the number that matters. The regulatory readout is not "abnormal / normal"; it is graded malformation typing — which malformation, how severe — feeding a teratogenic index. Both the 92% benchmark and this device answer a coarser question than the assay asks.

The correct reading is not "machine vision fails." It is that a computer-vision accuracy figure is a claim about a dataset, and loop_readiness is a claim about a laboratory, and this is the first source in the vault where the two were measured on the same organism and disagreed.

The generalisable form, which is the part worth keeping

A classifier can be trained to high accuracy against labels a human assigned by looking, and still carry no information about the outcome the label is a proxy for. Human graders at 3-4 hours post-fertilisation are themselves unreliable — the paper says so, which is why the technicians in the comparison were only counting. So the machine may well have learned the human's grading faithfully. It learned a proxy. The proxy did not predict survival.

For a closed loop this is the worst available failure mode, and it is the same structural error the vault recorded on 2026-08-14 as learning the assay, not the hazard: the system is confident, internally consistent, fast, and optimising against a quantity that is not the quantity of interest. Nothing inside the loop can detect it, because the loop's ground truth is the label. It took raising 117,956 embryos to 96 hours and counting who hatched — an experiment outside the loop entirely — to see it.

And this is the check nobody runs. The vault holds thirty methods graded automatable-open-loop and twenty-six graded closed-loop-ready. For almost none of them has anybody published the analogous measurement: does the automated call predict the biological outcome, as opposed to agreeing with the human who wrote the training labels.
