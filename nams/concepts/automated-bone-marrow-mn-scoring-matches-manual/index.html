Specimen-prep automation for a micronucleus assay — but the animal version, not the NAM

Fits this map's established theme exactly: getting the right biological material, correctly enriched and correctly counted, into an imaging instrument before any scoring happens. Here the material is bone-marrow erythrocytes from exposed rodents, and the enrichment problem is removing non-target nucleated cells before the Metafer slide-scanner counts micronucleated cells — the same class of problem this map already tracks for fish embryo (TG 236), zebrafish teratogenicity, and zebrafish behavioural DNT specimen handling.

Why it does not become evidence for genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus. That map's standing question is whether a machine-vision scorer can replace a human's eye on the in vitro (cultured human or mammalian cell) assay. This source validates automated scoring on the in vivo (rodent bone-marrow) counterpart — a different test guideline (TG 474, not TG 487), different cells, different sample-prep chemistry (erythrocyte enrichment vs. cultured-cell harvest), and an animal test being made faster and more objective rather than a non-animal method being validated. It is the same underlying imaging platform (Metafer) one of the in vitro map's own machine-vision candidates could plausibly also use, which is worth naming as a technology-transfer observation — see back-2026-in-vivo-micronucleus-automated-imaging — but this study tests neither the in vitro assay's cell-culture preparation nor any chemical panel the in vitro map tracks, so nothing here is treated as evidence for that map's loop_readiness, access_gate, or loop_blocker. Flagging the adjacency without inferring across it is the discipline this vault's cross-intervention checks exist to enforce.
