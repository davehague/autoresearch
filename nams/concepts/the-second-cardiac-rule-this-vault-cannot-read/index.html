The rule is the product, so the rule is not published — twice now, on the same endpoint

cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia carries an access_gate that the vault wrote on 2026-07-26 and has not had reason to revise:

Both halves are proprietary and held by the same company. The cells are NEXEL's commercial Cardiosight®-S product; the classifier is NEXEL's unreleased model. The vendor of the test system also owns the rule that interprets it.

That map's loop_blocker was recorded as a new shape: not a missing decision rule, an unpublished one. A working, fully mechanical neural-network classifier — no human scores anything, ROC-AUC 0.982 — whose weights, data and code are "available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request." Every other rule at the vault's top loop tier is readable in a public document. That one is a service one laboratory can perform.

Tonight the same shape arrives on the computational side of the same endpoint, from an unrelated company. Cardiosim-Tox is served from metaheart.kr/login; the corresponding author is employed by Meta Heart Co., Ltd. And the competing-interest statement is internally contradictory in a way worth quoting rather than paraphrasing: it declares "K.M.L. and A.L.F is employed by Meta Heart Inc." and then, in the same paragraph, that "the research was conducted in the absence of any commercial or financial relationships that could be construed as a potential conflict of interest." Both sentences cannot be true. This vault records the declaration as made and grades the source by what is verifiable: developers evaluating their own product, one of whom sells access to it. shared/evidence-model.md's bias watch applies without modification.

What two instances make

One vendor holding a rule is a business decision. Two unrelated vendors holding two rules on the same endpoint, by two different mechanisms, is a pattern in how cardiac safety prediction is being commercialised — and the mechanism is legible. Cardiac safety is the highest-value early screen in pharmaceutical development, the field agreed a shared reference set (the CiPA initiative's 28 drugs) that makes models comparable, and the remaining scarce good is the interpretation. So the interpretation becomes the product, and a product is not a publication.

This is not an accusation of bad science. Both models may be excellent, and this note disputes no number. It is an observation about what a closed loop can be built out of.

Why it belongs on the loop axis rather than the other two

access_gate is a modifier this vault carries precisely because it shows up in neither evidence_tier nor regulatory_status while deciding entirely whether anyone else can stand the loop up somewhere else. The vault's existing instances are physical — the VM7Luc4E2 cell line unobtainable from its source, HepaRG single-licensor, Corrositex the sole validated kit, Derek Nexus license-gated. These two are informational, and they gate something the physical ones do not: the ability to check.

Three specific consequences for a loop, none hypothetical:

  1. It cannot be version-pinned. The vault's standing determinism worry — recorded three separate ways since 2026-07-19 — is that a rule can be fixed without being reproducible. A model behind a web form is fixed only from the outside, and only until the weights are retrained. Nobody outside would observe the change.
  2. It cannot be audited against the applicability domain. The paper's best feature is its similarity-stratified validation, showing the model degrading on unfamiliar chemistry. A loop consuming the service gets a prediction, not the stratum it came from.
  3. It compounds with FDA's own framing. regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility holds the model-risk matrix: the more a model's output drives a decision without other evidence, the heavier the credibility burden. A model whose weights and training data are unavailable is the hardest possible case to discharge that burden for, and both cardiac rules in this vault are in it.

Nothing demotes. No Status or tier moves on cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia. This note extends a-decision-rule-that-exists-but-is-not-published from one instance to a pattern, and it records the distinction that instance made necessary: a working rule existing is not the same as a rule being available.
