The first AI drug-development tool to finish the qualification process, not just enter it

What changed

Every AI-driven Drug Development Tool this vault has tracked so far (regulatory-acceptance-fda-istand-ddt-qualification's Absentia Digital Liver Model, the liver organ-chip platform) is at the Letter-of-Intent stage — stage one of three, no public performance data. AIM-MASH AI Assist is the first instance of the opposite state: full qualification, completed, on the back of the largest validation dataset (1,400+ biopsies) any AI regulatory-acceptance instance in this vault currently carries, and with dual regulatory acceptance — EMA qualified the same tool earlier in 2025, ahead of this FDA action.

regulatory_status is recorded here as OECD-TG-adopted by structural analogy, not literal fact — there is no OECD Test Guideline involved; the field is used to mark "the strongest rung reached," and full DDT qualification (reusable by any sponsor without re-justification) is this vault's structural equivalent of a Test Guideline for a computational-connective or AI-scored tool. Flagged here rather than silently mapped, because it is the kind of axis-label stretch the vault's own rails warn against doing quietly.

Two DDT pathways, two different speeds, same regulatory thesis

FDA runs more than one Drug Development Tool qualification track. ISTAND is for novel methodologies with no existing home; the Biomarker Qualification Program is FDA's established track for imaging/biomarker tools. This concept and regulatory-acceptance-fda-istand-ddt-qualification are not duplicates — they are two procedurally distinct doors into the same regulatory thesis this vault was built to track (FDA now treats AI models as a NAM category), caught at opposite ends of their respective timelines: one just opened, one just closed.

Composes with, does not duplicate

regulatory-acceptance-fda-istand-ddt-qualification — same regulatory-acceptance thesis, different DDT program, different maturity stage. regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility — the FDA/EMA Guiding Principles framework this instance is a concrete completed case of: the pathologist accept/reject step is a textbook instance of the principle "human-centric by design" and of assessing "the complete system including human–AI interactions," not the model output alone.

What this is not

Not an animal-replacement instance. MASH biopsy scoring assesses a human disease's severity in a human clinical trial — it does not retire an animal toxicology study. Recorded in the regulatory-acceptance group because the mechanism (an AI tool becoming reusable regulatory evidence) is the vault's subject, not because this specific tool touches the vault's usual animal-replacement scope. Not evidence the underlying scoring algorithm is loop-ready without a human: the qualified Context of Use keeps a pathologist's accept/reject decision load-bearing on every score.
