The rail has a name, a citation, and an OECD guidance document

What the vault believed. Since 2026-07-11 the vault has carried a rail of its own devising: two assays in one battery that fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason cast one vote, not two — described in CLAUDE.md as "the vault's sharpest contribution to the autonomous-lab problem." Eight rows have accumulated, each one derived by walking two method maps and asking what they share.

What GD 263 says, verbatim:

"Finally, when using two or more test methods (see paragraphs 24 and 25), the conditional independence of these test methods should be considered (Adriaens et al. 2017a; Hoffman et al., 2008). This can help to decide which test methods to be included in the Top-Down / Bottom-Up approaches and optimise the overall performance of the approach chosen."

So the property is not a vault invention. It is conditional independence — the standard statistical term for what the vault has been calling a correlated blind spot — it has a literature going back to at least 2008, and it sits inside an adopted OECD guidance document as an instruction to assessors composing a battery. The vault should stop describing the rail as original. It is not.

What survives, and it is sharper than the original claim. Three things:

(1) The instruction is a sentence; there is no procedure. GD 263 says conditional independence "should be considered." It does not say how to measure it, what threshold makes two methods dependent enough to stop stacking them, or what to do when they are. It cites two papers and moves on. Nothing in the nine method modules that follow records, for any pair, whether they are conditionally independent — and the document contains a maximally-dependent pair whose relationship it states as a cross-reference (tg492-and-tg492b-are-one-measurement-read-twice).

(2) Every regulatory proposal the vault has caught building a correlated battery had this sentence available. The UK's national strategy proposes replacing the in-vivo fish test with the FET + RTgill-W1 + QSAR stack — two assays that both lack a nervous system and full metabolism — and does not mention their shared gap (regulatory-acceptance-uk-animal-replacement-strategy). The EU's REACH revision proposes the same pair. The EU-NETVAL thyroid battery carries two methods measuring transthyretin displacement by the same probe-competition principle (endocrine-thyroid-disruption-nams). The concept was documented in OECD guidance before those proposals were written. The gap is not conceptual; it is that nobody runs the check.

(3) It relocates the vault's contribution. The rail's value was never the observation that batteries can be correlated — a 2008 paper had that. It is (a) the cross-endpoint audit: actually walking every pair in a corpus and recording which ones share a failure mode, which nobody does because it is tedious and spans documents that don't cite each other; and (b) the loop argument, which the statistical literature has no reason to make: a human reading a concordant battery may discount it, while an automated loop reads concordance as confirmation, trusts the rule, and iterates — converting a known bias into a compounding one. Neither Adriaens 2017 nor Hoffman 2008 nor GD 263 says anything about automation.

One more thing GD 263 gets right that the vault derived independently. The remedy. GD 263: "the use of additional in vitro test methods suitable for identifying UN GHS Cat. 1 based, if possible, on different mechanism of actions, may be considered in case a negative result is obtained." That is the Tox21 counter-design — the fix for a correlated blind spot is a measurement that fails differently, not a bigger battery — which the vault derived from a 2013 robotics paper on 2026-07-13. It was already in eye guidance.

Correction recorded, and the rail keeps its rung. CLAUDE.md and the loop-readiness view describe the rail as the vault's own contribution. That framing is now wrong and should be softened: the rail is the vault's application of a documented principle, plus an argument about automation that the principle's literature does not make. The discipline of flagging it every time stands, unchanged.
