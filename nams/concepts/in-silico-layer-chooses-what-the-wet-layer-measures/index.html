A model picking the experiment, in a toxicology paper that has never heard of self-driving labs

Since 2026-07-12 this vault has been looking for one thing in particular: the crossover. Self-driving-lab work — a robot runs the assay, a model picks what to run next, the model updates — lives almost entirely in chemistry and materials science. Regulatory toxicology lives somewhere else and the two literatures barely cite each other. On 2026-07-30 the vault logged that the autonomous-lab half of the sweep had come back empty and that this was worth recording rather than glossing.

This note records a small instance of the crossover appearing from the other direction — not a self-driving-lab paper reaching into toxicology, but a toxicology method that has independently grown the front half of a loop without using any of the vocabulary.

What the structure actually is

The steatosis method has two layers, and the order matters:

  1. In silico. (Q)SAR, read-across and molecular docking predict which ligand-activated transcription factors — the master switches controlling fat handling in liver cells — a given chemical is likely to perturb. These are the molecular initiating events: the top of the causal chain.
  2. In vitro. HepaRG liver cells are then measured on the key events downstream of those switches: lipid accumulation, fatty-acid uptake, fat burning, lipid secretion, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic-reticulum stress, oxidative stress.

The model's prediction determines which wet measurements are worth making. That edge — model selects experiment — is the first half of what a closed loop is.

Why it is graded as a weak instance, explicitly

A closed loop has two edges. This has one.

Loop componentPresent?
Model selects the experimentyes
Robot executes itnot claimed; the assays are plate-based and could be
Experiment result updates the modelno
Second iterationno — nine chemicals, one pass
Adaptivitynone — the tiering is a fixed workflow, not a policy

Nothing here learns. The in-silico layer makes the same prediction on the second chemical that it would have made before seeing the first chemical's wet result. That is a pipeline, not a loop, and the distinction is the whole of what tonight's other loop-layer source (ray-srinivas-2026-coactive-cost-aware-pathway-bo) is about — a policy that decides what to buy next given what it has already learned, with prices attached.

The paper makes no automation claim at all. It does not describe itself as a loop, does not cite self-driving-lab work, and does not mention AI. Everything in this note above the brakes is the vault's reading of a structure the authors built for mechanistic reasons — the adverse outcome pathway told them what to measure — and not a claim they made. That is flagged rather than assumed, the same way the reagent-provenance loop inference was flagged on 2026-07-20.

Why record it anyway

Because the missing edge is a smaller gap than it looks, and knowing where the field is starting from changes what "closing the loop on a regulatory assay" would take. The hard parts of a closed loop in toxicology have generally been assumed to be robotics and interpretation. This shows the selection half already exists in a published method — it just runs once. Adding the feedback edge to a fixed tiered workflow is an engineering change. Adding a decision rule that turns the steatogenic score into a hazard call is not: that requires reference chemicals, a threshold and a published performance figure, and it is the same blocker sitting on nineteen other maps in this vault.

So the front half of the loop is nearer than the vault assumed and the back half is exactly as far away as it was. That is the useful shape of tonight's finding, and it is an open question rather than a result — graded stance: open-question, strength: single-study, on one paper.

The brakes

  • This is architecture, not performance. No metric attaches to it and none is claimed.
  • The vault's inference, not the authors'. Flagged above.
  • Nine chemicals, one laboratory, developers evaluating their own method, with a commercial QSAR firm supplying the in-silico layer. funding_flag: [method-developer, industry].
  • A model picking experiments is not a good thing by default. If the in-silico layer's predictions are systematically wrong for a chemical class, it will systematically decline to measure the key events that would have caught it — and unlike a battery that runs everything, a pipeline that selects will produce no evidence of what it skipped. Selection is a blind-spot generator. Recorded here because loop-move in this vault never means improvement.

Rebutted by: the-applicability-domain-check-cannot-see-an-activity-cliff — not a contradiction but a hard scope condition on this note's implicit reassurance: the in-silico layer that chooses what the wet layer measures is normally licensed by an applicability-domain check, and that check passed 235 of 235 chemicals in a 2026 benchmark including the ones the models got wrong. The choosing layer's guard is silent exactly where its choices are most wrong.
