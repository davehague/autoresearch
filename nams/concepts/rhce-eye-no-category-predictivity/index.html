RhCE "No Category" eye test (OECD TG 492) → eye irritation / serious eye damage

A chemical is applied to a reconstructed human cornea-like epithelium and viability is read by tetrazolium (MTT for the two reference methods, WST-8 or WST-1 for the me-toos) against a negative control. The output is one bit: mean tissue viability > 60% → UN GHS No Category; ≤ 60% → not a classification, but a referral onward to another in-vitro method "or, as a last option, in rabbits."

Both axes adopted: evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 492, adopted 14 June 2019, corrected 25 June 2025). Four validated test systems now sit in the guideline — EpiOcular™ EIT (VRM1), SkinEthic™ HCE EIT (VRM2), LabCyte CORNEA-MODEL24 EIT, MCTT HCE™ EIT — all of them proprietary reconstructed-tissue products, hence funding_flag: method-developer (a test-system dependency, not sponsored science; the validations were independently peer-reviewed).

The specificity looks bad and it does not matter — which is a design decision, not an excuse. Sensitivity runs 95–99%; specificity 63–72%, i.e. up to a 37% false-positive rate. The guideline's own position is that these FP rates "are not critical in the context of this Test Guideline," and it is right for a structural reason: a positive is not a call. Only the negative is a regulatory decision ("a negative result should be accepted for not classifying a chemical"). Everything at or below the cutoff simply costs more testing. The assay is deliberately asymmetric — permitted to be wrong in the expensive direction, never in the dangerous one. That is what "identifying chemicals not requiring classification" means in the title, and reading the specificity figure as a weakness is reading the wrong assay.

The false-negative side is where the vault should look, and the guideline hands over the number. FN rates "are within the in vivo Draize eye test within-test variability of 12%." The reference standard against which this method is graded is itself 12% irreproducible. This is the sharpest expression the vault has ingested of a theme it keeps circling — from the h-CLAT's 66% specificity to the ITS's LLNA-equivalence — that a NAM's apparent inaccuracy is partly the animal test's noise, now measured rather than asserted. When a method's error rate sits inside the reference test's own variance, "as good as the animal test" has stopped being a coherent target.

Scope, hard. TG 492 "cannot be used on [its] own to replace the in vivo rabbit eye test." It does not separate Cat 1 (serious, irreversible damage) from Cat 2 (reversible irritation), nor 2A from 2B. It is the initial step of a Bottom-Up approach (for chemicals expected not to need classification) or a late step in a Top-Down approach; TG 437 (BCOP), TG 438 (ICE), TG 460 and TG 491 fill the other rungs. Applicable to substances and mixtures — solids, liquids, semi-solids, waxes.

Where it sits in the vault. This is the first component method mapped under the eye defined approach (eye-irritation-serious-eye-damage-da, TG 467), and the two make different kinds of claim: the DA returns a full three-way GHS call (Cat 1 / Cat 2 / No Cat) from a fixed data-interpretation procedure; TG 492 returns the single bit above. The relationship is exactly the one the vault already maps in skin sensitisation, where the 2-out-of-3 DA (skin-sensitization-2o3-defined-approach) sits above its three adopted key-event assays. The eye column now has the same shape: a hub, and a component that composes into it.
