The escape hatch the vault was watching may be the harder one

What the vault has been tracking

genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus is described in this vault as the sharpest axis disagreement it holds: an adopted OECD Test Guideline (TG 487), internationally accepted, that is nonetheless human-in-the-loop because the readout is "micronuclei counted by a trained eye down a microscope."

The vault's watch item has been machine vision — a computer looking at the same microscope images and counting the same objects. It has found four demonstrations across four maps, and rates this assay's the strongest: Wills et al. 2021, imaging flow cytometry plus a deep-learning classifier, trained on two laboratories, scoring an unseen third without reconfiguration, and reproducing the human benchmark dose — the number the safety call actually turns on.

What this source did instead

Nothing so elaborate. Allemang & Pfuhler measured micronucleus induction with a commercial flow-cytometry kit:

"MN induction was measured flow cytometrically in both cell types using the Litron (Rochester, NY) in vitro MicroFlow method" — with ">5000 healthy nuclei collected per sample."

The difference matters and is easy to miss. Imaging flow cytometry — Wills's instrument — photographs each cell as it flows past and needs a classifier to interpret the photograph. Plain flow cytometry takes no picture at all. Cells are stained so that DNA fluoresces; each particle passes a laser and the instrument records how much light it emits and scatters. A micronucleus is a small DNA-containing body with a characteristic signal, distinguishable by thresholds on those channels. There is nothing to classify because there is no image.

Five thousand nuclei per sample. A trained human scorer counts a few thousand cells across a whole experiment, slowly, and disagrees with the next scorer.

Why the tier does not move — and this is the disciplined part

The readout is not the whole blocker. OECD TG 487's call is not "count the micronuclei." It is a statistical evaluation of the counts against concurrent controls and historical control ranges, plus a judgement about biological relevance and about whether cytotoxicity has crossed the level at which increases are considered uninformative. Counting is the input to that. Automating the input does not publish the rule.

And this source establishes nothing about the method's standing. Two authors used a commercial kit because it was convenient and high-throughput. They make no claim about its regulatory acceptability, and the vault did not verify what OECD TG 487 says about flow-cytometric scoring in this run. That is an open gap, and it is a live one, because if the guideline already admits an instrument readout then the vault's four-map "machine vision is the escape hatch" narrative has been aimed at a door that is partly open.

loop_readiness holds at human-in-the-loop. The blocker's reading changes for the second time — first on 2026-07-16 ("counted by eye" became a demonstrated-solvable problem rather than an architectural fact), now again: the solving may not require a classifier at all.

The generalisable lesson

The vault picked machine vision as the escape hatch because machine vision is what the papers about automating scoring are about — and papers about automating scoring are written by people building classifiers. Meanwhile the routine industrial practice had substituted a different instrument years earlier and published nothing framed as automation, because to them it was just how you run the assay.

A sweep that reads the automation literature will systematically over-find machine learning and under-find instrumentation. That is a sampling bias in the vault's own method, not in the field. It sits alongside the existing note that the vault's two micronucleus scorers share a co-author and are "closer to one demonstration reported twice."

And a related brake on the enthusiasm: the European Commission's Joint Research Centre — the unit hosting the EU's method-validation reference laboratory — has stated that "toxicologists will maintain responsibility for final interpretation and decision-making." Whether the counting is done by an eye, a classifier or a laser, that sentence is unaffected. It is an accountability constraint, not a capability one.
