Mammalian cell gene mutation (TG 476 / TG 490) → genotoxicity (gene mutation)

The in-vitro mammalian cell gene mutation tests expose a known number of mammalian cells to a chemical (± S9 metabolic activation), let induced mutations fix and express, then apply a selective agent so only mutant cells form colonies — counted as mutant frequency. TG 476 uses the Hprt (X-linked) or xprt (bacterial gpt transgene in AS52) locus with 6-thioguanine selection and detects gene mutations — base substitutions, frameshifts, small indels — but not large chromosomal deletions. TG 490 uses the autosomal-heterozygous thymidine kinase (Tk) locus in L5178Y tk+/− (mouse lymphoma, MLA) or TK6 tk+/− cells with trifluorothymidine selection; because the Tk locus tolerates larger lesions, colony sizing (small vs large colonies) lets it detect both gene mutations and chromosomal events — a wider spectrum than TG 476.

Both axes adopted: evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (historical single 1984 guideline split 28 July 2015 into TG 476 + TG 490). funding_flag: none — adopted guidelines, not commercial products. The field's standard caveat holds: no single genotox assay stands alone — these are read within a battery.

Relative to the vault, this completes the mammalian gene-mutation arm of the in-vitro genotox battery: it composes-with the bacterial gene-mutation Ames test genotoxicity-ames-bacterial-reverse-mutation (mammalian vs bacterial gene mutation — TG 476/490 catch mammalian-specific events Ames can miss) and the chromosome-damage micronucleus test genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus (TG 490's Tk locus overlaps into chromosomal damage, bridging the two endpoints). The mechanistic MoA layer genotoxicity-toxtracker-reporter-moa (TG 446A) sits above all of them to adjudicate equivocal calls.
