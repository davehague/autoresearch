Validating against the mechanism instead of against the animal

The argument. Traditional validation is "linear, stepwise, and can be a long and resource-intensive process" — one big interlaboratory ring trial producing one permanent verdict. That worked when a test method was a fixed protocol with a fixed readout. It does not work for a battery of assays, an integrated testing strategy, a machine-learning model that retrains, or an organoid whose donor cells vary. The framework replaces the single event with five pillars a method must clear continuously:

  1. Technical reliability — reproducibility targets fixed in advance (illustrative: ICC ≥ 0.75, CV ≤ 20% across donors and labs), not discovered afterwards.
  2. Biological relevance / mechanistic anchoring — the method must map onto a documented Adverse Outcome Pathway, the causal chain from a molecular event to the harm you care about.
  3. Predictive performance — external validation on held-out data (illustrative: sens/spec ≥ 0.80 with 95% confidence intervals).
  4. Uncertainty quantification — prediction intervals and calibration, not a bare classification.
  5. Data integrity and traceability — standardised metadata and audit trails.

Pillar 2 is the real break, and it matters more than the process redesign. The traditional reference standard for a non-animal method is the animal test it replaces: you are validated to the extent you agree with the rodent. That ceiling is absolute — a NAM can never score better than the animal study, including on the chemicals where the animal study was wrong about humans. Anchoring to an AOP instead asks a different question: does this method measure a step that actually causes the harm? A method can then be right in a way the animal comparison cannot express. This vault has hit that ceiling repeatedly — the zebrafish, DNT battery and thyroid maps all carry it — and this is the cleanest statement of the escape route it has ingested.

The fourth actor on the same door. This vault now holds four independent parties arguing, from four different motives, that the one-off ring trial is being demoted from the gate to a gate:

  • FDA CDER (regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework) — already accepts a fit-for-purpose NAM with no Test Guideline and no formal validation.
  • UKCVAM (ukcvam-validation-body-beyond-the-ring-trial) — a brand-new national validation body whose remit says "ring trials or innovative ways of agreeing regulatory readiness."
  • The AI-NAM credibility literature (validation-as-continuous-service-not-event, ai-decision-rules-are-not-deterministic) — a model that retrains cannot be validated by an event.
  • This framework — the ring trial is too slow and measures the wrong reference.

The uncomfortable corollary, stated plainly. Interlaboratory reproducibility is the only property in the vault's evidence_tier ladder that is genuinely hard to fake, because it requires a method to work on somebody else's bench with somebody else's hands. The tiers above and below it — regulatory adoption, peer review — are weaker signals. If ring trials become optional, formally-validated stops meaning "it reproduces elsewhere" and starts meaning "a body agreed it was ready." Those are not the same claim, and this vault grades on the first one. Nobody in the four-actor convergence has said what replaces it. That is the open question and this note does not pretend it is answered.
