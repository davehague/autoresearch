A continuous probability is not a hazard class

On 2026-07-26 this vault recorded the sharpest thing it knows about why regulatory toxicology has not been automated the way chemistry has: a GPT-5-driven autonomous laboratory beat the published state of the art for cell-free protein synthesis because its objective was dollars per gram — a scalar the instrument reports, computable in the well, with no interpretation step and no external authority. Regulatory toxicology's objective is a classification defined by a committee, whose reference answer lives in a validation report the loop cannot regenerate.

This paper produces something in between, and the gap it leaves open is worth naming precisely.

What it produces

A Bayesian model over continuous variables, returning a probability distribution over how much a collagen gene's expression changes. Its stated advance over earlier quantitative adverse outcome pathways is exactly this: those chopped each key event into discrete buckets — low, medium, high — before modelling, and binning destroys the gradation that tells a small effect from a large one.

That is an objective function. It is differentiable in spirit, it is graded, it gets better or worse continuously, and an active-learning loop could pick its next experiment to reduce its uncertainty. This is the first thing in the vault's regulatory-toxicology column that has that shape natively rather than by being crushed into a threshold.

What it does not produce

A hazard call. The paper estimates "a biologically relevant range in COL1A1 fold change that indicates an activated state" — a range, associated with elevated fibrosis odds. Somewhere between that range and a regulatory decision there is a cut-off, and nobody has set it. The committee step is displaced, not removed.

So the honest reading is a split the vault should carry forward:

  • For the exploration leg of a loop — deciding which chemical to test next, and how much uncertainty remains — a continuous probabilistic model is strictly better than a threshold, and this is a genuine advance.
  • For the decision leg — producing the classification a regulator receives — it is still waiting for the same committee. A probability of 0.61 that stellate cells activated is not an answer to "does this chemical require classification."

The same split was drawn on 2026-07-26 about Cell Painting, which produces a point of departure without naming an endpoint and was recorded as "the vault's best candidate for a machine-computable objective function, for the exploration leg of a loop and not the decision leg." Two independent methods have now landed in that same position from different directions, and neither of them is what a regulator asked for.
