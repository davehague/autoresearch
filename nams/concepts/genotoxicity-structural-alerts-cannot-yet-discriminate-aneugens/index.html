High hit rate is not discrimination — a computational aneugen screen mostly detects "genotoxic-ish"

What changed

in-silico-qsar-read-across already carries the vault's sharpest split-axes case for computational methods and a documented domain-gate failure (similarity-based applicability domain checks are structurally blind to activity cliffs). This adds a second, distinct limitation, specific to genotoxicity mechanism discrimination rather than applicability-domain reliability: the existing structural-alert toolset can flag "this looks genotoxic" reasonably well, but cannot yet tell a spindle-poison aneugen apart from a DNA-damaging clastogen or a safe chemical. The best of ten profilers tested reaches 88% sensitivity on confirmed aneugens — but the same profiler is more likely to flag a clastogen (92%) than correctly-called aneugens, and wrongly flags three in four safe chemicals. That is not a discrimination failure at the margins; it is close to indiscriminate on the axis the paper set out to measure.

Why this is a pushes-back, not merely a limits, note

The paper's own framing situates structure-based screening for aneuploidy as a promising New Approach Methodology extending the applicability of Adverse Outcome Pathways. The vault's existing in-silico-qsar-read-across map records status: consolidating on the strength of the QSAR/read-across method class generally. This note pushes back specifically on any inference that the class's general maturity extends to mechanism-specific genotoxicity discrimination — a distinct, harder sub-task the existing map's Status was never measured against, and one this paper's own data says is not yet solved.

The one genuine positive result, kept in proportion

Narrowing to the 40 tubulin-binding aneugens, the "H-acceptor-path3-H-acceptor" alert reaches higher positive predictive value (66%, rising to 100% on two small sub-groups) than the unrefined profiler — a real, mechanistically grounded signal. The authors' own read: this does not represent a significant enrichment, and Observed/Expected ratios near 1.0 mean the refined alert is not clearly beating what subset size alone would predict. Recorded as the paper's most promising thread, not its headline result.

Loop-relevance

A closed loop trusting any of these ten profilers' aneugen calls at face value inherits a ~70–75% false-positive rate on non-genotoxic chemicals — a concrete number for the vault's standing caveat that loop_readiness: computational-connective is never itself a trustworthiness claim.

Composes with

genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus — the wet-lab assay this computational layer would eventually feed or triage for: TG 487 and TG 474 both list the kinetochore/FISH staining needed to distinguish an aneugen from a clastogen as optional, which is exactly why the paper had to build its own labeled dataset from scratch rather than reuse an existing micronucleus database. Not a correlated_blind_spot — this is a computational pre-screen limitation, not two assays sharing a wet-lab failure mode — but the same underlying gap (aneugen/clastogen mechanism is under-recorded across the field's data infrastructure) shows up on both sides of the physical/computational divide.
