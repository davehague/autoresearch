The most automatable battery in the vault wrongly flags two chemicals in five

The number

The developmental-neurotoxicity in-vitro battery is 17 plate-based assays on human stem-cell-derived neural cells, and by sheer volume of machine-readable readouts it is the most automatable battery this vault holds. It has been the vault's highest-leverage gap since it opened: enormous automation potential, integrated by expert judgement rather than a fixed rule, with a Defined Approach an OECD aspiration and no date attached.

Until tonight the vault held no performance figure for it. Truong et al., positioning a whole-organism zebrafish assay as a complementary first tier, cite the battery at:

86% sensitivity, 60% specificity

In plain English: it catches roughly six of every seven real developmental neurotoxicants, and it wrongly condemns two of every five chemicals that are fine.

Why 60% is the interesting half

Sensitivity of 86% on an endpoint this hard is respectable. Specificity of 60% is not, and it lands on a specific and expensive failure mode: a false positive in a screening battery triggers the follow-up study the battery exists to avoid. In developmental neurotoxicity, that follow-up is a mammalian guideline study — long, costly, and using animals. A battery that misfires 40% of the time on safe chemicals spends much of its value replacing one animal study with a later, larger one.

It also puts the vault's genotoxicity numbers in company rather than in isolation. The in-vitro micronucleus test runs 30–54% specificity (or 65% on another reference set). Tonight's HepaRG work shows the standard cell line wrongly condemning six of nine known-harmless chemicals. Poor specificity is not one assay's defect; it is a recurring property of in-vitro batteries built to be sensitive, and it is the single most consistent finding across this vault's endpoint columns.

What it does and does not change

Status holds at contested. developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb was moved there on 2026-07-20 by the OECD Secretariat's own statement that the battery has never been formally validated as an integrated battery. A performance figure from a third party does not resolve that; it describes the thing that was never validated.

loop_readiness holds at automatable-open-loop. The blocker is the missing integration rule, and a specificity number does not supply one. If anything it raises the price of the missing rule: a battery whose components are individually machine-read but whose combined call is wrong 40% of the time on negatives is precisely a system you do not want an unsupervised optimiser iterating on. The vault's rail applies — an automated system reading a battery's concordant answer as confirmation compounds the error rather than catching it.

Two brakes on the number itself. It is cited, not measured, here — reported by authors proposing a competing first-tier assay, which is a motivated comparison, and the vault read only the abstract. And the zebrafish figures it is set against carry a five-chemical negative set, so "100% of DNT-negative compounds" is five chemicals. The vault records both numbers with their denominators visible and neither as settled.
