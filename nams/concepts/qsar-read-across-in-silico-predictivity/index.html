(Q)SAR & read-across → in-silico (standalone, within-IATA use)

(Q)SAR (quantitative structure–activity relationship models) predicts a toxicological endpoint from molecular structure/descriptors; read-across infers a data-poor chemical's toxicity from structurally and mechanistically similar, data-rich analogues (category formation). Both are purely computational — no wet assay at prediction time — and are governed by the OECD principles for (Q)SAR validation: a defined endpoint, an unambiguous algorithm, a defined applicability domain, appropriate goodness-of-fit/robustness/predictivity, and (ideally) a mechanistic interpretation. The free OECD QSAR Toolbox operationalises category formation and read-across.

Grading (broad class): evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic — the rubric's tier for structure-based reasoning without wet validation of the specific prediction; regulatory_status: proposed — broadly accepted as supporting evidence within an integrated assessment (e.g. REACH Annex XI permits read-across/(Q)SAR under conditions), not a stand-alone test replacement for most endpoints. funding_flag: none for the public framework (vendor platform performance claims carry industry separately). Performance is applicability-domain-bounded and endpoint-dependent — a prediction outside the training domain is not trustworthy, which is exactly why these read within a weight-of-evidence, not alone.

Relative to the vault, this is the cross-cutting computational tier that feeds every endpoint: it composes-with the genotox battery (genotoxicity-ames-bacterial-reverse-mutation, genotoxicity-mammalian-gene-mutation) as a tier-1 structural-alert screen, and with the carcinogenicity map (carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay) via structural alerts for genotoxic carcinogens. The one stand-alone adopted exception (ICH M7 as an Ames substitute) is graded separately in ich-m7-qsar-ames-replacement.

Rebutted by: the-model-layers-consensus-cleared-what-the-assay-layer-flagged — a specific limit on the common practice of combining several (Q)SAR tools by majority vote to raise confidence. Screening nine cosmetic UV filters, a majority consensus across six tools flagged hormone activity in one chemical while existing robotic assay data flagged three of the four it covered, including two the consensus cleared. Tools trained on overlapping public data are not independent votes, so agreement among them can be a shared blind spot rather than corroboration. Holds as a caution about consensus voting; it does not touch the within-IATA supporting-evidence standing described above.
