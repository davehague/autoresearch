One network, two kingdoms of animal, and the assumption doing the joining is not written down

Thyroid hormone is genuinely ancient. It sets growth and development across essentially all vertebrates, which is the biological warrant for the One Health move this source makes: assess human and environmental thyroid hazard against one adverse outcome pathway network rather than two parallel ones, and map both human in-vitro assays and fish eleutheroembryo assays (a fish after hatching and before it feeds itself — legally not a protected animal in the EU, so it counts as an alternative method) onto the same components.

The efficiency case is strong. Two regulatory communities currently generate separate evidence about the same chemical acting on the same hormone system, and cannot combine it. One network is how you would fix that.

The assumption underneath is that a key event means the same thing in both. That a chemical inhibiting the hormone-building enzyme in a human cell line, and the same chemical altering hormone-dependent development in a fish larva, are readings of the same node rather than two different nodes that happen to share a name. The retrievable text asserts the integration and — as far as can be read — does not argue the conservation.

Why this vault flags it rather than accepting it

This is not scepticism about thyroid biology being conserved; broadly it is. It is the vault's standing discipline about what makes two results two votes.

The correlated-blind-spot rail exists because assays that look independent share failure modes: the fish embryo test and the fish gill cell line both under-predict chemicals that need metabolic activation, because neither has full metabolism; three adopted endocrine guidelines read three different receptors through the same reporter backbone. In each case the vault's job was to notice that a battery casting what looked like several votes was casting fewer.

One Health integration is the same question turned inside out. Rather than two assays that are secretly the same measurement, it proposes two measurements from different species that are asserted to be the same measurement — and the risk runs in both directions:

  • If conservation holds where it is assumed, the human and fish arms are less independent than a weight-of-evidence tally implies, and a concordant human/fish result is being counted twice.
  • If conservation fails somewhere — a transport protein with no human equivalent, a developmental window with no fish equivalent, a deiodinase with different substrate preferences — then a discordant result is being read as uncertainty when it is actually information about which species the chemical acts in.

Neither reading is available without a stated map of which components are conserved, and the retrievable text does not supply one.

What this means for a loop

An automated system integrating this network would be executing the assumption without ever encountering it. The mapping is the machine-readable artifact; the conservation claim is the prose around it. A loop consumes the first and not the second — which is the general shape of this vault's domain_gate concern, appearing here at the framework layer rather than at the assay layer.

Recorded as an open question, not as a rail flag. No correlated_blind_spot row is set on any map from this source, and the reason is worth stating: the rail requires two named methods failing on the same chemicals for the same structural reason. Here there are no named methods, no named chemicals and no measured failure — only a structural risk in how a framework is composed. Firing the rail on that would cheapen it.
