"AI actively drives critical screening decisions" is a claim about the exact thing nobody can do yet

Organ-on-a-chip systems grow human cells in engineered microfluidic devices that reproduce some of an organ's architecture and fluid flow — the vault holds them at hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps for liver injury and nephrotoxicity-ciptec-aop-battery for kidney. Their data is rich, multi-parametric, and famously hard to turn into a verdict, which is exactly why the map's loop_blocker reads multi-parametric readout with no fixed data-interpretation procedure.

A 2026 review proposes that AI stop being the analysis step and become the driver:

"this integration elevates AI from a conventional post-experimental analytical tool into a comprehensive framework that actively drives quality control, response quantification, model integration, and critical screening decisions."

Four jobs are named, and only one of them would move anything on this vault's automation axis.

JobStatus on this vault's ladder
Response quantificationalready machine-readable. Not the blocker.
Model integrationthe computational-connective layer, already mapped
Quality controlthe "machine that declines its own data" pattern, now seen five times in this vault
Critical screening decisionsthe blocker itself

No status and no loop tier moves on this source. A review asserting that AI should make the call is not evidence that anyone has published a rule for making it. The distance between those two things is the distance this whole vault measures.

The part worth keeping is the barrier list

"we outline primary barriers to clinical translation, including inherent biological and engineering variability, model generalizability, the need for external validation, and clinical dose relevance."

Every one of those is a blocker this vault already carries, restated from inside the device-engineering literature:

  • Variability — every chip is hand-assembled. A decision rule that cannot survive that run-to-run spread is not a decision rule.
  • Model generalizability — a model tuned on one laboratory's chips is not evidence about another's; the applicability-domain problem, moved to the model layer.
  • External validation — the fourth independent constituency in this vault to name absent validation standards as the binding constraint, after the US Food and Drug Administration's model-credibility framework, the toxicologic pathology profession's roundtable, and a 28-author autonomous-science roadmap. None of the four cites the others.
  • Clinical dose relevance — whether a concentration that does something in a chip corresponds to a dose a person would receive; the in-vitro-to-in-vivo extrapolation problem (pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics).

⚠ The tension the review does not notice

It proposes AI as the interpretation layer, and lists absent validation as a barrier, in the same abstract. Those are not two problems; one is a proposed solution to the other, offered without acknowledging that the solution needs the missing thing in order to be assessable. You cannot validate a decision procedure that nobody has written down.

The honest floor

A review. No data, no system, no measurement, no error rate — evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic, strength: suggestive. Its framing is drug screening and clinical translation, not hazard assessment, and whether any surveyed platform touches a regulatory toxicology endpoint is not visible from the abstract, which is all that is readable: the full text is behind a subscription and the browser fetch rung returned HTTP 403.
