Two numbers that are one number

The paper reports that two hit-selection criteria applied to the same 22,000-well screen returned exactly the same hits — 269 inhibitors, 793 activators, under both an SSMD cut at 1.96 and an area-under-the-ROC cut at 0.975. It presents this as demonstrating the equivalence of the two approaches.

It does demonstrate that. It is worth being clear about what kind of fact it is.

The paper's own central identity is AUROC = Φ(β), where β is SSMD and Φ is the normal cumulative distribution function — a strictly increasing function. So β ≥ 1.96 and Φ(β) ≥ Φ(1.96) = 0.975 are the same inequality. Identical hit sets were not a finding; they were guaranteed the moment the identity was derived. A monotone relabelling of an axis cannot select different points on it.

Contrast the two criteria in the same table that do place the cut somewhere different: the Youden criterion at 0.90 pulls in about a third more hits (355 and 1,101), and the fixed-specificity rule pulls in slightly fewer (260 and 748). Those are real disagreements between real alternatives, and they are the interesting rows.

Why this is filed under the rail

This vault's correlated-blind-spot rail says: when two assays in one battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, that battery casts one vote, not two — and an automated system that takes their agreement as confirmation will iterate confidently into a systematic error rather than catch it. The canonical cases are physical: the fish embryo test and the RTgill-W1 fish-cell assay both lacking a nervous system and full metabolism; the reactive-oxygen-species assay and 3T3 NRU both lacking biotransformation.

Here the same failure has no biology in it at all. Two numbers, one measurement, one deterministic function connecting them. The dependence is total, and it is invisible in exactly the way the physical cases are: each metric has its own name, its own threshold, its own literature, and produces its own confident output.

The generalisation the rail wants, stated once: agreement is evidence only in proportion to the independence of what is agreeing. Two assays sharing a metabolic gap are partially dependent. A statistic and its own transform are perfectly dependent. Seven machine-learning models trained on one dataset — the shape found in tonight's other computational source, model-disagreement-as-a-machine-checkable-refusal-rule — sit somewhere between, and nobody has measured where.

What a loop should do with it

Nothing about this makes the SSMD framework wrong, and it is not why the Tox21 map holds its tier. It is a reading instruction for any system that aggregates: before treating two signals as two votes, check whether one is computable from the other. In statistics that check is cheap and rarely run. In a battery of wet assays it is expensive and this vault runs it every ingest. The cheap case failing is not a smaller problem than the expensive one — it is the same problem in the layer where an autonomous system does most of its reasoning.

The brakes

  • Single preprint, single author, no new data; the arithmetic point above is not the paper's claim and the paper is not wrong about anything it does assert.
