The escape hatch has a ceiling, and the EU's reference lab wrote it down

What the vault has been tracking

Five maps sit at human-in-the-loop. Four of them are blocked at the same place — a trained human scoring images down a microscope — and since 2026-07-15 the sweep has found a demonstrated machine-vision escape hatch for each one:

MapEscape hatchStrength
genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleusWills 2021 — imaging flow cytometry + deep learning, three labs, reproduces the human benchmark dosestrongest

The vault's standing prediction: whichever of these gets a validated automated scorer first — a ring trial, or the automated readout written into the guideline — is the next human-in-the-loop → automatable-open-loop promotion.

What this source says about that prediction

It says the promotion may not be available on those terms at all.

"AI algorithms excel at automated tasks, human expertise remains irreplaceable for confirming and interpreting the outcome and toxicologists will maintain responsibility for final interpretation and decision-making."

Written by the European Commission Joint Research Centre — whose unit hosts EURL ECVAM, the EU's method-validation reference laboratory — with EFSA and the German BfR on the byline.

Read the sentence carefully, because it separates two things the vault must keep separate. It does not say the scoring cannot be automated; the same paper documents the DNT battery's flagship assay being scored end-to-end by two convolutional neural networks. It says the responsibility for the interpretation stays with a person. In vault terms: the readout moves, the call does not.

That is a different claim from "the technology isn't ready," and it does not expire when the technology improves.

What this changes, and what it does not

No tier moves. This is one position statement in one review, not a guideline, not a validation result, and not binding on anyone. strength: suggestive.

What it adds is a second, non-technical blocker behind the technical one. The vault has been modelling the machine-vision escape hatch as: build a scorer → validate it → guideline adopts it → tier promotes. This source says step three may terminate somewhere short of the tier, because the regulatory framing places a human at the interpretation step as a matter of accountability, not of capability.

And it rhymes with a finding from a different jurisdiction. FDA's model-risk matrix (see regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility, ingested 2026-07-13) defines model influence as how alone the model is in the decision, and states explicitly that a human cross-check "would reduce the AI model influence" and therefore the evidentiary burden. Two regulators, two continents, both structurally rewarding a human in the loop. The vault's axis measures whether a machine could make the call. Both regulators are answering a question the axis does not ask: who is answerable when it does.

The honest counterweight

This is a review paper co-authored by, among others, two high-content imaging instrument manufacturers and two image-analysis software companies (see the source's conflicts field). A statement that a toxicologist must remain responsible for interpretation is not commercially costly to any of them — it is arguably the comfortable position for everyone in the room. Recorded as a position with institutional weight, not as a finding.
