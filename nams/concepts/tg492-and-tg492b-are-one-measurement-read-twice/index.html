The vault's most correlated pair, and the guidance concedes it in three words

The rail. Two assays in one battery that fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason cast one vote, not two — and an automated loop reading their agreement as confirmation will iterate confidently into the shared error. Every previous row on the vault's rail required detective work: two fish assays that both lack a nervous system; two phototoxicity assays that both lack biotransformation; three endocrine reporters that share the MMTV promoter.

This row required none. GD 263 describes each adopted eye method in a standard template with a field called "Scientific basis incl. MoA." For TG 492B, that field reads, in its entirety: "See Section 4.3.4." Section 4.3.4 is TG 492. The guidance does not assert a distinct mechanism for TG 492B because there isn't one.

What is shared:

  • The same tissue class — reconstructed human cornea-like epithelium (RhCE), stratified non-keratinised human corneal epithelial cells at an air–liquid interface.
  • The same readout — MTT dye conversion measured as optical density, with the same interference problem (chemicals that absorb near formazan's wavelength, or reduce MTT directly) and the same HPLC/UPLC workaround.
  • The same missing biology — neither construct has meaningful xenobiotic metabolism. A chemical whose eye hazard depends on a metabolite reads low in both.
  • The same reference standard — rabbit Draize data (TG 405), with its own documented ~12% within-test variability.

What differs: the exposure schedule, and therefore what the rule can conclude. TG 492 reads one exposure against one 60% viability threshold and can only say "no classification needed." TG 492B reads two or three exposures against 40/50/60% thresholds and returns all three GHS classes. That is a difference in the decision rule, not in the measurement.

So the pair is the vault's tightest correlation to date — tighter than TG 494 ↔ TG 492 (added 2026-07-19), where at least the two read different physical properties (electrical resistance vs cell viability) of the same tissue, and the guidance argued they sample one mechanism early and late. Here they read the same property of the same tissue and differ in when they look.

Metabolism is now the shared cause in five of the vault's eight blind-spot rows. Every in-vitro assay in this vault solves metabolic competence the same way — by not having it, or by borrowing a rodent's liver.

Why this one matters more than it looks. GD 263 tells assessors to combine several high-sensitivity rule-out methods "to increase the overall specificity of the testing strategy" (regulatory-sensitivity-demand-caps-specificity-at-60-80-percent) — and TG 492 and TG 492B are both on the list of adopted methods available for that. Combining them cannot increase specificity in the way the guidance intends, because their false positives are drawn from the same population. And GD 263 also, in the same document, tells assessors to consider "the conditional independence of these test methods" (oecd-names-conditional-independence-as-a-battery-design-rule). The instruction and the counter-example are in the same guidance document, twenty pages apart, and it never joins them.

The consequence for the eye defined approach. TG 467 composes TG 492 + TG 437 (DAL-1) or TG 491 + TG 437 (DAL-2). Substituting TG 492B for TG 492 in an informal weight-of-evidence stack would add resolution (three classes instead of one rule-out) while adding no mechanistic independence at all. If a formulator runs both TG 492 and TG 492B and they agree, that is one result, reported twice.

(The pairing and its consequences are a vault inference from the guidance's own cross-reference; GD 263 never states that the two methods are non-independent.)
