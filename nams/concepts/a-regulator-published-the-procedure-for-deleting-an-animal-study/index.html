A regulator published the procedure for deleting an animal study

This vault's dominant loop blocker has one sentence: nineteen method maps produce a clean number, and nobody has written down the procedure for turning that number into a decision. Three routes to such a rule have been seen — a committee writes one, everyone waits for more potency data, or somebody fits one to the published record (a-decision-rule-assembled-from-other-peoples-published-data).

This is a fourth thing, and it is not on that list, because the decision it procedurally specifies is not is this chemical hazardous. It is do we need to run the animal study at all — and a regulator wrote it down, with a flowchart, listing the inputs.

Why the shape matters more than the subject

Read the strategy as a program and its signature is unusual for regulatory toxicology:

  • Inputs are named and mostly non-animal. In-vitro comparative metabolism across species. In-vitro human Caco-2 intestinal permeability. A PBK model of internal exposure. The one animal input that remains is the rat ADME study — and the rat study is the thing being kept, not the thing being removed.
  • The branches are stated. Dissimilar dog-versus-human metabolite profile → waive. Well-characterised chemical class → waive. Mode of action where the dog is less sensitive than rodents → waive. Mode of action where the dog is more sensitive → still waivable, at the price of an extra uncertainty factor. That last branch is the interesting one: it converts a missing study into an explicit numerical surcharge on the safety margin rather than into a refusal.
  • It terminates. If the dog adds no information on internal exposure, the requirement may be waived.

What stops it being a rule a machine could execute is stated by EFSA itself: it is a "tiered, weight-of-evidence (WoE)-based approach." Weight of evidence is this vault's most-recorded blocker phrase. Nothing in the opinion says how much dissimilarity in a metabolite profile counts as dissimilar, or how large an extra uncertainty factor should be — that is left "depending on the available data and context of the assessment."

So: the most specific published waiver procedure this vault has seen, and it still has an unquantified judgement at every branch point. That is the same shape the developmental-neurotoxicity battery's decision path turned out to have on 2026-07-30 — a model consulted, then a person deciding the margin was big enough, with no published statement of how large the margin must be.

⚠ And a limit the regulator drew deliberately

The opinion permits PBK modelling to decide whether a species is informative, and forbids it from setting the number: "and not for the derivation of HBGVs. The application of standard regulatory uncertainty factors remains unchanged."

A model may tell you which experiment not to run. It may not tell you what is safe. For anyone reasoning about how much of the regulatory stack a model layer can occupy, that is a boundary drawn in writing by a regulator, in 2026, and it is drawn well short of the answer.
