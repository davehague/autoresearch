Regression-based DA (GL 497 Part III) → a quantitative point of departure for skin sensitisation

The regression-based DA reaches the same destination as sara-ice-quantitative-pod-skin-sensitization — a quantitative PoD for skin-sensitisation risk assessment — by a deliberately different route: multiple linear regression rather than Bayesian inference, running in a transparent spreadsheet rather than an app. Two model families are trained, one on LLNA EC3 values and one on weight-of-evidence human Potency Values (derived from DSA04, NOEL and EC3 data). The DA computes both and automatically takes the more conservative estimate — a design choice that builds a safety preference into the method itself.

Narrower inputs than SARA-ICE. Only kDPRA (TG 442C), KeratinoSens (TG 442D) and h-CLAT (TG 442E) are admissible, at least two of them. Where DPRA is positive, the kDPRA is the quantitative KE1 test that must be used — the ordinary DPRA's depletion percentage is not enough, because a regression needs a kinetic rate, not a binary. It also applies a vapour-pressure correction for volatility under topical application, an exposure-realism term the other DAs lack.

The under-protective tail is the number that matters. Overall agreement (86–87% within 10-fold) is close to SARA-ICE's, and the bias is balanced (geometric-mean fold-difference 0.86 vs LLNA, 0.99 vs human PV). But the guideline reports openly that 5.5–8.9% of chemicals have their potency under-predicted by more than 10-fold, depending on the reference set. In a risk-assessment context an under-predicted potency means a PoD that is too high — a dose called safe that is not. That asymmetry does not appear in a symmetric "within 10-fold" summary, and it is the single most important thing to carry forward about this DA. Roughly one third of estimates land within 2-fold, as with SARA-ICE.

Two DAs, one guideline, different failure modes. SARA-ICE returns a distribution (its uncertainty is legible) and runs ~2-fold below LLNA. The regression DA returns a point from a conservative-of-two rule, and has a documented long tail of under-prediction. They are not redundant and the guideline does not rank them; they are alternative interpretation procedures over an overlapping input set. Whether they agree chemical-by- chemical is not reported in the guideline text read here — an open question worth a later depth pass.

funding_flag: [industry, method-developer]. The regression DA descends from Natsch & Gerberick (2022); Andreas Natsch is at Givaudan (fragrance industry, a heavy user of sensitisation risk assessment). The guideline's Part III reference list cites Natsch 13 times. As with SARA-ICE, the mitigation is real but partial: independent evaluation by the OECD EG DASS against a curated reference set. Industry-originated, regulator- evaluated — recorded as exactly that.
