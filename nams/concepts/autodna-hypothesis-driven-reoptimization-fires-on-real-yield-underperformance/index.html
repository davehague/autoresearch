A closed loop's "what went wrong, and what should we try instead" step, caught firing on a real wet-lab yield problem

AutoDNA's Experiment Planner (EPA) / Hypothesis Proposer (HPA) / Literature Researcher (LRA) triad is this vault's automation-experiment-selection "deciding leg" implemented on physical hardware with a real underperformance signal, not a simulator with a known ground truth. When an enzymatic DNA-synthesis run came back below target yield, HPA proposed causal hypotheses (buffer choice, surfactant, cofactor concentration), LRA checked them against the literature, and EPA issued a revised procedure that the hardware-execution agents ran — reaching >98% yield, then deliberately declining a further time-minimization push once it traded away yield. That stop-when-the-tradeoff-reverses behavior is a small but real instance of multi-objective judgment exercised by the agent loop itself, not hard-coded.

Why this is worth a concept note on its own, separate from the source digest. Every other hypothesis-conditioned selection method on automation-experiment-selection (hypothesis-conditioned-selection-beats-classical-active-learning-on-symbolic-accuracy among them) is evaluated against a simulator whose true mechanism is known in advance, precisely because that is the only way to score whether the agent found the right answer rather than a plausible one. AutoDNA has no such ground truth to check against — the measure of success is simply "yield went up and stayed up," which is weaker evidence of correct mechanistic reasoning but is evidence from a real, uncontrolled wet lab, which the simulator-based results are not.

What this does not show. The hypothesis space tested here (buffer, surfactant, cofactor concentration) is small and chemically well-trodden for enzymatic DNA synthesis — the literature-retrieval step (LRA) is plausibly doing most of the work by surfacing known fixes, rather than the system discovering anything novel. No ablation isolates how much of the yield gain came from HPA's hypothesis generation versus LRA's literature lookup versus trial-and-error within the final procedure space. The paper does not claim otherwise.

No toxicology, no crossover. As with every source on this map, the relevance is architectural, not substantive — nothing here is a hazard call, nothing has a decision rule a regulator would ever examine.
