A regulator names the tools for a population it has never named before, and states its own blocker in plain words

regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework holds CDER's general NAM validation posture — fit-for-purpose, context-of-use, no formal validation required if the sponsor can argue the method answers the question asked. This workshop is the first source on this map scoped to a specific vulnerable population rather than a general NAM category: pediatric developmental safety, the question of whether a drug harms a still-developing organ in ways an adult trial or an adult-focused pharmacovigilance system cannot catch.

What is genuinely new. The workshop ties together, under one regulatory roof, four method families this vault already tracks on separate maps — secondary pharmacology screening (safety-pharmacology-ich-s7a-core-battery, though S7A itself is never cited), organoids (gastrointestinal-toxicity-human-colonoid for the gut, metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg-adjacent for hepatocyte CYP maturation), developmental-neurotoxicity models (developmental-neurotoxicity-neurosphere-assay-adjacent for the neuronal apoptosis models), and life-stage PBPK (pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics) — as a single proposed pediatric-context battery, while stating none of them has yet been validated for this specific use.

The loop-relevant sentence. "Off-target activity alone is a hazard flag, not a risk assessment." This is the pediatric-context instance of the vault's single most common loop_blocker shape: an instrument-readable signal with no published rule for converting it into a call. The workshop names this as the open problem directly, rather than the vault having to infer it from a method's own silence.

What it means for an autonomous laboratory, specifically

A pediatric-safety loop would need (1) a battery of machine-readable readouts across several organ systems and (2) a rule for combining off-target hits, organoid readouts and a life-stage PBPK exposure estimate into a single developmental-risk call. This source confirms the field has named (1) as an aspiration across four separate method families and has explicitly not built (2) — the workshop's own words rule out reading any of the proposed pediatric NAMs as loop-ready today, regardless of how automatable any one component's readout is.

The limits

A workshop report, not a validation study — no primary data, no reference set, no accuracy figure for the pediatric-specific application of any named tool. The one quantitative worked example (life-stage PBPK on a pyrethroid) is a single retrospective case that happened to come out reassuring; it is not evidence the approach generalizes. evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic is the vault's standing floor for a document that argues rather than measures — now the sixteenth-plus instance of the evidence-ladder gap logged since 2026-07-12 for review/opinion documents, re-surfaced rather than re-counted precisely here.
