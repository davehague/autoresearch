An assay that was asked where it stops working, and answered

Most method papers report what an assay detects. This one systematically probed what it cannot see, using activator/inhibitor pairs for eighteen signalling pathways implicated in human neurodevelopmental disorders, and reported the nulls as findings.

The two blind pathways

"STAT3 and TrkB pathways were outside the applicability domain of the Neurosphere Assay as they did not lead to any endpoint modulation."

Neither is obscure. STAT3 drives astrogliogenesis — and the authors immediately name the structural reason this matters: astrogliogenesis "is not assessed in the DNT IVB leaving an uncertainty in the covered neurodevelopmental endpoints." The pathway is invisible because the process it governs is not in the battery at all. That independently corroborates the OECD's own limitations list in tonight's companion source, which names "astrocyte maturation, myelination, and the peripheral nervous system" as incompletely covered.

TrkB governs dendritic arborisation, synaptic plasticity and neural network formation. The authors diagnose the null mechanistically — "Limited BDNF expression in our fetal Neurosphere model likely explains the lack of response" — and point at the remedy: "recently developed human NNF assays may complement the current DNT IVB by addressing this uncertainty."

The readout that fires for nothing

Sharper, and easier to miss. Neuronal migration (NPC2b) responded to none of the eighteen pathways. And in the authors' own earlier EFSA-supported screening study:

"the NPC2b endpoint is rarely affected by chemical exposure, with only one out of 120 compounds (pesticide deltamethrin) affecting neuronal migration."

Zero of 18 mechanistic probes. One of 120 chemicals. A readout with a hit rate that low is either measuring something genuinely rare, or is under-powered to detect what it is aimed at, and this study cannot separate those two explanations. The authors take the generous reading — "such negative results are valuable because they inform where the assay may not reliably detect specific pathway perturbations, thereby supporting its appropriate fit-for-purpose use within an IATA" — which is fair and is also the reading that a human expert integrating the battery can act on and a fixed decision rule cannot, unless someone writes the weighting down.

Why this belongs on the loop axis, not just the science axis

shared/evidence-model.md defines domain_gate as "an applicability-domain carve-out the loop must check before trusting the decision rule." Every existing example is chemical: TG 435 screening out pH 4.5–8.5, TG 249 bounded to log KOW −4 to 7, TG 494's measurable pre-test.

This is a domain gate expressed in biology rather than chemistry, and that makes it much harder for a loop to enforce. A robot can measure a partition coefficient or a pH and decline. No robot can measure whether the chemical in front of it acts through STAT3. The gate is defined over mechanism — precisely the thing you run the assay to find out. A loop cannot check this gate before trusting the rule; it can only be told the gate exists, which is the weaker case TG 494 was celebrated on 2026-07-19 for escaping.

Consequence, stated plainly: a negative result from this assay does not distinguish "this chemical is not a developmental neurotoxicant" from "this chemical acts through a pathway this assay is blind to." For a human inside an IATA that ambiguity is manageable — it is what the expert judgement is for. For an automated screen it is a systematic false-negative channel with no flag on it.

Grading, and the conflict read in both directions

funding_flag: [industry, method-developer] is mandatory here and the source digest states it fully: four of fifteen authors, including the senior author and two of three corresponding authors, hold equity in DNTOX GmbH, which sells DNT-IVB assay services.

And the bias points away from these particular findings. A group selling battery services published that their assay is blind to two disease-relevant pathways, that one of its seven readouts fires for essentially nothing, and that the battery omits a whole cell lineage. That is not what commercial pressure produces. The flag stays — it is not the vault's job to decide when a conflict happens to be inactive — but the honest note is that the conflict makes these limits more credible, not less.

strength: suggestive, on the authors' own framing: the pathway–readout links are "hypothesis-generating," pathway engagement was not confirmed by transcriptomics or phosphoprotein analysis, bioavailable concentrations were not measured, and inter-donor variability was not assessed. Status is not moved by this note alone; it is moved by dnt-ivb-never-formally-validated-as-a-battery, which this independently reinforces from the bench side.
