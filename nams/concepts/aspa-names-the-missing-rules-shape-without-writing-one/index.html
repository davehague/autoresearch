ASPA names the missing rule's shape, without writing one

risk-assessment-ngra has carried the same claim since 2026-08-05, repeated independently by five constituencies with no citation between them: the NGRA integration layer — the step that turns a set of NAM results into one safety decision — has no published, agreed procedure, so "expert judgement" does the integrating today, with the variability and loss of reproducibility that implies.

leist-2026-aspa-ngra-workflow (Leist et al. 2026, ALTEX 43(1):158–175, a 24-author ASPIS-cluster consortium spanning University of Konstanz/CAAT-Europe, RIVM, TNO, University of Birmingham, Fraunhofer ITEM, Certara, BfR, BASF, Leiden University, L'Oréal, Humane World for Animals Europe, Vrije Universiteit Brussel, Unilever and Universitat Pompeu Fabra; EU Horizon 2020/Horizon Europe-funded, grant No. 964537) is the first source this vault has found that answers the gap directly, by naming and publishing a procedure: the Alternative Safety Profiling Algorithm (ASPA), with a companion reporting tool, ASPA-assist.

What ASPA actually is

A flowchart grammar, not a decision rule. ASPA breaks a risk assessment into typed "building blocks" — operator tasks (data processing/combination) and decision points (DP), which take a single yes/no branch and steer the evaluation toward a different downstream path depending on the state of information. Its stated design goal is exactly the property a loop would need: "identical data input yields identical outcomes in the hands of independent assessors."

The honest state today: all E, no A

The DP identifier has a letter for exactly this axis. From the paper: "DP may require expert judgement (defined by an 'E' in the identifier) or they may be automated and data driven ('A' in the identifier). While at present, there are only E-DP, it is likely that ASPA will be increasingly automated and that some A-DP will be operated by AI tools." A second identifier marks whether a DP is one-way ("O") or part of an iterative loop ("L") — the schema has a reserved slot for exactly the closed-loop structure this vault tracks, unused in the published version. The paper's own disclaimer section is blunt about where that leaves it today: "ASPA is NOT a one-click risk assessment: It is not meant to be an automated process and will typically require expert judgement for decision-making in many cases."

Why this is a real move and not a restatement

A schema that reserves a slot for an automated, loop-capable decision node is a different object from a field with no agreed vocabulary for one at all. risk-assessment-ngra's prior sources named the missing-rule problem from the FDA, ICCVAM, the toxicologic-pathology roundtable and a 28-author autonomous- science roadmap, independently and with no shared schema. ASPA gives the next paper that publishes an agreed interpretation rule somewhere in NGRA a place to plug it in (an A-DP) without redesigning the workflow around it — which is infrastructure for the loop-readiness axis to move, not a move itself.

The honest limit

Reproducibility is asserted, not measured: the abstract and body report no inter-assessor concordance study, no accuracy metric against a reference standard, and no count of how many real case studies produced identical outcomes across independent operators — the paper's central empirical claim is untested by its own text. "Future ASPA versions are anticipated to automate some of the tasks" is a roadmap statement, cited to a 2024 opinion piece (Kleinstreuer & Hartung) about AI's role in toxicology generally, not to any ASPA- specific automation result. Nothing here moves loop_readiness on any method map — the systemic-toxicity assays ASPA would eventually integrate are exactly as automatable or non-automatable as they were before this paper, because ASPA governs the assembly step, not the assays.
