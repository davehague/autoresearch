EU roadmap to phase out animal testing — AI named as a pillar → the acceptance axis (EU side)

This is a regulatory-move claim, not a method claim. On 1 June 2026 the European Commission adopted a Roadmap towards phasing out animal testing for chemical safety assessments — the EU's official statement of direction across 15 regulatory domains (industrial chemicals, pesticides, biocides, pharmaceuticals, food/feed additives, cosmetics), with 30+ recommended actions on three pillars.

The load-bearing fact for this vault. Pillar 2 is "leverage research, artificial intelligence, and data-driven assessments" — the EU has put AI and computational methods at the centre of its non-animal-testing strategy, alongside "virtual controls" (a modelled or historical control arm) as a proposed substitute for animal control groups in pharmaceutical toxicology. That is a regulator naming a computational comparator as an acceptable stand-in for a live animal — a small, concrete step toward the state the loop's model half needs (in-silico-qsar-read-across is the vault's existence proof that a computational method can already be allowed to make a regulatory call under ICH M7).

What it is not — recorded, not softened.

  • Non-binding. No legal phase-out, no target date, no numeric acceptance thresholds. It is a direction of travel and a set of proposed actions, regulatory_status: proposed. evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted records only that the claims come from the issuing body's own primary document — the FDA-framework convention (regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework).
  • Does not fix the validation bottleneck. It names the problem (stakeholders acknowledged validation is too slow; the vault has the number — twelve years for TG 445A, metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg) but offers no timelines. It also names the endpoints where non-animal methods still do not exist — endocrine disruption, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity — the same coverage gaps the vault tracks (reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams is the vault's no-nam-exists case).
  • Not neutral. Its stated purpose is phasing out animal testing, so its optimism about non-animal methods is an agenda, not an assessment. funding_flag: [none] in the vendor/method-developer sense, but the advocacy framing is flagged in the source digest.

Why it matters to the acceptance axis. The two largest drug/chemical regulators — the US FDA (regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework, regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility) and now the EU — are simultaneously signalling that non-animal and AI-driven methods are the intended future, and both decline to make it binding or to resolve the validation delay. The vault now holds the primary regulatory-direction documents for both jurisdictions, and they agree on the destination while leaving the mechanism — how an AI-driven method actually earns acceptance — unwritten.
