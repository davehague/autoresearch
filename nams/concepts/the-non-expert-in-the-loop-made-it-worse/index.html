The non-expert in the loop made it worse

The assumption being tested

"Human-in-the-loop" is the standard answer to every reliability worry about an autonomous laboratory, and this vault holds it as a tier on its own axis: human-in-the-loop sits above bench-bound and below automatable-open-loop precisely because a person in the call is treated as the thing that makes an unreliable system safe. Several sources here lean on it as a design principle — agentic-ai-toxicologic-pathology-human-in-loop-by-design and robin-semi-autonomous-mind-not-hands among them.

Almost nobody measures it. This paper did, in three modes, and the answer is not monotonic.

What happened in each mode

Fully automated — the agent answered its own clarifying questions from its defaults.

Non-expert collaborator — a person without domain expertise answered them instead. Performance got worse. The paper attributes the gap to the model doing better on its own priors than on a non-specialist's supplied answers. A well-meant human input is not a null operation; it is an input, and a wrong one propagates.

Expert collaborator — one systems engineer with deep expertise in the target robotic platform. Significant improvement in both procedural correctness and numerical accuracy across most experiments. And still:

  • In several experiments the expert did not notice that the system had omitted critical stir-rate commands.
  • In one, the system initially omitted a density term in its calculations, flagged that omission itself in its output table, and the expert caught it — with the paper observing that a non-expert would likely have missed the flag. The machine's own self-report was the load-bearing safeguard, not the human's attention.
  • In the multi-plate protocol the system inserted an erroneous step and the collaboration was only "partially successful."

The authors' conclusion, in their words: human oversight "is not a universal solution for all errors", and the right division is experts on strategy, automated validation on the details — because attention is finite and drifts to the interesting parts, which is exactly where the boring failures aren't.

Why this matters to this vault specifically

It puts a measurement under a tier that has so far been an assumption. The human-in-the-loop rung on shared/evidence-model.md describes where trained judgment is load-bearing. It does not say the judgment is reliable, and this vault has never had a number on that. Now there is one data point, and it says the tier splits: expert-in-the-loop and non-expert-in-the-loop are different things, and only one of them beats full autonomy.

That has a direct read-across to the assays. When a guideline puts a person in the interpretation step — the in-vitro micronucleus assay's scorer, the eye-hazard IATA's weight-of-evidence integrator (eye-iata-integration-step-is-weight-of-evidence) — the protection it buys is a function of who that person is, and the guideline generally does not say. Related, from the other side: machine-vision-does-not-remove-the-human-from-the-regulatory-call and a-trained-scorer-has-its-own-applicability-domain.

And it cuts against a specific comfortable move. "Automate the assay, keep a human on the call" is the default proposal for every automatable-open-loop method in this vault. This result says that move is only protective if the human is an expert in the thing being checked, and even then it leaks on precisely the low-salience, high-consequence details — a stir rate, a density term, an incubation time — which are exactly the parameters an assay's reproducibility depends on. Compare a-written-protocol-hides-twelve-watch-fors-and-forty-one-steps: the steps a protocol leaves unwritten are the same steps a supervising human does not think to check.

Limits, stated

One expert. The expert arm is a single systems engineer, so this is an anecdote with a direction, not a distribution. The non-expert arm is better powered but the paper reports the degradation qualitatively rather than with an effect size. The task is chemical protocol authoring, not assay interpretation, and the read-across to a toxicological call is an inference this vault is making, not a result the paper reports. evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, strength: single-study. funding_flag: none — a US national laboratory, code released openly, no vendor in the byline, and a result that makes their own system look less supervisable than the standard story. That is a mark in its favour.
