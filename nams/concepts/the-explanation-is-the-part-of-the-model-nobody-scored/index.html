The explanation is the part of the model nobody scored

What the model is, and what it is for

The blood–brain barrier is the unusually tight lining of the brain's blood vessels — sealed cell junctions, pumps that eject intruders, enzymes that destroy others in transit. It keeps most things in the bloodstream out of the brain.

That makes a permeability predictor a prioritisation filter, not a hazard test. A chemical that never reaches the brain cannot cause brain injury however alarming it looks in a dish of neurons; a chemical that does reach it earns the expensive neurotoxicity capacity. That is the classic multi-fidelity structure — a cheap computed signal in front of an expensive measurement — and it is why two unrelated groups published one in the same fortnight.

BBBProfiler's construction is good practice. An SE(3)-equivariant network encodes the molecule's three-dimensional shape in a way that is built to be unchanged by rotating or translating it, which is the right inductive bias for a physical property. A second network encodes collision cross-section — a measurable quantity from ion-mobility mass spectrometry, effectively how much space the molecule sweeps out as it tumbles — plus a standard substructure fingerprint. Learned gating weights fuse the two.

The numbers, and what each one does not say

  • 93.72% area under the curve. For a yes/no classifier this is the probability the model ranks a random crosser above a random non-crosser: about 94 times in 100 it gets the ordering right. It is a ranking score. It says nothing about where to put the cut-off, and nothing about whether the probabilities are calibrated.
  • 89.19% recall. Of the chemicals that really cross, it catches about 89 in 100 and misses about 11. For a filter placed in front of neurotoxicity testing, those eleven are screened out and never tested. Precision and specificity are not in the abstract, so how many safe non-crossers get wrongly promoted to expensive testing is unknown from the retrievable record.
  • R² = 0.72 for the quantitative version — roughly 72% of the variation in measured permeability accounted for, 28% not. Useful for ranking; not a number to put in a risk assessment.
  • 2,023 external chemicals at 94.42% area under the curve. An external set that large is a real strength; most published models in this space do not have one. It is also higher than the scaffold-split number, which is the opposite of the usual direction and is unexplained in the retrievable text.
  • Eleven chemicals in the wet-lab check. This is the number to keep. Eleven cannot support a sensitivity estimate, a specificity estimate, or anything else. It is a sanity check.

Why "scaffold split" is the load-bearing phrase

A scaffold split divides a chemical dataset into training and test halves by structural skeleton, so the test set contains molecular families the model never saw. The easy alternative — a random split — leaves near-copies of training molecules in the test set, and a model can score well by recognising them.

The vault ingested chushak-2026-bbb-permeability-ml-consensus on 2026-08-17, three days before this paper, on the same endpoint. Its central methodological result was that split choice inflates reported area under the curve by roughly 13% on blood–brain-barrier datasets specifically.

So the comparison a reader should make is not "94% beats whatever the other one got." It is: this group reported the hard split, which makes 93.72% a materially stronger claim than the same number under a random split — and it is still self-reported, by the model's authors, with no independent evaluation, and the construction of both the scaffold split and the external set is unread behind a paywall.

⚠ The part with no number on it

"Model predictions were supplemented with natural-language explanations generated from a large language model."

Every accuracy figure in this paper belongs to the predictor. There is, in the retrievable record, no faithfulness check, no expert agreement rate, and no statement of whether the explanation is derived from the model's actual feature attributions or generated afterwards from the prediction and the molecule. The substructure-mask attribution analysis is a separate, quantitative step; the natural-language layer sits on top of it and is not scored.

And the explanation is the part a human reads. Nobody looks at a gating weight. They look at the paragraph, and the paragraph is what they act on.

This vault has been assembling exactly the framework that applies here. The FDA credibility approach it tracks scales the evidence you owe a regulator by model influence × decision consequence — and an explanation a person reads and acts on has influence. Two recent vault findings make the concern concrete rather than theoretical:

  • 2026-08-06 — the loop's deciding leg got its first measurement of reasoning, and the finding was that a right answer can arrive with wrong reasoning. A correct prediction is not evidence for the story attached to it.
  • 2026-08-20 (tonight, from a different source) — the-model-did-not-say-the-evidence-was-missing: across 20,088 trials, models produced confident scientific answers about figures they had never been shown, 95% of the time without mentioning the absence. A language layer's fluency is uncorrelated with whether it has grounds.

An unscored natural-language layer sitting on a scored numeric one is a named and measured risk, not a hypothetical — and it is becoming the default packaging for computational new-approach methods.

Where it lands

Nothing moves. in-silico-qsar-read-across gains a data point, not a status change. This model is in no guideline and there is no reason it should be yet: regulatory_status: none, evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, strength: single-study.

One thing genuinely worth carrying forward: the model's most influential learned feature, collision cross-section, is a measurable quantity (R² = 0.84 against permeability on its own). A computational model whose dominant signal can be checked against an instrument is in a different position from one whose signal is a learned abstraction — it has an external handle. That is the kind of property that makes a computational-connective method auditable, which is the precondition for it being trusted inside a loop.
