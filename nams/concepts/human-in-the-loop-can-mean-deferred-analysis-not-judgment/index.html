The same three words mean two unrelated things in two literatures, and this vault must not conflate them

What this adds

shared/evidence-model.md's loop_readiness ladder defines human-in-the-loop as: "Trained human judgment is load-bearing in the call — morphological scoring, expert weight-of-evidence." RoboChem-Flex's own "HITL" workflow — quenching a reaction slug with a denaturing surfactant, storing it, and manually walking it to a shared UHPLC before feeding the resulting integral back into the same Bayesian-optimization agent that would otherwise read an in-line signal — involves a human doing physical labor, but the decision itself never touches human judgment. The optimizer picks every next experiment exactly as it would with an in-line detector; only the sample-transport step is manual, to avoid the cost of dedicated in-line instrumentation.

Why this matters for the vault's own vocabulary

If a future ingest encountered "human-in-the-loop self-driving lab" as a phrase without reading the mechanism behind it, the vault's own ladder would risk misgrading it as the judgment-load-bearing tier rather than as automatable-open-loop or closed-loop-ready machinery with a slower, cheaper measurement path. This concept note exists specifically so that mistake does not happen on this map or any sibling map that later encounters the same phrase from the self-driving-lab literature (as distinct from the vault's own toxicology usage, which has so far been consistent).
