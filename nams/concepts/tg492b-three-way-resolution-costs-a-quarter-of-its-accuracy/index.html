Read the corners of the matrix, not the diagonal

The number the guideline leads with. "balanced accuracy of 74.4% (based on 151 chemicals) with correct predictions of 79% for Cat 1 (based on 50 chemicals), 69% for Cat 2 (based on 44 chemicals) and 75% for No Cat (based on 57 chemicals)." Three-quarters right. Against TG 492's 95–99% sensitivity for its narrower job, and TG 494's 96%, that is the weakest adopted eye method the vault holds — on its headline.

The headline is the wrong place to look, because this is the vault's first three-class confusion matrix. For a binary rule-out there are two ways to be wrong and one of them barely matters. For a three-class call there are six, and they have completely different consequences.

True (in vivo rabbit)→ Cat 1→ Cat 2→ No Cat
Cat 1 — blinding (N=50)79.2%20.8%0%
Cat 2 — irritant (N=44)18.3%69.2%12.5%
No Cat — harmless (N=57)1.8%23.3%74.9%

The best cell in the table is the zero. Of fifty chemicals that cause irreversible eye damage in the rabbit, this method labelled none of them as needing no classification. All 21% of its Cat 1 errors are under-grading severity — Cat 1 called Cat 2 — which still puts a hazard warning on the product. The one error class that could blind somebody has zero instances in the validation set.

The worst cell is not on the diagonal either. 12.5% of true Category 2 irritants — about five and a half of forty-four chemicals — were called No Category. These are real irritants that this method says need no label at all. That is the failure with a route to a person's eye, and it is buried inside a "69% correct for Cat 2" summary.

And a quarter of harmless chemicals get labelled. 23.3% of true No-Cat chemicals came back Cat 2. Nobody is hurt; a formulator is told to add a warning that isn't warranted, and possibly to reformulate. This is the ordinary cost of a safety margin, and it is the cell that would drive commercial complaints.

Why the errors sit where they do. Both problem cells straddle the Cat 1 / Cat 2 and Cat 2 / No Cat boundaries, and both boundaries are defined on reversibility — a property the method explicitly cannot measure ("The reversibility of tissue lesions cannot be evaluated per se"). It infers persistence from how fast cells die. Chemicals whose speed of killing doesn't track their persistence are exactly where it lands one class off. See tg492b-three-way-eye-classification-from-exposure-time.

Named chemistry-specific weak spots, from GD 263: "Test was validated with a limited number of mixtures and insoluble Cat.1 solids are often underpredicted." Gases and aerosols were never assessed at all.

The loop consequence. A closed loop reading this method's output as a categorical label will inherit a one-in-eight false-reassurance rate on real irritants and have no signal that it happened — the method returns a confident class, not a confidence interval, and its uncertainty is not exposed in the output. The guideline's own borderline-retest rule (tg492b-decision-rule-contains-its-own-retest-trigger) catches values near the cut-off, which is a partial remedy: it will catch a chemical that lands at 48% viability, and it will not catch one that lands confidently in the wrong class.

And the funder ran the study. The validation was designed and executed by EPISKIN/L'Oréal, which manufactures the tissue — GD 263: "a prospective validation study conducted by industry." Independently peer-reviewed, three laboratories, OECD-adopted; not independently executed. funding_flag: [industry, method-developer].
