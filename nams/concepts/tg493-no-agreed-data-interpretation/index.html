An adopted assay with an unadopted decision rule

Paragraph 21 of TG 493, verbatim:

"There is currently no universally agreed method for interpreting ER binding data. However, both qualitative (e.g. binder/non-binder) and/or quantitative (e.g. log IC50, Relative Binding Affinity (RBA), etc.) assessments of hrER-mediated activity should be based on empirical data and sound scientific judgment."

An adopted OECD Test Guideline, in force since 2018 and corrected as recently as June 2024, standardises how to run the assay in exhaustive procedural detail — and then declines to say what the result means.

Why the vault should care, and not just note it. This vault has spent a fortnight learning that the regulatorily meaningful object in modern toxicology is the data-interpretation procedure, not the assay:

  • GL 497 is the sharpest case. One battery (DPRA + KeratinoSens + h-CLAT) answers three different regulatory questions — hazard (Part I, the 2o3), potency (Part II, the ITS), dose (Part III, the quantitative PoD) — under three different DIPs, and the guideline forbids chaining them. What carries the adopted standing is the DIP plus an admissible set of information sources. The assays are interchangeable parts.
  • TG 467 (eye) and GD 397 (phototoxicity IATA) are the same shape: the adopted thing is the procedure for combining results.
  • TG 455, same receptor as TG 493, ships a decision table: positive if RPC_Max ≥ 10% of the positive control in 2-of-2 or 2-of-3 runs.

TG 493 is the negative case that completes the pattern. Here the adopted object is a measurement with no call attached. Two laboratories can run TG 493 correctly, agree perfectly on the log(IC50), and disagree on whether the chemical is an ER binder, because the guideline supplies no threshold and explicitly hands the question to "sound scientific judgment."

What that does to the regulatory_status axis. OECD-TG-adopted has been the vault's top acceptance grade, and it has been read — reasonably — as "there is an agreed, harmonised way to test this and to act on the result." TG 493 shows the grade is really two claims bundled together:

  1. an agreed way to generate the number (TG 493: yes), and
  2. an agreed way to turn the number into a decision (TG 493: no).

The axis collapses these. Most adopted TGs carry both; TG 493 carries only the first. It is still genuinely adopted, still genuinely useful, and its measurement is about as physically unambiguous as toxicology gets — but a downstream user who assumed OECD-TG-adopted meant "harmonised decision" would be wrong about this guideline, and the vault's own index would have told them so.

Filed as an open question about the model, alongside tg455-vm7luc-adopted-but-unrunnable and fda-fit-for-purpose-acceptance-without-validation — three findings this run, from three different sources, all saying the same thing from different directions: the acceptance axis is measuring something coarser than what it is being read to mean.
