A toxicokinetic model decided which in-vitro hits counted

Trace the decision path that produced this study's conclusion, because the interesting part is not the toxicology.

  1. Seventeen mostly-automated plate assays returned numbers. Sixteen were quiet.
  2. One was not: the metabolite moved the rat network-formation assay, with a half-maximal effect concentration between roughly 96 and 150 micromolar.
  3. A physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBK) model — a computer model of how a dose travels through the body and what concentration it actually reaches in tissue — was consulted, and said that concentration is not reached at the doses that produced the ambiguous animal findings.
  4. A person decided the gap was big enough.

No assay's decision rule appears anywhere in that chain. The measurement was automated. The one non-trivial number was real. What disposed of it was a second model plus an unquantified judgement about sufficiency.

Why this is a sharper statement of the blocker than the vault had

Since 2026-07-20 this map's loop_blocker has read "no fixed data-interpretation procedure — the battery is integrated by expert IATA judgement." True, and slightly abstract. This supplies the concrete shape:

  • Automating the wet work does not reach it (Tox21 proved that in 2013).
  • Automating the scoring does not reach it either — six of seven readouts on this battery's flagship assay are already scored end-to-end by two convolutional neural networks, and the tier did not move.
  • And automating the interpretation would require the loop to carry a toxicokinetic model in its inner loop, then apply a margin nobody has published.

The vault already grades that model layer: pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics is computational-connective — not an assay, but the model layer a loop would update. This case study is the first worked example in the vault where that layer is load-bearing in an individual chemical's call, not merely a bridge between in-vitro potency and human dose in principle.

What a loop would actually need

Three things, and only the first exists:

  1. The assays. Present, largely automated.
  2. A toxicokinetic model for the specific chemical. Present here because somebody built one for this compound. Not present in general, and building one is a research task per chemical, not a lookup.
  3. A published rule for how large the in-vitro-to-in-vivo concentration margin must be to dismiss a hit. Absent. This is the same missing object as everywhere else on this map, now located precisely.

The uncomfortable direction: if the margin rule were written down, this becomes automatable — and the automated version would be dismissing positive results on the strength of a model whose own uncertainty is not in the rule. A loop that iterates on "the model says the concentration is unreachable, discard" will discard confidently and fast, and its errors will all point the same way.

Honest brakes. One chemical, one metabolite, one case study, a negative outcome. Full text unobtainable, so the PBK model's parameters, its uncertainty treatment, and the margin the authors considered sufficient are all unread — the last of those is the number this note most wants and does not have. The registrant of the chemical and the vendor of the battery are both co-authors.
