The vault grades the assay. The robot fails before the assay starts.

This vault has spent fourteen months asking one question of every method it holds: could a machine run it? The loop_readiness ladder answers that with two tests — is the readout machine-readable, and is there a fixed rule for turning the readout into a call. Both tests are about the end of the experiment.

Nothing in the schema asks whether a machine can get the right biological material into the plate.

For three of the vault's methods that step is identical and it is the same step: pull several thousand zebrafish embryos out of a tank, count them, and discard the ones that are dead, unfertilised or the wrong genotype. fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet (an adopted OECD Test Guideline), developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity and developmental-neurotoxicity-zebrafish-behavior-ldtt all start there. It is the least interesting sentence in every protocol and, for a loop, it is load-bearing: an autonomous system that cannot stock its own plates is not autonomous, it is a well-instrumented assistant.

A commercial machine now does that step, and somebody who bought it measured it.

Note what kind of error 13.41% is. It is not noise — it is systematic and one-directional, and it comes with nearly three times the variance. The authors attribute the variance to "embryo density, debris interference, and orientation" — three properties of the sample, none of them a property of the chemistry anyone is trying to measure.

Why this belongs in a toxicology vault and not in a facilities newsletter

A miscount is an unrecorded change in n. Every dose-response curve, every median-lethal-concentration estimate, every "percentage of larvae showing an effect" divides by a number the machine supplied. If that denominator is short by one in seven and the shortfall varies with how crowded the sample was, the assay's statistics are being computed against a quantity that drifts with sample handling rather than with dose.

And the drift is invisible from inside the loop, in exactly the way the vault recorded on 2026-08-12 for culture medium and cell-line identity: a closed loop's founding assumption is that a change in the readout means a change in the chemistry. Embryo density is not in the loop's data model. Debris is not in the loop's data model. The loop sees a number and attributes it to the compound.

What this does NOT say

  • It does not say lab robots do not work. It says this device, at this software version, out of the box, in one facility, performs this way — and the same paper cites two other groups doing better on adjacent zebrafish tasks (97.9% success on a robotic handling system; 87-95% on mutant-versus-wild-type discrimination). The ceiling is not established here.
  • It does not move any assay map's loop_readiness tier. The tier grades readout and decision rule. This is upstream of both, which is precisely why it needed automation-specimen-handling to be coined rather than filed against an existing axis.
  • It is not a validation study and there is no toxicology in it. No chemical, no dose, no endpoint. The implication for the three zebrafish assay maps is this vault's inference from the handling step.

The honest counterweight, which is real

The machine does the counting and the quality classification in one pass; the technician was only counting, because morphological grading at 3-4 hours post-fertilisation is unreliable by eye anyway. And on the fluorescence-sorting task — separating glowing transgenic embryos from their non-glowing siblings, the job nobody enjoys — the machine is genuinely faster than either technician. The authors' recommendation is context-dependent adoption, and it is the right shape: automate the high-volume routine pass, keep humans on the accuracy-critical work. That recommendation is also, read as a loop statement, an admission — the step is automatable at the throughput layer and not at the correctness layer, which is the identical split this vault records at the other end of the pipeline, where thirty methods run robotically and stop for a human before the call.
