One destination, several roads, and the battery is watching some of the roads

The vault's steatosis method (hepatotoxicity-steatosis-battery) works by measuring seven upstream key events in a liver cell line — fat accumulation, fatty-acid uptake, mitochondrial fat burning, lipid secretion, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic-reticulum stress, oxidative stress — and combining them into one "steatogenic score." Which events to measure was chosen from a published causal pathway, which the vault recorded as an advance over choosing them from whatever assays were available.

This source says the road map has more roads than the battery watches.

The conserved core is real: PFAS-driven liver steatosis converges on a shared mechanism. What differs is how chemicals get there — the upstream route depends on the individual compound's physical chemistry and on the biological context. And the context that turns out to matter most is the one nobody was measuring: immune-related processes are central across multiple contexts, established here partly with new transcriptomic profiles from macrophages, the tissue immune cells the liver is full of and that a hepatocyte-only battery contains none of.

What follows for a battery, stated carefully

This is not a claim that the steatosis battery is wrong. It is a claim about what its negatives mean. A chemical that reaches the conserved core by a route none of the seven readouts covers produces seven quiet numbers and a low steatogenic score — and the score is not wrong about what it measured. It never measured the route that was taken.

The vault has an exact precedent and should name it. The developmental-neurotoxicity battery carries the vault's first biological applicability-domain gate: it is blind to two named signalling mechanisms, and its neuronal-migration readout fired for zero of eighteen probe pathways. The lesson recorded there was that a loop cannot measure which pathway a chemical acts through before deciding whether to trust the answer. This is the same shape, arriving on the liver, from a data-integration study rather than from a mechanistic probe.

The specific gap this opens

The steatosis map's domain_gate currently says the applicability domain is undefined in every direction that matters. This source lets the vault say something sharper than "undefined": there is at least one named, biologically motivated route into the endpoint — the immune one — that the battery's cell system cannot host at all, because the cells are not there.

That is a coverage statement, not a performance statement, and the difference matters. Nothing here measures how often the immune route is the operative one, or how many of the tested chemicals it would have changed the call on. Those numbers do not exist. What exists is a reason to expect that the battery's false-negative rate is structured rather than random — concentrated in chemicals whose action runs through cells the battery does not contain.

Not a correlated_blind_spot flag. The rail fires when two methods in one battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason. Here there is one method with a coverage hole. The distinction is kept deliberately: the rail's value is that it means one specific thing, and a coverage gap is a different object recorded in domain_gate.
