Human glucocorticoid receptor transactivation (OECD TG 454A) → endocrine disruption (non-EATS)

The glucocorticoid receptor (GR) is a ligand-activated nuclear-receptor transcription factor. Bound by cortisol or a synthetic glucocorticoid (dexamethasone, prednisolone), it homodimerises, binds glucocorticoid response elements (GREs) in target-gene DNA, and modulates transcription — governing stress response, inflammation, immune function and metabolism. TG 454A's test system is HMLN-hGR: an immortalised human HeLa line stably transfected with an MMTV-luciferase GRE reporter plus a GRα expression plasmid (pSG5-hGR). Luminescence reports transactivation, in agonist mode (chemical alone) and antagonist mode (chemical against a fixed agonist concentration, typically at ~80% of maximal induction).

Both axes adopted: evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 454A, published 2 July 2026; proposed by France and Canada, in the OECD workplan since 2022), with formally-validated reproducibility underneath from an independent blinded four-laboratory ring trial (Rivero-Arze et al. 2025, PMID 40683478) and an OECD peer review of that validation running October 2024 – February 2025.

What makes it a new column rather than a deeper one. OECD endocrine testing has been organised around the EATS modalities — Estrogen, Androgen, Thyroid, Steroidogenesis. This vault's endocrine entry to date has been the S: h295r-steroidogenesis-endocrine-predictivity (TG 456). The guideline states the GR "was identified as playing a key role in several non-EATS endocrine pathways" and that OECD recommended GR transactivation assays for its Conceptual Framework as early as 2012. TG 454A is therefore the first adopted OECD in-vitro method for a non-EATS receptor — a different axis of endocrine biology, not a better probe of an existing one.

Read the ring-trial numbers correctly — this is the two-axis trap in miniature. 97% inter-laboratory and 99.6% intra-laboratory concordance measure whether laboratories agree with each other. They are not a sensitivity/specificity against a reference set of known human GR-active chemicals, and they are emphatically not a predictivity against any apical in-vivo endocrine outcome. They are excellent reproducibility figures — among the tightest in this vault — and reproducibility is a precondition for predictivity, not evidence of it. No predictivity figure exists in the sources reached.

A specific, documented false-positive mechanism. The guideline warns that "some chemicals suspected to have an antagonistic effect were shown to be interfering chemicals and not specific GR antagonists." A chemical that inhibits luciferase, or is simply cytotoxic, depresses the signal exactly as a competitive antagonist does. The method requires an explicit discrimination step. The antagonist call is therefore structurally weaker than the agonist call — a limitation generic to reporter-gene antagonist modes, and named here rather than left implicit.

Scope. OECD Conceptual Framework Level 2 — "in vitro assays providing data about selected endocrine mechanism(s)/pathway(s)." A mechanism screen. It says a chemical engages the human GR; it does not say the chemical causes endocrine harm. funding_flag: method-developer records the HeLa-derived reporter line's academic-developer origin and the PEPPER-coordinated (public-private) ring trial; the weight rests on the blinded multi-laboratory design and the independent OECD peer review, not on a developer's self-validation.
