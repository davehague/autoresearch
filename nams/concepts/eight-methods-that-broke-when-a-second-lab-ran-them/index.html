The pattern, and why it decides an argument the vault is holding

Not one of these causes is biology. They are: an off-target receptor nobody had characterised; a plastic dish's sterilisation; a shipping delay; an undeclared local protocol tweak; a metabolic step that only some labs' preparations perform. Every one is invisible inside a single laboratory, and — this is the part that matters — invisible to any amount of better statistics applied to that laboratory's own data. More replicates in one lab will not find a sterilisation difference between two dish suppliers, because within that lab there is only one supplier.

That is the technical content of the claim that interlaboratory reproducibility is the one rung on the vault's ladder that cannot be manufactured by writing a better paper. formally-validated differs from peer-reviewed-method on exactly this property, and this is the evidence for why it is the right place to draw the line.

Two direct hits on things the vault already holds

The androgen-receptor case is the vault's own blind-spot row, seen from the validation side. The vault records endocrine-er-transactivation ↔ endocrine-ar-transactivation ↔ endocrine-glucocorticoid-receptor-transactivation as a correlated blind spot: shared MMTV promoter, each assay having to engineer away a glucocorticoid-receptor response, "receptor selectivity is built, not natural." Jacobs et al. record the same fact as a transfer failure — the GR cross-talk was what made the AR assay unreproducible between labs, and the knockout was the fix. Same biology, two different consequences: a shared vulnerability across the battery, and a variance problem within each assay. The ring trial is what surfaced it.

The 3T3 NRU case is a live method in this vault. phototoxicity-3t3-nru is an adopted OECD Test Guideline (TG 432). A laboratory experienced enough to be in a validation study had silently modified the SOP, and it took multi-lab work to notice. Nothing about being adopted protects a method from that.

The consequence for an autonomous laboratory

A closed loop is, structurally, one laboratory running very fast. Every failure in the table above is a between-laboratory variable, and a loop has none — it has one set of dishes, one shipping route, one instrument configuration, one local protocol. It can generate 36,000 datapoints and still be blind to all eight of these causes, because none of them varies inside it.

Worse: high internal reproducibility is what a loop optimises toward, and it is exactly the signal that looked "encouraging" in the ER CALUX lead laboratory before the method failed to transfer. A loop's consistency is not evidence that it is right. It is evidence that its unknown biases are stable.
