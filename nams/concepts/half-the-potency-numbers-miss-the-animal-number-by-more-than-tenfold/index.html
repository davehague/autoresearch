Half the potency numbers miss the animal number by more than tenfold

"Moderate concordance" is an accurate phrase and a soft one. Unpacked: for the gut-lining cell line, about half the estimates are wrong by more than a factor of ten against the chronic animal study; for the liver line, about three in ten are.

Two things have to be said at once about that, and the vault's discipline is not to collapse them.

It is not obviously bad. A point of departure is not the final safety number — regulators divide it by uncertainty factors that are themselves often tenfold or hundredfold. And the direction reported is conservative: the short-term in-vitro values tend to land low, which errs toward caution. For screening and prioritisation, which is the scope the authors claim, an order of magnitude is a usable resolution.

And the comparator is not truth. This is agreement with a rodent chronic study, and the vault now holds a direct measurement of how noisy that reference is: EFSA's 2026 review of 461 approved pesticides found that differences of up to threefold between two whole-animal studies of the same substance are ordinary experimental and biological variability (the-second-species-was-mostly-measuring-experimental-noise). A method scored against a single animal study is being scored against a number with that much slop in it before anything else goes wrong. Concordance with the animal is a transfer test, not an accuracy test.

⚠ The unexplained result

The more concordant of the two cell lines is HepG2, a liver carcinoma line with famously weak drug-metabolising enzyme expression — a limitation this vault has already recorded against it in a liver-automation context, where a robot produced a clean acetaminophen dose-response in HepG2 despite acetaminophen's toxicity being caused by a metabolite the cells barely make. Pesticides are a chemical class in which metabolic activation is common. That the metabolically weaker model agreed better with the whole animal is not explained in the abstract, and three readings are available with no way to choose between them from what is published: the transcriptomic response is dominated by parent-compound stress rather than metabolite toxicity; the liver-tissue origin matters more than the enzyme deficit; or the difference is noise across nineteen chemicals.

The number nobody has

There is no published criterion stating what concordance a transcriptomic point of departure must reach to be used in place of an animal study. The performance is reported against a target that does not exist. This is the same gap the vault recorded in a different shape when a kidney battery's authors declined on principle to publish a decision rule from three compounds, and when the European Union's validation laboratory said a solid data-interpretation procedure cannot be designed until more potency data exists. Here the data exists and the criterion still does not.
