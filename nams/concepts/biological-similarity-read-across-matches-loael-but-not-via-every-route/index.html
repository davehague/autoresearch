Biological-similarity read-across lands close on the direct route, far off on the reverse-dosimetry route

islam-2026-nam-read-across-conazole-case-study extends this map's read-across method class with a variant not previously recorded here: instead of (or alongside) structural fingerprints, the nearest-neighbour analogue selection is weighted by Tanimoto similarity over a high-throughput biological-response profile (CALUX® nuclear-receptor reporters, ToxProfiler, Cell Painting PLUS, primary-human-hepatocyte transcriptomics) rather than molecular structure alone — "the explicit application of kNN to HT biological response profiles ... for regulatory data gap filling" is the paper's own stated novel contribution.

The numbers, read against each other rather than cherry-picked. On the target chemical difenoconazole, read across from eight conazole analogues plus two metabolites:

  • Direct kNN prediction of the LOAEL: 6.57 mg/kg/day, against a true in-vivo value of 4.88 mg/kg/day — about 35% high, within the two-fold band the paper treats as a success.
  • The same kNN model, routed through PBK-based reverse dosimetry (predict an unbound concentration, convert to a dose): over-predicted by roughly 17-fold. The paper's own diagnosis: "compounded uncertainty from HT-PBK outputs and fraction unbound estimates" — each modelling step in the chain adds error, and chaining them compounds it rather than averaging it out.
  • A worst-case scenario (the single most conservative source compound, itraconazole) through the same reverse-dosimetry route landed within six-fold — better than the full-kNN reverse-dosimetry route, worse than the direct prediction.
  • The final NAM-derived Human Equivalent Dose came out roughly ten-fold lower (more conservative) than the traditional-approach LOAEL — the opposite direction from the 17-fold over-prediction above, because HED derivation used the minimum point-of-departure across assays deliberately, and the underlying PBK model is known (cited against the broader IVIVE literature) to under-predict hepatic clearance for low-clearance compounds like conazoles, which inflates predicted internal concentration and therefore lowers the back- calculated safe external dose. Two different numbers, two different directions, one workflow — a reader taking either number alone would characterise the method's accuracy in the opposite direction from the other.

What this adds to the map's standing split-axes picture. This map's status: consolidating rests on one narrow adopted domain (ICH M7, bacterial mutagenicity of pharmaceutical impurities) and treats everything else as a within-weight-of-evidence supporting tool. This case study is squarely in the "everything else" bucket — one proof-of-concept, one chemical class (conazoles), no regulatory submission, no stated concordance criterion for when a read-across prediction counts as close enough. Status does not move. It does add a second species of the map's standing applicability-domain finding (the-applicability-domain-check-cannot-see-an-activity-cliff): here the risk is not an undetected activity cliff but route-dependent error amplification — the same similarity-weighted analogue selection gives acceptable or wildly-off answers purely depending on which downstream calculation (direct LOAEL vs. reverse dosimetry) is applied to it, and the paper reports no rule for choosing between routes beyond citing which one is known to be noisier.

Honest ceiling. One case study, one chemical class, nine source/target chemicals.
