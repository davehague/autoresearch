Six in ten published (Q)SAR hazard models cannot be independently rerun — a third, much larger, independent measurement of the same gap

strength: strong, unusually for this map: not a perspective and not a ~50%-in-the-abstract audit, but a pre-registered-shaped systematic review (Web of Science, documented screening funnel, 274 publications kept of 2,433 screened) that reports its own denominator and a category-by-category breakdown, on one specific, regulator-relevant model family — (Q)SAR — rather than toxicology machine learning in general.

The third independent derivation of one structural claim

ml-toxicology-interpretation-and-code-reporting-gaps already named this map's central finding — Kappenberg et al.'s ~50% interpretation-method reporting rate across the 2022–2024 toxicology-ML literature — as the second independent derivation of Hartung's argued claim that "opaque AI pipelines risk magnifying the [reproducibility] problem." This is the third, and it is the strongest-shaped of the three: a domain-scoped systematic review with a documented search strategy, a stated screening funnel, and a denominator large enough (274 publications, 639 model instances) to break down by endpoint and by algorithm family. Hartung argued from principle; Kappenberg counted toxicology ML broadly and reported one number in an abstract; Piir et al. counted (Q)SAR specifically, reported the full funnel, and found the same order of magnitude — a majority of published models cannot be checked by anyone who was not in the room when they were built.

What "non-usable" means in practice, and why it is not a documentation nitpick

The 60.9% figure is not "the paper's writing was unclear." Non-usable means neither a machine-readable representation (PMML, ONNX) nor enough reported detail — descriptor names and software versions, model parameters, predicted values for the training and validation sets — exists to rebuild the model from the paper at all. There is, for six publications in ten, no way to get from the PDF to a running model without contacting the authors — and the paper does not check whether authors respond, so even that fallback is untested. This is the same shape cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia and in-silico-qsar-read-across have already logged individually — a working neural-network classifier "available… upon reasonable request," a cardiac ion-channel model served only from its developer's login-gated server — generalized here to a majority-case property of an entire model family rather than two isolated instances.

The honest ceiling, twice over

First: "potentially usable" (21.9%) is graded on whether the paper could be rebuilt from what it reports — the paper states plainly that whether these actually reproduce was not checked. The 17.2%+21.9% "usable" combined figure (39.1%) is therefore an upper bound on reproducibility, not a measurement of it. Second: this measures whether a model can be run, never whether the model that runs is right. A directly-usable web service could still be a bad predictor; a non-usable paper could describe an excellent one nobody can check. Usability and correctness are independent, and the paper does not conflate them — neither does this note.

Why usability tracked reporting discipline, not algorithm choice

Across the five major algorithm families reported (tree-based, SVM, ANN, instance-based/read-across, naive Bayes), the directly-usable proportion held in a narrow 14–20% band regardless of family. The variable that moved the number was endpoint and publication norm, not model architecture — skin sensitization (best proportion) already has established public web-service infrastructure (SkinSensDB, STopTox) that the field defaults toward; genotoxicity (worst proportion, despite the most publications) does not. This is a field where the habit of publishing a usable artifact, not the difficulty of building one, is the limiting factor.

What this adds for a loop, beyond restating the reporting gap

regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility's framing — model risk = model influence × decision consequence — asks a model to justify its influence on demand. An autonomous loop selecting among published models to update its own decision rule inherits this exact usability distribution: roughly six times in ten, the model it might want to adopt cannot be independently instantiated at all, only cited. The loop is left choosing among the minority of models that happen to have shipped a web service or a complete specification — a selection filter correlated with publication habit and endpoint, not with which model is actually best.
