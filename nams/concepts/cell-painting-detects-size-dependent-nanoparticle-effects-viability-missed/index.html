A morphological fingerprint finds a nanoparticle size effect a viability assay cannot see

phenotypic-profiling-cell-painting already holds this vault's account of Cell Painting as an exploration-leg, not decision-leg candidate: a method that produces a point of departure without naming a mechanism, and is more sensitive than standard cytotoxicity readouts on at least one validated dataset (primary human hepatocytes, 2026-09-27). This source applies the same technique to nanomaterials for the first time in this vault, and the size-dependence finding is the kind of result the method's whole design argument predicts.

What was done

Titanium dioxide nanoparticles, in two size fractions (under 25 nanometres, under 100 nanometres), dosed onto HepG2 human liver cancer cells for 24 hours at five concentrations. A standard viability assay ran alongside the full Cell Painting pipeline — fixed fluorescent dye panel marking cell compartments, automated high-content microscopy, machine-learning feature extraction into a morphological fingerprint.

What was found

The viability assay showed only modest reductions even at the highest dose, for either particle size — by the cheap, conventional readout, neither material looks very toxic. Cell Painting told a different story: concentration-dependent phenotypic changes appeared for both sizes, and the effect was both stronger and more consistent for the larger (<100 nm) particles — roughly 51% of measured morphological features altered at the highest dose, against roughly 29% for the smaller (<25 nm) fraction.

Read the direction carefully — it runs opposite to a naive "smaller is always worse" nanotoxicology intuition. The authors report the larger of the two size fractions produced the broader morphological signature, not the smaller. This vault draws no general rule from a two-size, one-material, one-cell-line comparison; it is recorded as the specific, size-ordered finding this paper reports, not generalised.

Why this lands on Cell Painting rather than opening a nanomaterial method map

This is an application of an existing method (phenotypic-profiling-cell-painting) to a new material class, not a new assay. It composes with nanomaterial-nam-landscape (the vault's landscape map for the whole nanomaterial-NAM domain) as a concrete instance of one of that map's named enabling technologies — Cell Painting is not named there specifically, but high-content phenotypic imaging is exactly the category of tool that landscape review was built to catalogue.

The domain_gate already on this map (variability is a property of the operator, not the test item) gains a nanomaterial-specific companion question it does not yet answer: nanomaterial-nam-landscape's own domain_gate names particle physical state (size distribution, surface coating, agglomeration, dissolution rate) as unfixed between preparation and the well — a property no existing Cell Painting source states it controlled for. Logged as an open question, not assumed either way.

The honest ceiling
