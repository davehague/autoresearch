The applied side names the missing decision rule, without knowing it is talking about robots

This vault's most repeated finding is a negative: twenty-four of its method maps carry a loop_blocker that amounts to "no agreed procedure converts the number this method produces into the call it is supposed to inform." Not "the assay is bad" — the assay often runs beautifully. Nobody wrote down what the answer means.

A critical review of nine published cosmetic-ingredient risk assessments lists what the field still needs:

"Future advancement still requires integration of metabolically competent models, artificial intelligence-driven prediction tools, micro-physiological systems using multi-organs, and standardized frameworks for interpreting NAM-derived points of departure."

A point of departure is the dose at the bottom of the toxic response — the number every downstream safety limit is divided down from. So the fourth item reads: there is no standard way to interpret the numbers that non-animal methods produce.

Why this is worth a note rather than a footnote

Because of where it comes from. The review is about cosmetics safety assessment. It cites no self-driving laboratory, no agentic AI, no lab robotics. Its authors are pharmacology and pharmacy academics writing for a toxicology review journal about industrial case studies.

And it arrives at the same object this vault reached from the automation side. The vault's version, from shared/evidence-model.md, is a question about machines: does the guideline publish a fixed, deterministic data-interpretation procedure? The review's version is a question about people: is there a standardised framework for interpreting these numbers? Same missing artefact, two constituencies, no citation between them.

That pattern now has five instances in this vault, and it is the strongest form of support the vault recognises — independent derivation. The others: the US Food and Drug Administration pricing model credibility by how alone the model is; the ICCVAM validation framework; the toxicologic pathology profession's roundtable naming absent validation standards; and a 28-author autonomous-science roadmap promoting verification from a cross-cutting concern to a first-class dimension. Five constituencies, five vocabularies, one hole.

The one that cuts the other way

The review's second requirement is AI-driven prediction tools — i.e. the applied side is asking for more model layer while also asking for a rule to interpret what the models say. Those two asks are in tension, and the review does not notice: adding a prediction tool does not supply an interpretation rule, and this vault has now measured that twice. A structure-only model matched a 49-target robotic assay panel and nobody could say which reading of that was right; a semantic knowledge graph raised a model's ranking score on 33 of 37 assays without improving the metrics a decision runs on (the-metric-that-improved-is-not-the-one-a-decision-uses).

A better predictor is not a decision procedure, and an autonomous loop needs the second one.
