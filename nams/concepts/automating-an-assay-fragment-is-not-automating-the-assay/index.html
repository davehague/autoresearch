Automating a fragment of an assay is not automating the assay — and the fragment is usually the easy part

This is a generalisable caution the vault should carry forward, prompted by the droplet Ames test (droplet-ames-removes-the-person-who-counts-colonies) but not specific to it.

The Ames test's shape, and what the demonstration covered

OECD Test Guideline 471 requires at least five bacterial strains at at least five concentrations, each run with and without S9 metabolic activation. Those three requirements are not padding; each one is a distinct detector:

  • Multiple strains because different strains catch different classes of mutation. TA98 — the one used here — detects frameshift mutations, where a letter is inserted or deleted and the whole downstream reading frame shifts. Base-substitution mutations, where one letter is swapped, need different strains. A single-strain result is a single detector's result.
  • ± S9 because a great many chemicals are harmless as supplied and mutagenic only after a liver has modified them. S9 is homogenised rat liver tipped into the well to supply that metabolism, because bacteria do not have it. Without the S9 arm, an assay that reports "not mutagenic" is reporting "not mutagenic as supplied."

The droplet work demonstrates one strain, a proper dose-response, and — critically — says nothing about S9 either way.

Why this note exists rather than a stronger claim

The abstract does not state that S9 was omitted. It does not mention S9 at all, the full text is paywalled, and the honest record is an open question rather than an accusation. stance: open-question is the correct grade and the vault should not upgrade it without the full text.

But the question has to be asked, because the answer determines whether a known blind spot is being rebuilt. This vault already records two assay pairs that fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, and both failures are the missing-metabolism failure: the ROS photoreactivity assay ↔ 3T3 NRU phototoxicity pair (a photo-inert parent with a photo-reactive metabolite walks through the whole phototoxicity assessment untouched), and the FET ↔ RTgill-W1 fish pair. If a high-throughput genotoxicity format cannot carry S9, then its cheapness is concentrated exactly where the assay is least informative — and a screening cascade that runs the cheap format first, at scale, on thousands of chemicals, will filter out bioactivation-dependent mutagens before anything expensive ever sees them.

That is the loop version of the problem, and it is worse than the human version. A person reading a single-strain no-S9 result knows to treat it as a screen. An automated system that has been given a threshold rule and a throughput advantage has every incentive to treat it as an answer, and will iterate on it — fast, confidently, and in one direction.

The rule to carry

When a source claims an assay has been automated, ask which fraction of the Test Guideline was automated, and whether the automated fraction is the discriminating one. Reagent savings and turnaround times are measured on whatever configuration was built; they say nothing about coverage. This is a distinct question from the vault's three axes and currently has nowhere to live in the frontmatter schema — logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md.
