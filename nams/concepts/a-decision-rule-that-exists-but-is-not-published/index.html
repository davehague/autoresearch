A fifth blocker shape: the rule is not missing, it is private

The vault's four blocker shapes, before tonight

  1. No agreed decision rule — 14 maps. The assay is machine-readable; nobody has published a rule. Tox21's state since 2013.
  2. Human judgement is load-bearing in the call — 5 maps. Split on 2026-07-25 into blocked at the scoring step (machine vision is the escape hatch) and blocked after it (no image classifier reaches it).
  3. Architecturally bench-bound — 2 maps, for two different reasons (animal-input vs no-nam-exists).
  4. Access gates — not on any axis, and they decide everything.

The fifth

This platform's rule is not missing. It is written, mechanical, and demonstrably effective:

four multielectrode-array features (beat period, rate-corrected field potential duration, spike amplitude, beating irregularity) → trained ensemble → risk score, threshold 0.5 → high / intermediate / low Torsades de Pointes risk.

No human scores anything. Median ROC-AUC 0.982. By the vault's deterministic ladder — machine-readable readout and a fixed data-interpretation procedure — that is a closed-loop-ready shape.

It is not closed-loop-ready, and the reason is one line in the declarations:

"The datasets generated and/or analyzed in the current study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request."

The weights, the training data, and the code are not published. A second laboratory cannot obtain the rule. It can obtain permission to ask for it.

Why "published" is load-bearing and not pedantry

Compare the vault's existing top-tier rules:

RuleWhere it lives
"viability ≤ 50%"OECD TG 439, a public document
2-out-of-3 concordanceOECD GL 497, a public document
three index thresholdsOECD TG 494, a public document
a rate-corrected field-potential neural networkan author's hard drive

The first three are rules a stranger can read and implement identically, forever, without contacting anybody. That is what makes them fixed in the sense the tier means: not merely "written down somewhere" but recoverable and reimplementable by an independent party. A trained model that has not been deposited is not a fixed procedure the field possesses; it is a service one laboratory can perform.

The distinction this adds to the axis

The vault has been accumulating ways a rule fails to be what closed-loop-ready implies. This is the fourth axis of failure and the most mundane:

PropertyQuestionFailure case in the vault
Fixedis it written down and applied mechanically?AI rules that retrain (2026-07-19)
Deterministicsame input, same output?a stochastic MCMC estimator (2026-07-24)
Sample-independentdoes the call depend only on this sample?TGx-DDI's unsupervised legs (2026-07-22)
Obtainablecan an independent party get the rule and run it?this platform (2026-07-26)

obtainable is not a scientific property at all. It is a publication practice — and it is the one that determines whether a rule is infrastructure or a product.

The connection to the vault's other modifier

This is access_gate reaching the decision layer. The vault records access gates on cells (VM7Luc4E2, HepaRG, SkinEthic™ HCE), kits (Corrositex), instruments (the Amnis ImageStream) and licences (Derek Nexus). Here both halves are gated at once: the cells are NEXEL's commercial Cardiosight®-S product and the classifier is NEXEL's unreleased model. The company that sells the cells owns the rule that interprets them.

And it is the same phenomenon single-instrument-methods-are-becoming-de-facto-standards describes one layer down — the European Commission's JRC reporting that commercial analysis software carries "the proprietary nature of the used algorithms with little insight of their precise makeup." Two sources tonight, in different fields, on the same problem: the software that turns a measurement into an answer is increasingly a trade secret.

Status and honesty

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop, loop_blocker set to the unpublished rule. evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory — one laboratory, no ring trial, and the laboratory is the vendor. regulatory_status: none.

None of this says the model is wrong. 0.982 on held-out compounds is a good number and the applied result (catching two cancer drugs that a viability assay missed) is a real finding. What it says is that nobody outside the company can check it, reproduce it, or run it — and that is a different deficiency from the one the vault's largest blocker category describes.
