The one in-vivo test that reads this endpoint is triggered one time in six

TG 443 (the Extended One-Generation Reproductive Toxicity Study) is the only OECD Test Guideline the review connects to developmental immunotoxicity, and only through an optional add-on — Cohort 3 — that measures the T-cell-Dependent Antibody Response (TDAR, see glossary) in offspring exposed during development. Under REACH, roughly 380 EOGRTS had been run by 2022; only about 60 (~16%) included Cohort 3.

This is the same shape of finding as regulatory-acceptance-japan-pmda-nams (an adopted requirement, rarely triggered in practice) and dao-sadrieh-2026-cder-nam-submission-landscape (a regulator counting what actually arrives, not what the rules allow) — except here the gap runs the other direction: it is an animal test, not a NAM, that is under-used, and there is no non-animal alternative to substitute when it isn't run. The developmental-immunotoxicity endpoint is not just NAM-poor; it is evidence-poor even on the animal side, because the one study designed to generate developmental-immune data is usually filed without the arm that would generate it.
