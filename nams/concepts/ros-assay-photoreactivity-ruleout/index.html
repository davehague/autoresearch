The ROS assay (OECD TG 495): 42% specificity is the design, not the defect

TG 495 is tier 2 of the phototoxicity IATA the vault mapped on 2026-07-11 (phototoxicity-iata, GD 397): TG 101 (UV-VIS absorption / MEC gate) → TG 495 (this) → TG 432 (3T3 NRU screen) → TG 498 (RhE topical confirmation). It is cell-free — chemistry in a buffer, a solar simulator, and two colorimetric probes.

What it measures, precisely. Photoreactivity: the generation of reactive oxygen species when the chemical absorbs light. Two pathways, two probes:

  • type II (energy transfer → singlet oxygen) — bleaching of p-nitrosodimethylaniline (RNO);
  • type I (electron/hydrogen transfer → superoxide anion, free radicals) — reduction of nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT).

Photoreactivity is the molecular initiating event. Phototoxicity is the adverse outcome. The guideline is disciplined about the difference: "the ROS assay was designed for qualitative photoreactivity assessment of chemicals." A TG 495 positive means the molecule makes ROS in the light. Whether that harms skin is what tiers 3 and 4 are for.

The number that matters, and how to read it

MetricValue
Sensitivity100%
Specificity (per laboratory)42–82%
Positive predictivity75–92%
Negative predictivity100%

(42 coded chemicals: 23 phototoxins, 19 non-phototoxic. No false negatives.)

A 42% specificity read as a stand-alone classifier is a disaster: more than half the safe chemicals get flagged. But TG 495 is never used as a stand-alone classifier. It occupies a tier where a negative closes the assessment and a positive only routes the chemical onward. With 100% sensitivity and 100% NPV, the only claim the tier actually makes — "this chemical is not photoreactive, stop here" — is, on the validation set, never wrong. Its false positives cost downstream testing. They do not cost a missed hazard.

This closes the vault's own open correction

On 2026-07-11 the vault recorded a vault-wide correction and flagged it as not yet retro-applied:

"The vault should stop reading standalone specificity as method quality and start reading it against the assay's position in its battery."

That correction was inferred from two cases — TG 492 (RhCE eye: 95–99% sensitivity, 63–72% specificity, where the guideline notes a positive "never classifies") and the h-CLAT (85/93/66, the sensitisation battery's most sensitive and least specific member). TG 495 is the third instance and the decisive one, because the guideline supplies the 100% negative predictive value that turns the argument from a plausible reading into an arithmetic one. A tier whose job is to rule out is graded by its NPV, not its specificity. The pattern is now confirmed across three endpoints (eye, skin sensitisation, phototoxicity) and it should be treated as a property of tiered batteries in general, not a quirk of any one assay.

Stated limits, from the guideline

  1. No metabolism. "The test has not been designed to address indirect mechanisms of phototoxicity, such as effects of metabolites of a test chemical." A photo-inert parent with a photo-reactive metabolite is invisible. (Note the family resemblance to the aquatic correlated blind spot — FET and RTgill-W1 both miss bioactivation-dependent toxicity for the same reason. Absent biotransformation is turning into the vault's most repeated single failure mode, now across three endpoint columns.)
  2. Solubility-bounded applicability domain — restricted to chemicals meeting the protocol's solubility criteria in DMSO or sodium phosphate buffer.
  3. A negative predicts a negative in vivo; a positive requires further data.

A refinement to the photoallergy blank

GD 397 established that photoallergy has no validated test method (recorded 2026-07-11). TG 495 does not close that, but it shades it: the guideline notes that "the photochemical assays such as ROS assay and UV/VIS spectral analysis can predict photoallergenic potential of tested chemicals, although there is still a substantial risk of false positive predictions." So the blank is a validation blank, not an absence of signal — the photochemistry carries information about photoallergy; nobody has validated it for that endpoint. The gap stays open, but it is now a narrower gap than "no method has any signal."
