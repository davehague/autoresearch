The environmental column solved the interpretation problem the human-health column has not

Twenty-four maps in this vault carry the same loop_blocker: no agreed procedure converts the number the method produces into the call it is supposed to inform. The assay runs, the instrument reports, and nothing published says what the answer means.

The aquatic side has one, and it is arithmetic.

PEC/PNEC — the predicted environmental concentration divided by the predicted no-effect concentration. Numerator: how much of the chemical is expected to end up in the water or sediment, from a release-and-dilution model. Denominator: the concentration below which no effect on aquatic life is expected, derived from ecotoxicity data with safety factors applied. If the ratio is below 1, exposure sits under the level judged harmless and the risk is acceptable. Above 1 it is not.

It is a division and a comparison. It is written into European chemicals regulation. It takes model outputs on both sides. A machine can compute it and act on it without convening anybody.

Why this is worth recording rather than assuming

Because it is a rare existence proof of the thing this vault keeps finding missing, and the reason it exists is instructive rather than encouraging.

The protection goal for the aquatic environment could be written as a concentration: no effect in the water column. That is a physical quantity a model can predict and a rule can compare against. The protection goal for human safety could not — "safe for people" resists reduction to one number, which is why every human-health endpoint in this vault ends in a judgement about margins, adversity, and relevance that nobody has written down as a procedure.

So the difference is not that the aquatic science is easier. It is that the aquatic question was answerable in a unit. That is a claim about how the decision was framed, and it is the single most transferable thing in this note for anyone thinking about which endpoints could be automated first.

What it does not mean

  • It does not make the aquatic maps loop-ready by association. ecotox-rtgill-w1-fish-cell-line is already closed-loop-ready on its own merits (an instrument-readable fluorescence readout plus a fixed procedure in OECD Test Guideline 249) and fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet is not. A rule at the risk layer says nothing about the assay layer underneath it, and this vault does not infer one axis from another.
  • The inputs to the denominator may not be animal-free. Predicted no-effect concentrations are conventionally derived from aquatic ecotoxicity data, some of which comes from fish tests. The abstract does not say which source was used here, and it is recorded as unknown rather than assumed. If a fish study feeds the denominator, the rule is machine-runnable and the pipeline is still not animal-free — two different questions, and this vault keeps them apart.
  • A rule being computable is not a rule being right. Safety factors in PNEC derivation are conventions, and the two aquatic assays this vault maps carry a documented shared blind spot — neither the fish embryo test nor the RTgill-W1 cell line has a nervous system or full metabolism, so both under-predict neurotoxicants and chemicals that only become harmful after the body transforms them. A loop computing PEC/PNEC over data from both would compound that gap at speed while the arithmetic looked clean.
