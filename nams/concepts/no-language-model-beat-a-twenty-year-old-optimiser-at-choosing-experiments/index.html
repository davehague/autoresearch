Twenty-year-old Bayesian optimisation, given no words at all, was not beaten by any frontier model given the full scientific context

GP-UCB — Gaussian-process upper-confidence-bound — is textbook Bayesian optimisation and has been since 2010. It fits a statistical surface to whatever you have measured, estimates both the expected value and the uncertainty everywhere else, and queries the point with the best optimistic bound. It knows no chemistry, no biology, no words. It is the thing a language model in an autonomous laboratory is supposed to improve on.

On 45 biology optimisation tasks, no model's confidence interval for "fraction of tasks where I beat GP-UCB" cleared the 50% coin-flip line. Under either prompting condition.

ModelTasks beaten (95% CI)
Gemini 3.1 Pro64% [47, 76]
Claude Opus 4.758% [44, 75] — on the 36 tasks it covered; ~47% extrapolated over the 9 its safety filter refused
Llama 3.1 8B Instruct11% [4, 22]

Point estimates ran GP-UCB ≈ domain-agnostic > domain-aware across all 30 rounds. The ordering survives a second Bayesian baseline (HEBO) and a 200-run version of the GP.

Two honest qualifications. The confidence intervals are wide — a deliberately conservative two-level bootstrap that resamples tasks and model runs and baseline runs. This is "not shown to beat," not "shown to lose." And a model that ties GP-UCB while also accepting a natural-language problem statement and no hand-built parameterisation may still be operationally preferable. What it is not is the thing the deployments assume, which is a system whose scientific priors buy it fewer experiments.

One incidental finding the vault should note. Claude Opus 4.7's safety filter refused 9 high-fit biology optimisation tasks outright — enough to drop its estimated pass rate by roughly 11 points if the refused tasks are scored as losses. For anyone reasoning about a language model inside a toxicology loop, where the subject matter is by construction "which chemical does harm and how much," that is a real operational consideration and nobody has measured it in this domain.

The measurement problem underneath, which matters as much as the result

Existing benchmarks score the final answer at a fixed round count. In an autonomous laboratory each round is a real plate, real reagent and real time, so a system that reaches the same answer twenty rounds earlier is enormously better and scores identically.

Switching to a trajectory metric — integrate the running best over the 30 rounds — names a different best model on 24 of 45 tasks (53%), twelve of them by three ranks or more. On 14 of the 24 the two winners land within 1% of each other by round 30, and the trajectory winner got there a median of 8 rounds earlier (round 7 versus round 15). The paper's opening example is two models hitting the same 210.4 mg/L antibiotic yield target at rounds 7 and 27, scored as tied.

This is a direct hit on how this vault reads every performance number it holds. Nearly all of them are endpoint measurements — sensitivity, specificity, concordance, area under a curve — and every one is an answer to "was it right?" rather than "how much did it cost to get there?" For a method being evaluated for a loop, the second question is the one that decides whether the loop is worth building. The vault has no column for it and no source has ever supplied one.

What this does not do

No status moves. automation-experiment-selection holds at emerging and at not-applicable on the loop axis — it is a component of the loop, so the axis is what it is measured against rather than a thing the axis grades.

And nothing here is toxicology. 45 biology tasks — microalgae cultivation, mammalian cell engineering, enzymatic biocatalysis — and not one is an assay, an endpoint or a Test Guideline. Every "outcome" is a regression model fitted to published literature (leave-one-out R² median 0.89), not an experiment. The crossover this vault watches for has still not happened on this strand. What has happened is that the deciding leg of the loop finally got measured against a real alternative, and did not win.
