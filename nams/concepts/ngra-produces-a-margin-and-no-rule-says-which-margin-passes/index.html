The integration layer produces a continuous number, and nobody has published which number passes

Roughly two dozen maps in this vault are stuck at automatable-open-loop for one identical reason: the assay returns a clean machine-readable number and no published rule says what number means hazardous. The vault has been recording that as a defect in individual Test Guidelines.

The layer above the assays has the same shape, and here it is arguably by design.

Next Generation Risk Assessment (NGRA) is the field's procedure for reaching a safety conclusion about a real product without animals. Three steps, quoted:

"(i) establishes points of departure (PoDs) from NAMs assay-derived phenotypic and transcriptomic data using approaches that incorporate Bayesian uncertainty modeling, (ii) derives estimates of internal exposure levels by using physiologically-based kinetic (PBK) models, and then (iii) calculates the Bioactivity-Exposure Ratio (BER)."

Measure where cells start reacting. Simulate what the body actually receives. Divide.

Why the output shape matters for a loop, in both directions

The good direction. Every other decision point in this vault is a classification — irritant or not, genotoxic or not, above or below 50% viability. A ratio is a continuous ordered quantity, and that is the kind of thing an optimiser can pursue, gradient over, and rank candidates by. The vault has recorded this distinction once before, on the transcriptomic point-of-departure map, as a genuine improvement in loop tractability over a hazard class.

The blocking direction. The published case studies report margins of >100-fold (coumarin), 27-fold (caffeine), and 16-to-338-fold (benzyl salicylate). No number anywhere says which of those passes. The paper's only stated decision criterion is a scope condition rather than a threshold:

"a protective safety assessment conclusion can only be determined with high confidence where the intended use case for the chemical being assessed does not require eliciting systemic effects."

A loop can compute the ratio and cannot conclude from it.

Two hand-set choices the arithmetic hides

Which dose metric you divide by is a decision, and it changes the answer. From the review's own worked example: "doxorubicin cardiotoxicity case study... Concluded AUC is better indicator than Cmax for this endpoint." Peak blood concentration and total exposure over time are different numbers. Nothing in the framework selects between them; it was chosen per endpoint, by people, on mechanistic grounds.

And the exposure the model computes may be of the wrong molecule. "Phenoxyethanol case study revealed the importance of considering metabolites, because a major stable metabolite introduced key uncertainties." The benzyl salicylate case goes further — the metabolite was the toxicological driver, not the parent. This is the vault's most-repeated blind spot (a dish with no liver) arriving at the integration layer, where it is not an assay gap but a modelling choice about which chemical to simulate.

Read the 16-to-338 range as what it is. A margin quoted across a twenty-one-fold span is not a measurement with error bars; it is a range of answers depending on which assay and which dose metric the assessor took.

What moves

loop_readiness on risk-assessment-ngra is not-applicable and stays there — this is a framework map, scored off the ladder like the vault's other integration and acceptance maps. What this note adds is the map's automation-relevant property in prose: the arithmetic is fully machine-executable end to end, and the criterion it feeds is not published. Steps (i) through (iii) are curve-fitting, simulation and division; a loop could run all three today on any ingredient with a PBK model. What it could not do is say whether the result is acceptable.
