SARA-ICE (GL 497 Part III) → a quantitative point of departure for skin sensitisation

The SARA-ICE defined approach is the vault's first NAM that outputs a dose rather than a class. Where the 2o3 DA answers is this a sensitiser? and the ITS DAs answer how potent — 1A or 1B?, SARA-ICE answers at what dermal dose does sensitisation become likely? — an ED01, the dose at which 1% of an HPPT-eligible human population would be sensitised, returned as a Bayesian posterior distribution with credible intervals rather than a single number. The reported PoD is the geometric mean of that distribution conditioned on ED01 < 60,000 µg cm⁻², a bound that assumes the chemical is a sensitiser — which is why the guideline forbids reusing it as a hazard call.

It runs on assays this vault already maps. Admissible inputs are DPRA and kDPRA (skin-sensitization-dpra, TG 442C, KE1), KeratinoSens (skin-sensitization-keratinosens, TG 442D, KE2), h-CLAT (skin-sensitization-hclat, TG 442E, KE3) and U-SENS (TG 442E, KE3), plus historical LLNA (KE4) and human patch-test data. At least two in-chemico/in-vitro sources covering two different key events are required; more inputs narrow the posterior. Existing in-vivo data may be used, but new in-vivo data must not be generated for it. No wet biology is added — this is a statistical layer that extracts a dose from measurements already taken, which makes it structurally kin to in-silico-qsar-read-across and to the pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics hub rather than to the assays it consumes.

Two axes, both adopted, on arrival. evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted and regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted — it sits inside adopted Guideline No. 497 (2 July 2026 edition). Adoption is what earns consensus under the evidence model, applied deterministically.

The number to hold onto is not 85%. "85% within 10-fold of the human reference" is a much weaker statement than an 85% classification accuracy: a ten-fold band on a dose is wide. Only ~1 in 3 estimates land within 2-fold. What is genuinely good is the absence of directional bias against human data (mean and median fold-difference 1.1) — SARA-ICE is not systematically over- or under-protective relative to humans, though it runs ~2-fold below LLNA-derived values. For a risk-assessment input consumed with uncertainty factors, an unbiased, distribution-valued estimate is arguably the right shape; it is not a precise one.

funding_flag: [industry, method-developer], and load-bearing. SARA descends from Reynolds, MacKay, Gilmour, Miguel-Vilumbrales & Maxwell (2019) at Unilever's Safety & Environmental Assurance Centre — the company that would use a PoD to justify an ingredient concentration. It was subsequently built out with NICEATM/NIEHS (a government body, which also hosts the app free) and independently evaluated by the OECD EG DASS against a curated 196-chemical reference set. That combination — developer origin, independent regulatory evaluation — is materially stronger than a developer self-validation and materially weaker than an independent origin. It is recorded as both, and neither half is dropped.
