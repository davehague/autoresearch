The same plate is reproducible or not depending on what you ask it

The measurement

Zebrafish larvae are raised in the wells of a plate, exposed to a chemical, and filmed. The lights go on and off on a schedule; healthy larvae respond to the dark with a burst of swimming. A camera tracks position over time and turns it into distance moved per interval. That is the Light-Dark Transition Test, and it is one of the cheapest whole-organism assays in toxicology.

Four laboratories ran it on 33 compounds under agreed protocol elements. Two kinds of call come off the same recording:

  • Developmental toxicity (DT) — is this larva damaged at all? Dead, deformed, failing to grow.
  • Developmental neurotoxicity (DNT) — is this larva's nervous system specifically damaged, at doses that did not just make it generally sick?
CallInter-laboratory concordance
Developmental toxicity84–100%
Developmental neurotoxicity68–84%

In plain English: on "is something wrong with this fish," four independent laboratories agreed almost perfectly. On "is the problem specifically neurological," the same four laboratories, reading the same kind of data from the same protocol, disagreed on up to a third of chemicals.

Why this is not a criticism of anybody's laboratory

The specific call is harder for a structural reason. A neurotoxicity call requires separating a behavioural change from generalised sickness — a poisoned larva swims less because its nervous system is damaged, or because it is simply dying. Making that separation means choosing a concentration window where the animal is alive and functional but the behaviour has shifted, and the boundaries of that window are a judgement. Different laboratories draw it differently.

Note what does not vary much: potency. When the labs agreed a compound was active, their dose estimates sat within a mean 3.2-fold of each other. For a whole-organism biological assay across four sites, three-fold is a good number. So the disagreement is not measurement noise. The measurement transfers; the classification does not.

The consequence for the loop, which is the point

The vault's loop ladder asks two questions: is the readout machine-readable, and is there a fixed rule for turning it into a call? This assay passes the first cleanly — a video tracker, no eye anywhere. It fails the second, and this result shows why the second is hard here rather than merely unfinished.

A fixed decision rule is not just missing paperwork when four expert laboratories, given the same numbers, land on different calls for a third of the chemicals. Writing down whatever rule any one of them used would produce a reproducible loop that reproduced one laboratory's judgement, not the field's.

This sharpens a claim the vault made on 2026-07-26 about self-driving labs: the reason the closed loop worked for cell-free protein synthesis was that its objective was dollars per gram — a number the instrument reports, computable in the well, with no external authority. Regulatory toxicology's objective is a classification defined by committee. Here is that abstraction made concrete: the general-toxicity question behaves like a measurable objective (84–100% agreement) and the neurotoxicity question behaves like a committee classification (68–84%), and they come off the same camera.

Honest brakes

  • Multi-laboratory is not a validation. No validation body ran this, no blinding is stated, and the protocol is "agreed-upon protocol elements" rather than a harmonised one. The vault downgraded the DNT in-vitro battery for reading standardisation as validation and will not make the inverse error here.
  • A contract research organisation selling zebrafish screening co-authored it, alongside public-sector laboratories (US NIEHS Division of Translational Toxicology, the National Research Council of Canada, Oregon State University). The public participation raises the weight; it does not remove the flag.
  • Read from the abstract only. The full text was not obtainable this run, so the exact statistical rule behind an "activity call" is unverified. Logged as an open gap.
