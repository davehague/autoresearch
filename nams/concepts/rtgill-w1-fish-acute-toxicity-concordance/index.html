RTgill-W1 fish cell line acute toxicity (OECD TG 249) → aquatic ecotoxicity

Confluent monolayers of RTgill-W1 — a permanent cell line from the gill filaments of rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss), one of the standard species of the in-vivo test it addresses — are exposed for 24 h in a defined, protein-free, animal-component-free medium (L-15/ex). Viability is then read three ways on the same cells: resazurin (metabolic activity), CFDA-AM (plasma-membrane integrity), neutral red (lysosomal integrity). All three EC50s are reported; the lowest is the conservative estimate and becomes the predicted 96 h LC50 for fish.

Both axes adopted: evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 249, adopted 14 June 2021), with an OECD validation report underneath and reproducibility demonstrated across laboratories. The guideline stresses the assay is "robust and transferable to laboratories without RTgill-W1 cell line and assay specific expertise."

The scope is narrower than the vault's other fish NAM, and the guideline sets it deliberately. TG 249's stated purposes are (i) predicting fish acute toxicity in product testing, (ii) range-finding and pre-screening ahead of a full fish test, and (iii) generating hazard information "in combination with other lines of evidence" (QSAR, weight of evidence) inside an ITS/IATA. It does not claim to replace TG 203 outright — unlike fet-fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-concordance (FET / TG 236), which is positioned to substitute for the adult acute fish test for many substances. Two adopted NAMs, same in-vivo target, different regulatory ambition.

The finding that matters is a shared blind spot, and it is a connect finding rather than a method finding. TG 249 names exactly where it under-predicts:

  • Neurotoxic chemicals acting "through specific ion channels or receptors typical of brain tissue appear less sensitive in the RTgill-W1 cell line assay compared to fish." A gill-epithelium monolayer has no brain.
  • Metabolism-dependent toxicity — allyl alcohol is markedly under-estimated because the cells do not convert it to the far more toxic acrolein. Tellingly, acrolein itself is predicted very well: the assay's toxicology is fine, its metabolic competence is not. The guideline's remedy is to test the suspected transformation product too.

The vault already records the same two failure classes for FET/TG 236 — neurotoxic, metabolism-dependent (and low-uptake) chemicals. So the aquatic column now holds two adopted NAMs aimed at the same in-vivo test that fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reasons: neither has a nervous system to poison, and neither has full biotransformation. They are not independent lines of evidence, and a battery that stacks them does not close the gap it appears to close. Recorded on both maps.

funding_flag: none, and unusually so: the line comes from recognised public depositories, the medium is defined and animal-component-free, a comparable assay exists as ISO standard 21115, and there is no proprietary kit or licensed test system anywhere in the guideline. Against the vault's run of commercially-gated human-health methods — HepaRG™ (TG 445A), Corrositex® (TG 435), EpiDerm™/KeraSkin™ (TG 498), EpiOcular™/SkinEthic™ (TG 492) — TG 249 is the cleanest commercially-unencumbered adopted method the vault holds, which sharpens the practical-accessibility axis flagged on 2026-07-10 by showing it is not inevitable.
