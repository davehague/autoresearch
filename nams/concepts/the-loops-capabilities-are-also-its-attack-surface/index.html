Every rung on the loop ladder is also a way in

This vault's third axis asks one question of every method: could a machine run it end to end. The ladder's top rung requires an instrument-readable readout and a fixed decision rule, and the vault treats climbing it as progress.

This is the first source in the vault that treats the same properties as exposure.

"[Self-driving laboratories] introduce new and complex safety and security risks that demand proactive attention."

And the three named surfaces are, precisely, the three things the vault's ladder measures as strengths:

"emerging risks related to system autonomy, data governance, and automation in experimental workflows."

The vault has no field for any of this. loop_readiness has five values and none of them asks who may issue the decision rule, who may change it, whether the change is logged, or what an adversary with write access to a threshold could cause the loop to conclude.

The relationship to the vault's own rail, stated exactly

The correlated-blind-spot rail says: when two assays in a battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, an automated system trusting the battery's decision rule compounds that error rather than catching it, because speed converts a known bias into a compounding one.

That is the accidental version of this paper's argument. Same mechanism — the loop's trust in its own rule, multiplied by its throughput — with an accident at the origin instead of an actor. This source names the deliberate version, and the vault should keep them apart: the rail is a science problem with a scientific fix (know the blind spot, discount the concordance); this is a governance problem with no scientific fix at all. The author's own position is that it is not even an engineering problem:

"safeguarding SDLs cannot fall to any single actor; instead, it requires coordinated action among researchers, institutions, and policymakers."

What this does not license

Nothing moves. No method's loop_readiness changes, no blocker is added, no tier is demoted. A perspective with no data cannot demote a map, and the vault's rule that loop-ready is never an endorsement already carries the weight this note would otherwise add. What it adds is a named, sourced reason that the rule exists, from a profession whose entire job is thinking about what goes wrong in laboratories.

And it is unusually thin evidence. No incident data, no vulnerability taxonomy, no tested mitigation. evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic — reasoning, not measurement. The mitigation strategies, which are the paper's stated contribution, were not read at all: the PubMed Central deposit is embargoed until 28 January 2027 and the publisher copy sits behind bot verification. This note records the risk framing and explicitly records that the remedies are unread.
