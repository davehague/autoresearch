There is no decision rule without a standardised measurement

The vault records automatable-open-loop on more maps than any other tier, and the blocker is almost always the same sentence: the readout is machine-read and nothing turns it into a call. For the inhalation map the phrasing is "no classification threshold published."

That phrasing implies a specific, tractable-sounding gap — somebody needs to publish a cutoff. This review says the gap is one layer lower, and the lower layer is much less tractable.

The measurement that has not been standardised

Across 144 studies and 105 volatile organic compounds:

  • Exposure system. Most studies dissolve the volatile chemical into the liquid medium and submerge the cells in it. Fewer use air-liquid interface culture, where cells have air above and medium below and the chemical arrives as vapour — which is both how a lung actually meets a solvent and the only arrangement in which "how much did the cells get" resembles the real exposure. The vault's inhalation map is built entirely on the second arrangement, and the literature it sits in mostly uses the first.
  • Cell model. Heterogeneous — no dominant reference system.
  • Endpoint. Clustered on cytotoxicity, oxidative stress, inflammatory signalling and apoptosis. Studies of metabolism, barrier function, morphology or transcriptomic response are "relatively uncommon."
  • Exposure duration. "Chronic or repeated exposures were rarely conducted" — nearly all of it is a single acute dose, which is not how anyone encounters cleaning products or furniture.

Why that blocks a rule rather than merely delaying one

A classification threshold is a number with a comparison attached: this readout above that value means irritant. It only means anything if the readout is produced the same way twice. Two laboratories reporting "40% viability loss" for the same chemical, one by dissolving it in medium and one by delivering it as vapour onto an air-exposed epithelium, have not made the same measurement, and no threshold reconciles them — the underlying quantity differs.

For a volatile chemical the specific unknown is dosimetry: how much of it actually reached the tissue. A concentration you dissolved into medium is not a delivered dose when the compound's defining property is that it evaporates. The review lists "improved dose characterization" among what the field needs.

And an independent review said the same thing two days earlier. On 2026-08-20 the vault ingested an organ-chip readiness assessment that graded lung the weakest of seven organ categories and named "standardized exposure methods, dosimetry, reference substances and cross-laboratory reproducibility" as the requirement for wider regulatory use (organ-chip-readiness-tracks-the-evidence-not-the-biology). Two reviews, different fields, different authors, no cross-citation, both putting delivered dose first.

What this means for a loop

It reorders the work. The vault's usual reading of automatable-open-loop is that the method is ready and the paperwork is behind — write the decision rule and the tier moves up. On this evidence the inhalation column is not in that position. The rule cannot be written yet because the quantity it would apply to is not yet a single quantity.

There is a sharper version of this for anyone building the loop rather than reading about it. An automated system does not notice that a measurement is incommensurable. It receives a number, applies the rule it was given, and returns a call — with exactly the same confidence whether the number came from a vapour exposure or a submerged one. The vault's correlated-blind-spot rail says a battery of assays that fail together casts one vote; this is the adjacent failure, where a single assay's number means different things on different days and nothing in the pipeline can tell.

Nothing on this map's axes moves. This is a review: no experiment, no new performance number, no regulatory change. The adopted acute inhalation tests remain the animal ones (the in-vivo Test Guidelines 403 and 436). What changes is the vault's description of what is in the way.
