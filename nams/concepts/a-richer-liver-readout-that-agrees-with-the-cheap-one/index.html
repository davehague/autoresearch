The expensive readout agreed with the cheap one, which is good news and an unanswered question

The vault has now met this shape four times. Somebody bolts a far richer measurement onto a cell-based toxicology assay — 94 morphological image features onto the rainbow-trout gill assay (2026-08-12), hundreds of Cell Painting features onto a generic screen, whole-transcriptome sequencing onto a liver line, and now 148 named lipid molecules onto primary hepatocytes. Every time, the richer readout works, is fully instrument-read, and arrives with no rule for turning it into a decision.

This one adds a wrinkle worth keeping: the authors checked whether their richer readout said anything different from the cheap one, and reported that it did not. Lipid effects "closely aligned with conventional toxicity markers."

That is a fair and useful result — it means the lipid signal is real biology and not instrument artefact, and it is exactly the sanity check a new readout should pass first. It is also, read as a loop claim, the question left open: a measurement that agrees with a cheaper measurement has not yet earned a place in a decision procedure. Adding it to a loop costs a mass spectrometer, a chromatography column, a sample-prep step and a run time per plate. The paper does not show a case where those costs bought a different call.

What the readout is actually good for, on the paper's own framing

Not classification. Mechanism. The stated aim is "mechanism-informed phenotyping" and a framework for cross-species comparison, explicitly complementing rather than replacing existing hepatotoxicity assessment. Which lipid classes move tells you something about how the cell is failing — the sort of information that feeds an adverse outcome pathway argument rather than a hazard call.

That distinction matters for where this sits on the loop axis. The measurement is automatable-open-loop by the deterministic ladder: machine-readable readout, no fixed data-interpretation procedure. It is the vault's dominant blocker, arriving for the sixteenth time, and here for the same underlying reason as Cell Painting — the method does not target a regulatory endpoint, so nobody is writing a rule for it.

The result that should not be smoothed over

Fialuridine produced the least lipid response of the three drugs, and fialuridine is the compound in that set with the worst human outcome — an antiviral that killed five patients in a 1993 trial by poisoning cellular power plants. Chlorpromazine and bosentan, both bile-handling toxicants, lit the readout up. Fialuridine barely moved it.

A readout is priced by its hardest case, not its easiest. If lipids are the window, mitochondrial poisoning is partly outside it — which is not a defect so much as a stated applicability boundary the paper does not state. Anyone composing this into a liver battery needs to know that the battery's lipid leg and its mitochondrial leg are not redundant, and that a chemical failing in the mitochondrial direction can walk past the lipid one. That is the vault's correlated-blind-spot logic run in reverse: here the two readouts are usefully uncorrelated, and the composition is stronger for it.

The brakes

  • Primary hepatocytes vary substantially between donors and the retrievable text does not state donor numbers per species or replicate structure.
