A right answer is not evidence of right reasoning — the decision leg's first real measurement

automation-self-driving-labs has recorded since 2026-07-31 that the loop's four legs are unevenly evidenced. Execution has two numbers from opposite methods. Retrieval has a 1,912-task benchmark. Experiment selection has a Bayesian optimal-design result and a physical campaign in which a blind language model placed fourth of four. The decision leg — turn the number into a call — had a proposed architecture and no measurement at all. This is the first source to measure it, and it measures something the vault had not thought to ask for.

The instrument. Rather than scoring the final answer, SCHEMA audits every intermediate step of an agent trajectory, cross-checks each factual claim against literature and web sources, and then weights each hallucination by how connected its concept is in a scientific concept graph built from benchmark answers and retrieved papers. A wrong claim about a hub concept counts for more than a wrong claim about an isolated one, because its consequences travel further.

The finding. Across ten models and two suites, accuracy and reasoning quality come apart:

  • DeepSeek-V4-Flash and GPT-5.4-mini both score 0.59 accuracy on the protein suite, with weighted hallucination severity of 0.362 versus 0.268. Identical score, materially different route to it.
  • Fewest errors is not safest. Qwen3.6-Plus had the lowest raw hallucination rate on the pathology suite — 0.009, under one bad step per hundred — and one of the highest weighted severities, 0.432. Its rare errors landed on the concepts everything else depends on.
  • The decoupling holds across two concept spaces the authors show are near-disjoint, which is their argument that it is a property of the agents rather than of one dataset.

Their conclusion: "terminal accuracy alone is an insufficient signal of agent reliability."

Why this breaks the obvious deployment design. The natural way to put an agent into a regulated workflow is: score it on a validation set, deploy if it clears a bar. This says a model can clear the bar on final answers while its intermediate reasoning is structurally wrong. In a closed loop that is not a cosmetic problem — the intermediate steps are what the next experiment is chosen from. A right answer reached by a wrong route still produces the wrong next experiment, and the loop keeps going.

And the repair asymmetry is the operational finding. Counterfactually correcting one hallucinated step and re-running fixed 46.1% of failures overall — but split by kind: 48.5% when the agent had the right evidence and connected it badly, 36.1% when it started from a false fact. "Once an agent commits to a false premise, subsequent steps inherit and amplify the misconception." Bad logic is recoverable; a bad premise is roughly a third recoverable.

Honest ceiling. Not peer reviewed. One group built the graph pipeline, generated the benchmark, defined the severity metric and reported the scores — the same construct-and-score-your-own-exam pressure recorded for the Pipette robotics benchmark, and it now carries every-agentic-benchmark-audited-had-validity-flaws as a standing discount. The hallucination labels are themselves model-produced, with no human-labelled subset and no inter-annotator agreement — the third instance in this map of a checking layer whose own ground truth was written by a language model, after the-failure-labels-were-written-by-another-model and the-instrument-that-audits-the-benchmarks-is-itself-unreliable. No toxicology anywhere in it; the transfer is this vault's inference. Mitigations recorded: code released, and the headline makes every model examined look worse rather than promoting one.
