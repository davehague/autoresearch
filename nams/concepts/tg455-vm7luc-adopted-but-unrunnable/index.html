TG 455's VM7Luc method: adopted, validated, and currently impossible to run

The full text of the note, which is the entire content of TG 455's 2 July 2026 correction:

"Users of Test Guideline 455 should be aware that the VM7Luc ER TA assay is currently not implementable because the cell line used is not available from the source mentioned. Actions are underway to remediate this situation."

The VM7Luc4E2 line was available "only … with a technical licensing agreement from the University of California, Davis" and from Xenobiotic Detection Systems Inc. (Durham, NC). It is now not available from those sources. The assay is therefore a fully validated, formally adopted, internationally recognised test method that no laboratory can currently perform.

Why this matters more than a supply-chain footnote. Every grading axis this vault tracks says the method is in perfect health:

evidence_tier (scientific validity)regulatory-adoptedNo — the validation record is untouched

On 2026-07-10 the vault named a third axis — practical accessibility — after finding three adopted methods gated behind commercial products (Derek Nexus, HepaRG™ licensed to Biopredic, Corrositex® as TG 435's sole validated kit). It concluded: "Scientific standing unimpeachable, practical accessibility bounded. Naming it; not modelling it yet."

This source is the argument for modelling it. In the HepaRG™/Corrositex® cases the third axis bounds access — you can run the method if you pay and sign. Here it zeroes it. The distance between "adopted" and "usable" is no longer a matter of degree; for this method it is total, and the two tracked axes register nothing at all. A model that cannot distinguish "the world's best-validated ER assay" from "an assay literally nobody can run" is missing a dimension, and this is the cleanest possible demonstration.

Two honest qualifications, so this is not over-read.

  1. TG 455 itself is fine. It is a Performance-Based Test Guideline with three reference methods; the STTA (hERα-HeLa-9903) and ERα CALUX methods remain implementable, and the ER transactivation endpoint is fully testable today. The map stays consensus. The failure is at the method level, not the guideline level — and the PBTG architecture is precisely what contains the damage. That is an argument for performance-based guidelines, and worth stating as one.
  2. "Actions are underway to remediate." OECD expects this to be fixed. It may be temporary. But it was permanent enough to require a formal correction to an adopted Test Guideline — the OECD does not amend TGs lightly — and it is the state of the world as of the current edition.

The compounding fact. This is the same cell line that was discovered to have been misidentified for years (vm7luc-cell-line-misidentification) — believed to be BG1 ovarian carcinoma, shown by DNA testing to be an MCF7 breast variant. One cell line, two independent failures of the physical substrate underneath a validated method, neither of them a failure of the science.

The generalisation the vault should carry. A NAM is not a paper and not a protocol. It is a protocol plus a physical dependency — a cell line, a tissue construct, a kit, a licensed model, a radioisotope. The regulatory and scientific axes both grade the protocol. The physical dependency is where methods actually die, and nothing in this vault currently watches it.
