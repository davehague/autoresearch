The blind-spot check on the genotoxicity battery — half independent, half not

The authors recommend using TGx-DDI "within a weight-of-evidence framework" alongside the standard battery, and note that combining it with the in-vitro micronucleus test (genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus) "enhances classification accuracy while retaining the ability to detect aneugens." The blind-spot rail requires the vault to ask, every time two assays are proposed as a battery: do they fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason?

The honest answer here is partly — and the two halves point opposite ways.

Where they are genuinely independent (the good half)

The mechanisms of detection do not overlap. TGx-DDI reads the cell's p53-mediated transcriptional response to DNA damage; the micronucleus assay counts physical chromosome fragments and whole chromosomes stranded outside the nucleus after division. A chemical that misdirects the mitotic spindle — an aneugen — is invisible to TGx-DDI by design and is exactly what the micronucleus assay was built to see (tgx-ddi-p53-dependency-defines-the-domain). Conversely, TGx-DDI's 86% specificity against the micronucleus assay's 30–65% means it can rescue a large share of the micronucleus assay's false alarms (tgx-ddi-ring-trial-performance). This is a complementary pairing, and complementary pairings are what a battery is supposed to be. The vault should say so as loudly as it says the other thing.

Where they are not (the half the rail exists for)

Both assays, as run, share two things:

  1. The same cells. Human TK6 lymphoblastoid line in both. Whatever TK6 cells cannot do metabolically, neither assay does.
  2. The same metabolic prosthesis. Neither TK6 culture has meaningful native xenobiotic metabolism, so both assays bolt on rat liver S9 — a subcellular fraction of homogenised rat liver plus an NADPH-regenerating system — to stand in for what a body's liver would do to the chemical.

So consider a pro-genotoxicant: a compound that is inert as dosed and only becomes DNA-reactive after an enzyme converts it. Whether either assay sees it depends entirely on whether rat S9 performs that specific conversion. If the activating route is human-specific, or runs through a phase-II conjugation step S9 does not reproduce, or is simply absent from rat liver, then the compound is negative in the micronucleus assay and negative in TGx-DDI for the same reason — and a weight-of-evidence rule reading two concordant negatives records that as increased confidence. It is one metabolic assumption reported as two results.

This is the identical structural pattern the vault already carries for FET ↔ RTgill-W1 (no nervous system, no metabolism — see uk-basket2-fish-battery-rebuilds-a-known-blind-spot) and for ROS assay ↔ 3T3 NRU (no biotransformation). Metabolism is the vault's single most recurrent correlated blind spot, and it recurs because every in-vitro assay solves it the same way: by borrowing a rodent's liver.

strength: suggestive — this is a mechanistic argument from the shared reagent, not a demonstrated discordance on a named compound. The vault states it as an argument and does not dress it as a result.

What it means for a loop

The complementarity is easy to encode and the correlation is easy to miss. An automated genotoxicity battery would very reasonably implement "TGx-DDI covers the false positives, micronucleus covers the aneugens" — that part is written in the paper. Nothing writes down "and both are blind to the same slice of metabolic activation chemistry." A loop that treats two negatives as corroboration will down-weight pro-toxicants as a class and steer its next-experiment selection away from them, because from inside the loop that region looks clean. The fix is not another cell-based assay bolted onto the same S9 — it is metabolically competent cells (HepaRG is one of the p53-verified lines the paper names, and is a real option here) or an explicit domain gate that routes suspected pro-genotoxicants out of the battery before the rule fires.

Both maps carry correlated_blind_spot for this.
