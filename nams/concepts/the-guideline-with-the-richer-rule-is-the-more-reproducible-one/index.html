Two adopted guidelines, one tissue technology, and the one with the more structured rule is the more reproducible

This is the closest thing to a controlled experiment on the vault's own thesis that has come through the sweep, and it was not designed as one.

Hold everything constant that a reproducibility argument usually cannot: the same laboratory, the same 234-substance reference database, the same analysis, the same three-dimensional reconstructed human epidermis technology, and in several cases the same commercial tissue product. Vary one thing — the structure of the written decision rule.

  • skin-irritation-rhe / OECD Test Guideline 439 decides irritation on one number against one threshold: cell viability at or below 50%. → Cohen's kappa 0.339 to 1.000.
  • skin-corrosion-rhe / OECD Test Guideline 431 decides corrosion on thresholds that differ by how long the chemical sat on the tissue, which is what lets it separate a fast, severe burn from a slower one. → Cohen's kappa 0.622 to 0.720.

Kappa measures agreement beyond chance; 0.6–0.8 is "substantial." The corrosion guideline's band is substantial and narrow. The irritation guideline's band runs from poor to perfect. The authors' own attribution: TG 431 "uses exposure-time-dependent thresholds" and is thereby more stable, while TG 439's trouble is "the single-threshold structure of irritation classification."

Why this is a finding and not bookkeeping

The vault's most-repeated observation is that most methods lack an agreed decision rule — 24 maps carry some version of "nobody has published what this number means." The implicit next step has always been: write the rule, and the method is loopable.

This says the rule's structure is itself a variable with a measurable cost. Writing a rule is not the finish line; a rule that maps a continuous, noisy measurement onto a hazard class through a single hard cutoff buys less reproducibility than one that reads the measurement along a second dimension. That refines the vault's habit of calling the missing-rule blocker "cheap to fix" — a qualification it already picked up from nephrotoxicity-ciptec-aop-battery, whose authors declined to publish a rule on the principled ground that three compounds are not enough evidence to write one against.

Two independent qualifications now, from opposite directions: the rule may be expensive to write honestly, and a cheaply-written one may not do the job.

What TG 431's own numbers say, and where they land against the vault's earlier correction

Sensitivity 97.3–100.0% — the corrosion test essentially never misses a corrosive. Specificity 61.9–77.6% — it wrongly flags between a quarter and two-fifths of genuinely non-corrosive chemicals. Accuracy 81.2–86.1%. That profile is the signature of a rule-in tier designed to be safe rather than precise, and it is the correct design for a test that gates whether something is treated as a chemical burn.

And there is a confirmation of this vault's own 2026-07-10 correction. skin-corrosion-rhe was corrected then, against the archived guideline text, to record that TG 431 does not discriminate sub-category 1B from 1C — the guideline says so itself. Tonight's numbers show why that disclaimer is right: scored on all three severity bands, exact sub-category accuracy is 53.8–60.7%, with 39.1–42.3% landing in the wrong severity while still correctly calling the chemical corrosive. The guideline declines to make a call it would get right about half the time, and that is the guideline behaving well.

For a loop, the shape of that error matters more than its size: under-calling a corrosive as non-corrosive is 0.0–4.3%. The errors are almost all severity-band shuffles inside a correct hazard call, not misses. An automated system that acts on "corrosive: yes/no" is on much firmer ground than one that acts on "how corrosive."

No tier moves

closed-loop-ready holds on both maps, and Status holds at consensus on both. Nothing here invalidates either guideline. The finding is about the variance inside an existing consensus, which is a thing a human reader of a single result would never see and an automated system iterating on many results would compound.
