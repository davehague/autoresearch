Catching the hazard before the step runs, instead of after — and only in simulation so far

automation-self-driving-labs's execution strand has, until now, measured the same shape of problem from the other side of the clock: a robot completes a step cleanly while silently producing a wrong result (a mis-capped vial, a reused pipette tip), and telling that happened after the fact is hard — AEGIS measured F1 0.71 on real hardware (0.47 at the smallest pipette volumes), and LabRobFail trained a much stronger detector but only against 20,000 synthetic, never-happened failures.

PREVENT is a pre-execution gate instead of a post-hoc detector: fuse a manipulation-level camera, a workspace-level camera, and a gas sensor through a hierarchical Behavior Tree, and decide whether to proceed, pause, or escalate before a risky step (an improperly capped vial, a gas leak) executes — explicitly framed around preventing toxic-material exposure to a human researcher, not just preserving data integrity.

Why this is a distinct failure-prevention shape, not a duplicate

AEGIS and LabRobFail both ask: did the robot just do something wrong, and can a detector tell? PREVENT asks a question one step earlier in the pipeline: is the next step about to be dangerous, given everything three sensor channels can see about the workspace right now? A perfect version of the first kind of system still lets the toxic exposure happen and only tells you afterward; a perfect version of the second kind prevents it. Both are needed, and the field has, until this source, only measured the first.

The honest ceiling

Zero false positives and zero false negatives is the result on the authors' own simulated test scenarios — there is no physical deployment in this version of the paper, no independent replication, and no real chemical-exposure incident of any kind that this system is shown to have actually prevented. The "minimal changes to existing software stacks" integration claim and the unimodal-vs-multimodal accuracy comparison are likewise simulation-only results. This is evidence that proactive, pre-execution hazard gating is architecturally buildable with current perception components — not evidence that it works under real sensor noise, real workspace clutter, or a real gas leak. Zero toxicology or biology content: this is chemistry/ materials lab automation.
