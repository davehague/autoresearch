The loop's deciding leg gets a discriminative-selection engine, tested where the answer is knowable — and the exact-vs-symbolic gap this map already tracks shows up inside its own results table

LLM-AutoSciLab's central move — choose the next experiment specifically to separate rival hypotheses, not just to reduce uncertainty in general — is the sharpest concrete instance yet of the "deciding leg" this map exists to track. Its own two benchmarks (ActiveSciBench-Chem, ActiveSciBench-GRN) show the same split automation-experiment-selection already names via murphy-2026: the gap between how well a model's output fits held-out data (exact accuracy) and whether it identifies the actual mechanism (symbolic accuracy) is not fixed across tasks — it is larger on the chemistry benchmark and smaller (in fact reversed in relative size) on the gene-regulatory-network benchmark. A single reported accuracy number cannot say which kind of "right" was measured without checking both, and this paper is unusual for reporting both.

The detector does not transfer, and the authors say so themselves. Symbolic accuracy is checkable only because every task is built on a simulator whose true mechanism is known in advance — the identical regime gap numerically-right-and-mechanistically-wrong-with-a-number-on-it already names. In regulatory toxicology the true mechanism is the object being sought, not a held-out answer key, so this paper's own evaluation method could not be run as-is on a real toxicological question.

Named, not attempted: the crossover. The paper's Impact Statement flags "over-reliance on model outputs in safety-critical domains such as drug discovery, where a plausible but incorrect mechanistic law could have downstream consequences" and calls for "domain-specific safety review" before real-world deployment — a direct acknowledgment of exactly the gap this vault tracks, with zero attempt made to close it inside the paper itself. Filed as the clearest explicit naming of the crossover risk this map has seen from a non-toxicology source.
