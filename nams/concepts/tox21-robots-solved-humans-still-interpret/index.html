Tox21: the robots arrived in 2013 and the humans never left

"assay steps including liquid transfers and diverse measurements in a fully integrated manner allowing hands-free assay completion"

…and, from the same paper:

"Class 5 curves and other cases … are manually inspected to correct the curve class, if necessary, normally by masking or unmasking data points." — Attene-Ramos et al. 2013 (PMID 23732176)

Why this note exists

Since 2026-07-12 this vault has graded every method against a third axis, loop_readiness, whose second rung reads: "automatable-open-loop — runs robotically at scale, but interpretation still needs a human, or no agreed decision rule exists. This is Tox21's state, and the field's most common one."

That sentence had no citation. Twelve maps were graded against a claim about a programme the vault had never ingested. This note supplies the primary source — and the source is stronger than the assertion it grounds.

The finding

The robotics are not the bottleneck, and they have not been the bottleneck for thirteen years. A Stäubli arm, compound carousels, incubators, acoustic dispensers and plate readers run ten thousand chemicals through a full fifteen-point dose–response curve, in triplicate, every week, untouched by human hands. That is not a prototype. It is a decade-old production system, publicly funded, still running.

What it does not have is a decision rule. The pipeline produces an AC50 (the concentration at which half the maximal effect occurs) and an efficacy value for every chemical in every assay. Nothing in the programme converts that number into "this chemical is a hazard" by a fixed, published procedure. The conversion is a research judgement, made by people, case by case.

And where the automated curve-fitting is uncertain — the ambiguous "class 5" curves — a trained human opens the plot and decides which data points to keep. Masking a point changes the fit; the fit determines the AC50; the AC50 is the answer. The human is not supervising the decision layer. The human is in it.

What this does to the vault's model

It confirms the automatable-open-loop tier and sharpens what the tier means. The vault has 12 maps at this rung, and it has been describing their blocker as "no agreed decision rule." Tox21 shows that blocker is not a paper-work gap waiting on a committee — it is the actual, staffed, human work that the automation did not remove. The wet lab was automated. The epistemology was not.

It also lands the vault's most-repeated claim on a firmer footing than assertion:

The gap between automatable-open-loop and closed-loop-ready is not a robotics gap. It is an interpretation gap. Tox21 is the existence proof, at the largest scale the field has.

Compare endocrine-er-binding-hrer (OECD TG 493), where the guideline itself says "there is currently no universally agreed method for interpreting ER binding data." Same shape, different scale: a perfect measurement that nobody has agreed how to read.

The honest caveat

funding_flag: [method-developer]. This is the builders describing their own robot, and shared/evidence-model.md says the automation-hype variant of the bias watch applies exactly here: "a vendor's claim that an assay is 'fully automated' is a funding_flag claim about loop_readiness." The words "fully integrated" and "hands-free" are marketing until independently checked, and they are discounted accordingly.

But the load-bearing sentence in this note cuts against the authors' interest. Nobody advertising a hands-free robot volunteers that humans manually correct its output. That the Tox21 team said so anyway is what makes it credible — and it is the sentence the vault's third axis has been resting on, uncited, since the axis was invented.
