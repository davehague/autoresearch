One plate in nine is thrown away by rule, not by judgement

The object

Buried in the abstract of a multi-laboratory zebrafish study is a sentence that matters more to an autonomous laboratory than the headline result:

"A novel three-tiered quality control framework removed 10.9% of plates passing basic viability criteria, providing a systematic approach applicable to multi-well plate-based behavioral assays."

Read what that says. These are plates whose fish were alive and healthy enough to pass the ordinary acceptance check. They were then discarded anyway, by a stated framework, because something else about them made the behavioural data untrustworthy. One plate in nine.

Why an automated system needs this specific thing more than it needs a better assay

A closed loop is a system that measures, decides, and then chooses what to measure next. The decision step gets all the attention. The step before it — deciding whether this measurement should be believed at all — is the one that silently determines whether the loop converges on biology or on an artefact.

A human operator does this constantly and invisibly. They glance at a plate, notice the edge wells look odd, notice the fish in column 3 were slow to be loaded, and quietly re-run it. None of that is written down anywhere, and none of it survives the transition to an unattended machine. A loop with no refusal rule does not become more careful when the data is bad; it becomes confidently wrong at speed, which is this vault's standing objection to automating an assay whose failure modes are undocumented.

The vault called TG 494 "the first assay in the vault that can determine, by measurement, that it should decline to answer." This is the second, at a different level of the stack: TG 494 asks "is this chemical one I can validly test?"; this asks "is this run one I can validly read?" A real loop needs both, and the vault had only ever seen one.

And a third instance arrived in the same run: hellmold-2026-mobot-3d-liver-spheroid-automation's laboratory robot performs "on-deck image-based quality control" — photographing each well and checking the tissue before proceeding. Three unrelated sources, one night, all building the same missing component.

The honest deflation

Nothing here is validated or adopted. This is one paper's framework, described in an abstract, in a study led in part by a company that sells the screening service. The three tiers are not recorded in this vault, because the full text was behind a subscription and the vault does not invent the contents of a framework it has not read. That is a live open gap.

And a refusal rule has a cost that nobody reports. TG 494's 32% refusal rate is invisible in its headline 96% catch rate, because the catch rate is measured only on the chemicals it accepted. The same arithmetic applies here: this assay's "93% of DNT-positive compounds" is measured on the 89.1% of plates that survived quality control. A performance number and a refusal rate belong in the same sentence, and almost never appear in one.
