PBPK modeling gets scoped out for a drug class the map hasn't carried before: antibody-drug conjugates

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) link a cytotoxic small-molecule payload to an antibody via a chemical linker, so a usable pharmacokinetic model has to track three coupled processes: the intact antibody's disposition, how fast and where the linker releases its payload, and the free payload's own pharmacokinetics once released. This review surveys physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) modeling — a mechanistic, organ-by-organ simulation of drug movement through the body — as applied to that three-part problem, covering model hierarchy, the two-pore vascular-transport theory used for large-molecule transport across blood-vessel walls, parameterization, and named regulatory-use scenarios. It explicitly frames the work as a New Approach Methodology responding to FDA's announced phase-out of in-vivo monoclonal-antibody testing.

Why this lands on pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics. The map's standing identity is computational- connective — it is the model layer a closed loop would update, not a physical assay. This source adds a new application domain (ADCs/biologics) to that identity without adding new evidence about the model class's performance: no case study, no accuracy number, no model-vs-observed-data comparison. It is scoping work, not validation — a third independent voice this stretch naming the FDA phase-out as the reason a computational method needs that scoping now, for a drug class the map had not previously named.

Nothing moves. loop_readiness stays computational-connective by rule 1 of the ladder (no physical assay); evidence_tier/regulatory_status unaffected — a scoping review names a driver, it does not supply a validated model or a regulatory action.
