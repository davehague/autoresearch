The two axes were never quite orthogonal, and the coupling runs the wrong way

What the vault has asserted, in three places. CLAUDE.md: the three axes "move independently and are never collapsed into each other." shared/evidence-model.md on loop_readiness: "Orthogonal to the other two." vault/index.md: "Loop-readiness is orthogonal to the other two axes." The vault's whole discipline is refusing to infer one axis from another, and that discipline has produced its best findings — the adopted TG nobody can run, the loop-ready assay regulators have never heard of.

Where the coupling is. Look at what actually separates the top two rungs of evidence_tier:

TierThe distinguishing property
formally-validated"ECVAM/ICCVAM ring trial, inter-lab reproducibility"
peer-reviewed-methodpublished assay, defined applicability domain, some replication

Interlaboratory reproducibility is the entire difference. And it is the only claim in the ladder that cannot be manufactured by writing a better paper: it requires the method to work on unfamiliar equipment, with unfamiliar hands, on blinded chemicals. Regulatory adoption is a committee's decision. Peer review is a literature process. Reproducing elsewhere is a fact about the world.

ICCVAM's footnote makes that property conditional, for two named method classes (interlaboratory-evaluation-is-optional-for-the-automatable-cases): "large scale roboticized high-throughput screening assays and machine learning-based in silico approaches."

Those two classes are not a random pair. They are the two ends of the loop. The robot half and the model half. A closed loop is precisely a large-scale roboticised screen wired to a machine-learning model. The composite object the vault exists to study is, in ICCVAM's framework, assembled entirely from the two components that may be excused from demonstrating interlaboratory reproducibility.

So the coupling, stated plainly: the more automatable a method is, the more likely it is exempt from the one piece of evidence the vault's own ladder treats as hardest to fake. Climbing loop_readiness correlates with being permitted to skip the top of evidence_tier. Not by intent — the exemption is written for feasibility, not as a reward — but the arrow points that way and the vault had no representation for it.

This is the second time the axis has turned out to have a price, and the two prices are different. On 2026-07-13 the vault recorded FDA's model influence finding: model risk = model influence × decision consequence, and a closed loop maximises influence by construction, so every rung the vault treats as progress, the regulator treats as rising risk and a heavier evidentiary burden (regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility). Tonight's finding runs the opposite direction: for the components, automatability lowers the evidentiary bar. Both are true. They are not in conflict, because they apply at different levels and are written by different agencies:

  • FDA, at the level of the decision: the more alone your model is in the call, the more credibility evidence you owe. Autonomy costs.
  • ICCVAM, at the level of the method: if your method is a robot or a model, interlaboratory evaluation may be waived. Autonomy is excused.

The combination is the uncomfortable part. A closed loop is built from components admitted on a lighter evidence standard, and then placed in the configuration that demands the heaviest one. The cheap step and the expensive step are the same step, seen by two regulators.

What the vault should do. Not demote anything — the exemption is permissive, non-binding, US-only, and targeted. Three concrete changes:

  1. Stop writing "orthogonal" without qualification. The axes are conceptually independent and procedurally coupled at the top of the evidence ladder. That is a more accurate and more interesting claim.
  2. When a map is graded formally-validated, record whether the interlaboratory evidence actually exists or whether the tier was earned some other way. For the wet assays in this vault it exists and is cited. For the automatable ones it is worth checking, one at a time.
  3. Watch for the first method admitted under the exemption. A robotic screen or an ML model that reaches regulatory use with no interlaboratory data at all would be the concrete instance, and the vault has none yet. evidence_tier would need a new rung, or an honest annotation, rather than a quiet promotion.

(The coupling argument is a vault inference. ICCVAM never mentions automation as a rationale, never mentions loop-readiness, and does not claim to be lowering any bar — it claims to be matching the evidence to the context of use. What is sourced is the footnote.)
