The battery's shared blind spot now has a named candidate repair, and the party naming it sells it

What the flag was

On 2026-08-01 this vault's monthly cross-method pass raised its sharpest open question against its own flagship. The adopted skin-allergy battery — OECD Guideline 497's 2-out-of-3 defined approach — combines three assays and calls a chemical an allergen when any two agree. The vault asked its standard question: do two of these fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason?

The candidate answer was pro-haptens — chemicals that are inert as supplied and become allergens only after an enzyme inside skin transforms them. DPRA is a test-tube peptide-reactivity measurement with no cells at all and therefore no enzymes. KeratinoSens and h-CLAT run on immortalised cell lines (HaCaT keratinocytes, THP-1 monocytes) whose skin-relevant metabolic competence is limited. If none of the three can activate a pro-hapten, then a pro-hapten can be missed three times for one reason — and the 2-out-of-3 rule reports two concordant negatives as a pass. A battery casting one vote while appearing to cast several.

That flag was raised as a vault inference with no source behind it, and the correlated_blind_spot field was deliberately left unset pending a citation. It has stayed unset for 26 days.

What arrived

Two documents, both about a fourth assay rather than about the three in the battery.

The draft OECD Test Guideline for SENS-IS (24 July 2024), para. 11, in full:

"The reconstructed human epidermis (RhE), as the SENS-IS test system, includes metabolic systems inherent to such skin models. Therefore, chemicals requiring enzymatic bioactivation to exert their skin sensitisation potential (i.e. pro-haptens) can be detected by the test method. Chemicals activated through abiotic transformation (i.e. pre-haptens) are also detected."

And the developer's 50-chemical blinded study, which reports the assay "identified sensitisation as a hazard for the majority of substances in this pre- and pro-hapten-enriched dataset, including compounds requiring abiotic or metabolic activation."

The mechanism is coherent and not exotic. SENS-IS runs on lab-grown human skin tissue rather than a cell line — real keratinocytes in a layered structure, carrying more of the enzyme machinery that makes a pro-hapten dangerous. If your problem is missing enzymes, using tissue that has them is the obvious fix.

Why this does not close the flag, stated precisely

It is an assertion about one tissue, not a measurement of three assays. Para. 11 says SENS-IS detects pro-haptens. It never says DPRA, KeratinoSens or h-CLAT do not. Nobody has run the 2-out-of-3 battery and SENS-IS on a shared pro-hapten set and reported the disagreement. The comparison that would settle this has not been published, and the vault should not manufacture it by putting two sources next to each other.

The evidence is the vendor's, on the chemical class its competitors are said to fail. Both authors of the 50-chemical study who declare an interest are ImmunoSearch co-founders and shareholders; ImmunoSearch owns the assay, the tissue model and the draft guideline submission. The vault's standing policy grades a method developer's claim about its own assay's sensitivity sceptically. This is that, aimed at the specific weakness of the incumbent.

There is no number. "The majority." "Broadly consistent with LLNA EC3 trends." On a 50-chemical blinded study, the publisher abstract reports no sensitivity, no specificity, no accuracy, no per-chemical table, and the full text is bot-walled behind an open-access publisher's 403. A claim that a method solves a known failure mode, published without a hit rate, is a directional finding and nothing more.

And the assay's own evidence contradicts the clean version of the claim. The same paper reports that for aromatics needing heavy metabolic processing, the standard epidermis-only tissue under-called the potency, and a thicker full-thickness model was needed. So the metabolic competence of reconstructed epidermis is real and also insufficient for part of the very class it is being credited with solving — which is a more honest picture than para. 11 gives, and it comes from the developer.

The decision this leaves for a human

correlated_blind_spot remains UNSET on the 2-out-of-3 battery and on its three component maps. Nothing was changed there tonight beyond recording this arrival.

What has changed is the shape of the open question. It was: is the vault's inference about the battery correct, and does anyone have a citation? It is now: the vault has a second candidate repair to a rail row whose evidence comes entirely from the party selling the repair. The first was HepaRG cells for the micronucleus assay's false-positive rate — single-lab, industry-authored, with sensitivity falling while specificity rose, logged on 2026-07-27 as needing a policy decision the rails files do not cover.

Two instances is a pattern, and it is a predictable one. The organisation with the strongest incentive to demonstrate that an incumbent battery has a shared blind spot is the organisation selling the alternative. That does not make either claim false. It does mean the rail's repairs will systematically arrive pre-loaded with the exact bias the rail exists to resist, and a loop that adopts a repair on the strength of the repairer's own evidence has automated a conflict of interest.
