Somebody else drew the coverage map, and it has the same holes

Next-generation risk assessment (NGRA) is the exercise of assembling non-animal methods into a safety decision without an animal study, and its first move is always selection: which assays cover the biology this chemical might hit? risk-assessment-ngra holds the frameworks for doing that. This source is the practical companion — an inventory of what is actually available, per body system, written for exactly that decision.

"chemical testing strategies need to cover a broad range of physiological systems to account for mechanistic specificity of contaminants. Researchers and regulators thus face the challenge of navigating this interdisciplinary and rapidly growing space."

Its answer covers five systems: endocrine, immune, cardiovascular, neurological, hepatic.

Why the boundary is the finding

This vault has been building a coverage picture bottom-up — 75-plus method maps, each graded on its own evidence, and a monthly connect pass that reads the gaps out of what is missing. This review builds one top-down, from a different discipline (the senior author is a wildlife-contaminant toxicologist), for a different purpose, with no knowledge of this vault. The two pictures agree, including on the holes.

The four systems the review covers heavily are the four this vault covers heavily: endocrine (nine maps), hepatic (four), neurological (four developmental-neurotoxicity maps), immune (two).

The systems absent from the review are the ones this vault records as thinnest:

  • Reproductive / fertility — reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams, the vault's only bench-bound map with bench_bound_reason: no-nam-exists. A 2026 coverage guide does not include it, which is what "no method to describe" looks like from the outside.
  • Renal — nephrotoxicity-ciptec-aop-battery, emerging.
  • Respiratory — inhalation-airway-epithelium-ali, emerging.

One divergence is a real signal rather than a gap. The review includes cardiovascular, where this vault has a single emerging map (cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia) — heart cells grown from reprogrammed stem cells. If an independent inventory found enough cardiovascular material to write a section, this vault is under-read there. That is a concrete sweep target, not a coverage gap in the field.

And one absence is a category difference, not a hole. Genotoxicity and carcinogenicity — which this vault covers more thoroughly than anything else — are not "physiological systems." Damage to DNA is a mechanism that cuts across every organ, which is why it gets its own battery rather than a body-system chapter.

Graded at the floor, deliberately

evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic — a narrative review with no measurement, ingested abstract-only because the accepted manuscript sits behind Oxford University Press's paywall. No assay named in this review can be cited from this source, because none is visible. The claim taken is the one the abstract states outright: the scope of the inventory.

And the weakness is worth stating plainly: a review's scope is also an author's choice. Six people deciding what to include is weak evidence about a field. The vault's coverage claims stand on its own maps; this corroborates the shape from an independent direction, which is worth exactly that much and no more.
