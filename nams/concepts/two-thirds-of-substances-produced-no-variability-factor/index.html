The denominator: two chemicals in three got no answer

The number. 53 substances went into the screen — industrial chemicals, consumer-product ingredients, pharmaceuticals and pesticides, across a concentration range spanning six orders of magnitude (0.3 nM to 300 µM). Eighteen produced a cytotoxic concentration–response the method could fit. The other 35 produced nothing usable, and therefore produced no variability factor at all.

This is not the same as a reassuring result. A chemical with no measurable cytotoxicity in lymphoblastoid cells has not been shown to have low inter-individual variability; it has been shown to be outside what the assay can see. Anyone consuming a TDVF05 downstream sees the 18 numbers. The 35 silences are the part that determines whether the method can cover a real chemical inventory, and the abstract does not carry them.

Read against the vault's own precedent, which cuts both ways. On 2026-07-19 the vault treated OECD TG 494's 32% refusal rate as the most interesting property in the ingest — "the first assay in the vault that can determine, by measurement, that it should decline to answer" (eye-irritation-vitrigel-tg494). Declining to answer is a virtue when it is explicit: TG 494 runs a pre-test, fails the chemical out, and says so. Here the decline is implicit — the curve simply doesn't fit — and nothing in the output distinguishes "this chemical has low variability" from "this assay could not see this chemical." A loop consuming the 18 numbers has no way to tell how many chemicals it silently dropped, unless the pipeline emits the failures as first-class results rather than as missing rows.

And the same shape appears in tonight's other model-layer source. Chang et al. 2026 report that "over 50% of tested chemicals lacked cHTS data or failed quality control" (ivive-reproduces-the-animal-answer-for-liver-and-not-for-development). Two independent methods on two different legs of next-generation risk assessment, each reporting an in-range coverage of roughly a third to a half, each headlining the performance on the subset that worked. The performance figures in this field are routinely conditional on a denominator that is not in the headline — the vault has now recorded this pattern three times (TG 494's 96%-on-68%, this, and the IVIVE coverage), and it is becoming the more reliable finding than any single method's numbers.

What would change the reading. A stated, checkable in-advance rule for which chemicals this method can assess — the way TG 494's pH/phase-separation/absorbance pre-test works. The paper gives a mechanistic hint (the cells have "limited metabolic capacity", so the chemicals selected were "generally considered direct acting") but no operational gate. Until there is one, the applicability domain is discovered by running the assay and seeing whether it worked, which is the expensive way to find out.
