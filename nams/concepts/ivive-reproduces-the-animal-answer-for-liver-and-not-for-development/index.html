The bridge holds for the liver and collapses for the fetus

What the model layer was asked to do. Take a concentration that was active in a dish, run it backwards through a physiologically based toxicokinetic model to find the daily human dose that would produce that blood concentration — the equivalent administered dose — and see whether it lands where the animal study landed. This is pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics, the vault's connective hub, being tested against the numbers regulators actually publish rather than described as a capability.

The endpoint-by-endpoint result is the finding.

EndpointAgreement with the animal-derived point of departure
Liver (hepatotoxicity)"9 of 10 chemicals showed minimal EADs within a 15-fold range" — works
Nervous system (neurotoxicity)"within 10-fold … for half of the chemicals (8 chemicals) and between 10- and 20-fold for an additional 4" — works about half the time
Developing fetus (developmental toxicity)1 of 6 within 10-fold; the rest off by 83–250 fold — does not work

Against the published guidance values rather than the raw points of departure the spread is far wider: 2- to 5,745-fold (neuro), 7.3- to 2,556-fold (hepato), 1.2- to 17,042-fold (developmental).

The abstract and the results section say different-sized things. The abstract offers "PoDs for a majority of chemicals can be predicted using such data." That is defensible for liver, arguable for nerve, and false for development. The vault grades on the results section, and records the gap between the two — this is the same pattern flagged on 2026-07-19 against Ahn et al., where a paper with clean funding, shared data and listed limitations still summarised itself more warmly than its own numbers support.

The developmental result is the one that matters most here. Developmental toxicity is where this vault's biggest blanks already are: reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams is bench-bound with no-nam-exists, developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb moved to contested on 2026-07-20, and developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity is single-lab emerging. Now the computational bridge — the leg that was supposed to make plate data mean something in a human — fails on the same endpoint, off by two orders of magnitude on five chemicals out of six. The developmental column is thin at the assay layer and thin at the extrapolation layer, and the two failures are independent. Six chemicals is a signal, not a verdict, and the note says so.

The reassuring number, read correctly. "Majority of chemicals (20/24) had MOEs exceeding 100" — for 20 of 24 chemicals, estimated real-world exposure sits at least a hundredfold below the concern dose. Only tetrachloroethylene (a dry-cleaning solvent) exceeded its guidance value. That is a safety-margin result, not a validation result. A method can be comfortably protective and still be poor at reproducing the number it is meant to replace, and conflating the two is how a screening tool gets promoted past its evidence.

The coverage ceiling, in the authors' own limitations. "Over 50% of tested chemicals lacked cHTS data or failed quality control." The bridge cannot be evaluated where the screening data underneath it is missing — and the screening data comes from automation-tox21-qhts-platform, the robot that has been running since 2013. Thirteen years of hands-free screening and half the priority chemicals still have no usable data. Same shape as tonight's variability source, which could derive a factor for 18 of 53 substances (two-thirds-of-substances-produced-no-variability-factor).
