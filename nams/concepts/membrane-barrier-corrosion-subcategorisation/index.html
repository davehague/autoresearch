In-vitro membrane barrier test (OECD TG 435) → skin corrosion sub-categorisation

An artificial membrane "designed to respond to corrosive substances in a manner similar to animal skin in situ" sits above a chemical detection fluid (e.g. a cresol-red pH-indicator system). The measured quantity is the breakthrough time: how long the chemical takes to penetrate the barrier and trip the indicator. Because the GHS corrosion sub-categories are themselves defined by exposure duration, a time readout maps onto them directly — 1A at < 3 minutes, 1B at > 3 minutes and < 1 hour, 1C at > 1 hour and < 4 hours.

Both axes adopted: evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 435, adopted 19 July 2006).

The unique capability, and a correction it forces. TG 435 is the only adopted in-vitro method that separates 1B from 1C. The vault previously recorded that TG 431 (RhE) "sub-categorises 1A/1B/1C." Read against the archived TG 431 PDF, that is wrong: TG 431's own text states that "a limitation of this Test Guideline is that it does not allow discriminating between skin corrosive Sub-category 1B and Sub-category 1C," and that it discriminates 1A versus 1B-and-1C versus NC. TG 430 (TER, excised rat skin) gives a bare Cat-1 call with no sub-categorisation at all. So the three adopted corrosion methods are parallel stand-alone options, not a cascade, and each resolves a different slice of the GHS call. Corrected here; vault/index.md and skin-corrosion-rhe updated to match.

The applicability wall is the trade. A chemical must first pass a compatibility/qualification step (and, for strong acids and bases, a timescale categorisation test). The guideline states plainly that "some corrosive chemicals and chemical mixtures may not qualify for testing" and that "aqueous substances with a pH in the range of 4.5 to 8.5 often do not qualify for testing" — then supplies the statistic that makes this workable: 85% of chemicals tested in that pH range were non-corrosive in animal tests. The excluded domain is mostly a domain where the answer would have been "not corrosive." Mostly. A corrosive near-neutral chemical is invisible to this method, and the reassurance is a population statistic, not a property of the chemical in hand.

A 3Rs distinction worth holding. TG 435's membrane is entirely synthetic — no cells, no tissue, no animal-derived material, hence no donor variability, no culture drift, no species question. On a strict replacement reading it is the most complete animal replacement of the three corrosion methods, ahead of TG 431 (reconstructed human epidermis) and well ahead of TG 430, which still uses excised rat skin — a reduction, not a replacement. The same replacement line separates this method from cfu-gm-neutropenia-mtd-prediction, whose model cannot run without a prior mouse in-vivo study.

funding_flag: method-developer — the one validated reference test method under TG 435 is Corrositex®, a single commercial, proprietary kit. The guideline is framed as a performance-standards guideline that other membrane-barrier methods may in principle meet, which is what keeps it from being a vendor specification; in practice Corrositex® is TG 435. An adopted method gated behind one supplier is a regulatory-accessibility constraint of the same family as the license-gated Derek Nexus leg of the GL 497 ITS (its-da-skin-sensitization-potency) and the patented HepaRG™ line under TG 445A (cyp-induction-heparg-biotransformation).

Edition caveat. The archived bytes are the 2006 adoption text; the current OECD edition is 2015. The sub-categorisation and applicability claims above have not been re-read against the 2015 revision. Recorded, not assumed away.
