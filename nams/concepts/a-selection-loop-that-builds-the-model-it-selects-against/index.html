The objection was that the chooser needs a model it does not have. This chooser builds one.

automation-experiment-selection was carved out of the self-driving-lab map on 2026-08-12 around one finding, and the finding was a dependency: a cost-aware selection policy needs, for every cheap diagnostic it might buy, a likelihood model — how much does this measurement tell me about which hypothesis is right. In materials chemistry those are hand-specified from domain knowledge, and the authors of that work called robustness to getting them wrong "an open question." In toxicology the number does not exist at all. Nobody has published how much a cheap in-vitro reporter tells you about a chronic in-vivo outcome — which is the missing decision rule, restated. The conclusion recorded there was uncomfortable: the maps stuck at automatable-open-loop are not blocked at the back of the loop only; the same missing piece blocks the front.

This source is the first candidate answer the vault has seen, and it works by refusing the premise.

The system does not require the likelihood model up front. A language model proposes candidate mechanisms from a natural-language description of the domain; Bayesian machinery maintains a posterior over which of them is right and what its parameters are; value of information — how much would this experiment reduce my uncertainty about which hypothesis wins — picks the next intervention; the result updates the posterior. And when the truth is outside every hypothesis on the table (the honest case, which most Bayesian machinery quietly assumes away), an out-of-sample predictive check notices and the proposer is asked to extend the list.

The author's own summary of why it works: discovery and design reinforce each other. The experiment identifies the mechanism the proposal introduced; the identified mechanism improves the forecasts; better forecasts expose subtler errors, which prompt the next proposal.

Precision about which of the map's two complaints this addresses

The map records two distinct gaps. This source addresses the first and not the second.

Addressed — the likelihood dependency. Value of information is computed inside the posterior the loop is maintaining, so it needs no external table of "how informative is assay X about outcome Y." The informativeness is derived, per step, from the current hypothesis set.

Not addressed — action cost. The map's second finding was that standard acquisition functions "do not price actions, so a $100 diagnostic and a $5,000 optimization plate cannot be weighed within one budget." Value of information prices information gain, which is the numerator. The denominator — what the experiment costs in money, time, animals or reagent — is not in this machinery either. The two halves are different objects and the vault should not let one stand in for the other. A loop that maximises information per experiment while every experiment costs the same amount is still not a loop that can be run on a real budget.

Three brakes, and the third is the one that would bite in toxicology

  1. Nothing physical happened. Three simulated benchmarks. No chemical, no plate, no robot, no assay, no toxicological endpoint. The nearest contact with this vault's subject is a simulated neuron and simulated channel-blocking drugs. Not peer reviewed; a single-author preprint eight days old.
  2. The author names the regime gap himself. Of the stochastic setting in the biology benchmark: "This is the regime real experiments occupy, and the one setting our other benchmarks (deterministic ODEs + observation noise)." Two of the three benchmarks are explicitly acknowledged not to be that regime.
  3. ⚠ The design space is a menu somebody already wrote. Physics: a fixed menu of 13 probe launches, chosen in advance by a language model "to cover the relevant dimensions." Chemistry: the nine elementary kinetic mechanisms are supplied to the proposer as vocabulary, and the author is explicit that the contribution is "identifying which multiplicative composition the data support, not blind symbol search."

The third brake is the one that matters here, because writing the menu is most of the problem in toxicology. The vault recorded this exact object on 2026-08-10 as a first: chemical mixtures arrived as the only map blocked at the front of the loop, because no published rule selects which of a combinatorially unbounded set of real-world co-exposures to put on the plate. That is menu construction, and this paper takes the menu as given.

So the honest summary is a partial answer with the hard half assumed. The machinery for choosing within a well-specified hypothesis space and a well-specified action set is now demonstrably better than the LLM-only baselines. Specifying those two things for a toxicological endpoint remains unattempted by anyone, in either literature.
