A machine-vision classifier beats the average pathologist on the exact assay this vault's newest map says a human still has to score

digital-pathology-whole-slide-imaging was coined four days ago (2026-10-01) on a single industry-authored source that digitized the histopathology channel (slides on screen instead of under a microscope) without touching the diagnostic act itself — that map's own text named the missing next step explicitly: "a study that trains and validates a computer-vision classifier on digitized whole-slide images against pathologist consensus... would move this map toward automatable-open-loop." This is that study, arriving four nights later, from a completely unrelated research group.

TRACE, a Vision Transformer trained on 157 preclinical rat liver/kidney toxicity studies, was benchmarked against ten board-certified veterinary pathologists scoring the same 100 slides. Against the pathologists' own consensus, TRACE beat the average individual pathologist by +10.2% on Quadratic Cohen's kappa — a standard inter-rater agreement statistic — and outperformed pathologists on 6 of 9 lesion types outright, reaching 87.6% kappa on its best lesion (necrosis, "distinctive and unequivocal").

Why this doesn't jump straight to closed-loop-ready

The readout (a whole-slide image, scored into a quantification score) is now machine-readable — that part of the ladder is satisfied. But TRACE's conversion from its continuous quantification score into the five-point severity scale (normal→severe) is learned via leave-one-out cross-validation against the pathologist consensus itself, not a published, externally fixed threshold anyone could apply without re-deriving it from a reference panel. That is a candidate automated procedure, not yet an agreed one — the vault's deterministic ladder reads this as "machine-readable readout, no fixed interpretation procedure," which is automatable-open-loop, one rung below closed-loop-ready.

The honest floor

One preprint, unreviewed and unreplicated more than two years after posting (July 2024 → retrieved October 2026, no journal publication found). evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, strength: single-study. The ten-pathologist reader study is more rigor than a bare self-report, but the dataset is rat liver/kidney only, and the reader study's own finding — "large inter-observer variability across all lesions" — means the target TRACE is matched against is not a stable ground truth either. This is the escape-hatch candidate, not the escape.
