Nobody had told the optimiser what things cost

This vault's question has two halves — a robot runs the assay, and an AI picks the next experiment. Nearly everything ingested so far has been about the first half, or about whether a regulator would accept the output. This note is about the second half, and about one specific thing missing from it.

Bayesian optimisation is the standard machinery for given what I have measured, what should I measure next? It fits a model to the results so far, then picks the experiment expected to be most informative. It has driven most of the self-driving-lab results in this vault.

It treats every candidate experiment as costing the same. In a real laboratory they do not:

"standard acquisition functions do not price actions, so a $100 diagnostic and a $5,000 optimization plate cannot be weighed within one budget."

The proposal is to split the campaign in two: first buy cheap diagnostics chosen to maximise how much they narrow down which approach is right, per dollar; then commit the rest of the budget to optimising within the approach that survived.

Read the first row and then read the other two. Where the obvious first guess is wrong, spending a little on diagnostics first saves about five-fold. Where the first guess happens to be fine, the diagnostics buy almost nothing — and on one case the policy is marginally worse than its own ablation. The method is insurance, its value is the size of the mistake it prevents, and the case demonstrating that is one the authors built and call hypothetical.

And no laboratory experiment was performed. The response surfaces are synthetic functions the authors designed, constrained to match selected published endpoints from a real campaign. Their own words: "absolute numbers are benchmark results, not laboratory claims." Graded evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic accordingly — the vault's weakest tier, correctly applied.

Why it matters here, and exactly where the transfer stops

The abstraction transfers. Nothing about "price your actions and discriminate before you commit" is specific to precipitating rare earths. A toxicology campaign faces the same shape: a cheap reporter assay and an expensive organ-on-chip run differ by orders of magnitude in cost, and no published prioritisation scheme this vault holds prices them. Tonight's other ingest builds a ranking pipeline for the developmental-neurotoxicity battery (dnt-battery-integration-pipeline-produces-a-ranking-not-a-call) with no cost term in it at all.

And then it stops, on the vault's own blocker. The policy needs, for each cheap diagnostic, a likelihood model: how much does this measurement tell me about which hypothesis is true? Here those are "specified from domain knowledge" by hand, and the authors flag robustness to getting them wrong as "an open question."

In toxicology that number does not exist. Nobody has published how much a cheap in-vitro reporter assay tells you about a chronic in-vivo outcome — which is a restatement of the missing decision rule sitting on nineteen maps in this vault. So this method's central input is downstream of the blocker, not independent of it. That is a sharper and more useful conclusion than "materials work does not transfer to toxicology": the selection layer cannot be adopted first and the interpretation layer solved later, because the selection layer consumes the interpretation layer's output.

It also cuts against a comfortable reading of the vault's own position. Nineteen maps stuck at automatable-open-loop look like nineteen instances of one missing piece. They are not: the same missing piece blocks the front of the loop as well as the back.

The brakes

  • Simulation only. No wet-lab result. The authors name the missing work — replay against real campaign logs, then closed-loop deployment.
  • Tiny decision spaces. One or two knobs, three candidate pathways, two modelled. The advantage at realistic scale is "hypothesize[d], but [not] demonstrated."
  • The theory does not cover the code. The cited guarantees are for exact EC² and idealised sequential GP-UCB; the implementation is posterior-impurity selection with threshold stopping and batched empirical-Bayes optimisation, "for which no competitive ratio is claimed."
  • funding_flag: [industry, method-developer] — both authors are at Coactive Science and the method is named after the company. This is the automation-hype posture shared/evidence-model.md warns about. Credited, separately, for putting "synthetic benchmark" in the abstract and volunteering five limitations rather than burying them.
  • Materials chemistry. Nothing here has touched a biological assay.
