Five readouts, one cell, one vote

This vault's correlated-blind-spot rail has always been about two maps: two fish assays that both lack a nervous system and both under-predict neurotoxicants; two phototoxicity assays that neither metabolise, so a harmless-looking parent chemical with a dangerous breakdown product walks through the whole cascade untouched; three hormone assays sharing a genetic backbone.

Tonight it fires in a new place — inside a single battery.

The structure

Two papers, built independently, from the same European research programme, using the same design method (pick what to measure from a published causal pathway), each land on the same architecture: five different mechanisms, measured five different ways, all inside one immortalised cell line.

  • Cholestatic liver injury: bile transporter changes, bile canalicular dynamics, endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial impairment, oxidative stress — all in HepaRG.
  • Kidney injury: mitochondrial depolarisation, reactive oxygen species, viability, membrane rupture, lysosomal injury — all in ciPTEC (conditionally immortalised proximal tubule epithelial cells).

Each battery is presented, correctly, as mechanistically anchored: it does not just ask did the cells die, it asks which step in the known causal chain moved first. That is a genuine advance over a single cytotoxicity readout, and the sensitivity ordering it produces — mitochondria first, cell death last — is useful.

Why the agreement is worth less than it looks

A cell line is a single sample from human biology. It carries one donor's genotype, one immortalisation artefact, and whatever transporter, enzyme and receptor expression profile that particular line happens to have. Every readout in the battery is filtered through it.

So the failure mode is shared by construction. If the cholestatic mechanism of a chemical runs through a bile transporter HepaRG under-expresses, then the transporter readout misses it, the canalicular readout misses it (no transport, no dynamics), the stress readouts miss it (no accumulation, no stress), and the battery returns five-way mechanistic agreement that the chemical is clean. Not one vote against four — five wrong votes that look like corroboration.

Both papers disclose the underlying limitation honestly, in the language of biological realism: the kidney paper states its line "cannot fully recapitulate the structural complexity, vascularisation, and multicellular interactions of the kidney in vivo." That is a statement about how lifelike the model is. It is not a statement about vote independence, and the two are different claims. The second one is this vault's.

What it means for a loop, which is the sharp end

A human reading a five-way concordant result over-trusts it once. An automated system does something worse: it takes concordance as a confidence signal and uses it to choose what to test next. High agreement selects for more chemicals like the one that agreed — which, if the agreement came from a shared blind spot, means the loop steers itself directly into the region where its instrument is blind, and does so faster the more confident it gets.

The remedy is unchanged and it is not a bigger battery. A sixth HepaRG readout adds nothing. A second cell system that fails differently — primary human hepatocytes from several donors, or a co-culture, or a metabolically distinct line — adds everything. The vault has been repeating this since the fish assays; here it applies at a scale one level smaller than it was written for.
