A third instrument for the "at what dose" question — a gradient-boosted tree with a Shapley explanation

skin-sensitization-quantitative-pod-da records the vault's newest claim type: not is this a skin allergen but at what dose does it become one. Two defined approaches answer it and are adopted in OECD Guideline No. 497 (SARA-ICE, a Bayesian model returning a dose distribution; and a multiple-regression approach). ExSERA is a third candidate, and it is not adopted.

What it is

XGBoost — gradient-boosted decision trees, many small trees each correcting the last — run as a regression, so it outputs a number rather than a class. 21 input variables:

  • results from the three adopted non-animal assays this vault already maps individually: DPRA (skin-sensitization-dpra, a pure chemistry test — does the substance bind a peptide?), KeratinoSens™ (skin-sensitization-keratinosens, do skin cells switch on their oxidative-stress defence gene?), and h-CLAT (skin-sensitization-hclat, the human Cell Line Activation Test — do immune cells put activation markers on their surface?);
  • molecular descriptors — computed numbers describing the molecule;
  • structural alerts — known reactive sub-structures.

Target: murine LLNA EC3. The Local Lymph Node Assay puts a chemical on a mouse's ear and measures immune-cell proliferation in the draining lymph node; EC3 is the concentration that triples it. Lower EC3, stronger allergen. This target choice is the finding worth carrying forward and is treated separately in a-model-anchored-on-a-mouse-number-cannot-outgrow-it.

How to read the accuracy claim

"Within the applicability domain, 80% of internal validation and 65% of external validation predictions fell within a five-fold range of the measured LLNA EC3 values."

"Within five-fold" is a wide band, not an accuracy. It means the prediction may be five times too strong or five times too weak and still count as a hit. For a potency number that feeds a safe-use dose, that is a real margin, and the guideline-adopted tools carry the same kind of band — ~85% within ten-fold, only about a third within two-fold. So ExSERA's band is tighter than the adopted tools' headline band, and the comparison is not valid as stated: different reference data (mouse EC3 only, versus mouse and human patch-test data), different and much smaller sets, no common benchmark. Neither the paper nor this vault should read it as a ranking.

The 80% → 65% drop is the honest number. On 38 chemicals from an independent database, roughly one in three misses even the five-fold band. That is the figure a reader should carry, not the 80%.

"Within the applicability domain" is a condition on everything above. The domain's definition and the fraction of chemical space it excludes are not in the abstract, so the reported performance covers an unstated share of chemicals. That is the same domain_gate discipline the vault applies to adopted assays, applied here to a model.

The explainability claim, credited and bounded

"Variable contribution analysis using Shapley Additive exPlanations showed that all three in vitro assays were the most influential contributors, with higher toxic activity correlating with stronger predicted sensitization."

SHAP hands each input variable a share of each individual prediction, so a user can ask why this answer rather than only what answer. The authors' framing is explicitly regulatory — "to be suitable for regulatory use, machine learning models must ensure transparency and interpretability" — and it lands directly on regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility, where the FDA's draft matrix prices model risk as model influence × decision consequence and charges a credibility burden proportionate to it.

Two honest bounds. First, the Shapley result is a good sanity check and not a validity argument — it shows the model leaned on the intended mechanism assays in the intended direction, which is genuinely reassuring against shortcut learning, but Shapley describes the model, not the chemistry. Second, explainability is not independence. A transparent model in a closed loop is still the only voice in the decision, which is precisely what FDA's "model influence" factor measures.

Standing

evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method — published, defined applicability domain, an independent external set, which is the right design. Not formally validated, not ring-trialled, in no guideline. regulatory_status: none — "demonstrates potential for regulatory application as a defined approach" is a bid, not a status. Bias watch recorded and not laundered: a co-developer of h-CLAT is a co-author, and the Shapley analysis reports h-CLAT among the most influential inputs. The paper is not selling h-CLAT, and the funding statement was not readable behind the paywall — funding_flag: [none] is provisional.
