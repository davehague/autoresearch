The unstandardised edge — between the agent and the instrument

automation-self-driving-labs holds the reasoning half of the loop (Robin: an agent system that generated every hypothesis, plan and analysis while humans ran the experiments) and automation-tox21-qhts-platform holds the robotic half (a Stäubli arm running 10,000 chemicals a week since 2013 while humans still interpret). The vault's standing summary is "the robots exist, the reasoning exists, the wiring-together on a safety assay does not."

This is the first source about the wiring.

The gap, as the authors frame it

EdgeStandardStatus
agent ↔ toolMCP (Model Context Protocol, Anthropic)standardised
agent ↔ agentA2A (Agent2Agent, Google)standardised
agent ↔ instrumentnone"every such system rebuilds [it] from scratch"

The reason the existing standards don't stretch is stated crisply, and it is the sentence worth keeping: existing device standards were "built for deterministic software clients rather than probabilistic, goal-directed agents."

An instrument, unlike a software tool, is stateful, safety-critical, exclusively owned, physically embodied, and returns "measurements with units, calibration, and uncertainty." A function call can be retried; a plate cannot be un-pipetted.

The four primitives

  1. InstrumentCard — signed capability and physical-limit description.
  2. First-class reservation — exclusive locking of instrument and sample.
  3. Safety-fence handshake — operator-confirmation tokens cryptographically bound to a specific task and its parameters, gating "hazardous and irreversible operations."
  4. MeasurementResult — every result "physically typed (QUDT/UCUM), calibration-anchored, uncertainty-bearing, and reproducible by construction."

Two of the four answer problems the vault found independently

⚠️ VAULT INFERENCE, flagged as one. The paper does not mention toxicology, FDA, model risk, or regulation anywhere. These connections are this vault's, not the authors'.

The safety-fence handshake is a mechanism for the thing FDA charges for. regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility records that model risk = model influence × decision consequence, that model influence measures how alone the model is in the decision, and that FDA's own worked example says a human cross-check "would reduce the AI model influence." The vault noted the implication and left it: a builder wanting to buy down that price has to demonstrate where the human was.

An operator-confirmation token cryptographically bound to a specific task and its parameters is exactly that demonstration, in a machine-checkable, tamper-evident form. It is the first artefact the vault has seen that could turn a model-influence claim from narrative into evidence. Two literatures that do not cite each other, one pricing human involvement and the other, months later, specifying a cryptographic receipt for it.

The MeasurementResult schema answers Hartung's stated bottleneck. hartung-2025-agentic-ai-lab-automation named the loop's real constraint as the input contract — "many 'FAIR' datasets are discoverable only through bespoke portals or lack harmonized ontologies." Units, calibration anchor and uncertainty carried on every result by construction is a direct structural answer. Again: no citation between them.

Honest ceiling — and it is low

An arXiv preprint. Not peer reviewed. No implementation, no instruments driven, no campaign run, no independent adopter. The closed-loop campaign is walked through the protocol as an illustration, not executed. evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic, strength: suggestive — the vault's floor for a no-primary-data reasoning document, now the fifth instance of the missing review/perspective/specification rung.

And the automation-hype watch applies to the class. shared/evidence-model.md requires that a claim about loop_readiness made by the people selling the capability be graded as sceptically as a method developer's claim about their own assay's sensitivity. No commercial sponsor is stated here, but this is a protocol proposal assessed by its own proposers.

The relevant comparison is unflattering. MCP and A2A matter because they were adopted, not because they were specified. A protocol with no implementations is a proposal, and the history of laboratory interoperability standards — SiLA, OPC-UA, AnIML, SiLA 2 — is a history of specifications that solved the problem on paper. Nothing here touches a toxicology assay. The crossover the sweep exists to find has not happened in this source either; the map's status does not move.
