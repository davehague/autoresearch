Three percent — and it is not the same question the vault's other numbers answered

The vault holds a run of automation numbers that read, at a glance, like a field converging on competence:

SourceNumberWhat was being measured
ProtoPilot (2026-07-20)88.24% instrument pass rateCan it drive an Opentrons OT-2 through a given molecular-biology protocol?
AEGIS (2026-07-28)F1 0.97 plan checkingCan it spot an error in a protocol somebody wrote?
Pipette (2026-07-22)37–72% task successCan a robot policy perform one named manipulation, in simulation?
This3.3%Given 45 stations and a goal, can it design the work at all?

These are not four attempts at one quantity. They are four different questions, and this is the hardest and the least studied: the others hand the agent the plan.

What "executable" means here is deliberately unambitious. Not correct chemistry, not novel, not efficient, not safe — merely the laboratory could have carried this instruction out. Do the named stations exist, can they do what was asked, in that order, with those materials, inside their declared physical limits. One in thirty passed.

The shape of the failure is the useful part

It is length. Only three executable workflows exceeded 30 operations; the longest was 44. So the regime in which agents reliably emit runnable work is short work.

That lands badly on this vault specifically. A regulatory assay protocol is long by construction, and the length is not incidental — it is where the regulatory content lives. OECD Test Guideline 439 (the lab-grown human skin test for irritation) does not merely say "apply chemical, read viability": it specifies the tissue lot, the pre-incubation, the exposure duration, the rinse volume, the post-incubation, the positive and negative controls that must run on the same plate, and the viability threshold. Strip the operations and you have not simplified the assay, you have stopped performing it.

And reliability compounds along that length, which this map has already priced from the other end (wet-lab-robot-manipulation-is-the-weakest-link): at a generous 95% success per step, a 20-step protocol completes 36% of the time. Planning executability and execution reliability are two independent multipliers, and both fall off with exactly the property a Test Guideline has most of.

What it does not establish

No human baseline is reported. Nobody measured what fraction of a chemistry graduate student's first-draft workflows would be executable in this same laboratory with its same declared limits. So 3.3% is a joint statement about the agents and about how unforgiving this particular constraint surface is, and the study does not separate them. A reader who takes 3.3% as "agents are 30× worse than people" is reading something the paper does not say.

And the 3.3-versus-28.1 spread is itself a warning against the headline. An eight-fold gap between the average and the best system means the aggregate is describing a model roster, not a capability ceiling.

Chemistry, not toxicology. No hazard endpoint, no guideline, no regulator — the sixteenth consecutive loop-layer source on this map with no crossover. The transfer above is the vault's inference.
