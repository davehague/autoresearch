More assays without an integration rule manufacture false positives

This vault's sharpest rail says: when two assays in a battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, that battery casts one vote, not two. The fish embryo test and the RTgill-W1 fish cell line both under-predict neurotoxicants because neither has a nervous system. The reactive-oxygen-species assay and the 3T3 NRU phototoxicity test share a biotransformation gap, so a photo-inert parent with a photo-reactive metabolite walks through the whole phototoxicity decision tree untouched.

That is a dependence problem, and its consequence is false confidence: agreement between two blind assays reads as corroboration.

This source names a different problem on the same object, and its consequence runs the other way.

The size problem

"Expanding test batteries without predefined integration rules inflates false-positive accumulation."

Take the dependence problem away entirely — assume every assay in a battery is perfectly independent, well characterised and individually specific. Now grow the battery. Under the convention that any single positive result raises a concern (which is the default when no integration rule has been published, because there is nothing else to do with a lone positive), the probability that at least one assay fires spuriously rises with every assay added. A battery of seventeen individually-specific tests is not seventeen times more informative than one; under an any-positive rule it is substantially more alarmist.

This is the multiple-comparisons problem wearing a lab coat, and it is the reason the argument is worth recording separately from the rail rather than folded into it:

Correlated blind spot (the rail)assays share a failure modeagreement misread as corroboration; false negatives survivea mechanistically different assay
Battery size without a rule (this)many chances to firespecificity decays as the battery grows; false positives accumulatea published integration rule

And the same missing object fixes both. A predefined data-interpretation procedure — GL 497's 2-out-of-3, a defined approach over the eye-hazard modules, the DNT battery's aspirational Defined Approach — decides in advance what a lone positive means, which caps the false-positive accumulation, and can be written to weight correlated assays as one vote.

Where this bites in the vault

The vault's two largest un-ruled batteries are the ones this argument is about. The developmental neurotoxicity in-vitro battery is 17 assays integrated by expert judgement with no rule, and its measured specificity is 60% — it wrongly flags two of every five safe chemicals. The eye-hazard guidance composes nine methods and integrates them by unspecified weight of evidence. Both were already recorded as blocked on the same missing object; this supplies a second, independent reason why that object matters, and it is a reason about correctness rather than about automation.

Honest brake, stated plainly: this is a commentary. Nothing is measured, no arithmetic is shown in the accessible text, and the authors are employees of a chemical company that pays for the tests it is arguing there should be fewer of. The DNT battery's 60% specificity is a real number from a different source and is consistent with the claim; it is not a test of it.
