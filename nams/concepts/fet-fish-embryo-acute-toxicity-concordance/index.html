FET zebrafish-embryo test (TG 236) → acute aquatic (fish) toxicity

The Fish Embryo Acute Toxicity test exposes newly-fertilised zebrafish (Danio rerio) eggs to a chemical for 96 h and reads lethality from four apical endpoints (coagulation of the embryo, lack of somite formation, lack of a heartbeat, non-detachment of the tail-bud). Because embryos ≤120 h post-fertilisation are not a protected life stage, FET is an animal-reduction/replacement NAM that stands in for the adult acute fish test (OECD TG 203). Adopted as OECD TG 236 (2013; guideline updated 2025) on the strength of an OECD inter-laboratory validation (2012–2014).

Both axes adopted: evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted. This coins the aquatic/environmental (ecotoxicity) endpoint — a distinct domain from the vault's mammalian-health maps; FET predicts aquatic lethality, not human hazard, and doesn't bear on the human-toxicity through-lines. The honest applicability limit: FET correlates strongly with adult-fish acute toxicity for baseline/narcotic chemicals but under-predicts for neurotoxic, biotransformation-dependent, or low-embryo-uptake substances — the embryo lacks the adult's fully developed gill/nervous/metabolic systems — so it is used with that domain caveat, not as a blanket TG 203 replacement. funding_flag: none — an OECD-run validation.
