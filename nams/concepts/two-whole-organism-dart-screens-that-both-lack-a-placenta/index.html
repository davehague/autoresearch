Two species, three assays, and not a placenta between them

The correlated-blind-spot rail exists for one situation: two methods in the same battery that fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason. When that happens the battery casts one vote rather than two, and an automated system reading the concordance as confirmation compounds the error rather than catching it.

Tonight's C. elegans platform makes a third member of a group that has this property, and the vault had not written the row.

The compound class that walks through all three

Mammalian developmental toxicity has mechanisms that require a mammal:

  • Placental transfer — whether the chemical reaches the embryo at all is decided by a barrier none of these organisms possesses.
  • Placental toxicity — some chemicals harm the fetus by damaging the placenta, not the fetus. There is no placenta to damage here.
  • Maternal bioactivation — where the harmful species is a metabolite the mother's liver makes and passes across. A zebrafish embryo in a dish and a worm on a chip both receive the parent compound.

A chemical that is toxic only through one of these routes returns clean in all three assays, in agreement, at whatever throughput you like. Three concordant negatives look like strong evidence and are one negative repeated three times.

Why it matters more here than for the existing rail rows

The vault's existing rows — fish embryo acute toxicity paired with the RTgill-W1 fish cell line, the reactive oxygen species assay paired with the 3T3 neutral red uptake test — concern assays used as alternatives to each other. This trio is different: it is a complementary set. The C. elegans platform reads reproduction, the zebrafish teratogenicity assay reads malformation, the behavioural test reads neurodevelopment. They are exactly the kind of set someone would assemble into an integrated approach precisely because they cover different endpoints — and the coverage they appear to give is broader than the coverage they have.

Honest limits on this claim

This is mechanistic reasoning, not a measured false-negative rate, and it is graded accordingly (in-silico-mechanistic, suggestive). No source in the vault reports how often a placenta-dependent developmental toxicant is missed by any of the three, because none of them has been run against a chemical set selected for that mechanism. That absence is itself the finding to record: the shared gap is structurally obvious and, as far as this vault's reading goes, unquantified.

The remedy is not a fourth non-mammalian organism. It is a method with a maternal-fetal interface — which the vault does not currently hold for any species.
