Four laboratories agreed, and an imager did the reading

Two properties rarely show up in the same source, and both are here.

The first is inter-laboratory reproducibility. This vault's evidence ladder separates its top two rungs on exactly one property: whether the method works on somebody else's bench with somebody else's hands. It is the only claim in the ladder that cannot be manufactured by writing a better paper. Four laboratories ran the soft agar colony formation assay on a shared cell model and their rankings agreed at Spearman 0.988 to 1 — against the authors' own honest statement that, at this sample size, random data would land anywhere between −0.64 and 0.64. That is not a marginal result.

The second is that nothing here is scored by eye. Colonies are counted by a high-content imaging instrument and analysis software; the companion assay reports a bioluminescent glow proportional to cellular ATP. This matters because it is a direct contrast with the vault's existing carcinogenicity map: carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay measures transformation in the same broad sense, has better regulatory standing (two OECD Guidance Documents, an endorsed validation), and is stuck at human-in-the-loop for one reason — its transformed foci are judged morphologically by a trained human, and the guidance itself names that subjectivity as a weakness.

Same endpoint, two assay families, and the readout puts them two loop tiers apart. The vault has recorded this pattern before, most sharply with two zebrafish assays run on the same plate at the same life stage landing in different tiers because one reads a near-binary measurement and the other reads a taxonomy of malformation types. Here the split is between counting objects and judging shapes.

What the reproducibility does not buy

Predictivity was not tested. There is exactly one test article: a single tumour-suppressor knockout in a single cell line, chosen because it produces the strongest available signal. Four laboratories agreeing about one positive control establishes that the protocol transfers. It says nothing about whether the assay correctly classifies an unknown edited cell product — and no reference set, no negatives, and no chemical or edit diversity are present to test that with.

And a battery of one is not a battery. GILA's ATP readout and SACF's colony count both measure anchorage-independent growth in the same cell line under the same manipulation. Run both and their agreement is close to one measurement reported twice — the intra-battery form of the vault's correlated-blind-spot rail, which the vault first had to name in July 2026 when two separate five-readout batteries turned out to live in one cell line each.
