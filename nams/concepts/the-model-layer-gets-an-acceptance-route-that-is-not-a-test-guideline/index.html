The model layer gets an acceptance route that is not a test guideline

This vault grades regulatory acceptance on a ladder that starts at "an OECD Test Guideline exists." That ladder was built for assays. It has no rung that fits a computer model, and the vault's four computational-connective maps — the model layer a loop would update — have been sitting awkwardly against it since they were coined.

ICH M15 is a route built for exactly that layer. ICH is the International Council for Harmonisation, whose technical guidelines the US, EU and Japanese drug regulators all follow; M15 sets general principles for model-informed drug development — using models of the drug and the body to answer questions that would otherwise take an experiment. Decisions it already supports, per this paper: first-in-human dose justification, bridging pharmacokinetics between formulations, drug–drug interaction assessment, and extrapolation to children and pregnant women.

Nothing in that route requires the model to be called a new approach methodology, and nothing requires a Test Guideline. A method map graded regulatory_status: none on the assay ladder may nonetheless have a live acceptance path here. That is a real gap in how this vault has been scoring the model layer.

The price is the same price, from a second direction

On 2026-07-13 the vault recorded FDA's model-risk matrix — model influence × decision consequence, where influence is how alone the model is in the decision — and the observation that a closed loop maximises model influence by construction, so every rung the vault treats as progress the regulator treats as rising evidentiary burden.

ICH M15 arrives at the same place by a different road: model risk assessed rigorously, training data and validation performance documented, context of use stated, and the whole apparatus scaled up "particularly for high-impact decisions." Two instruments, two jurisdictional origins, one proportionality rule.

The recommended mitigation is worth carrying because it is architectural rather than procedural: pair the AI with a mechanistic model — physiologically based pharmacokinetics, or quantitative systems pharmacology — "to preserve biological interpretability." Read as design guidance for a loop, that says the model layer should not be a single learned function; it should be a learned component constrained by a causal one.

The number to keep

The paper cites animal-to-human safety concordance for approved anticancer drugs as "moderate," with success rates "generally between 50% and 60%." The comparator a replacement method must beat is right somewhere between half and six times in ten on this class of drugs. That is a cited summary rather than a measurement made here, and it should be quoted as such — but it is the number that decides whether "as good as the animal test" is an ambitious target or a low one.

What this is not

A perspective paper by four authors, two of whom are national-regulator staff advocating for machinery they participate in. No submission is shown here to have been accepted on model evidence in place of an animal study, and the authors explicitly disclaim "immediate replacement of animal studies," describing instead a transition in which animal studies become "scientifically exceptional rather than just procedurally standard."
