FDA/CDER's documented NAM-accepted drug approvals — the fit-for-purpose route, with worked examples

A regulatory-move claim that confirms the vault's FDA-framework map (regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework). That map's thesis, drawn from CDER's March 2026 draft guidance, is that a non-animal method "does not necessarily need to be validated" to carry regulatory weight — acceptance attaches to (method × context of use × submission), not to the method. This source, a review by FDA/CDER/OND (Office of New Drugs) officials, supplies the worked examples:

  • Kalydeco (cystic fibrosis): an in-vitro chloride-transport assay (does the drug fix the CFTR ion channel in a dish?) expanded the approved indication to additional genetic variants without a new clinical trial per variant.
  • Galafold (Fabry disease): an in-vitro enzyme-activity assay enabled indication expansion.
  • Veopoz (CHAPLE disease): an ex-vivo complement-mediated red-cell-lysis assay (CH50) supported approval for a disease with no animal model.
  • Kimmtrak (uveal melanoma): human-tissue in-vitro assays demonstrated safety where no pharmacologically relevant animal model existed.

Why regulatory_status: none is the honest grade — and why that is the point. None of these acceptances is a Test Guideline. On the vault's method-centric ladder every method here sits at none ("no guideline") — and each was accepted anyway, case by case, for a specific drug's specific question. That is not a contradiction; it is precisely the framework map's argument that the acceptance axis measures the wrong unit. none means "no guideline," never "unusable," and here are the approved drugs that prove it.

The honest brake — recorded, not softened. This is a curated account with no numbers: four named successes and "a significant number" of iPSC cardiac submissions, told by the office that granted them. There is no denominator, no acceptance rate, no record of NAMs that were rejected. It confirms the route exists and operates; it says nothing about how often it works, and it is the regulator narrating its own record — funding_flag: [none] in the vendor sense, but an institutional selection bias to read for.

The loop angle. Two of the four (Veopoz, Kimmtrak) were accepted because no animal model existed — the absence of the animal comparator became a reason to accept the human-relevant method. That is the direction every loop built on non-animal methods needs the regulator to keep moving, and it converges with the EU roadmap's "virtual controls" (eu-roadmap-phase-out-animal-testing-ai-pillar): both regulators, independently, are treating the missing animal arm as something a human-relevant or modelled method can fill.
