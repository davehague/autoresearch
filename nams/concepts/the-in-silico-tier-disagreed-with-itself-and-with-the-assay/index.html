The model layer of the loop, disagreeing with itself on fourteen molecules

The vault's map of computational methods, in-silico-qsar-read-across, is its computational-connective flagship — the model layer a closed loop would actually update, and the one place in toxicology where computation has already been allowed to replace a wet assay (under ICH M7, two complementary (Q)SAR methods plus expert review substitute for the bacterial mutagenicity test for pharmaceutical impurities).

This study uses that layer for its more ordinary job — screening first, to decide what to test — and reports two failures of it in one clause.

It disagreed with itself. "Limited agreement across platforms." Run the same fourteen structures through more than one prediction tool and the tools do not concur about which molecules bind the estrogen receptor.

It disagreed with the assay. The computational tier reported only moderate binding affinity across the set; the adopted wet assay downstream called 13 of 14 positive.

Why the vault should record this rather than shrug at it

Neither observation is surprising to anyone who has used these tools, and neither is a scandal — the OECD (Q)SAR validation principles exist precisely because a prediction outside its applicability domain (the chemical space a model was trained on and can be trusted within) is not trustworthy, and consumer-product preservatives and fragrance carriers are not obviously inside any particular platform's domain. The result is expected. What makes it worth a note is where it sits in a loop.

The computational tier is the cheap step, and its job is to choose. Everything downstream inherits its choices. A closed-loop testing system built on this shape would run the model, prioritise, and spend its plate budget accordingly — and if the model both disagrees with its peers and mis-ranks against the wet assay, the loop has not saved effort, it has silently re-shaped what gets measured. That is the in-silico-layer-chooses-what-the-wet-layer-measures pattern the vault recorded on 2026-08-01, now with a disagreement number attached to the choosing layer. Except it is not a number: it is the phrase "limited agreement," and the absence of a concordance statistic is itself the finding's main weakness.

The rail, pointed the other way — and this is the part worth keeping

The vault's correlated-blind-spot rail says redundant instruments that fail together cast one vote, not two. multiple-frontier-models-are-one-vote-not-six extended that to language models: querying six frontier systems for hypotheses samples from something close to one epistemic source, so consulting a second model is a bigger battery, not an independent check.

QSAR platforms on this set behaved in the opposite way — they diverged. And divergence is not automatically better news than convergence. Convergence hides a shared blind spot behind apparent corroboration; divergence tells you at least one instrument is wrong but not which, and hands the choice back to a person. For a human assessor that is a manageable annoyance — you look at both and use judgement. For an automated loop it is an unresolved arbitration with no rule to resolve it, which is the same shape as the-battery-is-loopable-the-routing-between-its-tiers-is-not one tier earlier: the components work, the joining rule is missing.

The honest generalisation is the one the-instrument-that-audits-the-benchmarks-is-itself-unreliable already gestured at from the agentic-AI side: models converge on what they propose and diverge on what they judge, and the vault should carry both behaviours rather than promoting one into a law.

Limits

One study, fourteen chemicals, one laboratory, and the disagreement is reported qualitatively — no platform is named, no concordance statistic is given, and no per-chemical comparison is available from the abstract. Full text unread. Status on in-silico-qsar-read-across does not move and neither does its loop_readiness: a single application study reporting expected applicability-domain behaviour is not a credible higher-tier result against a consolidating map whose adopted narrow use (ICH M7) is untouched by any of this.
