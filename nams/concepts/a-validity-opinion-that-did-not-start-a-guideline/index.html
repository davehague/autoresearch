The science was signed off. The guideline never started.

This is the acceptance axis and the validity axis coming apart in the cleanest way the vault has recorded, with dates attached to the gap.

What happened. Two non-animal genotoxicity follow-up methods run on lab-grown human skin were submitted to EURL ECVAM, the EU's reference laboratory for alternatives to animal testing. Its independent scientific advisory committee (ESAC) spent from February 2023 to January 2025 peer-reviewing them and concluded that the evidence supports their scientific validity — for the RS Comet assay, "sufficient and adequate … for use with chemicals that test positive in the Ames or mammalian cell gene mutation tests, including chemicals that require bioactivation."

What did not happen. An OECD Test Guideline. The project to write one entered the OECD work plan in January 2019 and has been "on hold since then pending independent peer review." The peer review it was waiting on arrived eighteen months ago. The recommendation stage is logged as "Not applicable." The status is "Under evaluation."

Why the vault keeps these axes apart, restated with a date

evidence_tier asks does the science hold up? regulatory_status asks has anyone accepted it? This case shows they are not the same measurement and not even the same clock:

  • Validity: established, by an independent committee, January 2025.
  • Acceptance: under-validation. No Test Guideline. No adoption anywhere. The European Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety had already placed the comet assay in its "tier 2" cosmetic toolbox before ESAC ruled, which is a real form of standing but not a guideline.

If the vault inferred acceptance from validity — the single most tempting shortcut in this domain — it would record these methods as adopted. They are not.

The second case of validated-and-inert, and the contrast is instructive

The vault already holds one: CFU-GM myelotoxicity (immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm) — formally validated by an independent ECVAM international blind trial, regulatory_status: none twenty years on. But that one has a reason. ESAC endorsed it only "as a substitute to using a second species," because the method's prediction model consumes a prior mouse study. A method that needs an animal study as an input cannot anchor a replacement guideline. The blockage is architectural and it is stated.

This pair has no such reason on the record. The stated precondition for the guideline work was independent peer review; the peer review says the science holds. What is holding the guideline now is not in any document this vault has read. Recorded as an observation with a specific hole in it, not as a claim that the delay is unjustified — the ESAC opinion's own caveats were not retrievable (see the source digest), and a qualification inside them is one very plausible explanation.

What it means for the loop

Nothing moved on the automation axis on 24 January 2025, and that is the point. A validity opinion is not a data-interpretation procedure. The RSMN assay's rule was already fixed and published before ESAC looked at it; the RS Comet's rule still reads the testing laboratory's own historical controls after ESAC looked at it. What would move the automation axis is a Test Guideline that publishes a portable interpretation procedure — and that is precisely the document that has not been written.

For anyone prioritising loop infrastructure, this is the case that separates two things usually spoken of together. "Get it validated" and "get it into a guideline" are different projects, on different timescales, gated by different bodies, and clearing the first does not start the second.

Rebutted by: two-skin-genotox-assays-that-fail-on-the-same-chemistry — the opinion's conclusion that the RS Comet's validity holds "including chemicals that require bioactivation" sits against both validation papers' own named false negatives (IQ, 4-chloroaniline, 2-AAF, DMBA), each attributed to skin's enzyme complement differing from liver's. The tension is a scope condition rather than a flat contradiction: "sufficient for the method's stated follow-up purpose" is a narrower claim than "no correlated metabolic gap between the two methods," and only the first was made. Anyone citing this opinion as evidence that the pair covers bioactivation-dependent chemicals is over-reading it.
