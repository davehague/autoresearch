A platform paper with no toxicant, filed where it is useful rather than where it has data

This source carries no chemical dosing and no hazard endpoint — the same shape this vault has already filed once this month (lee-2026-human-arteriole-mps-vasoactivity, 2026-10-29, on vascular-toxicity-aop-informed-ml): a platform-development paper, not a method in its own right, and not yet gradable on the loop_readiness ladder because it carries no toxicological claim to grade.

What was built

A clinostat bioreactor — a device that rotates culture vessels to simulate microgravity-like conditions, used here to keep HepG2 spheroids suspended and well-mixed without the shear stress of active perfusion — cultured 3D liver spheroids self-assembled from single-cell suspensions, followed for 28 days. Three measurement streams ran in parallel: 1H NMR exometabolomics (tracking which metabolites the spheroids secrete into the surrounding medium over time, by nuclear magnetic resonance), standard gene-expression assays, and functional liver-marker assays (albumin, urea).

What was found

Dynamic (bioreactor-rotated) and static spheroids reached similar final size. Albumin secretion stabilised after an initial maturation period, and both energy metabolism and hepatic function (albumin, urea production) held steady across a days-14-to-28 functional window — the paper's proposed answer to "when is this platform ready to dose, and for how long does it stay representative." Fetal- and cancer- associated traits (elevated alpha-fetoprotein, a marker HepG2 carries from its cancer-cell-line origin; progressive formate release linked to one-carbon metabolism) persisted throughout, which the vault reads as an honest reminder that HepG2 is a hepatocellular-carcinoma-derived line, not primary liver tissue, regardless of how stable its function looks.

No chemical was tested. This is a characterisation study establishing a baseline the authors propose using to "design repeated-dose toxicity studies" and to benchmark other liver models against — explicitly a platform for future work, not a toxicity result.

Why it lands on the repeat-dose map rather than the liver column's existing HepaRG cluster

repeat-dose-systemic-toxicity-nam is this vault's thinnest endpoint and its most explicit coverage gap — a tiered QSAR+ToxCast+PBK concern-call framework with no formal concordance and three independent literatures (2026-08-05, 2026-08-06, 2026-08-19) converging on "no validated NAM framework answers this question." A platform specifically built to support repeated-dose liver testing, with a stated functional window, is directly relevant to that gap even though it supplies no concern call itself — it is infrastructure for a future method, not a method.

It is also, structurally, the vault's one non-HepaRG liver platform with a stated multi-week stability claim. hepatotoxicity-cholestasis-battery, hepatotoxicity-steatosis-battery and metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg all run in the same licensed HepaRG line — the vault's largest single correlated-blind-spot cluster. This platform runs HepG2, a different (and more widely available, non-single-licensor) immortalised liver line with its own well-documented low metabolic competence relative to HepaRG. It does not resolve the HepaRG cluster's shared-substrate problem — it is a different single substrate, not a metabolically complete one — but it establishes that a repeated-dosing-capable platform does not require HepaRG specifically, which matters for anyone trying to diversify the liver column's cell-line dependence.
