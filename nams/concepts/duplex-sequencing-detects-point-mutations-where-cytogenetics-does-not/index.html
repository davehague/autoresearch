A sequencing-based mutagenicity NAM auto-coins a new genotoxicity map, and its cleanest result is a battery-composition finding

Auto-coins genotoxicity-duplex-sequencing-heparg-mutagenesis. Duplex sequencing is methodologically distinct from every genotoxicity NAM the vault already tracks: not colony selection at a marker locus (TG 476/490), not counting stained micronuclei under a microscope or via imaging flow cytometry (TG 487), not a 64-gene expression signature (TGx-DDI), not a GFP-reporter mode-of-action screen (ToxTracker, TG 446A). It reads point mutations directly off sequenced DNA, at single-molecule sensitivity, with a mutational-signature layer (COSMIC) none of the vault's other genotox NAMs offer.

Why the etoposide result matters more than the topline dose-response curves. Etoposide is a well- characterized clastogen (chromosome-breaking agent) with a topoisomerase-II mechanism that does not necessarily generate point mutations. It produced a strong signal on standard cytogenetic (chromosome-damage) endpoints run in parallel in the same paper, and essentially no signal on duplex-sequencing mutation frequency. Read against genotoxicity-mammalian-gene-mutation's own battery-composition note (TG 476/490's gene-mutation detection is complementary to, not redundant with, TG 487's chromosome-damage detection, "a deliberate overlap, not redundant"), this is a second, independently sourced instance of the same structural point: a point-mutation assay and a chromosome-damage assay are measuring different mechanisms, and a battery needs both rather than more of either.
