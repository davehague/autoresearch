The three blanks, drawn again by people with no knowledge of this vault

"major gaps remain for one-for-one replacement of systemic toxicity, reproductive and developmental toxicity, and carcinogenicity animal tests."

and, flatly:

"Direct replacements for systemic, reproductive and developmental toxicology, and carcinogenicity studies are not available."

Why a statement this unsurprising is worth a note

Because it is the third independent drawing of the same map. The vault's own coverage picture — built one ingested source at a time, from unrelated papers, over seven weeks — carries exactly these three as its headline blanks: repeat-dose systemic toxicity (repeat-dose-systemic-toxicity-nam, emerging, 12-chemical proof of concept, no adoption pipeline), reproductive/fertility (reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams, the vault's only bench-bound: no-nam-exists map — not a poor method, no method), and carcinogenicity (carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay, scored by a trained human down a microscope).

And the endpoints this review says are replaced are, one for one, the endpoints where this vault holds its densest, highest-tier and most loop-ready maps — skin sensitization, skin and eye irritation, phototoxicity. The vault's coverage is not an artifact of what its sweep happened to find. It reproduces the field's actual shape.

The shape of the boundary, and it is not arbitrary

Look at what the two lists have in common internally.

Replaced: local effects. Something is applied to a surface and that surface reacts — skin, eye, absorption. The tissue that gets the dose is the tissue that gets damaged, and you can grow that tissue.

Not replaced: effects that require an organism. Systemic toxicity is what happens somewhere else after the blood carries it there. Reproductive toxicity needs two generations. Carcinogenicity needs two years and a whole body.

The line is not "hard chemistry versus easy chemistry." It is "does the answer require a body." That is why the three blanks have stayed blank while the replaced list filled in, and it is why they will not be closed by a better plate reader.

The loop reading, and it is the uncomfortable one

The vault's closed-loop-ready tier holds 26 maps. Nearly all of them are on the replaced side of this line. A machine could today run a substantial fraction of the endpoints that are already solved, and essentially none of the ones that are not.

Automatability is concentrated exactly where the scientific problem is already finished. That is not an argument against building loops — running the solved endpoints faster and cheaper is worth real money and real animals. It is an argument against reading the loop-readiness table as a measure of progress toward replacing animal testing. The tests a machine could run are largely the tests that no longer need a decision.
