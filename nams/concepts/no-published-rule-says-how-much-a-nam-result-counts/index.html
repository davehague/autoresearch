No published rule says how much a NAM result counts

The vault's dominant loop blocker, one level higher than the vault records it

Twenty-plus maps in this vault carry a version of the same loop_blocker: no agreed procedure converts the number this method produces into the call it is supposed to inform. The vault has always recorded that as a property of the individual assay — Test Guideline 493 produces clean binding numbers and the guideline itself says no agreed interpretation method exists; the Tox21 platform produces fifty million data points and nothing turns them into a hazard call.

This source says the same thing about the layer above all of them, and says it in the regulators' own words. Even where every assay in a battery has a decision rule, the step that combines the battery's answer with everything else known about the chemical does not.

The three questions, verbatim, because they are effectively a specification for the missing rule:

● "When and how can NAM data resolve conflicting lines of evidence?" ● "When can NAM data alone be considered sufficient for decision-making?" ● "How should uncertainty be quantified and compared across different data types?"

And the sentence describing what happens in the vacuum:

"In practice, such questions are often resolved through expert judgement, introducing variability and reducing reproducibility."

Why this is the sharpest sentence in the source for an autonomous lab

loop_readiness in this vault is assigned by a deterministic ladder, and step 3 of that ladder is: human judgment load-bearing in the call → human-in-the-loop. Apply that ladder to the integration layer and it returns human-in-the-loop, from the authors' own description of current practice, with no ambiguity.

The consequence is arithmetic. A pipeline is as loopable as its least loopable step. A machine can run the assays; a machine can apply each assay's decision rule; and then the outputs arrive at a step whose published procedure is expert judgement. Every closed-loop-ready badge in this vault describes a segment of a chain that terminates in a human.

That is not an argument against automating the assays — the assays are where the cost and the throughput are. It is an argument about where the highest-leverage missing artifact in the field is, and this source places it above the assay layer rather than inside it. The vault's stage-07 connect pass has been reporting the same thing from the other direction: batteries that clear end-to-end on their components and then need a weight-of-evidence step. See eye-iata-integration-step-is-weight-of-evidence, more-assays-without-an-integration-rule-manufacture-false-positives and structured-judgement-is-not-a-decision-rule.

The one worked example, and what it costs to have made it

The authors name Defined Approaches for skin sensitisation (OECD Guideline No. 497, 2025) as the case where integration was actually solved — a fixed, published procedure that takes the results of named assays and returns a classification without expert deliberation. It is the vault's canonical closed-loop-ready battery (skin-sensitization-2o3-defined-approach).

Their next sentence is the finding: "However, similar success stories remain limited." One endpoint, out of everything a chemical has to be assessed for, has this.

What it does not license

  • An editorial. No measurement of assessor variability is reported here — the claim that expert judgement reduces reproducibility is an assertion, not a study. The vault holds an independent measurement of that shape in nam-variance-judged-against-animal-test-variance and automation-shrinks-variance-not-the-decision-gap, neither of which is this paper's evidence.
  • "No harmonised rule" is not "nothing works." Assessments are made every day. The claim is that what makes them is not written down in a form anyone else — human or machine — can re-execute and get the same answer.
  • Cosmetics-shaped evidence base, generalised by the authors to regulatory frameworks broadly. The generalisation is theirs.
