ER transactivation (OECD TG 455) → endocrine disruption, the "E" of EATS

OECD endocrine testing is organised around four modalities — Estrogen, Androgen, Thyroid, Steroidogenesis. Until this ingest the vault held the S (h295r-steroidogenesis-endocrine-predictivity, TG 456) and a non-EATS receptor (glucocorticoid-receptor-transactivation-non-eats, TG 454A). TG 455 opens the E, and it is the modality the whole endocrine-disruptor programme was built around: the OECD's high-priority activity on endocrine disrupters began in 1998, and the estrogen receptor is where it started.

The mechanism read. Estrogens bind ER (ERα, and to a lesser extent ERβ); the receptor–ligand complex binds estrogen response elements (EREs) in target-gene DNA and transactivates transcription. TG 455's methods place a firefly luciferase reporter downstream of EREs in a stably transfected line, so luminescence reports ER transactivation — in agonist mode (chemical alone) and antagonist mode (chemical against a fixed agonist concentration).

Performance-Based, which matters here more than usual. TG 455 is a PBTG: it defines the endpoint and a set of performance standards, and admits any method that meets them, rather than fixing one protocol. Three methods currently qualify — STTA (hERα-HeLa-9903), VM7Luc ER TA (VM7Luc4E2), and ERα CALUX. That architecture is what saves the guideline when one of its methods becomes unusable (tg455-vm7luc-adopted-but-unrunnable): the endpoint survives the loss of a method, because the guideline was never a single method to begin with. The same object-versus-implementation distinction the vault drew for defined approaches under GL 497 — a DIP is separable from its assays — reappears here one level down: a PBTG is separable from its test methods.

Scope, in the guideline's words. The methods "are being proposed for screening and prioritisation purposes, but can also provide mechanistic information that can be used in a weight of evidence approach," and "results should not be directly extrapolated to the complex signaling and regulation of the intact endocrine system in vivo." Level 2 of the OECD Conceptual Framework. It says a chemical engages the ER. It does not say the chemical causes endocrine harm, and it emphatically does not say anything about fertility — see reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams, which exists to stop exactly that inference.

A decision rule, unlike its binding sibling. TG 455 ships a concrete positive/negative table (RPC_Max ≥ 10% of the 1 nM E2 positive control, in 2-of-2 or 2-of-3 runs). Its companion TG 493 — which reads ER binding rather than ER activation — ships no agreed decision rule at all (tg493-no-agreed-data-interpretation). Two adopted OECD guidelines on the same receptor, one with a standardised call and one without.

funding_flag: method-developer records that two of the three cell lines are commercially licensed (VM7Luc4E2 via UC Davis and Xenobiotic Detection Systems; ERα CALUX via BioDetection Systems). The ICCVAM/NICEATM validation of the VM7Luc method and the OECD peer review are independent of the developers, and carry the weight.
