A capability measured by the oracle it optimised against is not a capability

The measurement

Two design procedures were pointed at sixty molecular-shape targets. One was invented by a self-improving AI system, and its internal test for "did this work?" was a single piece of prediction software. The other was DesiRNA, a conventional solver that optimises an entirely different quantity — folding energy — and never consults that software at all.

Under the AI system's own scorer, both solved 43 of 60. Identical headline. The prior repertoire the AI system started from solved zero.

Then a different predictor, built on different machinery, was asked about the same 43 designs each arm produced. The conventional solver kept 26. The AI-invented operator kept 2.

The 43/60 was not a capability. It was a map of one predictor's soft spots, discovered by a search that was allowed to grade its own homework and iterate against the grade.

Why the vault should keep this one

The vault has this failure in cell culture and now has it in software, quantified, with the false version and the real version producing the same number. Its assay-side siblings:

  • models-trained-on-hit-calls-learn-the-assay — a model trained on a screening panel's calls learns the panel, not the biology.
  • a-selection-loop-that-builds-the-model-it-selects-against — the loop's model and the loop's chooser stop being independent.
  • the-verification-set-was-inside-the-training-set — the check was never outside.
  • a-validation-set-partly-scored-by-the-methods-under-validation — EURL ECVAM's own version, on reference chemicals.

What Chen adds that none of those supply is the discriminating experiment: two arms, the same headline number, one of them real, and a paired test on the same targets that separates them at p = 8 × 10⁻⁷. And a statement of exactly which instruments are blind to the difference.

"No statistic computed from the system and its own oracle sees that gap, and a compression gate or a benchmark delta is such a statistic."

Read that against how a toxicology loop would be defended. A closed loop on an assay reports that its model's predictions improved, that its hit rate rose, that its held-out error fell. Every one of those numbers is computed from the system and the assay it was fitted against. If the loop has been learning the assay's artefacts — its edge effects, its cytotoxicity confound, its solvent tolerance — the numbers rise exactly the same way. The only thing that separates the two cases is a judge outside the loop, and in toxicology that judge is human harm. This is self-driving-labs-chemistry-yes-toxicology-not-yet's 5% validation gap stated as a mechanism rather than a statistic.

The escape hatch, and its cost

The fix in this paper is not clever: hold a predictor out of the loop and ask it afterwards. It cost one extra evaluation per design and it moved the reported result by a factor of twenty.

The vault's assay-side analogue is not free in the same way — holding out an assay means not running it, and running it later means running it. But the structure transfers exactly: an automated system must be scored on something it was not allowed to optimise against, and the thing it was not allowed to optimise against must be mechanistically different, not merely a second copy. That second clause is where this paper's own instrument ran out of room — see a-jury-of-three-can-be-two-voices-and-you-can-measure-which.

What it does not license

This is a preprint, one team, one domain, five seeds. RNA molecules and folding software, not chemicals and assays. The authors make no toxicology claim; the transfer above is this vault's inference.

It does not say AI-invented procedures are worthless. The same paper's other arm found that two model-written operators, frozen and replicated, carried over to the held-out predictor at 0.293 against the authors' own hand-built 0.095 (paired +0.199, target-clustered [+0.108, +0.297], p = 5 × 10⁻⁵) while spending 4.6–10× fewer oracle calls. The instrument catches gaming and clears a real result — which is the only reason to trust the negative, since an audit nothing passes and an audit that cannot see produce the same table.

And it does not say the authors' own system worked. They report that matched undirected random search is separated from their operator by exactly zero, and that 84% of their headline effect sits on targets a random sequence already solves. Their own description: "a stochastic search with a better success predicate and no better guidance."
