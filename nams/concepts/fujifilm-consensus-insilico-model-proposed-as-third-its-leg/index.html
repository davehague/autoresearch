A third candidate in-silico leg for the ITS, and a proposed all-computational replacement for DPRA — both self-validated by their single industry developer

The finding, plainly

OECD GL497 Part II (the ITS potency defined approach, tracked at skin-sensitization-potency-its-da) scores three information sources — KE1 (DPRA/ADRA), KE3 (h-CLAT/GARD®skin/U-SENS™), and an in-silico prediction (currently Derek Nexus or the OECD QSAR Toolbox) — and sums them to a 0–7 total that maps to UN GHS 1A/1B/Not Classified. Fujifilm proposes a new consensus in-silico model for the third slot, reporting performance that would, if adopted, sit alongside or above the incumbent two in-silico options. It goes further than a drop-in replacement for the in-silico leg alone: a companion protein-binding model is proposed to replace the wet-chemistry KE1 assay (DPRA) itself with a computational prediction, which — if it held up — would turn two of the ITS's three legs computational, leaving only h-CLAT as a physical assay.

Why it lands on two maps

The in-silico-leg proposal is this map's (skin-sensitization-potency-its-da) subject directly — a third candidate at an already-scored, already-computational position. The DPRA-replacement proposal is a different kind of claim, filed as a ripple onto skin-sensitization-dpra: not a new in-silico addition alongside an existing wet assay (as with the ITS leg), but a proposed substitution for one. skin-sensitization-dpra already carries one other zero-data computational proposal in the same direction (skin-sensitization-computational-md-ai-descriptors, MD/docking descriptors feeding a deep-learning model) — this is the second, and unlike that one, this proposal reports actual (self- validated) performance numbers.

What it does not do

No independent replication. All four authors are Fujifilm employees; Fujifilm developed the model and is the commercial beneficiary of its adoption into a regulator-published guideline. The reported 77%/70% and ~80%/~70% figures are the developer's own validation-set numbers, not an OECD EG DASS re-evaluation — contrast skin-sensitization-potency-its-da's own 2026-10-05 entry, where U-SENS's potency numbers were evaluated against OECD's own 196-chemical reference set, a materially stronger evidentiary position than this source holds. No change to Status, evidence_tier, or loop_readiness on either map — GL497 still names only Derek Nexus and the QSAR Toolbox at the in-silico position; this is a candidate, not an adoption. regulatory_status: proposed, not under-validation — no validation body has taken this model up yet.
