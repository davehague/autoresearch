Automated parts, animal input, bottom rung — and the ladder gets it right for once

This vault's loop-readiness ladder is applied in a fixed order, first match wins, and rung 2 is blunt: if the method's own prediction model consumes an in-vivo study, it is bench-bound. Not "mostly automated with a caveat." Bottom rung, with bench_bound_reason: animal-input.

The founding case was the CFU-GM myelotoxicity assay — a bone-marrow test that is fully quantitative, has a fixed decision rule, and would otherwise read as loop-ready. It is bench-bound solely because its scaling model takes a prior mouse maximum-tolerated dose as an input. Fixable only by inventing a new prediction model, never by better robotics.

Tonight the same rung catches something much larger, and the honest reading is that the rule is working.

The Integrated Approach to Testing and Assessment described here — a structured way of combining several kinds of evidence into one regulatory conclusion — names three inputs: "mechanistic data, traditional toxicology findings, and exposure modeling." The middle one is the conventional animal study. The assessment does not merely sit beside an animal study; it consumes one, by construction, as a named ingredient.

The uncomfortable part: nearly every component is automated

  • Electroencephalography — continuous machine-readable electrical recording. No human scorer.
  • Sensor-based behavioural monitoring with AI — replaces a technician looking into a cage at intervals. Explicitly framed as reducing "reliance on episodic observer-dependent measurements."
  • Exposure modelling — software, entirely. The vault's computational-connective tier is exactly this object.
  • Mechanistic data — in-vitro readouts, most of which in this vault are plate-reader numbers.

So the ladder produces an assessment graded at the bottom rung whose components are nearly all at the top. That is not a defect of the grading. It is the grading doing its job, and it is the sharpest available statement of why "percentage of steps automated" is a useless metric for this vault's question.

Two things this note is careful not to say

It does not say the strategy is bad. A well-instrumented animal study feeding a structured integration is a serious piece of safety science, and this vault does not certify or condemn a method for a real testing programme.

It does not say central-nervous-system toxicity is unautomatable. This source describes what one group of practitioners does; it does not test whether a non-animal route exists. The vault has a live and separate case of the shape where nothing exists — bench_bound_reason: no-nam-exists, recorded for reproductive fertility — and keeping the two labels apart is the whole point of having two. animal-input means somebody would have to invent a different prediction model. no-nam-exists means somebody would have to invent an assay. For anyone deciding where to spend, those are different investments, and this endpoint is the first kind.

And the integration step has the vault's other standing defect

Even setting the animal input aside, the combining step is an expert weight-of-evidence judgement with no published scoring algorithm — the identical blocker the vault recorded for the eye-hazard guidance (eye-hazard-iata-gd263), where all nine composed methods produce clean instrument numbers, several are individually closed-loop-ready, and the integration is unspecified. Machine vision, the escape hatch for assays scored by eye, does not reach this blocker — there is nothing to see.

So this map is blocked twice over, at two different heights, and only the lower block is recorded in its tier. The upper one is recorded here.
