The ITS defined approach (OECD GL 497 Part II) → skin sensitisation potency

The Integrated Testing Strategy (ITS) is the second of GL 497's three defined approaches. It covers KE1 (protein binding, TG 442C) and KE3 (dendritic-cell activation, TG 442E) of the skin-sensitisation AOP — deliberately skipping KE2 — and adds an in-silico prediction. Each information source is scored, and the scores are summed into a total battery score of 0–7: KE1 → 0–3 (banded on % peptide/amino-acid depletion), KE3 → 0–3 (banded on MIT or EC150 in µg/mL), in-silico → 1 (positive) or 0 (negative). The data interpretation procedure reads the total: 6–7 → UN GHS 1A (strong sensitiser); 2–5 → UN GHS 1B (other sensitiser); 0–1 → Not Classified. Hazard falls out as a by-product — a score of ≥2 identifies a Cat. 1 sensitiser.

Both axes adopted: evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD Guideline No. 497, Part II, 2 July 2026 edition).

The honest performance statement, which is not "better than the animal test." Against human potency reference data the ITS achieves 54–75% accuracy where the LLNA scores 60% on the same data. The guideline reports that 8 of 10 ITS permutations equal or beat the LLNA. That is a claim of rough equivalence — the ITS range straddles the animal test's single value, on 37–64 chemicals. Equivalence at no animal cost is a genuine result and a sufficient basis for replacement; it is not the claim "the non-animal method is more accurate." Against LLNA reference data the numbers look better (potency 66–80%; hazard balanced accuracy 78–88%), but that measures agreement with the animal assay, not with humans. The guideline also flags that its reference data are imbalanced (more Cat. 1 than NC), making balanced-accuracy "more uncertain, particularly in the case of the human data comparison."

The failure mode is under-protective, and the guideline says so. Two ITS permutations scored below the LLNA against human data "due to several underpredictions of 1A sensitisers as 1B sensitisers" — calling a strong sensitiser moderate. The guideline attributes part of this to reference chemicals sitting near the 1A/1B boundary (its term: GHS 1A-) and instructs that "selection of these specific DAs needs to be justified." An under-prediction of potency is the direction that costs someone something.

A defined approach is an object separable from its assays. The 2021 edition fixed two variants — ITSv1 (DPRA + h-CLAT + Derek Nexus) and ITSv2 (DPRA + h-CLAT + OECD QSAR Toolbox). The 2026 edition keeps these as the "classic ITS DA" but admits alternative information sources at every position — ADRA alongside DPRA at KE1; GARD®skin and U-SENS™ alongside h-CLAT at KE3 — generating a space of ITS permutations, each separately evaluated. The battery changed underneath the DA. The same happened to the 2o3 DA in Part I (EpiSensA, LuSens joining KeratinoSens™ at KE2). What is adopted is a DIP plus an admissible set of information sources, not a fixed triple of assays.

Two accessibility flags on the same axis. The classic ITSv1's in-silico leg, Derek Nexus, is a commercial, license-gated tool; ITSv2's OECD QSAR Toolbox is free. Two otherwise identical adopted DAs differ in who can afford to run them. GARD®skin and U-SENS™ are likewise proprietary platforms. Same family as the licensed HepaRG™ line under TG 445A (cyp-induction-heparg-biotransformation) and the proprietary Corrositex® kit under TG 435 (membrane-barrier-corrosion-subcategorisation).

The ADRA extension comes from Imamura et al. (2022), J Appl Toxicol 42(7):1159–1167 (reference 8). The OECD EG DASS independently re-parameterised the scheme before adoption — moving the 1A cut-off from 7 to 6 and re-basing it from ECETOC categories onto UN GHS sub-categories — and evaluated it against curated reference sets. Developer origin plus independent regulator re-parameterisation: stronger than self-validation, weaker than independent origin. Recorded as both.

An explicit non-composition edge. GL 497 states that Part III's quantitative point-of-departure DAs "cannot be used in Part I or Part II." One battery, three DIPs, three regulatory questions — and no chaining between them. See sara-ice-quantitative-pod-skin-sensitization and regression-based-quantitative-pod-skin-sensitization.
