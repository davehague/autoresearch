The auditor needs an auditor

The companion finding to every-agentic-benchmark-audited-had-validity-flaws, and it is the more uncomfortable half. The same paper that shows five widely used benchmarks carry validity flaws also measures how well its own flaw-detector agrees with human graders, and the answer is: it depends enormously on what you are looking for, and on which model is looking.

Flaw class (n)GPT-5.4 F1Sonnet 4.6 F1
Guessing (79)0.740.54
Ground truth access (333)0.500.50
Answer format ambiguity (163)0.300.37
Tool failure (167)0.270.14

How to read F1 here. The specificity numbers all look excellent — 0.84 to 0.97. That is misleading on its own, because these flaws are rare: a scanner that says "no flaw" almost always is right almost always, and still gets nearly every positive call wrong. F1 combines catching real flaws with not crying wolf, and on tool failure it lands at 0.27 and 0.14 — most of what the scanner flags is not a flaw, and most real flaws are missed.

The structural point, and it is the vault's rail one layer up

The problem: benchmark numbers may not mean what they claim. The proposed instrument: have a language model read the transcripts and tell you. The measurement being checked and the checker share an upstream cause — the same training corpora, the same convergent post-training, the same failure at reading ambiguous specifications that produced the ambiguity in the first place.

The vault recorded this shape on 2026-07-25 as multiple-frontier-models-are-one-vote-not-six: consulting a second model is a bigger battery, and the fix for a correlated blind spot is a measurement that fails differently. Here the correlation lands not between two assays and not between two hypothesis-generators, but between an evaluation and its audit — and the entire agentic-AI field's quality-assurance story runs through that pair.

The honest counter-signal, recorded because it cuts against the vault

The two models disagreed with each other a lot. On tool failure, GPT-5.4 caught roughly three flaws in four (sensitivity 0.74) and Sonnet 4.6 caught roughly one in six (0.17). On guessing it was 0.93 versus 0.44. Those are not two samples from one distribution.

That is evidence against the strong reading of "multiple frontier models are effectively one vote." The Bisht et al. result the vault holds measured semantic similarity of generated hypotheses; this measures agreement on a labelling judgement, and the two are different operations. The vault should carry both: models converge on what they propose and diverge on what they judge, at least here, at least on this task. Neither result generalises to the other without argument.

What it means for a loop

A closed loop needs a quality-assurance layer — something that says "this run is not trustworthy" — and the same argument applies wherever that layer is a model. The vault already holds three instances of this pattern from the laboratory side and each carries the same crack:

  • AEGIS — pre-flight plan checking at F1 0.97, live physical monitoring at 0.71, and 0.47 at the smallest pipette volumes.
  • LabRobFail — a failure detector whose 20,000 ground-truth labels were written by GPT-5.4.
  • This — a benchmark auditor at F1 0.14 to 0.74 against humans.

In every case the checking layer degrades hardest on the thing it exists to catch. For a hazard-testing loop that matters more than for a coding benchmark, because the failure that matters there is the quiet one: a half-delivered dose, an artefact read as a clean negative, a chemical called safe.
