Instrument substitutions are automation that nobody files as automation

The finding, and why a two-author methods note is in a vault about closing loops

An assay had a step where you cook a microplate at 90 °C for an hour. Health Canada replaced it with forty minutes under a UV lamp and got the same answers.

Nothing in this paper mentions robots, screening throughput, or automation. It is a digestion-protocol modification in a toxicology journal. And it is precisely the class of source this vault wrote down, on 2026-07-27, that its own sweep is structurally bad at finding.

That entry came from a near-miss: the vault had spent two weeks and four maps tracking machine vision as the escape hatch for assays scored by eye, and then a routine industrial paper turned out to score the micronucleus assay by plain flow cytometry — no images, no classifier — mentioned in a single sentence of its methods (micronuclei-are-already-counted-by-machine). The lesson logged then:

A sweep that reads the automation literature over-finds machine learning and under-finds instrumentation, because instrument substitutions do not get written up as automation.

Tonight is the second confirmed instance, and it arrived by accident again — through a journal sweep, not through any automation query.

What actually changed, in loop terms

Three properties of the swap, none of them argued by the authors:

1 — A 90 °C hour is a silent data-corruption step. Evaporation concentrates the sample; warping plastic changes the optical path. Neither produces an error. The plate reader returns a number either way, and the number is wrong in a direction nothing downstream can detect. This is the same shape as a-readout-the-test-substance-can-digest, filed the same night: a well-formed, in-range, confidently wrong measurement.

2 — An oven step is a plate transfer; a lamp is a fixture. Every movement of a plate between instruments is a place an automated run drops, mis-seats, or mislabels — the vault's measured version of that is robots-fail-at-the-joins-not-the-motions and the-robot-upstream-of-the-assay-loses-one-embryo-in-seven. Removing a high-temperature incubation removes a join.

3 — Faster and cooler is throughput, and throughput is what makes an experiment-selection loop worth building. A loop that can only run one plate a day cannot pick a next experiment often enough to matter.

And it moves the loop tier by exactly nothing, which is the point

This map is automatable-open-loop. Its blocker is not the wet work. It is that no agreed data-interpretation procedure exists — nothing turns an IC₅₀ from this assay into a regulatory call, for this assay or for any other thyroid method (thyroid-no-validated-in-vitro-method). A better digestion step does not write that rule and does not move the tier.

Keeping those two apart is the whole discipline of the third axis. Making the bench work easier and making the decision automatable are different investments, and the literature that reports the first is not the literature that reports the second. The vault's own ladder is explicit that the decisive question is the decision rule, not the readout — tg493-no-agreed-data-interpretation is the standing example: a fully quantitative, fully instrument-read adopted Test Guideline that is still not closed-loop-ready because the guideline itself says no agreed interpretation method exists.

So the honest summary is: the wet half of this assay got cheaper tonight and the deciding half did not move. That is worth recording precisely because it is the kind of progress that reads, from a distance, like progress toward a loop.

What it does not license

Abstract only — the paper is paywalled with no free deposit. The Z′ values, the IC₅₀ table, the replicate structure and how many "additional reference chemicals" were run are all unread. "Equivalent" is the authors' word and the vault cannot check what it covers.

One laboratory, one lamp, no inter-laboratory work. Nothing about transferability.

MCT8 disruption has no Test Guideline, no defined approach and no cutoff. This is a modification to a research screen, not a validation of one, and it changes neither the scientific-validity nor the regulatory-acceptance axis of this map.

And a cell-uptake measurement is not a thyroid effect in a person. No apical outcome appears anywhere in this work.
