A pharma R&D head writes the industry's own autonomy ladder, and still finds no Level 4

automation-self-driving-labs exists to track the loop machinery itself, split into an execution strand (can the physical act be trusted) and a verification strand (is the instrument scoring it sound). cheng-2026-human-led-agent-driven-autonomous-labs-life-sciences (Cheng, Ma, Shen, Hupalowska, Rood, Zhang, Agrawal, Bakan, Lee, Regev & Ma, 2026; Preprints.org 202608.0273, unreviewed) is a Perspective that spans both strands at once rather than resolving to either — it proposes the organizing taxonomy this map has been assembling evidence against piece by piece.

The ladder, and the strict definition of "autonomy"

L0 (manual) through L5 (general-purpose autonomous lab), mapped onto instrument-level automation (L1), integrated workcells (L2), AI-assisted automation (L3), domain-specific autonomous labs (L4), and cross-domain general-purpose labs (L5). The paper is explicit that autonomy is reserved for L4/L5 only — "systems that sustain closed-loop operation across multiple stages and cycles with failure recovery, verifiability, safety constraints, and audit trails" — and that most current "automation," however impressive, is L2/L3: humans still set goals, choose experiments, diagnose failures, and decide how to adapt, even when execution itself is heavily roboticized.

Where the vault's own prior findings land on this ladder

Scored against this ladder, the real-biology execution demonstrations this map already holds — BioMARS (multi-agent robotic cell passaging), the coding-agent-vs-fine-tuned-VLA transfer result, roboCulture's decide-and-act loop on optical density — are L3 at best: adaptive within one narrow workflow, not sustaining a multi-stage closed loop with its own failure recovery. The paper's own named exemplars (Virtual Lab, LUMI-lab, GPT-5-Ginkgo) fare the same: "the extent of autonomous recovery, machine-verifiable state, provenance, and sustained closed-loop operation varies across these systems. Fully demonstrated Level 4 autonomy therefore remains an open frontier." This is a second, independent framework-level confirmation of the thing this map has found experiment after experiment: narrow, well-instrumented demonstrations, not sustained closed loops.

The three-gate safety architecture — a structure for the execution-assurance problem, not a fix

Pre-execution validation (Gate 1), execution-time safeguards (Gate 2), post-execution auditing (Gate 3) is an architectural answer to the same execution-assurance gap AEGIS quantified numerically (adjusted F1 0.97 pre-run, falling to 0.47 at the smallest pipette volumes — docs/backlog.md, 2026-07-28) and SafeLab found behaviourally (Safe Success Rate trailing Success Rate by >30 points — ingested 2026-10-01). Naming three gates is not the same as having a validated detector at any of them; the paper cites no accuracy figure for its own Gate 1/2/3 and defers that to future benchmark/evaluation work (its own Figure 2c).

Bias watch

Senior/corresponding author Aviv Regev is Head of Genentech (Roche) Research and Early Development, writing alongside a named roadmap for the autonomy ladder a major pharmaceutical company has itself publicly committed to building. funding_flag: industry, per shared/evidence-model.md's "automation-hype variant" — graded with the same scepticism applied to any vendor's "fully automated" claim, even though this paper's content is architectural rather than a performance claim about a specific deployed product, and its honest admission (no demonstrated L4) runs against the obvious promotional interest.
