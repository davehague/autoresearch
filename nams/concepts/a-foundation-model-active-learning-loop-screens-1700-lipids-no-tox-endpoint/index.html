A real closed loop, real hardware, real in vivo confirmation — and still the chemistry/materials side, not the toxicology side, of this vault's crossover question

automation-experiment-selection exists to track whether anything can be trusted to choose the next experiment. LUMI-lab is a clean, well-instrumented instance of exactly that — a foundation model proposing candidates, robots executing, an automated assay reading transfection potency, the loop updating itself — run to completion over 1,700+ designs and confirmed, not just predicted, in a mouse model. It sits alongside mombot-active-learning-converges-on-measured-id50-biology-not-chemistry and agentic-ai-closes-a-real-biology-loop-on-yeast-metabolism as evidence that closed-loop experiment selection can work on real biological material, not only on chemistry/materials benchmarks with a known ground truth to score against.

Still the wrong side of the crossover this vault is watching for. The readout is mRNA-delivery potency and, downstream, gene-editing efficiency — a pharmacological efficacy chain, not a hazard call. No chemical here was screened for toxicity, and no regulatory content appears anywhere in the retrievable abstract. The loop's own uncertainty-handling — the standing question this map asks of every source — is not independently assessed here either; the claimed success is read off the downstream potency numbers rather than off a validated confidence calibration, the same gap this map has flagged repeatedly.

Funding flag matters here specifically. Several authors have filed a patent on the model and the discovered lipids, including the headline comparison against Moderna's own vaccine lipid — the kind of platform-performance claim this vault's bias watch (automation-hype variant) names explicitly: graded with the same scepticism as a method developer's claim about their own assay.
