The comet assay's scoring bottleneck has an open-source machine-vision fix — and it still doesn't tell you whether the DNA damage it counts means "positive"

The comet assay has been "human-in-the-loop" the same way the vault's other eye-scored assays are: a trained person looks at a microscope image and sorts each cell into a damage class. AIComet removes that person. A YOLOv11 object detector, trained on 24,581 hand-classified nuclei, both finds and classifies every nucleus in an image using the exact four-class-plus-undefined scheme OECD TG 489 already specifies — and it does this fast enough (~3,500 nuclei in 10 seconds) to score orders of magnitude more cells per sample than any human scorer realistically would.

The evidence for the tool itself is unusually strong for this vault's automation column. Most machine-vision escape hatches recorded here arrive with a single-lab benchmark and nothing else. This one was checked against 11 to 13 independent expert investigators from separate laboratories, on three image sets the model had never seen — different cell lines, different stains, different scanners — and its scores landed within about a point of the experts' own medians on all three. It is also open source (code and trained weights published, not "available upon reasonable request" — the phrase this vault has flagged as an unfulfilled promise on at least two other maps), and funded entirely by an academic charity and a doctoral fellowship, with an explicit no-conflicts declaration.

What it does not do is decide anything. The output is a continuous DNA-damage quantification score, 0–100 arbitrary units — a measurement, not a verdict. Nothing in this source converts that score into a genotoxic/non-genotoxic call. For the in vivo animal protocol OECD TG 489 actually governs, that call comes from a statistical trend test and pairwise comparison against concurrent and historical negative controls — a step this paper does not touch, and its own validation cohort (human biomonitoring blood samples, plus cultured HeLa/THP-1 cells) never runs through that regulatory pipeline at all.

So the comet assay now has the same shape of loop-readiness gap the vault already recorded for genotoxicity-3d-skin-comet and genotoxicity-tgx-ddi-transcriptomic-biomarker: the readout is solved, and the interpretation is not. But it arrives at that gap by a cleaner route than most — no access_gate on the tool itself, no vendor claim to discount, an inter-laboratory comparison instead of a single lab's word.
