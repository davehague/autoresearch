FDA's model-risk matrix prices autonomy — and the vault has been tracking it as if it were free

"Model risk is a combination of two factors: (a) model influence, which is the contribution of the evidence derived from the AI model relative to other contributing evidence used to inform the question of interest and (b) decision consequence … The ratings for decision consequence and model influence are independently determined." — FDA, Considerations for the Use of AI to Support Regulatory Decision-Making (DRAFT, January 2025)

Read the grading before the finding

evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted records the standing of the sourcing instrument — the claim is quoted from the regulator's own primary document — exactly as the vault graded fda-fit-for-purpose-acceptance-without-validation. The framework itself is a DRAFT (regulatory_status: proposed), nonbinding, "Not for Implementation," comments closed April 2025, and no final version is confirmed. strength: suggestive, deliberately.

One further honesty flag, and it is important: this guidance was written for AI models as evidence producers in a dossier, not for AI agents running assays. Reading it as a verdict on autonomous laboratories is an extrapolation by this vault, not a claim FDA makes. It is flagged as such. What makes the extrapolation worth making is that the matrix keys on a property — how alone the model is in the decision — which is the one property an autonomous loop cannot reduce without ceasing to be autonomous.

The finding

Look at what model influence actually measures. It is not:

  • how accurate the model is,
  • how complex or opaque it is,
  • how much data trained it.

It is: how much of the decision rests on the model, and how little rests on anything else.

That is a measure of autonomy. And it is one of the two multiplicands of risk. So:

Closing the loop maximises model influence by construction. A closed loop is the configuration in which the model is the sole determinant. There is no version of "the AI picks the next experiment and makes the call" in which the AI's evidentiary contribution relative to other evidence is anything but maximal. The other multiplicand — decision consequence — is fixed by the question, not by the architecture. So the only lever autonomy touches, it pushes all the way up.

And everything downstream is proportional: "the stringency of the credibility assessments and the performance acceptance criteria, the risk mitigation strategy, and the type and extent of documentation … should be commensurate with the AI model risk."

FDA's own examples say it out loud — in both directions

  • Clinical. An AI model decides which trial participants skip inpatient monitoring. Influence: high, "because the AI model will be the sole determinant of which type of patient monitoring a participant undergoes." Consequence: high. → Risk: HIGH.
  • Manufacturing. An AI vision system checks vial fill volumes; a bad call means medication errors, so consequence is high. But humans independently verify fill volume on a sample of every batch at release, so the AI "will not be the sole determinant." FDA's own words: "Measuring fill volume through release testing would reduce the AI model influence, and therefore the model influence would be determined to be low." → Risk: MEDIUM.

Same consequence. Same agency. Same matrix. The difference is a human doing an independent check — and that difference is worth a whole risk tier.

Stated plainly: under this framework, the cheapest way to lower your regulatory burden is not to build a better model. It is to make the model matter less.

What this does to the vault's third axis

The vault added loop_readiness on 2026-07-12 and has been climbing it like a ladder: bench-bound → human-in-the-loop → automatable-open-loop → closed-loop-ready, with the top rung as the goal and each map's loop_blocker as the thing to remove. Nineteen maps sit at the top rung today.

FDA's matrix runs the same axis in reverse. Every rung the vault treats as progress is a rung FDA treats as rising model influence, and therefore rising model risk, and therefore a heavier evidentiary burden.

These are not contradictory — they are the two halves of one true statement, and the vault has only been holding one of them:

loop_readiness measures whether a machine could make the call. model influence measures what it costs you when it does. The vault has been measuring the capability and ignoring the price.

shared/evidence-model.md already insists that "closed-loop-ready is not an endorsement — it means a machine could make the call, not that it should." This concept supplies the regulator's own reason why, and it is not an ethical caveat. It is an evidentiary invoice, itemised by FDA.

The correct reading, and its limits

Nothing here forbids a closed loop, and nothing here says an autonomous method cannot be accepted. FDA's framework is a proportionality rule, not a bar: high risk means more credibility evidence, not no. A closed-loop system that produces genuinely more evidence — full provenance, monitored drift, characterised applicability domain, an orthogonal control like Tox21's cytotoxicity multiplex (tox21-cytotoxicity-multiplexing-shared-failure-mode) — can in principle meet a high-risk bar that a sloppier human-in-the-loop system would fail.

The finding is not "regulators won't allow loops." It is "regulators have already written down the price of one, and it scales with exactly the thing that makes a loop a loop." That price belongs on the vault's third axis, and it is not there yet.
