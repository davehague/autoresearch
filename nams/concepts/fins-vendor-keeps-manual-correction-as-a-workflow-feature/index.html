The fourth machine-vision attempt at this map's blocker is the first to build the human back in by design

developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity's loop_blocker is graded morphological malformation typing scored by eye. Three prior sources tested whether a machine could take that call: Sivaprasad 2026 (academic Vision Transformer, binary detection, 92%/98%, single study), Al-Absi 2026 (a purchased commercial classifier, independently evaluated by the buyer, whose quality labels turned out statistically unrelated to survival/hatching/deformity), and Pei 2026 (academic, eight named phenotype categories plus an automated EC50 fit matching manual scoring within ~2-9%). FINS is a fourth, and the first built by the same institution that sells it, evaluated only in its own paper — the same self-report/self-evaluation shape as Al-Absi's device before an independent buyer measured it.

What is different, and worth keeping. Unlike a classifier sold as a drop-in replacement, FINS's own description makes manual correction of the model's calls a first-class, named feature — "a seamless workflow for both automated and expert-reviewed scoring" — rather than a limitation the vendor is trying to engineer away. Read plainly, the platform's stated design does not attempt to remove the human from the call; it speeds the human's work (>70% less annotation time) and surfaces confidence scores for review. That is a real, useful automation gain that does not touch loop_readiness, which asks whether the call itself no longer needs a person, not whether the person works faster.

Why direction: limits rather than loop-move. No exact accuracy/F1 numbers are available in the open literature (full text gated), no fixed decision rule is described, and the vendor's own architecture keeps the human load-bearing in the call by design. This does not promote loop_readiness on developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity, which stays human-in-the-loop.

Bias watch — the vendor-versus-buyer gap, a second instance. developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity's 2026-08-15 entry on Al-Absi 2026 named this pattern precisely: a manufacturer's self-report of an automated scorer's accuracy is a funding_flag claim graded exactly as sceptically as a method developer's claim about their own assay's sensitivity, and the distance closes only when an independent buyer or lab measures the tool against ground truth. FINS has not yet had that measurement; six of eight authors are its own vendor's employees, named explicitly in the paper's own competing-interest statement.
