The third category comes from the clock, not from the biology

What was missing. Every eye method in this vault before tonight answers one half of the question. TG 492 and TG 494 can only say "no classification needed" — a negative terminates, a positive means "keep testing." TG 460 can only say "serious damage." TG 496 straddles slightly (both kits rule out; one kit rules in Cat 1, badly). Nothing single could return the middle class, Category 2 — reversible eye irritation — which is where most real consumer chemistry lives. Only the TG 467 defined approach, which combines two assays, could resolve all three.

What TG 492B does. Same tissue as TG 492: reconstructed human cornea-like epithelium, at least four viable cell layers, grown at an air–liquid interface. Same readout as TG 492: the MTT dye conversion, read as optical density. The only thing that changes is how long the chemical stays on the tissue, and that the guideline reads the pattern of viability across several durations rather than one number at one time.

  • Liquids (TTL): three exposures — 5 minutes neat, then 16 and 120 minutes at 20% dilution.
  • Solids (TTS): two exposures — 30 and 120 minutes.

Why time carries the category. GHS Category 1 means irreversible damage and Category 2 means reversible damage. A method that measures cell death at one instant cannot see reversibility. A method that measures how fast the tissue dies can approximate it: a chemical that kills the epithelium within five minutes neat behaves like one that will destroy the cornea outright; one that only kills it after two hours of dilute exposure behaves like an irritant. The kinetics stand in for the persistence.

That substitution is the method's cleverest idea and its deepest limitation, and the guideline says so: "The reversibility of tissue lesions cannot be evaluated per se in the HCE TTT test method." It infers the Cat 1 / Cat 2 boundary from cytotoxicity speed; it does not measure the property the boundary is defined on. That is the mechanistic root of the performance ceiling in tg492b-three-way-resolution-costs-a-quarter-of-its-accuracy — and it is precisely the same blind spot GD 263 names for the ex-vivo Cat-1 methods, which "underpredict 14-47% of the in vivo Cat. 1 chemicals … mostly those inducing persistent effects" (bcop-and-ice-share-a-persistence-blind-spot).

What it means for the loop. A three-way classifier is a materially different object from a rule-out for anything automated. A rule-out returns "safe" or "I don't know" — half its outputs are a request for another experiment, i.e. a branch the loop has to plan around. A three-way classifier terminates on every input. TG 492B is the first adopted eye method whose output is always an answer.

Do not read that as endorsement. Always answering is not the same as always being right, and this method's headline accuracy is the lowest of any adopted eye method in the vault. A loop that prefers it because it never returns "I don't know" would be optimising for terminability, not correctness.
