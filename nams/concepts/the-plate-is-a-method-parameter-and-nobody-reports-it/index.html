The consumable is part of the method, and it is not in the method description

The assay. Before an immune-stimulating antibody drug goes into a human for the first time, regulators expect the sponsor to check in a dish whether it makes immune cells dump a flood of cytokines — the reaction that put six healthy volunteers in intensive care in the 2006 TGN1412 trial. The standard format sticks the antibody to the bottom of a well and adds human donor blood immune cells (PBMCs). There is no Test Guideline; the source states that "methodology is not standardized."

What was varied, and what happened. GSK held the cells, the antibody and the readout fixed and changed only the plate:

  • Costar polystyrene — strongest, most sensitive response. The recommendation.
  • Falcon polystyrene — "reduced cytokine induction, and lower apparent potency, reflecting slower association of mAbs onto Falcon polystyrene." Same plastic family, different manufacturing. The antibody reaches the surface more slowly, so less of it is presented, so the response is smaller and the drug looks less dangerous than it is.
  • Polypropylene — "higher background stimulation of PBMCs." The cells are partly activated before the drug arrives, compressing the signal against the noise.
  • Well shape — "essentially no difference" between flat- and round-bottom Costar wells. This is the control that matters: it rules out geometry and localises the effect to surface chemistry.
  • A saturation ceiling — Costar plates are saturated at 10 µg/mL OKT3 (2 µg/well). Above that, more antibody is more antibody in solution and no more antibody on the surface, so any dose-response drawn through that region is measuring nothing.

Why this is a loop finding and not a laboratory-practice note

The vault has been circling this class of problem for a month and has never had it measured. The backlog carries an item opened 2026-08-26 and extended 2026-08-27 stating the generalisation: a closed loop inherits every fixed pre-analytical parameter of the protocol it automates, and fixed parameters never appear in a result table. Its three prior instances were all inferred from guideline text — the phototoxicity tiers sharing one light source, ambient oxygen across the whole in-vitro column, and the SENS-IS assay under-calling potency on standard-thickness tissue. This one is an experiment. Somebody varied the consumable and watched the answer move.

The failure has the three properties that make it dangerous to an automated system, and it has all three at once:

  1. It is silent. The plate produces a clean dose-response curve and a plausible potency. Nothing in the output says "wrong plastic."
  2. It is perfectly reproducible. A laboratory on Falcon plates gets the same attenuated answer every run, every day, every donor. Repetition cannot detect it. A second laboratory buying the same plate reproduces it exactly — which would read, in a ring trial, as inter-laboratory agreement.
  3. It is directional, and it points the unsafe way. The bias is toward lower apparent potency in an assay whose whole job is to catch a hazard before a human is dosed. A false-reassurance bias in a safety screen.

And a loop is structurally unable to catch it. The plate type is not a variable in the data model; it is a fact about the deck the robot is loaded with. A closed loop cannot detect a bias that is constant across every experiment it runs — it has no contrast to measure against. It can only inherit the bias and iterate confidently on top of it, which is the vault's standing objection to trusting a decision rule without knowing what it shares with itself.

Note what the recommended fix actually is. The paper's answer to "the method is under-specified" is "we therefore recommend using Costar polystyrene plates." A catalogue number. That is sound practice and it is also an access_gate-shaped answer: the specification for a regulator-expected safety assay becomes a dependency on one manufacturer's surface chemistry, unwritten in any guideline.

What this does not say

It does not say the cytokine release assay is wrong, or that in-vitro CRS testing does not work. It says the assay is format-sensitive and the format is not specified anywhere binding. And the study is one company, one laboratory, one very strong positive control (OKT3 anti-CD3) — whether a weak agonist shows the same plate spread is unmeasured, and every magnitude is unread behind a subscription.
