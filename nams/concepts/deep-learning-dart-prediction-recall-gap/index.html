Deep learning aimed at the fertility blank — and the recall gap

reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams is the vault's documented blank: no non-animal method exists for apical developmental and reproductive toxicity, bench_bound_reason: no-nam-exists. This is the model layer taking a run at it — and the honest result is that the blank does not narrow.

The approach is sound in shape. Twenty-three US EPA ToxCast bioassays (ToxCast is the EPA's high-throughput chemical screening programme — the same automated infrastructure as automation-tox21-qhts-platform) were pre-selected by mechanism, not by data availability: estrogen signalling, angiogenesis, retinoic-acid receptor modulation, steroidogenesis and others repeatedly implicated in DART. A graph neural network predicts activity in each; the 23 predictions are aggregated by majority vote into one DART call. Validation is against ECVAM ReProTect, whose labels come from in-vivo evidence.

The number, and the number that is missing

Reported on 90 reference chemicals: F1 0.68, precision 0.93, recall 0.57, accuracy 0.61, specificity 0.81.

The paper's abstract calls this "balanced predictive performance … confirming the reliability and generalizability of the fine-tuned DGCL model."

⚠️ VAULT INFERENCE — arithmetic on the paper's own reported numbers, flagged as this vault's, not the authors'. The evaluation set is 75 positive and 16 negative — about 83% positive. A model that calls every chemical toxic therefore scores precision 0.83, recall 1.00, F1 ≈ 0.91. The reported F1 of 0.68 sits well below that trivial majority-class baseline, on the very metric the authors selected as primary because of class imbalance. The paper reports no baseline comparison. The class balance is stated openly in the paper's own Table 1; this is not a hidden defect, it is an uncomputed one.

The direction of the error is the wrong one for this tier

Recall 0.57 means the model misses 43% of chemicals with established reproductive or developmental toxicity.

The paper frames the precision/recall trade-off as conservatism: "higher precision and lower recall … These features are appropriate for an early-stage screening tool meant to help prioritize chemicals rather than to make final negative decisions." The authors do concede "sensitivity to some positive chemicals may still be limited."

The vault's accumulated reading of screening tiers points the other way, and it is arithmetic, not preference. A screening or rule-out tier is graded on the reliability of its negative call:

  • TG 495 (ROS photoreactivity): 100% sensitivity, no false negatives, specificity 42–82% — and the vault recorded that as the case turning "read specificity against battery position" into an arithmetic finding.
  • TG 492 (RhCE eye rule-out): sensitivity 95–99%, specificity 63–72%; the guideline says the false positives "are not critical" because a positive never classifies.
  • TG 496's OI® at 54% sensitivity is flagged in the vault as the honest weak spot, "misses nearly half" — the reason the guideline forbids it standing alone.

A first-tier screen with 57% recall is in TG 496 OI® territory, not TG 495 territory. High precision on an 83%-positive set is close to free; missing two of every five real toxicants is the expensive failure. Calling it "conservative" inverts which direction conservatism runs in a safety screen.

What it does and does not change

Nothing moves. reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams stays bench-bound / no-nam-exists; in-silico-qsar-read-across stays consolidating / computational-connective. One group, one pipeline, no independent replication, no ring trial, no regulatory standing → single-lab-exploratory / none / single-study.

What it contributes is a clean instance of a reporting problem, and its cleanliness is the point: funding_flag: none, publicly funded (Korea Environmental Industry & Technology Institute), academic authors with no stake in the assays or any platform, data shared, limitations listed, and the failure of their own headline method (multi-task learning) reported honestly. A paper doing almost everything right still ships a conclusion its numbers do not carry — because no convention required the one comparison that would have caught it. That is ml-model-reporting-reproducibility's finding arriving as a worked example rather than a statistic.

One thing worth borrowing. The model carries a computed applicability domain — nearest-neighbour distance on MACCS molecular fingerprints, threshold mean + 0.5σ, out-of-domain predictions excluded.
