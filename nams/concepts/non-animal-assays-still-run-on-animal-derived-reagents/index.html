The non-animal methods are running on mouse tumour and cow blood

Every method in this vault is here because it replaces an animal. This note is about what is still inside them.

A veterinary-school 3Rs group read the materials lists of the seven human-cell-based assays in the developmental neurotoxicity in-vitro battery. All seven have removed fetal bovine serum — the component everyone knows about, the one Good Cell Culture Practice names. All seven still contain animal-derived material, mostly undeclared and mostly unexamined:

WhatWhere it comes fromHow widespread
Matrigel"Murine Engelbreth-Holm-Swarm (EHS) sarcoma extract" — a mouse tumour2 of 7
EHS lamininthe same mouse tumour4 of 7
Bovine serum albumincattle bloodacross supplements; also the carrier growth factors ship in
Fibronectinbovine blood plasma2 of 7
Antibodiesmouse hybridoma; goat polyclonalimmunodetection workflows

≈16 mice per 100 mL of Matrigel. An assay adopted to avoid animals, whose scaffold is farmed from them.

The scientific problem, separate from the ethical one

"Animal-derived matrices introduce significant limitations, including batch-to-batch compositional and mechanical variability and undefined growth factor content, which can confound lineage commitment and reproducibility."

Undefined growth factor content means the plate contains biologically active molecules nobody has enumerated, at concentrations nobody has measured, varying between lots. For a differentiation assay — which is what most of these are — that is not an impurity. It is an uncontrolled arm of the experiment.

And the field's knowledge of how bad it is, is itself structurally suppressed:

"Potential limitations such as reduced cell adhesion or altered differentiation efficiency are likely underreported, as negative or non-optimizing results are rarely published."

The same shape as ml-model-reporting-reproducibility — nothing here says the assays are wrong; it says the record needed to check is not being kept. Two 2026 sources, one auditing model reporting and one auditing reagent provenance, arriving at the identical diagnosis about different halves of the pipeline.

Why this needs its own map rather than a line on the DNT map

The finding is scoped to DNT because that is what was audited, but nothing about it is specific to DNT. Matrigel is in organoid protocols everywhere. BSA is in most supplement formulations. The vault already logged an instance from a different endpoint entirely on 2026-07-18: α-Sens®, the new OECD TG 442D skin-sensitisation method whose stated novelty is being fetal-bovine-serum-free — "removing an animal-derived product from a non-animal test." That was recorded as a curiosity. It was an instance of this.

shared/evidence-model.md has no field for it, and the existing candidate does not fit. access_gate is about availability — the VM7Luc4E2 cell line nobody can obtain, single-licensor HepaRG, sole-kit Corrositex. This is the opposite: you can buy Matrigel this afternoon. You cannot buy the same Matrigel twice. A gate you can pass and a gate you can pass differently each time are different objects, and only one of them is currently modelled.

The loop consequence — flagged as a vault inference

⚠️ The paper never mentions AI, automation, closed loops or active learning. Everything in this section is the vault's reading.

A closed loop's founding assumption is that a change in the readout means a change in the chemistry. An undeclared lot-to-lot shift in a mouse-tumour extract violates that assumption silently. A human operator eventually learns the folklore — the cells went strange after the new Matrigel lot — and discounts it. An active-learning loop has no representation for reagent lot at all; it sees a shifted dose–response, treats it as information, and updates on it.

This is the automation-tox21-qhts-platform counter-design problem in a new place. Tox21's answer to correlated error was a measurement that fails differently, multiplexed into the same well. The analogous answer here is a chemically defined input contract — and the paper argues for exactly that on 3Rs grounds, without knowing it is also the automation argument. Two literatures, one remedy, no citation between them.

Honest brakes

  • This is a review, not a measurement. It documents that batch variability exists and matters, by citation. It contains no original quantification — no "X% of assay variance." Do not let a later run round this up into a number it does not contain.
  • The replacements are not validated. "Current evidence is largely based on proof-of-concept studies, and systematic comparisons between animal-derived and fully defined alternatives across DNT-IVB endpoints remain scarce." And swapping has consequences: "Changes in baseline phenotype, sensitivity, or readouts, have been reported even when technical adaptation is successful." Removing the mouse tumour may change the answer, and nobody has systematically checked by how much.
  • Cost is not uniformly the barrier. Recombinant albumin is €0.0022 per 5 mL dearer — effectively free. Recombinant laminin is ~2× (€622/mg vs €296/mg). The obstacle is mostly revalidation effort and incumbency, not price.
  • funding_flag: none. An EU PARC-funded veterinary-school 3Rs group auditing the supply chain of assays it does not sell — the same clean-outsider posture as the statistics department in ml-model-reporting-reproducibility, and the structural opposite of the automation-hype variant.
  • Not bench_bound_reason: animal-input. That label is for a method whose prediction model consumes an in-vivo study (the CFU-GM mouse MTD). Animal-derived reagents and animal-derived model inputs are different problems with different fixes; collapsing them is exactly the error bench_bound_reason was split in two to prevent.
