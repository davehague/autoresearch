Swapping the step-3 assay in the 2-out-of-3 battery — what it costs, and what the accuracy number hides

OECD Guideline 497 adopts defined approaches for skin allergy: fixed recipes that run several non-animal assays and combine them with a published rule. The vault's flagship is the "2 out of 3" (2o3) approach — three assays covering the first three steps of the biological pathway to skin sensitisation, and whichever answer two of them agree on is the answer. It is skin-sensitization-2o3-defined-approach, and it is the clearest closed-loop-ready case here: every readout is an instrument, and the guideline calls its own combination rule "a transparent, rule-based approach requiring no expert judgment."

OECD is now considering letting labs substitute alternate assays at each step. Nardelli et al. (2025) test one such swap: replace skin-sensitization-hclat (the h-CLAT, which measures the CD86 immune marker on THP-1 cells) with skin-sensitization-u-sens (U-SENS™, which measures the same CD86 marker on U937 cells — a different immortalised human white-blood-cell line).

The result, plainly.

2o3 with U-SENS™ at step 3vs the mouse LLNAvs human data
plain 150% cut-off71% balanced accuracy (168 chemicals)77% (66 chemicals)
with the borderline band77% (142 chemicals)88% (55 chemicals)

Balanced accuracy averages the hit rate on true sensitisers with the hit rate on true non-sensitisers, so a coin flip scores 50%. 71% against the animal reference is a long way above chance and a long way from settled.

The accuracy gain is a shrinking denominator, and this is the part to hold onto. Applying the band did not make a single call more correct. It withdrew the chemicals the assay could not resolve — 26 of 168 against the LLNA (15%), 11 of 66 against human data (17%) — and the score on what was left went up. That is honest and defensible behaviour for an assay. It is not "the substituted battery is 88% accurate." The substituted battery is 88% accurate on the six-sevenths of chemicals it agrees to answer about, and silent on the rest. Anyone reading the headline number without the sample sizes reads a better test than exists.

For comparison, the incumbent battery with h-CLAT at step 3 is recorded on skin-sensitization-2o3-defined-approach at 72–97% balanced accuracy against human data (a 2021 figure). The U-SENS™ substitution therefore lands at or below the bottom of the incumbent's published range on the plain cut-off, and inside it only after the no-call band is applied. This is a substitution that holds performance roughly level while narrowing what gets answered — not an upgrade.

Why the vault takes this seriously anyway. Guideline 497's 2026 edition already established that what the guideline adopts is a data-interpretation procedure plus an admissible set of information sources, not a fixed triple of assays. This is the first source in the vault that measures what one of those substitutions actually costs — and it comes with a real number attached, from the substituting party's own hand.

funding_flag: [industry, method-developer] — U-SENS™ is L'Oréal's assay and three of the five authors are L'Oréal's. This is a developer arguing for their own product's admission to a guideline. The analysis is public and checkable and the borderline model runs against the developer's commercial interest (it makes their assay refuse to answer more often), but it is a submission, not an independent validation, and it carries no ECVAM or ICCVAM imprimatur.
