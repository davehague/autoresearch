CNN scoring of Bhas 42 transformed foci → carcinogenicity (the third escape hatch)

The cell transformation assay (carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay, OECD GD 214 SHE / GD 231 Bhas 42) is stuck at loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop for one reason: transformed foci are scored by eye, by morphology, and the guidance names that subjectivity as a known weakness. This is a loop-move claim about that exact bottleneck — and the first one this map has ever carried. Until now it was the only one of the vault's three eye-scored maps (with micronucleus and zebrafish teratogenicity) with no demonstrated escape hatch.

What Masumoto et al. (2021) showed. An off-the-shelf 18-layer ResNet (a convolutional neural network) learns to classify a colony image as a transformed focus or not — the judgment a trained human otherwise makes. On the chemical it trained on (the tumour promoter TPA) it scored AUC 0.95; on two chemicals it had never seen it dropped to AUC 0.87. Its clearest value is consistency: novice human scorers labelled anywhere from 20% to 80% of the same images as positive, and the network removes that spread.

Why the map does NOT move to loop-ready — three reasons, all recorded.

  1. It scores foci, not the endpoint. Contrast imaging-flow-cytometry-micronucleus-deep-learning-scoring: the micronucleus scorer reproduced the benchmark dose, the regulatory number. This one stops at classifying individual colony images; the focus count → carcinogenicity call is still downstream, and the CTA already can't stand alone.
  2. Single-lab, single-chemical-trained, not validated. One lab, trained largely on TPA, generalisation to unseen chemicals already visibly weaker (0.95 → 0.87). No ring trial, not in the guidance → evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, strength: single-study, regulatory_status: none for the automation (the CTA's own science axis is untouched — never infer one axis from another).
  3. The ground truth is the thing it replaces. Trained on expert labels, so it standardises to the subjective human call, not away from it. A more consistent subjective scorer, not an objective one.

What it does change: the reading of the blocker, and the vault-level tally. "Scored by eye" is now a demonstrated-solvable problem for all three eye-scored maps rather than two of three. But this is the weakest of the three demonstrations: micronucleus (Wills 2021) is inter-lab and reproduces the regulatory answer; zebrafish (Sivaprasad 2026) is single-lab but scores the malformation directly; this one is single-lab and only classifies foci. The honest asymmetry: the map that most needed an escape hatch — carcinogenicity, the hardest endpoint to replace — got the thinnest one.

The one axis where it is cleanest. funding_flag: none — academic and public-sector authors, no competing interests, no instrument or assay vendor. The micronucleus escape hatch is scientifically stronger but industry-co-authored (funding_flag: [industry], the automation-hype variant). Here the science is thinner but the bias watch is clean. Neither trade — nor the zebrafish one — is yet validated, and none is in a guideline. Whichever of the three gets a validated automated scorer first (an ECVAM/ICCVAM ring trial, or adoption of the automated readout into the guideline/guidance) is the vault's next likely human-in-the-loop → automatable-open-loop promotion.
