The rule is written down. It is still not the same rule in two buildings.

The vault's loop-readiness ladder asks a binary question at its top rung: is there a fixed, deterministic data-interpretation procedure? Usually the answer is a clean no — nobody wrote one. This assay is the awkward case where somebody did, and the answer is still not yes.

The reconstructed-skin Comet assay measures % tail DNA with software, not with a person. The number that comes out is objective. What happens to that number is a three-part test, and the third part is:

is this result outside this laboratory's historical negative-control range (mean ± 2 SD)?

That is a rule whose parameters are local. Lab A and Lab B, running the same chemical on the same tissue with the same scoring software, can produce the same measured value and reach different verdicts, because their accumulated control distributions differ. Neither is wrong. The rule says so.

Why this is a distinct blocker shape and not just "no rule"

The vault's 23 no-rule maps mostly mean nobody has published a procedure. This is different, and it is worth its own name:

  • automatable-open-loop for a new reason. Not "the interpretation step is missing," but "the interpretation step exists and is not sample-independent."
  • It is the same structural defect the vault already recorded for TGx-DDI (genotoxicity-tgx-ddi-transcriptomic-biomarker), whose three-classifier rule is fixed but whose two unsupervised legs are batch-relative — so a loop choosing its own next compounds continuously changes what its own classifier is conditioned on. Two entirely unrelated genotoxicity methods, one from 2021 and one from 2025, land on the same failure.
  • And it is the object the vault's own article "The rule that depends on what else was on the plate" is about. A third instance, from a third direction.

What it means for a loop specifically

An autonomous system running this assay is not executing a portable procedure. It is executing a procedure plus a dataset — the historical controls — and the dataset is a property of one building, one tissue lot supply, one scoring configuration.

Three consequences:

  1. Transfer is not free. Standing the loop up elsewhere means accumulating a new historical control distribution first, which takes time no software change can compress.
  2. The loop moves its own goalposts. Every negative control it runs joins the distribution it is judged against. Run enough of them under a drifting condition and the acceptance window tracks the drift.
  3. It is invisible in the axes. Nothing in evidence_tier or regulatory_status records this. It is precisely the class of thing the domain_gate and access_gate modifiers exist to catch, and neither of them fits: the barrier is not the chemical and not a licence, it is the laboratory's own past.

Recorded as an open question rather than a settled downgrade: the vault does not know whether the historical control criterion is load-bearing in practice or whether the other two criteria carry the calls on their own. The validation paper does not decompose it.
