The validation body says the rule cannot be written yet

The vault's single largest loop blocker is no agreed decision rule: nineteen method maps produce clean instrument numbers that nobody has written down a procedure for converting into a hazard call. For most of this vault's life that has been described as the cheapest blocker to fix — historical debt, a committee job.

On 2026-07-28 that description got its first qualifier, from a proof-of-concept kidney screen whose authors declined to publish a rule because three compounds were too few to justify one. Tonight the same argument arrives from the top of the evidence ladder, about a formal 18-method validation study run by the European Union's own validation laboratory across fifteen laboratories:

"Without sufficient data and clear criteria to interpret the results it is not possible to discriminate weak from strong responses… Only when having more data from chemicals with different potency, a solid data interpretation procedure can be designed for the methods, and potency might be assessed."

The circular dependency, stated by the people it constrains

The structure is: you cannot write the rule without potency data across a range of strengths; you cannot efficiently generate that data without a rule that makes the methods usable at scale. Their proposed way out is a tiered potency assessment — epidemiology first, then human in-vitro data, then animal data — which is another year of committee work of the kind that produced the chemical set itself.

What changes in the vault

Nothing demotes and nothing promotes. What changes is the description of the blocker, and it should now be consistent across the index:

  • The nineteen automatable-open-loop maps are not, in general, waiting for someone to write down a rule everyone already agrees on.
  • Two independent instances now say the rule is not yet writable: a single-lab proof of concept (2026-07-28) and a formal multi-laboratory validation study coordinated by EURL ECVAM (tonight). One is the weakest evidence setting in the vault and the other is close to the strongest, and they agree.
  • The vault's automatable-open-loop tier definition says "no agreed decision rule exists." It should not be read as "no one has bothered."

This is a confirmation, not a new finding — but a confirmation that crosses from single-lab-exploratory to formally-validated in one night is worth recording as such. See the-kidney-arrives-with-a-plate-reader-and-no-rule for the case it confirms.
