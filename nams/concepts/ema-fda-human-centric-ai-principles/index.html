EMA + FDA: the regulators' unit of evaluation is a human–AI system

Read the grading before the finding

evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted records the standing of the sourcing instrument — a joint primary document from two regulators. It records nothing about the force of the principles, which is zero. This is not a guidance. It is explicitly "intended to lay the foundation for developing good practice." regulatory_status: proposed, strength: suggestive. Nothing in it binds anyone to anything.

What makes it worth ingesting is not its force. It is the agreement. EMA and FDA did not have to align here, and did — which makes these ten principles the best available forecast of what the eventual binding rules for AI-driven methods will be shaped like. The vault's premise is that "the rules are being written right now." This is the outline both agencies agreed to write from.

The finding, in three principles

#1 — "Human-centric by design." "The development and use of AI technologies align with ethical and human-centric values." Of the ten things two regulators could have said first about AI in drug development, this is what they chose. It is a values statement, not a technical control — but it is the frame the other nine sit inside, and frames decide what later rules look like.

#2 — "Risk-based approach." "…proportionate validation, risk mitigation, and oversight based on the context of use and determined model risk."

This is the load-bearing one for this vault. It is FDA's January-2025 model-risk machinery (fda-model-influence-penalises-autonomy) — the framework in which model influence rises with autonomy — now carrying EMA's signature too. The finding that closing the loop maximises the multiplicand the regulator prices was, until this document, an extrapolation from one nonbinding US draft. It is now the shared vocabulary of both major regulators. That is a real strengthening, and it should be said plainly.

#8 — "Risk-based performance assessment." "Risk-based performance assessments evaluate the complete system including human–AI interactions, using fit-for-use data and metrics appropriate for the intended context of use."

Read the unit of evaluation. It is not the model. It is the model, plus the human working with it, assessed together. A fully autonomous loop is not a system that scores badly against this principle — it is a system this principle does not have a vocabulary for. The regulators are writing rules for a centaur, and the vault is grading assays for a robot.

And it is a NAMs document, on page one

AI is anticipated to "…decrease reliance on animal testing by improving the prediction of toxicity and efficacy in humans."

The regulators' stated case for AI in drug development is a NAMs case. This is the third document in three runs (with FDA's CDER NAM guidance and FDA's AI guidance) in which the AI question and the animal-replacement question turn out to be the same question. The vault opened a regulatory-acceptance column reluctantly, as a flagged convention departure. It is now the column where the vault's own central question is being decided.

What this does NOT say — the honest limit

"Human-centric" is not "human-operated." Nothing in these ten principles forbids an autonomous loop, and a well-instrumented automated system with human oversight of the system rather than of each call is plainly compatible with all ten — arguably it is more compatible with #6 (data governance, full provenance) and #9 (scheduled drift monitoring) than a human eyeballing plates ever was.

The honest reading is narrower, and still worth having:

The regulators are building their vocabulary around a human–AI system. An autonomous loop is not the default case they are writing for — and the burden of showing one is safe will fall on whoever builds it, under rules drafted with someone else in mind.

That is a prediction about where friction will appear, not a prohibition. It is suggestive, and it is the single most useful thing this vault can tell someone building toward an autonomous lab: the science may be ready before the vocabulary is.
