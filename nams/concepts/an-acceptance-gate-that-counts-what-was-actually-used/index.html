An acceptance gate that counts what was actually used

This vault holds six maps on the regulatory_status axis, and until tonight they were all the same kind of document:

  • The US Food and Drug Administration's drug-review centre saying it will accept a fit-for-purpose non-animal method with no Test Guideline at all — a policy.
  • The joint FDA–European Medicines Agency artificial-intelligence principles — a framework.
  • The European Commission's roadmap to phase out animal testing — a plan.
  • The UK's replacement strategy — a strategy.
  • The US EPA's statutory list under the Toxic Substances Control Act — a list, conferring standing by aggregation.
  • The international model-informed-drug-development route (ICH M15) — a door.

Six shapes, and all six are statements about the future tense. What a regulator will accept, will prioritise, will list. None of them measures behaviour.

The seventh shape: the receipt

Japan's medicines regulator went into its own case files for medicated cosmetics — products in Japan's quasi-drug tier, between a cosmetic and a drug, which carry an active claim and therefore require a safety submission — and counted.

22 of 115 tests. 10 of 25 products. Five years.

The by-test figure is the one to read: about one in five. Four in five safety tests in these submissions were still done in animals. The by-product figure of 40% is higher only because a product counts as a user if it used a non-animal method anywhere, which means those products used one for an endpoint or two and animals for the rest.

The pattern is the finding

Uptake was highest "in tests with established regulatory standards, particularly ocular irritation assessments."

Adoption tracks the existence of a written, adopted, published rule almost exactly, and does not extend past it. Where a guideline says what to run and how to read the number, applicants run it. Where no guideline exists — even where a scientifically strong non-animal method exists — applicants default to the animal.

That is the same variable this vault's third axis turns on. The property separating closed-loop-ready from automatable-open-loop is whether somebody published a fixed data-interpretation procedure. It now appears to be the property that separates "used" from "not used" as well — which would mean the missing decision rule is not only the loop's blocker but the field's adoption blocker, and the same paragraph fixes both.

Recorded as a hypothesis this source is consistent with, not as a demonstrated cause. One country, one product category, chosen because it is the favourable case, with denominators in the low hundreds.

Why it is uncomfortable rather than merely interesting

Every other acceptance source in this vault reads as forward motion. This one is the same field measured at the point of use and the fraction is one in five. Nothing here demotes any method — a method being adopted and a method being used are separate facts, and this vault does not infer one from the other any more than it infers loop-readiness from either.

But it puts a number on a distance the vault could previously only gesture at. An autonomous laboratory that can run an adopted, loop-ready, plate-reader assay end to end is, on this evidence, automating the minority case. The bottleneck being removed is not the one carrying most of the traffic.
