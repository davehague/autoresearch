Cytotoxic masking is a second shared failure route through the battery

The rail, restated

This vault's sharpest standing claim: when two assays in one battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, that battery casts one vote, not two. Two agreeing measurements that share a blind spot are not two independent votes — and a decision rule that counts agreement cannot tell the difference. For a person reading a battery result this is a problem. For an automated system it is worse, because the system will read concordance as confirmation and iterate, fast and confidently, in the wrong direction.

The vault flagged this on the skin-sensitisation battery on 2026-08-01, during a monthly cross-method sweep, on the pre-hapten / pro-hapten problem: chemicals that are not reactive themselves but become sensitisers after air oxidation or after skin enzymes metabolise them. DPRA has no cells and therefore no metabolism; KeratinoSens and h-CLAT are immortalised cell lines with limited skin-relevant metabolic competence. Three chances to miss the same chemical for one shared reason — and the 2-out-of-3 rule then reports two concordant negatives as a pass.

That flag was recorded honestly as a vault inference, not a sourced finding, with the correlated_blind_spot field deliberately left unset pending a citation, and the open question logged for a human.

The second route, and it is not the same one

This source describes a different shared failure, on the same two assays, from a completely different cause. For both KeratinoSens and h-CLAT, the consortium names "the risk that cytotoxic components may mask responses induced by sensitizing constituents."

The mechanism is arithmetic, not biology. Both assays are read against cell viability: KeratinoSens requires viability above 70% for a call, and h-CLAT reads its two activation markers alongside a viability stain and runs a cytotoxicity dose-finder first. If a mixture contains a component that is simply toxic at the concentrations where a different component would have registered as a sensitiser, the sensitising signal never gets a chance to appear — the cells are already dying, and the assay reports nothing, which is scored as a negative.

Two cell-based key events, one shared masking mechanism, two quiet negatives out of three. The rule calls it a pass.

This is not a restatement of the metabolic flag. The metabolic gap misses chemicals that need activating; this one misses chemicals that are travelling with something poisonous. A mixture could be caught by neither, either, or both. What they have in common is only the shape — and the shape is the finding.

The third, smaller shared route: the dye

The MTT viability dye is used in Test Guideline 439 (lab-grown human skin, irritation, viability ≤50% classifies as an irritant) and in KeratinoSens. Plant material attacks it two ways: constituents reduce MTT directly — producing the colour with no living cell involved — and pigments absorb at the measurement wavelength. Either fakes viability.

Both guidelines have controls for it, and this matters: Test Guideline 439 carries a killed-tissue control and a colorant control with computed corrections, and KeratinoSens removes the treatment before reading viability, with neutral red uptake or PrestoBlue named as substitute readouts if interference persists. This is what a mature adopted method looks like — the interference is documented and the correction is written into the procedure.

It is recorded here anyway, because it is a cross-endpoint correlation the vault had not noted: the same dye, hence the same interference, sits under a skin-irritation call and a skin-sensitisation key event. A loop running both against the same botanical would be applying two corrections for one shared artefact, and would have to apply them — an automated system that skipped the colorant control would return two wrong answers from one cause.

What this does to the flag

It supplies half of what the 2026-08-01 flag was waiting for and not all of it.

The flag needed a citation for the claim that the battery's components share a failure mode. There is now a published, multisector-consortium statement of one — a different one, in the same shape, on the same two assays. That is enough to stop treating the shared-failure question as a private vault suspicion.

It is not enough to set correlated_blind_spot and close the item, for three reasons stated plainly:

  1. It is a different mechanism. The human decision pending since 2026-08-01 was about the metabolic gap, and this source says nothing about pre- or pro-haptens.
  2. No experiment has been run. This is a study-design paper. The masking risk is a stated technical expectation, not a measured false-negative rate. There is no number here, and there will not be one until the consortium runs the eleven reference botanicals.
  3. It is bounded to mixtures. For a single defined chemical, "a cytotoxic constituent masks a sensitising constituent" has no referent — there is one constituent. So this is a domain_gate finding first and a correlated_blind_spot finding second, and the honest move is to record it on both maps as a flagged candidate and let the human close it.

What a loop should take from it

The two batteries in this vault that clear end to end — every component instrument-read, a published deterministic rule on top — are this one and the phototoxicity integrated approach. Both now carry more than one shared-failure route. The uncomfortable summary the vault reached on 2026-08-01 stands and has got slightly worse: the two things an autonomous lab could actually run today are the two whose concordance means least.

The design consequence is specific and cheap: a loop running a battery on an undefined mixture should treat a concordant negative as a weaker signal than a concordant positive, because every shared failure mode on this battery — metabolic, cytotoxic, solubility, greasiness threshold — pushes toward the negative. The 2-out-of-3 rule is symmetric. The failure modes are not.
