The commonest key event in toxicology is the cell dying

⚠ Abstract-only ingest. The full text of this paper could not be retrieved (see the source note — it is CC BY licensed and the host still refused). Everything below is the authors' own summary of their counts.

What was counted

An adverse outcome pathway is a causal chain from a chemical touching a molecule to visible harm; the steps in between are key events. AOP-Wiki is the public collection. This paper treats that collection as data: curate it, map it onto six clusters of human disease that chemicals are known to be able to trigger, and count which key events keep showing up.

Key eventIn plain termsClusters it appears in
Cytotoxicitythe cell dies6 of 6
Oxidative stressdamaging reactive chemistry builds up inside the cell5 of 6
Mitochondrial dysfunctionthe cell's power supply fails4 of 6

Why this is a correlated-blind-spot finding, arrived at forwards

The vault's sharpest rail says: when two assays in a battery fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason, that battery casts one vote, not two. Every previous instance in the vault was found backwards — two assays already sitting in a battery, inspected, and discovered to share a gap. The fish embryo test and the RTgill-W1 fish cell line share the absence of a nervous system. The reactive-oxygen assay and the 3T3 neutral-red uptake test share the absence of biotransformation.

This one runs forwards. It is a selection rule, and the rule's own output is the three least specific readouts in toxicology. Cell death, oxidative stress and mitochondrial failure are not mechanisms so much as final common paths — what a cell does when almost anything serious goes wrong. That is precisely why they appear in nearly every pathway, and precisely why assays reading them will tend to fire together, on the same compounds, for the same reason.

A panel weighted toward them is not fifteen independent votes. The vault already holds this failure mode measured in a real screening platform: on the Tox21 robotic platform, "cytotoxicity mimics activity" across every cell-based reporter assay on the panel, and the platform's answer is not cross-assay concordance but a viability control multiplexed into the same well — see tox21-cytotoxicity-multiplexing-shared-failure-mode and cytotoxic-masking-is-a-second-shared-failure-route-through-the-battery. a-battery-inside-one-cell-line-is-not-five-independent-votes records the same arithmetic from the cell-line side.

So the honest reading of this paper is two-sided, and both sides are real:

  • A frequency-ranked key-event list is a genuine, repeatable answer to "why these assays and not others?" — the question an-aop-network-used-as-a-battery-design-tool first raised and nobody had answered systematically.
  • Frequency and independence are opposite selection criteria. The events that recur most are the ones that discriminate least. Adopted OECD guidance already names the alternative criterion — "the conditional independence of these test methods should be considered" (oecd-names-conditional-independence-as-a-battery-design-rule) — and a frequency ranking does not compute it.

Whether the authors' full text addresses this is unread and the vault does not assume either way. Their abstract does say they integrate the "key characteristics of toxicants" framework to reach "coverage beyond current AOP limitations", which is a coverage argument, not an independence one.

The loop reading

For an autonomous lab this is the front of the loop — what goes on the plate — and it is the first source in the vault to propose a machine-executable answer. A curation-and-count over a public database is a procedure a machine could run and re-run as the database grows.

And that is exactly why the correlation matters more here than in a human's hands. A human reading three concordant generic-stress assays may ask why they agree. A loop reads concordance as confirmation, trusts the decision rule, and iterates — fast, and with growing confidence — in the direction the shared gap points. The rail's standing sentence applies without modification: automation converts a known bias into a compounding one.

What it does not license

  • Nothing was tested. No panel was built, no chemical run, no sensitivity or specificity exists.
  • Frequency in a database is not importance in biology. A key event may recur because it is generic, because it is well studied, or because AOP authors reuse each other's boxes. The paper's own design concedes the collection is incomplete.
  • Abstract-only. The curation criteria, the cluster definitions, and the identity of the 15 assays are all unread by this vault.
