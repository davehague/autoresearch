A genomic signature classified six wearable-device chemicals correctly; the animal tests it replaces mostly didn't

GARDskin's core classification algorithm is itself adopted in OECD TG 442E — this paper is not evidence for that. What it adds is new: the first primary data in this vault on GARDskin's two protocol extensions (GARDskin DR, dose-response/potency; GARDskin MD, solid-material/medical-device extracts), applied to a chemical domain — wearable-device leachables — that the adopted guideline was never built for, and a head-to-head against the animal tests those chemicals already have documented clinical allergy histories against.

What was measured

A human myeloid leukemia line (SenzaCell, ATCC PTA-123875) is exposed to a test chemical for 24 hours; total mRNA is quantified for 196 named genes (the GARDskin Genomic Prediction Signature) by NanoString nCounter; the gene-expression vector is fed to a published support-vector-machine model producing a Decision Value; mean DV >= 0 (n=3 replicates) classifies the chemical a sensitizer. Every step after exposure is instrument- or software-determined — no microscope, no manual scoring. GARDskin DR runs the same assay across six concentrations and interpolates the lowest dose giving a positive DV (cDV0), convertible to a potency value in micrograms/cm^2 analogous to a human NESIL. GARDskin MD runs the assay on ISO 10993-12 polar/non-polar extracts of a solid material; either extract scoring positive classifies the material.

The animal comparison, read at the strength it has

All six chemicals are already documented causes of allergic contact dermatitis in people who wore devices containing them (glucose sensors, smartwatch screen-protector adhesives, nail polish, spectacle frames) — that is why they were chosen. Against that real-world positive-control set:

Read this as a sensitivity comparison only — it cannot be anything else. The study's own design choice was to run zero non-sensitizer chemicals, stated explicitly as deliberate (the potency-assessment goal needs positives). That means no false-positive rate, no specificity, and no accuracy figure is computable from this paper in either direction — "GARDskin caught what the animal tests missed" is true and is also the only kind of claim this dataset can support.

Potency cross-check

GARDskin DR's potency rankings correlated closely with SARA-ICE (the OECD-evaluated quantitative point-of-departure defined approach, skin-sensitization-quantitative-pod-da) and with a regression model built from kDPRA + KeratinoSens data on the overlapping chemicals — three different model families agreeing on rank order (IBOA and SO60 strongest, NNDMA weakest), while differing in absolute potency by up to 4-5x, with SARA-ICE consistently the most conservative. The authors' own reading is instructive: the disagreement between the two defined approaches, which share the same input data, was larger than the disagreement between GARDskin DR (a single assay) and the regression DA — a data point against the assumption that combining more assays automatically narrows the answer.

Why this is graded single-lab-exploratory despite the base method's adopted status

Three different maturity levels are bundled in one paper. The base GARDskin classification (OECD TG 442E) is regulatory-adopted. GARDskin DR has several prior publications (cited, not re-verified here) applying it to potency/risk-assessment questions. GARDskin MD is explicitly named by the authors as still in ISO TS 11796 feasibility/pre-validation — not yet interlaboratory-validated. This specific application (wearable-device leachables, six chemicals, one laboratory) sits at the bottom of that stack: evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, regulatory_status: proposed for the device-testing use case specifically.

The bias to hold next to the finding

Two of eight authors are employed by Apple Inc., which manufactures wearable devices and whose product category is exactly what this paper argues GARDskin is "well adapted to address." The other six are employees of SenzaGen AB, which markets and sells the GARD assays. The paper declares no external funding and no conflicts "related to the content" — the vault records the affiliation itself as the bias regardless of that declaration, per the standing rule that a device manufacturer validating a testing pathway for its own product category is graded exactly as sceptically as a method developer's claim about their own assay. → skin-sensitization-gardskin
