TGx-DDI ring trial — what four labs actually got

The design. Four independent laboratories — one university (Georgetown), two pharmaceutical/consumer-goods companies (Sanofi, Procter & Gamble) and one contract research organisation (Burleson Research Technologies) — each ran the same 64-gene expression signature on human TK6 cells against 14 compounds whose identity they did not know (6 known DNA-damaging, 8 known not), plus three controls. Two rounds: fixed concentrations first, then concentrations optimised per compound after range-finding. 47 valid tests.

The results (optimised round).

MetricNumberWhat it means
Sensitivity100% (CI 82–100%)Every DNA-damaging compound in the panel was caught. Zero misses out of six.
Specificity86% (CI 67–96%)About one safe compound in seven was wrongly flagged as damaging.
Accuracy91% (CI 80–98%)91 calls in 100 correct.
Inter-lab agreementKappa/PABAK/AC1 0.61–1.0"Good" to "perfect". Two of the four labs agreed on every single call.
Technical reproducibilityPearson r > 0.84The same reference RNA gave near-identical readings at every site.

Why the specificity number is the headline and the sensitivity number is not. Sensitivity near 100% is what you expect and demand from a genotoxicity screen — missing a mutagen is the unacceptable error, and every assay in this space is tuned to avoid it. The field's actual problem is the other error. The paper puts the in-vitro micronucleus assay's specificity at "30% to 54%" or "65%" depending on the dataset: the adopted, consensus-status, regulatorily-required chromosome-damage test (genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus) wrongly calls somewhere between a third and two-thirds of harmless chemicals genotoxic. Each false alarm costs a follow-up study, historically an animal one. 86% roughly halves that error rate. That is the contribution.

What the trial does not establish. Fourteen compounds is a small panel, and the confidence intervals say so honestly: the true sensitivity is somewhere between 82% and 100%, and "100%" is the observation "zero misses out of six", not a property of the method. Specificity's interval (67–96%) is wide enough that it overlaps the upper end of the micronucleus range. The comparison is directionally solid and quantitatively soft, and this note will not report it as settled.

Grading. evidence_tier: formally-validated — a multi-site blinded interlaboratory ring trial with reported concordance statistics is exactly what that tier means, and this vault has few sources that clear it. regulatory_status: proposed — a final qualification package is submitted to FDA's Biomarker Qualification Program; nothing is accepted. Those two axes are recorded separately and the second is not inferred from the first.

Bias, stated plainly. funding_flag: [industry]. Funded by FDA's Drug Development Tool program (public money), but two of the four labs are the sponsor's own commercial partners, a third is a commercial CRO, and the biomarker's originating group is on the author list and is the party seeking qualification. This is a developer participating in the validation of their own biomarker. It is a strong trial; it is not an independent one in the ECVAM/ICCVAM sense, and the vault does not upgrade it as though it were.
