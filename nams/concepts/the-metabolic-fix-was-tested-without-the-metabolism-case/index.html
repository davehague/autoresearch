The metabolically competent cell line was tested on chemicals that need no metabolism

The shape of the gap

A pro-genotoxicant is a chemical that is harmless as you find it and becomes DNA-damaging only after the body chemically alters it — the liver's enzymes turn a benign parent molecule into a reactive one. Benzo[a]pyrene in smoke is the textbook case. This is the single most consequential thing a metabolically inert test cell cannot see, and it is the stated structural cause of the vault's correlated_blind_spot across three genotoxicity maps: micronucleus, the TGx-DDI transcriptomic biomarker, and the human variability panel, all in lymphoblastoid cells with rat liver S9 extract or nothing.

HepaRG is the obvious answer. It is a human liver cell line with a working balance of phase I and phase II metabolism — the enzymes that do the activating and the detoxifying. Put the assay in a liver cell and it should see the pro-genotoxicants that TK6 misses.

That is not what was tested. Verbatim from the limitations:

"Compounds requiring metabolic activation were not selected for this study because the intention was to focus on the question of whether such an approach would retain a high sensitivity for direct-acting genotoxins."

Why this is a fair experiment and an incomplete claim, at the same time

The experimental logic is sound. Before you adopt a new test cell you check it has not lost the old cell's strength. Directly-acting genotoxins are the assay's bread and butter; if HepaRG missed those, nothing else would matter. It nearly passed — 12 of 14 against TK6's 14 of 14 — and the two misses (ENU, o-toluidine) are discussed. That is a well-posed question, correctly answered.

The claim that travels is bigger than the question. The headline is "HepaRG cells showed a high overall accuracy of 86%, compared to 75% for TK6 cells." The reason offered for the gap is metabolic competence. And the entire gap is on the false-positive side — chemicals that TK6 wrongly condemns because it cannot detoxify them. So metabolic competence is demonstrated to help with detoxification, in a chemical set from which the activation cases were removed by design.

The authors themselves say the next step out loud: HepaRG "may be suitable for regulatory testing" only "with further examination of chemicals requiring metabolic activation."

What this means for a loop, and it generalises past this assay

A closed loop evaluating whether to adopt a better test system will read the accuracy delta — 75% to 86%, with a mechanistic explanation attached — and the applicability-domain statement, which says the method is for genotoxicity. Neither field encodes "the validating chemical set was chosen to exclude one mechanism." That fact lives in one sentence of a limitations section, and it is the most decision-relevant sentence in the paper.

This is a specific instance of a pattern the vault keeps finding: the number that decides whether to trust a method is usually not in the fields that describe the method. TG 494's headline 96% catch rate is measured only on the 68% of chemicals it accepts, and no OECD reporting template requires the refusal rate. TG 492B's domain gate must be told to the machine rather than measured by it. Here, the chemical selection criterion is the caveat, and it is prose.

Nothing demotes. The result stands: HepaRG really does clear 9 of 9 known false positives where TK6 clears 3 of 9. The vault records the finding and the boundary of the evidence for it together, and keeps correlated_blind_spot set on all three maps — because the shared bioactivation blindness has been credibly addressed and not yet demonstrably fixed.
