Being right and being able to explain why are different scores, measured on the OECD's own pathway data

quantitative-aop-probabilistic-modelling's standing subject is the gap between an adverse outcome pathway existing as a qualitative causal diagram and an adverse outcome pathway computing anything — Ladeira et al.'s cardiotoxicity network (2026-09-27) found fewer than 40% of its key-event relationships carry any quantitative backing at all. ToxReason attacks a neighbouring question: when a language model is asked to walk that same molecular-initiating-event-to-adverse-outcome chain and name a verdict, do its prediction accuracy and its reasoning quality move together?

The decoupling

Built from 193 held-out chemicals across liver, heart and kidney, scored against the OECD AOP-Wiki (Release 2.7) plus the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database and ChEMBL: the closed model with the best mechanistic- reasoning score (GPT-5.1, judged by a second LLM for hallucination avoidance, causal coherence and biological fidelity) has the worst prediction accuracy of the five closed models tested. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-70B inverts it — comparatively strong prediction, middling reasoning. A small model, fine-tuned with a two-stage reinforcement-learning recipe against the AOP-matched training data, is the only configuration tested that wins on both axes at once, beating every frontier closed model on prediction while also improving its own reasoning score — the opposite of the trade-off the closed-model field displays.

Why this belongs next to, not inside, Ladeira's qualitative gap

Ladeira measured how much of a published, expert-curated AOP network carries quantitative support at all. ToxReason measures something downstream of that: given the pathway knowledge exists, can a model reason over it reliably, and does reasoning reliability track prediction accuracy closely enough that one could stand in for the other operationally (e.g., trust the prediction because the stated reasoning sounds right). On this evidence, no — a model's explanation quality is not a trustworthy proxy for whether its call is correct, in either direction. That is a caution about using LLM-generated justification as a substitute for checking the prediction itself, not a claim about AOPs or qAOPs as objects.

Ceiling

Pure in-silico, no wet-lab content anywhere — every datapoint comes from existing public databases, not a new assay. Part of the "ground-truth" reasoning this benchmark trains against was itself generated by an LLM (Claude Haiku 4.5), not authored by a toxicologist, which the authors do not flag as a limitation but this vault does. The reasoning score is an LLM judge's 0-10 rating, cross-checked against an algorithmic sequence-alignment score at only moderate agreement (Spearman ρ = 0.837) — a real but imperfect proxy for "is this mechanistically sound," graded by another model rather than a toxicologist. The authors' own ethics statement states outputs are not standalone evidence for regulatory or clinical decisions.
