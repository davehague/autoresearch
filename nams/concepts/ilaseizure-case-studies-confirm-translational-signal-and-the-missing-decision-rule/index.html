The method developer's own paper says the quiet part about its decision rule out loud

neurotoxicity-seizure-liability-in-vitro-battery was auto-coined 2026-08-19 on a single company's SAR paper with zero concordance figures, and its loop_blocker named the missing piece precisely: nothing published converts the ion-channel panel plus the MEA firing profile into a seizure-liability call. This follow-on paper, from the same lab, does not fix that — it confirms it in the developer's own words: "we do not yet have sufficient data to develop a specified decision framework." A potent ion-channel hit is explicitly downgraded from a stop/go signal to "an alert for further investigation."

What the paper adds instead is four retrospective translational anchors the founding paper lacked entirely: a known clinical seizure drug (amoxapine) reproduced at a clinically relevant exposure; a species-selective GLP toxicology finding explained after the fact by a dog-only circulating metabolite; and a compound ("Compound B") whose in vitro no-effect/effect boundary (10 µM / 100 µM) brackets its in vivo no-effect/convulsion boundary (38 µM / 148–297 µM plasma) — the single cleanest quantitative in vitro-to-in vivo translation point either paper has produced. All four are retrospective and developer-selected, not blinded predictions, and no formal statistic is computed across them — the honest read is "directionally consistent in four hand-picked cases," not "validated."

For a loop: this is the useful shape of a negative result. An automated system reading only the abstract ("case studies demonstrate the assay's potential") would see confirmation; reading the method, it would see the same primary author naming the exact decision-procedure gap the vault had already flagged — the kind of distinction only visible by reading past the headline framing, the same failure mode this vault's data-layer maps (see nam-data-processing-pipeline) keep finding between a paper's narrative and its numbers.
