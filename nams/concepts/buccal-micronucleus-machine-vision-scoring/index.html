Machine-vision scoring of the buccal micronucleus cytome assay → genotoxicity biomonitoring

The buccal micronucleus cytome assay (BMCyt) scores exfoliated human cheek cells for micronuclei, nuclear buds, karyorrhexis, karyolysis, and pyknosis — a panel of cell-fate endpoints read by a trained scorer down a microscope. It is a human-biomonitoring method: unlike everything else in this vault, it does not predict whether a chemical would harm someone, it measures genotoxic/cytotoxic damage that has already happened in a person who was exposed. Minimally invasive (a cheek scrape, no needle), it is usable in vulnerable populations and is a NAM by definition (non-animal), but it belongs to a different category than the vault's predictive hazard assays: an exposure/effect biomarker, not a screening test. This concept note opens genotoxicity-buccal-micronucleus-cytome as the vault's fourth eye-scored human-in-the-loop map — and it opens already carrying its escape hatch, rather than acquiring one in a later ingest.

What Smith et al. (2026) is. A review, not a primary study. It reports no original dataset and no original performance number of its own; every figure below belongs to a study it cites. That distinction is the whole honesty constraint of this note.

The numbers, attributed, and the pattern worth naming. The most granular figure it cites (Rodrigues et al., a CNN via imaging flow cytometry) reaches 98% accuracy on mononucleated cells and 95% on binucleated cells — but only 82–85% on the micronucleated phenotype itself, the class that actually carries the genotoxicity signal. That is the textbook class-imbalance failure a software engineer recognises immediately: strong on the common, easy classes, weakest exactly where the rare positive signal lives. A Faster R-CNN (Panchbhai et al.) reaches >90% average precision for micronucleus quantification; three further approaches (YOLOv7 variants, an attention-augmented AlexNet, U-Net segmentation) are reported only qualitatively or as a wide range, with no single reproducible number for two of them.

Why this does NOT move the new map to loop-ready — recorded at the moment the map opens, not added later.

  1. Secondary evidence. This is a synthesis of other groups' unvalidated results, not a primary study with its own dataset and number → weaker than any of the vault's other three machine-vision escape hatches (Wills 2021 micronucleus, Sivaprasad 2026 zebrafish, Masumoto 2021 cell transformation), all of which at least reported an original result.
  2. Not an independent second opinion. Co-author Paul Rees also co-authored Wills et al. 2021 (imaging-flow-cytometry-micronucleus-deep-learning-scoring), the vault's strongest existing micronucleus escape hatch. This review is one lab group's view of a field it is itself active in, not two unconnected groups converging independently on the same conclusion.
  3. Weakest on the class that matters. The single most granular number available (82–85% on the micronucleated phenotype) is the worst of the set — the opposite of reassuring for a biomonitoring tool whose entire purpose is calling the positive class correctly.
  4. No universal standard, by the authors' own admission. The review states plainly there is no agreed benchmark or standard for AI-based buccal-cell micronucleus detection, and flags dataset labour-intensity, lack of positive controls, poor generalisation across imaging conditions, black-box opacity, and heavy compute requirements as open problems → regulatory_status: none.

Reading this against the vault's other three eye-scored maps. genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus, developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity, and carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay were each opened blocked, and each later acquired a demonstrated machine-vision escape hatch in a subsequent ingest — see zebrafish-machine-vision-malformation-scoring for the same pattern on a different assay. This is the first time the sequence runs the other way: the map opens and its escape hatch lands in the same ingest. That is a real change in shape worth recording, but it does not make this the strongest demonstration — it is the weakest of the four, for the reasons above.

Our honest read. funding_flag: none — the review declares no funding and no conflicts. But "no industry money" is not the same as "independent evidence": the author overlap with Wills 2021 means this should be read as an extension of one lab group's existing machine-vision programme, not a second, unconnected line of evidence. A loop-move for a new endpoint, not a validity or acceptance claim — it says nothing about whether BMCyt reliably measures human genotoxic exposure, only that its eye-scoring bottleneck is an active research target with early, unvalidated, and unevenly-reported results.
