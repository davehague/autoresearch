The fix everyone assumed would work, measured for the first time in this vault

This vault's most-repeated structural finding is the metabolic-competence gap: cells in a dish mostly cannot convert a chemical the way a body does, so a substance that is only harmful after conversion reads negative and the assay is not wrong — it never made the harmful molecule. The rail names it in the fish embryo test and the fish gill cell line (neither has full metabolism), in the reactive-oxygen-species photoreactivity assay and the 3T3 mouse fibroblast phototoxicity assay (the identical gap, so a photo-inert parent with a photo-reactive metabolite walks through the entire phototoxicity scheme untouched), and in the-parent-compound-tested-clean-and-the-metabolites-did-not, where a food antioxidant tested clean and three of its metabolites damaged zebrafish hearts.

Each of those is evidence that the gap is real. None of them is evidence about what closing it would do. The vault has been carrying an implied fix — use a system that can do the chemistry — and on 2026-08-12 recorded a candidate machinery for supplying it as an upstream step (metabolic-competence-as-a-modular-upstream-step).

This source is the first time in this vault that somebody actually ran the comparison. Same readout, same chemicals, both systems, and — the methodological care that makes it worth taking seriously — chemicals ranked on the concentration measured inside each system rather than on the dose added to the water, so the comparison is of biology and not of uptake.

The result, stated without smoothing

Nine and nine, and not the same nine. Both the zebrafish embryos and the matured HepG2 cells identified nine chemicals as reactive-oxygen-species inducers, and the two sets "did not completely overlap." The metabolically competent model did not contain the simpler one. Each sees something the other does not.

Four of fifteen missed by both. Of the chemicals the literature reports as acting primarily through oxidative stress, four came back negative in both systems. That is a shared floor neither model's design explains away.

And the direction that runs against the expectation: of the seven chemicals whose primary mechanism is something other than oxidative stress, the whole-organism model flagged three; the cell line flagged none. Adding a functioning organism did not just reveal hidden truth. It also produced calls on chemicals the simpler system correctly left alone.

Two honest brakes on reading that as "false positives." First, a chemical acting primarily by another mechanism may still generate reactive oxygen species — "primary mechanism" is a label about emphasis, not an exclusion — so the vault records the count and refuses the word. Second, the exposure windows differ by a factor of 24 (24 hours in the fish, 1 hour in the cells), which is an alternative explanation for divergence that has nothing to do with metabolism at all. The paper's own frame is that it "explore[s] potential explanations for discrepancies" — the discrepancy is the finding, and it is not resolved.

What this changes in the vault

It does not remove the metabolic-competence gap, and nothing here demotes any map. The gap is documented from guideline text (OECD Test Guideline 249's own allyl-alcohol example) and from a worked mechanistic case, and this source contradicts neither.

What it removes is the tidiness of the remedy. "Supply the missing chemistry and the blind spot closes" was a hypothesis the vault was carrying as though it were nearly a plan. On the one dataset that tests it, a model with the chemistry does not dominate a model without it — it trades one error profile for a different one. For any programme deciding where to spend, that is the difference between an upgrade and a choice.

And the reading for a loop is sharper than the reading for a person. A human comparing two model outputs sees two partly overlapping lists and asks why. An automated system running both and treating agreement as corroboration would be reading a coincidence of counts as a convergence of evidence — nine and nine looks like replication and is not. Where the two disagree, nothing published says which to believe, so the disagreement produces no decision and no next experiment. See two-models-agreed-on-a-count-and-not-on-a-list.
