The cheap proxy is what closes the loop — and its answer key is also automated

The unglamorous leg

Read a self-driving-lab paper and the attention goes to the model. In this campaign the model chose where to start; the thing that made the loop possible at all was the measurement.

The three quality scores this loop optimises against would conventionally take three instruments, one sample at a time: eyeball the coverage, run a profilometer or atomic-force microscope for uniformity, run X-ray diffraction for phase purity. The authors get all three off one line-scan. A hyperspectral camera returns a datacube — a full light spectrum at every pixel — and three different analyses run over the same cube:

ScoreHow it comes out of the cube
Coveragecomputer-vision segmentation separating film from bare glass; the fraction of covered pixels
Uniformityprincipal-component analysis over every pixel's spectrum, then the average roughness of the resulting score surface
Phase-purity proxyconvert reflectance to an optical absorption edge per pixel, fit the edge automatically, and score the shape of the distribution of ~50,000 per-pixel bandgaps — one clean peak means one substance, extra peaks mean impurity

~50,000 bandgaps in under five minutes. All three scores for a sample within ten. The authors state directly that this is what sets the loop's cycle time and makes rapid feedback practical.

The second thing it removed, which is easy to miss

"That same pipeline turned the analysis of the campaign into an automated step, so the volume of data collected was no longer bounded by the effort required to interpret it."

Two bottlenecks, not one. A loop can be limited by how fast it runs experiments and by how fast anyone can read them, and the second is invisible in a throughput number. This vault's own screening-infrastructure map records the same shape from the other direction: Tox21 can run ten thousand chemicals in a week and the interpretation of what came back is where it stops being a loop.

⚠ But the proxy is not the property, and it says so

Everything above is a stand-in. An optical edge is not a crystal structure, and the authors are precise about where the substitution leaks:

  • It cannot separate two phases whose optical edges are closer together than the per-pixel fitting precision. Two different substances that happen to absorb at similar wavelengths read as one.
  • The uniformity metric only sees variation captured by the first principal component. Heterogeneity expressed in later components is invisible to it.
  • A single Tauc exponent is applied to every pixel, so the fitted quantity "should be read as an effective optical edge rather than as a transition-resolved bandgap."

⚠⚠ And the check on the proxy is itself an automated method with a known failure mode

This is the sentence worth carrying out of the paper:

"Automated whole-pattern refinement has been shown to return confident but incorrect phase assignments at scale in autonomous synthesis campaigns, so the structural label used to check an optical proxy carries uncertainty of its own."

So the loop's architecture is: a fast automated proxy guides the search, and a slower automated structural method is the ground truth the proxy is spot-checked against — and the ground truth has a documented tendency to be confidently wrong at scale. The paper's own structural conclusion for its product is called "provisional pending Rietveld refinement against an atomistic model and an independent measurement of the stoichiometry"; two alternative products were not excluded; and the bandgap agreement with theory "reflects a partial cancellation of two large opposing errors."

Nothing in that is a flaw in this paper. It is a description of what an autonomous campaign's evidence base actually looks like once it is fast enough to be autonomous.

Why a toxicology reader should care

Substitute the nouns and the paragraph becomes a description of this vault's central problem.

In the campaignIn regulatory toxicology
optical phase-purity proxya plate-reader reporter assay, or a transcriptomic signature
X-ray structural assignmentthe apical outcome — an animal study, or a human clinical record
"cannot separate phases whose edges are closer than the fit precision"the borderline band around an adopted cut-off, where applying the rule is uninformative
"confident but incorrect phase assignments at scale"an automated call on the answer key itself

The vault has been recording, source by source, that the fast machine-readable measurement a loop can afford is systematically a different quantity from the one the decision is about — the kidney battery's five curves against clinical nephrotoxicity, the transcriptomic point of departure against a chronic study, the blood–brain-barrier model against neurotoxicity. This source adds the part that is usually left implicit: once the answer key is also produced automatically, the loop's ability to detect that its proxy has drifted depends on a second automated method with its own failure mode. A loop can be self-consistent and wrong, and speed does not surface that — it buries it under agreement.
