A fixed rule, a machine-readable number, and a three-fold spread in agreement

skin-irritation-rhe is one of the vault's cleanest closed-loop-ready entries and has carried loop_blocker: none since 2026-07-05. The reason is textbook: a chemical goes onto a piece of lab-grown human skin, a dye reports how many cells survived, and OECD Test Guideline 439 says viability at or below 50% means the chemical is a Category 2 irritant. One instrument number, one published cutoff, one call. Rung 5 of the ladder in shared/evidence-model.md, no argument.

The guideline licenses several commercial tissue models as interchangeable — EpiDerm, SkinEthic, KeraSkin, epiCS — all validated against the same performance standards. A 234-substance reference database now lets somebody ask what those interchangeable models actually do on the same chemicals.

Cohen's kappa 0.339 to 1.000. Kappa scores how much two raters agree beyond chance: below 0.4 is poor, 0.6–0.8 substantial, above 0.8 near-perfect. The band spans nearly the whole scale. Accuracy 66.4–100.0%. Specificity — of the chemicals that genuinely are not irritants, how many the test correctly clears — 60.2–100.0%.

The authors' explanation is the part that belongs in this vault

Not "some models are worse." The stated cause is the shape of the rule: "variability in specificity linked to the single-threshold structure of irritation classification." The unstable chemicals are the borderline ones — Category 2 (irritant) and Category 3 (mild irritant) substances sitting close to the line — and the method "relies on a single 50% cell viability cutoff for binary classification."

And it is class-dependent in a way with a mechanism attached. Strong acids and bases and neutral organic solvents agree well. Surfactants and reactive organics do not, and the proposed reason is that surfactants "induce membrane-disruptive effects through lipid solubilization" rather than direct cytotoxicity, producing "progressive rather than abrupt cytotoxic responses." A gradual dose-response crossing a hard threshold is exactly the geometry that turns small measurement differences into different hazard classifications. Surfactants are also most of the household and personal-care chemistry this test exists to serve.

Why the tier holds anyway, and what changes instead

Apply the ladder honestly: the readout is machine-readable (absorbance), and a fixed data-interpretation procedure is published in an adopted guideline. Rung 5. closed-loop-ready HOLDS. Demoting it would be inferring the loop axis from a reproducibility finding, which is exactly what this vault refuses to do.

What changes is a domain_gate — the applicability carve-out a loop must check before trusting the rule. Precedent is direct: skin-sensitization-u-sens carries a 128–176% grey zone around its adopted 150% cutoff, recorded as a domain_gate and not a demotion, on 2026-08-08. This is the same object, on a different endpoint, found by a different route.

⚠ The fourth way a written-down rule fails to be what the tier's name implies

The vault has been accumulating these, and they are not the same failure:

  1. fixed ≠ deterministic — the rule's estimator is itself stochastic (population-variability-human-lcl-panel, Markov chain Monte Carlo, 2026-07-24);
  2. fixed ≠ sample-independent — the rule is conditioned on what else was on the plate (genotoxicity-tgx-ddi-transcriptomic-biomarker, 2026-07-22);
  3. fixed ≠ transferable — the rule compares against the testing laboratory's own historical controls (genotoxicity-3d-skin-comet, 2026-08-02);
  4. fixed ≠ stable — tonight. The rule is a single hard cutoff on a continuous measurement, and the measurement's own spread across equally-valid implementations is wide enough that chemicals near the line land on either side of it.

The fourth is the simplest and the most general. It needs no Bayesian model, no batch effect and no local history. It applies, in principle, to every threshold rule in this vault — and that is most of the closed-loop-ready tier. What makes it a finding rather than a truism is that somebody measured the spread and it turned out to be large.

For a loop specifically: an automated system reading 51% viability and an automated system reading 49% on the same chemical in two licensed tissue models will emit opposite hazard classifications with identical confidence, and neither will emit anything indicating it was close. The vault's standing worry — speed converts a known bias into a compounding one — applies without modification.
