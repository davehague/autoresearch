This source has all three at once, and it ran.

Natural-language goal → SOP → instrument code → an Opentrons OT-2 physically executing it → results → revised protocol. Not a walk-through, not a simulation. Bacteria grew in 96 of 96 wells; 24 of 24 colony PCRs produced the correct 868 bp amplicon; plasmid construction succeeded in 3 of 3 replicates with Sanger confirmation.

And the leg that makes it a loop rather than a pipeline is documented, not asserted: in the DNA-assembly run, "ProtoPilot identified and resolved two distinct experimental failures during execution… revised the protocol after failures in vector amplification and transformation." The system was wrong, saw that it was wrong, and changed what it did.

What this does to the vault's central question — less than it first appears, and something important

Since 2026-07-13 the vault's answer has been: no closed loop runs on a regulatory toxicology assay. That remains exactly true. Toxicology is not mentioned once in this paper; the confirmed absence is recorded in the source digest.

What changes is the diagnosis of why. The vault has been treating the gap as architectural — the mind and the hands existed apart, the wire between them was unspecified, and somebody would have to build the missing thing. This source builds it, on a commodity liquid handler, in a preprint, in synthetic biology.

So the remaining gap is domain, not invention. What a tox loop still needs is a validated assay, an applicability domain the loop can check, a fixed decision rule at the end, and a regulator prepared to accept the output. The vault tracks all four. Fourteen of its maps are stuck on the third one — including, as of tonight, the DNT battery with ~650 chemicals run, CNN-automated scoring, and an OECD aspiration where its decision rule should be (dnt-defined-approach-is-an-aspiration-not-a-deliverable).

The two halves are now visibly waiting on each other in the same vault: a loop with nothing to point at, and a battery with nothing to decide it.

Why the domain gap is real and not a formality

Synthetic biology has an unusually generous ground truth. A Sanger read is right or wrong, cheaply, within a day, and every task demonstrated here resolves on that timescale. Toxicology's ground truth is human harm, and it may never arrive at all.

That is the same asymmetry automation-self-driving-labs already holds a number for: GNoME proposed 380,000 structures, fewer than 5% independently validated — in materials, where checking means trying to grow a crystal. A loop that runs well where verification is same-day tells you about engineering. It tells you nothing about whether the loop can be trusted where verification is a twenty-year epidemiological question, and the vault should not let it.

The determinism problem, one layer down — and the vault has seen it twice this week

"Self-evolving" here does not mean retraining weights. It means a skill library that accumulates: "skills are created from expert rules, protocol context, SDK information and prior task trajectories; evaluated…; and revised into more reliable reusable routines," backed by a task-experience base storing "completed trajectories, user preferences, validation results, execution traces and feedback."

A system whose behaviour on task N depends on what it learned from tasks 1…N−1 is not the same system on Tuesday as it was on Monday — without one weight changing.

ai-decision-rules-are-not-deterministic (Hartung & Kleinstreuer, ingested 2026-07-19, written by the former Head of ECVAM and a NICEATM scientist) reported that "static validation processes [are] potentially outdated almost as soon as they are completed" for exactly this class of object, and proposed a companion agent that back-tests prior predictions and retroactively alerts previous users. That was framed as a concern about future AI-based test methods. This is a working system with the property, running on real hardware, three weeks later. (The connection is the vault's; ProtoPilot cites no validation literature.)
