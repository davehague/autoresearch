The missing chemistry can be a separate step, not a better cell

Almost every blocker this vault records sits at the interpretation layer: an instrument reads the plate cleanly, and nobody has written down what the number means. Twenty-seven maps are stuck there.

The metabolic-competence gap is not that kind of blocker, and the difference matters for anyone deciding where to spend. Cells in a dish mostly lack the enzymes that convert a chemical into what a body would actually produce, so a compound that is only harmful after conversion is called safe by a test system that never manufactured the harmful molecule. That is a failure at the chemistry layer — upstream of any decision rule, and unfixable by writing one. This vault's rail names it as the shared cause in several of its correlated-blind-spot rows: the fish gill cell assay and the fish embryo test both miss bioactivation-dependent compounds; the reactive-oxygen-species and 3T3 phototoxicity assays share the identical gap.

The usual proposed fix is a better test system — hepatocytes, liver-derived lines, organ chips, anything that brings its own metabolism. That works and is expensive, and it drags a second problem in with it: the vault already records that three of its four liver maps run on the same immortalised cell line, so "add metabolism" has repeatedly meant "add the same metabolism, three times, and count it as three votes."

This source proposes the other shape: make metabolic competence a modular upstream step. Engineer yeast to carry a chosen human cytochrome P450 enzyme, puncture the yeast so the chemical can reach it, run the conversion in a vessel, then take what comes out and test that in whatever assay you were going to use. The enzymes are decoupled from the biology being measured.

Why the shape is loop-relevant even though the evidence is thin

  • The step is schedulable. Roughly three hours to prepare, four to react, cofactor regeneration handled internally rather than by an operator dosing it in. That fits inside an automated run.
  • Its outputs are instrument-read — luminescence, or chromatography/mass-spectrometry peaks.
  • It is composable. A pooled "cocktail" of several enzymes narrowed down to identify which one acted is a search procedure, which is the kind of thing an experiment-selection layer can drive.
  • It attacks a blind spot rather than a bottleneck, and the vault's standing remedy for a correlated blind spot is a measurement that fails differently — not a bigger battery. Supplying the missing chemistry is that remedy in its literal form.

The honest brakes, and they are severe

This is a protocol chapter. No results, no accuracy figure, no reference chemicals, no inter-laboratory data, no failure rate, and no head-to-head comparison against human liver microsomes, the incumbent it argues against. evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory is the honest floor and is generous; the vault's ladder has no rung for an instruction manual, which is the same missing-rung schema gap logged many times over.

It produces molecules, not verdicts. An enzyme bag tells you what a chemical becomes. Whether the result is harmful is a separate assay's job, with a separate decision rule that this does not supply. So it closes the chemistry half of the gap and leaves the interpretation half exactly where it was — and now with more compounds to interpret, since every parent chemical becomes several metabolites.

And it is not toxicology. The framing is drug metabolism and pharmacology; no hazard endpoint, no Test Guideline, no validation pipeline is named. regulatory_status: none. The application to a hazard-testing blind spot is this vault's inference and is labelled as such.

Rebutted by: metabolic-competence-did-not-subsume-the-simpler-model — a scope condition on the premise rather than a contradiction of the platform. This note treats supplying the missing chemistry as a remedy for a known under-prediction. On the one dataset that tests that premise head to head (22 chemicals, one reactive-oxygen-species readout, internal concentrations measured), a metabolically competent whole-organism model did not detect a superset of what a metabolically limited cell line detected — each flagged nine chemicals, the two sets did not fully overlap, four chemicals were missed by both, and the whole-organism model flagged three of seven chemicals acting by another primary mechanism where the cell line flagged none. Adding metabolic competence traded one error profile for another rather than dominating. Nothing there tests an enzyme-bag preparation specifically, and the shape of the fix proposed here is untouched; what is qualified is the assumption that closing the chemistry gap is straightforwardly an improvement.
