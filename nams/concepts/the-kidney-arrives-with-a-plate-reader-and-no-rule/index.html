The kidney arrives fully instrument-readable, and stops one step short

Until tonight this vault held no method for kidney injury at all. Nephrotoxicity was a blank — one of the endpoint-coverage gaps the monthly connect pass exists to surface.

The blank closes with a five-readout screen in human proximal tubule cells, and the way it closes is worth reading carefully against the third axis.

Readout: fully machine-readable. Fluorescence at four wavelength pairs, absorbance at two. No morphology, no histology, no trained eye anywhere. On the vault's deterministic ladder that clears the hurdle that blocks the micronucleus assay, the cell transformation assays and zebrafish teratogenicity — all of which are human-in-the-loop because someone has to look down a microscope and judge.

Decision rule: absent, and the authors say so. The platform is "best suited for mechanistic screening and prioritisation … rather than as a standalone determinant of clinical nephrotoxicity acceptability." Nothing converts five concentration-response curves into "this chemical is nephrotoxic." Interpretation means reading the ordering in which key events fire, which is judgement.

Inputs: no animal study consumed. Not bench-bound.

So: rung 4. automatable-open-loop. A robot can generate every number and nobody has written down what the numbers mean.

Why a brand-new endpoint landing in the vault's largest blocker category is the finding

Eighteen maps already sit here. The usual story is that they got here — an assay was adopted decades ago under a guideline that never specified interpretation, and the gap is historical debt.

This one starts here. A method built in 2026, designed from a causal pathway, with instrument readouts chosen for quantification, arrives without a decision procedure — and its authors decline to write one, not from oversight but on principle, because they judge three compounds insufficient to justify it. That is the correct scientific call and it is also a precise illustration of why the blocker persists: the rule cannot honestly be written until there is enough evidence to write it against, and writing it is what makes the method usable enough to generate that evidence.

The vault has described this blocker as "a committee problem, not a biology problem" and as "cheap to fix." This case argues the cheapness is conditional. The committee cannot convene on three platinum compounds.

The loop reading, stated without endorsement

A machine could run this assay today, end to end, and produce five curves. It could not make the call, and should not — automatable-open-loop says an AI could run the wet work, and says nothing whatever about whether the resulting screen is fit to decide anything about a kidney.
