NAM framework → repeat-dose systemic toxicity concern class

Repeat-dose systemic toxicity is the hardest endpoint for NAMs — no single assay reproduces a whole-organism 28/90-day study. This 2025 framework instead composes three NAM streams into a tiered concern call: (Q)SAR in-silico flags (carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, repro/dev, endocrine, neurotoxicity → strong/moderate/low); ToxCast in-vitro bioactivity in a potency–severity matrix (potency from AC50: High <0.1 µM / Medium 0.1–10 µM / Low >10 µM); and PBK models (httk, PK-Sim, GastroPlus) predicting 14-day plasma Cmax to tier systemic bioavailability. Output: Low / Medium / High concern.

Two axes, held apart. evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method (a published, structured framework) but strength: single-study — a 12-chemical proof-of-concept with no formal concordance statistics, and the authors note the NAM calls trended toward lower concern than conventional in-vivo categories, needing cutoff tuning to align (stance: open-question). regulatory_status: none — not in any adoption pipeline. Acknowledged gaps: missing assays (methemoglobinemia, cardiotoxicity), parent-vs-metabolite uncertainty, and reliance on expert judgement. Its PBK layer composes-with pbk-qivive-ivive-regulatory-acceptance. The frontier of NAMs for systemic tox — honest about being unfinished.

Bias watch — corrected 2026-07-09 (PDF archived, funding statement read). The framework is the output of an ECETOC Task Force ("Staged assessment for low tonnage chemicals"), entered into the EU EPAA NAM Designathon 2023. ECETOC states it "is financed by its membership, which are the leading companies with interests in the manufacture and use of chemicals, biomaterials and pharmaceuticals"; the author affiliations are predominantly industry (Corteva, Dow, BASF, Syngenta). So funding_flag: [industry], not the none this note carried at first ingest — that grade was set from the abstract before the PDF was archived. The flag is load-bearing: the framework's own finding is that its NAM calls trend toward lower concern than the conventional in-vivo categories, and the party proposing the cutoffs that would resolve that has a direct interest in where they land. No claim here is withdrawn — the science stands as published — but it is industry-originated self-assessment, not independent validation, and is recorded as such.
