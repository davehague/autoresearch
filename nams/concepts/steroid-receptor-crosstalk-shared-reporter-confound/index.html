Steroid-receptor crosstalk: the endocrine column's correlated confound

The vault's second correlated-failure finding across adopted methods — and where the first one (FET / RTgill-W1, 2026-07-11) was biological, this one is molecular.

What the guideline says

"The glucocorticoid receptor (GR) is expressed in 22Rv1 origin cells alongside AR, endogenously. The minimal GR-mediated response can interfere with the AR-mediated response because the GR is structurally similar to the AR and shares hormone response elements that exhibit cross-talk with the AR. To eliminate GR expression in cells, a GR-knockout 22Rv1/MMTV cell line was developed using the … CRISPR/Cas9 system." — OECD TG 458, Annex E

The shape of the problem

The steroid receptors — AR, GR, PR, MR — are close structural relatives that bind overlapping DNA response elements. A luciferase reporter sitting downstream of such an element does not know which receptor activated it. Selectivity is not a property the assay has; it is a property the developers had to build. TG 458's own comparison table shows three different builds of the same fix:

MethodHow it stops reading the GR
AR-EcoScreen™"Minimal GR crosstalk due to the selection of an appropriate androgen responsive element" — choose an ARE the GR discriminates against
AR-CALUX®"No or little GR, ER and PR expression" — choose a host cell that barely expresses the competition
22Rv1/MMTV_GR-KO"GR knock-out" — delete the competing receptor with CRISPR/Cas9

Three adopted methods; one confound; three engineering answers.

Why this reaches across the vault

The reporter backbone in TG 458's Method 3 is the MMTV LTR (mouse mammary tumour virus long terminal repeat). That is the same promoter:

  • TG 454A (endocrine-glucocorticoid-receptor-transactivation) uses MMTV-luciferase in the HMLN-hGR line to read the glucocorticoid receptor — the very receptor TG 458 has to delete.
  • TG 455 (endocrine-er-transactivation) places VM7Luc4E2's four EREs upstream of an MMTV promoter to read the estrogen receptor, and reassures the reader that this promoter "exhibits only minor cross-reactivity with other steroid and non-steroid hormones."

One viral promoter is the reporter substrate for three different adopted OECD receptor assays. The MMTV LTR is, historically, a glucocorticoid-responsive element — that is what it was characterised as — and it is being used to read androgen and estrogen signalling in guidelines that each, separately, argue the crosstalk down to "minimal." Each guideline handles its own case competently. None of them cites the others. Nobody has stated the problem jointly.

The consequence for battery reasoning

This is the same failure of independence the vault caught in the aquatic column, in a new place. If a chemical is screened across the endocrine Level-2 panel — TG 455 (ER), TG 458 (AR), TG 454A (GR), TG 456 (steroidogenesis) — the four results look like four independent mechanistic votes. They are not fully independent: three of them read reporters on overlapping response elements, and the selectivity that separates them is a cell-line engineering artefact rather than a fact of biology. A promiscuous steroid-receptor ligand — and promiscuity across AR/GR/PR is common — is exactly the chemical class where the panel's votes are most correlated, and it is exactly the class the panel most needs to characterise correctly.

Note also that this compounds the artefact TG 454A already documents: interfering or cytotoxic chemicals mimic competitive antagonists in reporter-gene antagonist modes. So the endocrine panel now has two named, shared, structural false-signal mechanisms running across its adopted members.

Flagged, not resolved. No source read in this run quantifies the residual crosstalk, and none of the four guidelines treats it as a cross-guideline problem. The vault records it as an open question for a human, logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md — and as the second demonstration that battery independence is an assumption, not a property.
