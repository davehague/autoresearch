The metabolism prosthesis has a candidate replacement, and it was tested on the chemicals that expose it

This vault named the fix before the paper arrived, and that is the only reason this modest study is worth a concept note. On 2026-07-22, setting the correlated_blind_spot between the TGx-DDI transcriptomic biomarker and the in-vitro micronucleus assay, genotoxicity-tgx-ddi-transcriptomic-biomarker recorded:

"The fix is not another cell assay on the same S9; it is metabolically competent cells (HepaRG is one of the p53-verified lines the paper names, and is a real option here) or an explicit domain gate routing suspected pro-genotoxicants out before the rule fires."

Saleh et al. ran the first half of that sentence.

What the prosthesis is and why it couples two assays

Cultured human cells barely metabolise anything. A liver does two things to a foreign chemical: phase I (usually oxidation — which is where a harmless molecule can be turned dangerous) and phase II (conjugation — which is usually where the danger gets mopped up). Cells in a dish do neither well.

The field's universal workaround is rat liver S9: homogenised rat liver, spun down, tipped into the well. It works, it is cheap, and it is the same prosthesis everywhere — which is precisely the problem. A pro-genotoxicant is inert as you dose it and DNA-reactive only after conversion. It is detected only if rat S9 performs that specific conversion. If the route is human-specific, or runs through a step S9 does not reproduce, the chemical reads negative in the transcriptomic biomarker and negative in the micronucleus assay for the identical reason — and a weight-of-evidence rule counts two negatives as increased confidence.

One metabolic assumption, reported as two independent results. That is the vault's rail, and metabolism is its most recurrent instance: FET ↔ RTgill-W1, the ROS assay ↔ 3T3 NRU, TGx-DDI ↔ micronucleus.

Why spheroids are the structural answer rather than a nicer culture

A spheroid is a ball of cells rather than a flat sheet. Cells have neighbours on every side, oxygen and nutrient gradients form, and liver cells in that arrangement keep making their metabolic enzymes for days instead of losing them over hours. HepaRG is the human liver line that retains the phase I/phase II balance best; this vault already tracks it as the cell system behind OECD Test Guideline 445A, the adopted in-vitro metabolism method (metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg).

So a 72-hour exposure in HepaRG spheroids is not "the same assay in a fancier plate." It is the same assay with the borrowed rat liver removed and a working human one grown in its place. If it works, the shared failure mode does not just shrink — it stops being shared, because each assay would carry its own metabolism rather than both leaning on one external reagent.

What was actually shown, and it is less than that

The abstract reports no numbers. Not one. The finding is two adjectives — "generally aligned" and "effectively distinguishing" — and the first of them concedes that something did not align.

Three specific gaps decide how much this is worth, and none is answerable from what is public:

  1. No 2D-versus-3D comparison is described. Without running the same 15 chemicals in 2D with rat S9 as a comparator, the study cannot show the spheroid's own metabolism did better. It shows the biomarkers survive the transfer.
  2. It is not stated whether the 2D-derived classifier thresholds were applied unchanged or re-fitted. If unchanged, that is a genuinely strong portability result. If re-fitted on these fifteen chemicals, it is close to circular. The two readings are far apart and the abstract does not separate them.
  3. Fifteen chemicals, one study, no reported replication or blinding. The ring trial that earned this map its consolidating status had four laboratories and blinded samples. This has neither.

What it does and does not move

The correlated_blind_spot stays exactly where it is. The blind spot is a property of the assays as currently specified and validated — TK6 cells plus rat S9 — and nothing here changes that specification. What changes is that the escape route now has a data point instead of only an argument, on a panel deliberately loaded with the chemicals that expose the gap.

And loop_readiness does not move either. TempO-Seq is instrument-read; that was already true. The decision rule is unchanged. A metabolically competent cell model makes an assay's answers better; it does not make them more automatable. The axes stay separate, which is the point of having three of them.

One thing to watch, flagged now. GENOMARK is a second transcriptomic genotoxicity biomarker, developed at Sciensano, and two of this paper's authors are there. On the GENOMARK half of the result this is a developer reporting on their own biomarker; on the TGx-DDI half it is not. The vault has no map for GENOMARK and one source is not enough to coin one — logged as a gap rather than guessed at.
