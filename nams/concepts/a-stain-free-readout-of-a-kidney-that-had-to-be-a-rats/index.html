A stain-free readout of a kidney — and the kidney had to be a rat's

The escape hatch is real

This vault tracks a specific blocker on the third axis: a trained human is load-bearing in the call. It sits on the in-vitro micronucleus assay, the cell transformation assay and zebrafish teratogenicity, and the vault has been collecting demonstrated machine-vision escape hatches from it — imaging-flow-cytometry-micronucleus-deep-learning-scoring, bhas42-cnn-transformed-foci-scoring, zebrafish-machine-vision-malformation-scoring, buccal-micronucleus-machine-vision-scoring.

This is one of the strongest such demonstrations yet, and it is pointed at the biggest target of all: histopathology. In a conventional preclinical safety study the definitive readout is a pathologist looking at stained tissue slices and describing what changed. It is expensive, slow, and it is a morphological judgment made by a person.

SLAM removes both halves of that. No dye is added — the tissue's own molecules emit or transform the laser light, so what comes back is four physical measurements rather than a stain someone chose: NAD(P)H and FAD autofluorescence (the cell's energy-carrying molecules, reporting metabolic state), second harmonic generation from collagen (i.e. scarring), and third harmonic generation from structural boundaries. A feature-based classifier then makes the injured/not-injured call at balanced accuracy 0.944 in the outer medulla — meaning that on an evenly split set it is right about 94 times in 100, averaged across both classes so that a lopsided sample cannot flatter it.

It is also spatially and temporally honest in a way a single summary number is not: the outer medulla is more sensitive than the cortex (0.944 vs 0.860), the loudest damage peaks at Day 6, and fibrosis-related features are still readable at Day 29 — four weeks after one dose. That kind of resolution is what the multi-channel design buys, and the classifier's reliance on inter-channel correlations confirms it.

And then the input

Rung 2 of the deterministic ladder in shared/evidence-model.md: does the method's own pipeline consume an in-vivo study? It does — it is one. Every image is of tissue from a rat that was dosed with cisplatin and killed. First match wins, so the tier is set before the readout question is ever reached: bench-bound / bench_bound_reason: animal-input.

Why this is not an accusation

The work is good and its authors are not overclaiming: their own closing phrase is "preclinical safety studies." But "new approach methodologies" is an author-supplied keyword on this paper, and separating loop_readiness from "non-animal" is precisely this vault's discipline. A stain-free imaging pipeline run on a rat kidney is a Refine — the third of the 3Rs (Replace, Reduce, Refine) — not a Replace. The honest downstream consequence deserves saying: an animal study that becomes cheaper, faster, more objective and better-sampled becomes more competitive against its replacement, not less used. Nothing in the paper claims otherwise; nothing in the paper notices it either.

What it does and does not do to the kidney map

Nothing moves. nephrotoxicity-ciptec-aop-battery holds Status emerging and loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop. This is a different method — not a variant of the ciPTEC battery, not a scorer for it, not a validation of it — so it is recorded as a ripple and a contrast, not as evidence about that method.

The contrast is the point:

  • The vault's only non-animal kidney method is a five-readout plate-reader battery in one immortalised cell line, demonstrated on three platinum drugs, whose authors decline to write a decision rule because three compounds do not justify one (the-kidney-arrives-with-a-plate-reader-and-no-rule).
  • The animal-side kidney method in this note has a working classifier, region-level resolution, a 29-day time course and a 0.944 balanced accuracy.

On this endpoint, the instrumentation of the animal study is further along than its replacement is. That is not an argument for animals. It is a statement about where the engineering has been spent, and it is the kind of thing that decides which way a field drifts.

The limits, plainly

One compound. Cisplatin is the textbook nephrotoxicant — the easiest possible positive, and there are no negatives in the design to be wrong about. One species, one laboratory, one instrument. No sensitivity, no specificity, no concordance with human kidney injury, and no head-to-head against a pathologist is reported in the retrievable record, so the claim "as good as histopathology" is not supported and is not made here. evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, regulatory_status: none.
