Level zero: the part of the method the vendor won't tell you

The claim

Before any number exists there is a machine, and the machine has settings. Blum et al. call this level zero and put it explicitly outside the numbered data levels, because it is not data — it is the configuration that determines what the data will be.

"Modern laboratory instruments often only have an on/off button, a start button, and some sample input. Researchers may not be allowed or able to change hardware parameters or associated software settings."

And the consequence, stated as an observed phenomenon rather than a risk:

"it may happen that variability is observed among results although the test system, the exposure scheme, and many other NAM elements defined in the test method description remain unchanged."

Their examples: optical filter bandwidth; photomultiplier or camera gain settings; electrode impedance and amplifier characteristics for microelectrode array devices; ambient temperature and CO₂ control.

The independent corroboration, from a different panel, the same year

piergiovanni-2025-imaging-based-methods-regulatory-tox — a European Commission Joint Research Centre expert group on imaging methods, 24 authors, sharing only two names with the Blum panel — reaches the same place from the imaging side and puts a sharper edge on it:

"Manufacturers are reluctant to disclose details on the instrument settings and controls"

with the stated consequence that "only few imaging-based in vitro methods would be used for regulatory toxicology." It adds a practice problem the Blum panel does not mention: "many researchers relying on the acquired images themselves to set the acquisition parameters for each experiment" — the settings are being tuned per run, by eye, against the data being collected, and not recorded. And a materials problem nobody would guess: "Current standards inadequately address plate bottom flatness."

This is the vault's rail applied to evidence rather than to a battery, in the good direction. Two papers agreeing is usually worth one vote if they share a cause. These two share almost no authors, appeared in different journals (ALTEX, Archives of Toxicology), were convened by different institutions (University of Konstanz / CAAT-Europe, and the JRC), and approach from different technical domains (general NAM data pipelines, high-content imaging). They converge anyway. That is closer to two votes than one.

What it means for a loop

An autonomous laboratory is defined by the machines it owns. Level zero is precisely the layer that does not travel — and it is the layer a loop cannot introspect, because the vendor has not exposed it. Three concrete consequences:

  • A loop cannot verify it is running the method it thinks it is running. It can check that the protocol was followed and the acceptance criteria passed, and still be sampling a different physical quantity than the laboratory that validated the assay.
  • access_gate gets a mechanism. The vault records "Amnis ImageStream" and "sole-supplier EPISKIN" as commercial barriers. Level zero says the barrier is not only can you buy the box but can you configure the box the same way, and the answer to the second is often no even if you own one.
  • Reproducibility drift is silent. Blum et al. note that "the analytical device even within one given lab can change" as suppliers and firmware move. A loop running for a year on the same protocol is not running the same method at the end of it, and nothing in the data will say so.

Compare eight-methods-that-broke-when-a-second-lab-ran-them: of the eight documented transfer failures in jacobs-2024-ring-trials-reproducibility-crisis, the causes include a plastic dish's sterilisation method and a shipping delay. Level zero is that category, generalised — the variables outside the biology, which only a second laboratory ever finds.
