Two tests that both measure the wrong half of the definition

What GHS Category 1 actually means. Not "severe damage" — irreversible damage. The UN classification turns on whether the eye recovers, which in the rabbit test is assessed by watching for up to 21 days. A chemical can produce modest initial injury that never heals and be Category 1; a chemical can produce dramatic initial injury that fully resolves and be Category 2.

What BCOP and ICE measure. BCOP (TG 437) uses excised bovine eyes and reads corneal opacity plus permeability to a fluorescent dye, at a fixed time after exposure. ICE (TG 438) uses excised chicken eyes and reads corneal swelling, opacity and fluorescein retention, likewise at fixed times. Both measure the magnitude of acute injury. Neither can watch for 21 days — the tissue is dead.

So they fail on the same chemicals, and GD 263 says so:

"Currently accepted methods for identifying UN GHS Cat. 1, like BCOP and ICE, underpredict 14-47% of the in vivo Cat. 1 chemicals (see Table 4.1), mostly those inducing persistent effects without occurrence of initial high level injuries (classified in vivo based only on persistence of effects)."

Between one in seven and one in two of the chemicals that permanently damage an eye are called something milder by these methods, and the shared cause is structural, not statistical: the property the classification is defined on is the property neither method can observe.

The confirming instance, also in the document. The BCOP OP-KIT "was found to be under-predictive for identification of UN GHS Cat. 1 agrochemical mixtures (Kolle et al., 2015)," and GD 263 attributes it directly: "This could be due to the fact that the majority of the tested agrochemical mixtures (n=19 out of 21) in this study were classified in vivo based on persistence of effects only, which is a type of effect known not to be identified per se by the currently OECD adopted in vitro methods aiming at the identification of UN GHS Cat 1." Not "by BCOP" — by the currently OECD adopted in vitro methods, plural. The guidance generalises its own blind spot across the whole top-down tier.

This is a correlated blind spot of a kind the vault has not recorded before. Its eight existing rows all share a missing biological subsystem — no nervous system, no metabolism, a shared promoter, a shared cell lineage. This one shares a missing time dimension. The methods are mechanistically diverse (opacity, permeability, swelling, dye retention across two species' excised eyes) and still fail together, because the regulatory question has a temporal component that no acute readout has access to.

And it reaches into tonight's other new map. TG 492B infers the Cat 1 / Cat 2 boundary from how fast cells die precisely because it cannot measure reversibility either (tg492b-three-way-eye-classification-from-exposure-time) — a cleverer approximation of the same missing dimension, and the mechanistic reason its errors concentrate at that boundary. The persistence blind spot is not a property of two ex-vivo assays; it is a property of the whole adopted eye column, and the vault should carry it that way.

GD 263 states the remedy, and it is the Tox21 counter-design: "the use of additional in vitro test methods suitable for identifying UN GHS Cat. 1 based, if possible, on different mechanism of actions, may be considered in case a negative result is obtained with a first in vitro test method used for this purpose." But note what this cannot fix: adding a differently-mechanised acute method does not add a temporal measurement. Mechanistic diversity is the right axis for a shared-subsystem blind spot; it is the wrong axis for a shared-missing-dimension one.

The loop consequence. An automated eye assessment that stacks BCOP, ICE and an RhCE method and takes concurrence as confirmation will systematically clear the slow-persistent chemicals — and the more methods it stacks, the more confident it becomes, because they all agree for the same reason.

Bonus finding recorded here because it belongs to these two methods: TG 437 and TG 438 are not replacement methods. They run on eyes taken from slaughtered cattle and chickens. Two of the nine adopted "non-animal" eye tests in this IATA still consume animals, which is the vault's replacement-vs-reduction line (immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm) running through the middle of an adopted battery.
