The comparator has a published failure rate, and it has not moved since 1963

Every method in this vault is graded, ultimately, against a system built on animal studies. This is the first source the vault holds that measures how well that system performs at its actual job.

A registered systematic review pooled sixty years of published drug-attrition statistics — 32 publications, 60 attrition datasets, 36 failure-reason datasets, spanning 1963 to 2017:

  • Mean clinical attrition ran at roughly 80% to 91%. For every ten drugs that reached the point of being tried in humans, between eight and nine never became a medicine.
  • ⚠ It did not improve. It got worse. "Clinical drug attrition has not improved in the last six decades, peaking at ∼91% in the 2000s–2010s."
  • ⚠ About 77% of the stated failure causes were biological — safety, efficacy, or pharmacokinetics. Not commercial, not strategic, not funding. Those three are exactly what preclinical testing is run to establish.

What this does and does not license

It does not show that animal models caused those failures. An attrition rate is a fact about drug development as a whole. To indict the animal study specifically you would need to show, drug by drug, that a preclinical result predicted the opposite of the trial outcome — and this design cannot do that. The failure reasons are as reported by the reporting party, which is a self-report, not a diagnosis. The authors' closing inference ("prevailing preclinical approaches — many of which heavily rely on animal models — often have limited ability to predict clinical outcomes") is a step past their own data, and it is the step their affiliations bear on.

And survivorship runs the other way, hard. Drugs the animal study correctly stopped never entered the clinical denominator at all. This design is structurally blind to the animal test's true positives — the exact blind spot named, on the same night and from an unrelated discipline, by the genotoxicity editorial's "conceptual catch 22" (the-adopted-genotox-nam-was-ring-tested-against-the-rodent-assay): human data does not exist precisely where the old method worked. Two independent literatures, both saying the comparator cannot be evaluated on the cases where it succeeded.

What it does license, and it is enough to be worth recording

The premise this vault runs on is now a measured quantity rather than an assumption. The vault's founding statement is that non-animal methods matter because animal-based prediction is imperfect. Until tonight that was a background belief in the field's rhetoric. It is now a number with a stated method behind it: 80-91%, not improving, three quarters of it biological.

That reframes what "validated" means on this vault's top rung. A method that agrees closely with an animal study is being certified against a system with this record — which is not an argument for accepting weaker evidence, but is an argument against reading concordance-with-animal as concordance-with-truth. See validation-paradigm-nam-confidence, whose central finding is that the field's own validation body has already declared the old one-size-fits-all paradigm obsolete.

The loop reading

None, directly — this touches no assay, no readout and no decision rule, and moves nothing on the loop_readiness axis.

Indirectly it prices one thing sharply. A closed loop's value proposition is that it can run vastly more experiments than people can. If the field's dominant failure mode is that the experiments predict the wrong thing, running more of them faster is not obviously the fix, and this vault should keep saying so while it grades methods for automatability.

The interests to declare, and there are two pointing the same way

funding_flag: [industry, method-developer], plus an advocacy interest the vault's flag values cannot express. Four authors are at Doctors Against Animal Experiments and the European Coalition to End Animal Experiments — advocacy organisations campaigning to end animal testing. Four more work for companies selling the replacements, including Emulate, whose own Liver-Chip validation this vault already carries as developer-run (hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps). A review concluding that the field urgently needs non-animal methods, written by organisations campaigning for them and companies selling them, is directionally interested from both ends at once.

The mitigations are real and deserve equal weight: a prospectively registered protocol (osf.io/46mw5) fixed before the search, restrictive stated inclusion criteria, independent academic co-authors (Utrecht, Aarhus, Murdoch, Johns Hopkins CAAT), and an outcome that is arithmetic over other people's published numbers rather than a judgement call. The counting is checkable. The closing inference is the part to read sceptically. Graded strength: suggestive on that basis, not on the numbers being weak.

Rebutted by: five-whole-body-properties-a-dish-is-argued-not-to-reach — accepts that the incumbent has an unimproving failure rate but denies the inference drawn from it, arguing that the replacements cannot supply five specific whole-system properties (whole-body biodistribution, immune handling, organ clearance, chronic toxicity, therapeutic index) and that context of use, not the comparator's failure rate, should decide what may be replaced. The tension resolves into a scope condition rather than a contradiction: both claims can hold at once, and if they do the honest reading is that nothing currently answers those five questions reliably — a coverage gap, not a case for either method. Note the mirrored conflicts of interest, and weigh them the same way: this note's authors include advocacy organisations and replacement vendors; the rebutting note's author is a therapeutics co-founder and shareholder whose own product class depends on a whole-body biodistribution claim.
