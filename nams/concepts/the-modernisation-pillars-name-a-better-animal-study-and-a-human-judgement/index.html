The guideline is being opened, and two of its five modernisation pillars point away from a machine

The event matters more than any of its content. A required animal study does not disappear because a better method exists; it disappears when the instrument that requires it is rewritten. ICH S7A had gone 25 years without revision. A multi-stakeholder workshop in Brussels in February 2026 — industry, trade associations, regulators, contract research organisations and animal-protection NGOs — met to say what a revision should contain. That is the mechanism, and this vault has seen it running very few times.

The reported themes, from the paper's own abstract:

  1. a modality- and technology-agnostic framework — the guideline was written for small molecules and now has to cover biologics, cell and gene therapies, oligonucleotides;
  2. greater integration of secondary pharmacology — screening a drug against off-target receptors and channels, largely in-vitro and computational work;
  3. use of state-of-the-art functional in vivo approaches;
  4. application of weight-of-evidence decision frameworks;
  5. clearer interpretation of functional findings, including the concept of adversity — agreeing when a measured change counts as a harm.

The stated goal is "a modernized, risk-based framework… enabling innovation, global regulatory harmonization, and implementation of the 3Rs principles."

⚠ Read pillars 3 and 4 against this vault's third axis

Pillar 3 is a Refine, not a Replace. The 3Rs are Replace, Reduce, Refine. A New Approach Methodology is a Replace technology. "State-of-the-art functional in vivo approaches" means a better-instrumented animal study — telemetry rather than restraint, continuous recording rather than spot checks. Genuinely better science and genuinely better for the animals. It moves nothing on the loop axis, because the loop axis asks whether a machine could run the method end to end, and there is an animal in it.

This vault recorded the identical shape one day earlier, on a different endpoint. neurotoxicity-cns-safety-nams-ai, opened 2026-08-13, holds a review whose emerging capabilities are electroencephalography for seizure liability and "advanced sensors with artificial intelligence for continuous, non-invasive monitoring of animal behavior." On the two endpoints where automation investment is heaviest in this vault's sweep, the automation is being pointed at the animal study. Two independent sources, two days apart, same direction.

Pillar 4 is the vault's most-recorded loop blocker, arriving as a design goal. A weight-of-evidence framework is a documented procedure in which a qualified human weighs several imperfect signals and decides. It is not an algorithm. This vault holds two maps blocked at exactly that step and nowhere else: eye-hazard-iata-gd263 — nine methods, several of them individually closed-loop-ready, integrated by an unspecified expert judgement — and hepatotoxicity-cholestasis-battery, same shape. Machine vision, the escape hatch for most of the vault's other blockers, does not reach this one, because nothing is being looked at; a decision is being made.

The honest counterweight, which this note owes the reader

Pillar 4 could go the other way and this note is not entitled to assume it won't. A weight-of-evidence framework that is genuinely written down — as OECD Guideline 497's 2-out-of-3 rule is written down — is a fixed decision procedure and would be a promotion, not a blocker. The same applies to pillar 5: agreeing when a functional change is adverse is a real scientific gap, and closing it with a written criterion would be the single most loop-relevant thing this revision could do. The abstract does not say which way either goes, and the full text is behind Elsevier. Logged as an open question, not resolved in either direction.

What does not move

Nothing is adopted. No draft exists. No method is named. No number is reported. This is regulatory_status: proposed at best, and the underlying requirement — three vital functions, assessed before first human dose — stands exactly as written in 2000.
