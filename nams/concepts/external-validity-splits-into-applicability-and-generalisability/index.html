External validity splits into applicability and generalisability

Internal validity asks whether an experiment's own result is trustworthy — controls, blinding, replicates, statistics. Toxicology has appraisal instruments for that.

External validity asks the harder question: the result is correct, but is it about the thing you care about? A poorly-metabolising cell line, dosed at a concentration no human bloodstream reaches, can return an impeccably rigorous number that answers nobody's question.

This paper's contribution is to say external validity is not one property and to name the two halves:

  • Applicability — does this study address this question? Per-study. Checkable at screening time, before you have anything else on the table.
  • Generalisability — does the finding hold beyond the conditions tested? A property of the body of evidence. Not assessable from one study, by construction.

Their recommendation follows from the split: the forthcoming INVITES-EX tool should assess "the applicability of individual studies" — because a per-study checklist can do that and cannot do the other one.

⚠ What this does to the vault's third axis

This vault carries a frontmatter modifier called domain_gate, defined as the applicability-domain carve-out a loop must check before trusting the decision rule. Its instances are all of one kind:

  • OECD Test Guideline 435's pH window (much of pH 4.5–8.5 screened out)
  • Test Guideline 249's solubility bounds (log KOW −4 to 7)
  • Test Guideline 494's runnable pre-test — the vault's favourite, because a robot can measure whether the chemical is in domain rather than being told
  • The DNT battery's biological gate (blind to STAT3- and TrkB-mediated mechanisms), the vault's hardest case, because it cannot be measured before the answer is known

Every one of those is applicability. They are per-chemical, per-study checks of whether this particular run addresses the question.

The vault has no field for generalisability, and that omission now has a name. Generalisability is what tells you whether a result established in one laboratory, on one cell lot, with one instrument, holds anywhere else. And this paper's structural point is that you cannot get it from a single study — it is a property that only appears when several studies are compared.

The loop consequence, stated plainly

A closed loop is one laboratory running very fast. One instrument configuration, one reagent supplier, one local protocol, one shipping route. That architecture maximises internal consistency by construction — high internal consistency is what an optimiser optimises toward.

It produces, structurally, exactly zero evidence about generalisability. Not weak evidence: none, because generalisability is defined across studies and a loop is one study repeated.

This sharpens rather than repeats the finding recorded on 2026-07-26 from Jacobs et al.'s eight documented inter-laboratory transfer failures — a loop's consistency is not evidence that it is right; it is evidence that its unknown biases are stable. That was an empirical observation from eight case histories. This is the definitional reason it must be true, and it supplies the missing vocabulary: the loop can establish applicability and cannot establish generalisability, and the vault's schema records only the first.

Honest brakes. A conceptual framework paper: nothing measured, no instrument released, no inter-rater agreement, no adoption by any regulator. The group defining external validity is the group that will build the tool operationalising it. Full text unobtainable, so the operational criteria themselves are unread — the distinction above is the paper's headline, not its machinery.
