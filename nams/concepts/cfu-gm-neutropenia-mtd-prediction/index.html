CFU-GM myelotoxicity assay (Pessina 2003 / ESAC 2006) → immunotoxicity (myelotoxicity)

Human and murine myeloid progenitor cells (colony-forming unit — granulocyte/macrophage) are cultured in semi-solid medium and exposed to a xenobiotic; inhibition of colony formation is the readout. The prediction model is deliberately mechanism-naïve: it takes the mouse in-vivo MTD and scales it by the human/mouse ratio of in-vitro progenitor sensitivity to predict the human MTD — the acute exposure producing the maximum tolerated fall in absolute neutrophil count.

The axes split as far apart as this vault's vocabulary allows: evidence_tier: formally-validated, regulatory_status: none.

On validity. An ECVAM-coordinated international blind trial (Pessina et al., Toxicol Sci 75(2):355–367, 2003; PMID 12883091) tested 20 drugs — 14 antineoplastics, 2 antivirals, 3 therapeutic agents, 1 pesticide. The model correctly predicted the human MTD for 17 of 20; of the three misses, two fell within a 6-fold error and one at roughly 10-fold. Inter-laboratory reproducibility of the GLP-compliant SOP was confirmed. funding_flag: none — the validation was run by an independent European reference laboratory, not by a developer or a sponsor. This is the cleanest validation provenance in the vault.

On acceptance: nothing. There is no OECD Test Guideline for the CFU-GM assay. What exists is the ESAC statement of 21 March 2006, whose exact wording is the whole story:

"unanimously endorsed the CFU-GM assay for predicting acute neutropenia in humans as a substitute to using a second species, such as the dog, for this purpose. It should be noted that the test relies on the availability of mouse MTD data and is, therefore, not a full replacement method, but is intended to reduce the overall numbers of animals needed in toxicity testing. Performance standards for the assay should be developed."

The endorsement is narrow (replace the second species, not the first). The performance standards ESAC asked for — a prerequisite for a Test Guideline — were never developed.

The ceiling is architectural, not political. The usual shape in this vault is good science waiting on slow regulators. Here the method's own design explains the stall: a prediction model that consumes a mouse in-vivo MTD cannot be a replacement method, so the only guideline it could anchor is one for reducing animal use. Contrast OECD TG 430 (transcutaneous electrical resistance), which uses excised rat skin and is an adopted TG: TG 430 consumes rat tissue; the CFU-GM model consumes a rat-or-mouse study. That distinction — between a method that uses animal-derived material and a method that cannot run without a prior in-vivo experiment — is where the replacement line actually falls. Compare membrane-barrier-corrosion-subcategorisation, whose membrane is entirely synthetic.

Where it sits. il2-luc-immunotoxicity-screening (TG 444A) is adopted and explicitly a battery component; the battery it names includes myelotoxicity CFU-GM assays. So the vault now holds two nodes of that unmapped battery, adopted and unadopted respectively, and they fail in opposite ways: TG 444A is regulatorily accepted but reads a single mechanism (T-cell cytokine transcription) with no published sensitivity/specificity; CFU-GM has a real, independently validated quantitative predictivity against human clinical data and no regulatory standing at all.
