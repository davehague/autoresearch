The vault has been asking whether AI can replace the animal test. Here it is making the animal test better.

This vault opened its loop-readiness axis on 2026-07-12 to answer one question: which methods could a machine run end to end? Every source since has been graded against that. The implicit model has been a race — assays climb toward machine-runnability, and the animal test is the thing being climbed away from.

This source is a reminder that the automation is not obliged to run in that direction, and on the endpoint where brain safety is decided, it is not.

The paper's emerging capabilities are electroencephalography (electrodes reading the brain's electrical activity, to catch seizure risk) and advanced sensor technologies with artificial intelligence for continuous, non-invasive monitoring of animal behaviour. Both are genuine improvements, and the improvement is precisely characterised by the authors: continuous instead of episodic, objective instead of observer-dependent, across the full day/night cycle instead of during working hours. They "reduce reliance on episodic observer-dependent measurements."

That is machine vision replacing a human scorer — the exact escape hatch this vault tracks on assays scored by eye — applied to an animal in a cage rather than to a slide under a microscope.

Why this is a push-back and not just a curiosity

The 3Rs are Replace, Reduce, Refine. All three are legitimate; they are not interchangeable; and a New Approach Methodology is specifically a Replace technology. This paper's own closing formulation is "integrating refined in vivo methods with New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) and digital technologies" — which is honest, and which means the AI content and the NAM content are different halves of the sentence.

The push-back is against a reading this vault could easily drift into: that a paper carrying "NAMs" and "AI" in its title is evidence about replacement. It is evidence about instrumentation. The vault records the strategy as described and refuses to infer a replacement claim the authors do not make.

And the honest counter-argument deserves stating, because it is strong. Refinement is not a distraction from replacement; a continuously monitored, quantitatively scored animal study produces a better reference answer, and every non-animal method in this vault is ultimately graded against a reference answer. The vault has repeatedly recorded how bad those reference answers are — animal-test variance is itself a limiting factor, and one method's reference standard had to be assembled by committee because no animal test could supply it. Improving the comparator is upstream work on every replacement claim in the field. That case is real. It is also not the case the title makes.

The bias watch, said plainly

Four of nine authors including the corresponding author are at Neurocrine Biosciences, a company developing central-nervous-system drugs. One is at Charles River Laboratories, a contract research organisation whose business is running preclinical animal studies — the studies this paper recommends refining rather than replacing. funding_flag: [industry].

The counterweight is equally real and goes in the same sentence: two authors are at NICEATM, the US body that evaluates alternative toxicological methods, and one is at FDA CDER, the US drug regulator. This is the same composition the vault recorded for the thyroid microtissue validation on 2026-07-18 — interested parties inside a regulator-adjacent exercise. Neither half cancels the other, and the vault records both rather than choosing.

For a loop

Nothing here is loop-shaped in this vault's sense, and the reason is worth being precise about. The sensors are machine-readable, the EEG is machine-readable, the behavioural scoring is a model. Every component is automated. What the components feed is an assessment that consumes a conventional animal study — see an-integration-that-consumes-an-animal-study-is-bench-bound-at-the-top — so the assembled thing sits at the bottom of the loop-readiness ladder while being built almost entirely out of automated parts.

That combination is new in this vault and is the reason this note exists: automation of the components is not evidence about automation of the decision, and this is the clearest case of the two coming apart.
