Six prediction tools voting together are not six votes

A screening workflow for cosmetic ingredients ran two layers over the same nine sunscreen UV filters:

  • The model layer — six free structure-based prediction tools, combined by majority consensus.
  • The assay layer — existing robotic cell-assay data from the US EPA's ToxCast programme, available for four of the nine.

They disagreed, and in the direction that matters.

"Using a majority consensus approach for integrating the in silico tools, ER activity was predicted only for ES. ToxCast data showed EAS potential for three of the four UV filters: OC and BMD (ER and AR antagonists), and ES as an ER agonist."

ER is the oestrogen receptor and AR the androgen receptor — the protein switches oestrogen and testosterone act through (endocrine-er-transactivation, endocrine-ar-transactivation). So the tools' consensus flagged one chemical; the assay data flagged three of four, including octocrylene and avobenzone, which the consensus had cleared.

The structural point, and it is the vault's rail arriving somewhere new

shared/evidence-model.md states the correlated-blind-spot rule for assays: two methods in one battery that fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason cast one vote, not two — and an automated system that trusts the battery's decision rule compounds that error rather than catching it.

These six tools are structure-based models. They are trained on overlapping public toxicity datasets, they encode overlapping structural alerts, and several draw on the same underlying regulatory data compilations. A hazard that is not visible in a molecule's structure — or is visible only after the body metabolises it — is invisible to all six for one shared reason. Majority voting across them converts a shared blind spot into apparent agreement, which is worse than a single tool being wrong, because the agreement reads as confidence.

The counter-reading, which has to be carried in the same breath

ToxCast's receptor-antagonist assays are confounded by ordinary cytotoxicity. A chemical that kills the cell also switches off the reporter, and looks exactly like a blocker. This vault records that confounder on the Tox21 map already. Whether the octocrylene and avobenzone antagonist calls survive a cytotoxicity counter-screen is not stated in the abstract, and the full text is behind a subscription.

So: the disagreement between the layers is real and documented. Which layer is right is not established here. That distinction is the whole finding — a loop facing this disagreement has no published rule telling it which side wins, and the missing rule sits precisely at the seam between the cheap layer that scales and the expensive layer that does not.
