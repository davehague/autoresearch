Automation magnifies error, and steers the agenda toward what is easy to automate

"opaque AI pipelines risk magnifying the problem unless we embed transparency from the outset." "research agendas may gradually shift from curiosity-driven exploration toward problems that are computationally convenient." — Hartung 2025

strength: suggestive — a perspective's argument, no primary data. Its value is where it comes from: the AI-and-automation side of the literature, reaching the vault's own conclusion by a different road.

(1) Amplification — the rail, corroborated from outside

shared/evidence-model.md states the vault's sharpest claim: "This is a real problem for a human reading a battery result. It is a much worse problem for an AI loop, which will trust the decision rule, take the concordant answer as confirmation, and iterate — confidently and fast — straight into a systematic error. Speed converts a known bias into a compounding one."

That was the vault's own reasoning, argued from the assay side. Hartung reaches it from the AI side: automation magnifies irreproducibility rather than averaging it out.

Two independent derivations of the same structural claim is the strongest form of support this vault recognises, and it is worth naming what makes it independent: the vault got there by looking at why two assays fail on the same chemicals; Hartung got there by looking at what happens to a scientific field's error rate when opaque pipelines scale it. Neither argument needs the other. They converge anyway.

The practical form: a closed loop does not check a battery's shared blind spot — it inherits it, then reads the resulting concordance as confirmation, then allocates more experiments toward the region it is confidently wrong about. Speed is not neutral. Speed is a multiplier on whatever bias the decision rule already carries, and it multiplies in the direction of confidence.

The counter-design is in this vault too, and it is not "more assays": it is an orthogonal measurement that fails differently — Tox21's cytotoxicity multiplex (tox21-cytotoxicity-multiplexing-shared-failure-mode).

(2) Agenda drift — the finding this vault should take personally

"research agendas may gradually shift from curiosity-driven exploration toward problems that are computationally convenient."

An active-learning loop maximises expected information gain over the space it can reach. It cannot value an experiment it cannot run. So as loop infrastructure matures, effort flows toward whatever is already automatable — and away from everything else.

Apply that to the vault's own loop-readiness table, and it is a prediction with names on it:

TierCountWhat agenda drift does to it
closed-loop-ready19Gets more attention. Already loopable; every marginal automation dollar lands here.
automatable-open-loop12Gets attention if someone writes the decision rule — a paperwork problem, cheap to fix.
human-in-the-loop3Loses ground. genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus (a human squints down a microscope), carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay, developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity.
bench-bound2Loses most. immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm (its model eats a mouse MTD), reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams (no non-animal method exists at all).

Read the bottom row. The endpoint with no non-animal method whatsoever — fertility and multigenerational toxicity, the vault's largest documented blank — is also the endpoint an automation-driven agenda has the least incentive to work on. Now add tonight's thyroid finding (thyroid-no-validated-in-vitro-method): 67 in-vitro methods, zero validated, and the regulatory answer today is still dose an animal.

The endpoints that most need a non-animal method are, structurally, the ones a self-driving lab has the least reason to build one for. Automation does not distribute its benefits evenly across a field's problems — it concentrates them where the robots already work.

That is not an argument against autonomous labs. It is an argument for who decides what they work on, and it is the strongest reason in this vault for keeping a human at the portfolio level even when the assay level is fully automated. The loop can pick the next chemical. It should not be the thing that picks the next endpoint.

Why this belongs in a toxicology vault at all

Because it converts the vault's third axis from a capability score into a strategy question. Reading loop_readiness as a scoreboard — 19 ready, 12 nearly, 5 not — invites exactly the drift Hartung describes: work the 19, ignore the 5. The 5 are where the animals still are.
