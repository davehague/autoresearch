Why this map exists

Everything else in this vault answers a hazard question asked once per chemical. Is this substance an allergen, a mutagen, an eye irritant. Ask it, answer it, file it.

Batch release is a different object. Before every manufactured lot of vaccine ships, it must be tested — for potency (does this lot actually induce the protective response it is licensed to induce), for safety, and for pyrogenicity (would injecting it cause a fever, typically from bacterial contamination). Historically several of these are animal tests. They are repeated per lot, for the commercial life of the product, in national control laboratories worldwide.

So this is the repetitive, fixed-specification, high-volume, pass/fail case — structurally the easiest kind of decision to automate, and structurally the opposite of the novel-chemical hazard call the rest of this vault studies. That the vault had 78 maps and none of them touched it is a coverage gap worth naming.

What we believe now

Almost nothing yet, and the map says so. What the founding source establishes is the shape of the obstacle, and the shape is the finding: the reported barriers are fragmented regulatory requirements, limited access to reference standards, and inconsistent validation and implementation pathways — plus the proposed responses of reliance and work-sharing (one regulator accepting another's assessment) and capacity building for national control laboratories.

No scientific or methodological barrier appears in that list. Nothing about a readout that cannot be automated, a rule nobody has written, or a method that does not work.

A reference standard is a physical, characterised material of agreed potency that every laboratory measures against, so a result means the same thing in Bangkok and Brussels. When its supply is limited, a perfectly good method is unrunnable — which is why this map carries an access_gate in the position where most maps carry a decision-rule blocker.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — because the axis cannot be answered from what was read, not because it does not apply. The deterministic ladder in shared/evidence-model.md asks three questions: is the readout instrument-readable or human-scored, does a fixed data-interpretation procedure exist, and does the method's own model consume an animal study? The source names no method, so all three are unanswerable. stages/02-ingest is explicit that an unstated value is recorded as a gap and never guessed, and that rule is being applied literally here rather than reasoned around.

What can honestly be said is a hypothesis, and is labelled as one: if replacement methods for lot release are cell-based assays read against a licensed specification, then the two things this vault normally finds missing — an objective readout and an agreed decision rule — may both already exist, because a product licence is a written specification. That would make this the vault's cleanest candidate for a genuinely loopable testing domain. Nobody has checked, this source does not check, and the vault is not asserting it.
