What we believe now (second-hand, unverified — see warning)

A microfluidic organ-interaction chip pairs precision-cut liver slices (PCLS) with 3D HK-2 kidney-cell spheroids, using plug-in biomimetic culture cups (MIMICups) and scalable flow-channel plates, built to test whether hepatic metabolism changes what the kidney receives. The reported headline: a rise in urea (an impaired-clearance marker) after exposure to triptolide (a diterpenoid natural product) appeared only in the combined liver-kidney system, not in a kidney-only chip — i.e. a kidney-only screen would reportedly have missed or under-estimated the effect. Multi-omics analysis reportedly implicated linoleic-acid metabolism and vesicle-mediated processes, with reduced cubilin and GLUT1 transport-protein levels in the kidney module. None of these specifics has been independently verified against primary text.

Why this would matter, if confirmed. nephrotoxicity-ciptec-aop-battery runs a single immortalised proximal-tubule cell line with no liver module and no systemic metabolic context — its own correlated_blind_spot already names single-cell-line limitation directly.
