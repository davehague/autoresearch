Virtual & generative control groups — study-design-reduction — method

🚩 CONVENTION DEPARTURE — flagged for human review. Auto-coined 2026-08-26. Its endpoint (study-design-reduction) is not a toxicological endpoint — it is a property of how a study is assembled, not of what harm is being measured. The vault has coined non-endpoint maps before (the regulatory-acceptance frameworks, the validation paradigm, the loop-infrastructure maps), and this is the first that is about study design itself. Status: emerging (2026-08-26). One source, one group, one retrospective benchmark, full text unread. evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, regulatory_status: none.

What we believe now

The claim. About half the animals in a repeat-dose toxicity study are there to be the denominator. You dose one group, you keep an identically handled undosed group, and everything the study concludes is treated − control. A virtual control group (VCG) deletes those animals by reusing control data from studies already completed. The field has been working on this for years, and the weakness is exactly what "concurrent" was buying: a control measured in another laboratory in another year does not cancel this laboratory's instrument drift, feed batch, animal supplier or staff.

GanCtrl proposes a different move. Instead of looking up controls, generate them — from the same study's time-matched treated animals. Whatever room, week, analyser and technicians the treated arm experienced is carried in the input, so it is carried into the generated baseline. Concurrency is inherited rather than measured. On rat repeat-dose studies across 38 clinical-pathology endpoints (the routine blood panel: liver enzymes, kidney markers, cell counts), the generated controls differed from the real concurrent controls by "smaller than intra- and inter-laboratory baseline variation", still permitted detection of drug-induced signals, and preserved endpoint correlations such as ALT–AST (the two liver enzymes that move together when the liver is injured).

⚠ The finding, and it is easy to read past. Against historical-data VCGs, the two approaches were "comparable in the single-laboratory setting", and GanCtrl "outperformed VCGs when data from multiple laboratories were combined." Single-site historical controls are the easy case and the least useful one — a laboratory with a deep single-site archive could simply run the controls. The case that would actually retire animals is the pooled multi-site archive, and that is precisely where between-laboratory variation poisons historical controls and where the generative approach is untouched, because it never leaves the study it is generating for.

What this is not, said plainly. It is Reduction, not Replacement — the paper's own opening sentence places it there. The treated animals are still dosed. There is no version of this method that runs without a live rat colony.

And what it does not touch. Clinical pathology is numbers. The other half of a repeat-dose study is histopathology — a pathologist reading tissue sections down a microscope — which this work does not address and which the vault separately records as the least automatable readout class in toxicology.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: computational-connective — rule 1 of the deterministic ladder fires: there is no physical assay. This map is model layer, scored off the ladder like in-silico-qsar-read-across and pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics.

The thing the tier cannot express, and the reason this map exists. An autonomous laboratory running on GanCtrl still has a vivarium in it, because the model's input is a live study's treated arm. The automatable part is the arithmetic that used to require a second cohort. Loop-ready is not the same as animal-free, and this is the vault's cleanest illustration of the gap. Same shape as immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm one level down — an otherwise fully quantitative method graded bench-bound: animal-input for the sole reason that its prediction model consumes a mouse study.

A second-order risk worth encoding. A real control group is not only a baseline; it is a second, independent observation of the laboratory. A dosing error, a mis-calibrated analyser or a sick colony contaminates the treated arm — and a real concurrent control, contaminated equally, cancels it. A generated control inherits the contamination through the model instead, and no independent measurement remains to reveal the discrepancy. No accuracy statistic computed against historical studies can quantify what that second observation would have caught.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Animal-input dependency (structural, the sharpest edge): immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm is the vault's existing case of a method that is quantitative, reproducible, independently validated — and never adopted, because its prediction model consumes an in-vivo mouse maximum tolerated dose. This map is the same dependency relocated from an assay's arithmetic into a model's training input. The pair is the vault's evidence that "how automatable" and "how animal-free" are different axes, and that the second one decides regulatory adoption more often than the first.
  • Composes-with (the endpoint this reduces): repeat-dose-systemic-toxicity-nam holds the vault's work on replacing the repeat-dose study outright. These two are opposite strategies on the same target and should be read together: one tries to delete the study, the other to run the study with fewer animals. Recorded as complementary, not redundant — and worth saying that reduction methods are the ones that get deployed while replacement methods are the ones that get validated.
  • Model-layer siblings (redundancy? no): in-silico-qsar-read-across and pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics are the vault's other computational-connective maps. All three are models rather than assays; none overlaps this one in function. QSAR predicts a hazard from structure, PBK translates a concentration into a dose, and this generates a baseline. The shared property worth naming is that all three are the layer a closed loop would update, and all three are graded off the assay ladder.
  • Method-input quality (cross-cutting): ml-model-reporting-reproducibility holds the vault's standing complaint that machine-learning methods in toxicology are reported without the detail needed to reproduce them. This source is a live instance — architecture, corpus and per-endpoint error are all unstated in what is publicly readable — and it is a method whose output is a substitute for a measurement, which raises the reporting bar rather than lowering it.
