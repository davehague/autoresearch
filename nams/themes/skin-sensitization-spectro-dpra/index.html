Spectro-DPRA — spectrophotometric peptide reactivity (skin sensitisation KE1 variant) — method

🚩 AUTO-COINED 2026-09-16, flagged for human review — a new child map under the skin-sensitisation endpoint, alongside skin-sensitization-dpra (the base HPLC method) and the KE2 precedent of two alternative methods holding separate maps for the same AOP slot (skin-sensitization-keratinosens vs skin-sensitization-u-sens). Bar applied: a distinct instrumentation/readout principle (spectrophotometric absorbance vs HPLC), its own formal OECD PS No. 303 validation record, and its own (partial) performance numbers — not a trivial re-run of the base assay. Whether a single 4-lab validation study clears the bar for a standalone map versus a subsection of skin-sensitization-dpra is a judgment call. Git is the undo.

Status: consolidating (2026-09-16) — evidence_tier: formally-validated (4-laboratory GLP validation with transferability, proficiency, within- and between-lab reproducibility), regulatory_status: under-validation (following the OECD's own process for a "similar or modified" DPRA-class method, PS No. 303, but has not yet cleared that process's formal similarity criteria).

What we believe now

Spectro-DPRA is a modification of the OECD TG 442C direct peptide reactivity assay (DPRA) — the in-chemico key-event-1 (covalent peptide/protein binding) screen in the skin-sensitisation battery — that reads peptide depletion spectrophotometrically rather than by HPLC. A 4-laboratory GLP validation study following the OECD's PS No. 303 process for "similar or modified" DPRA/ADRA-class methods found: proficiency testing 8/10 substances within reference range per peptide; within-lab reproducibility 83.3-100% (12 substances); between-lab reproducibility 85% (20 substances). On the formal PS No. 303 predictive-capacity comparison, Spectro-DPRA did not meet the required similarity criteria: accuracy 74.6%, sensitivity 70.9%, specificity 81.4%. On a separate 40-substance set outside that formal comparison, performance was markedly better: accuracy 85.2%, sensitivity 82.2%, specificity 97.0% — in plain terms, on that set it correctly calls about 85 of every 100 chemicals, catches about 82 of every 100 true sensitisers, and wrongly flags only about 3 of every 100 truly safe chemicals. The authors' own conclusion: the method "did not completely conform" to the OECD similarity criteria but showed "high predictive accuracy" on the additional set.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready — Spectrophotometric absorbance is instrument-readable, and the call (percentage peptide depletion against a banded threshold) is the same class of deterministic chemistry read as the base DPRA — a machine could read the plate and apply the call. This is a readout-mechanism judgment, not an endorsement of regulatory readiness: the method has not yet cleared the OECD's own formal similarity bar for "modified method" acceptance (see Status above), which is a regulatory_status fact, orthogonal to whether the readout itself is machine-executable.

  • Blocker: none, on the readout/decision-rule axis. The open item is regulatory, not technical: OECD PS No. 303 acceptance is pending.
