Next Generation Risk Assessment (NGRA) — the layer that assembles NAMs into a decision — framework map

🚩 CONVENTION DEPARTURE — auto-coined 2026-08-05, flagged for human review. Not a method map. A framework map of the integration layer that sits above every method map in this vault — the procedure by which a set of non-animal method results becomes an actual safety conclusion about an actual product. Its endpoint (risk-assessment-framework) is not a toxicological endpoint. The vault's fifth non-method map, alongside validation-paradigm-nam-confidence, automation-self-driving-labs, automation-tox21-qhts-platform and the regulatory-acceptance-* family. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-08-05). One source, and it is a critical review of nine already-published case studies covering seven chemicals — no primary data, a very small applied base, and the vault's tier ladder still has no rung for a review, which is now the nineteenth instance of that schema gap.

Sub-threads: (1) coverage — which endpoints the integration layer actually reaches, and where it stops · (2) the missing rule — that within reach, no published procedure does the integrating, and the evidence ordering would outrank its answer if one existed [reviewed 2026-08-25 @ 8 bullets]

Why this map exists

Every other map in this vault grades one method: does this assay work, has anyone accepted it, could a machine run it. None of them answers the question a company or a regulator actually asks, which is not does this assay work but at the amount a person is exposed to, is this ingredient safe enough to use.

NGRA is the field's name for answering that second question without animals. It is the assembly layer, and it had no home in this vault. Every component it uses is already mapped here; the procedure for combining them was not.

What NGRA is

From the sourcing review's own definition:

"a human-centric, exposure-led, hypothesis-based framework that integrates new approach methodologies (NAMs), including (1) in silico modeling and 2) mechanistically informed in vitro assays, and in chemico methods, to characterize potential human health hazards under realistic exposure conditions."

Three design commitments sit in that sentence:

  • Human-centric — the reference organism is a person, not a rodent. There is no animal study to be concordant with, which quietly removes the comparator every method in this vault is scored against.
  • Exposure-led — you start from how much of the chemical reaches the body and work backward to which tests are even needed, rather than testing first and interpreting afterwards.
  • Hypothesis-based — each assay answers a stated question rather than filling a box on a checklist.

What we believe now

It works, on a small number of published cases, and it stops at the skin.

The reviewed case studies produced usable safe-use concentrations with margins of safety above regulatory thresholds. And:

"There are currently no apparent validated NGRA frameworks for systemic toxicity endpoints where significant gaps remain for complex toxicological endpoints, even though defined approaches for skin sensitization and eye irritation have gained regulatory acceptance."

Systemic toxicity — harm after the chemical is absorbed and distributed, as opposed to damage where it touches — is the half of toxicology where animals are still used. On this evidence, the integration layer for it does not exist in validated form.

And as of 2026-08-25 the map holds a second, harder claim about the same layer, made by regulators rather than by method developers: even where an integration procedure could be written, the surrounding evidence ordering would not let its answer win. Eight European risk-assessment scientists describe regulatory practice as ranking human data above animal data above in-vitro above in-chemico/omics above in-silico/read-across, with that ordering "deeply embedded in legislation and guidance (e.g. REACH)", and name the result a paradox: "NAMs are expected to replace animal testing, yet they are positioned in a way that prevents them from doing so." Their three unanswered questions — when NAM data can resolve conflicting evidence, when it alone suffices, and how uncertainty is compared across data types — are effectively the specification for the rule this map exists to track. What fills the gap today is stated plainly: "In practice, such questions are often resolved through expert judgement, introducing variability and reducing reproducibility."

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — a framework, not an assay and not a model, scored off the ladder.

The automation-relevant fact is not a tier, it is a boundary, and it is worth stating precisely because it is the same boundary the loop-readiness axis finds from the other side:

  • Where the assembly procedure is published and adopted (skin sensitisation's 2-out-of-3 rule; eye irritation's defined approaches), the underlying assays are also the ones this vault grades closed-loop-ready. A machine could run the assays and the assembly.
  • Where the assembly procedure does not exist (everything systemic), the underlying assays are also the ones stuck at automatable-open-loop or below.

These two facts are related but not the same fact, and the vault must not collapse them. An adopted assembly rule is a statement about acceptance; a fixed data-interpretation procedure is a statement about automatability. They coincide here because local, fast endpoints are both easy to reproduce in a dish and easy to write a rule about — one cause, two effects, and neither is evidence for the other.

  • 2026-08-18 — ⚠️ THE INTEGRATION LAYER HAS THE VAULT'S MOST-REPEATED BLOCKER, ONE FLOOR UP. Status HELD at emerging; loop_readiness HELD at not-applicable; no method map moves. Ingested rusyn-2026-ngra-cosmetics-wide-adoption (Current Opinion in Toxicology 47:100601, DOI 10.1016/j.cotox.2026.100601, PMID 42549259, PubMed Central PMC13431049, read in full). The workflow is three steps and all three are arithmetic: "(i) establishes points of departure (PoDs) from NAMs assay-derived phenotypic and transcriptomic data using approaches that incorporate Bayesian uncertainty modeling, (ii) derives estimates of internal exposure levels by using physiologically-based kinetic (PBK) models, and then (iii) calculates the Bioactivity-Exposure Ratio (BER)." Curve-fit, simulate, divide. A loop could run all three today on any ingredient with a PBK model. What it could not do is conclude: the four published case studies report margins of >100-fold (coumarin), 27-fold (caffeine, via a 10-step read-across), 16-to-338-fold (benzyl salicylate) and a qualitative pass (benzophenone-4) — and no number anywhere says which margin is acceptable. The only stated criterion is a scope condition, not a threshold: "a protective safety assessment conclusion can only be determined with high confidence where the intended use case for the chemical being assessed does not require eliciting systemic effects." Twenty-odd method maps in this vault are stuck at automatable-open-loop for want of exactly this shape; the layer above them has it too. One genuine improvement worth naming: the output is a continuous ratio, and an optimiser can rank on a ratio where it cannot rank on a hazard class. → ngra-produces-a-margin-and-no-rule-says-which-margin-passes

  • 2026-08-18 — ⚠️ AND TWO HAND-SET CHOICES SIT INSIDE THE ARITHMETIC, EITHER OF WHICH CHANGES THE ANSWER. First, which dose metric you divide by: "doxorubicin cardiotoxicity case study ... Concluded AUC is better indicator than Cmax for this endpoint" — peak blood concentration and total exposure over time are different numbers, chosen per endpoint by people on mechanistic grounds, with nothing in the framework selecting between them. Second, which molecule you simulate: "Phenoxyethanol case study revealed the importance of considering metabolites, because a major stable metabolite introduced key uncertainties," and in the benzyl salicylate case the metabolite was the toxicological driver, not the parent. And read the 16-to-338 range as what it is: not error bars, but a twenty-one-fold spread of answers depending on which assay and which dose metric the assessor took.

  • ⚠️ THE VAULT'S DOMINANT BLOCKER HAS A WORKAROUND ALREADY IN COMMERCIAL USE, AND NOBODY HERE HAD LOOKED AT IT. Status HELD at emerging; no method's tier moves. Ingested huang-costin-2026-benchmark-materials-cosmetic-nams (NAM Journal 100111, DOI 10.1016/j.namjnl.2026.100111, PMID 42519109, PubMed Central PMC13382591, peer reviewed, diamond open access CC BY, 7 July 2026). More than twenty maps in this vault stall at automatable-open-loop for one reason: the assay returns a clean machine-readable number and no published rule states what number means hazardous. The vault has been treating that as a research gap awaiting a committee. The cosmetics and personal-care industry did not wait — it replaced the absolute question with a relative one. Run the candidate formulation and a benchmark material — a product already on the market with a known safety and consumer-complaint history — side by side in the same assay on the same day, and ask whether the candidate ranks no worse. The review's own framing: benchmarks "become of particular interest when safety testing is based on New Approach Methodologies ... that allow for toxicity ranking of the prototypes and provide mechanistic insights without having an established prediction model." The missing decision rule is not treated as a blocker; it is treated as the condition under which benchmarking becomes the method. → benchmarking-replaces-the-prediction-model-with-a-comparison
  • ⚠️ AND THE ESCAPE HATCH DOES NOT CLOSE THE LOOP, FOR A REASON WORTH STATING PRECISELY. A side-by-side comparison is fully machine-executable — measure A, measure B, compare — and needs no threshold, no committee and no reference database. What is not machine-generable is the comparator's authority, which comes from the criterion sitting in the middle of the authors' own selection list: extensive market experience data. The benchmark product counts as an acceptable answer because large numbers of people have used it and the complaints did not come — an epidemiological fact accrued over years, held partly in proprietary complaint databases. Benchmarking converts an interpretation problem into a provenance problem. That is a real trade and arguably a good one (human exposure is better evidence than expert consensus), but a loop treating a benchmark comparison as self-contained has relocated its ground truth from the bench into a company's records.
