Next Generation Risk Assessment (NGRA) — the layer that assembles NAMs into a decision — framework map

A framework map of the integration layer that sits above every method map in this vault — the procedure by which a set of non-animal method results becomes an actual safety conclusion about an actual product. Its endpoint (risk-assessment-framework) is not a toxicological endpoint. Why this map exists

Every other map in this vault grades one method: does this assay work, has anyone accepted it, could a machine run it. None of them answers the question a company or a regulator actually asks, which is not does this assay work but at the amount a person is exposed to, is this ingredient safe enough to use.

NGRA is the field's name for answering that second question without animals. It is the assembly layer, and it had no home in this vault. Every component it uses is already mapped here; the procedure for combining them was not.

What NGRA is

From the sourcing review's own definition:

"a human-centric, exposure-led, hypothesis-based framework that integrates new approach methodologies (NAMs), including (1) in silico modeling and 2) mechanistically informed in vitro assays, and in chemico methods, to characterize potential human health hazards under realistic exposure conditions."

Three design commitments sit in that sentence:

  • Human-centric — the reference organism is a person, not a rodent. There is no animal study to be concordant with, which quietly removes the comparator every method in this vault is scored against.
  • Exposure-led — you start from how much of the chemical reaches the body and work backward to which tests are even needed, rather than testing first and interpreting afterwards.
  • Hypothesis-based — each assay answers a stated question rather than filling a box on a checklist.

What we believe now

It works, on a small number of published cases, and it stops at the skin.

The reviewed case studies produced usable safe-use concentrations with margins of safety above regulatory thresholds. And:

"There are currently no apparent validated NGRA frameworks for systemic toxicity endpoints where significant gaps remain for complex toxicological endpoints, even though defined approaches for skin sensitization and eye irritation have gained regulatory acceptance."

Systemic toxicity — harm after the chemical is absorbed and distributed, as opposed to damage where it touches — is the half of toxicology where animals are still used. On this evidence, the integration layer for it does not exist in validated form.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — a framework, not an assay and not a model, scored off the ladder.

The automation-relevant fact is not a tier, it is a boundary, and it is worth stating precisely because it is the same boundary the loop-readiness axis finds from the other side:

  • Where the assembly procedure is published and adopted (skin sensitisation's 2-out-of-3 rule; eye irritation's defined approaches), the underlying assays are also the ones this vault grades closed-loop-ready. A machine could run the assays and the assembly.
  • Where the assembly procedure does not exist (everything systemic), the underlying assays are also the ones stuck at automatable-open-loop or below.

These two facts are related but not the same fact, and the vault must not collapse them. An adopted assembly rule is a statement about acceptance; a fixed data-interpretation procedure is a statement about automatability. They coincide here because local, fast endpoints are both easy to reproduce in a dish and easy to write a rule about — one cause, two effects, and neither is evidence for the other.

A narrative review — the vault's floor rung again, and the twentieth logged instance of the missing review tier. (1) The two headline numbers, and how to read them. Across 448 chemicals with EPA ToxCast screening data, 89% had a non-animal point of departure lower than the animal-derived one; across 5,801 chemicals on Canada's Domestic Substances List, 95.20% had protective non-animal values. Both are cited through this review from Paul Friedman et al. (2020) and Beal (2021), unread here. This is the field's strongest empirical case and it is a one-sided criterion: it bounds the direction of the error and places no bound on its size. A method wrong by 17,000-fold in the protective direction scores identically to one wrong by 5% — and this vault holds exactly that distribution from an independent 2026 analysis (ivive-reproduces-the-animal-answer-for-liver-and-not-for-development). Recorded because a loop optimising a protectiveness objective converges on conservatism, which is free under that objective and useless under any other. → protective-is-not-the-same-as-accurate (2) Two of the review's three barriers are engineering, not science. Assay results are reported against nominal concentration — what was pipetted, not what reached the cell — and protocols, data formats and analysis pipelines are unharmonised across platforms. Neither blocks a loop from running; both block one from being built, and unlike the scientific barrier they have known fixes. Ties directly to nam-data-processing-pipeline and ml-model-reporting-reproducibility. → the-harmonisation-barrier-is-the-loop-s-input-problem (3) Corroborates the boundary this map already carries, from a fourth direction. Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Sits ABOVE, not beside, the method maps. This is the first map in the vault whose relationship to the others is vertical. Its components are pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics, in-silico-qsar-read-across, quantitative-aop-probabilistic-modelling, skin-sensitization-2o3-defined-approach and the IATA hubs — it consumes their outputs rather than competing with them, and no method's Status or loop_readiness is affected by anything on this map.
  • Distinct from validation-paradigm-nam-confidence, and the distinction matters. That map asks what does it take to trust a method. This one asks how do you combine trusted methods into an answer. A method can be fully validated and still have no framework that uses it, which is precisely the reported state of the systemic endpoints.
  • Distinct from the regulatory-acceptance-* family. Those track what a jurisdiction will accept. NGRA is the technical procedure a submitter would run; acceptance of it is a separate fact, and the review reports acceptance only for the skin-sensitisation and eye-irritation defined approaches.
  • Composes-with repeat-dose-systemic-toxicity-nam — and this is the sharpest edge. That map is the vault's attempt at a NAM framework for exactly the endpoint class this review says has no validated integration framework. Its own loop_blocker already records hand-tuned concern cutoffs and an expert-judgement dependence the framework's authors name as an open gap. Two sources, two literatures, the same verdict about the same endpoint class. No status moves on either — this is corroboration of a negative, which is what the vault should expect a lot of here.
  • Global-ripple: walked every map. Refutes nothing, duplicates nothing, moves no Status and no loop tier. The claim is about a layer none of the other maps occupy.
  • Blind-spot check: no new correlated blind spot. NGRA is not a battery member; it is the thing that reads batteries. But it inherits every blind spot underneath it — an NGRA assembled from skin-sensitisation assays that all miss pro-haptens, or from aquatic assays that all lack metabolism, reports one confident answer built from correlated inputs. That inheritance is the shape a human should look at first if this map ever grows, and it is recorded here rather than as a correlated_blind_spot field, which takes sibling method slugs and has no way to express "inherits from everything below."
