What we believe now

  • 2026-08-30 — MAP OPENED. istand-accepts-first-ai-driven-drug-development-tool. FDA's ISTAND (Innovative Science and Technology Approaches for New Drugs) program — a pilot pathway for qualifying novel drug-development tools that don't fit any of the agency's other established qualification tracks — accepted a Letter of Intent from Absentia Labs for its "Digital Liver Model", an AI system predicting drug-induced liver injury (DILI) risk from a candidate drug's chemical structure. FDA's own announcement calls this the first AI-driven Drug Development Tool ever accepted into ISTAND, and the first in silico DDT accepted under the program for a DILI context of use. What this is not: a validated tool, a completed qualification, or evidence the model works. ISTAND runs three stages — Letter of Intent → Qualification Plan → full qualification — and Absentia is at stage one. No architecture, training data, or accuracy figure has been published; the one independent signal available is that Absentia placed in FDA's own precisionFDA Hepatotoxicity Predictive Modeling Challenge, which is a competition placement, not a peer-reviewed validation.

    Why this earns a new map rather than folding into regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework or regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility: those two track, respectively, a general permission for fit-for-purpose NAM submissions and a set of principles an AI model should satisfy. Neither describes a specific model moving through a pathway that would make it reusable evidence for other sponsors — closer in kind to how an OECD Test Guideline lets any lab invoke TG 442E without re-proving h-CLAT works, except for a computational tool. That reusability-by-qualification shape did not exist anywhere else in the vault's regulatory-acceptance group before tonight.

    Composes with, does not duplicate: hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps's own 2026-07-28 bullet already records a separate ISTAND Letter of Intent (accepted January 2026) for a liver microphysiological-system (organ-chip) platform evaluated by the 3Rs Collaborative / FDA-CDER partnership. That is a different applicant, a different technology class (physical organ-chip vs. pure in silico model), and a different LOI date — two independent DILI-prediction tools are now inside the same qualification pipeline, pursuing the same endpoint by opposite means (wet tissue vs. no tissue at all). Neither map's Status or loop_readiness moves from this cross-link; it is recorded because a reader assessing "is DILI prediction on track for regulatory reuse" needs both data points, not one.
