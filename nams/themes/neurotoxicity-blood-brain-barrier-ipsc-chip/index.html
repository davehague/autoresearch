What we believe now

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) is the vascular interface that decides whether a chemical in the blood ever reaches brain tissue at all — a prerequisite question for any central-nervous-system toxicity, sitting upstream of every other method in the neurotoxicity-cns endpoint group. neurotoxicity-seizure-liability-in-vitro-battery already names the absence of exactly this check as one half of its own correlated_blind_spot: "Neither has a blood-brain barrier, so a compound that could never reach the brain is flagged by both [legs]." This map is the vault's first method that could fill that specific gap.

Founding source: kurtenbach-2026-static-microfluidic-bbb-pilot (Kurtenbach, Hahn, Thevis & Lamshöft 2026, NAM Journal). A Bayer AG team's static, human-induced-pluripotent-stem-cell (hiPSC)-derived BBB model — already validated against human clinical PET brain-penetration scans in prior work (Kurtenbach et al. 2025, 2026) — was transferred onto a commercially available microfluidic chip platform, to test whether physiological flow adds measurable value for human brain-exposure prediction.

The result runs against the field's general expectation that more physiological complexity helps. The microfluidic format preserved basic tissue function — stable glucose/lactate metabolism, no overt cytotoxicity, measurable barrier integrity and efflux-transporter activity (verapamil ± cyclosporin A) — but showed reduced in-vivo-like barrier characteristics and lower brain-penetration predictive performance than the static format, for the three benchmark compounds tested (loperamide: low permeability; flumazenil: high permeability; verapamil: efflux substrate) against the same PET-derived concentration/exposure-time conditions used in the original static validation. A metabolic signature accompanies the result: microfluidic cells shifted toward a lower lactate-to-glucose ratio (≈1.5), consistent with a more oxidative metabolic state under flow-induced shear — a genuinely different cell physiology, not simply the validated model with flow added. The authors' own framing favours the simpler format: static culture offers standardisation, robustness and throughput "important for future regulatory implementation."

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop. Readout is fully instrument-read: TEER (electrical resistance across the tissue), sodium-fluorescein and compound permeability (plate-reader / quantitative sampling), and an efflux ratio computed from two permeability measurements. No step is scored by a trained human eye. But no fixed, published decision procedure exists to turn any of these numbers into a pass/fail brain-exposure or neurotoxicity-hazard call — the founding source is a platform-comparison pilot against continuous PET benchmarks for three compounds, not a validated classification method. A loop could run every measurement in this method today; it could not yet decide anything from the result.

access_gate: all three cell types — brain microvascular endothelial cells, astrocytes, pericytes — come from one commercial supplier (Fujifilm Cellular Dynamics Inc.) and one proprietary donor. Standing this method up elsewhere depends on that single vendor relationship continuing.

The finding most relevant to automating this method is a warning, not a path forward. The static→ microfluidic transfer is the kind of "let's automate it on a chip" move a loop-building effort would naturally reach for, and here it measurably degraded the one performance dimension that matters (brain-penetration prediction) while preserving every dimension that looks fine on a dashboard (homeostasis, no cytotoxicity, measurable barrier). A loop optimising for "the assay still runs" would not have caught this; only comparison against the external PET benchmark did.
