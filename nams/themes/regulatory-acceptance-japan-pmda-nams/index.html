This map holds the seventh shape: the receipt.

Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) — the national regulator that reviews medicines, devices and the intermediate quasi-drug tier — went into its own approval files and counted how often applicants actually submitted a non-animal method.

The category is medicated cosmetics: quasi-drugs sitting between a plain cosmetic and a drug, carrying an active claim (a sunscreen with a declared active, an anti-dandruff shampoo, a whitening lotion) and therefore requiring a safety submission. That makes it the favourable case by construction — skin irritation, eye irritation and skin sensitisation all have adopted OECD Test Guidelines and have had for years. If non-animal methods are being used anywhere, they are being used here.

The number

Across approvals from 2020 to 2024:

Non-animal methods were used in 22 of 115 tests, and in 10 of 25 products.

  • By test: about one in five. Four in five safety tests in these submissions were still done in animals.
  • By product: 40% — higher only because a product counts as a user if it used a non-animal method anywhere. Those products used one for an endpoint or two and animals for the rest.

Uptake was concentrated "in tests with established regulatory standards, particularly ocular irritation assessments" — the endpoint for which this vault holds nine adopted in-vitro Test Guidelines, five of them mapped, several graded closed-loop-ready. → an-acceptance-gate-that-counts-what-was-actually-used

⚠ The pattern, and the hypothesis it suggests

Adoption tracks the existence of a written, adopted, published data-interpretation rule almost exactly, and does not extend past it. Where a guideline says what to run and how to read the number, applicants run it. Where no guideline exists — even where a scientifically strong non-animal method does — applicants default to the animal.

That is the same variable this vault's third axis turns on. The property separating closed-loop-ready from automatable-open-loop is whether somebody published a fixed data-interpretation procedure. It now looks like the property separating used from not used as well — which would mean the missing decision rule is both the loop's blocker and the field's adoption blocker, and the same paragraph fixes both.

Recorded as a hypothesis this source is consistent with, not a demonstrated cause. One country, one product category, denominators in the low hundreds.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — an acceptance gate, not a method. Scored off the ladder.

Its consequence for the loop is indirect and uncomfortable. Every other acceptance source in this vault reads as forward motion — agencies announcing they will accept more, lists growing, roadmaps published. This one measures the same field at the point of use and returns one in five. Nothing demotes: a method being adopted and a method being used are separate facts, and this vault does not infer one from the other any more than it infers loop-readiness from either.

But it prices a distance the vault could previously only gesture at. An autonomous laboratory that can run an adopted, loop-ready, plate-reader assay end to end is, on this evidence, automating the minority case. The infrastructure would be pointed at the traffic that is already moving.
