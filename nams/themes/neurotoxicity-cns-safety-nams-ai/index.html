What we believe now

The endpoint. Harm to the brain and spinal cord in a developed organism — seizures, sedation, tremor, sensory changes, cognitive effects. It is a leading cause of drug attrition (candidates abandoned during development) and is unusually hard to detect early, because most of what matters is a behaviour or a sensation rather than a lesion you can stain for. That is a different problem from developmental neurotoxicity, which asks whether a chemical disrupts a nervous system while it is being assembled — see developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb. Different biology, different tests, different blockers.

What this source describes. A joint review by a CNS drug company, a contract research organisation, the US alternative-methods evaluation body (NICEATM) and the US drug regulator (FDA CDER), setting out how the field should improve. Its components:

  • Electroencephalography (EEG) — electrodes reading the brain's electrical activity — to detect and characterise seizure liability, i.e. whether a drug makes seizures more likely.
  • Advanced sensor technologies with artificial intelligence for "continuous, non-invasive monitoring of animal behavior", explicitly to "reduce reliance on episodic observer-dependent measurements" — a technician looking into a cage at intervals and writing down what they see.
  • An Integrated Approach to Testing and Assessment (IATA) combining "mechanistic data, traditional toxicology findings, and exposure modeling" to support regulatory decision-making "while aligning with the 3Rs principles."
  • Regulatory case studies in drug development.

⚠ Read what that adds up to, because the title does not say it. The authors' own closing formulation is "integrating refined in vivo methods with New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) and digital technologies." The 3Rs are Replace, Reduce, Refine; a NAM is specifically a Replace technology; and the AI here is instrumentation for the animal study. It makes that study continuous, objective and better-sampled. That is real and valuable work, and it is the refine branch. On the endpoint where AI is being applied hardest in this vault's sweep, the automation investment is going into the arm the vault exists to find alternatives to. See the-ai-on-this-endpoint-was-pointed-at-the-animal-not-at-its-replacement.

The honest summary. A picture of current practice from people who do it, with no numbers in it. It does not demonstrate that adult CNS toxicity cannot be assessed without animals — it does not test that — and this map does not record such a claim. What it records is that the strategy currently offered, by this group, consumes one.

⚠️ Bias watch — said plainly, both halves

Four of nine authors including the corresponding author are at Neurocrine Biosciences Inc, a company developing CNS drugs to which this strategy would be applied. One is at Charles River Laboratories, a contract research organisation whose business is running the preclinical animal studies the paper recommends refining rather than replacing. One is at a pathology consultancy. funding_flag: [industry].

The counterweight is real and belongs in the same breath: two authors are at NICEATM, the US body that evaluates alternative toxicological methods, and one is at FDA CDER, the US drug regulator. This is the same composition the vault recorded for the thyroid microtissue validation on 2026-07-18 — interested parties inside a regulator-adjacent exercise, with the regulator in the room. Both halves are true; neither cancels the other; and the vault records both rather than choosing.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: bench-bound, bench_bound_reason: animal-input — the vault's third map at this tier, after CFU-GM myelotoxicity (animal-input) and reproductive fertility (no-nam-exists).

The ladder is applied in order and first match wins. Rung 2: does the method's own prediction model consume an in-vivo study? The IATA names "traditional toxicology findings" as one of three inputs. It does. Bottom rung, before any question about readouts is reached.

And the uncomfortable part is what that hides: nearly every component here is automated.

ComponentMachine-readable?
ElectroencephalographyYes — continuous electrical recording, no human scorer
Sensor-based behavioural monitoring with AIYes — explicitly replaces episodic human observation
Exposure modellingYes — software end to end
Mechanistic in-vitro dataYes — plate-reader readouts, in this vault's experience

So the ladder returns the bottom rung for an assessment built almost entirely out of top-rung parts, and that is the grading working correctly. It is the sharpest available demonstration of why "percentage of steps automated" is a useless metric for this vault's question. See an-integration-that-consumes-an-animal-study-is-bench-bound-at-the-top.

Two distinct blockers, at two heights, and only the lower one sets the tier. Remove the animal input and the strategy would still be blocked at the top by an expert weight-of-evidence integration with no published scoring algorithm — identical to eye-hazard-iata-gd263, where all nine composed methods produce clean instrument numbers, several are individually closed-loop-ready, and the combining step is unwritten. Machine vision, the escape hatch for assays scored by eye, does not reach that blocker.

animal-input and no-nam-exists are kept apart deliberately. This map is the first: somebody would have to invent a different prediction model. The fertility map is the second: somebody would have to invent an assay. For anyone prioritising where to spend, those are entirely different investments.
