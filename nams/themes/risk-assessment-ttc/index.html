What we believe now

A threshold of toxicological concern is a generic exposure level, per structural chemical class, below which a substance is treated as posing no appreciable concern without being tested. It is built by taking a large historical database of chemicals that have been tested, sorting them into a few classes by how reactive their chemistry looks, and reading off the low end of each class's no-effect-dose distribution.

Why this matters more than its size suggests. Twenty-four maps in this vault record the same blocker: no agreed procedure exists for turning the number a non-animal test produces into the decision it is supposed to inform. Recorded that many times, it reads as a claim about the field. TTC shows it is a claim about one branch of the field.

  • The "do not test" branch has agreed numeric rules and has had them for decades. TTC is used by the European Food Safety Authority, by the US FDA for food-contact substances, and by the cosmetics industry inside Next Generation Risk Assessment.
  • The "here is what the test result means" branch does not.

The asymmetry has a cause worth stating: a "do not test" threshold only has to be conservative, and being wrong costs an unnecessary experiment. An interpretation rule has to be right in both directions, against a reference standard this vault repeatedly documents as noisy, committee-built, or missing. → toxicology-does-have-an-agreed-numeric-rule-and-it-is-for-not-testing

The internal version, and what it needs

Classical TTC is expressed as micrograms per person per day swallowed — an external dose, because the historical database was built from oral studies. That breaks for a cosmetic absorbed through skin or a chemical inhaled, since the fraction reaching the bloodstream differs enormously by route.

An internal TTC (iTTC) would be stated as a concentration in blood or tissue, making it route-agnostic. Building it means converting historical oral no-effect doses into internal concentrations, which is a physiologically based pharmacokinetic model — and that model's dominant input is how fast the liver destroys the chemical. That is the girder this vault's one source supplies, for 207 substances. Composes-with pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics, which holds the modelling layer itself.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: computational-connective — no physical assay, so the deterministic ladder's first rung applies and the map is scored off the assay ladder, alongside in-silico-qsar-read-across and pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics.

⚠ And this is the most loop-relevant object in the vault, which is why the tier understates it. For a closed loop, TTC is not a hazard measurement — it is a gate on whether to run an experiment at all. Every autonomous-laboratory result in automation-self-driving-labs needs an acquisition rule for choosing the next experiment, and the recurring finding is that the inputs such rules need (a quantified prior, a quantified decision consequence) do not exist in computable form in regulatory toxicology. TTC is a counter-example: a published, numeric, machine-checkable rule for declining to test. A loop can apply it today, inside the domain gate.

Two standing qualifications, both recorded rather than encoded as blockers:

  1. The rule's own inputs are animal studies. Thresholds are summarised from historical in-vivo repeat-dose data. The measurement feeding the internal version is animal-free; the threshold is not. A loop can apply the rule and cannot derive it for a new structural class from non-animal evidence — the animal-input shape the vault records for immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm, appearing at the level of a decision rule.
  2. No internal threshold value exists yet. The source's own tense is future. Until a number is published, the internal version has nothing for a loop to encode.
