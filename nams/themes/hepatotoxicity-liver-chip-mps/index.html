Human Liver-Chip / MPS — hepatotoxicity (DILI) — method

Status: emerging (2026-07-06) — split axes, bias-flagged. evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method (one sizeable blinded study) but regulatory_status: under-validation (FDA ISTAND pilot, not adopted), and the key validation is developer-run (funding_flag: industry / method-developer). The vault's first entry for the hepatotoxicity endpoint, previously a headline coverage gap.

Sub-threads: (1) the injury call — does this platform predict drug-induced liver injury well enough, on whose evidence, and against what cheaper comparator · (2) the quantitative-pharmacology use — clearance, drug–drug interaction, and what concentration the cell actually receives in a perfused system [reviewed 2026-08-20 @ 10 bullets]

First declaration, made at the threshold. The map crossed 10 dated bullets tonight and had never carried one. These are two different regulatory questions asked of the same device, and the source ingested tonight is the reason the split is worth naming: a regulator's unit of judgment is the context of use, and "a model intended to rank relative DILI risk requires a different validation strategy from one intended to estimate hepatic clearance." Strand (1) holds the predictivity claim, its developer-provenance problem, the structure-only comparator, the workflow automation, and the missing decision rule. Strand (2) holds the dose domain_gate and the drug-interaction work — evidence about how much, not whether, and validated against a different answer key. Two strands, so not fork-due; both are still arguments about one device and neither yet carries enough of its own evidence to stand as a map.

What we believe now

The human Liver-Chip is a perfused microphysiological system (organ-on-chip) co-culturing primary human hepatocytes with non-parenchymal cells to reconstitute liver metabolism and microenvironment. In the largest single reported Organ-Chip evaluation to date (Ewart et al. 2022, PMID 36473994), it reached 87% sensitivity and 100% specificity for drug-induced liver injury (DILI) on a blinded 27-drug panel (870 chips, 3 donors). The eye-catching specificity is real but the panel is curated and the study was run by the platform's developer (Emulate) — a developer validation, not an independent ring trial. Kept emerging: promising, single-study, developer-led, regulatorily unadopted.

  • 2026-07-10 — An adopted human-liver TG arrives — for a different question. Status holds at emerging. metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg (OECD TG 445A) is the first adopted OECD in-vitro method using a human hepatic model — the HepaRG™ line this map already named among the hepatic systems to watch.   • 2026-08-15 — A domain_gate is SET on a variable the whole field treats as known: the dose. Status HELD at emerging; loop_readiness HELD at automatable-open-loop. Ingested hartauer-2026-rifampicin-liver-chip-induction (Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics, DOI 10.1002/cpt.70418; UNC Eshelman School of Pharmacy + Javelin Biotech, five of ten authors employed by the chip's vendor) ran a perfused human liver tissue chip for seven days on three donors with 10 μM rifampicin and measured the intracellular drug concentration alongside the induction endpoints. It "differed substantially" from the concentration applied to the medium. → the-dose-the-loop-controls-is-not-the-dose-the-cell-receives (1) Why this earns a modifier rather than a bullet. A closed loop's control variable is concentration: the model picks a dose, the robot delivers it, and everything the loop learns about potency is a function of that number. If the map from nominal to actual is compound-specific and time-varying, the loop is learning a composition of two functions and crediting all of it to the first. On one compound that is a calibration offset; across a structurally diverse campaign it distorts the potency ranking, which is precisely what an active-learning loop optimises against when choosing where to sample next. (2) The coupling is the sharp part. Rifampicin induces the very transporters that govern its own intracellular concentration (this map's sibling finding on metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg). Exposure is not merely uncertain — it is a feedback term of the response being measured. A loop assuming dose is exogenous has assumed the feedback away. (3) The counterweight the chip is owed. The only reason anyone knows this is that the group measured it. A static 96-well plate has the same problem in unknown size and does not look. Reporting an inconvenient measurement is the behaviour the bias watch is meant to reward — recorded alongside, not instead of, the funding_flag: [industry, method-developer]. (4) What is unread and is not being filled in. The abstract gives no ratio, no direction and no donor spread. Whether intracellular exposure ran higher than nominal (accumulation) or lower (efflux and binding) changes what a loop should do about it, and the vault does not know which. Subscription publisher, full text unread.

  • 2026-08-17 — The chip beats both standard prediction tools on the hardest drug-interaction case, and the margin is behind a paywall. Status HELD at emerging; loop_readiness HELD at automatable-open-loop; domain_gate unchanged and now pointed straight at this study. mettler-2026-liver-chip-cyp3a4-ddi-net-effect (Drug Metabolism and Disposition 100359, PMID 42537548, DOI 10.1016/j.dmd.2026.100359 — eight of ten authors Novartis, a ninth a university microphysiological-systems head; funding_flag: [industry, method-developer]) ran six drugs that both block and induce CYP3A4, the liver's workhorse drug-metabolising enzyme, through a perfused liver-chip and measured midazolam clearance as the probe. (1) The problem is genuinely hard and the result is genuinely on it. Blocking makes a second drug accumulate; induction makes it vanish; the net effect can point either way and cannot be read off either mechanism alone. Against clinical reference values, "liver-chip predictions showed closer alignment and higher accuracy" than both a mechanistic static model and full physiologically based simulation. Enzyme activity held for three days and stayed inducible by rifampicin. (2) The mechanistic detail a screening loop would get wrong. Most precipitants raised CYP3A4 messenger RNA — the cheap, scalable, gene-expression signal that says "induction" — while the functional net effect came out as induction for some drugs and inhibition for others. The readout that automates and the readout that decides point different ways on part of this set. (3) The abstract's own counterweight, which is why it is credited. "Although constant exposures offered the strongest quantitative performance, the ability to apply dynamic PK profiles represents a distinctive feature of this platform." The flat condition predicted better than the platform's selling point, and they put that in the abstract. (4) Nothing moves, for three reasons. No effect size of any kind is available — paywalled, confirmed oa_status: closed, no free deposit, body unread. "Closer than the comparator" is a comparison, not a threshold, so the map's loop_blocker (multi-parametric readout, no fixed data-interpretation procedure) is untouched. And a company evaluating a platform for its own internal decisions is not an independent validation. → the-chip-beat-the-static-model-and-the-margin-is-unread (5) ⚠ The unread fact that matters most is this map's own domain_gate. Set 2026-08-15: in a perfused chip the nominal concentration is not what the cell receives, and rifampicin induces the very transporters governing its own intracellular level. This study perfuses, shapes concentration over time deliberately, and uses rifampicin as its induction control. Whether it measured intracellular exposure or worked from nominal concentrations is exactly what the abstract does not say — logged as a gap, not estimated. (6) Global-ripple: walked every map. Confirms-and-extends metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg (the adopted CYP-induction Test Guideline reads induction alone; this reads the net of induction against inhibition, which no adopted method does) and edges to pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics (the reference values against which the chip was scored were themselves generated by physiologically based modelling — the chip was benchmarked against a model, not against patients directly).   • 2026-08-20 — AN INDEPENDENT ACADEMIC REVIEW REACHES THIS MAP'S OWN SPLIT-AXES READING, AND SUPPLIES THE COMPARATIVE FRAME THE VAULT LACKED. Status HELD at emerging; loop_readiness HELD at automatable-open-loop; evidence_tier and regulatory_status unchanged. Ingested byeon-2026-mps-contexts-of-use-readiness (International Journal of Stem Cells, DOI 10.15283/ijsc26088, PMID 42618331, published online 20 August 2026 — the day of ingest; open access CC BY-NC, full text read). Three academic bioengineers at Gachon University, funded by the National Research Foundation of Korea, no competing interests declared and no stake in any of the platforms discussed; funding_flag: [none]. It is a review, so every performance number in it is second-hand and the vault grades it strength: suggestive — the vault's evidence_tier ladder still has no rung for a review, the schema gap logged since 2026-07-12.

    (1) The headline, and it is counterintuitive. Grading seven organ-chip categories for regulatory readiness, the review finds that readiness "depends less on biological complexity than on the evidence supporting a defined context of use" — and states outright that "a more biologically complex model is not necessarily more ready for regulatory use." Liver leads; multi-organ and tumour, the most physiologically elaborate systems in the field, are graded early-stage. What liver has and the others do not is four evidence properties — a named regulatory question, reference-compound testing, quantitative performance metrics, sustained regulatory engagement — and not one of them is a property of the tissue. Kidney, gastrointestinal absorption and blood–brain-barrier work are developing; lung, tumour and multi-organ are early-stage. The authors label these comparative descriptions, explicitly not regulatory categories. → organ-chip-readiness-tracks-the-evidence-not-the-biology

    (2) It carries this map's own foundational number, and a second one this map did not have. The blinded 870-chip / 27-drug study at 87% sensitivity with no false positives among the non-hepatotoxic drugs tested is ewart-2022-liver-chip-dili-performance, which this map has recorded since 2026-07-06 as developer-run; the review reproduces it without that qualifier and this map keeps it. New here: a separate FDA / National Center for Toxicological Research evaluation of InSphero liver microtissues at 80% sensitivity and 89% specificity for drugs carrying withdrawals or boxed warnings — catching about 80 of every 100 problem drugs and wrongly flagging about 11 of every 100 clean ones. Different platform, and not developer-run. Lower sensitivity and a stated specificity rather than a perfect one on a curated panel: that is what an independent second look usually does to a headline, and it is the closest thing this endpoint has to an outside check.

    (3) The regulatory status, said precisely. FDA accepted a Letter of Intent into ISTAND — the route by which a tool rather than a drug gets a regulator's blessing — in September 2024, the first organ chip proposed as a drug-development tool for liver injury, now at the qualification-plan stage. Then the sentence most coverage omits: "the platform should not yet be described as qualified." This map's regulatory_status: under-validation was already correct and is now corroborated in print by a disinterested party.

    (4) ⚠ Three of the five named barriers are not biology, and one of them is a variable this vault had not recorded. "Changes in device geometry, material composition, surface properties or fluidic resistance can alter cellular behavior and assay outcomes" — so a regulatory-grade chip needs controlled material specifications, validated production, lot-release criteria, traceability and formal change control, and the field has no agreement on which manufacturing changes force revalidation. Stack the cell and matrix lots on top and a chip campaign has at least three independent batch variables, none of which appears in the readout. A loop can standardise its own protocol and cannot standardise its own consumable — a campaign spanning two device lots has an unlabelled confound running through it, and an active-learning policy will attribute the step change to whichever chemicals followed it. The second non-biological barrier is auditability: "the analytical path from raw data to the reported outcome" must be reconstructable, including links between manufacturing lots and assay results, where current reporting is processed summary numbers. The third is that most validation still sits inside developer laboratories, which "cannot determine whether performance is intrinsic to the method or dependent on local expertise, customized equipment or undocumented protocol adjustments." The review's unusual recommended fix: publish the failed transfers. → the-chip-is-a-manufactured-part-and-the-lot-is-an-uncontrolled-variable

    (5) What it says about AI, which cuts against the enthusiasm. "AI may improve image analysis, cell tracking, phenotyping and multimodal data integration, but it cannot compensate for poorly designed experiments or unstandardized inputs." A model gets a context of use too, plus representative data, external validation, version control and monitoring for performance drift — and that last requirement is the one a closed loop makes harder: a loop exists to update the model, and a model that keeps updating keeps moving out from under the qualification it was granted. Digital twins "will require transparent separation of experimentally supported components from model assumptions." The authors' priority: "not algorithmic complexity but reproducible and auditable computation."

    (6) loop_readiness does not move, and it is worth saying why not. Nothing here supplies the missing data-interpretation procedure. A chip returning a multi-parametric readout with no published rule for converting it into a call is exactly as un-loopable after this source as before. What arrives are two obstacles to replicating a loop and to believing its output — the manufacturing lot and the audit trail — which the vault had under-recorded and which sit alongside access_gate rather than on the tier ladder.

    (7) The tumour milestone, correctly bounded. FDA allowed an investigational-new-drug amendment (BAL0891 plus tislelizumab) to proceed in 2025 on efficacy evidence generated exclusively in a human vascularised patient-derived organoid system, with no animal proof-of-concept efficacy study — reported by the developers as a first. The review: "this milestone represents the context-specific use of MPS evidence in a regulatory submission rather than formal qualification of the platform." A regulator accepting one submission is not a regulator accepting a method, and this is the cleanest published statement of that distinction the vault holds.

    (8) Global-ripple. No other map's Status or loop tier moves. Confirming edge to nephrotoxicity-ciptec-aop-battery — the review independently grades kidney systems developing and needing "multicenter testing, standard reference compounds and broader industrial use", which is the same diagnosis this vault reached from a proof-of-concept in one cell line on three platinum drugs whose authors decline to write a decision rule. Ripples also to inhalation-airway-epithelium-ali (lung graded early-stage, needing standardised exposure and dosimetry), neurotoxicity-cns-safety-nams-ai (blood–brain-barrier work graded the most quantitatively tractable nervous-system application), pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics (organ-chip permeability data feeding published pharmacokinetic models is named as the clearest near-term gastrointestinal use), nam-data-processing-pipeline (the raw-to- reported audit path is that map's subject) and regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility (context of use applied to the model layer, and performance-drift monitoring as a standing obligation). No new correlated_blind_spot row — a review naming reproducibility gaps is not a finding that two assays fail on the same chemicals.

  • 2026-08-21 — A liver chip read by mass spectrometry, and the cleanest signal in it answers no question. All axes HELD — Status emerging, loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop, same blocker. Ingested morisseau-2026-liver-chip-metabolomics-fatty-acids (NAM journal vol. 2, article 100122, PMID 42603961, DOI 10.1016/j.namjnl.2026.100122 — Compiègne/CNRS with Paris-Saclay and the University of Tokyo, no competing interests declared, funding_flag: [none], academic and not a platform vendor). HepG2/C3A human liver cells in a perfused microfluidic chip, exposed 2–7 days to oleic acid (the main monounsaturated dietary fat), palmitic acid (the main saturated one) and their mixture, with gas-chromatography mass-spectrometry metabolomics on the secreted medium. (1) The design has a real contrast in it. Oleic acid is repeatedly reported to protect hepatocytes against palmitic acid's toxicity, so the three arms are toxic, protective and both — and the quantity that matters is the difference between them. The authors report treatment-specific signatures: oleic acid "altered intracellular lipid secretion", the mixture "shifted metabolism from the lipid production to their consumption", palmitic acid alone produced changes "consistent with a perturbation in oxidative stress, energy metabolism and a high oxalic acid production." (2) ⚠ And the proposed biomarker rose in all three. "Nicotinamide levels increased in all treatments," offered as a candidate exposure biomarker needing further validation. As an exposure marker that is a reasonable claim. As a loop input it is the worst kind of number: instrument-read, reproducible, mechanistically plausible, and carrying zero information about the contrast. A campaign optimising against it would show strong consistent signal while being unable to tell the protective arm from the toxic one — the vault's recurring shape of a metric that improves without the decision improving, arriving this time from biomarker discovery rather than from benchmarking. → a-biomarker-that-moves-under-every-exposure-discriminates-nothing (3) The tier does not move, and the reason is the fourth measurement technology in a row. Metabolomics clears the readout test outright — a mass spectrometer, no human at a microscope anywhere in the chain — and then stops at the identical place as every other liver method in this vault: no published procedure converts a metabolite panel into a call. No threshold, no score, no reference chemicals with known labels. What this adds is a concrete warning about the obvious fix: "find the biomarker and set a threshold on it" is exactly the process that would have promoted nicotinamide. (4) Brakes, and they are heavy. One immortalised cancer-derived cell line, one laboratory, one device. The exposures are nutrients, not chemical hazards — nothing here shows the system detects a chemical that causes fatty liver. No reference panel, no sensitivity, no specificity, no applicability domain. And the full text is unread: the article is CC-BY-NC-ND open access and ScienceDirect still would not serve it (HTTP 403 on both PDF routes, captcha on the landing page), so replicate counts, concentrations and statistics are outside what the vault can see, and the nicotinamide result could dissolve under a multiple-comparison correction nobody here can inspect. (5) Sub-thread check — a third strand was considered and declined. This is neither the injury call (strand 1) nor quantitative pharmacology (strand 2); it is the platform used to observe a mechanism rather than to make a call. One abstract-only study on a nutrient exposure is not a separable argument, so the declaration is unchanged and deliberately not re-stamped. The condition that would open a third strand is worth writing down: a second and third source using this platform for mechanism or biomarker discovery with no hazard call in sight.
