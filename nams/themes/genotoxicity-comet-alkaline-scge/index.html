What we believe now

The problem this exists to solve. The alkaline comet assay (single-cell gel electrophoresis) measures DNA strand breaks by how far fragmented DNA migrates in an electric field, producing a comet-shaped image per cell. It is used in vitro, in regulatory in vivo animal studies (OECD TG 489, adopted 2014, revised 2016), and directly in human biomonitoring — drawing blood from real people to measure whether an actual environmental or occupational exposure caused detectable damage. Unlike this vault's other comet-assay map (genotoxicity-3d-skin-comet), which runs the same underlying technique inside lab-grown 3D human skin as a dermal follow-up test, this map covers the standard, tissue-agnostic assay — the form used across regulatory genotoxicity testing generally and human biomonitoring specifically.

The scoring bottleneck. Two ways to read the assay both carry high variability: image-analysis software (coefficients of variation up to 305% on some parameters across studies) and visual scoring by eye (25–105% inter-investigator variation on identical premade slides). Visual scoring is still used in over 40% of published human-biomonitoring studies as of 2020, precisely because image analysis under-performs at the low damage levels typical of non-occupationally-exposed populations. Because visual scoring is slow, studies conventionally score only ~100 cells per person — a number this map's founding source shows was never validated against the uncertainty it produces (see below).

AIComet. A YOLOv11 object detector, fine-tuned on 24,581 hand-classified nuclei, locates and classifies every nucleus in a fluorescence-microscope image using the same four-class-plus-undefined scheme OECD TG 489 already specifies. No person counts anything. Validated against the lab's own manual scorer on 327 healthy volunteers (R²=0.92) and, more importantly, against 11–13 independent expert investigators from other laboratories on three open-access image sets it had never trained on — its scores landed within about a point of the experts' medians on all three. Scores ~3,500 nuclei in 10 seconds, vs. the ~100 a human realistically manages. Open source (code and weights published, not "available upon request"); academic/ charity-funded with an explicit no-conflicts declaration — a cleaner provenance than most automation claims this vault's bias-watch rail has had to discount.

The sensitivity finding. Scoring the conventional 100 cells carries a median 19.4% relative-difference uncertainty against the full-sample score (95% CI 0.8%–79.4%); reaching 10% uncertainty needs 310 cells, a defensible upper bound needs 900 — numbers manual scoring was never going to reach across a study of hundreds of people. The tool does not just speed up the existing practice; it exposes how much noise that practice was running on. → counting-more-cells-is-now-free-and-that-changes-what-100-cells-was-worth

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop.

  • Readout: instrumented, deterministic, and open source. YOLOv11 detection + classification, no human scoring step, a fixed arithmetic formula turning per-nucleus classes into a sample score. Repeat runs on the same image give identical output — no even the intra-operator variability a human scorer has.
  • Decision rule: absent, and absent at a different point than this vault's other comet map. genotoxicity-3d-skin-comet has a decision rule that exists but is not portable (it reads the testing laboratory's own historical control range). This map has no decision rule of any kind evaluated yet — the founding source supplies a damage-quantification score, not a hazard classification, and does not attempt the statistical-comparison step TG 489's in vivo protocol actually specifies for turning a score into a call. → comet-assay-scoring-is-now-machine-executable-with-no-hazard-rule-attached
  • Promotion path. A study that runs AIComet (or an equivalent open scorer) against TG 489's actual statistical trend-test procedure on in vivo samples, publishing the threshold that separates a genotoxic call from background, would move this map toward closed-loop-ready without any change to the wet method — structurally the same promotion path already on file for genotoxicity-3d-skin-comet and genotoxicity-tgx-ddi-transcriptomic-biomarker.

⚠ Loop-ready is not endorsement, and here it is barely even a foothold. A tool that measures DNA damage faster and more consistently than a person is not the same thing as a tool that says whether a chemical is genotoxic. The two most valuable things this source measured — inter-lab scoring agreement and the real cost of under-sampling — say nothing about where the hazard line should sit.
