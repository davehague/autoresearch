CFU-GM myelotoxicity assay (Pessina 2003 / ESAC 2006) — immunotoxicity (myelotoxicity) — method

Status: consolidating (2026-07-10) — validated science, regulatorily inert. evidence_tier: formally-validated (ECVAM-coordinated international blind trial; unanimous ESAC endorsement; inter-laboratory reproducibility demonstrated), regulatory_status: none (no OECD Test Guideline exists, twenty years on). Not consensus, because nothing adopted it. Not fading, because nothing superseded it and nothing refuted it. The axes have simply stopped moving together — this is the widest split in the vault.

What we believe now

Human and murine myeloid progenitor cells (colony-forming unit — granulocyte/macrophage) are cultured in semi-solid medium and exposed to a xenobiotic; inhibition of colony formation is the readout. The prediction model is deliberately mechanism-naïve: it takes the mouse in-vivo MTD and scales it by the human/mouse ratio of in-vitro progenitor sensitivity to predict the human MTD — the acute exposure producing the maximum tolerated fall in absolute neutrophil count.

On validity, the provenance is the best in this vault. An ECVAM-coordinated international blind trial (Pessina et al., Toxicol Sci 75(2):355–367, 2003; PMID 12883091) tested 20 drugs — 14 antineoplastics, 2 antivirals, 3 therapeutic agents, 1 pesticide. The model correctly predicted the human MTD for 17 of 20; of the three misses, two fell within a 6-fold error and one at roughly 10-fold. The GLP-compliant SOP reproduced across laboratories. funding_flag: none — validation was run by an independent European reference laboratory, not by a developer or a sponsor. No method in this vault has a cleaner validation lineage.

On acceptance, there is nothing. No OECD Test Guideline. What exists is the ESAC statement of 21 March 2006 (24th Meeting), and its exact wording carries the whole finding:

"unanimously endorsed the CFU-GM assay for predicting acute neutropenia in humans as a substitute to using a second species, such as the dog, for this purpose. It should be noted that the test relies on the availability of mouse MTD data and is, therefore, not a full replacement method, but is intended to reduce the overall numbers of animals needed in toxicity testing. Performance standards for the assay should be developed."

The endorsement is narrow (replace the second species, not the first). The performance standards ESAC asked for — a prerequisite for a Test Guideline — were never developed. (The ESAC statement was read from its own archived PDF. The Pessina paper's PDF is a scan with no text layer, so the 17/20 figure comes from the PubMed abstract of record, not from the archived article body — recorded in the source note, not glossed.)

The ceiling is architectural, not political, and that is the lesson. The vault's default story is good science waiting on slow regulators. Here the method's own design explains the stall: a prediction model that consumes a mouse in-vivo MTD cannot be a replacement method, so the only guideline it could ever anchor is one for reducing animal use — a category OECD human-health Test Guidelines are not built to hold. Contrast OECD TG 430 (transcutaneous electrical resistance), which uses excised rat skin and is adopted: TG 430 consumes rat tissue; the CFU-GM model consumes a rat-or-mouse study. That is where the replacement line actually falls, and it is not where an evidence tier alone would put it.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: bench-bound — The most instructive bench-bound case in the vault. Colony counts are quantitative. The scaling model (mouse MTD x human/mouse sensitivity ratio -> human MTD) is fixed arithmetic. By every other test on this axis it reads loop-ready — and it is not, because the model's own input is a mouse experiment. This is architectural, not political: no amount of better science fixes it, only a new prediction model does. It is also why bench_bound_reason exists as a separate field.

  • Blocker: the prediction model consumes a prior mouse in-vivo MTD as an input
  • Bench-bound because: animal-input

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Battery composition — the named neighbour, now mapped (composes-with): immunotoxicity-il2-luc (TG 444A) is written by its own guideline as a battery component, and the battery it names includes myelotoxicity CFU-GM assays. The vault now holds two nodes of that battery, and they fail in opposite directions: TG 444A is regulatorily adopted but reads a single mechanism (T-cell cytokine transcription) with no published sensitivity/specificity; CFU-GM has an independently validated quantitative predictivity against human clinical data (17/20) and no regulatory standing at all. Neither is a stand-alone immunotoxicity test. Composing them would give hazard coverage across two immune compartments (adaptive T-cell signalling; myeloid progenitor viability) — but no defined approach exists, and this vault flags candidate batteries rather than adopting them.
  • The replacement line, made precise (a distinction, not a link): the CFU-GM model needs a prior in-vivo study; skin-corrosion-membrane-barrier-tg435's membrane is entirely synthetic; TG 430 uses excised rat tissue. Ordered by animal dependence these are replacement, replacement, and reduction — and CFU-GM is the only one whose dependence is on an experiment rather than a material. That is why it never became a TG despite being the better-validated method. Worth reading alongside skin-corrosion-rhe.
  • The inverse of the hepatotox case (structural, not biological): hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps is emerging — strong performance (87% sens / 100% spec) undercut by developer-run validation and no adoption. CFU-GM is consolidating — comparable performance, independently validated, and still no adoption. Read together they separate the two reasons a good method fails to be accepted: who ran the validation, and what the method structurally is. Only the first is fixable by doing more science.
  • Not an endocrine or metabolic edge: GR agonism (endocrine-glucocorticoid-receptor-transactivation) suppresses lymphocyte function and is classically myelo-sparing rather than myelotoxic; no source read here tests the interaction. Noted as absent, not as an edge.
