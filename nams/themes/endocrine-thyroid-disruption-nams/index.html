Thyroid disruption NAMs — the last EATS modality, and the blank is no longer total

Status: consolidating (moved from emerging on 2026-07-18). evidence_tier: formally-validated (an interlaboratory validation, up from a systematic inventory), regulatory_status: under-validation (up from none — a method is now demonstrably in a validation pipeline; there is still no Test Guideline).

⚠️ Read the size of this move correctly. What arrived is a reliability result — the assay transfers between labs — on four chemicals at three labs. What did not arrive: any sensitivity, specificity or apical-concordance figure, any Test Guideline, and any decision rule. The status moved because independent labs reproduced the method, which is exactly what consolidating means — not because thyroid is solved. The 2024 headline below still stands as the description of the field.

What we believe now

"validated Level 2 assays for THSD are still completely lacking today." — Vergauwen et al. 2024 (PMID 39931575) (THSD = thyroid hormone system disruption. "Level 2" = the OECD Conceptual Framework's in-vitro mechanistic tier — the rung TG 455, TG 458, TG 493 and TG 456 all sit on.)

On 2026-07-12 the vault named thyroid as "the last EATS modality, and the endocrine column's highest-value single gap." It is closed — and the gap was never in the vault. It is in the world.

EATS is the four-modality core of endocrine screening. The vault holds three of them as adopted OECD Test Guidelines with in-vitro methods and published decision rules. The fourth has none:

T — thyroidNONE—

67 in-vitro thyroid methods exist. Zero are adopted Test Guidelines.

  • 1 Level-2 method is graded "validated" — and it is CYP induction (TM2009-14), which is not a thyroid assay. It is the HepaRG method the vault already holds as metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg (OECD TG 445A). The thyroid column's only validated in-vitro entry is borrowed from the metabolism column.
  • 8 are in EU-NETVAL validation: TPO-AUR (thyroperoxidase inhibition, Amplex UltraRed readout), tyrosine iodination, TTR-ANSA and TTR-FITC (transthyretin displacement), DIO1 (deiodinase), TH glucuronidation, TR-reporter (INDIGO/CALUX), T-screen.
  • 50+ are unvalidated (ToxCast/Tox21 thyroid assays, sodium-iodide-symporter and transporter variants, thyroid microtissue and organoid methods).
  • 1 has already FAILED validation — the TPO luminol assay, rejected as non-specific to thyroperoxidase. The status lifecycle moving backwards, recorded in a source, on an endpoint the vault has never held before.

The biology is not what is missing. The inventory walks the entire thyroid axis — hormone synthesis (thyroperoxidase, sodium-iodide symporter), serum transport (transthyretin, thyroxine-binding globulin), metabolism (deiodinases, glucuronidation, sulphation), cellular uptake (MCT8, OATP1C1), receptor response (TRα/TRβ). It is a mechanistically complete panel with no regulatory standing whatsoever.

⚠️ The inversion — the animal test exists and the NAM does not

Thyroid does have adopted OECD Test Guidelines. They are animal tests: the Amphibian Metamorphosis Assay (TG 231), XETA (TG 248), LAGDA (TG 241) — and since 2016, thyroid endpoints written into the standard in-vivo rodent guidelines.

The regulatory answer to "is this chemical a thyroid disruptor?" is, today, still: dose an animal.

Estrogen and androgen got validated in-vitro Test Guidelines from 2009 onward. Thyroid got none — separated from its sibling modalities by roughly fifteen years of validation effort it never received, not by any deficit in the underlying science.

Set that against phototoxicity-iata, which has NAMs and no animal test at all (its reference standard is human clinical data). Thyroid is the exact mirror. Two endpoints in this vault now break its default question — "is the NAM as good as the animal test?" — from opposite directions.

2026-07-18 — the first crack: an interlaboratory validation, from the other jurisdiction

Deisenroth et al. 2026 (Toxicological Sciences, DOI 10.1093/toxsci/kfaf166) reports the first interlaboratory validation the vault holds on this endpoint — of the human thyroid microtissue assay: three-dimensional microtissues grown from primary human thyroid cells, which measure thyroid hormone synthesis directly rather than probing one fragment of the pathway.

That last point is the scientific interest. Almost everything in the 67-method inventory measures a piece of the thyroid axis — an enzyme inhibited (thyroperoxidase), a transport protein displaced (transthyretin), a receptor fired. The microtissue measures the output the whole axis exists to produce.

What the study did, and what it showed:

  • Three laboratories. Method transfer, training, and within-lab performance evaluation, against standard operating procedures written for the purpose.
  • Four blinded reference chemicals (three true positives, one true negative), across three independent human thyroid donors.
  • Result: the model transferred reliably, with reproducible effects on hormone synthesis and consistent bioactivity across the reference set. Dynamic range, precision and screening-quality metrics met the pre-set acceptance criteria. The authors note the results "shed insight into areas for improvement."

What it does not show — recorded plainly, because the gap is large:

  1. No predictivity. Not one sensitivity, specificity or apical-concordance number exists. This study asked "does the assay give the same answer in different hands?" — not "is the answer right?" Those are different questions and only the first has been answered.
  2. Four chemicals. A reference set of four is enough to check transferability and nowhere near enough to characterise an applicability domain.
  3. No decision rule. Nothing here converts a hormone-synthesis measurement into a regulatory thyroid- disruptor call. The map's loop_blocker survives.
  4. No Test Guideline. regulatory_status: under-validation, not adopted.

⚠️ Funding — stated, not laundered. The industry collaborators were LifeNet Health LifeSciences, Bayer Crop Science and Corteva Agrisciences. Two of those three are agrochemical companies whose own products are screened for thyroid disruption — a direct interest in the method that will judge their compounds. funding_flag: [industry]. The counterweight is real and also belongs in the record: the study was coordinated by the US EPA with NICEATM (the US validation body), and an independent international validation management team monitored progress. This is not a developer self-validating in private; it is a regulator-coordinated exercise with interested parties inside it. Both halves are true.

⚠️ The structural finding: thyroid now has TWO validation tracks, in two jurisdictions, and they are different methods. The map already catalogues eight methods in EU-NETVAL validation (the EU route). The thyroid microtissue assay is not one of them — it is a US EPA/NICEATM effort. So the endpoint the vault called its purest blank is now being worked on from both sides of the Atlantic, on non-overlapping method sets, with no shared decision rule between them. That is more shots on goal, and it is also two bodies of evidence that will not straightforwardly combine.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — HELD (2026-07-18). The deterministic ladder:

  • Readout? Machine-readable across the board — fluorescence (TPO-AUR), fluorescent-probe displacement (TTR-ANSA/FITC), luciferase (TR-CALUX), proliferation (T-screen). A robot could run all eight tomorrow.
  • Decision rule? Still none. → rung 4.
  • Inputs? No in-vivo study consumed by the methods' own models. Not bench-bound.

And it would not matter. Automating thyroid screening today would produce a great deal of data nobody has agreed how to read — the same diagnosis as developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb and automation-tox21-qhts-platform.

⚠️ What changed on 2026-07-18, precisely. Until tonight this map was the vault's most extreme case of its dominant blocker: DNT and Tox21 have validated assays awaiting a rule; thyroid had neither. Deisenroth et al. supplies one validated-for-reliability assay. Thyroid has therefore stopped being the extreme case and joined the ordinary one — it now sits alongside DNT and Tox21 as "the wet work is solved-ish, the interpretation layer is missing," rather than below them. The tier does not move (no decision rule appeared, and rung 4 is where "no agreed decision rule" lands), but the distance to a promotion shortened, and that is the honest reading of the night.

⚠️ Correlated blind spot — caught before the battery is built

TTR-ANSA and TTR-FITC measure the same molecular initiating event (displacement of thyroid hormone from transthyretin, its blood transport protein) by the same assay principle (competitive displacement of a fluorescent probe), differing only in which probe. The inventory's own taxonomy files both under one Level-2 block. They share every failure mode: no cells, no metabolism, no competing serum proteins.

Two of the eight EU-NETVAL methods; one vote, not two.

(Flagged as a vault inference from the source's taxonomy, not a claim the source makes — suggestive.)

Why this one is different from the vault's other three blind spots: FET↔RTgill-W1, ROS↔3T3, and the MMTV trio were all caught in batteries that already exist, built from adopted guidelines, years or decades late. The finding could only warn; it could not change the design.

The thyroid battery does not exist yet. The eight methods are in validation right now and how they will be combined into an IATA is still being decided. This is the one moment where the rail could be applied before the concrete sets — and the question worth putting to the OECD thyroid expert group is not "do both methods work?" but "has anyone run a metabolically-activated transthyretin displacer past both of them?"

  • Completes the EATS core — and the anti-edge still holds. Thyroid is a fifth mechanism screen, not a route to an apical endpoint. Stated again because this is the vault's most repeated category error.
  • ⚠️ But thyroid does NOT share the endocrine column's molecular confound — and that is worth saying. The vault's second blind spot is the MMTV-LTR promoter shared across TG 455 (ER), TG 458 (AR) and TG 454A (GR), where receptor selectivity is engineered, not natural. The thyroid methods are built on entirely different chemistry — peroxidase enzymology, cell-free transthyretin binding, deiodinase activity — so a thyroid result is structurally independent of the steroid-receptor panel, in the way TG 493 (cell-free) and TG 456 (hormone synthesis) are. Thyroid adds a genuinely independent vote to the endocrine battery. Its own correlated blind spot is internal (TTR-ANSA ↔ TTR-FITC), not shared with its siblings.
  • Composes-with metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg — and the link is literal. The vault already holds it. A chemical can disrupt thyroid signalling without ever touching the thyroid — by inducing the liver enzymes that destroy the hormone. The connect layer's cleanest compose-with to date: an adopted method from one column is the only validated entry in another.
  • Composes-with in-silico-qsar-read-across — 12 thyroid (Q)SAR models are statistically validated but none has been submitted to the OECD QSAR Toolbox for regulatory peer review. Added 2026-07-18 (Deisenroth microtissue validation)

  • ⚠️ The microtissue is the counter-design to this map's own blind spot — and it arrived by accident. The map's correlated_blind_spot is TTR-ANSA ↔ TTR-FITC: two methods reading the same molecular initiating event by the same principle, differing only in the fluorescent probe. The counter-design rule the vault extracted from automation-tox21-qhts-platform is that the remedy for a correlated blind spot is a measurement that fails differently, not a bigger battery. The thyroid microtissue measures hormone synthesis output from intact human thyroid tissue — it fails on entirely different things than a cell-free probe-displacement assay does, and it is metabolically competent in a way the TTR pair explicitly is not (the map's own domain_gate warns that a chemical whose metabolite displaces thyroid hormone reads as negative on the TTR arm). This is the orthogonal vote the thyroid column needed. Flagged as a vault inference — Deisenroth et al. make no claim about the TTR methods, and the two tracks are in different jurisdictions with no shared battery design.
  • The inversion HOLDS, and should not be quietly retired. The map records the vault's first inversion — thyroid's animal tests are adopted Test Guidelines (TG 231 Amphibian Metamorphosis, TG 248 XETA, TG 241 LAGDA) while its non-animal methods are not. Nothing tonight changes that. A reliability validation is not a Test Guideline. The regulatory answer to "is this chemical a thyroid disruptor?" is, today, still: dose an animal.
  • Sharpens the open gap, does not close it. docs/open-gaps-log.md names the EU-NETVAL outcome for the eight methods as the thing to watch. That watch item stands and now has a sibling: the US/NICEATM microtissue track. Two jurisdictions, two non-overlapping method sets, no shared decision rule — the question worth asking is no longer only "will a thyroid method validate?" but "will the two tracks produce one rule or two?"
