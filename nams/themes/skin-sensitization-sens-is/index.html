What we believe now

SENS-IS grows a piece of lab-made human skin (reconstructed human epidermis — real human keratinocytes in a layered sheet, not an immortalised cell line), doses it at five concentrations, and reads 64 genes off it by RT-PCR. Its verdict is arithmetic: a gene counts as switched on at more than 1.25-fold over control, and the chemical is called a skin allergen when ≥7 of a named 21-gene "SENS-IS" set and/or ≥7 of a named 17-gene "REDOX" set clear that line, in two replicates on tissue from two different batches. Potency comes from the lowest concentration still positive across 100 / 50 / 10 / 1 / 0.1%, banded into weak, moderate, strong and extreme, and mapped onto the two GHS regulatory sub-categories (1A = positive at ≤1%; 1B = positive at 10% and above).

What is genuinely different about it, in this column. Every other adopted skin-allergy assay reads one step of the allergy pathway — chemical binding to protein, or one cell type's stress response, or immune-cell activation — which is why they are combined into batteries. SENS-IS reads gene programmes relevant to the first three in one tissue, and it delivers hazard and potency from a single run, where OECD Guideline 497 needs three separate defined approaches (and forbids chaining them) to answer those questions.

The reproducibility numbers split, and the split is the honest headline. Five labs, 26 chemicals: 96% between-lab agreement on the yes/no allergen call — near-total agreement. 80.8% on the three-way strength call across all 26 chemicals (the draft's headline 84% covers 21 of them). Read plainly: run a chemical past five laboratories and they will nearly always agree whether it is an allergen, and about one chemical in five comes back with a different strength label depending on who ran it. Accuracy against the mouse LLNA for the 1A/1B split is 86.26% — right about five times in six, against a mouse test that is itself imperfect.

Standing, from the draft itself, para. 7: accuracy "is comparable to that of the LLNA, a stand-alone test method", but "it is recommended that the SENS-IS assay be used in conjunction with other sources of information." The guideline does not present it as a stand-alone replacement, and neither does this map.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready, loop_blocker: none — and this map's whole interest is that the qualifications live in the modifiers rather than in the tier.

Readout: RT-PCR cycle counts. An instrument number, not a picture a trained human scores. Decision rule: fixed, numeric, complete, and published in a draft regulatory instrument — sens-is-fixed-prediction-model-on-human-skin-tissue holds it step by step — including two automatic rejection gates that run before analysis (a tissue-viability check on HSP90AA1; an excessive-irritation check that rejects a concentration if more than 20 of 23 irritation genes fire). Para. 35 goes further than any other guideline in this vault: the cycle values "are pasted in a dedicated spreadsheet program" that computes the verdict, and "the spreadsheet document is an integral part of the SOP." The guideline ships an implementation. By the deterministic ladder in shared/evidence-model.md — machine-readable readout plus a fixed data-interpretation procedure — this is rung 5.

And three things sit alongside the tier, permanently:

  1. The rule's constants are user-retunable — sens-is-prediction-model-parameters-are-user-retunable. Paras. 29, 31 and 32 permit changing the threshold, the gene identities and the gene count with scientific justification, and state the 1.25 was calibrated to one commercial tissue brand and "could be adjusted" for another. That is every free parameter. It does not demote the tier — the rule is fixed for a configuration and a machine applies it perfectly once pinned — but it makes the configuration part of the loop's state, to be versioned and re-checked on any transfer.
  2. The setup choice is unruled and fails under-protectively — full-thickness-escalation-is-an-unruled-pre-analytical-choice. Nothing published says when to escalate to full-thickness tissue, the default under-calls potency for metabolically demanding aromatics, and the failure is invisible in the output because the assay returns a well-formed band either way.
  3. access_gate, three layers deep — proprietary gene panels, a non-public spreadsheet that is the actual classifier, and a commercial tissue whose brand determines the threshold. "Loop-ready" here means ImmunoSearch's loop is ready. Replicating it elsewhere means re-deriving the classifier.

Loop-ready is not an endorsement. A machine could make this call. That says nothing about whether the call is right, and the map's own developer-funded evidence base is the reason to keep saying so.

The pro-hapten question — and what this map does NOT authorise

The reason this assay matters beyond its own row is the claim in the draft's para. 11: reconstructed human epidermis "includes metabolic systems inherent to such skin models", therefore pro-haptens — chemicals that only become allergens after a skin enzyme transforms them — "can be detected by the test method", and pre-haptens (air-oxidised) too. The developer's 50-chemical blinded study reports the assay caught "the majority" of a pre-/pro-hapten-enriched set.

On 2026-08-01 this vault's connect pass flagged exactly that chemical class as a candidate shared blind spot across the adopted 2-out-of-3 battery — DPRA has no cells and no enzymes; KeratinoSens and h-CLAT run on immortalised lines with limited skin metabolism; so a pro-hapten can be missed three times for one reason and the two-of-three rule reads two concordant negatives as a pass. That flag was raised as a vault inference with no citation, and correlated_blind_spot was deliberately left unset.

It stays unset. reconstructed-epidermis-metabolic-competence-as-pro-hapten-answer states why in full: para. 11 asserts something about this tissue and never measures the other three assays; nobody has run the battery and SENS-IS on a shared pro-hapten set; the 50-chemical study reports no sensitivity, no specificity, no accuracy and no per-chemical table; and the evidence comes from the company selling the alternative. What the vault records instead is that the flag now has a named candidate repair sitting in the OECD pipeline — the second such repair to arrive from the party that would profit from it, after the HepaRG proposal against the micronucleus assay's false-positive rate (2026-07-27, still awaiting a human policy decision).
