Testicular steroidogenesis — Leydig-cell and testis models — male gonadal hormone synthesis — method

This is that candidate arriving — and the answer it brings is that the corner does not close the endpoint either. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Why this is not a duplicate of endocrine-steroidogenesis-h295r. That map is OECD Test Guideline 456, the adopted steroidogenesis screen, which runs on a female adrenal carcinoma line. This map is the testis-derived model family, which is a different organ, a different regulatory standing (none), and — per this run's source — reads biology TG 456 structurally cannot. Keeping them separate is the point: the two maps disagree with each other, and that disagreement is the finding.

What we believe now

Testicular steroidogenesis is the testis's manufacture of sex steroids — principally testosterone, made by the Leydig cells that sit between the sperm-producing tubules. The command chain runs from the pituitary: luteinising hormone arrives, binds the LHR/LHCGR receptor on a Leydig cell, and the cell steps up testosterone synthesis. Chemicals can disrupt this at either end — by inhibiting the synthesis enzymes, or by interfering with the hormonal command.

The adopted assay covers one end and not the other. Řehůřková et al. (2026), reviewing 1,533 studies, report that H295R cells "lack a fully functional LHR/LHCGR signaling pathway" and that the line's "relevance to testicular steroidogenesis is debated due to fundamental differences between adrenal and gonadal cells, female and male physiology." See h295r-cannot-receive-the-signal-that-drives-a-testicle.

The testis-derived alternatives are numerous and unstandardised. The review's targeted comparison covers eight testicular systems alongside H295R — primary Leydig cells, the immortalised lines MA-10, TM3, mLTC-1, R2C, BLTK-1, and testis explants — plus stem-cell-derived Leydig-like cells and 3D testicular systems in the wider inventory. What they mostly are:

"Most models rely on rodent-derived, cancerous cell lines cultured in two-dimensional monolayers, with limited representation of human and immature Leydig cells."

And the field has no agreed answer key. Of 23 reference chemicals traced across testosterone, progesterone, androstenedione and estrogens, four — forskolin, genistein, prochloraz, ketoconazole — behaved consistently. For the remaining nineteen, "data … are scarce or inconsistent." See 1533-papers-of-testicular-models-and-four-usable-reference-chemicals.

Standing, in the authors' words: "most available models are from rodent origin and lack formal validation and OECD regulatory acceptance."

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — and the reason it is not lower is worth being precise about, because the naive read of "no validated model" is "unloopable," and that is the wrong axis.

Applying the ladder in order:

  1. Is there a physical assay? Yes — cells in a plate, chemical added, hormones measured in the medium. Not a pure model, so not computational-connective.
  2. Does the method's own prediction model consume an in-vivo study, or does no NAM exist at all? Neither. There are many non-animal models; the problem is that none is validated. This is emphatically not bench-bound: no-nam-exists — that is the grading on reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams for the fertility endpoint, where there is genuinely nothing. Here there are 1,533 papers' worth of somethings.
  3. Is human judgement load-bearing in the call? No. The readout is a hormone concentration. Immunoanalytical techniques in 85% of studies — an antibody plate read, a number out of an instrument — with mass spectrometry in a few. Nothing is scored by eye.
  4. Machine-readable readout, no fixed data-interpretation procedure? Yes — this is the match.

loop_blocker: no standardised model, no agreed decision rule, and — the binding one — no reference-chemical set. This is a distinct and unusually clean blocker shape, and it is worth naming as its own kind: the calibration blocker. A robot could run these plates tomorrow and produce hormone numbers at scale. It would have nothing to compare them against. Nineteen of twenty-three candidate reference chemicals do not have a stable expected answer across models, and a ring trial cannot be run without one — so the field cannot execute the study that would move this map up the evidence axis, let alone the loop axis.

domain_gate: rodent, cancerous, 2D, adult. Three species/biology carve-outs and one developmental one. The developmental gap is the sharpest: male reproductive tract programming happens in a window that adult-cell models do not represent.

Loop-ready is not endorsement, in the most literal available form. automatable-open-loop here means a machine could generate the numbers. The vault's grading also says nobody knows what the numbers mean. Those are compatible statements and this is the map that shows why the axes must not be collapsed.

Status HELD at emerging; loop_readiness HELD at automatable-open-loop; the domain_gate gains one qualified exception. Ingested cham-2026-testis-organoid-cadmium-validation (Cham, Ibtisham, Guerrero, Al-Dissi & Honaramooz 2026, Toxicologic Pathology, DOI 10.1177/01926233261465410; University of Saskatchewan + Masaryk University) exposed their own previously-published three-dimensional testis organoid to cadmium — the textbook testicular poison, used here as a positive control — at 0.01–100 μM. Cadmium accumulated inside the tissue concentration-dependently; at 10 and 100 μM cord formation, germ cell number, cell proliferation, testosterone and estradiol all fell while apoptosis/necrosis and DNA damage rose; whole testis-cell assembly disrupted only at 100 μM. → a-testis-organoid-passes-the-easy-test-and-the-hard-one-is-not-a-test-yet (1) Every result is in the expected direction and the expected order, which is why it moves nothing. A positive control demonstrates sensitivity to a known-bad compound and nothing else. No negatives were tested, so there is no specificity number, no false-positive rate, and no accuracy figure of any kind — and none is claimed. The title's word "validation" is the authors' usage for "we checked our model responds"; it is not validation in this vault's sense (no validation body, no ring trial, no reference panel, no blinding, no pre-specified criteria). evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, funding_flag: [method-developer]. (2) The domain_gate earns a partial exception and it is worth stating precisely. The gate reads: most available models are rodent-derived, cancerous lines in 2D monolayer, with limited representation of human and of immature Leydig cells. A three-dimensional organoid that reassembles into cords is a genuine step away from the "2D monolayer" clause. Whether it is a step away from the "rodent-derived" clause is UNKNOWN — the retrievable text does not state the species, and the vault will not guess. If these are rodent organoids the model substitutes rat cells for a rat study, which is a Reduction result rather than a human-relevance one, and that distinction decides most of what this paper is worth. (3) It does not touch the binding blocker, and the blocker is not model quality. It is the missing reference-chemical set — only 4 of 23 candidates (forskolin, genistein, prochloraz, ketoconazole) behave consistently across models, and cadmium is not among them. An automated system could run this organoid tomorrow and have nothing to calibrate its thousands of hormone numbers against. Adding a ninth model to a field of eight with no answer key is rational for one laboratory and does not close the endpoint. (4) One thing the vault could not determine and has logged rather than assumed: whether cord formation, testis-cell assembly and germ-cell number were scored by a pathologist's eye or by automated image analysis. That decides where this model sits on the loop ladder. The map's tier is set by the family's headline readout — hormone output, instrument-read by immunoassay — and holds.
