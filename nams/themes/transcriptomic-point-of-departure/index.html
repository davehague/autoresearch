Transcriptomic points of departure — potency estimation — method

Status: emerging (2026-07-31) — 🚩 auto-coined, needs a human verdict. One laboratory, 19 chemicals, no inter-laboratory data. evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method, regulatory_status: none, loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop. Opens a new group in this vault: a method that answers how much, not whether — potency estimation rather than hazard classification.

What we believe now

A point of departure is the dose at which a chemical starts doing something; regulators divide it down by uncertainty factors to reach a permissible human exposure. Classically it comes from an animal study.

A transcriptomic point of departure (tPOD) replaces "find the lowest dose with a visible adverse effect in an animal" with "find the dose at which the cell's gene expression starts moving." Cells are dosed across a concentration range, whole-transcriptome sequencing measures every gene at every dose, a curve is fitted to each responding gene to find its benchmark dose, and the tPOD is summarised from the most sensitive end of that distribution. Dish concentration is then converted to a swallowed human dose through mass-balance modelling and in-vitro-to-in-vivo extrapolation.

The evidence here is 19 pesticides in Caco-2 (gut lining) and HepG2 (liver) cells. tPODs were obtained for all 19, spanning 0.032 to 880 µM — four and a half orders of magnitude, so the method discriminates potency rather than flagging everything. Caco-2 responses skewed to stress pathways, HepG2 to metabolic ones. Converted to human equivalent doses, ~50% (Caco-2) and ~70% (HepG2) landed within one order of magnitude of the chronic animal study's point of departure — "moderate concordance", with the in-vitro values tending to run conservative. Authors position the output for screening and prioritisation, not for setting exposure limits.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — and it earns the tier from an unusual direction. Every step from sequencer output to final dose is a published algorithm with published parameters: curve fitting, model selection, gene filtering, summarisation, extrapolation. Nothing is scored by eye and no committee is consulted. What is missing is not the procedure. It is the criterion the procedure's output should be compared against.

  • Blocker: the pipeline produces a number, not a call. Nothing published says how far below a tPOD a human exposure must sit, or what concordance a tPOD must reach before it may replace a chronic study. The performance is reported against a target that does not exist.
  • Why the shape matters. A continuous, ordered quantity is something an optimiser can pursue; a hazard class is not. This is the same property the vault recorded on a Bayesian quantitative adverse outcome pathway on 2026-07-30 — continuous variables rather than bins, because binning destroys the gradation an optimiser needs. Two independent sources in two days landing on the same design preference for the same reason, neither mentioning automation.
  • Promotion path. A published acceptance criterion — a stated margin, or a stated required concordance — would move this to closed-loop-ready with no change to the assay at all. The whole gap is a sentence nobody has written.
  • ⚠ And a loop that optimises this number without that sentence will generate very well-ranked evidence about a decision it cannot make.

A loop consuming this number inherits both models' uncertainty and can see neither.
