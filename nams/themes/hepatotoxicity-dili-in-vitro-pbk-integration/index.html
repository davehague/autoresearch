What we believe now

Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) — a drug damaging the liver — is a leading cause of drug failure in development and of post-approval withdrawal, and it is hard to see coming before a person takes the drug.

This method predicts it with a margin-of-safety comparison: how close does the concentration a patient actually experiences get to the concentration that hurts liver cells in a dish?

  • Numerator: Cmax, the peak concentration in blood plasma. In the prospective version this is not measured in anybody — it is simulated by a whole-body physiologically based kinetic model (PK-Sim) whose own inputs are predicted from chemical structure.
  • Denominator: the lowest of three IC50s — cytotoxicity (kills liver cells), mitochondrial toxicity (poisons their energy machinery), and inhibition of the bile salt export pump (the transporter that clears bile acids out of the cell; block it and they accumulate and corrode it). An IC50 is the concentration producing half the maximum effect, read off a dose-response curve.
  • Rule: ratio ≥ 0.25 → flag as a liver-injury risk.

On 241 drugs it discriminates well — ROC AUC 96% using measured Cmax and 91% with the simulated one (pick a harmful drug and a safe drug at random; that is how often the method ranks the harmful one higher, against 50% for a coin flip). Removing the human measurement costs about five AUC points and changes 13.94% of individual calls.

The operating point is the finding. At the frozen threshold the rule runs at 100% specificity and 51% sensitivity: on this dataset it wrongly flagged no safe drug, and it stayed silent on half the drugs that actually cause liver injury. That is the inverse of nearly every other assay in this vault, which lean the other way — over-call to avoid missing a hazard. This one never cries wolf and often says nothing at all.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready — three plate-reader IC50 readouts plus a computational exposure estimate, combined by a fixed division and a fixed comparison. By the vault's deterministic ladder that is rung 5: nothing in the call requires a trained human. In the prospective form no living organism is consulted at any point — arguably the most completely machine-runnable hazard call in the vault.

And loop-ready is not an endorsement, emphatically here.

  • A perfectly specific, half-sensitive rule is the most dangerous possible input to a loop that reads silence as safety. When it fires, believe it. When it does not fire, it has said nothing — and the non-firing is indistinguishable from a clean result. There is no abstention state, no error bar, no signal that the question was outside what the battery can see. → a-fully-in-silico-dili-rule-that-never-false-alarms-and-misses-half
  • The missed half has a structure, and the structure reproduces. All three assays are the same experimental object measured three ways. See the correlated_blind_spot below and three-liver-assays-with-one-shared-blind-spot-immune-mediated-dili. Running the battery again does not surface the gap; it reproduces it, which an active-learning loop reads as reliability.
  • The one constant that turns a score into a decision is a human judgement. 25%, chosen for plausibility over a fitted 1.1%. Recorded as a domain_gate.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Composes-with — the exposure half. pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics is the vault's map for the models that convert a concentration in a dish into a dose in a person. This method is that map's machinery pointed at a specific clinical endpoint, and it supplies something the vault did not have: a measured cost for going fully in-silico on the kinetics. Substituting a simulated Cmax for a measured one costs about five ROC-AUC points and flips 13.94% of individual calls. That number is the closest thing the vault holds to a price for the last human-derived input in a hazard pipeline.

  • ⚠️ CORRELATED BLIND SPOT (cross-battery), set 2026-08-08. hepatotoxicity-cholestasis-battery also reads bile salt export pump inhibition, as one of its five key events. Different group, different funding, different cell system — and the same measurement of the same transporter. A hepatic assessment that consults both batteries and takes their agreement on a cholestatic drug as two independent confirmations is counting one. Flagged on both maps.

  • Composes-with / contrast — the tissue models. hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps (organ-on-chip) attacks the same endpoint from the opposite direction: build a more faithful liver and measure it, rather than take crude measurements and model the exposure. The two are complementary in exactly the way the intra-battery components here are not — an organ chip does contain multiple cell types and can run prolonged dosing, which is the specific gap named in this battery's blind spot. The pairing is the interesting one for a loop: this method is cheap, fast and fully automatable with half the sensitivity; the chip is slower and richer. A staged loop that used this rule as a positive oracle and escalated its silences to a chip is the obvious architecture, and nobody in this run's literature proposes it.

  • Global-ripple. hepatotoxicity-steatosis-battery and metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg — same endpoint family, different mechanisms and different cell systems; no confirm/refute edge, and notably no shared HepaRG substrate, since this battery's data are drawn from mixed literature cell systems rather than one licensed line. Outward, in-silico-qsar-read-across is the neighbouring computational hub: this method's property inputs (lipophilicity, solubility, clearance) come from in-silico predictors of exactly the kind that map tracks, so the rule inherits that layer's error as well as its own.
