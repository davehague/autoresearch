The validation paradigm (ICCVAM 2024) — validation-paradigm — framework map

Status: emerging (2026-07-25) — 🚩 auto-coined 2026-07-25, convention departure — a validation-paradigm map. Its endpoint, validation-paradigm, is not a toxicological endpoint, and its subject is neither an assay nor an acceptance gate but the machinery that decides what counts as evidence. The vault has five sources arguing about what validation should become and no home for the argument; this is the home. loop_readiness: not-applicable — the seventh non-method map, alongside the four regulatory-acceptance gates, the loop machinery, and the reagent supply chain.

Sub-threads: (1) the procedure — what a validation must do, what it may skip, and who decides · (2) the answer key — whether anything exists to validate a method against [reviewed 2026-08-18 @ 11 bullets]

Why the declaration was written tonight. The map crossed the 10-dated-bullet threshold on 2026-08-18 (to 11) and had never declared its structure. Two strands, not three: the ICCVAM five-step framework, its footnote-1 automation exemption, the applicability-domain definition and the battery-composition failure mode are one argument about the procedure — what it requires and what it lets you off. Separate from all of them is the question tonight's two arrivals land in: what is the reference answer, and does one exist at all. That is not a claim about the procedure; it is a claim that the procedure has nothing to point at. Under the fork threshold (≥10 bullets AND ≥3 declared strands), so no fork — recorded deliberately, because the temptation to split "the exemption" off from "the procedure" would be splitting a footnote from its framework.

What we believe now

The vault's evidence_tier axis has an owner, and in the US it is ICCVAM — the Interagency Coordinating Committee on the Validation of Alternative Methods, spanning 17 federal agencies. In March 2024 its Validation Workgroup published Validation, Qualification, and Regulatory Acceptance of New Approach Methodologies, replacing the paradigm it names as obsolete: "The outdated one-size-fits-all strategy for validation does not work in an advanced field like NAMs."

The old paradigm is OECD GD 34 and the 1997 ICCVAM criteria, and the report says why it broke: those resources "described a validation model that is flexible in principle, but in practice has demonstrated various limitations such as being lengthy and resource-intensive," and "are not always compatible with many modern approaches to toxicity testing, which place less emphasis on replacement of in vivo tests with a single alternative method."

Five key concepts, interdependent, all requiring independent review: context of use (COU) — "the purpose for which the NAM is intended (e.g., screening, hazard identification, potency evaluation, point of departure for quantitative risk assessment)" — biological relevance, technical characterization, data integrity, information transparency. And the governing sentence: "The COU determines the detailed criteria and implementation of the key concepts." There is no fixed bar.

Five technical steps, with feedback loops between them:

"1) initial vetting of the scientific relevance of the NAM, 2) conceptual evaluation, 3) within-laboratory evaluation, 4) statistical data analysis and reporting, and 5) interlaboratory evaluation (if needed¹)."

⚠️ Footnote 1 is the finding

"There could be examples of methods for which interlaboratory evaluation is not needed or not feasible, such as large scale roboticized high-throughput screening assays and machine learning-based in silico approaches."

Two named exemptions, and the vault holds a map for each. Large-scale roboticised high-throughput screening is automation-tox21-qhts-platform. Machine-learning-based in silico approaches is in-silico-qsar-read-across and ml-model-reporting-reproducibility. They are the robot half and the model half of a closed loop — the two components this vault exists to study, both named as possibly not needing to demonstrate that they reproduce in another laboratory.

Read the sentence in two halves. "Not feasible" is unobjectionable: you cannot re-run a 10,000-chemical robotic screen in a second lab at reasonable cost, and a trained model has no second lab. "Not needed" is asserted with no argument and no substitute requirement. Data integrity and information transparency are the plausible intended replacements — the report does require that "Use of computational algorithms, e.g., machine learning models, and software (including version number) should be well-documented to ensure reproducibility" — but documenting a model is a claim about the record, not about the world. → interlaboratory-evaluation-is-optional-for-the-automatable-cases

⚠️ And it couples two axes the vault documents as orthogonal

evidence_tier separates formally-validated from peer-reviewed-method on exactly one property: inter-laboratory reproducibility. That is the only rung in the ladder that cannot be manufactured by writing a better paper — it requires the method to work on unfamiliar equipment with unfamiliar hands. Footnote 1 makes that property conditional for the two most automatable method classes. So: the more automatable a method, the more likely it is exempt from the evidence the vault grades highest.

Second time the loop axis has turned out to have a price, and the two prices run in opposite directions. FDA's model-risk matrix (2026-07-13) says autonomy costs: model risk = model influence × decision consequence, and a closed loop maximises influence by construction, so every rung the vault treats as progress the regulator treats as a heavier evidentiary burden (regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility). ICCVAM's footnote says autonomy is excused, at the level of the component. Both are true; they apply at different levels and are written by different agencies. The uncomfortable combination: a closed loop is assembled from components admitted on a lighter standard and then placed in the configuration that demands the heaviest one. → automatability-buys-an-exemption-from-the-hardest-to-fake-evidence

The vault's open question, answered — and the answer is "nothing"

On 2026-07-22 the vault logged: "the ring trial is being demoted by four actors with four motives, and nobody has said what replaces it." The four were FDA CDER (accepts fit-for-purpose NAMs with no validation), UKCVAM ("ring trials or innovative ways of agreeing regulatory readiness"), the AI-NAM literature (a retraining model cannot be validated by an event), and Kopańska & Hartung's next-generation-validation framework (the ring trial is too slow and measures against the wrong reference).

ICCVAM is the fifth, it is the one with the authority, and it names the mechanism: nothing replaces it. The ring trial becomes conditional on the context of use, and the agency decides case by case. That is not a replacement instrument; it is the removal of a fixed one.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — this is not an assay. It is the axis that decides what an assay's evidence is worth, and it is scored off the ladder like the regulatory-acceptance framework maps.

Its consequence for the loop is nonetheless direct, and it is the opposite of the usual direction. Every other finding in this vault about autonomy adds a requirement. This one removes one, for exactly the two things a loop is made of. Nothing here says a robotic screen or an ML model is less trustworthy than a ring-trialled assay — it says the evidence that would settle the question may not be collected.

  • 2026-08-23 — The answer-key strand gets its most concrete complaint yet, and it is a structural one about redundancy rather than a philosophical one about reference standards. Status HELD at emerging; loop_readiness HELD at not-applicable; sub-thread declaration UNCHANGED and deliberately NOT re-stamped. Ingested zarn-2026-internal-external-validity-plant-protection (Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology 172:106203, single independent author, Zürich, funding_flag: [none]; commentary, no new data, abstract-only ingest — Elsevier paywall). (1) The argument. Regulatory toxicology of pesticide active substances rests on OECD Test Guideline-compliant, GLP-compliant animal studies. Those frameworks "ensure harmonized study conduct and technical quality assurance" but "do not themselves guarantee internal and external validity" — internal validity being whether this study's own result is trustworthy (power, controls, replication), external validity whether it transfers to the population you care about. A protocol standard makes two labs do the same thing; it does not ask whether either had the power to see what it reported nothing about. (2) The propagation point, which is the new one. Under Mutual Acceptance of Data, one OECD-compliant study is accepted across every adhering jurisdiction "based on the same underlying study data." The system therefore contains no independent repeat anywhere — one study, run once, and any error in it propagates everywhere simultaneously with no second lab whose disagreement would surface it. Named cases: chlorothalonil (animal population structure influencing what could be detected in carcinogenicity studies) and aclonifen ("divergent findings between broadly comparable studies"). → the-answer-key-is-run-once-and-shared-everywhere (3) The negative-result argument, and the asymmetry that gives it teeth. "Negative findings do not necessarily indicate absence of effects in susceptible subpopulations" — a study powered for the typical outbred animal is silent about an effect concentrated in a sensitive minority, and a regulatory decision reads that silence as a clean result. For a drug there is a correction stage downstream: human trials, then mandatory post-marketing pharmacovigilance. For a pesticide there is not — "no mandatory, substance-specific post-authorisation surveillance system comparable to pharmacovigilance." The animal study is close to the last word, not the first. Hence the author's oddly specific and obviously right remedy: targeted confirmation of pivotal NEGATIVE findings, not positive ones. → a-negative-result-is-a-statement-about-the-population-you-tested (4) What this does to the vault's own axis, stated as an implication and NOT acted on. Every evidence_tier in this vault is ultimately agreement with an animal result. If the reference is unreplicated, a published specificity of 86% against it is not the same measurement as 86% against a stable reference, and no confidence interval anywhere in this vault accounts for uncertainty in the answer key. That is a limitation of the axis, not a criticism of any assay on it. Changing the grading rule on one single-author commentary would be exactly the overreaction this vault exists to avoid — logged, not applied. (5) Brakes, and they are load-bearing. Single author, no new data, no numbers, no power calculation, no worked case. evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic — the vault's honest floor for a reasoning document, and the nth instance of the missing review/perspective rung. The word "NAM" does not appear in the abstract; every connection drawn to non-animal methods here is the vault's own inference and is flagged as such in both concept notes. And the obvious defence is not engaged: a conservative system deliberately trades detection power for throughput and covers the gap with uncertainty factors. (6) Sub-thread re-check. Both concept notes land in strand (2) the answer key — whether anything exists to validate a method against, which is exactly what that strand was declared for on 2026-08-18. No new strand, no change to the number or identity of strands, so no re-stamp per shared/evidence-model.md.

  • 2026-08-25 — THE FLOOR THE WHOLE VALIDATION ARGUMENT IS TRYING TO CLEAR GETS NAMED, AND IT IS A PAPERWORK STANDARD RATHER THAN A SCIENTIFIC ONE. Status HELD at emerging; loop_readiness HELD at not-applicable. Ripple from tonight's ingest of chaudhry-2026-nams-conundrum-evidence-hierarchy (Archives of Toxicology guest editorial, published today; no primary data; funding_flag: none). Traditional regulatory toxicology runs guideline-driven studies under Good Laboratory Practice — a formal, inspected quality system covering study plans, sign-off, calibration, raw-data retention and reconstructability — "whereas many NAMs operate within less formalised validation frameworks." The replacement vocabulary this map has been tracking (scientific validity, qualification in a context of use, fitness for purpose) is "increasingly discussed" but "the lack of universally accepted criteria creates a credibility gap that continues to limit regulatory uptake." The vault's evidence_tier axis grades what a method's science shows; it has never graded whether the study that showed it ran under an auditable quality system, and a regulator asks both. ⚠ And the loop reading here runs the OTHER way from every other finding tonight, which is worth saying plainly: an autonomous lab is structurally good at exactly what GLP asks for. The clearest instance the vault holds is not from toxicology — in yu-2026-ai-engineer-certifiable-design-aip, also ingested tonight, a closed loop kept "WebSocket logs, SHA-256 integrity manifests, and solver input decks" so that every question a third-party certifier raised could be traced to the computational step that produced the answer, and the authors report it "substantially accelerat[ed]" the review. Auditability is one of the few dimensions on which a machine-run assay is better placed than a human-run one. It is also not the dimension that decides: GLP says a study was recorded properly, not that its answer should outrank a rodent's. → glp-is-the-quality-floor-nams-are-compared-against
