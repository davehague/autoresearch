The validation paradigm (ICCVAM 2024) — validation-paradigm — framework map

Status: emerging (2026-07-25) — 🚩 auto-coined 2026-07-25, convention departure — a validation-paradigm map. Its endpoint, validation-paradigm, is not a toxicological endpoint, and its subject is neither an assay nor an acceptance gate but the machinery that decides what counts as evidence. The vault has five sources arguing about what validation should become and no home for the argument; this is the home. loop_readiness: not-applicable — the seventh non-method map, alongside the four regulatory-acceptance gates, the loop machinery, and the reagent supply chain.

What we believe now

The vault's evidence_tier axis has an owner, and in the US it is ICCVAM — the Interagency Coordinating Committee on the Validation of Alternative Methods, spanning 17 federal agencies. In March 2024 its Validation Workgroup published Validation, Qualification, and Regulatory Acceptance of New Approach Methodologies, replacing the paradigm it names as obsolete: "The outdated one-size-fits-all strategy for validation does not work in an advanced field like NAMs."

The old paradigm is OECD GD 34 and the 1997 ICCVAM criteria, and the report says why it broke: those resources "described a validation model that is flexible in principle, but in practice has demonstrated various limitations such as being lengthy and resource-intensive," and "are not always compatible with many modern approaches to toxicity testing, which place less emphasis on replacement of in vivo tests with a single alternative method."

Five key concepts, interdependent, all requiring independent review: context of use (COU) — "the purpose for which the NAM is intended (e.g., screening, hazard identification, potency evaluation, point of departure for quantitative risk assessment)" — biological relevance, technical characterization, data integrity, information transparency. And the governing sentence: "The COU determines the detailed criteria and implementation of the key concepts." There is no fixed bar.

Five technical steps, with feedback loops between them:

"1) initial vetting of the scientific relevance of the NAM, 2) conceptual evaluation, 3) within-laboratory evaluation, 4) statistical data analysis and reporting, and 5) interlaboratory evaluation (if needed¹)."

⚠️ Footnote 1 is the finding

"There could be examples of methods for which interlaboratory evaluation is not needed or not feasible, such as large scale roboticized high-throughput screening assays and machine learning-based in silico approaches."

Two named exemptions, and the vault holds a map for each. Large-scale roboticised high-throughput screening is automation-tox21-qhts-platform. Machine-learning-based in silico approaches is in-silico-qsar-read-across and ml-model-reporting-reproducibility. They are the robot half and the model half of a closed loop — the two components this vault exists to study, both named as possibly not needing to demonstrate that they reproduce in another laboratory.

Read the sentence in two halves. "Not feasible" is unobjectionable: you cannot re-run a 10,000-chemical robotic screen in a second lab at reasonable cost, and a trained model has no second lab. "Not needed" is asserted with no argument and no substitute requirement. Data integrity and information transparency are the plausible intended replacements — the report does require that "Use of computational algorithms, e.g., machine learning models, and software (including version number) should be well-documented to ensure reproducibility" — but documenting a model is a claim about the record, not about the world. → interlaboratory-evaluation-is-optional-for-the-automatable-cases

⚠️ And it couples two axes the vault documents as orthogonal

evidence_tier separates formally-validated from peer-reviewed-method on exactly one property: inter-laboratory reproducibility. That is the only rung in the ladder that cannot be manufactured by writing a better paper — it requires the method to work on unfamiliar equipment with unfamiliar hands. Footnote 1 makes that property conditional for the two most automatable method classes. So: the more automatable a method, the more likely it is exempt from the evidence the vault grades highest.

Second time the loop axis has turned out to have a price, and the two prices run in opposite directions. FDA's model-risk matrix (2026-07-13) says autonomy costs: model risk = model influence × decision consequence, and a closed loop maximises influence by construction, so every rung the vault treats as progress the regulator treats as a heavier evidentiary burden (regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility). ICCVAM's footnote says autonomy is excused, at the level of the component. Both are true; they apply at different levels and are written by different agencies. The uncomfortable combination: a closed loop is assembled from components admitted on a lighter standard and then placed in the configuration that demands the heaviest one. → automatability-buys-an-exemption-from-the-hardest-to-fake-evidence

The vault's open question, answered — and the answer is "nothing"

On 2026-07-22 the vault logged: "the ring trial is being demoted by four actors with four motives, and nobody has said what replaces it." The four were FDA CDER (accepts fit-for-purpose NAMs with no validation), UKCVAM ("ring trials or innovative ways of agreeing regulatory readiness"), the AI-NAM literature (a retraining model cannot be validated by an event), and Kopańska & Hartung's next-generation-validation framework (the ring trial is too slow and measures against the wrong reference).

ICCVAM is the fifth, it is the one with the authority, and it names the mechanism: nothing replaces it. The ring trial becomes conditional on the context of use, and the agency decides case by case. That is not a replacement instrument; it is the removal of a fixed one.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — this is not an assay. It is the axis that decides what an assay's evidence is worth, and it is scored off the ladder like the regulatory-acceptance framework maps.

Its consequence for the loop is nonetheless direct, and it is the opposite of the usual direction. Every other finding in this vault about autonomy adds a requirement. This one removes one, for exactly the two things a loop is made of. Nothing here says a robotic screen or an ML model is less trustworthy than a ring-trialled assay — it says the evidence that would settle the question may not be collected.

  • 2026-07-26 — ⚠ The automation exemption is CONTESTED, not settled — and the objection comes from industry. Status HELD at emerging; nothing demotes, but the vault's framing changes. Ingested Jacobs et al. 2024 (Arch Toxicol 98(7):2047-2063), published the same year as the ICCVAM framework and never citing it. (1) It argues the opposite conclusion: "Ring trials are indispensable to demonstrate the robustness and reproducibility of a new method", and on making them optional, "We explain why this is simply not an option." (2) It grants NO exemption to automated, high-throughput or machine-learning methods — the category ICCVAM's footnote 1 excuses appears in its abbreviation list and nowhere in its argument. (3) The receipts. Unlike the five framework papers in this thread, it argues from a case list: eight named methods that passed in the developing laboratory and failed on transfer, with causes that are not biological — a cell line's off-target glucocorticoid-receptor cross-talk (AR transactivation, fixed only by a knockout line); bacteriology dishes with different regional sterilisation (embryonic stem cell test); a transatlantic shipping delay correlating with invalid runs (EST-1000); an experienced lab's undeclared SOP modification (3T3 NRU, detected only because several labs ran it); MCF7 proliferation at 54% (14/26) between-lab agreement, discontinued after 2012. This is the technical case for why inter-laboratory reproducibility is the one rung on the vault's ladder that cannot be manufactured by better statistics — none of those causes varies inside a single laboratory. (4) The consequence for a loop is uncomfortable and nobody on either side has addressed it: a closed loop is one laboratory running very fast. It has one instrument configuration, one dish supplier, one shipping route, one local protocol. High internal reproducibility is what it optimises toward — and that is precisely the signal that looked "encouraging" in the ER CALUX lead lab before the method failed to transfer. A loop's consistency is not evidence that it is right; it is evidence that its unknown biases are stable. (5) Concession recorded: the authors accept reliability and relevance "could potentially be separated" and propose modernising ring-trial design from binary agreement to continuous-data comparison — a change to how reproducibility is measured, not whether. (6) ⚠ Bias flag pointing the other way, a first for this vault: five of seven authors work for Dow, Procter & Gamble, BASF and Givaudan — companies that pay for ring trials and would save money if they became optional. funding_flag: [industry], argued against short-term commercial interest.   • 2026-07-28 — A THIRD design arrives, and it is neither a ring trial nor an exemption from one. Status HELD at emerging. Ingested lafollette-2026-3rsc-fda-liver-mps-cross-platform-dili (Regul Toxicol Pharmacol 106189) — the 3Rs Collaborative / FDA-CDER evaluation of eight commercial liver microphysiological systems. This thread has held two positions on what validation should be: ICCVAM's context-of-use-conditional framework with interlaboratory evaluation "if needed", and Jacobs et al.'s insistence that ring trials are "indispensable". This is a third object. (1) It varies the METHOD and holds the QUESTION fixed — eight different technologies, one blinded compound set, one narrowly stated context of use — where a ring trial holds the method fixed and varies the laboratory. The deliverable is "category-level performance expectations rather than rank individual systems": a statement about what a method class is worth. The vault has no vocabulary for this and its evidence_tier ladder has no rung for it — the ladder grades a method, and this grades a category. → a-validation-that-grades-the-category-not-the-method (2) ⚠ It does NOT supply the inter-laboratory evidence, and it is important not to let it look like it does. Each platform is run by its own vendor in its own laboratory, so platform and laboratory are confounded and the observed variance cannot be decomposed. Every one of Jacobs et al.'s eight documented transfer failures is a failure this design would not detect, because nothing is transferred: a cell line's off-target receptor cross-talk, regionally different dish sterilisation, a transatlantic shipping delay, an undeclared SOP modification — all of them appeared only because a second laboratory ran the same method. → platform-and-laboratory-vary-together-in-a-cross-platform-trial (3) The mitigations are real and worth naming: blinded compounds paired as four high-risk drugs against four low-risk structural analogues (removing the easy win of noticing that toxic molecules look different), and compound selection, blinding and all statistics held by NIH-NIEHS/NICEATM — the same organisation ICCVAM's framework cites for coordinating the thyroid microtissue prevalidation this vault holds. The independent-analysis arrangement is exactly the data integrity and information transparency concept the ICCVAM framework names. (4) The uncomfortable read, recorded as a question rather than a finding: this is what validation looks like when the ring trial becomes conditional. Not the ICCVAM footnote's automation exemption — these are wet assays, run by hand — but the same underlying move, a bespoke design negotiated around a context of use, with the participants co-designing the study that judges them. funding_flag: [industry, method-developer].   • 2026-07-30 — ⚠️ THE BATTERY PROBLEM HAS A SECOND FAILURE MODE, AND IT RUNS OPPOSITE TO THE RAIL. Status HELD at emerging; nothing moves. Ingested landsiedel-2026-frustra-laborare-epistemic-limits (ATLA, DOI 10.1177/02611929261472447, PMID 42522211 — three BASF toxicologists; funding_flag: [industry], declared verbatim as "employees of BASF, a chemical company, which is using toxicity testing for commercial purposes", funding declared none, and two of the three co-authored Jacobs et al. 2024 already in this thread — a continuation of a position, not an independent second voice). (1) ⚠ The claim that matters: "Expanding test batteries without predefined integration rules inflates false-positive accumulation." This is structurally distinct from the correlated-blind-spot rail and the vault had been recording only the rail. The rail is about dependence — two assays failing on the same chemicals for the same reason cast one vote, and agreement is misread as corroboration, so false negatives survive. This is about size — even with perfectly independent assays, an any-positive convention gives every added test another chance to fire spuriously, so specificity decays as the battery grows and false positives accumulate. Opposite direction, same object, and the same missing thing fixes both: a published integration rule. The vault's two largest un-ruled batteries are the cases — developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb at 60% specificity (wrongly flags two safe chemicals in five) and eye-hazard-iata-gd263, integrated by unspecified weight of evidence. (2) The loop's experiment-selection criterion arrives from the regulatory side. "Value-of-information frameworks should guide whether tests meaningfully shift decisions" — an assay whose every outcome leads to the same action is worth nothing. That is expected information gain, the acquisition function of every Bayesian-optimisation-driven laboratory, in a paper that never mentions automation. The third documented convergence between the two literatures, after the autonomous-science roadmap independently deriving this vault's verification thesis (2026-07-28) and OECD naming conditional independence as a battery-design rule (2026-07-25). The criterion transfers; the inputs do not — expected information gain needs a quantified prior and a quantified decision consequence, and regulatory toxicology has neither in computable form. (3) Reproducibility named as the binding constraint, symmetrically — for animal and non-animal methods, which is the half the vault tends to leave out: the animal reference a new method is scored against is itself noisy. Consistent with this map's existing thread; adds no evidence to it. (4) "Exposure realism and toxicokinetic plausibility function as gatekeepers of relevance" — a gate applied before asking whether a dish result is real, and unusually for this vault a machine-checkable one. Worked empirically the same night in jackson-2026-dnt-ivb-contextualize-in-vivo-apical. evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic, strength: suggestive — a commentary: no data, no arithmetic, no case series.   • 2026-07-30 — ⚠️ THE VAULT'S domain_gate MODIFIER GETS A FORMAL DEFINITION, AND IT TURNS OUT TO BE HALF OF ONE. Status HELD at emerging; a schema gap opens. Ingested whaley-2026-external-validity-in-vitro-invites-ex (Regul. Toxicol. Pharmacol. 171:106185, PMID 42476442, DOI 10.1016/j.yrtph.2026.106185 — 18 authors spanning the Norwegian Institute of Public Health, Lancaster University, the Italian Istituto Superiore di Sanità, Radboud, the European Commission Joint Research Centre, the US NIEHS/NTP and Johns Hopkins (Hartung); funding_flag: none, one author declaring Horizon Europe PARC support). (1) External validity is two properties, not one. Applicability — does this study address the question asked? Per-study, checkable at screening. Generalisability — does the finding hold beyond the conditions tested? A property of the body of evidence, not assessable from one study by construction. Their recommendation follows: the forthcoming INVITES-EX appraisal tool should assess applicability only, because that is the half a per-study instrument can reach. (2) ⚠ Every domain_gate in this vault is applicability. TG 435's pH window, TG 249's log KOW bounds, TG 494's runnable pre-test, the DNT battery's biological gate — all per-chemical, per-study checks. The vault has no field for generalisability at all. (3) ⚠ And generalisability is precisely what a closed loop cannot produce. A loop is one laboratory running very fast: one instrument configuration, one reagent lot, one local protocol. It maximises internal consistency by construction and generates structurally zero evidence about generalisability — not weak evidence, none, because the property is defined across studies and a loop is one study repeated. This is the definitional reason for what Jacobs et al. showed empirically on 2026-07-26 from eight transfer failures — a loop's consistency is not evidence that it is right; it is evidence that its unknown biases are stable — and it supplies the vocabulary that observation was missing. evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic, regulatory_status: proposed, strength: suggestive — a conceptual framework paper: nothing measured, no instrument released, no inter-rater agreement.
