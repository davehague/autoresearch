Why this map exists, and why it is not the QC map

vaccine-qc-animal-free-batch-release holds a narrow, structurally easy question: per-lot, fixed-specification, pass/fail testing repeated for a product's commercial life. This map holds a different, harder question: whether the animal studies that decide if a vaccine candidate is safe enough to test in humans at all — and safe enough to keep testing through late development — can be replaced by non-animal methods that actually predict human-specific immune responses, which the field has long documented animal models handle poorly. mRNA and viral-vector platforms are named as the proximate driver: they are new enough that the safety-testing playbook built for older platforms does not obviously transfer.

What we believe now

The founding source organizes the whole pipeline into three stages where animals are still used:

  1. Early screening — candidate immunogenicity/toxicity triage, where in vitro and computational methods could substitute before an animal study is ever designed.
  2. Late nonclinical development — the GLP-style animal safety study immediately before first-in-human dosing. The source points specifically at virtual control groups here (generative models that synthesize a study's control arm from historical/treated-animal data rather than dosing a fresh control cohort) — a NAM category already tracked elsewhere in this vault (study-design-virtual-control-groups), where the standing finding is that it is Reduction, not Replacement: the input is still a live animal experiment.
  3. Batch release — potency, pyrogenicity, adventitious-agent testing. The same three sub-endpoints vaccine-qc-animal-free-batch-release already tracks, confirmed as the only one of the three stages where this vault already has method-level detail (the Monocyte Activation Test for pyrogenicity).

No performance number, no named decision rule, and no single method is singled out as ready for stages 1 or 2 — the paper is explicit that it draws on stakeholder discussion, not a systematic evidence review. 3Rs refinement (group housing, humane endpoints, pain management, scaled blood sampling) is given equal billing with replacement, a reminder that even this roadmap's best case keeps animals in the loop for some fraction of development.
