Tri-culture heart-on-a-chip (iPSC cardiomyocyte + fibroblast + endothelium) — cardiotoxicity — method

🚩 NEW MAP, auto-coined 2026-09-14, inside the existing cardiotoxicity endpoint group (opened 2026-07-26 by cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia). A genuinely different method from that map: a multi-cell-type microfluidic organ-chip reading contractility, not a single-cell-type multielectrode array reading electrophysiology through an AI classifier. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-09-14). One laboratory (IDIBELL/CIBER-BBN, Barcelona), two compounds, no interlaboratory replication, no reference-compound panel with known clinical cardiotoxicity outcomes. evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory. regulatory_status: none — the paper cites CiPA as the existing standard it aims to extend beyond, but makes no claim of ICH S7B or FDA/EMA alignment for this specific platform.

What we believe now

The platform. Three cell types — cardiomyocytes, cardiac fibroblasts, and endothelial cells — all differentiated from a single human iPSC line (fully isogenic, removing donor variability as a confound), cultured in a four-chamber microfluidic chip. Cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts sit on one side of a porous membrane; endothelial cells line the other side under continuous perfusion, mimicking coronary drug delivery. Contractile kinetics (beating rate, contraction duration, relaxation time, sarcomere length) are read by video microscopy and two open-source analysis tools (OpenHeartWare; ImageJ's MuscleMotion).

The finding. Isoproterenol (a β-agonist) produced the same chronotropic response with or without the endothelial layer, confirming the small molecule crosses the barrier freely. Doxorubicin did not. Cardiomyocytes cultured with fibroblasts alone lost 72% of their beating rate and showed sarcomere disarray under doxorubicin; the same cardiomyocytes, cultured with the added endothelial layer, kept normal contractile function and sarcomere organization — even though the endothelial cells themselves showed barrier damage (VE-cadherin disassembly). The endothelial layer appears to absorb the drug's toxicity at its own cost, hiding the cardiomyocyte-level effect a simpler two-cell model would have measured.

Why this matters beyond one drug. If an endothelial layer systematically protects (and masks damage to) cardiomyocytes for a class of compounds, then any cardiotoxicity NAM that omits it — including cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia's single-cell-type MEA platform — could be reading a false negative for exactly the compounds this platform would flag as harmful to the vascular interface, and conversely a model trained without an endothelial arm has no way to learn this masking effect exists. This is a single-compound demonstration, not a general rule, and the vault does not extend it further than that.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop. The readout itself is a good candidate for automation — video microscopy plus open-source kinematic analysis is already computational, and contractility metrics are exactly the kind of instrument-readable signal this vault grades favourably elsewhere. The blocker is not the instrument, it is the missing rule downstream of it: no numeric threshold exists to convert "72% reduction in beating rate" into a cardiotoxic/non-cardiotoxic call, so a human judges the magnitude. This is the same shape of blocker automation-tox21-qhts-platform has held since 2013 — the robotics/imaging half is finished before the interpretation half is written — arriving again in a different organ and a different instrument class.
