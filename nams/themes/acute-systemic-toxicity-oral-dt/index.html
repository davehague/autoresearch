Acute oral toxicity — the AFSA decision tree — method map (a proposed strategy, not an assay)

🚩 AUTO-COINED 2026-08-21 — flagged for human review. The vault's 92nd map, opening a new endpoint group (acute-systemic-toxicity) that had no entry at all. It is a slightly awkward fit for a "method" map, because the object is a decision strategy rather than an assay — the same shape as eye-hazard-iata-gd263 and skin-sensitization-2o3-defined-approach, both of which are also integration strategies over other people's tests, so the precedent exists. Status: emerging (2026-08-21). evidence_tier: in-silico-mechanistic, regulatory_status: proposed, loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop. One source, and it is a proposal with no performance data of any kind — no reference chemicals, no concordance against rodent hazard classifications, no sensitivity, no specificity, no false-negative rate. Nothing here is validated and nothing is adopted.

What the endpoint is

Acute oral toxicity asks the field's crudest question: swallow this once, in quantity — does it kill you, and roughly at what dose? The regulatory answer has historically been a rodent LD50 (the single dose that kills half the animals dosed) or one of its refined successors that use fewer animals to sort a chemical into a hazard band. It drives labelling, transport classification and worker protection, and it remains one of the highest-volume animal tests still running.

Two properties make it awkward for everything this vault does:

  1. The answer key is itself an animal test. No human acute-lethality dataset exists to validate against. A non-animal method here is graded on whether it reproduces the rodent study's classification — the comparator problem the vault records at nam-variance-judged-against-animal-test-variance, arriving in its purest form.
  2. It is a systemic, whole-body, time-course endpoint — the exact class the vault's monthly coverage sweep found to be uniformly unsolved. Everything past emerging in this vault is an endpoint where the chemical acts locally, on contact, and fast.

What we believe now

The Animal-Free Safety Assessment (AFSA) Collaboration — an advocacy-plus-industry-plus-contract-lab consortium — has published a decision tree aligned to the OECD's Integrated Approach to Testing and Assessment (IATA) framework: a "transparent, modular workflow integrating in silico tools, in vitro assays, and WoE strategies" for acute oral hazard identification, intended to support decisions under the EU's REACH chemicals regulation and "ensur[e] that animal testing is used only as a last resort."

Its stated target is procedural, not scientific: regulatory uptake of existing non-animal methods is inconsistent "also due to a lack of structured guidance," and the tree is the structure. It is aimed at "inform[ing] regulatory guidance updates."

What it is not. Not adopted, not validated, not evaluated. One OECD staff member is a co-author; that is not OECD adoption and must not be read as one.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop, and the reason is a single clause in the source's own abstract: "IATA integrate existing information in a Weight of Evidence (WoE) assessment."

The vault's ladder is deterministic and step 3 catches this before step 4 or 5 can: human judgement is load-bearing in the call. Not in the measurements — every in-silico tool and in-vitro assay the tree can call is machine-runnable — but in the step that converts the collected picture into a hazard classification.

This is the eye-hazard-iata-gd263 pattern exactly: nine adopted eye tests, every one of them producing a number a machine can read, assembled by a weight-of-evidence step, and therefore not machine-runnable as a strategy. Every component is loopable and the strategy is not.

Why this matters more than a single map. The vault's dominant blocker is "no agreed data-interpretation procedure exists," recorded on twenty-plus maps, and the implied hope has been that guidelines will eventually write those procedures down. Here is the field writing one down for a whole systemic endpoint — and what it wrote down is where to think, not what to conclude. That is a data point about the direction of travel, and it is the one this map exists to carry.
