Human recombinant ER binding (OECD TG 493) — endocrine, the "E" read one layer earlier — method

Status: consensus (2026-07-12) — regulatorily adopted on both axes. evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 493, a Performance-Based Test Guideline; adopted 18 July 2018, corrected 25 June 2024). funding_flag: none — the vault's only adopted endocrine method with no commercial licensing gate.

What we believe now

TG 493 is the vault's first cell-free receptor method and its first method that reads a molecular interaction rather than a biological response. No cells, no transcription, no reporter — recombinant human ERα protein, a radiolabelled reference ligand, and a competition.

The measurement. The assay "measures the ability of a radiolabeled ligand ([3H]17β-estradiol) to bind with the ER in the presence of increasing concentrations of a test chemical (i.e. competitor)." Two components: a saturation binding experiment (characterising the receptor preparation and documenting ER specificity) and a competitive binding experiment (the test). Output: a displacement curve → log(IC50) and a relative binding affinity (RBA) expressed against 17β-estradiol. Two reference methods — Freyberger-Wilson (FW) (full-length hrERα) and CERI (ligand-binding domain only).

Binding is not activation — and that is why this method exists. A chemical that binds ERα may activate it (agonist), block it (antagonist), or occupy it with no transcriptional consequence at all. TG 493 cannot distinguish these. It reports only that the chemical reaches the ligand-binding site. TG 455 reports what the receptor then does. Neither subsumes the other, and the OECD Conceptual Framework carries both at Level 2 for that reason.

The admission — an adopted assay with an unadopted decision rule. Paragraph 21, verbatim:

"There is currently no universally agreed method for interpreting ER binding data. However, both qualitative (e.g. binder/non-binder) and/or quantitative (e.g. log IC50, Relative Binding Affinity (RBA), etc.) assessments of hrER-mediated activity should be based on empirical data and sound scientific judgment."

The guideline standardises how to run the assay in exhaustive procedural detail, then declines to say what the result means. Two laboratories can execute TG 493 perfectly, agree on the log(IC50) to the decimal, and disagree on whether the chemical is an ER binder — because no threshold exists and the call is handed to "sound scientific judgment."

Contrast TG 455, same receptor, same programme, which ships an explicit table (positive if RPC_Max ≥ 10% of the positive control in 2-of-2 or 2-of-3 runs). And contrast GL 497, where the adopted object is a data-interpretation procedure. TG 493 is the negative case that completes that pattern: what is adopted here is a measurement, not a call.

No sensitivity/specificity against a reference chemical set appears in the guideline text reached. Recorded as a gap, not filled.

A distinct accessibility gate. TG 493 requires [3H]17β-estradiol — a radioisotope, and therefore a radiochemistry permit. Not a commercial licence (HepaRG™, Corrositex®, Derek Nexus), not an unobtainable cell line (TG 455's VM7Luc): a third species of the practical-accessibility constraint, and the one that most cleanly separates "can afford it" from "is licensed to handle it."

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — The cleanest case on the axis. log(IC50) and RBA are fully quantitative and instrument-read — and the assay is still not loopable, because TG 493 itself says there is no agreed way to interpret the numbers. A machine can measure this perfectly and still not know what it means. Quantitative readout does not equal a decision rule; this map is the proof.

  • Blocker: the guideline states outright that no universally agreed method exists for interpreting ER binding data
  • Access gate: requires a radioisotope handling permit — bears on whether the loop can be stood up outside the lab that built it.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Composes-with endocrine-er-transactivation (TG 455) — NOT redundant with it, and the distinction is load-bearing. Superficially two adopted methods "for the estrogen receptor." In fact they read adjacent nodes of one pathway: ligand→receptor (TG 493), then receptor→DNA→transcription (TG 455). The redundancy-hunting arm of §6.5 would flag these as overlapping; the correct verdict is composition, and the correct warning is the inverse of a redundancy warning — a TG 493 positive is not an ER-activity finding, and reading it as one over-calls every silent binder as an endocrine active. This is the first time the connect layer has caught a false-redundancy: two methods that look duplicative and are not.
  • The crosstalk confound does NOT reach this map — and that is informative. steroid-receptor-crosstalk-shared-reporter-confound afflicts the reporter-gene assays (TG 455, TG 458, TG 454A) because a reporter downstream of a shared response element cannot tell which receptor fired it. TG 493 is cell-free and reporter-free: it measures displacement from a purified ERα protein, so there is no other receptor in the tube to cross-react. The binding assay is structurally immune to the confound that afflicts its transactivation siblings. That makes TG 493 an unusually valuable independent vote in an endocrine weight-of-evidence assessment — precisely because it shares no machinery with the others. A genuine, and rare, piece of good news for battery independence.
