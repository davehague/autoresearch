Self-driving labs — the loop layer itself — loop-infrastructure map

🚩 CONVENTION DEPARTURE — flagged for human review. Not a method map, not a framework map — an infrastructure map of the loop machinery this vault's whole question is about. Its endpoint (loop-infrastructure) is not a toxicological endpoint. The vault's fourth non-method map, and the third auto-coined in three runs. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-07-13). The sole source is a peer-reviewed perspective — no primary data — so every concept is strength: suggestive. evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method records only the standing of the sourcing instrument; the vault's tier ladder has no rung for a review or perspective, and that schema gap is now logged twice (here, and for draft regulatory instruments on 2026-07-12).

Why this map exists

The vault has spent 45 sources asking "could a machine run this assay?" — and had never ingested a single source about the machines. stages/01-sweep says the crossover between the two literatures is the highest-value find available. This is the first source that sits in both.

And it crosses in the unexpected direction. It is not an AI researcher discovering toxicology. It is Thomas Hartung — former Head of ECVAM, the EU's method-validation body, now Director of Johns Hopkins' Center for Alternatives to Animal Testing — writing about agentic AI, in a venue for AI researchers.

What we believe now

Self-driving labs work. In chemistry.

A self-driving lab (SDL) is the closed loop this vault's question names: a model proposes an experiment, robots run it, results return, the model updates, and it picks the next one — autonomy in the loop, not in any single step.

Chemistry and materials — loops that ran and produced things:

  • ChemCrow — "a fully autonomous synthesis of twenty-nine organosilicon compounds, eight of which were previously unknown."
  • A-Lab — autonomous solid-state synthesis of inorganic powders.

Life sciences — architectures, in the subjunctive:

  • Organ-on-chip + high-content imaging + deep-learning analytics.
  • iPSC-derived organoid concentration–response feeding a Bayesian optimiser that picks the next dosing schedule.

Read the grammar. The chemistry entries are things that happened. The biology entries are things that could be assembled. No closed loop on a regulatory toxicology assay is cited — because there isn't one.

The vault's central question is genuinely open. It is not a question the literature answered somewhere the vault hadn't looked. Until tonight the vault was asserting that; now it is sourced — and sourced from the person with the strongest incentive to claim otherwise, who does not.

The 5% — the number to keep

DeepMind's GNoME proposed 380,000 stable crystal structures. Fewer than 5% have been independently experimentally validated.

That is the field's own measurement of the gap between what a generative loop proposes and what the world confirms — from the domain where SDLs work best, where ground truth is a crystal you can grow or not. Toxicology's ground truth is human harm, and it is enormously harder to check.

A loop that proposes confidently and validates rarely is not a discovery engine — it is a hypothesis firehose. In materials, the cost of a wrong confident answer is a crystal that fails to grow. In toxicology it is a chemical called safe.

The bottlenecks — and they are not the robots

  • Data, not hardware: "many 'FAIR' datasets are discoverable only through bespoke portals or lack harmonized ontologies." The loop's constraint is the input contract.
  • Amplification: "opaque AI pipelines risk magnifying the [reproducibility] problem." Automation does not average out a field's errors — it scales them.
  • Agenda drift (the one he takes most seriously): "research agendas may gradually shift from curiosity-driven exploration toward problems that are computationally convenient."
  • Regulatory friction: "AI can shave years off pre-clinical optimization, but agencies still require explainability dossiers that reconcile black-box embeddings with pharmacological intuition."

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — this map is the loop. It is what the third axis is measured against, not a thing the third axis grades. That the vault's ladder has no rung for the loop machinery itself is a schema gap, logged for a human.

  • 2026-07-22 — The part of the loop this map never measured, measured — and it is the weakest. Ingested Liu et al. 2026 ("Pipette", arXiv 2606.12936, preprint, simulation only), a 12-task benchmark for general wet-lab robot manipulation: sample handling, culture-ware, device operation, precision placement. With 30 demonstrations per task, general robot-learning policies score 37–72% task success (ACT 60.3%; SmolVLA 40.4%→71.8% with augmentation; pi0 37.3%→44.1%). This map's inventory of the loop has been mind (Robin) / hands-at-scale (Tox21) / wire (LAP) / executor (ProtoPilot) / ledger (ToxMCP) — every one of them software or fixed-purpose automation. The moment an AI wants a different vessel or an unplanned step it needs general manipulation, and general manipulation is at 44–72% in a simulator with no surface tension, no condensation and no transparent labware.   • 2026-07-26 — ⚠ The most complete closed loop the vault holds — and it is the clearest evidence yet that the blocker is the objective function, not the robot. Ingested Smith et al. 2026 (bioRxiv 2026.02.05.703998; OpenAI + Ginkgo Bioworks). GPT-5 designed, a robotic cloud lab executed, and the results fed back: 36,000 reaction compositions, 580+ plates, ~150,000 datapoints, six months, starting zero-shot. Result: cell-free protein synthesis at $422/g against a $698/g published state of the art (40% cheaper) with titer up 27% in tubes and 188% in plates. (1) Why it worked, and this is the finding: the objective was a scalar the instrument reports — dollars per gram, with titer read as fluorescence off a plate reader. No interpretation step, no external authority, ground truth in every well. Regulatory toxicology has no such number: its objective is a classification defined by a committee, whose reference answer lives in a validation report the loop cannot regenerate. This reframes the vault's largest blocker category (15 maps, "no agreed decision rule") from a paperwork problem into the loop's missing reward signal. (2) First measured execution-error rate from production: 2 of 480 AI-designed plates (<1%) had fundamental design flaws — a constraint override and a nanolitre-to-microlitre conversion divided by 10⁶ — and both were caught after execution, because the failure was loud. A hazard-testing loop's equivalent silent failure produces a plausible negative, not a blank plate. Partly answers the execution-reliability schema gap logged 2026-07-22; the gap stays open. (3) What made it safe was a hand-written Pydantic type schema plus hand-written protocols — the authors state they deliberately did not let the model write the protocols. One of the two failures was fixed by editing the schema, not the model. A schema can encode physically executable; it cannot encode scientifically meaningful (the glucose-and-ribose plate was schema-valid). loop_readiness: not-applicable unchanged; Status held at emerging — a non-peer-reviewed preprint written and funded by the two companies selling both halves of the system, funding_flag: [industry, method-developer], the vault's maximum.
