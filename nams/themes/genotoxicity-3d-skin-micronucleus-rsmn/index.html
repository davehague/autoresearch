3D reconstructed human skin micronucleus assay (RSMN) — genotoxicity — method

What we believe now

What a micronucleus is. When a cell divides badly, a fragment of a broken chromosome — or an entire chromosome that failed to travel — can be left stranded outside the main nucleus in its own small membrane. That is a micronucleus, and counting them is one of the field's core measures of chromosome-level genetic damage. The standard cell-culture version is OECD Test Guideline 487 (genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus).

What this version changes. It runs the count inside reconstructed three-dimensional human skin (MatTek EpiDerm) with the chemical applied topically. The tissue supplies its own metabolism, so unlike the standard assay it does not need an external rat-liver enzyme preparation ("S9 mix") added to activate chemicals that only become dangerous after processing. Its declared job, like its companion, is following up a positive from the standard battery for a chemical people put on their skin.

How well it works.

Result
Accuracy against in-vivo genotoxicity outcomes80%
Sensitivity — catches real genotoxicants75%
Specificity — correctly clears safe chemicals84%
Within-laboratory reproducibility84%
Between-laboratory reproducibility77%

Plainly: it catches about three in four chemicals that really do damage chromosomes, and wrongly flags about one harmless chemical in six. The 43 coded chemicals deliberately included misleading positives — chemicals that flag in the standard battery but are not genotoxic in an animal — which is exactly the population the assay exists to sort out.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop — and it is the vault's cleanest demonstration that the two halves of "could a machine run this?" are separate questions.

  • The decision rule is fixed and published. A chemical is positive when "there were two or more concentrations of the test chemical that produced statistically significant increases in the % MN-BN outside the upper 95% control limit"; a lone top-dose positive requires independent confirmation. No expert weight of evidence, no committee, no unwritten judgement. Three lines of code could apply this. That is the property twenty-three maps in this vault are blocked for lacking.
  • The measurement is a person. A trained scorer examines 500 cells per tissue to establish what fraction have divided, then 1,000 of those divided cells per tissue to count how many carry a micronucleus. Morphological judgement, down a microscope, per tissue, across four laboratories and 43 chemicals.
  • So the ladder's step 3 fires before step 5 can be considered. Human judgement is load-bearing in producing the number, therefore human-in-the-loop — regardless of how good the rule downstream is. → a-fixed-statistical-rule-behind-a-human-microscope

The escape hatch is plausible and undemonstrated. The vault holds the field's strongest automated micronucleus scorer — imaging flow cytometry plus a deep-learning classifier, which scored an unseen third laboratory and reproduced the human benchmark dose (Wills et al. 2021, on genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus). That work is on the standard suspension-culture assay. Scoring micronuclei in cells recovered from a three-dimensional tissue is a different imaging problem, and nothing in this vault demonstrates the transfer. Recorded as an open question, not counted as a pending promotion.

⚠ And note what this does to the genotoxicity column's leverage calculation. The 2026-08-01 monthly connect concluded that automated micronucleus scoring is the highest-leverage single fix in this column. There are now three eye-scored micronucleus assays in the vault: TG 487, genotoxicity-buccal-micronucleus-cytome, and this one. The leverage went up; the demonstrated escape hatch still covers only the first.

⚠ Correlated blind spot — with its own companion

correlated_blind_spot: [genotoxicity-3d-skin-comet]. See two-skin-genotox-assays-that-fail-on-the-same-chemistry. Different biology read (chromosome damage here, DNA strand breaks there), same tissue, same metabolic gap: this assay's named false negatives are 2-AAF and DMBA, its companion's are IQ and 4-chloroaniline, and both papers attribute them to skin's enzyme complement differing from liver's. Two concordant negatives from this pair on a bioactivation-dependent chemical are one vote, not two — and an automated system reads the concordance as confirmation and iterates on it.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Composes-with, downstream: this is a follow-up test for positives arising in genotoxicity-ames-bacterial-reverse-mutation and genotoxicity-mammalian-gene-mutation, and it addresses the same "misleading positive" problem that genotoxicity-tgx-ddi-transcriptomic-biomarker targets from a completely different direction — TGx-DDI reads a 64-gene expression signature and arrived closed-loop-ready; this reads morphology by eye. Two routes to the same job, at opposite ends of the automation ladder, and the loop-ready one is adopted nowhere while this one is adopted nowhere either.
  • Duplicate-check against genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus: not a duplicate. Same biological event counted, different test system (3D tissue vs suspension culture), different exposure route (topical vs direct), different metabolic arrangement (tissue-intrinsic vs added S9), different regulatory standing (adopted TG vs no guideline), and a different role in the assessment (primary battery member vs dermal follow-up). Cross-linked in both directions.
  • A third row for the vault's "adopted/validated but eye-scored" pattern. With TG 487 and the buccal cytome assay, the genotoxicity endpoint now holds three separate methods whose science is respected and whose readout is a trained human. That is not a coincidence of this endpoint — it is what counting rare morphological events costs, and it is the clearest single argument in this vault for why validating an automated scorer would move more maps than any other available intervention.
