In-vitro micronucleus test (OECD TG 487) — genotoxicity — method

Status: consensus (2026-07-06) — regulatorily adopted on both axes. evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 487). The vault's first entry for the genotoxicity / mutagenicity endpoint, previously a headline coverage gap.

What we believe now

The in-vitro mammalian cell micronucleus test applies a chemical (± S9) to cultured mammalian cells and scores micronuclei in divided interphase cells, detecting both clastogenic (acentric-fragment) and aneugenic (whole-chromosome) events in one assay. Adopted as OECD TG 487 (2010; revised July 2023), ECVAM/ESAC-endorsed. It is a core member of the standard in-vitro genotoxicity battery (with the Ames test TG 471 and gene-mutation assays) and reduces reliance on the in-vivo micronucleus test (TG 474). Scope caveats are the field's: no single genotox assay is sufficient alone, and in-vitro genotox tests carry a known false-positive tendency that follow-up resolves.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop — The vault's sharpest axis-disagreement case: an adopted OECD Test Guideline (consensus, OECD-TG-adopted) that is nonetheless un-loopable, because the readout is morphological scoring by a human. Regulatory acceptance says nothing about automatability. The escape hatch is machine vision, and the sweep watches for it — and on 2026-07-16 it landed here, harder than anywhere else in the vault.

  • Blocker: micronuclei are counted by a trained eye down a microscope.
  • Escape hatch — demonstrated, not yet adopted (2026-07-16). Wills et al. 2021 automate the scoring with imaging flow cytometry (cells photographed one at a time in flowing suspension) + a deep-learning classifier (DeepFlow). Trained on two labs, it scored an unseen third lab without reconfiguration (85% on the micronucleus-positive class) and reproduced the human benchmark dose — the number that drives the safety call. This is a rung stronger than the zebrafish demonstration (developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity): inter-laboratory and regulatory-answer-matching, not single-lab and slower-than-human. The map still does not move to loop-ready, and the reasons are recorded: the scorer is (1) not part of the guideline (regulatory_status: none for the automation itself — never inferred from the adopted assay), (2) a single peer-reviewed method with no ECVAM/ICCVAM ring trial, (3) access_gated to the Amnis ImageStream instrument and co-authored by that instrument's maker (funding_flag: [industry]), and (4) still ~25% wrong on the key micronucleus-positive class, needing ~19,000 human-labelled cells to train. A trained human remains load-bearing in the regulatory call.
  • Next promotion path. A validated automated scorer — an independent ring trial, or adoption of the automated readout into the guideline — would move this map human-in-the-loop → automatable-open-loop. As of 2026-07-17 all three of the vault's eye-scored human-in-the-loop maps carry a demonstrated escape hatch (developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity since 2026-07-15, carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay since 2026-07-17). This one remains the strongest — inter-lab and benchmark-dose-matching — but none is validated, so the promotion is still open.

  • Composition with carcinogenicity: the genotox battery covers genotoxic carcinogens; the non-genotoxic ones are the domain of carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay — complementary, not redundant.
  • Loop-move edge (added 2026-07-16, completed 2026-07-17): the machine-vision escape hatch that Wills et al. demonstrate here is the same pattern the vault first saw in developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity (Sivaprasad 2026, zebrafish malformation scoring) and now, as of 2026-07-17, in carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay (Masumoto 2021, a CNN scoring transformed foci). All three eye-scored human-in-the-loop maps now carry a demonstrated escape hatch — they share one blocker (a human scoring morphology) and therefore one class of fix (a validated automated scorer), an infrastructure link, not a battery-composition link. The micronucleus case is the strongest of the three: inter-lab and benchmark-dose-matching, vs zebrafish (single-lab, direct malformation scoring) and cell transformation (single-lab, foci classification only). None is validated.
  • Global-ripple: distinct endpoint/mechanism from the skin/eye local-toxicity maps and the systemic maps (DNT, hepatotox, repeat-dose) — no confirm/refute edge; a coverage-breadth addition within the genotoxicity endpoint group.
