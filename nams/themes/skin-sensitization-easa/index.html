EASA — electrophilic allergen screening assay (skin sensitisation KE1 variant) — method

🚩 AUTO-COINED 2026-10-08, flagged for human review — a fourth named KE1-slot variant under the skin-sensitisation endpoint, alongside skin-sensitization-dpra (base HPLC method), ADRA (open gap, no map yet) and skin-sensitization-spectro-dpra (spectrophotometric DPRA modification). Bar applied: a distinct probe chemistry (nitrobenzenethiol/pyridoxylamine vs DPRA's cysteine/lysine peptides), its own formal 4-laboratory federal validation record, and its own performance numbers — the same bar this vault already applied to Spectro-DPRA. Git is the undo.

Status: consolidating (2026-10-08) — evidence_tier: formally-validated (4-laboratory federal/DoD interagency validation with transferability, proficiency, within- and between-laboratory reproducibility), regulatory_status: under-validation (proposed for OECD TG 442C inclusion and GL 497 evaluation, not yet adopted).

What we believe now

EASA reads skin-sensitisation key-event-1 (covalent small-molecule binding to skin proteins) via plate-reader probe depletion — absorbance (4-nitrobenzenethiol, NBT) or fluorescence (pyridoxylamine, PDA) — rather than the adopted DPRA/ADRA family's HPLC peptide-depletion readout. A 4-laboratory federal interagency validation (CPSC/NIST, FDA/CDRH, Defense Centers for Public Health-Aberdeen, Burleson Research Technologies), run under the OECD's own "similar or modified method" process (TG 442C Performance Standards No. 303), found within-laboratory reproducibility 96% and between-laboratory reproducibility 85% (both exceeding the 80% requirement), and accuracy 83% / sensitivity 87% / specificity 76% against the mouse Local Lymph Node Assay — specificity falling short of the Performance Standards' own 80% bar, accepted by the Validation Management Team given a small, domain-constrained negative-chemical set. Practical advantage over the adopted KE1 family: up to 7 chemicals per run (vs 2 for DPRA/ADRA, 1 for kDPRA) and under an hour of instrument time (vs up to 54 hours setup-to-read), on a standard plate reader rather than HPLC. Shared limitation, stated by the source itself: like DPRA, ADRA and kDPRA, EASA has no metabolic capacity to detect pro-haptens — chemicals that require bioactivation before becoming reactive. A separate, earlier developmental comparison (92 chemicals, cited but not re-verified in this validation) found EASA's specificity collapses to 27% against human patch-test reference data even though it holds near parity with DPRA against the animal (LLNA) reference — a divergence this vault logs as an open question, not a failed validation (see the concept note below).

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready — Plate-reader absorbance/fluorescence is fully instrument-readable, and the hazard call (significant probe depletion against fixed, pre-calibrated interquartile-range quality-control bands) is deterministic arithmetic — the same class of chemistry-not-biology readout as DPRA and Spectro-DPRA. This is a readout-mechanism judgment, not an endorsement of regulatory readiness: EASA has not yet cleared OECD inclusion in TG 442C or evaluation in GL 497, which is a regulatory_status fact, orthogonal to whether the readout itself is machine-executable.

  • Blocker: none, on the readout/decision-rule axis. The open item is regulatory acceptance, not technical readiness.
