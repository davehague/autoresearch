Zebrafish behavioural assessment (Light–Dark Transition Test) — developmental neurotoxicity — method

🚩 NEW MAP, auto-coined 2026-07-27. The vault's developmental-neurotoxicity column held two maps, both cell-based: the 17-assay in-vitro battery and the neurosphere assay forked out of it on 2026-07-26. This is the column's first whole-organism method, and it arrives with something neither of the others has — multi-laboratory reproducibility data. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: consolidating (2026-07-27). evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method, regulatory_status: none.

Four independent laboratories reproduced the method on a shared compound set with a defined applicability statement and no live rebuttal — which is the vault's definition of consolidating, applied deterministically. It is explicitly not formally-validated: no validation body managed the study, no blinding is stated, and the protocol is described as "agreed-upon protocol elements" rather than a harmonised one. The vault downgraded developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb on 2026-07-20 precisely for inferring validation from multi-party standardisation, and it will not make that inference in the other direction here.

What we believe now

The method. Zebrafish larvae are raised in the wells of a multi-well plate and exposed to a chemical during development. On a fixed schedule the lights go on and off. A healthy larva responds to darkness with a burst of swimming; a larva whose developing nervous system has been damaged responds differently. A camera tracks each larva's position and converts it into distance moved per time interval. That is the Light–Dark Transition Test (LDTT).

The evaluation. Four laboratories, 33 compounds — 28 suspected developmental neurotoxicants (organophosphate and pyrethroid insecticides including deltamethrin and parathion; flame retardants TDCIPP, TMPP and BPDP; neonicotinoids; nicotine) and 5 expected negatives.

The headline the abstract does not lead with is the pair 84–100% versus 68–84%. Same fish, same plates, same runs — and reproducibility depends on which question is asked of the data. Separating "this animal's nervous system is damaged" from "this animal is sick" requires choosing a concentration window where the larva is alive and functional but behaving differently, and where that window starts is a judgement. (one-plate-two-questions-two-reproducibilities.)

And this vault does not report "detects 100% of DNT-negative compounds" without its denominator. Five negatives is not a specificity claim.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — machine-read throughout the measurement, no rule at the end.

  • The readout is unambiguously machine-readable. A video tracker sampling position over time. No morphology, no microscope, no scorer, no classifier needed. This is a stronger readout position than most of the vault's automatable-open-loop members.
  • The blocker is the rule, and this map is the vault's best evidence that the rule is genuinely hard. Elsewhere the vault describes the missing decision rule as "nobody has published a rule for turning the number into a call" — which sounds like unfinished paperwork. Here, four expert laboratories given the same numbers landed on different calls for up to a third of chemicals. Writing down whichever rule one of them used would produce a reproducible loop that reproduced one laboratory's judgement, not the field's. That is a different and more expensive problem than a missing document.
  • ⚠ Same fish, same plate, different tier. developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity uses zebrafish embryos of comparable age in comparable plates and is human-in-the-loop, because its readout is a person grading malformation types down a microscope. Behaviour is a measurement; malformation is a taxonomy rendered by a trained eye. This is the cleanest demonstration in the vault of shared/evidence-model.md's rule that "readout objectivity drives the tier, never assay format" — cleaner than the existing FET comparison, because these two assays are frequently the same experiment. (same-fish-different-loop-tier.) The practical corollary: the unit that carries loop_readiness is the readout, not the test system.
  • A refusal rule, and the vault's second. The study's "novel three-tiered quality control framework" removed 10.9% of plates passing basic viability criteria — a mechanical rule for discarding data before interpreting it. OECD TG 494's pre-test was the first (eye-irritation-vitrigel-tg494), and it refuses chemicals; this refuses runs. A real loop needs both. (a-published-rule-for-throwing-plates-away.) Its three tiers are not recorded here — the full text was behind a subscription and the vault does not invent the contents of a framework it has not read.
  • And the refusal has an unreported cost, as refusals always do. The 93% detection figure is measured on the 89.1% of plates that survived quality control. Exactly the arithmetic the vault named for TG 494, whose headline 96% catch rate is measured only on the 68% of chemicals it accepts.

automatable-open-loop is not an endorsement. It says a machine can take this measurement. It does not say the measurement is a good proxy for human developmental neurotoxicity, and the source does not claim it is.

⚠ Correlated blind spot — flagged as a VAULT INFERENCE

correlated_blind_spot: [fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet, developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity].

The source does not say this. It is the vault applying the rail to a battery the source proposes.

The paper positions this assay as tier one, feeding developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb and then mammalian studies. A tiered strategy is a battery with an order. Ask the rail's question of it: do the tiers fail on the same chemicals for the same structural reason?

  • Within the zebrafish column, this assay, developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity and fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet are the same organism at similar life stages, sharing limited xenobiotic metabolism and chemical-uptake constraints. The vault already records that shared limitation as the documented cause of FET's existing rail row with ecotox-rtgill-w1-fish-cell-line ("no nervous system, no metabolism" — the nervous-system half does not apply here, the metabolism half does). A compound that only becomes neurotoxic after metabolic activation can read clean in all three, for one reason.
  • Across the tiers it is worse, and the reason is chaining. The in-vitro battery is human stem-cell-derived neural cells with no liver and no systemic metabolism either. A tier-one whole-organism screen with limited metabolism handing off to a tier-two cell battery with none is not two independent looks at bioactivation — it is the same gap twice, and the first tier gates the second. A negative at tier one means tier two is never run. That is the compounding shape lcl-metabolic-gap-multiplies-rather-than-repeats describes, applied to a screening cascade: an automated tiered strategy trusting a tier-one negative does not merely repeat the blind spot, it acts on it by declining to look further.

Nothing demotes on this inference. It is recorded as an inference, on both this map and the FET map, for a human to confirm or reject.

  • 2026-08-15 — The shared input step gets measured, and it is unreliable in a way this map's own findings predict. Status HELD at consolidating; loop_readiness HELD at automatable-open-loop. Every plate of this four-laboratory behavioural test starts with correctly-staged, viable larvae. Ingested al-absi-2026-zebrafish-automated-embryo-sorting (Biology 15:1237), which benchmarked a commercial AI embryo sorter over 117,956 embryos: 13.41% systematic undercount (p = 0.004), quality classes uncorrelated with survival, hatching and deformity (p > 0.05), 50.8% false negatives on fluorescence sorting. New map: automation-specimen-handling. Why this map should care more than the other two zebrafish maps. Its central finding is that four expert laboratories with a fully machine-read readout still produced different developmental-neurotoxicity calls on up to a third of chemicals (68–84% concordance) while agreeing 84–100% on whether the larva was harmed at all. That gap is about the judgement. Tonight's source adds a second, humbler channel of between-site variance sitting upstream of the judgement: how many larvae were actually in the well, and whether the ones excluded as non-viable were non-viable. Neither the concordance study nor this map's domain_gate accounts for it, and no published protocol element in the "agreed-upon protocol elements" covers specimen counting. No tier move, no Status move, no new correlated_blind_spot row. The finding is upstream of what the loop axis grades. Recorded as a ripple, and as a reminder that this map's honest reading of its own reproducibility numbers may be a slight over-estimate of how comparable the four laboratories' inputs were.
