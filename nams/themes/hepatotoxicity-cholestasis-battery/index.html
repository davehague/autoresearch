Cholestasis in-vitro / in-silico test battery (ONTOX) — hepatotoxicity — method

🚩 AUTO-COINED 2026-07-28 — flagged for human review. The vault's 62nd map and the second entry under the hepatotoxicity endpoint, alongside hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps. Coined because cholestasis is a mechanistically distinct liver injury with its own adverse outcome pathway network and its own battery, not a readout of the liver-chip map. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-07-28). evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, regulatory_status: none. One laboratory, seven reference chemicals, explicitly "the initial iteration" of a proof-of-concept. Nothing here is validated and nothing is adopted.

What we believe now

Cholestasis is a liver injury in which bile stops flowing properly out of the liver; the bile acids that back up damage the tissue. Some drugs and industrial chemicals cause it, prediction is poor, and — as the source paper puts it — the animal-based approaches it would replace are themselves unreliable for this endpoint.

The battery is assembled from a published adverse outcome pathway network — a curated causal chain from a chemical's first molecular effect through to the injury — which determines what to measure:

Key event measuredWhat it means in plain English
Bile transporter changesthe pumps that move bile out of the liver cell stop working properly
Bile canalicular dynamicsthe tiny channels that carry bile between cells stop moving
Endoplasmic reticulum stressthe cell's protein-folding machinery is overloaded
Mitochondrial impairmentthe cell's power supply degrades
Oxidative stressreactive chemistry accumulates faster than the cell can clear it

All five are measured in HepaRG cells, one immortalised human liver line. A computational half sits alongside: (Q)SAR models (statistical prediction of toxicity from chemical structure), structural-alert rules, and molecular docking (simulating whether the molecule physically fits its target).

Seven reference chemicals. Five known cholestatic, two known not. The weight-of-evidence integration ranked perfluorooctanesulfonic acid (PFOS), cyclosporine A and 1-naphthyl isothiocyanate highest; both non-cholestatic compounds were correctly identified.

The published headline is high specificity, and it rests on two chemicals. Two out of two is not an error rate; a single additional false positive would move the observed value from 100% to 67%. Held at emerging accordingly and the denominator is attached to the claim permanently. → high-specificity-on-two-negative-controls

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: human-in-the-loop — by the deterministic ladder in shared/evidence-model.md:

  • Readout? Mostly instrument-based, but not verified. The full text was not retrievable; whether bile canalicular dynamics is read by automated image analysis or by a human at a microscope is unknown. Logged.
  • Decision rule? No fixed procedure. Results are "integrated and quantified using a weight of evidence approach." Rung 3 of the ladder names expert weight-of-evidence explicitly as human judgement in the call.
  • Inputs? No in-vivo study consumed. Not bench-bound.

Blocker: expert weight-of-evidence integration across five in-vitro key events and three computational streams, with no published scoring algorithm retrievable.

This is the same blocker shape as the eye-hazard guidance (eye-hazard-iata-gd263): every component produces a clean number, several would be loop-ready alone, and the integration is the unspecified step. Machine vision does not reach this blocker, because there is nothing to look at — the missing artefact is an arithmetic rule, not a scoring model.

⚠️ Correlated blind spot — and it is a new shape for the rail

All five in-vitro readouts sit in one cell line. Five agreeing measurements sharing one biological substrate are not five independent votes. Any chemical whose cholestatic mechanism runs through a transporter, enzyme or receptor that HepaRG expresses atypically fails all five simultaneously, and the battery reports mechanistically-anchored, five-way agreement that the chemical is clean.

Every previous instance of this rail in the vault has been two separate maps. This one is five readouts inside a single map — the rail applied one level smaller than it was written for. The correlated_blind_spot field takes sibling slugs and has been given a description instead; schema departure logged.

And the second row is a genuine sibling. metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg (OECD Test Guideline 445A, the adopted assay for whether a chemical switches on liver metabolising enzymes) runs in the same HepaRG line. Any hepatic testing battery composing both inherits that line's expression profile twice and counts it as two independent measurements.

The remedy is unchanged and it is not a bigger battery. A sixth HepaRG readout adds nothing. A second cell system that fails differently — primary human hepatocytes from several donors, or a metabolically distinct line — adds everything. → a-battery-inside-one-cell-line-is-not-five-independent-votes

Updated 2026-08-01 — a third row, and the argument stops being an argument. Tonight opens hepatotoxicity-steatosis-battery on the same HepaRG line, so three of the vault's four hepatic maps now share it. And cell-line-identity-outweighs-culture-medium-in-hepatic-transcriptomes supplies the measurement this section previously had to reason toward: in hepatic models, cell-line identity is the dominant source of transcriptomic variance — roughly 12,600–14,100 genes separate one line from another or from real human liver cells, against 1,000–2,300 for swapping the entire culture medium. The shared substrate is not a secondary concern that a careful battery design can absorb; it is the largest term in the variance, and it is fixed before any assay is chosen. The one hepatic map in this vault not built on HepaRG is hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps, which makes it the single independent liver vote the vault holds — subject to the unverified Matrigel question flagged on nam-reagent-provenance-animal-derived-components.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Same organ, different injury (composes-with, NOT redundant): hepatotoxicity-liver-chip-mps reads apical drug-induced liver injury in a perfused multi-cell system — did the liver get hurt. This map reads one specific mechanism of liver injury in a static monolayer — did bile flow stop, and through which step. Neither substitutes for the other, and no source read here tests the pairing.
  • ⚠️ Shares a cell line with an adopted Test Guideline (composes-with, and the composition is the problem). metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg is OECD TG 445A, adopted, closed-loop-ready, and runs in HepaRG. TG 445A's own protocol names hepatotoxicity, cholestasis and steatosis among the adversities that enzyme induction can drive — so these two maps are natural battery partners, and that is exactly why the shared cell line matters. Recorded on both maps.
  • Same design method, different organ (structural sibling): nephrotoxicity-ciptec-aop-battery, coined the same night, from the same EU programme stream, with the identical architecture — five AOP-derived key events in one immortalised cell line — and the identical intra-battery blind spot. Two independent groups converging on one design pattern in one run is a signal about where the field is going, and the pattern carries its weakness with it.
  • Composes-with in-silico-qsar-read-across — the computational half of this battery is (Q)SAR, structural alerts and docking, which is that map's subject matter. Flagged with a caution: the AOP network that chose the key events and the models predicting them are built from the same published literature, so the two halves are correlated by construction, not independent confirmation. (Vault inference.)
  • Composes-with repeat-dose-systemic-toxicity-nam — organ-specific injury is a building block of repeat-dose assessment, and ONTOX's stated programme goal is repeated-dose replacement. Not redundant.
