TGx-DDI transcriptomic biomarker — genotoxicity — method

Status: consolidating (2026-07-22) — the vault's first genotoxicity entry that reaches formally-validated on a blinded four-laboratory ring trial with reported concordance statistics. evidence_tier: formally-validated, regulatory_status: proposed (a qualification package is submitted to FDA's Biomarker Qualification Program; nothing is accepted). consolidating, not consensus — adoption is what earns consensus, and nothing has been adopted. Not contested either: no result pushes back.

What we believe now

TGx-DDI is a 64-gene expression signature read in human TK6 lymphoblastoid cells. Instead of looking for physical damage, it measures which genes the cells switched on in response to the chemical and classifies that pattern as DNA damage-inducing (DDI) or not. It exists, in the authors' own words, "to address the limitations of traditional in vitro genotoxicity assays, particularly their low specificity."

Four independent laboratories — Georgetown University, Procter & Gamble, Sanofi, Burleson Research Technologies — ran it blind on 14 compounds (6 DDI, 8 non-DDI) plus 3 controls, 47 valid tests, in two rounds. Optimised-round performance: sensitivity 100% (95% CI 82–100%), specificity 86% (95% CI 67–96%), accuracy 91% (95% CI 80–98%). Interlaboratory agreement (Cohen's Kappa / PABAK / AC1) 0.61–1.0, with two of the four labs agreeing perfectly; reference-RNA correlation r > 0.84 between all site pairs.

The specificity number is the whole contribution, and the sensitivity number is not. Near-100% sensitivity is table stakes for a genotoxicity screen — every assay in the space is tuned to never miss a mutagen. The field's actual problem is the opposite error: the paper puts the adopted in-vitro micronucleus assay's specificity at 30–54%, or 65% depending on dataset, meaning it wrongly flags between a third and two-thirds of harmless chemicals as genotoxic, each false alarm historically triggering a follow-up animal study. 86% roughly halves that. (tgx-ddi-ring-trial-performance.)

Stated honestly: 14 compounds is a small panel. "100% sensitivity" is "zero misses out of six", and the confidence interval says the truth could be as low as 82%. The specificity interval (67–96%) overlaps the top of the micronucleus range. Directionally solid, quantitatively soft. And the biomarker's developers are on the author list and are the party seeking FDA qualification — a strong ring trial, but not an independent ECVAM/ICCVAM one. funding_flag: [industry].

It is not a replacement and the authors do not claim it is. Recommended use is "within a weight-of-evidence framework"; combined with the micronucleus assay it "enhances classification accuracy while retaining the ability to detect aneugens" — which TGx-DDI structurally cannot do.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready — instrument-readable readout and a published, fixed data-interpretation procedure. Readout: NanoString nCounter digital molecule counting (also validated on microarray, RT-qPCR, RNA-Seq and TempO-Seq) — no human scoring anywhere in the chain. Decision rule: run three classifiers — nearest shrunken centroid probability analysis, PCA, hierarchical clustering — and call DDI-positive if any one is positive. That is the deterministic interpretation procedure OECD TG 493 famously lacks and the reason TG 493 sits a rung lower despite a fully quantitative readout.

  • Blocker: none.
  • ⚠ But the tier carries a caveat the vault has not seen before. Two of the three classifier legs (PCA, HCA) are unsupervised and batch-relative — where a sample projects, and which cluster it joins, depend on what else was analysed alongside it. The rule is fixed; it is not sample-independent. In a lab running the standard reference set every time, that distinction is invisible. In a closed loop that chooses its own next compounds, the loop is continuously changing the thing two of its three legs are conditioned on. Practical requirement: a loop running this assay must pin its reference set and run it every batch, treating the reference panel as protocol rather than as overhead to be optimised away. (tgx-ddi-decision-rule-is-fixed-but-batch-relative.)
  • domain_gate — biological, not chemical, and it has two halves in two different places. The signature is p53-enriched, so it detects the cell's response to DNA damage rather than the damage itself. A compound-side check (is this an aneugen? a kinase inhibitor? a transcription inhibitor?) needs mechanistic annotation the assay does not supply. A cell-side check (does this batch still have working p53? do ATF3, CDKN1A, GADD45A respond?) is an ongoing QC obligation, not a one-time setting. Skip either and you get confidently wrong answers with excellent inter-lab reproducibility — the worst combination, because the reproducibility looks like validation. (tgx-ddi-p53-dependency-defines-the-domain.)

closed-loop-ready is not an endorsement here and the caveats above are why. A machine could read the plate and make the call. Whether it should, on a chemical whose mechanism has not been checked against the domain gate, is a different question with a different answer.

⚠ Correlated blind spot — the rail

TGx-DDI and the in-vitro micronucleus assay (genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus) are proposed as a battery, and they are half independent and half not — both halves matter:

  • Genuinely complementary on mechanism. TGx-DDI reads a p53 transcriptional response; micronucleus counts physical chromosome fragments. Aneugens are invisible to the first by design and are exactly what the second was built for. This is what a battery is supposed to look like.
  • Correlated on metabolism. Both run in TK6 cells and both bolt on rat liver S9 for metabolic activation, because neither culture has meaningful native xenobiotic metabolism. A pro-genotoxicant — inert as dosed, DNA-reactive only after enzymatic conversion — is detected by both only if rat S9 performs that specific conversion. If the route is human-specific or runs through a step S9 doesn't reproduce, the compound reads negative in both for the same reason, and a weight-of-evidence rule records that as increased confidence. One metabolic assumption, reported as two results. (tgx-ddi-micronucleus-share-the-s9-metabolism-gap.)

Metabolism is this vault's single most recurrent correlated blind spot — FET ↔ RTgill-W1, ROS ↔ 3T3 NRU, now TGx-DDI ↔ micronucleus — and it recurs because every in-vitro assay solves the same problem the same way: by borrowing a rodent's liver. The fix is not another cell assay on the same S9; it is metabolically competent cells (HepaRG is one of the p53-verified lines the paper names, and is a real option here) or an explicit domain gate routing suspected pro-genotoxicants out before the rule fires.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Battery composition (composes-with, and the rail hit): genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus — complementary on aneugens, correlated on rat-S9 metabolism. See the rail section above; both maps carry correlated_blind_spot.
  • The same job, a different layer (composes-with): genotoxicity-toxtracker-reporter-moa (OECD TG 446A) is also a follow-up/interpretation tool for the primary genotox battery rather than a primary detector — and it is also reporter-based and instrument-read. The two overlap in purpose and differ in what they resolve: ToxTracker separates direct DNA reactivity from indirect oxidative/protein stress; TGx-DDI separates DDI from non-DDI. A chemical that is a non-genotoxic p53 modulator is a known TGx-DDI false positive and is exactly the case ToxTracker's oxidative/protein-stress reporters would classify correctly. Worth checking whether they are redundant or genuinely staged — logged as an open gap; nothing in this source compares them.
  • Primary battery members (composes-with): genotoxicity-ames-bacterial-reverse-mutation (gene mutation in bacteria — also carries a known false-positive tendency, the same problem TGx-DDI targets) and genotoxicity-mammalian-gene-mutation.
  • Carcinogenicity edge (composes-with): distinguishing DNA-damaging from non-DNA-damaging mechanisms is the genotoxic/non-genotoxic split that carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay turns on.
  • Reagent provenance (limits): the rat liver S9 activation step is an animal-derived component, and this is a method routinely described as non-animal. nam-reagent-provenance-animal-derived-components.
  • Model layer (composes-with): the classifier is a trained statistical model over 64 features; its reporting and versioning obligations belong with ml-model-reporting-reproducibility, and if it is ever retrained the change-control question in ai-companion-agents-validation-as-a-running-process applies directly.
  • Global-ripple: no edges to the skin, eye, phototoxicity, endocrine, inhalation, DNT or ecotoxicity columns — this is a genotoxicity-and-carcinogenicity method. Its regulatory edge is to regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework: FDA's Biomarker Qualification Program is the acceptance route being used here, not an OECD Test Guideline, and if qualified it would be the first transcriptomic biomarker qualified by FDA CDER — a precedent for the whole omics column, not just this assay.
