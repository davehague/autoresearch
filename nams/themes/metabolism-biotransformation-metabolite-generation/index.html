Biotransformation as a supplied step — generating the metabolites an assay cannot make — biotransformation / metabolism — method

🚩 NEW MAP, AUTO-COINED 2026-08-12 — flagged for human review. It joins the existing biotransformation-metabolism endpoint group rather than opening a new one, alongside metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg. The case for coining it rather than folding it into that map: OECD Test Guideline 445A asks whether a chemical switches metabolising enzymes on — a mechanistic question about the chemical. This asks the opposite-facing question of running those enzymes as a service to manufacture a chemical's metabolites so that something else can test them. Same enzyme family, two different objects: one is an assay about metabolism, the other is metabolism as infrastructure for other assays. Folding them together would bury the distinction that makes the second one interesting for the blind-spot rail. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Why this map exists

The vault's correlated-blind-spot rail has one dominant cause, and it is chemical rather than interpretive. Cells in a dish mostly do not carry the enzymes that convert a chemical into what a body would actually produce. So a compound that is harmless as supplied and dangerous once converted reads negative, and the assay is not wrong about what it measured — it never made the dangerous molecule. That single gap accounts for several of the rail's rows:

  • ecotox-rtgill-w1-fish-cell-line ↔ fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet — neither has full metabolism, so both under-predict bioactivation-dependent compounds. OECD Test Guideline 249's own text supplies the worked case: allyl alcohol is under-predicted because the cells cannot convert it to acrolein, and acrolein itself is predicted very well.
  • phototoxicity-ros-assay ↔ phototoxicity-3t3-nru — a photo-inert parent with a photo-reactive metabolite walks the entire tiered phototoxicity scheme untouched.

The field's usual answer is a better test system — hepatocytes, liver-derived lines, organ chips. That works, costs a great deal, and drags a second problem in: this vault already records that three of its four hepatic maps run on the same immortalised cell line, so "add metabolism" has repeatedly meant adding the same metabolism several times and counting it as several independent votes.

This map holds the other shape: metabolic competence supplied as a modular step upstream of whatever assay you were going to run.

What we believe now

One proposed platform, documented as a procedure and not yet demonstrated as a result.

Take Schizosaccharomyces pombe (fission yeast), engineer it to express a chosen human cytochrome P450 enzyme — the main family that chemically modifies drugs and pollutants — then chemically permeabilise the yeast so a test chemical can pass through the wall while the enzyme stays in its native membrane. The result is a semi-intact whole cell that behaves as a reaction vessel with the enzyme already correctly installed: the author's "enzyme bag."

The stated scope is a complete human CYPome library — all 57 human P450 enzymes, including the "orphan" CYPs whose natural substrates are unknown — with extension claimed to phase II conjugating enzymes (UGTs, SULTs), insect P450s, and baker's yeast as an alternative host. High-throughput screening runs through hierarchical enzyme-bag cocktails: pool several enzymes, then narrow down to identify which one acted.

The claimed advantage over human liver microsomes — ground-up liver membrane fragments bought from a supplier, the incumbent way of adding metabolism to a dish — is cost, technical simplicity and "physiological relevance" from the enzyme sitting in a real membrane. That last claim is asserted, not demonstrated, and this map does not adopt it.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — by the deterministic ladder in shared/evidence-model.md, applied in order. There is a physical assay (so not computational-connective). Its model consumes no in-vivo study and a NAM does exist (so not bench-bound). No human judgement is load-bearing in producing the metabolites (so not human-in-the-loop). The readouts are machine-read — luminescence off a plate reader, chromatography or mass-spectrometry peaks — but no fixed data-interpretation procedure exists, which is rung 4 and not rung 5.

And the blocker here has an unusual shape worth stating precisely. The step is not blocked as an operation: 3 hours to prepare, 4 hours to react, cofactor regeneration internal rather than operator-dosed, outputs an instrument reads. A liquid handler could run it inside a working day. What is missing is any rule for what to do with the answer — which metabolites matter, at what yield, and how a downstream assay's verdict on a metabolite combines with its verdict on the parent. It closes the chemistry half of the blind spot and leaves the interpretation half exactly where it was, with more compounds in it, since every parent becomes several metabolites.

  • Access gate: the recombinant yeast strains and the CYPome library. Distributability and terms are not stated in the material read. Recorded as an unknown, not as clear — and this vault has three adopted methods already gated behind a single supplier, so the question is not idle.

The paper names the standing state of that problem — known metabolites are "only a tiny fraction of possible structures in the mammalian metabolome, leaving extensive unidentified signals as metabolic 'dark matter'." Automating identification moves the bottleneck one step downstream without removing it, and makes it worse in one specific way: cheaper naming means more named compounds with no toxicological data and no rule for what to do with them.
