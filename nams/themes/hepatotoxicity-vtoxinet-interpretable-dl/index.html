What we believe now

vToxiNet is a "virtual adverse outcome pathway network": a deep learning architecture whose internal structure is organized around the Reactome pathway hierarchy (see glossary), not a generic black-box net. It takes chemical descriptors, high-throughput screening assay responses, and transcriptomic signatures as input, and returns a hepatotoxicity prediction plus gene- and pathway-level attribution — a candidate mechanistic explanation, not just a verdict. The authors frame this explicitly against this vault's own standing complaint about the model layer: most ML hepatotoxicity models are either structure-only (in-silico-qsar-read-across) or black boxes with no mechanistic output.

The honest gap: the abstract's own performance claim — "promising predictive performance" in cross-validation and external validation — is a value judgement, not a number this run can independently assess. If vToxiNet's accuracy sits near the vault's standing structure-only comparator (Tox21's 49-assay panel barely beat chemical structure alone on DILI, 0.74 vs. 0.73 AUC-ROC; docs/backlog.md 2026-07-29), the real contribution here is the pathway attribution, not raw predictivity — a question only the paywalled full text can settle.
