What we believe now

Duplex sequencing (an error-corrected next-generation-sequencing technique) tags each original DNA strand with a unique molecular barcode before amplification, letting it distinguish a true, single mutated DNA molecule from the PCR/sequencing errors that swamp ordinary NGS at this sensitivity. Run in HepaRG cells — a human liver-cell line that retains its own native CYP drug-metabolizing enzymes, unlike most standard genotoxicity test systems that need an added rat-liver S9 metabolic-activation mix — it quantifies chemically-induced point-mutation frequency, substitution-type spectra, and (via COSMIC signature matching) plausible mechanism of mutagenesis.

Barutcu et al. 2025 tested six reference chemicals: EMS and ENU (direct alkylators) produced clear dose-responsive mutation-frequency increases with alkylation-consistent signatures; BAP (a metabolism- dependent pro-mutagen) and cisplatin (a DNA-crosslinker) produced modest, signature-consistent increases; cyclophosphamide (an alkylating prodrug) showed minimal mutagenicity under the conditions tested; etoposide, a clastogen, produced a strong chromosome-damage signal in parallel cytogenetic assays but no point-mutation increase — the paper's cleanest demonstration that this assay and chromosome-damage assays detect different mechanisms rather than duplicating each other.
