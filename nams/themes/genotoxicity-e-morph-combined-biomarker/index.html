E-Morph extended assay — combined estrogen-receptor-agonism and DNA-damage biomarker screen — method

🚩 AUTO-COINED 2026-11-02 — the vault's 135th map — flagged for human review. A genuinely new named method (an extension of BfR's existing E-Morph Screening Assay) addressing a question this vault had not mapped: distinguishing genotoxic from non-genotoxic, nuclear-receptor-mediated carcinogens inside one screening assay. It reads two mechanistically distinct signals (estrogen-receptor agonism; DNA-damage response) on the same plate, in two cell lines, and composes with but duplicates neither endocrine-er-transactivation (a different readout technology for a related question) nor the genotoxicity battery (genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus, genotoxicity-comet-alkaline-scge — different damage-detection chemistry entirely). Whether a correlational proof-of-concept earns its own map versus a section on an existing one is a human call; logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-11-02). One laboratory, one chemical library, a correlational study with no classification rule and no independent replication. evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, regulatory_status: none.

What we believe now

The base E-Morph Screening Assay (ESA) reads estrogen-receptor agonism in MCF-7 human breast cancer cells. This extension adds two more markers to the same wells: gamma-H2AX (lights up at DNA double-strand breaks — the cell's own damage-response signal) and phospho-histone H3 (a marker of actively dividing cells, used here as an indirect aneugenicity signal — aneugens disrupt chromosome segregation during division rather than breaking DNA directly). The same combined readout runs in parallel in HepG2 liver cells, which lack MCF-7's estrogen-receptor pathway but share general DNA-damage and cell-cycle biology, as a cross-cell-line check on the genotoxicity signal specifically.

Why this matters for risk assessment, stated plainly: a genotoxic carcinogen and a non-genotoxic, hormone-receptor-driven carcinogen get different safe-dose logic in practice — genotoxic mechanisms are conventionally treated as having no safe threshold, receptor-mediated ones are not. Telling them apart today usually needs two separate test systems, run and interpreted together. This assay proposes answering both questions on one plate.

What was shown, across a chemical library at six concentrations starting at 100 micromolar: genotoxic-effect detection (gamma-H2AX) correlated strongly between MCF-7 and HepG2; within MCF-7, gamma-H2AX induction correlated with ER-agonist activity; in HepG2, gamma-H2AX correlated with the phospho-histone H3 aneugenicity signal. This establishes internal consistency across cell lines and markers — it does not establish accuracy against any genotoxicity or carcinogenicity reference standard, and none is reported in the retrievable abstract (full text paywalled; see source digest). → e-morph-extended-assay-pairs-estrogenicity-with-dna-damage-in-one-screen
