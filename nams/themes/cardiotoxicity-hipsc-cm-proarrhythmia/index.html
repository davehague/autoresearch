hiPSC-cardiomyocyte proarrhythmia assessment (MEA + AI) — cardiotoxicity — method

🚩 NEW MAP and a NEW ENDPOINT GROUP, auto-coined 2026-07-26. cardiotoxicity has been an unlisted coverage gap in this vault since it opened — drug-induced heart damage is among the largest causes of pharmaceutical failure and withdrawal, and the vault has held no map for it. What we believe now

The problem. Some drugs disturb the heart's electrical rhythm badly enough to cause Torsades de Pointes — a specific and sometimes fatal arrhythmia. For decades the standard screen has been the hERG assay: does the compound block one particular potassium channel? The paper's premise, and the premise of the CiPA initiative (Comprehensive In Vitro Proarrhythmia Assay) behind it, is that this single-channel surrogate "lack[s] sufficient predictive accuracy", because repolarisation is "governed by multiple interacting ion channels."

The method. Human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes — heart muscle cells regrown from reprogrammed adult cells — are cultured on a multielectrode array, a grid of electrodes that records the electrical field of every beat. Four features are extracted: beat period, FPDcF (rate-corrected field potential duration — the dish equivalent of the QT interval on an ECG), spike amplitude, and beating irregularity. A trained classifier turns those four numbers into a three-way risk call.

ROC-AUC 0.982 in plain terms: pick one genuinely dangerous drug and one genuinely safe drug at random, and the model ranks the dangerous one as riskier about 98 times in 100. That is a very good score computed on 16 compounds by a model fitted on 12. An artificial neural network has far more parameters than that dataset has examples; the paper reports 10,000 resampling iterations and confidence intervals in a supplementary figure but never raises overfitting as a limitation. The vault does.

The applied finding is the more useful one. Of 12 cancer drugs, four "exhibited minimal to no significant reduction in impedance signals" — the cells were alive and attached. Two of those four, sunitinib and erlotinib, showed significant FPDcF prolongation after long exposure and were scored high-to-intermediate risk. A survival readout saw nothing; a function readout saw the harm. (functional-cardiotoxicity-is-invisible-to-a-viability-assay.)

That distinction generalises past this endpoint. Most cell-based assays in this vault measure survival — MTT absorbance is the readout behind OECD TG 439 (skin irritation), TG 431 (skin corrosion), TG 492 and TG 492B (eye), TG 498 (phototoxicity in reconstructed skin); neutral red uptake is TG 432's. That whole column answers one question extremely well: did the cells die? Cardiotoxicity needed a functional assay before it could have a NAM at all, because its endpoint is invisible to survival.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — and this map is the reason the vault now has a fifth blocker shape.

  • The readout is excellent for a machine. Electrical traces at 9.6 kHz across an electrode grid, reduced to four numbers, tracked over five days. No morphology, no scoring, no microscope. In readout terms this is closer to eye-irritation-vitrigel-tg494's electrical-resistance measurement than to anything scored by eye.

  • The rule exists, is mechanical, and cannot be obtained. Four features → ensemble → threshold 0.5 → three classes. By the deterministic ladder that is a closed-loop-ready shape. It fails on a property the ladder never had to name, because until tonight every candidate rule was public:

    obtainable is not a scientific property. It is a publication practice — and it decides whether a rule is infrastructure or a product. (a-decision-rule-that-exists-but-is-not-published.)

  • access_gate at both layers, held by one company. The cells are the vendor's product; the classifier is the vendor's unreleased model. This is the same phenomenon single-instrument-methods-are-becoming-de-facto-standards records from the European Commission's JRC one layer down — "the proprietary nature of the used algorithms" — arriving at the decision layer.

  • A new experiment-selection problem: WHEN, not WHAT. Every other closed-loop-ready method in this vault reads an endpoint at a fixed time, or at a fixed set of durations (TG 492B's several exposure lengths). Here the finding only appeared at ≥72 hours and grew to 120. A loop running this assay is not primarily choosing which chemicals to test next — it is choosing when to look. The vault has not encountered that before, and TG 492B's retest trigger (ingested 2026-07-25, the tier's first mechanically-specified experiment-selection rule) is the nearest relative.
