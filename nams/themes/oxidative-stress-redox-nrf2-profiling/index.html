What we believe now

The endpoint. Cells hold a running balance between reactive oxygen species — chiefly hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) — and the machinery that clears them. Chemicals that push that balance damage proteins, membranes and DNA, so "does this substance perturb redox homeostasis, and does the cell mount its defence" is a mechanistic hazard question that sits upstream of several apical endpoints rather than being one itself.

The method. R3ACT (Redox & Antioxidant Capacity Tracker) is a live-cell platform in HaCaT keratinocytes — an immortalised human skin cell line — adapted to 5 kPa oxygen rather than the ~19 kPa of a room-air incubator. It reads two things:

ReporterWhat it measuresHow it performed
HyPer7.2 (cytosolic + mitochondrial)Hydrogen peroxide, in real time, per compartmentThe weaker half. Gave compartment-specific mechanistic context but "often revealed transient fluctuations rather than consistent material-induced H₂O₂ increases."
POINTER (Nrf2 transcriptional reporter)Whether the cell has switched on its antioxidant defence programmeThe load-bearing readout. Carried all four reported material calls.

Around them sits a machine-readable stack: viability-gated concentration selection, oxygen-controlled plate-reader measurement, then high-content microscopy with deep-learning single-cell analysis as a confirmation layer.

What it reported on four materials. Zinc oxide — strongest Nrf2 activation, reproducible across two runs and confirmed by microscopy. Polystyrene nanoplastics — likewise. Perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA) — borderline at the plate reader and NOT confirmed by microscopy. Salicylic acid — Nrf2 activation, which the authors explicitly read as antioxidant-response engagement and not as a skin-sensitisation call.

The honest summary. A mechanistically thoughtful assay at the earliest possible stage of evidence, whose most interesting output so far is not a hazard call but a caution about how a molecular event is interpreted elsewhere — see the through-line.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — Rung 4 of the deterministic ladder, and cleanly so. The readout is entirely machine-obtainable: fluorescence off a plate reader, images scored by a deep-learning model, concentrations chosen by a viability gate. No human eye is load-bearing anywhere. What is missing is the other half — "predefined performance criteria" do not exist, so nothing states what magnitude of Nrf2 induction, in what fraction of cells, at what concentration, is a positive.

And there is a second, subtler gap that the vault should name rather than fold into the first. On one of four materials the two readouts disagreed: the plate reader said borderline, the microscopy did not confirm. The disagreement was resolved — correctly, and to the authors' credit, in the conservative direction — but by judgement, not by a written arbitration rule. A loop running this platform would meet that disagreement on a substantial fraction of a real library and have nothing to consult. This is the same shape as tonight's other finding on endocrine-er-transactivation: the components are machine-runnable and the rule that joins them is absent.

access_gate recorded: the reporter constructs' availability is not stated, and a platform built on two specific genetically encoded lines is not obviously replicable elsewhere.
