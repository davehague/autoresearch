Population inter-individual variability — human LCL donor panel — method

🚩 AUTO-COINED 2026-07-24 — flagged for human review. A new CLAIM TYPE, not a new toxicological endpoint. Every hazard-assessment method in this vault answers is this chemical dangerous (a class), how dangerous (a potency band), or at what dose (a point of departure). This one answers how much do people differ — a spread. Its endpoint field, inter-individual-variability, is a risk-assessment parameter, not an organ or a toxicological effect, which is a departure from the per-endpoint filing convention. The nearest precedent inside the vault is skin-sensitization-quantitative-pod-da (OECD GL 497 Part III), logged on 2026-07-09 as "a new claim type, not a new endpoint" when it produced a dose rather than a class. This is the second such case, and it goes one step further out: a multiplier applied to somebody else's answer. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-07-24) — a single well-designed research programme, peer-reviewed, with a defined method and stated limitations, and no independent laboratory has reproduced it. evidence_tier: peer-reviewed-method; regulatory_status: none (no Test Guideline, no guidance, and no regulatory programme has yet used one of these factors to set a real limit). Not consolidating — that requires multiple independent labs. Not emerging-by-default either: the design is strong, the panel is large, and the mechanism is corroborated by genetics within the study.

What we believe now

The convention it targets. Every chemical safety limit is built by taking a dose that looked acceptable and dividing it by uncertainty factors for what the test could not see. One of those covers inter-individual variability — people are not identical, and whoever sets the limit does not know who is at the sensitive end. The conventional value for the toxicodynamic half of that factor (how differently people respond once a chemical reaches the tissue, as distinct from how differently they absorb and clear it) is 10^0.5 = 3.16. It is a chosen number. It has not been measured per chemical.

What the method does. Screen a chemical against 131 human lymphoblastoid cell lines — immortalised white blood cells from named donors in the 1000 Genomes reference panel, spanning Utah/European (CEU), British (GBR), Tuscan (TSI) and Yoruba/Nigerian (YRI) ancestry, sex-balanced. Fit a concentration–response curve per donor. Compute TDVF05 — the ratio of the population's median EC10 to its 5th-percentile EC10. That number is how much lower a dose it takes to affect a sensitive person than a typical one, measured rather than assumed.

What it found (Alshammari et al. 2026, Toxicological Sciences 209(4):kfag038, PMID 41883125). 53 substances screened; 18 produced usable cytotoxicity. Median TDVF05 3.8; range 1 to 46.2.

The median reassures and the distribution does not. 3.8 against a default of 3.16 reads as "the convention was about right," and the abstract calls it "largely consistent with default assumptions." The results section says "About half of these chemicals exceeded the default TDVF05 of 10^1/2" — for roughly one substance in two the assumed margin is too small. Two chemicals (dexamethasone, doxorubicin) exceeded even the factor of 10 that is meant to cover variability in both handling and response; doxorubicin's spread is 46-fold. Both readings come from the same data. For anyone setting a limit they have opposite consequences, and this vault records the second. (population-lcl-panel-measures-what-a-default-factor-assumes.)

Why the variation happens is not a mystery in this study. A genome-wide association analysis across the 131 donors put the associated loci "primarily containing transporter and metabolism genes" — differences in how cells move a chemical in and out and chemically process it. That is a mechanistic explanation rather than unexplained noise, which is what makes the numbers arguably transferable. It is also, precisely, the finding that undercuts the assay system: see the blind spot below.

The coverage nobody quotes. 53 substances in, 18 out. The other 35 produced no factor at all — not a reassuring one, none. And the decline is implicit: a curve that would not fit, not a pre-test that failed. Nothing in the output distinguishes "this chemical shows low variability" from "this assay could not see this chemical." (two-thirds-of-substances-produced-no-variability-factor.)

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready. Apply the ladder in order: it is a physical assay (not computational-connective); its model consumes no in-vivo study (not bench-bound); no human judgement enters the call (not human-in-the-loop); the readout is machine-readable and the interpretation procedure is fixed and published (not automatable-open-loop). Readout: CellTiter-Blue resazurin fluorescence on a FLIPR Tetra plate reader, dosed by "an automated liquid handler." Rule: a downward Hill model per individual/batch/chemical, EC10 as the benchmark response, TDVF05 = "the ratio between the median and 5th percentile adjusted EC10." No microscope, no scorer, no expert weight-of-evidence anywhere.

  • Blocker: none.
  • ⚠ And it tests the tier's definition a third time, in a new way. closed-loop-ready requires a "fixed, deterministic data-interpretation procedure." Twenty-two of the tier's members earn that through arithmetic a committee wrote down. This rule's central estimator is a Markov chain Monte Carlo sampler — an algorithm that works by drawing random samples. Two runs on identical plate data give slightly different EC10 estimates unless the seed is pinned, and TDVF05 is a ratio of two percentiles of a distribution of such estimates, so tail noise propagates into the number that gets multiplied into a limit. Fixed ≠ deterministic — the third distinct way the vault has now found a written-down rule failing to be what the tier's name implies, after fixed ≠ sample-independent (TGx-DDI, 2026-07-22) and AI rules are not reproducible (Hartung & Kleinstreuer, 2026-07-19). Nothing demotes: the rule is published, mechanical and judgement-free, which is what the tier grades. The remedy is trivial and exactly the kind of thing an optimiser deletes as overhead — pin the seed, version the sampler, log both with the result. (tdvf-derivation-is-a-stochastic-bayesian-pipeline.)
  • domain_gate — chemical (needs to be direct-acting) and practical (34% of substances yield anything). Unlike eye-irritation-vitrigel-tg494's measurable pre-test, this domain is discovered by running the assay and seeing whether it worked.

closed-loop-ready is not an endorsement, and here the gap is unusually wide. A machine could run this panel and emit a factor for every chemical it can see. Whether that factor should then be multiplied into a human safety limit is a regulatory question nobody has answered — regulatory_status: none — and the blind-spot argument below is a reason to be slow about it.

⚠ Correlated blind spot — the rail, in a new shape

Lymphoblastoid cells have no meaningful native xenobiotic metabolism, and the authors say so. The vault already carries that exact gap in the same cell lineage: the in-vitro micronucleus assay (genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus) and TGx-DDI (genotoxicity-tgx-ddi-transcriptomic-biomarker) both run in TK6, itself a human lymphoblastoid line, and both bolt on rat liver S9 precisely because the cells cannot metabolise. This panel uses no metabolic activation at all. Metabolism is now the shared cause in four of seven rows on the rail.

But the failure has a different shape here, and that is the finding. Every previous row is two assays voting on the same question — two votes that are really one. The error is additive. A variability factor is not a vote; it is a multiplier applied to another assay's answer. If the hazard assay is blind to a pro-toxicant and the variability factor for that chemical was derived in cells blind the same way, then the wrong dose is divided by the wrong factor, in the same direction, for the same reason. The errors compose rather than sit side by side. No existing row on the rail has this structure.

The concrete failure: a bioactivation-dependent chemical reads weakly cytotoxic here, its measured spread is narrow, and it receives a low variability factor — "people don't differ much on this one." Meanwhile the study's own genetics say the variability is driven by "transporter and metabolism genes." The panel found that metabolism drives variability, in a system that barely metabolises. Whatever spread lives in full hepatic activation is not in these numbers, and the method returns a confident small factor rather than an abstention. (lcl-metabolic-gap-multiplies-rather-than-repeats.)

Flagged as a vault inference. The paper never mentions TGx-DDI, the micronucleus assay, or automation; the metabolic limitation and the GWAS result are sourced, the cross-method pairing and the compounding argument are the vault's.
