What we believe now

A human induced-pluripotent-stem-cell (iPSC) model of early neuroectodermal differentiation — the developmental step by which embryonic stem cells commit toward the nervous-system lineage — is exposed to a test chemical, then read two ways: cytotoxicity, normalized to the chemical's maternal plasma Cmax (the peak blood concentration a pregnant person actually reaches at a real dose), and a biologically anchored 190-gene targeted transcriptomic panel derived from a prior published transcriptome dataset. On an independent set of 18 developmental toxicants and non-toxicants, a random-forest classifier built on cytotoxicity alone reached AUC 0.852 — "robust discrimination for a complex and heterogeneous toxicological endpoint," in the authors' own words (IfADo, Dortmund; Hengstler lab).

The transcriptomic panel is more sensitive and did not improve the classification. It detected perturbation at concentrations substantially below the cytotoxic threshold, but adding it to the classifier bought no accuracy gain over cytotoxicity alone. What it bought instead was mechanism: Principal Component Analysis and a clustering method the authors call Differentiation Pattern Clustering (DiPaC) separated two distinct transcriptional failure modes — impaired progression toward neuroectodermal identity, versus activation of a divergent (off-target) developmental program — illustrated with methoxyacetic acid (the former) and dimethadione (the latter). A pass/fail cytotoxicity call cannot see that distinction at all.

No prior map in this vault addressed developmental toxicity via an iPSC cytotoxicity/transcriptomic-biomarker readout. developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity reads whole-organism structural malformation; the DNT battery's human-cell maps read a battery of 8 specific neurodevelopmental processes rather than a general cytotoxicity-anchored developmental-toxicity call.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — both readouts are instrument-measured (a plate-based viability assay; a targeted gene-expression panel), so nothing here is scored by a trained human eye. The blocker is the missing agreed, validated decision rule: the random-forest classifier is a single-lab fit to 18 compounds with no inter-laboratory reproducibility work and no external held-out benchmark, not a published interpretation procedure a second lab could run and expect the same answer from.

The finding most relevant to a loop. Adding a richer, more sensitive, more mechanistically informative readout did not buy a better decision rule — the 190-gene panel bought mechanistic insight a classifier cannot currently use. An automated system optimizing for classification accuracy alone would see no reason to run the transcriptomic layer at all; a system trying to understand why a compound is a developmental toxicant would need it regardless of what it does to AUC. More informative and more decisive are different properties, the same gap this vault has already logged on the model-layer side (the-metric-that-improved-is-not-the-one-a-decision-uses, 2026-08-05) arriving here in a physical assay instead of a trained model.
