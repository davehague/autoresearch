Digital pathology / whole-slide imaging — the histopathology data channel — input-quality map

🚩 AUTO-COINED 2026-10-01, flagged for human review. Not a method map in the usual sense — an input-quality map, closest sibling nam-reagent-provenance-animal-derived-components (also a quality record for a channel underneath assays rather than an assay itself) and ml-model-reporting-reproducibility (also about whether the record is kept well enough to check). Its endpoint (method-input-quality) is not a toxicological endpoint, and its subject — whole-slide digital imaging of histopathology slides — is not a NAM at all; it is the readout channel several of this vault's existing methods and a great deal of traditional (animal) toxicology depend on. The vault's 122nd map. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (updated 2026-10-05, held). Two sources, neither independently replicated, neither citing the other, from unrelated groups (one industry/CRO, one academic) → strength: single-study each, evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory. regulatory_status: none — no OECD, FDA, or other regulatory body has formally qualified a digital-pathology platform as the text of this source states it; GLP validation is an internal quality-system certification, not a regulatory acceptance event on this vault's regulatory_status ladder.

Why this map exists

Histopathology — a trained pathologist judging tissue morphology under a microscope — is the readout for a large share of traditional (animal) toxicology endpoints this vault exists to find replacements for, and it recurs as a scoring step inside several NAMs this vault already holds (microscopy-scored morphology appears across the genotoxicity and carcinogenicity columns, e.g. genotoxicity-in-vitro-micronucleus and carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay). Nobody had checked whether the channel that readout travels through — physical slide versus digital image — changes anything, or what has to be true before any future machine-vision classifier could even be attempted on it.

What we believe now

A multisite contract research organization (Charles River Laboratories, with a Sanofi co-author — the sponsor side of the same workflow) describes the Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) validation of a cloud-based digital pathology platform for primary pathology evaluation of non-clinical toxicology studies: upload, download, microscopic evaluation of tissue on a whole-slide image (WSI), and archiving. The diagnostic act itself does not change — a trained veterinary/toxicologic pathologist still looks at tissue morphology and renders the call, now from a screen rather than a physical slide. No sensitivity/specificity/concordance figure, kappa statistic, or sample size is present in the retrieved abstract; this reads as a process/ implementation description of a validation programme rather than a data table, and that absence is recorded as an honest evidence gap, not assumed favorable.

Bias watch. Five of six authors are Charles River employees validating the platform their own CRO operates and sells as a service; the sixth is from Sanofi, the sponsor side. Graded per shared/evidence-model.md's bias-watch rail exactly as sceptically as any other method-developer self-validation — a stated no-competing-interests declaration does not change that the validated system is the validating organization's own commercial infrastructure.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop (promoted 2026-10-05, from human-in-the-loop) — a candidate machine-vision classifier now exists and demonstrably beats the average trained pathologist on the readout this map tracks, but the call is not yet backed by an agreed fixed procedure.

  • Readout: a digital whole-slide image (the founding source's GLP-validated channel), now shown by a second, independent source (TRACE, jaume-2024-trace-histopathology-ai-toxicologic-pathology) to be scorable by a trained vision model, not just viewable by a human on screen. Machine-readable in practice, not just in principle.
  • Decision rule: a candidate one exists — TRACE's learned quantification-to-severity mapping — but it is calibrated via leave-one-out cross-validation against one reader panel's consensus, not a published, externally fixed threshold a second lab could apply without re-deriving it. No agreed procedure yet, which is exactly the distinction that keeps this at automatable-open-loop rather than closed-loop-ready.
  • Promotion path, now narrower. What would move this to closed-loop-ready: independent replication of TRACE-shaped performance on a second cohort, by a second group, converging on a shared, externally specified threshold — the same bar this vault has already watched the genotox column clear for adopted defined approaches.

⚠ One beaten-average-pathologist result is not a validated replacement, and it is one lab's preprint. The vault's standing discipline — never infer loop_readiness from an adjacent fact — still applies: a single, unreplicated reader-study result that beats an average (not every) pathologist is evidence of a plausible escape hatch, not a demonstrated one. loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop is never closed-loop-ready, and neither reading is an endorsement of using TRACE in a real regulatory submission today.

⚠ Correlated blind spot check

No correlated_blind_spot recorded. This map is not a battery component and shares no chemical-failure-mode evidence with any existing map; it is an infrastructure/channel fact, not an assay run against chemicals.
