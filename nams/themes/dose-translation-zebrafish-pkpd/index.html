Zebrafish disease model → human dose translation — translational-dose-prediction — method

🚩 CONVENTION DEPARTURE — flagged for human review. Auto-coined 2026-08-26. Two departures at once, and both are real. (1) Its endpoint (translational-dose-prediction) is not a toxicological endpoint — it is a bridge, like the vault's dose-bridge map. (2) ⚠ The source is about efficacy, not toxicity. It is an anti-tuberculosis drug's exposure–response, translated to patients. It is ingested because it is one of the few published cases where a non-animal method's quantitative prediction was checked against a human clinical outcome, which is the validation direction almost nothing in this vault is measured in. No toxicological hazard claim follows from it. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-08-26). One source, one drug, one laboratory, retrospective, evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, regulatory_status: none, full text unread.

What we believe now

The chain. A previously established dose–effect relationship for bedaquiline in zebrafish larvae infected with Mycobacterium marinum (a fish mycobacterium producing a granulomatous disease structurally like human tuberculosis) was rescaled to human potency using the ratio of minimum inhibitory concentrations between that bacterium and M. tuberculosis, then combined with a clinical population pharmacokinetic model to simulate what two published phase 2a trials (25–400 mg daily) would have measured in patients' sputum.

The result, and this vault rarely gets two fit statistics out of a paywalled abstract: *"a mean absolute percentage error of 10.37% and a root mean squared error of *0.74 log₁₀ colony-forming units per millilitre of sputum" across all doses and timepoints. For scale, 0.74 log₁₀ is a factor of about 5.5 in bacterial count — respectable on an endpoint whose clinical range spans orders of magnitude, and the alternative early-translation evidence is rodents and rabbits.

Why a zebrafish counts as a New Approach Methodology at all. Under EU Directive 2010/63/EU a zebrafish embryo or larva is not a protected animal until it feeds independently, around five days post-fertilisation. That is a regulatory definition, not a biological claim, and the vault draws the same line for the fish embryo acute toxicity test. Larvae are also transparent, millimetre-scale and well-plate compatible — a known robotics target.

⚠ The conditional that carries the whole result. The alignment is reported as following from "accounting for in vitro drug sensitivity." The whole-organism model on its own was not the predictor; the predictor was a three-part chain — living model organism for the response shape, a cheap in-vitro measurement for the potency offset, a human pharmacokinetic model for the exposure. The most biologically complete component still needed a correction from the simplest possible experiment, because the discrepancy was a property of the pathogen substitution rather than of the host. When a model substitutes more than one thing at once, each substitution needs its own bridge.

And it is retrospective. The trials were published before the prediction. That is a fit to known data, not a forward prediction, and the difference is the difference between an explanation and a method.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop. Rule 4 of the deterministic ladder: the readout is instrument-readable — bacterial burden in transparent larvae, by imaging or plating — but no fixed data-interpretation procedure exists.

Walk the chain and the blocker localises cleanly:

  • The fish leg is automatable in principle. Well-plate compatible larvae, imaging readout. The vault already holds automated zebrafish embryo sorting and machine-scored zebrafish behaviour as separate maps.
  • The pharmacokinetic leg is arithmetic — the layer the vault grades computational-connective.
  • The correction leg blocks it. Someone must decide that a potency ratio is needed at all, choose the organism pair, and apply it. On this compound that decision was made with the trial results in hand. No published procedure makes that choice for a new drug.

The failure mode worth encoding, because it is silent. An automated pipeline that skipped the correction would output a confident, precise, wrong dose, with no internal signal that anything was missing — nothing in a zebrafish experiment reveals that its bacterium is not the patient's bacterium. That knowledge lives outside the loop, in the head of the person who chose the model.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Dose bridge (composes-with, the closest map in the vault): pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics holds the host-side version of this argument — a concentration in a dish becomes a dose in a person only through a pharmacokinetic model. This map adds the agent-side version: when the model organism's pathogen is also a substitute, a second bridge is needed, and it comes from a completely different kind of experiment. The two together are the vault's clearest statement that a translation is a chain of bridges, each covering one substitution.
  • External-validity thread (extends, and this is the reason to keep the map): validation-paradigm-nam-confidence holds the vault's work on what validation should mean, and the standing complaint — recorded from several sources — that non-animal methods are graded on agreement with animal tests whose own agreement with humans is not in the frame. This source is the counter-example: a human comparator, in clinical units, with two error statistics. Worth reading as a template for what a strong external-validation claim looks like, independent of whether this fish is the right model.
  • Zebrafish family (redundancy? no — different questions entirely): developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity, fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet and developmental-neurotoxicity-zebrafish-behavior-ldtt all use the same organism at a similar life stage. They ask does this chemical harm the fish; this map asks what dose in a person reproduces an effect measured in the fish. Same animal, opposite direction of inference. Cross-linked as a family, explicitly not redundant — and the family is worth noting for a different reason: it is the vault's largest cluster of methods on one organism, and the loop tiers within it differ, which is the vault's standing evidence that format does not decide loop-readiness, readout objectivity does.
  • Automation edge (composes-with): the vault holds automated zebrafish embryo sorting and machine-scored larval readouts as existing work. Those are the components that would make this map's fish leg loopable, and they exist. The blocker is one layer up, in the translation, which is worth stating because it is the vault's recurring shape: the robots are usually not the problem.
  • Coverage observation, not an edge (the sharpest thing here): you can validate against humans for tuberculosis because patients are treated and measured. You cannot for developmental neurotoxicity or carcinogenicity, because the human experiment is not one anybody runs. The endpoints where non-animal methods are hardest to validate are precisely the endpoints where human data cannot be generated on purpose. That is a structural constraint on the whole field rather than a property of any assay, and it belongs in the open-gaps log.
