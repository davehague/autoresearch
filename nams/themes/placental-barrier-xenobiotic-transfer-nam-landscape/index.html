What this map is, and what it is not

This map tracks placental xenobiotic transfer — the dosimetry question of how much of a maternal exposure reaches the fetal circulation, and by what route. It is upstream of, and distinct from, the vault's existing reproductive/developmental maps, which ask whether an exposed fetus/embryo shows an effect: whether fertility is impaired (reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams), whether hormone-driven reproductive-tract development is disrupted (reproductive-female-ovary-endometrium-chip, reproductive-testicular-steroidogenesis-leydig-models), or whether structural malformation results (developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity). None of those maps' assays can be correctly dosed or interpreted without knowing how much of a chemical actually crossed the placenta in the first place — this map is the missing input layer.

What we believe now

  • 2026-09-30 — MAP COINED. A field audit, not new data — but it is the vault's first source to score NAM maturity on Technology Readiness Level (TRL), and the TRL score exposes an axis-disagreement the vault's own evidence_tier/regulatory_status split would otherwise flatten. Ingested brownbill-doman-2026-placental-transfer-xenobiotics-dart-nams (Journal of Physiology, Topical Review, Open Access, University of Manchester, funding_flag: [method-developer] — the corresponding author co-leads the international "PlaNet" ex vivo-perfusion consortium and is reviewing, and TRL-scoring, his own field's leading method). (1) Two real physical NAM tiers, TRL-scored. Ex vivo dual-perfusion of a donated term human placental lobule — both maternal and fetal circuits independently cannulated, reproducing two-sided placental blood flow — is scored TRL 7 ("system prototype demonstrated in an operational environment") and is already used by regulators as weight-of-evidence for fetal drug exposure, generated across multiple independent academic labs partnered with industry. In vitro placental-barrier-on-chip and organoid models (BeWo, primary trophoblast or trophoblast-stem-cell-derived, mimicking the two-cell-layer vasculosyncytial membrane) sit at TRL 4 ("validated in a laboratory") — real, published, but structurally unable to reach the physical thinness of the real membrane and not yet cross-validated against the ex vivo gold standard. → placental-nam-trl-mismatch-adopted-in-practice-not-validated (2) The animal DART comparator's own anatomy may be structurally biased, not just imprecise, for one whole drug class. Human placenta is haemomonochorial (one cell layer between maternal/fetal blood); rabbit is haemodichorial (two); rat is haemotrichorial (three) — by Fick's law, more layers slows small-molecule diffusion. For monoclonal antibodies specifically — the exact drug class the FDA's 2025-26 roadmap targets first for animal-testing elimination — the human yolk sac (the first-trimester antibody-transfer route) is ephemeral and regresses by ~11 weeks, while the differently-structured rodent/rabbit yolk sac persists and transfers antibodies throughout gestation; one cited comparison found rat/rabbit fetal IgG transfer running 3–6× higher than in cynomolgus monkey at an equivalent stage. → placental-barrier-species-structure-may-overestimate-antibody-exposure (3) loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop, by a route the vault has not seen before. Rule 4 of the deterministic ladder applies (machine-readable readout — analytical concentration measurement — no fixed decision rule), but the practical blocker underneath it is not a missing rule alone: the field's own most mature NAM structurally cannot be automated or scaled past its access_gate, because its raw material is fresh, consented, term human placental tissue obtainable only at a handful of university hospitals. The review states outright that this model may never reach an "industrially relevant environment" TRL for this reason — a ceiling this vault's loop-readiness ladder has not encountered in this shape (an access_gate on the organism-derived starting material, not on a commercial instrument or cell line). (4) No single NAM replaces the animal model; this is explicitly a pipeline, not a competition. The ex vivo/in vitro wet-lab tiers feed compartmental PBPK-style models (the review cites a worked doravirine example) that predict fetal exposure — the same computational-connective role this vault already tracks in pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics. The review's own honest ceiling: even a fully mature human placental NAM pipeline is expected to narrow, not eliminate, uncertainty — likely to "within one order of magnitude" of a true human exposure value.

On the PARC claim: Brownbill & Doman confirm the in vitro half (chip/organoid models remain TRL 4, unvalidated against the ex vivo standard) but complicate the "no validated NAM" framing by naming a different technology — ex vivo dual-perfusion of real donor tissue, not an in-vitro reconstruction — that is TRL 7 and already used informally as regulatory weight-of-evidence, despite carrying no formal validation either. Read together: PARC and this review actually agree on the substance (nothing here is formally validated) and differ only on emphasis (PARC's "NAMs" reads as in-vitro reconstructions; Brownbill's TRL-7 candidate is intact human tissue, ex vivo not in vitro — a taxonomy distinction the vault had not previously drawn). This is the same loop_blocker shape already on this map — a machine-readable concentration reading with no guideline converting it into a pass/fail DART call — now with a concrete numeric illustration of how far a mechanistic in-vitro dose can sit from a physiologically realistic one.
