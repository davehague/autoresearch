The nanomaterial NAM landscape — an unmapped domain gets its first entry — landscape map

Why this map exists

Every method map this vault holds grades a small molecule's hazard profile. A nanomaterial's hazard depends on its physical state — size distribution, surface coating, agglomeration, dissolution rate — which is not fixed at synthesis and can drift before an assay ever runs. No existing map's frontmatter has a field for that. This review is the vault's first evidence the domain is populated with active methodology, not merely a theoretical gap in coverage.

What we believe now

A 39-author, multi-institutional nanosafety-consortium review (Horizon Europe-funded: CHIASMA, AlChemiSSts, MACRAMÉ) synthesizes enabling technologies for non-animal nanomaterial risk assessment: 2D/3D cell culture and precision-cut lung slices; organoids and microphysiological systems (vascularised, multi-organ); single-cell multi-omics (mass cytometry, spatial transcriptomics, scRNA-seq); label-free imaging (hyperspectral, enhanced dark-field microscopy — necessary because many nanomaterials cannot be fluorescently tagged without altering their surface chemistry); and a computational layer — AutoML, generative AI, nano-QSAR, physiologically based kinetic (PBK) modelling, and in vitro-to-in vivo extrapolation (IVIVE) anchored to adverse outcome pathways.

The central claim is a condition, not a result: nanomaterial NAM acceptance needs the same biological-relevance/reproducibility/context-of-use bar molecular NAMs already face, plus physicochemical characterization, exposure control, and dosimetry — because the test article's identity itself is not stable.

Regulatory coordination: OECD harmonisation and the Mutual Acceptance of Data (MAD) system are named as the cross-jurisdiction mechanism; the EU's 2009 cosmetics animal-testing ban and REACH's alternatives requirement are the most concrete facts recovered. Industrial readiness is claimed across pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, agrochemicals, antimicrobials and environmental sectors, offered as scalability evidence rather than a validation result.
