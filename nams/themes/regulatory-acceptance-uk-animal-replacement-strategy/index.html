UK "Replacing animals in science" strategy (11 Nov 2025) — the acceptance axis, UK side — framework map

Its "endpoint" (regulatory-acceptance) is not a toxicological endpoint; it maps a regulatory-policy object (a national strategy), not an assay. It is the vault's fourth regulatory-framework map and its third jurisdiction (US FDA ×2, EU, now UK). Auto-coined by the nightly auto run and logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-07-22) — deliberately. evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted on the concepts records only that the claims come from the issuing government's own primary document. The strategy itself is regulatory_status: proposed — a non-binding policy paper, every target hedged "we will aim to", with the Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986 remaining the only operative law.

What we believe now

On 11 November 2025 the UK government (DSIT, Home Office, Defra) published a five-year strategy for replacing animals in science. Two things in it are load-bearing for this vault, and neither is the one the press ran with.

(1) It is a government-issued readiness taxonomy. Part III sorts fourteen named animal tests into three "baskets" by how mature the replacement is — basket 1 "mature replacement technologies already exist" (8 tests), basket 2 "alternatives exist but are not validated or sufficiently robust" (5), basket 3 "no suitable alternative currently exists" (1) — each with a dated target. The basket boundary is drawn on validation status, not science quality, which is exactly the evidence_tier / regulatory_status split this vault insists on. It is the first time a national government has published a readiness grading directly comparable to the vault's own, over methods the vault already tracks (uk-three-basket-readiness-taxonomy).

(2) The 2026 endpoint targets are aims, not mandates — and the coverage says otherwise. Skin irritation, eye irritation and skin sensitisation each carry "By the end of 2026 we will aim to apply only validated alternative methods to satisfy UK regulatory requirements." Trade and campaign coverage renders this as a hard requirement ("must use non-animal methods"). It is not. The vault graded against the primary text (uk-2026-targets-are-aims-not-mandates) — the leads-are-not-sources rail earning its keep on a live case.

Institution built: UKCVAM, the UK Centre for the Validation of Alternative Methods — hub-and-spokes, filling a stated gap ("no national coordination of validation efforts and no current dedicated laboratory capacity", the post-Brexit hole where EURL ECVAM used to be). Its remit says the spokes will "explore international interlaboratory ring trials or innovative ways of agreeing regulatory readiness" (ukcvam-validation-body-beyond-the-ring-trial).

Money the document itemises: £30m (preclinical translational models hub), £8m (OpenBind), up to £12m (data infrastructure), £6.2m (seven regulatory-innovation centres). The widely-quoted £75m total is press framing and is not in the primary text.

Concrete deliverable to watch: from 2026, biennially, UK regulators (MHRA, FSA, Environment Agency, HSE, VMD) are to publish a coherent list of alternative methods acceptable as data sources plus a list of regulatory priorities. That list, if it appears, is a far better acceptance signal for the vault's skin, eye and sensitisation maps than any target date.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — a policy document, not an assay. But the strategy is a loop-relevant document twice over, and neither way is in its own text.

  • It names AI as a central enabler and creates CEiRSI, an in-silico regulatory-science centre with MHRA, FDA and EMA. And it says nothing about how an AI model would itself be validated — the hole that regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility and fda-2025-ai-regulatory-decision-making-draft-guidance exist to name. A government planning to lean on AI for regulatory decisions without a stated model-credibility position is a gap worth tracking.

  • Basket 2 proposes an automatable ecotoxicity battery out of two assays that share a failure mode — see the rail, below.

⚠ The rail hit

Basket 2 proposes replacing the in-vivo fish acute toxicity test (OECD TG 203) under UK REACH by end-2028 with OECD TG 249 (RTgill-W1 gill cell line) + OECD TG 236 (fish embryo) + QSAR, as a weight-of-evidence combination. This vault flagged that exact pair as a correlated_blind_spot on 2026-07-11: neither has a functioning nervous system and neither has full biotransformation, so neurotoxicants and bioactivation-dependent compounds are missed by both, for the same structural reason. Two tests, one vote. Nothing in the strategy acknowledges it. Full argument: uk-basket2-fish-battery-rebuilds-a-known-blind-spot.
