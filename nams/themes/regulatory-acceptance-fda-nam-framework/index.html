FDA CDER's NAM validation framework (draft, March 2026) — the acceptance axis itself — framework map

What we believe now

FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research issued General Considerations for the Use of New Approach Methodologies in Drug Development (draft, March 2026). Its central sentence:

"It is important to note that for a NAM to be considered for review in drug development, the test does not necessarily need to be validated. However, as FDA gains confidence in these tools, they could be formally adopted to reduce or replace specific animal tests. A fit-for-purpose NAM, even if not validated, may adequately address specific toxicological concerns. When a NAM is used it is always in the context of the weight of evidence (WOE)…"

The framework — four features, none of them "has a Test Guideline":

  1. Context of Use (COU) — the specific regulatory decision the NAM is meant to inform. Examples given include supporting patient-monitoring intensity, supporting dose selection, explaining a mechanism behind an observed adverse event, and — strikingly — "justifying not using an animal species because it does not add value to regulatory decision-making."
  2. Human biological relevance — do the cell types, species of origin and architecture actually recapitulate the human physiology the conclusion depends on?
  3. Technical characterization — robust, reliable, reproducible; aligned to OECD GIVIMP (2018).
  4. Fit-for-purpose — does it answer the question actually being asked, in this COU?

Validation ≠ qualification, and CDER separates them: validation establishes "the accuracy, reliability, and relevance of a procedure … for a specific context of use"; qualification is an FD&C §507 Drug Development Tool determination. This guidance covers validation only. It sets no numeric acceptance thresholds of any kind.

Statutory basis. Section 3209 of the Food and Drug Omnibus Reform Act of 2022 (the FDA Modernization Act 2.0 provisions) amended FD&C §505 and PHS §351 "to clarify that data from nonanimal testing may be sufficient to support an IND or BLA." The legal permission predates this guidance by four years; what the guidance adds is how CDER will judge such data.

Why this map exists: the acceptance axis is measuring the wrong unit

The vault's regulatory_status ladder —

OECD-TG-adopted > under-validation > proposed > none

— silently encodes a method-centric theory of acceptance: a method gets validated, earns a Test Guideline, becomes usable. Every rung is a property of the method.

CDER describes a parallel route in which none of that is required. A sponsor may bring a NAM with no formal validation and no Test Guideline and have it carry regulatory weight, provided they can show it is fit for purpose for a defined context of use inside a weight-of-evidence argument about a particular drug. On that route acceptance is not a property of the method — it is a property of the triple

(method × context of use × submission)

The same assay can suffice for one dossier and fail for the next. That is not a rung on the vault's ladder; it is a different ladder, and it is not attached to the method at all.

The consequence, stated plainly: regulatory_status: none does not mean "cannot be used regulatorily." It means "has no guideline" — a much weaker claim, about a different unit, than the vault has been narrating.

The tension this map must carry, not resolve

  • For the fit-for-purpose route: formal validation is slow, and slowness has a cost. The vault has the number — TG 445A was ECVAM-validated in 2014 and adopted in 2026: twelve years (metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg). The Physicians Committee, whose interest runs toward replacing animal tests, welcomed the provision precisely because "the lengthy validation process can sideline promising, human-centric methods while drug developers continue to rely on historical animal testing."
  • Against it: validation is what makes a performance claim trustworthy. "Fit-for-purpose, argued by the sponsor, judged case-by-case" is exactly the regime in which a developer-run 87%/100% panel starts doing regulatory work it has not earned. The vault's funding_flag exists because that failure mode is common, and three of the maps this framework most obviously benefits carry developer flags.

Both are true. FDA is trading throughput for rigour, deliberately, with a credibility framework meant to stop the trade becoming a giveaway. Whether it holds is an empirical question nobody can answer yet.
