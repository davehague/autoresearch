Fish Embryo Acute Toxicity (FET) test (OECD TG 236) — aquatic ecotoxicity — method

Status: consensus (2026-07-07) — regulatorily adopted on both axes, with a documented applicability limit. evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 236). Coins the vault's first aquatic / environmental (ecotoxicity) endpoint — a distinct domain from the mammalian-health maps.

What we believe now

The Fish Embryo Acute Toxicity test exposes newly-fertilised zebrafish (Danio rerio) eggs to a chemical for 96 h and reads lethality from four apical endpoints (coagulation, no somite formation, no heartbeat, non-detached tail-bud). Because embryos ≤120 h post-fertilisation are not a protected life stage, FET is an animal-reduction/replacement NAM standing in for the adult acute fish test (OECD TG 203). Adopted as OECD TG 236 (2013; guideline updated 2025) on an OECD inter-laboratory validation (2012–2014). Honest limit: FET correlates strongly with adult-fish acute toxicity for baseline/narcotic chemicals but under-predicts for neurotoxic, biotransformation-dependent, or low-embryo-uptake substances — so it is applied with that domain caveat, not as a blanket TG 203 replacement.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — Whole-organism, but do not read that as unloopable — zebrafish embryos are well-plate compatible and the four apical endpoints (coagulation, somite, heartbeat, tail-bud) are near-binary. Readout objectivity, not assay format, sets the tier. Shares a blind spot with RTgill-W1: neither has a nervous system or full metabolism, so both miss neurotoxicants and bioactivation-dependent compounds. Stacked, they are one vote, not two.

  • Blocker: the four apical endpoints are near-binary but not formalised into a published DIP
  • Correlated blind spot: shares a failure mode with [ecotox-rtgill-w1-fish-cell-line]. In a battery these are one vote, not two — and an automated loop trusting the decision rule will compound the error rather than catch it.

Status holds at consensus; the applicability caveat above is not softened — it is now known to be structural rather than incidental. ecotox-rtgill-w1-fish-cell-line (OECD TG 249, RTgill-W1 gill cell line) is also adopted, also aimed at TG 203, and its guideline documents the same two failure classes this map has carried since 2026-07-07: neurotoxic chemicals (TG 249: those "acting through specific ion channels or receptors typical of brain tissue" are "less sensitive" in the assay than in fish) and metabolism-dependent toxicity (TG 249's worked case: allyl alcohol, under-predicted because the cells do not form acrolein). Two independent-looking NAMs, one correlated blind spot — see the connect note below. Also worth holding: TG 249 claims less than this map does (pre-screening / ITS evidence, not replacement of TG 203).

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • Same organism, different endpoint (contrast — NOT redundancy): the zebrafish embryo is also the model in developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity, but that map reads human developmental hazard / teratogenicity (morphological malformation, teratogenic index) whereas FET reads aquatic apical lethality (ecotox). Same fish, different biology — cross-linked so the two are not collapsed.
  • Global-ripple: this is an ecotoxicology endpoint — it predicts aquatic lethality, not human hazard, and has no confirm/refute edge against any human-health map (skin, eye, sensitisation, genotox, systemic). A coverage-breadth addition that opens the environmental-toxicity column.
  • Redundancy watch — RESOLVED 2026-07-11, and the answer is the uncomfortable one. The second aquatic NAM has landed: ecotox-rtgill-w1-fish-cell-line (TG 249). It is not an independent line of evidence alongside this one. Both are adopted, both stand in for TG 203, and both under-predict the same two chemical classes for the same structural reasons — neither has a nervous system to poison (so ion-channel / receptor neurotoxicants read low), and neither has full biotransformation (so toxicity requiring bioactivation reads low; TG 249's allyl-alcohol→acrolein case is the cleanest demonstration in either guideline). The practical consequence: a weight-of-evidence assessment that stacks FET and RTgill-W1 looks like two votes and is closer to one, and the chemicals it misses are precisely the neurotoxic and the bioactivation-dependent — the ones where under-prediction is least acceptable. Flagged, not resolved: nothing in the vault's aquatic column supplies the missing competence. This is the first correlated blind spot the connect layer has caught between two adopted methods, and it is a stronger caution than any single method's caveat: battery independence is an assumption, and here it fails.
  • Partial redundancy (stated plainly): the two overlap heavily in what they predict (acute fish lethality) while differing in what they are (whole developing embryo vs isolated gill epithelium). Overlapping claims with a correlated failure domain is the least useful combination a battery can have — recorded so the column's apparent "two-method coverage" is never read as depth it does not have.
