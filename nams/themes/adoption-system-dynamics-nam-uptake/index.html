NAM adoption — the system that decides whether a validated method gets used — system map

🚩 NEW MAP, AUTO-COINED 2026-08-10 — CONVENTION DEPARTURE, flagged for human review. Not a method map, not a framework map, not an endpoint-coverage map. Its endpoint (adoption-system) is not a toxicological endpoint. It is the vault's seventh non-method map, alongside validation-paradigm-nam-confidence, risk-assessment-ngra, automation-self-driving-labs, automation-tox21-qhts-platform, nam-reagent-provenance-animal-derived-components and the regulatory-acceptance-* family. The case for coining it rather than folding it into validation-paradigm-nam-confidence: that map holds the machinery that decides what evidence counts. This one holds what happens after a method has counted and been accepted and is still not used. Those are different objects and the vault already had the measurement proving the second one exists. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Why this map exists

The vault grades three things, and all three are properties of the method: does the science hold, has a regulator accepted it, could a machine run it.

None of them predicts whether anyone uses it — and the vault already holds the measurement proving they don't. Japan's medicines regulator went into its own approval files for 2020–2024 and counted what applicants actually filed: non-animal methods in 22 of 115 tests (about one in five) and 10 of 25 products, concentrated where an adopted Test Guideline exists (regulatory-acceptance-japan-pmda-nams). A method being adopted and a method being used are separate facts, and the vault could measure the gap without being able to say anything about what produces it.

This map is where the answers to that go.

What we believe now

One proposed answer, with no numbers attached: it is a feedback structure, not a list of obstacles.

The CHANGE project ran two three-day workshops in Oslo — 2024 "Explore" and 2025 "Reflect" — with practitioners from regulatory agencies, industry, civil-society organisations and academia, recorded the sessions, and analysed the transcripts by multi-step thematic analysis guided by systems-thinking principles. The output is a causal model in which "structural, economic, social, cultural and technical factors ... are interlinked through reinforcing and balancing feedback loops that promote or inhibit the effective use of NAMs."

Why the distinction is not word-play. If uptake is a list of independent barriers, the rational strategy is to work the list — fix the cheapest, then the next. If it is a feedback structure, a point fix gets absorbed and the system returns to its prior behaviour, because whatever produced the barrier is still producing it. What you need instead is a leverage point: somewhere a small intervention changes how the whole structure behaves. See nam-uptake-is-a-feedback-system-not-a-technical-gap.

Two leverage points are named, and both land on things this vault already tracks.

  1. "Enhancing the stability and reliability of the supply chain." The vault has been recording exactly this object one assay at a time, as the optional access_gate modifier — HepaRG's single licensor, Corrositex as the sole validated kit, the VM7Luc4E2 cell line unobtainable from its source, Derek Nexus behind a licence — plus nam-reagent-provenance-animal-derived-components for the animal-derived components inside methods sold as animal-free. This says it is not a footnote, it is a system-level determinant, and it is invisible to all three axes. → the-reagent-supply-chain-is-a-named-leverage-point
  2. "Ensuring that actors in the regulatory toxicology system have both educational and institutional support to effectively change established practices." This has no representation in the vault at all. Every rung of the loop-readiness ladder asks whether a machine could execute a method. No rung asks whether the organisation around the machine can absorb the change. On this model that is one of the two places where intervention would actually work.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — this map is not a method and has no readout, no decision rule and no inputs to grade. Recorded here because the reason is itself a finding: the ladder measures a machine's capability to run an assay, and this source's central claim is that the binding constraint on non-animal methods is not the machine's capability but the system's structure. The most loop-ready assay in this vault is still not run by most of the laboratories entitled to run it, and no axis here can express that.

not-applicable now carries three distinct meanings across the vault — the axis does not apply (regulators, loop infrastructure, supply chains), the axis is not assignable from what was read (vaccine batch release, logged 2026-08-09), and now the axis measures the wrong actor. Logged.

Not scientific confidence, not cost, not automatability. And note that it binds even where acceptance has already been granted — what companies want is not "a guideline exists" but "a regulator will take my data for my substance and not punish me for having generated it," which is why the proposed remedy is voluntary data sharing under "safe-space" conditions. That is a request for indemnity, not for science. → regulatory-acceptance-is-the-dominant-adoption-barrier-by-user-report (4) ⚠ THE FINDING THE VAULT SHOULD KEEP: the most physiologically advanced platform is the least used. The push toward realistic biology and the push toward automatability point in opposite directions, and this is the first evidence the divergence shows up in what laboratories actually buy. Deflationary reading recorded and probably the larger part of it: food safety is a low-margin sector asking narrow questions, and nobody buys an organ chip to screen a flavouring. → the-most-advanced-platform-is-the-least-used (5) It independently reaches this map's second leverage point. One sector, with a regulator and an evidentiary culture that do not generalise to pharmaceuticals or industrial chemicals. The second is the one this map did not hold. This vault's standing reading of a strong method with regulatory_status: none is regulator-shaped — the committee has not met, the guideline is queued. This names a different blockage: the developers do not turn up. A validation is published, cited, real, and nobody enters it into the process that would adopt it. That is not inertia, it is absence, and unlike inertia it is something an identifiable party could fix cheaply.
