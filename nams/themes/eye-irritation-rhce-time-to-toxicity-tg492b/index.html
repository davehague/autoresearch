RhCE Time-to-Toxicity (OECD TG 492B) — eye irritation — the first single method that returns a category

Status: consensus (2026-07-25) — 🚩 auto-coined 2026-07-25 · evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted (OECD TG 492B, adopted 30 June 2022, corrected 25 June 2024), loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready. All three axes agree, which is unusual here and is not the interesting part. The interesting part is that the vault's newest adopted eye method has its weakest headline accuracy — and that reading the accuracy correctly requires reading a 3×3 matrix rather than a number.

What we believe now

A lab-grown human corneal tissue that sorts a chemical into all three UN GHS eye-hazard classes on its own, and is "recommended as a full replacement to the in vivo Draize acute eye irritation test for classification of chemicals."

Every other eye method in this vault answers half the question. TG 492 and TG 494 rule out only ("No Category" or "keep testing"). TG 460 rules in only. TG 496 straddles weakly. Resolving the middle class — Category 2, reversible eye irritation, where most consumer chemistry lives — has required the TG 467 defined approach, which combines two assays. TG 492B is the vault's first adopted single method that returns a three-way classification.

And the mechanism for the third category is the clock. Same tissue as TG 492 (reconstructed human cornea-like epithelium, ≥4 viable layers, air–liquid interface). Same readout as TG 492 (MTT dye conversion read as optical density). The only change is that the chemical is applied for several different durations and the rule reads the pattern across them — hence "Time-to-Toxicity." Liquids get three exposures (5 min neat; 16 and 120 min at 20% dilution); solids get two (30 and 120 min).

Why time works: GHS Category 1 means irreversible damage and Category 2 means reversible. A single snapshot of cell death cannot see reversibility; how fast the tissue dies approximates it. That substitution is the method's best idea and its deepest limit — the guideline states plainly that "the reversibility of tissue lesions cannot be evaluated per se in the HCE TTT test method." See tg492b-three-way-eye-classification-from-exposure-time.

The decision rule (Table 5) — pure arithmetic on thresholds and times, no expert judgement:

  • It never calls a blinding chemical safe. Zero of fifty. All its Cat 1 errors under-grade severity (Cat 1 → Cat 2), which still labels the product hazardous. That is the failure direction you want.
  • The dangerous cell is not on the diagonal: 12.5% of genuine irritants were called "No Category" — about one in eight real irritants declared to need no warning label.
  • 23.3% of harmless chemicals get over-labelled as Cat 2. Cost, not danger.
  • Reproducibility: 85–95% within-lab (liquids) / 100% (solids); 90% / 100% between labs, three laboratories including assay-naïve ones.

Both error concentrations straddle boundaries defined on reversibility — the property the method cannot measure. See tg492b-three-way-resolution-costs-a-quarter-of-its-accuracy.

⚠️ The number has a noise floor the guideline does not state. It is scored against the rabbit Draize test, which TG 492 records as carrying ~12% within-test variability. How much of the missing 25.6% belongs to the method and how much to the reference is not determinable here — and ICCVAM's 2024 framework states this as a general requirement (nam-variance-judged-against-animal-test-variance).

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: closed-loop-ready, loop_blocker: none. Instrument readout (optical density, or HPLC peak area), fixed arithmetic decision rule, no human in the call. The vault's 25th member of this tier.

And it arrives carrying something the tier has never had: a machine-evaluable rule for repeating the experiment. Paragraph 44 — borderline results, defined as non-concordant replicates and/or mean viability within ±5% of the cut-off, trigger a second test, and a third if the first two disagree. That is uncertainty sampling with a budget of three, written into an adopted regulatory decision rule. The vault's loop has four legs and this is the first time the experiment-selection leg has been found inside a Test Guideline rather than in the autonomous-lab literature. See tg492b-decision-rule-contains-its-own-retest-trigger.

Two brakes carried alongside the tier, per the rail that loop-ready is never an endorsement:

  • access_gate — one validated supplier (EPISKIN's SkinEthic™ HCE). Same shape as HepaRG's single licensor and Corrositex's sole kit. A loop built on it cannot be stood up elsewhere without buying the tissue.

  • domain_gate — and note what kind. TG 494's domain_gate is measurable: a robot can run its pH and absorbance pre-test and decline. TG 492B's exclusions (MTT interference, gases/aerosols, insoluble Cat 1 solids, mixtures) are told, not measured — the loop must be given them. TG 492B is the more capable method and the less honest one about its own edges.

  • Correlated blind spot: shares a failure mode with [eye-irritation-rhce-no-category, eye-irritation-vitrigel-tg494, eye-irritation-serious-eye-damage-da]. In a battery these are one vote, not several — and an automated loop trusting the decision rule will compound the error rather than catch it.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

  • ⚠️ Correlated blind spot — the vault's tightest, and the guidance concedes it in three words. eye-irritation-rhce-no-category (TG 492) and this map are the same tissue technology reading the same property by the same readout, differing only in exposure schedule. GD 263's standard template field "Scientific basis incl. MoA" for TG 492B reads, in full, "See Section 4.3.4" — TG 492's module. Tighter than the TG 494 ↔ TG 492 pair added 2026-07-19, where the two at least read different physical properties (electrical resistance vs viability) of the same tissue. Consequence: running both TG 492 and TG 492B and taking their agreement as confirmation produces one result reported twice. → tg492-and-tg492b-are-one-measurement-read-twice
  • Battery membership. A component of, and an alternative route through, eye-hazard-iata-gd263 — GD 263 routes suspected Category 2 chemicals to "either the Defined Approaches of TG 467 based on a top-down or bottom-up approach or the time-to-toxicity test of TG 492B." So this map and eye-irritation-serious-eye-damage-da (TG 467) are substitutes at the same decision point, and GD 263 names them together as "stand-alone methods that can predict three UN GHS categories."
  • Composes-with, and the independence is partly real. eye-macromolecular-ordered-matrix-tg496 (TG 496) is acellular — protein-matrix denaturation, no tissue, no cells — so it is the one eye method in the vault that is mechanistically independent of the RhCE column. That pairing is the good case the rail exists to distinguish from the bad ones (the Tox21 counter-design), and it is now the only structurally independent vote among five eye maps. Honest limit: neither has metabolism, so both are jointly blind to metabolite-mediated eye hazard.
  • The persistence gap runs through this map too. GD 263 reports that BCOP (TG 437) and ICE (TG 438) "underpredict 14-47% of the in vivo Cat. 1 chemicals … mostly those inducing persistent effects," because they measure acute injury and cannot observe recovery. TG 492B has the same missing dimension and handles it more cleverly (kinetics as a proxy) rather than solving it. The persistence blind spot is a property of the whole adopted eye column, not of two ex-vivo assays. → bcop-and-ice-share-a-persistence-blind-spot
  • MTT-column sibling (structural, cross-endpoint). The same readout chemistry carries skin-irritation-rhe (TG 439), skin-corrosion-rhe (TG 431), eye-irritation-rhce-no-category (TG 492) and phototoxicity-rhe-pt (TG 498). All share the MTT interference problem and the same HPLC/UPLC workaround. A single reagent chemistry underpins the vault's largest block of closed-loop-ready methods — worth naming as a systemic dependency, adjacent to the reagent-provenance finding in nam-reagent-provenance-animal-derived-components.
  • Global-ripple: walked every other map. Two near-edges named and refused. This is not a skin-corrosion method despite the shared tissue-plus-MTT architecture — the corneal and epidermal constructs are different tissues with different barriers, and GD 263 treats skin-corrosion data as a separate module feeding the eye assessment (a corrosive to skin is presumed a Cat 1 to eye), which is a compose-with, not an equivalence. And it is not a potency method: it returns a hazard class, not a dose, so it is not comparable to skin-sensitization-quantitative-pod-da's quantitative point of departure.
