Why this map exists

Safety pharmacology is not toxicology, and this vault had quietly assumed it was. Toxicology asks whether a substance damages an organ, a chromosome or an embryo — usually over repeated doses, days to months. Safety pharmacology asks whether a single dose disturbs a function that keeps you alive, immediately, at concentrations a patient would see. Blood pressure. Heart rhythm. Breathing. Consciousness and coordination.

Different question, different tests, different guideline — and a guideline with an unusual property. Most of the regulatory instruments in this vault say assess this hazard. ICH S7A says: run these measurements, on these systems, before anybody takes the drug. §2.10.1: "The effects of a test substance on the functions listed in the safety pharmacology core battery should be investigated prior to first administration in humans."

That makes it one of the few places where the animal requirement is concrete enough to be removed, and where removing it would have an immediate, countable effect.

What we believe now

The core battery is three organ systems, per §2.7: "the cardiovascular, respiratory and central nervous systems are usually considered the vital organ systems that should be studied in the core battery."

LegThe guideline's own wordsWhat it takes to run
Central nervous system (§2.7.1)"Motor activity, behavioral changes, coordination, sensory/motor reflex responses and body temperature should be evaluated. For example, a functional observation battery (FOB), modified Irwin's, or other appropriate test can be used."A trained person handling a dosed rodent and scoring how it moves, reacts and behaves. Human judgement is the instrument.
Cardiovascular (§2.7.2)"Blood pressure, heart rate, and the electrocardiogram should be evaluated. In vivo, in vitro and/or ex vivo evaluations, including methods for repolarization and conductance abnormalities, should also be considered."The only leg where the year-2000 text names a dish, and the only one where this vault holds a candidate method.
Respiratory (§2.7.3)"Respiratory rate and other measures of respiratory function (e.g., tidal volume or hemoglobin oxygen saturation) should be evaluated. Clinical observation of animals is generally not adequate to assess respiratory function, and thus these parameters should be quantified by using appropriate methodologies."Instrumented breathing measurement in a live dosed animal. More instrumented than the CNS leg and no less animal-dependent.

Mapped onto this vault's coverage, the three legs come out completely differently:

  • Cardiovascular → a candidate exists and is not ready. cardiotoxicity-hipsc-cm-proarrhythmia — human stem-cell-derived heart cells on a multi-electrode array. emerging, one laboratory, no interlaboratory replication, and the decision rule is a trained neural network whose weights and code are "available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request." On 2026-08-14 the vault recorded a second cardiac decision rule held privately, on the computational side, from an unrelated company (the-second-cardiac-rule-this-vault-cannot-read).
  • Central nervous system → the vault's freshest bench-bound entry, and the automation is pointed the wrong way. neurotoxicity-cns-safety-nams-ai, opened 2026-08-13, records a strategy whose emerging capabilities are electroencephalography and "advanced sensors with artificial intelligence for continuous, non-invasive monitoring of animal behavior."
  • Respiratory → nothing. Not a poor method — none. The vault's only respiratory map, inhalation-airway-epithelium-ali, models the lining of the airway being injured by an inhaled substance. That is a different question from whether a systemic dose stops an animal breathing normally. There is no organ to biopsy and no dish that breathes.

And a 25-year-old guideline is being opened. A February 2026 Brussels workshop — industry, trade associations, regulators, contract research organisations, animal-protection NGOs — reported themes for a revision. Two of the five are the two least machine-executable things a guideline can specify: "use of state-of-the-art functional in vivo approaches" and "application of weight-of-evidence decision frameworks." See the-modernisation-pillars-name-a-better-animal-study-and-a-human-judgement for the counterweight this vault owes that reading — a weight-of-evidence framework that is genuinely written down would be a promotion, and the abstract does not say which way it goes.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: bench-bound, bench_bound_reason: no-nam-exists — rung 2 of the deterministic ladder in shared/evidence-model.md, and it is not close.

⚠ This map carries both bench-bound reasons at once, in different legs, and that is a first. The vault keeps animal-input and no-nam-exists apart on purpose, because they are entirely different investments: animal-input means somebody must invent a different prediction model; no-nam-exists means somebody must invent an assay. Here:

  • Respiratory function: no-nam-exists. Nobody has built it. Assay development, from zero.
  • Central nervous system function: no-nam-exists in the strict sense, with a wrinkle — the endpoint is behaviour in an intact organism, which is not obviously the kind of thing a dish can have. That is a harder problem than "nobody has got round to it."
  • Cardiovascular: neither, quite. A NAM exists and is emerging. Its blocker is not the ladder's — it is an unpublished rule and a vendor holding both the cells and the classifier.

The frontmatter records no-nam-exists because that is the binding constraint on the battery as a whole: you cannot replace a three-part requirement by solving one part.

What would move this map, concretely. Not a better animal study — that is a Refine and moves nothing here. Three things, in ascending order of difficulty: (1) the cardiovascular candidate publishing its decision rule and reproducing across laboratories; (2) anyone at all proposing a non-animal readout for respiratory function; (3) the revised guideline replacing "assess the effects on these systems" with a written, system-by-system evidence standard that a non-animal method could be shown to meet. The third is the only one the workshop is actually about, and the abstract does not say whether it is on the table.
