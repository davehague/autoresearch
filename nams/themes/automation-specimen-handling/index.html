Why this map exists

The vault's whole question is could a machine run this assay end to end, and its three axes answer it by looking at the end of the experiment: is the readout instrument-readable, is there a fixed rule for turning the readout into a call, does the method's own model consume an animal study.

Nothing in the schema asks whether a machine can start the experiment.

That is not a pedantic gap. For three of the vault's methods the first step is identical and non-trivial: pull several thousand zebrafish embryos out of a tank, count them, and discard the dead, the unfertilised and the wrong genotype. fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet (OECD Test Guideline 236, adopted), developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity and developmental-neurotoxicity-zebrafish-behavior-ldtt all begin there. It is the least interesting sentence in every protocol, it consumes — per this map's founding source — 40–60% of a technician's experimental time, and an autonomous system that cannot stock its own plates is a well-instrumented assistant, not a loop.

What we believe now

One commercial device has been measured at scale, by its buyer, and it is good at throughput and bad at correctness. Al-Absi et al. (2026, Qatar University, Bionomous EggSorter purchased commercially, manufacturer uninvolved and explicitly so) ran 117,956 embryos through the machine and through trained technicians:

TaskMachineHumanReading
Counting17.29/min (counts + classifies)25.88 senior / 24.53 junior (counts only)slower per embryo, p < 0.001 — but one pass, two jobs
Counting accuracy−13.41% systematic undercount (p = 0.004), spread ±130reference, spread ±48loses one embryo in seven, consistently, ~3× noisier
Quality classificationfour named categories—statistically unrelated to survival, hatching and deformity (p > 0.05)
Fluorescence sorting3.82/min2.84 / 2.05machine wins on speed
Fluorescence accuracy50.8% false negatives—throws away half the embryos it was told to find

96.1% of the embryos the machine labelled "unfertilized" hatched. An unfertilised egg cannot hatch.

The authors' own conclusion is the honest one: the system's "primary reliable application remains counting rather than quality-based sorting." They attribute the shortfalls to algorithm training rather than hardware, note the vendor ships a custom-training application, and cite two other groups doing better on adjacent zebrafish tasks (97.9% success on a robotic handling system; 87–95% on mutant-versus-sibling discrimination). The ceiling is not established here. The shipped, out-of-the-box performance is.

Two findings, and the second is the transferable one

(1) A miscount is an unrecorded change in n. Every median-lethal-concentration estimate and every "percentage of larvae affected" divides by a number the machine supplied. A one-directional 13.41% shortfall that varies with how crowded the sample was puts sample handling into the denominator of the assay's statistics.

(2) A classifier can be accurate against its labels and carry no information about the outcome those labels name. Human graders at 3–4 hours post-fertilisation are themselves unreliable — which is why the technicians in the comparison were only counting. The machine may well have learned the human's grading faithfully. It learned a proxy, and the proxy did not predict survival. For a closed loop this is the worst available failure mode: the system is fast, confident, internally consistent, and optimising a quantity that is not the quantity of interest, and nothing inside the loop can detect it because the loop's ground truth is the label. It took raising 117,956 embryos to 96 hours and counting who hatched — an experiment outside the loop entirely — to see it. → a-classifier-whose-labels-do-not-predict-the-thing-they-are-named-after

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — this map is not a method and the ladder does not grade it. But the map exists to record what the ladder cannot see, and the shape of tonight's evidence is the same split the vault finds at the other end of the pipeline: automatable at the throughput layer, not at the correctness layer. Thirty of the vault's methods run robotically and stop for a human before the call. Specimen handling now reads the same way from the opposite end.

Never read this as "lab robots do not work." One device, one software version, one facility, one strain, manufacturer's default settings, no custom training. It is one measurement, and it is the first one the vault has of its kind.
