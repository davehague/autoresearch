Why this map exists

Almost every map in this vault protects people. There is a second half of toxicology whose subject is fish, frogs and the water they live in — it has its own animal tests, its own regulators, and its own replacement pressure, because a protected vertebrate is a protected vertebrate whether it is a rat or a rainbow trout.

The vault holds exactly two methods for that entire column (fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet, OECD TG 236; ecotox-rtgill-w1-fish-cell-line, OECD TG 249) — both adopted, and already flagged as not independent of each other. It has never held a source describing the column as a whole. This is that source, and it turns out the column has been inventoried.

What we believe now

Somebody has surveyed the whole aquatic column and published the survey as data.

A systematic review screened 4,275 unique articles from PubMed, triaged with SWIFT-Active Screener (a machine-learning tool that ranks records by predicted relevance so reviewers read the likely-relevant ones first), and read 1,310 in full. Scope: non-animal methods — in silico, in chemico, in vitro and ex vivo — for fish, amphibians and marine mammals.

The deliverable is not a narrative:

"A database containing all the reviewed information is freely available and contains 18 metadata fields including for example the captured assay type, species, endpoints, and readiness level."

And the vault has read none of it. The abstract states the database exists, is free, and carries that field; the full text that would carry the URL and the scale definition is publisher-gated to this machine. So this map records, precisely: an inventory exists, with a per-assay readiness field, whose contents are unknown.

The one thing this map must not do is assume that field means what loop_readiness means. "Readiness" in a regulatory-toxicology review almost certainly means readiness for regulatory use — maturity, standardisation, validation status — and this vault's axis grades readiness for machine execution. Those are different questions, and collapsing them is the single mistake the vault's whole three-axis discipline exists to prevent. Recorded as an open question, not a finding.

The column's stated problem is acceptance, not science: "While NAMs have gained traction in human health risk assessment, their regulatory acceptance for environmental safety remains limited due to technical, scientific and policy barriers." Three named gap axes: "readiness, standardization, and mechanistic integration."

The definitional problem this column has and the vault's schema cannot hold

Fish embryos before independent feeding are not legally protected animals in the European Union, so an embryo assay counts there as a replacement. Not every jurisdiction agrees, and the review says so:

"While embryo assays may not be considered a NAM by all geographical regions and stakeholders, information from existing embryo assays was also reviewed to reduce the reliance on protected vertebrate life stages."

Whether a method is a NAM at all has, for this endpoint, different answers in different countries. The vault's regulatory_status ladder (OECD-TG-adopted > under-validation > proposed > none) assumes one global answer per method, and it cannot express a method that is a replacement in Brussels and not elsewhere. This bears directly on fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet, which is an embryo assay and which the vault grades consensus without qualification. Logged as a schema gap; no value changes tonight, because the review is describing a disagreement rather than reporting a regulatory action.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — a landscape map, not a method. Nothing here is an assay and there is nothing to grade.

Two automation-relevant facts recorded in prose instead:

  • The traditional tests are described as "resource-intensive, low-throughput", which is the property a loop would attack — and the review offers no throughput figure for any replacement.
  • An AI already chose what this review read. SWIFT-Active Screener ranked 4,275 records so that reviewers read the probably-relevant ones first. That is a small, real closed loop applied to the literature rather than the bench, and it arrived without comment in a methods sentence. The vault records it because the question "what did the machine decide not to show a person" is the same question here as at the plate.

Open gaps

  • The database was never opened. No URL in the material read. Its rung count, rung definitions, assignment procedure and per-assay values are all unknown. The highest-value follow-up this map has: retrieve the database and compare its readiness scale against this vault's loop_readiness ladder — either it is a different axis (likely) and the difference is itself the finding, or it is a partial rediscovery, and that would be the first external corroboration the third axis has ever had.
  • Full text gated. Not recorded because not read: the endpoint-by-endpoint coverage table, which named assays were scored where, the recommendations, and any figure for how much of the column has no method at all.
  • Invertebrates and terrestrial organisms are out of scope, so the vault still has no coverage picture for them and no map that could hold one.
  • The embryo definitional split (a NAM in the EU, contested elsewhere) has no home in the vault's regulatory_status ladder.
