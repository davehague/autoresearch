DCFH reactive-oxygen-species quantification, zebrafish embryo vs matured HepG2 — oxidative stress — method

🚩 NEW MAP, AUTO-COINED 2026-08-13 — flagged for human review. The second method under the oxidative-stress endpoint group, alongside oxidative-stress-redox-nrf2-profiling (the R3ACT reporter platform, coined 2026-08-09). Coined rather than folded into that map because it is a different measurement — R3ACT reads the cell's antioxidant response through genetically encoded reporters; this reads reactive oxygen species directly with a chemical probe, in two systems at once. Same mechanism, opposite ends of it. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: emerging (2026-08-13). evidence_tier: single-lab-exploratory, regulatory_status: none, strength: single-study, funding_flag: none. One laboratory (RIVM, the Dutch national public-health institute), 22 chemicals, no replication by anyone else, no Test Guideline anywhere near it.

Sub-threads: none — one dated bullet; nothing to declare yet. [reviewed 2026-08-13 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now

The endpoint. Normal metabolism produces chemically aggressive oxygen-containing molecules — reactive oxygen species — and cells run machinery to mop them up. A chemical that tips that balance leaves excess species that damage proteins, membranes and DNA. Oxidative stress is a mechanism, upstream of several kinds of harm rather than being one itself.

Why anyone wants a reliable assay for it. The stated motivation is non-genotoxic carcinogens — substances that cause cancer without directly damaging DNA, and which therefore walk past the entire standard genetic-toxicity battery. Sustained oxidative stress driving repeated tissue repair is a recognised route by which they work. A mechanism-based assay for it would close a real coverage gap.

The method. The DCFH assay: a probe molecule that is non-fluorescent until reactive oxygen species oxidise it, at which point it glows. Measured on a plate reader. Run here in two systems side by side:

SystemWhat it isExposure
Zebrafish embryosA whole organism with functioning metabolism, transparent, well-plate compatible4 days old, 24 h
Matured HepG2A human liver cancer cell line — the workhorse of cheap liver screening, notoriously metabolically feeble — under a protocol intended to push it closer to real liver1 h

The methodological care that makes it worth taking seriously: chemicals were ranked on analytically determined internal concentrations, i.e. what actually got inside each system, not on what was added to the water. Without that, the comparison would be measuring uptake and calling it biology.

What it reported on 22 chemicals (15 designated primary oxidative-stress inducers, 7 acting primarily by another mechanism):

  • Nine positives in each system — and the two sets of nine did not fully overlap. Neither contains the other.
  • Four of the fifteen oxidative-stress chemicals were detected by neither system.
  • Three of the seven other-mechanism chemicals were flagged in the zebrafish embryos. Zero of seven in the cell line.

The honest summary. This is not a method proposal and does not read like one. It is a comparison whose result is a discrepancy the authors explore and do not resolve, and its value to this vault is almost entirely as evidence about a hypothesis the vault has been carrying — see the through-line.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: automatable-open-loop — rung 4 of the deterministic ladder, and it gets there for the usual reason plus one unusual one.

  • Readout? Fully machine-obtainable. Plate-reader fluorescence, plus analytical chemistry for internal concentration. No human eye is load-bearing anywhere, in either system — including the whole-organism arm, which is the point the vault keeps making about not conflating "organism" with "unloopable."
  • Decision rule? None. The output is a rank order of chemicals by reactive-oxygen-species-inducing potential. A rank order is not a hazard classification, and nothing states the threshold. → rung 4.
  • Inputs? No in-vivo mammalian study is consumed by the method's own model. Not bench-bound. (The zebrafish embryo is a whole organism, but at a life stage treated in the EU as an alternative method rather than as a protected animal — a legal boundary, not a biological one, and the vault records it as such.)

And there is a second missing rule that the vault should name separately rather than folding into the first. Nothing published arbitrates a disagreement between the two systems. On this dataset that is not an edge case — the two disagree on which chemicals are positive while agreeing on how many. A loop running the pair has three candidate combination rules and this result damages all of them: trusting concordance requires independence that is unestablished; trusting the more biologically complete model ignores that it also produced calls on three of seven chemicals acting by another route; escalating disagreements to a human is the definition of human-in-the-loop, and would be the correct grading if anyone had actually written that rule down. Nobody has, so it is not a rule and the tier stays where it is. See two-models-agreed-on-a-count-and-not-on-a-list.

Loop-ready is not endorsement here, in its most direct form. A machine could run both arms of this comparison tomorrow, at scale, and produce two rank orders it has no procedure for combining.

Cross-intervention / connect (§6.5)

Titles-scan run against the full concept index before writing any link above.

  • Composes-with, and does NOT duplicate, oxidative-stress-redox-nrf2-profiling. Two methods, one mechanism, opposite ends: species generated vs response mounted. In principle they are the two halves a battery would want. In practice neither has a decision rule, so a battery of them has two numbers and no procedure — and if one were ever built, the first question to put to it is whether both arms fail on the same chemicals, which nobody has asked.
  • Anti-edge, stated to prevent a category error the vault is prone to. Zebrafish embryos appear in four maps now, and they are not four readings of one system: acute lethality (fish-embryo-acute-toxicity-fet), malformation scoring (developmental-toxicity-zebrafish-teratogenicity), swimming behaviour (developmental-neurotoxicity-zebrafish-behavior-ldtt) and now a molecular stress probe. Same animal, four different questions, three different loop tiers. Organism identity is not method identity — and the shared limited-larval-metabolism flag across them is the one thing that is shared, which is exactly why this source's partial result on metabolic competence matters to all four.
  • ⚠ Blind-spot check — a row was considered and refused. The temptation is to record the fish/cell pair as a new correlated_blind_spot on the grounds that both missed four chemicals. The rail requires a shared structural reason, and none is identified: the four missed chemicals are unnamed in the accessible text and no mechanism is proposed for why both systems are blind to them. A shared miss without a shared cause is a coverage gap, recorded in domain_gate, not a rail row. Logged as an open question.
