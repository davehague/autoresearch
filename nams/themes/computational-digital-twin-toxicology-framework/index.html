Why this map exists

Every method on this vault's "In-silico (standalone) & the model layer's quality record" group, plus pbk-qivive-toxicokinetics and several organ-chip maps, has been graded separately — on the premise that PBPK, QSP, QSAR, AOP networks and organ-chips are related but distinct tools. This source argues the opposite: that fragmentation itself is the problem, and that none of those tools alone captures "the patient" (an individually parameterized, continuously updated, mechanistically-grounded representation). That is a claim about the vault's own taxonomy, not evidence about any one method's accuracy — which is why it earns a framework map rather than a bullet on an existing one.

What we believe now

A University of Hradec Kralove (Czech Republic) team proposes digital twin — a continuously updated, mechanistically grounded, individually parameterized digital representation of a biological system — as the organizing layer toxicology's predictive tools currently lack. Traces the concept from aerospace engineering through biomedicine into toxicology, defines five conditions a true toxicological digital twin must meet (parameterized from measured individual data; dynamically updated during use; hybrid mechanistic + data-driven; forward simulation of scenarios; bidirectional model↔real-world data flow), and proposes a four-layer architecture: data, mechanistic, AI, and simulation.

The decisive test, and it is a negative result stated plainly. Six published or widely cited systems were scored against the five conditions — a quantitative systems toxicology liver-injury platform with a regulatory record (Watkins 2020), a cardiac twin (Corral-Acero et al. 2020; Niederer et al. 2021), an oncology twin (Wu et al. 2022), individualized PBPK (Gonçalves et al. 2026), QSP virtual populations (Allen et al. 2016; Wang et al. 2024), and — as a non-biomedical comparison — an Earth-system digital twin (Bauer et al. 2021). None of the six satisfies all five conditions. PBPK and QSP are named as today's most mature "proto-twin" components; none of the six reaches full dynamic, bidirectional, individually-parameterized operation.

Named architectural gaps, not just "more work needed": continuous data flow (most toxicology data arrives episodically, not streamed — the opposite of what a twin needs); uncertainty quantification; feedback-driven recalibration; and regulatory-grade validation. Named translational barriers, stated without softening: data heterogeneity across institutions and jurisdictions (interoperability "mature for laboratory values," "almost nonexistent for longitudinal exposure histories"); validation standards; interpretability; regulatory acceptance; and the ethical custody of patient-level models. Federated learning is named as a partial data-sharing fix, with privacy leakage, heterogeneity and governance flagged as still unresolved even there.

Named applications (not demonstrated, named as motivating use cases): drug development, personalized risk assessment, mass-poisoning response, chemical-biological-radiological-nuclear (CBRN) preparedness, and antidote discovery for unknown toxins.

The closing claim is unhedged and worth stating exactly: toxicology "has most of the ingredients needed for digital-twin implementation; what remains is the integration work, two unresolved scientific problems, and the institutional willingness to address them." The review names the integration gap as institutional and engineering, not fundamentally scientific — a stronger claim than this vault usually lets a single perspective piece make unqualified, and recorded here as the authors' claim, not the vault's independent finding.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: computational-connective — not a physical assay. Per shared/evidence-model.md, this is scored off the ladder entirely: a digital twin, if built, is the model layer a closed loop would update, not a thing graded on readout-objectivity or decision-rule fixedness. The review's own named gaps — continuous data flow, uncertainty quantification, feedback-driven recalibration, regulatory-grade validation — are exactly the properties a closed loop would need from its model layer and exactly what the review says no current system has all of. This is the clearest single-source statement in the vault of what a loop's model layer still lacks, system-wide, rather than method-by-method.
