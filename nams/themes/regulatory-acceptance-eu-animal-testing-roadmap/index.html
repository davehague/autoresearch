What we believe now

On 1 June 2026 the European Commission adopted the Roadmap towards phasing out animal testing for chemical safety assessments — its official statement of direction, spanning 15 regulatory domains (industrial chemicals, pesticides, biocides, pharmaceuticals, food/feed additives, cosmetics) with 30+ recommended actions on three pillars:

  1. Accelerate the development and validation of non-animal methods.
  2. Leverage research, artificial intelligence, and data-driven assessments — including, for pharmaceuticals, "reducing the need for certain tests based on in vitro or in silico approaches" and "virtual controls" (a modelled/historical control arm) in place of animal control groups.
  3. Strengthen cross-sector collaboration — the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) to launch a Collaborative Platform on Alternatives to Animal Testing.

What it is not. Non-binding; no legal phase-out, no target end date, no acceptance thresholds; a progress-review conference planned for 2029. It names the validation bottleneck (stakeholders acknowledged validation is too slow) and the coverage gaps (endocrine disruption, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity) but resolves neither — no validation timelines, no new methods.

Why this map exists: the acceptance axis now has two jurisdictions

The vault's regulatory_status ladder is method-centric (a method earns a Test Guideline). The two US framework maps already showed acceptance can attach to (method × context of use × submission) rather than to a method (CDER, regulatory-acceptance-fda-nam-framework) and that AI acceptance is priced by model influence (FDA/EMA, regulatory-acceptance-ai-model-credibility). This EU roadmap adds the second regulator running the same direction of travel: non-animal and AI-driven methods are the intended future. The destination is now stated by both the FDA and the EU; the mechanism — how an AI-driven method earns acceptance — is written by neither.

The tension this map must carry, not resolve

  • The signal is real. The largest chemical/drug regulator to commit at this breadth (15 domains) putting AI as one of three pillars, and naming a computational comparator ("virtual controls") as an acceptable substitute for a live animal arm, is a genuine move toward letting the loop's model half carry regulatory weight.
  • The signal is cheap. Non-binding, no dates, no thresholds, and — crucially — no fix for the validation delay the vault already quantifies (twelve years for TG 445A, metabolism-cyp-induction-heparg). A roadmap that names the bottleneck without a timeline is an intention, not a mechanism.

Both are true. A regulator can commit to a destination and still leave the road unbuilt. This map records the commitment and refuses to read it as arrival.
