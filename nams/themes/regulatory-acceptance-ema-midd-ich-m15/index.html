What we believe now

ICH is the International Council for Harmonisation — the body whose technical guidelines the US, EU and Japanese medicines regulators all agree to follow. ICH M15 sets general principles for model-informed drug development (MIDD): using computer models of a drug and of the body to answer questions that would otherwise require an experiment.

The claim this map records is not that MIDD replaces animal testing. It is narrower and, for this vault, more consequential: there exists an established international route by which the output of a computer model becomes acceptable regulatory evidence, and it runs entirely outside the Test Guideline system.

Decisions the source states model-derived evidence can already support:

  • justifying the starting dose for a first-in-human trial
  • bridging pharmacokinetics across formulations of the same drug
  • assessing drug–drug interactions
  • extrapolating to populations rarely studied directly — children, pregnant women

Alongside it, the EU Pharma Package (approved 2026) now defines non-clinical studies to include "in vitro, ex vivo, in silico, or in chemico" methods — in-silico evidence written into the legal definition of a non-clinical study. EMA's supporting machinery: a dedicated regulatory-acceptance pathway for NAMs, Innovation Task Force briefing meetings, scientific advice, a 3Rs Working Party (established 2022; biennial report 2023–2024), an updated guideline on regulatory acceptance of 3Rs approaches in progress, and an International Medicines Regulators Working Group on 3Rs.

Why this map exists — it changes how the vault reads its own acceptance axis

The vault's regulatory_status ladder is OECD-TG-adopted > under-validation > proposed > none. That ladder grades assays. The four computational-connective maps — (Q)SAR and read-across, PBK/QIVIVE, the NAM data-processing pipeline, and the machine-learning reporting record — are the model layer a loop would update, and they have all been graded against a ladder built for something else.

A model can be regulatory_status: none on the assay ladder and still have a live, well-defined acceptance route here. That is a real reading error the vault has been at risk of, and it is now recorded. What it does not mean: that any specific map's grade changes tonight. Nothing here demonstrates an accepted submission. The correction is to the interpretation, not to a value.

The price, arriving from a second jurisdiction

On 2026-07-13 the vault recorded FDA's model-risk matrix — model risk = model influence × decision consequence, where influence is "the contribution of the evidence derived from the AI model relative to other contributing evidence", i.e. how alone the model is — and the observation that a closed loop maximises that quantity by construction, so every rung the vault treats as progress the regulator treats as a heavier evidentiary burden.

ICH M15 lands in the same place from a different direction. An AI model in a submission requires "rigorous assessment of model risk, comprehensive documentation of training data and validation performance, and clear articulation of the regulatory decisions they aim to support," with defined contexts of use, transparent reporting, sensitivity and uncertainty analyses, reproducibility, standardisation and independent validation — "particularly for high-impact decisions."

Named failure modes for AI toxicity predictors: overfitting, limited transparency, poor generalisability, non-representative training datasets.

The recommended architecture is worth carrying as design guidance, not just policy. Couple the AI with a mechanistic model — physiologically based pharmacokinetics, or quantitative systems pharmacology — "to preserve biological interpretability." Read for a loop: the model layer should not be one learned function; it should be a learned component constrained by a causal one.

The comparator number

The source cites animal-to-human safety concordance for FDA-approved anticancer drugs as "moderate," with success rates "generally between 50% and 60%" depending on endpoint and species, against a background of ~90% clinical failure for candidates that looked good preclinically. The thing a replacement has to beat is right somewhere between half and six times in ten on this class of drugs. Quoted as the source quotes it — a cited summary, not a measurement made here — and load-bearing, because it decides whether "as good as the animal test" is an ambitious target.

The explicit brake

The authors do not claim replacement. Verbatim: not "immediate replacement of animal studies" but a progressive transition in which animal studies become "scientifically exceptional rather than just procedurally standard."
