What we believe now

Cherradi et al. 2026 (Frontiers in Toxicology, PMID 41908553; PredictCan Biotechnologies SAS with Centre Antoine Lacassagne, funding_flag: [industry, method-developer]) built a 384-well "individual-centric" liver spheroid: HepG2 hepatocyte-like cells, TWNT-1 hepatic stellate cells and THP-1-derived monocyte/macrophage cells (90,000:10,000:1,000 per well), cultured a minimum of two weeks in a proprietary medium containing 50% human serum from one consented donor. A drug's dose-response viability and mechanistic-marker readout is scored by a proprietary, confidentiality-protected AI algorithm returning a no-DILI / less-DILI / most-DILI call, benchmarked against FDA's own DILI-concern categories.

Thirteen compounds tested, unevenly: FDA reference no-DILI drugs (lidocaine, streptomycin, salbutamol), less-DILI (donepezil, acetaminophen at therapeutic dose), most-DILI (troglitazone, sitaxentan, bosentan, ximelagatran), and the centerpiece — ribociclib, scored against three real patients' own donor spheroids, correctly predicting grade-3 DILI in the one who developed it clinically and no DILI in the two who did not. Ximelagatran's hepatotoxicity was detected only under chronic (16-day), not acute, exposure — matching its actual clinical withdrawal profile (Phase III, delayed liver injury). Preliminary 100% concordance with FDA categories is reported across the tested set. The authors' own brake, stated twice: this is a proof-of-concept, and "meaningful estimates of sensitivity and specificity require... 50-100 drugs."

Loop readiness

automatable-open-loop. The readout half is fully machine-run: liquid handling into 384-well plates, CellTiter-Glo luminescence, dose-response curve fitting.
