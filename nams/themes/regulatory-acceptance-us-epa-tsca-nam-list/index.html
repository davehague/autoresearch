US EPA's TSCA list of alternative test methods — the acceptance axis, fifth gate — framework map

🚩 NEW MAP, auto-coined 2026-07-27 — CONVENTION DEPARTURE, flagged for human review. Not a method map. Its endpoint (regulatory-acceptance) is not a toxicological endpoint. It is the vault's fifth regulatory-acceptance gate, after US FDA's drug-review centre, the joint FDA–EMA artificial-intelligence principles, the EU animal-testing phase-out roadmap and the UK replacement strategy — and it closes a conspicuous hole: the US EPA is the agency that actually decides what gets tested for industrial chemicals and pesticides in the United States, and the vault had no map for it. Logged in docs/open-gaps-log.md. Git is the undo.

Status: consensus (2026-07-27) — evidence_tier: regulatory-adopted, regulatory_status: OECD-TG-adopted. The list is a statutory instrument in force, not a proposal: the Toxic Substances Control Act (TSCA, the 1976 US law governing industrial chemicals) obliges EPA at section 4(h)(2)(C) to "maintain and regularly update a list" of non-animal methods. Adoption is what earns consensus, applied deterministically. The qualifier that travels with it is the whole point of this map: the list is in force; the list is not a validation.

What we believe now

What the instrument is. A published table of non-animal methods, strategies and tools that a company may use instead of an animal test when meeting TSCA testing requirements. EPA expanded it substantially on 2 June 2026, alongside a parallel action for pesticides under FIFRA (the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide and Rodenticide Act) and a new stakeholder nomination pathway. The page carried "Last updated on July 23, 2026" at retrieval.

What listing means, in EPA's own words:

"Many of the NAMs have been reviewed and established by different organizations (e.g., OECD, ICCVAM, and ICATM) and meet the Section 4(h)(2)(C) criteria for scientific relevance (i.e. accuracy) and reliability (i.e., repeatability/reproducibility)."

Three readings the vault holds simultaneously and refuses to collapse:

  1. It is real acceptance. A submitter can point at the list. That is a regulatory_status fact with statutory backing, and it applies to methods this vault tracks.
  2. EPA validated nothing. The determination is inherited from OECD, ICCVAM and ICATM. A listing and the OECD Test Guideline behind it are one fact, not two — the vault must not count them as independent evidence. That is the correlated-blind-spot rail's logic applied to acceptance rather than to biology.
  3. The sentence says "many," and there is no disclaimer. The relevance-and-reliability claim covers the list in aggregate, entry-level evidence statements are absent, and nothing on the page separates listed from endorsed or reminds a reader that fitness for purpose is still judged case by case. (a-regulatory-list-is-not-a-validation.)

⚠ The contradiction this map exists to hold

The developmental-neurotoxicity in-vitro battery is on a US federal list under a heading that states its entries meet criteria for scientific relevance and reliability. On 2026-07-20 this vault moved developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb to contested on the OECD Secretariat's own words, written with NICEATM and EFSA: "no formal validation procedure has yet been completed for the DNT-IVB as an integrated battery."

Both statements are current, public and governmental. They are reconcilable — EPA files the battery under "Data Integration Tools, Predictive Models, and Assessment Frameworks" rather than among validated test methods, and its relevance sentence is a blanket one. They are not reconcilable by anyone reading the list, who gets a category heading and a NEW tag. developmental-neurotoxicity-dnt-ivb stays contested — a list does not un-contest a validation gap, and the vault never moves evidence_tier on a regulatory_status fact.

The nomination pathway, and what is missing from it

EPA opened a route for researchers, companies and NGOs to nominate methods. Three assessment factors:

  1. Context of use — which TSCA decision it supports; fitness for that purpose.
  2. Biological relevance — mechanistic basis, reference chemicals.
  3. Reliability — quality assurance, applicability domain, predictivity, reproducibility, independent review.

An interlaboratory study appears nowhere. "Independent review" is the nearest thing, and it is one item inside one of three factors. This is the third jurisdiction the vault has seen make the ring trial conditional rather than required — after ICCVAM's 2024 "interlaboratory evaluation (if needed)" and FDA CDER's fit-for-purpose route. See validation-paradigm-nam-confidence, where that argument is contested by Jacobs et al. 2024's eight documented transfer failures.

Loop readiness

loop_readiness: not-applicable — this is not a method. It says nothing about whether anything on it can be automated, and the vault does not infer the loop axis from the acceptance axis in either direction.

But it changes what a loop can read. An automated system reasoning about which methods it may use will find a government list far easier to parse than a validation report: a table, authoritative, free of qualifiers, and machine-readable. The qualifiers are the entire content. A loop that treats list membership as validation concludes the developmental-neurotoxicity battery is reliable on the same night the vault's sources show its own guideline body saying it never completed formal validation as a battery.

Second-order, and more interesting: the model layer is now on the same list as the assays. ToxCast, GenRA, SeqAPASS, ECOTOX and a 17-assay battery sit in one statutory instrument beside adopted OECD Test Guidelines. The vault grades that layer computational-connective and scores it off the loop ladder on the reasoning that a model is not something a robot runs. That reasoning holds scientifically and has just stopped holding regulatorily. (the-model-layer-is-now-on-the-same-list-as-the-assays.)
