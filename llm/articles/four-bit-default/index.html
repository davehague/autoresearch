4-bit is still the default, and everything interesting moved inside it: the state of post-training quantization

Synthesized 2026-07-16, refreshed 2026-07-28, 2026-08-08, 2026-08-16 and again 2026-08-21 from post-training-quantization (status: consolidating). Six new dated bullets since the last version. The headline has now survived thirty-seven of them; what keeps changing is the list of decisions hiding underneath it. As of 2026-08 the four-bit claim is the least interesting true thing on this map — the arguments are all about allocation, calibration, the optimizer, and what you measured. Three things moved in the week to 2026-08-21, and the first of them is a crack in this article's own title. A training-time quantization pass reached the local stack, and where it lands, the ladder this article tells you to climb stops ranking the way it does. Eight bits stopped being free, on a paired test the field's usual unpaired one cannot see. And the optimizer turned out to be a lever separate from the objective — worth twelve points at two bits and actively harmful at four, which finally gives a mechanism to this article's oldest warning about perplexity. Defaults here rot in months; check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim, and what has stopped being the question

Quantize an open-weights model's weights to 4 bits after training and you keep the quality that matters. That still holds, from three independent directions:

  • Inference, scaling family — AWQ (MIT Han Lab, MLSys 2024 Best Paper) protects ~1% of weight channels, identified from the activation distribution, via mathematically equivalent per-channel scaling. No backpropagation, no reconstruction. [T1]
  • Inference, reconstruction family — GPTQ (IST Austria/ETH, ICLR 2023) quantizes column-by-column and compensates the remaining weights using approximate second-order information from a small calibration set. 175B in ~4 GPU-hours at 3–4 bits, negligible degradation. [T1]
  • Training substrate — QLoRA (UW, 2023) backprops through a frozen 4-bit base into LoRA adapters and matches 16-bit fine-tuning; 65B on a single 48GB card. [T1]

In July 2026 "4-bit" meant weights, stored small and upcast for an FP16 matmul. It now reaches the whole forward pass, it has been checked in the file format people download, and the interesting disagreements have all moved one level down: which tensors are even inside the budget, what the calibration corpus is doing at low bit-widths, which optimizer draws the assignment, and whether the benchmark you scored on can see the damage at all.

One thing changed on 2026-08-21 that this article's title does not cover, and it is stated up front rather than buried. Everything above is post-training quantization: take a finished checkpoint, spend calibration data, recover what you can. At small scale there is now a shipped alternative that skips the recovery step by never creating the loss; the section "The exception to the title" below states it. It does not displace anything here. It does mean "how many bits" and "which format" are questions whose answers depend on a training decision you may or may not have made, and this article's advice below is advice about compressing a checkpoint somebody already finished.

What to do

  • Default the deploy path to 4-bit weight-only quantization. FP16 serving of open weights is the thing that needs a stated reason. [T1, three groups]
  • Stop treating 8-bit as the free step. Measure it like any other. This is a reversal, and it comes from a design change rather than a new method. Hold a retrieval task fixed, vary only bitsandbytes precision, and score with a trial-matched paired test — the test the design admits and the field does not use — and INT8 shows a real penalty in two of three models (odds ratios 1.84 and 2.50) where the unpaired test on the identical cells finds nothing (p = 0.837 / 0.410 / 0.197) (the proactive-interference study). INT8 is still meaningfully safer than INT4 (odds ratios 2.58–20.00 there). It is no longer a step you take without measuring. Bounded to bitsandbytes NF4 and LLM.int8(), three instruction-tuned models at 3.8–7B, greedy decoding, short prompts. [T1]
  • Read quantization damage at your operating point, not from the pooled average. Same study, same metric, same model: pooled across all conditions the FP16 → INT4 gap reads 1.1–2.4 points; at the high-interference operating point it reads 7–13 (Qwen2.5-7B 81.0% → 68.3%, McNemar odds ratio 20.00). The average is taken over a range that is mostly saturated. The damage is specific rather than generic — it appears when superseded values are semantically confusable with the target, reverses sign on an arbitrary-integer control (β = +0.053 against −0.046, both p < 10⁻⁷), and shows up as a rise in how often a stale-but-real earlier value is retrieved (21.5% → 24.6% of trials) rather than a change in which one. If your workload updates facts within a context — agent state, multi-turn correction, retrieval over revised documents — the pooled number is not your number. [T1]
  • At a fixed memory footprint, buy parameters and pay for them in bits. The first measurement in this vault at a fixed budget rather than a fixed model: Qwen2-VL-2B at fp16 against Qwen2-VL-7B at 4-bit, identical inputs, 5,700 predictions across a clean condition and six photographic degradations. The 7B-4bit arm dominates on every axis — accuracy 0.935 vs 0.904, error-detection AUROC 0.98 vs 0.95, selective risk (AURC) 0.008 vs 0.017, and threshold robustness under shift (the VLM uncertainty study). [T1, one study]
  • Treat "4-bit" as a family, not a setting. Two conditions hide inside the name. The format's internal bit allocation: a serving benchmark reporting 21–87% higher throughput "on the same 4-bit weights" was comparing MLX 4-bit against Q4_K_M with no quality evaluation of any kind (the Apple Silicon serving comparison) — that is a runtime claim with the format difference unpriced, not evidence about either format. And the calibration corpus, below. [T1/T2]
  • A weight format can be wrong for your machine without being wrong about bits. Every format here is argued over on quality-per-bit, footprint and GPU-kernel throughput. Q8_0 stores 32 INT8 weights behind one FP16 scale, contiguously — good for cache locality and for the self-describing single file that won local distribution, and structurally hostile to long vector registers, because a wide load pulls scales in with weights and separating them costs explicit packing and masked reduction whose cost scales with vector length alongside the arithmetic it was meant to amortize. At 2048-bit vector length that is 28% of cycles on packing and 60% on packing plus reduction, with the naive packed implementation running 1.5× slower than the narrow baseline; reproduced on Arm SVE, so it is the format and not the instruction set (the long-vector FlashAttention study). Relevant if you are running on RISC-V RVV or Arm SVE hardware; irrelevant on a CUDA card. Untested and obvious: whether a planar layout (all scales, then all weights) fixes it, and where Q4_K_M's super-blocks land. [T1]
  • Below three bits, spend on the optimizer; at four bits, stop — you are fitting the proxy. The decision every method here makes is how the interval is drawn; the discrete assignment inside it has been nearest-level rounding since GPTQ, whose curvature only shapes the error correction applied afterwards. Condition the assignment on the curvature that survives the still-continuous suffix's best response — a Schur complement, provably no larger than the block it replaces — and refit the row scale and zero-point in closed form, and 2-bit Qwen3-4B moves 258.96 → 81.95 perplexity and +11.88 points of mean zero-shot, with objective, calibration set and group size unchanged (SchurQuant). The margins over the strongest baseline across eight models track the bit-width exactly: +9.65 points at 2 bits, +2.16 at 3, +0.68 at 4. And the same strictly-better optimizer makes the model worse at 3 bits (15.79 → 21.26 perplexity) and 4 bits (14.16 → 15.52) — because layer-wise reconstruction is a surrogate, and within one method the local objective falls monotonically with refinement while perplexity flatlines after four sweeps and then oscillates. That is the mechanism behind the next bullet's warning, arrived at from the opposite direction. Practical: the output is the same per-row group format GPTQ emits, so every existing kernel runs it, at 32–46× GPTQ's quantization wall-clock. No code released. [T1, eight models]
  • Do not pick a quant level from the perplexity column. Across 13 llama.cpp formats on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, four 5-bit formats span 0.03 perplexity and 3.4 GSM8K points (a unified llama.cpp quantization evaluation). Confirmed at an order of magnitude larger by a third party who was measuring something else entirely: across one 8B ladder (FP16, W8A8, two W4A16 and four W4A8 variants) WikiText2 spans 0.56 while HumanEval spans 10.98 (the speculation-meets-quantization study). Converting a file costs 27–43 seconds, so measuring beats guessing. [T1 ×2]
  • Expect the damage to be task-shaped, and pick your rung against your workload. The 8-, 6-, 5- and 4-bit K-quants all sit within about half an aggregate point of FP16 (Q4_K_M: 69.15 vs 69.47, at 69.4% smaller). Only 3-bit moves, and unevenly — at Q3_K_S, GSM8K falls 9.3 points and MMLU 4.2 while HellaSwag falls 0.6. Precision is load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and near-free for single-step commonsense. [T1]
  • Report your calibration corpus, and distrust results that do not — but only below 4 bits. This is the most-replicated confound on the map and it now has a boundary. Holding everything fixed and varying only the corpus: HumanEval 29.3 (C4) / 13.7 (MATH) / 25.1 (GitHub-Code) on Mixtral at 2.5 bits (AlphaQ); GSM8K 31.77 → 42.30; and, largest, Math-500 0.00 → 58.40 on Qwen3-4B at ternary (the reasoning-aware ternary study). The ladder inside that last one is the informative part — generic web text 0.00, domain-relevant question-answer pairs 24.20, the same questions carrying the target's own chain-of-thought traces 58.40 — so what the corpus must contain is the multi-step derivation precision loss attacks first, not merely the topic. The boundary: the same intervention is worth +2.2 at W4A16 against +58.8 at W2A16. Four bits is comparatively robust; recipe leakage is a low-bit phenomenon. [T1 ×3]
  • Treat the bit budget as an allocation problem, and check which tensors are inside it. Four independent partitionings, four groups, one rule — see the next section. Then open the checkpoint: under both bitsandbytes NF4 and AWQ the language-modeling head is still a BF16 torch.nn.Linear, 1.19 GB on Qwen3-8B-Base and one of the largest dense tensors left in a "4-bit" file (the LM-head compression study). [T1]
  • If you are fine-tuning a quantized base, initialize jointly. At 2.5-bit on LLaMA-2-13B the same base gives 19.39 WikiText-2 perplexity under QLoRA's zero-initialized adapter and 5.22 under a jointly-solved one; at 2.25- and 2-bit QLoRA fails to converge while LoftQ reaches 7.69. The sub-3-bit cliff is partly a property of the initialization, not of the bit-width. [T1, two groups with PiSSA]
  • Check whether your adapter can still merge before you choose the quantization. Adapter training is documented across nine quantized backends, and merging keeps breaking as quantization gets more aggressive — AQLM cannot merge at all, torchao merges correctly only for int8_weight_only and is otherwise silently wrong, Intel Neural Compressor and Transformer Engine list it unsupported (the PEFT quantization guide). Aggressiveness and mergeability are in direct tension, and no paper says so because each works with one base format. [T2]
  • Pick format and runtime as one decision. GPTQ/AWQ checkpoints run on GPU-serving stacks; GGUF quants run on llama.cpp and Ollama. The artifact menu is local-inference's territory. [T2]
  • Treat 4-bit-base LoRA as the single-card baseline every bigger training plan has to beat (peft-methods). [T1]
  • When a repair and one more bit are both on the table, spend the bit. Four independent attempts to route a fix through a derived importance or optimality signal have now come in, and three lost outright: a cost-matched sweep of five weight-space repairs in which a random-weight control tied all five (the margin study); an oracle gate on flipped mixture-of-experts routes beating a rate-matched random gate by +0.4% with an interval spanning zero (the route-flip study); and a rotation proved to be the only orthogonal family commuting with rotary position embeddings, derived in closed form, verified to attain its own optimum, and measured worse than applying no rotation at all — 10.6499 against 10.5764 perplexity on Llama-3.2-3B (the RoPE-aligned rotation study). The fourth won, and its own control prices the win — see below. [T1 ×4]
  • Re-measure per model, per bit-width and per task family — the constants do not transfer, and now there is a reason why. Decompose a quantized module's signal-to-noise ratio and it splits three ways: the weight's magnitude against its own quantization error, the energy of task-specific activations along the full-precision weight direction, and the activation energy along the quantization-error direction (the SNR decomposition). Only the first is weight-space. Two modules with identical weight-space error can cost arbitrarily different amounts, because the other two terms belong to the workload. That is also why weight-space repairs lose: an importance score moves the first term, an extra bit moves all three. [T1]
  • Assume the damage is worse on your model than on the paper's, because you are at the small end — but only compare within a model family. Across Qwen3 0.6B–14B, larger models show consistently lower source error and lower accumulated error, with smaller models in "sustained amplification" where larger ones preserve or attenuate. Compression damage is also multiplicative in the decision margin, so a model with a thinner margin loses more from the same perturbation. Two compounding disadvantages, both at the end this vault's readers are forced to use. The scope condition arrived 2026-08-19 and is a narrowing, not a reversal: run eight checkpoints under one method, one bit-width and one codebase at 2 bits and two of three families run the other way — Llama3.2-1B → 3B → Llama3-8B loses 15.30 → 21.14 → 26.08 points and Qwen3-0.6B → 4B → 8B loses 10.00 → 19.67 → 23.78, with only Llama2 behaving as predicted. Correcting for the ~38% floor those multiple-choice tasks collapse to, retained headroom separates by family (Llama2 57–70%, Qwen3 34–41%, Llama3.x 29–32%) and barely by size. The amplification law holds within a family; model size is not usable as a predictor of what a 2-bit run costs a given modern checkpoint, and the most likely driver — training-token density — is untested. [T1 ×3]
  • Everything on this map is an English result. Three recorded calibration findings varied what the calibration text was about. Vary what language it is in — or leave it at the field's silent default — and W3A16 GPTQ degrades English perplexity 1.35× while degrading Arabic 4.37×, Korean 3.49×, Japanese 3.04×, Hindi 2.73×, Chinese 2.39×, Russian 2.12×, ordered by script distance and replicated across two model families with different tokenizers (the language-conditional dequantization study). Domain is a choice somebody made; language is a default nobody recorded. A per-language rank-2 correction at 0.12% of parameters recovers 70–83% of the non-Latin perplexity gap in 15–20 minutes on one L4 — and collapses to 8.5% recovery at 7B. [T1, one study]
  • At matched footprint, quantize rather than prune, and the margin is roughly an order of magnitude. This is the cheapest rule the map has produced in a fortnight and it is now measured on two unrelated instruments. A full FP16→INT3 sweep on Qwen2.5-1.5B costs 1.58 points of aggregate trustworthiness, while one step from dense to 2:4 sparsity costs Gemma-1.1-7B 10.1 (the SLM trustworthiness study); on a capability suite, removing 25% of parameters costs 13–17 points after recovery fine-tuning against about 1.6 for the whole quantization sweep (the knapsack pruning study). Pruning and quantization are not two points on one footprint curve. Carve out fairness, which swings widest and in both directions — a score that improves when you damage the model is reading refusal behaviour. See structured-pruning. [T1 ×2]

Allocation is the real subject, and it has replicated four ways

The strongest thing this map now knows is not a bit-width. It is that a quantization budget is an allocation, not a global setting — established by four groups partitioning the parameter space four different ways and reaching compatible answers.

Partition byResultSource
Tensor class2-bit on the 96.6% of Mixtral-8×7B that is experts costs 3.85 MMLU points; 2-bit on the 3.4% that is not — attention, embeddings, router — costs 11.5the Mixtral offloading study [T1, venue-less]
LayerRank layers by the power-law tail exponent of their weight spectra, solve a global integer program: Qwen1.5-MoE at 3.5 average bits matches BF16AlphaQ [T1]

The tensor-class row is the best-replicated finding on the map, and it has since been confirmed from the largest instance available: the strongest open-weights release to date holds its MoE experts in MXFP4 with the attention projections, shared experts and routers held higher (Kimi K3) — three groups, three methods, one conclusion. Experts are the cheap parameters; the shared path is not, because error on a weight every token traverses compounds through the residual stream.

Pair that with the opposite rule for adapters. At a fixed 0.28M trainable budget, adapters on the feed-forward matrices score 89.99 on MNLI against 88.58 on the query projection, and upper layers beat lower ones by 10.7 points (AdaLoRA). Precision where error compounds, adaptation where the model specializes. Nobody has tested the combination.

Kimi K3 is also a push-back on this article's framing rather than on any of its numbers: its weights are the 4-bit weights, produced by quantization-aware training running from SFT through RL, so the model never existed in full precision after pre-training. There is no BF16 control arm anywhere in the report, so it supplies no effect size — but it is the first sign that the largest open models may stop being post-training-quantized at all.

Fit the approximation in the geometry the consumer reads

Three unrelated arrivals have now said the same thing, which is enough to name it as one idea.

An LM-head error reaches the softmax with no normalization, residual or learned transform left to absorb it, so fitting the head's correction in weight-space error is fitting the wrong objective. Fit it in an activation-derived metric instead and top-1 agreement goes 76.95% → 93.05% at matched storage, recovering 3.7–3.9× on that tensor at relative perplexity 1.007 against storage-matched INT4's 1.151 (the LM-head study). The same shape appears in cross-model KV transfer, where attention-output cosine correlates +0.57 with downstream retention while regression R² correlates −0.20. And in the arithmetic: INT4 proxy weights used only to rank which SwiGLU channels to compute beat FP16 proxies by up to 9.0 points at 70% sparsity, while using the same proxy outputs as values costs 18.1–24.3 (the FFN sparsity study).

That quietly indicts the default GPTQ and AWQ share, since both minimize a weight- or activation-space reconstruction error rather than the quantity the next layer actually consumes. Nobody has re-run either under an output-geometry objective.

The outlier story inverted, and a third group confirmed it by accident

Every scaling method accommodates outliers. QuaRot (ETH Zurich / EPFL / Microsoft Research / IST Austria, NeurIPS 2024) says the basis is arbitrary. In a pre-norm RMSNorm architecture, multiplying an input-side weight matrix on the left by an orthogonal Q and its paired output-side matrix on the right by Qᵀ leaves the network's function unchanged, because RMSNorm divides each row by its norm and a rotation preserves norms. Pick Q to be a randomized Hadamard matrix, fuse it in, and a spike in one channel becomes a small contribution to all of them — there are no outliers left to protect. Cost: 1.5 Hadamard transforms per layer, each O(d log d).

At 4-bit weights, activations and KV cache on LLaMA-2-7B against a 5.47 FP16 perplexity baseline:

SmoothQuant was engineered for W8A8 and does not extend downward; rotation does. The enemy is outlier concentration, and concentration is basis-dependent.

As of 2026-08-15 that statement needs one word changed: what a rotation buys is its mixing support, not its structure. An outlier is a peak concentrated in few coordinates, and a norm-preserving map that spreads one peak over b channels leaves a peak of at least |x|/√b. That floor is the whole mechanism, and a controlled interpolation over b tracks it end to end — K range 14.82 → 9.64, relative quantization error 0.02306 → 0.00954, and the end-to-end perplexity gap +0.4158 → +0.0039 as b goes 2 → 128, reaching parity with full-head mixing only at b = 64–128 of 128 (the RoPE-aligned rotation study). The corollary is the sharpest negative result on this map. The only orthogonal family that commutes with rotary position embeddings is per-pair planar rotation — b = 2 — and the paper proves that converse, derives the closed-form optimal angle, verifies the implementation attains that minimum, and measures it worse than the identity: 10.6499 against 10.5764 perplexity, with replacement costing up to +1.334 across four checkpoints and holding at every long-context point through 128K. The diagnosis is transferable and is the practical takeaway: the certificate minimizes a position-averaged second moment, while the deployed INT4 quantizer sets its step from a tokenwise max−min range over groups spanning many pairs. Align a transform with the statistic the quantizer reads, not with the architecture's own decomposition — and read off any proposed transform's mixing support before anything else. It also puts a scope condition on the cheap block-diagonal rotations now appearing on the KV-cache side: b = 4 alone still costs +0.226, so those methods' wins rest on their full-width Hadamard component rather than on their small blocks. [T1, one group, four checkpoints]

The confirmation to trust is the incidental one. A paper about speculative decoding needed a 4-bit baseline, built one with GPTQ at group size 128, and reported the same recipe with and without a Hadamard rotation in adjacent rows: WikiText2 8.73 → 8.55, GSM8K 73.18 → 73.69, HumanEval 53.05 → 57.93 (the speculation-meets-quantization study). A third group, not the method's authors, recovering 4.88 of the 8.54 HumanEval points 4-bit cost, for one preprocessing step.

Two practical consequences of end-to-end 4-bit, pointing opposite ways. Compute becomes reachable on a consumer card: with both operands in INT4 the multiply runs on 4-bit tensor cores with no dequantize in the inner loop — measured on RTX 3090s, prefill runs 1.97–2.16× faster on a 7B and up to 3.33× on 70B, at 3.6–3.9× lower peak decode memory. That reverses the llama.cpp rule where weight-only quantization slows prefill by up to 28%. And the bill scales inversely with model size, which is the wrong direction for anyone reading this: the six-task zero-shot average falls 1.09 points at 70B, 2.80 at 13B and 4.18 at 7B. Every "retains 99% of zero-shot performance" framing of end-to-end 4-bit is the 70B row.

The KV cache half now has a second, stronger source: a vector quantizer with proved near-optimal distortion (within ≈2.7× of the information-theoretic floor, lower bounds also proved) whose first step is a random rotation — the same primitive from a different tradition — and which is data-free, quantizing at 4 bits and d=1536 in 0.0013 s against Product Quantization's 239.75 s, because there is no codebook to fit (TurboQuant). Two independent groups now step around the calibration problem rather than solving it.

What the benchmark could not see

Damage is now known to be three-dimensional, and only the first dimension is what anyone reports.

Task-shaped — the K-quant sweep above. Signal-shaped — at comparable accuracy, error-detection AUROC on the internal token-probability signal reads 0.95 (fp16) / 0.80 (4-bit) / 0.98 (larger model at 4-bit) (the VLM uncertainty study); a quantized model can be roughly as right and considerably worse at knowing when it is wrong, which an accuracy-only evaluation is blind to. Process-shaped — across 30,000 reasoning chains under greedy decoding, accuracy moves ≤3.1 points while the composition of failures shifts on small models (the silent-failures study).

Apply a large discount to that third one and keep applying it: the paper's own tables put the biggest distributional move at FP32 → FP16 rather than at the four-bit step, and its conclusion describes as an increase what every table shows as a decrease. The honest reading is "reducing precision shifts the reasoning-failure distribution on small models," which is a claim about precision generally rather than about 4 bits.

The last step in the pipeline is a lever, and its own authors priced it

Every method above decides how the interval is drawn — which channels are protected, which basis, which scales, what the group size is. Then, at the very end, each weight is assigned to the floor or the ceiling of its interval by scalar distance, and until August nobody on this map had asked whether that assignment was the best one available given the interval.

Near an interval's midpoint the two assignments incur nearly identical error, so the decision is genuinely underdetermined — which is the opening. Train a conditional diffusion model on 64×64 patches of the model's own weights, use its continuous reconstruction as evidence about which way a midpoint-ambiguous weight should go, admit flips densely at the midpoint and preserve round-to-nearest toward the boundaries, then pick among candidate matrices by leading-singular-value agreement with the original. Changing at most 1% of assignments buys 0.2–1.3 points at 4 bits and 0.1–1.6 at 3 across eight models from 1B to 2.7B, and beats the strongest calibration-based rounding optimizer — SignRound 67.0, this 67.4 on Gemma 2 2B — with no calibration text at all (ReRound).

That last property is the interesting one rather than the point total. This map has recorded the calibration confound on four separate axes — content, domain, corpus and language — and a rule fitted only to the weights has no corpus to leak. It is the first method here structurally immune to the confound rather than merely untested against it.

Two things fall out and both are actionable. Roughly a third of the gain needs no signal — which makes this the fourth time on this map a random control has taken a substantial share of a result attributed to a scoring function, and the demand it generates is now standing: no rounding-revision or importance-scoring result here is readable without a matched random control. And on the model where the method posts its largest relative gain, two off-the-shelf parameterizations still beat it outright. So sequence the work: sweep the format first, because that costs minutes of loading, and only then consider the rounding, which costs 1.76–3.43 hours of diffusion training plus 3.65–9.55 hours of inference per model — on two RTX 4090s, so it is within a reader's reach, and it is roughly twelve GPU-hours for at most 1.6 points. Inference-time cost is exactly zero; the artifact is an ordinary low-bit checkpoint, and it composes with another method's scales for a further +0.5.

Bound it: every model is under 3B, every task is zero-shot multiple choice, and there is no generative or multi-step evaluation anywhere — which is precisely the family this map says quantization damage lands on.

Stacking is not free, and this is the only place it has been measured

Every number in this article was produced with one lever pulled. The local stack people actually build — 4-bit weights, plus a quantized KV cache, plus sparsity, plus speculative decoding — has never been measured as a stack, and every "these compose" sentence in this vault is an extrapolation from single-lever results.

One direct measurement now exists and it went the wrong way. Attention-side arithmetic reduction at 80% retention is close to free on a dense model — it holds the dense summarization baseline from 1B to 70B and moves long-context retrieval scores by at most 0.6 points. Applied to an INT8 LLaMA 3.1 8B it costs 4.0 points on COPA (81.40 → 77.40), and 8.4 at 50% retention (the matrix-product reduction study).

The mechanism is already on this map twice — damage is multiplicative in the decision margin, and the surviving fraction is an activation-side quantity — so a model that has already spent margin loses more from an identical second perturbation. What is new is that it has been observed rather than predicted. Treat the headroom for a second compression as a property of the already-compressed model, and measure it there. [T1, one small benchmark, INT8 rather than 4-bit]

The exception to the title

Everything above compresses a checkpoint somebody already finished. On 2026-08-19 a vendor shipped the other thing, in a format you already run.

Liquid AI released Q4_0 GGUF checkpoints for four LFM2.5 models — 230M, 350M, 1.2B-Instruct and 2.6B — produced by quantization-aware distillation: a high-precision teacher distilled into a quantized student, so the model is trained inside the 4-bit grid rather than pushed onto it afterwards. Across GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro, IFEval, IFBench, Multi-IF and BFCLv4 plus one scale-appropriate math task, mean of five repeats, they retain 97.1 / 96.5 / 97.4 / 96.6% of their BF16 in-format ceiling (the LFM2.5 QAD release). [T2]

This vault has read a training-time quantization result before — a frontier lab holding mixture-of-experts expert weights in MXFP4 with quantization-aware training running from supervised fine-tuning through the whole of reinforcement learning (Kimi K3) — and filed it as unreachable, because MXFP4 is a microscaling block format with datacenter hardware support and not a llama.cpp K-quant. This one is a GGUF file, loaded with --hf-file, measured on a MacBook, a mini-PC, a Galaxy S26 Ultra and a Raspberry Pi 5.

What it changes about the advice above. Q4_0 is the oldest and simplest rung on the llama.cpp menu and the one this article's own ladder tells you to skip. Trained into, it matches Q5_K_M quality within evaluation variance at 4–33% higher decode throughput for the two smallest models, and Q4_K_M quality at 3–14% higher for the 1.2B and 2.6B — and matches a strong external post-training checkpoint (Unsloth's UD-Q4_K_XL) where one exists. So the rule "take the highest rung that fits" is a statement about post-training quantization specifically. Once the training run has happened inside a format's grid, the extra bits in the rung above buy nothing and the simpler format's better-optimised dot-product kernels are free throughput. The ladder ranks compression methods for a fixed checkpoint, not formats in general.

Two reasons this is a section and not a headline. The recipe is entirely undisclosed — no loss, no teacher identity, no token budget, no description of how the 4-bit grid is simulated during training, no tensor list, no block size, and the benchmark and throughput tables are images rather than text, so only the four retention percentages and the two throughput ranges are quotable. And every model is between 230M and 2.6B, which is exactly where this article says post-training damage is worst, and therefore exactly where a training-time repair has the most room to show a win. Nothing here is measured at 8B or above, where the gap it closes is already inside benchmark noise — which is where most readers of this article sit.

The control arm arrived the same week, by accident, and is the sharper half. A 150M CPU-first model planned the identical thing: spend the final 5% of training with fake quantisation on the deployment format's exact 4-bit grid, verified against the runtime's own kernel rather than reimplemented. It produced a non-finite loss on its first step, was disabled, and the failure was never diagnosed (Daedalus-150M). The finished model therefore carries the full penalty — ~6% perplexity at Q4_0 (9.18 half-precision against 9.75 on held-out encyclopedic text) — against 2.5% measured on the same project's 5B-token ablation. [T1, single study]

That second pair of numbers is the finding to carry furthest, and it is uncomfortable. The four-bit penalty more than doubled between a short run and a converged one. A better-fit model has sharper, less redundant weights, and the cost of rounding them onto a coarse grid is not constant in training length. If that holds generally, every quantization delta in this article measured on a short run is an under-estimate of what the same recipe costs at convergence — biased in the flattering direction. It is one model, one seed, one author, and the two figures come from different checkpoints, so training length is confounded with everything else that differed. Nobody has run the controlled version.

Two things that arrived in August and are not yet advice

The two-sided quantizer became affordable, and nobody has checked whether it is worth affording. GPTQ's curvature model is one-sided — H ≈ E[xxᵀ] ⊗ I_m, geometry from input activations, output coordinates assumed independent — and it became the default because it was cheap enough to run at 175B, not because it was the most faithful objective. Two-sided Kronecker-factored Hessians capture cross-output correlations and cost O(m²n²), quartic where GPTQ is cubic. Anti-diagonal parallelism plus a recursive divide-and-conquer construction makes them cubic: O(mn(m+n)), measured at 60× on an 8192×8192 matrix on a single workstation card (BaKron). The comparison that would justify switching — two-sided against one-sided at matched bit-width, at 4 bits and below — is appendix material rather than the paper's claim, there is no code link, and the authors state that at today's calibration sizes the excluded terms dominate end-to-end time anyway. The barrier moved; nobody has walked through it. [T1, one study]

The quantization run has its own memory budget, and this map has never reported it. Every entry here states what the quantized model costs. None states what running the quantizer needs resident — which for curvature-based methods is what decides whether the method is available at all. The same paper drops Hessian accumulation from O(ℓ) accelerator-resident to O(log ℓ), streaming activations from host RAM, and says plainly that the prior requirement was impractical without many GPUs. Ask it of any method before adopting it: round-to-nearest needs nothing, GPTQ needs one layer's Hessian, the two-sided family needed more than a consumer machine had. [T1, one study, suggestive]

Not settled

  • Whether quantization-aware training is worth its cost above 3B. The one shipped local-stack result spans 230M–2.6B, precisely the range where post-training damage is largest. At 7–8B the K-quant sweep already puts 4-bit within half an aggregate point of FP16, so there may be nothing left for a training pass to recover. The experiment: one 7–8B model, the same distillation recipe, Q4_0 against a post-training Q4_K_M baseline, scored on GSM8K and a code benchmark rather than commonsense. Until someone runs it, "train into the format" is small-model advice.
  • Whether the four-bit penalty really grows with training length. One project reports 2.5% at 5B tokens and ~6% at 59.9B on the same architecture and format, but from different checkpoints, so the confound is total. If it replicates it invalidates the flattering half of a lot of published quantization deltas. The experiment: one model, three checkpoints along one training run, the same quantizer and the same held-out set — cheap, and nobody has done it.
  • What a second compression costs on top of the first, at four bits. The whole local stack rests on an assumption nobody has tested, and the one direct measurement — on INT8, on one small benchmark — cost four points where the single lever cost nothing. The run is a 2×2: one 8B model, dense and INT4, each with and without a 4-bit KV cache, on a task with enough items to carry an interval. If the interaction is superadditive at four bits, every composition claim in this article needs a caveat.
  • Whether the rounding lever survives above 3B or a generative task. The result is positive on eight models and every one is under 3B, on four zero-shot multiple-choice tasks. Both restrictions point the same way: the small end is where quantization error is worst, so a repair that works there may be repairing an amplification larger models do not have, and multiple-choice accuracy is exactly the instrument that misses derivation damage. One 7–8B model, the same pipeline, scored on GSM8K and a code benchmark. The code is released.
  • Whether the language penalty survives at four bits. The measurement is W3A16 GPTQ only, and this map's entire practitioner-facing position is built at four bits, where the content axis of the same confound is worth +2.2 points against +58.8 at two bits. So the four-bit default may absorb the language penalty almost entirely, or may not, and the difference decides whether this is a scope condition on everything here or a footnote about aggressive formats. One 3B model, English and Arabic evaluation sets, GPTQ and AWQ and a GGUF K-quant, at 4 and 3 bits — a day on one card.
  • Nobody has run a reasoning benchmark on an activation-quantized model. Still the map's sharpest gap, and embarrassingly specific. All end-to-end 4-bit accuracy evidence is perplexity plus six commonsense zero-shot tasks — PIQA, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, Arc, LAMBADA — which is exactly the family the K-quant sweep found least sensitive to quantization damage. A 4.18-point drop on the easy half is a reason to expect worse on GSM8K and MMLU, and nothing more. The one number that would decide whether a 7B is usable at W4A4 does not exist.
  • AWQ vs GPTQ head-to-head, still. The critique's premise is measured — corpus swings a task benchmark by up to 58 points at ternary — but on MoE bit allocation with GPTQ underneath, so it establishes the axis matters without ranking the families on it. The experiment: one dense model, one bit-width, AWQ vs GPTQ, three calibration corpora, scored on GSM8K and MMLU rather than commonsense. Everything needed is public.
  • How much of this article is confounded by an unreported knob. The foundational comparisons above were produced with corpora that were usually WikiText2 or C4 and rarely stated as a condition. Nobody has re-run them under matched calibration. The 2026-08 boundary result is a partial reprieve — at 4 bits the effect is about 2 points — and it is one study.
  • Whether the four allocation partitions compose. Tensor class, layer, modality and expert have each been shown to matter; no source varies two. A rotated model has never been combined with mixed-precision allocation. The modality result is also the weakest of the four: the authors never run the reversed assignment (text at 4, vision at 8), so the direction of the asymmetry rests on a mechanism story.
  • The 8-bit indifference width. At 8 bits, four quantizers on a vision tower sit within 0.19 points — so a CLIP-class encoder's floor is twice the 4-bit floor this map carries for language-model weights, and nobody has explained why. [T1, one study]
  • Merge-then-quantize vs quantize-then-adapt. If LoRA inserts high-ranking singular vectors orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum, a merged adapter hands the quantizer a spectrally different matrix than the base — and every method here calibrates against distributional assumptions (NF4's normality premise, AWQ's salience channels, GPTQ's second-order reconstruction). Unmeasured.
  • Where the low-bit cliff actually comes from. At least three separate mechanisms sit on it — the bit-width, the adapter initialization, and now the calibration recipe — and no source varies more than one at a time.
