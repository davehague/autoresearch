4-bit is still the default, and 4-bit now means more than weights: the state of post-training quantization

Synthesized 2026-07-16, refreshed 2026-07-28 from post-training-quantization (status: consolidating). Eleven new dated bullets since the last version, and the headline survived all of them while changing what it denotes. The defaults below rot in months — check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim, and what changed under it

Quantize an open-weights model's weights to 4 bits after training and you keep the quality that matters. That has held since this article was first written, from three independent directions:

  • Inference, scaling family — AWQ (MIT Han Lab, MLSys 2024 Best Paper) protects ~1% of weight channels, identified from the activation distribution, via mathematically equivalent per-channel scaling. No backpropagation, no reconstruction. [T1]
  • Inference, reconstruction family — GPTQ (IST Austria/ETH, ICLR 2023) quantizes column-by-column and compensates the remaining weights using approximate second-order information from a small calibration set. 175B in ~4 GPU-hours at 3–4 bits, negligible degradation. [T1]
  • Training substrate — QLoRA (UW, 2023) backprops through a frozen 4-bit base into LoRA adapters and matches 16-bit fine-tuning; 65B on a single 48GB card. [T1]

What has changed is the scope of the word. In July 2026 "4-bit" meant weights, stored small and upcast for an FP16 matmul. It now reaches the whole forward pass, it has been checked in the file format people actually download, and the error has been shown to be structured in two ways nobody had measured.

What to do

  • Default the deploy path to 4-bit weight-only quantization. FP16 serving of open weights is the thing that needs a stated reason. [T1, three groups]
  • Do not pick a quant level from the perplexity column. Measured across 13 llama.cpp formats on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, four 5-bit formats span 0.03 perplexity and 3.4 GSM8K points — perplexity and task quality rank the menu differently (the K-quant sweep). Converting a file costs 27–43 seconds, so measuring beats guessing. [T1]
  • Expect the damage to be task-shaped, and pick your rung against your workload. The 8-, 6-, 5- and 4-bit K-quants all sit within about half an aggregate point of FP16 (Q4_K_M: 69.15 vs 69.47, at 69.4% smaller). Only 3-bit moves, and it moves unevenly — at Q3_K_S, GSM8K falls 9.3 points and MMLU 4.2 while HellaSwag falls 0.6. Precision is load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and near-free for single-step commonsense. [T1]
  • Spend precision on the shared path and compress what routes. A 16-cell sweep on Mixtral-8×7B: taking the 96.6% of parameters that are experts to 2 bits costs 3.85 MMLU points; taking the 3.4% that are not — attention, embeddings, the router — to 2 bits costs 11.5. Three times the damage from a thirtieth of the parameters, because error on a weight that every token traverses compounds through the residual stream (the offloading study). [T1, venue-less]
  • Pair that with the opposite rule for adapters. At a fixed 0.28M trainable budget, adapters on the feed-forward matrices score 89.99 on MNLI against 88.58 on the query projection, and upper layers beat lower ones by 10.7 points (AdaLoRA). The pair yields a rule neither finding states alone: precision where error compounds, adaptation where the model specializes. Nobody has tested the combination. [T1 each]
  • Report your calibration corpus, and distrust results that don't. Holding model, method and bit budget fixed on Mixtral-8×7B at 2.5 bits and varying only the calibration corpus, HumanEval reads 29.3 (C4) / 13.7 (MATH) / 25.1 (GitHub-Code) — a 2.1× spread, with MMLU moving 10.8 points (AlphaQ). There is no corrective rule: GitHub-Code calibration scores worse on HumanEval than generic C4, so "calibrate on your domain" is not the fix. [T1, one study]
  • If you are fine-tuning a quantized base, initialize jointly. At 2.5-bit on LLaMA-2-13B the same base gives 19.39 WikiText-2 perplexity under QLoRA's zero-initialized adapter and 5.22 under a jointly-solved one; at 2.25- and 2-bit QLoRA fails to converge while LoftQ reaches 7.69. The sub-3-bit cliff is partly a property of the initialization, not of the bit-width. [T1, two groups with PiSSA]
  • Pick format and runtime as one decision. GPTQ/AWQ checkpoints run on GPU-serving stacks; GGUF quants run on llama.cpp and Ollama. The artifact menu is local-inference's territory. [T2]
  • Treat 4-bit-base LoRA as the single-card baseline every bigger training plan has to beat (peft-methods). [T1]

The outlier story inverted, and it is the biggest change since July

Every method above accommodates outliers. AWQ protects the salient channels; SmoothQuant migrates their difficulty into the paired weight rows by an algebraically identical per-channel rescale; the 4-bit mixed-precision family keeps 128 or 256 outlier channels in higher precision outright. All of them take the outlier basis as given.

QuaRot (ETH Zurich / EPFL / Microsoft Research / IST Austria, NeurIPS 2024) says the basis is arbitrary. In a pre-norm RMSNorm architecture, multiplying an input-side weight matrix on the left by an orthogonal Q and the paired output-side matrix on the right by Qᵀ leaves the network's function unchanged, because RMSNorm divides each row by its norm and a rotation preserves norms. Pick Q to be a randomized Hadamard matrix, fuse it into the weights, and every coordinate mixes into every other — so a spike in one channel becomes a small contribution to all of them and there are no outliers left to protect. The cost is 1.5 Hadamard transforms per layer, each O(d log d).

The comparison that matters is one table read across its rows, at 4-bit weights, activations and KV cache on LLaMA-2-7B against a 5.47 FP16 perplexity baseline:

The 8-bit method pushed to 4 bits collapses entirely. Per-channel scaling was engineered for W8A8 and does not extend downward; rotation does. The general statement replaces two narrower ones: the enemy is outlier concentration, and concentration is basis-dependent. It also explains something the vault had recorded as a coincidence — FlashAttention-3 reaching for the same random rotation to quantize attention activations in-kernel is the same theorem applied transiently rather than fused.

Two practical consequences, and they point in opposite directions.

Compute becomes reachable on a consumer card. Once both operands are INT4 the multiplication runs on 4-bit tensor cores with no dequantize in the inner loop. Measured on RTX 3090s — chosen because the authors target consumer GPUs — prefill runs 1.97–2.16× faster on a 7B at batch 1–64 and up to 3.33× on 70B, with 3.6–3.9× lower peak decode memory. That reverses a rule this vault had from llama.cpp measurement, where weight-only quantization slows prefill by up to 28% because dequantizing packed weights costs more than the smaller read saves. Weight-only quantization slows prefill; weight-and-activation quantization speeds it up.

And the bill scales inversely with model size, which is the wrong direction for anyone reading this. The six-task zero-shot average falls 1.09 points at 70B, 2.80 at 13B and 4.18 at 7B. Every "retains 99% of zero-shot performance" framing of end-to-end 4-bit is the 70B row. The same pattern shows in calibration: round-to-nearest with no calibration data at all is lossless at INT8 on both sizes, while at INT4 it costs 2.27 perplexity on the 7B against 0.34 on the 70B.

So: W4A4KV4 is demonstrated and not yet priced for the models a local user runs. Treat it as a serious option on a 70B and as an experiment on a 7B until someone publishes the number in the next section.

Not settled

  • Nobody has run a reasoning benchmark on an activation-quantized model. This is now the map's sharpest gap and it is embarrassingly specific. All of the end-to-end 4-bit accuracy evidence is perplexity plus six commonsense zero-shot tasks — PIQA, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, Arc, LAMBADA — which is exactly the family the K-quant sweep found to be least sensitive to quantization damage. A 4.18-point drop on the easy half is a reason to expect worse on GSM8K and MMLU, and nothing more. The one number that would decide whether a 7B is usable at W4A4 does not exist.
  • AWQ vs GPTQ head-to-head, still, and now better specified. The critique's premise is measured — the calibration corpus swings a task benchmark 2.1× — but it was measured on MoE bit allocation with GPTQ underneath, so it establishes that the axis matters without ranking the two families on it. The experiment: one dense model, one bit-width, AWQ vs GPTQ, three calibration corpora, evaluated on GSM8K and MMLU rather than commonsense tasks. Everything needed to run it is public.
  • How much of this article is confounded by an unreported knob. If calibration corpus moves task scores by double digits, every published GPTQ-family number above was produced with a corpus that was usually WikiText2 or C4 and rarely stated as a condition. Nobody has re-run the foundational comparisons under matched calibration.
  • Below 4 bits is a slope with at least three separate mechanisms on it — the bit-width itself, the adapter initialization (LoftQ), and the allocation across layers — and no source varies more than one. A rotated model has never been combined with mixed-precision allocation; the rotation paper names mixture-of-experts as future work, and the vault already holds two independent MoE allocation results it could be joined to.
  • The KV cache is quantizable and the local stack's version is ungraded. KIVI does 2-bit KV tuning-free by matching granularity to structure — keys per-channel, values per-token — near-lossless at 2.6× less peak memory. But those numbers were measured on multi-head-attention models, and every model you would actually run ships with grouped-query attention, which already shrinks the cache architecturally. The in-vault KV figures are an upper bound on what precision adds to a model people deploy, and llama.cpp's own KV-quant options have no graded comparison at all.
  • Merge-then-quantize vs quantize-then-adapt, sharpened rather than closed. If LoRA inserts high-ranking singular vectors orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum, a merged adapter hands the quantizer a spectrally different matrix than the base — and every method here calibrates against distributional assumptions (NF4's normality premise, AWQ's salience channels, GPTQ's second-order reconstruction). Unmeasured.
  • Quantization is arriving at training time too, and it teaches the same lesson from the other side. DeepSeek-V3 trained a 671B model in FP8 and had to quantize activations in 1×128 tiles and weights in 128×128 blocks, because the Hopper tensor core accumulates FP8 products at roughly 14 bits — per-tensor scaling was not enough. Granularity is the whole game at both ends of the pipeline. Note the expiry date: they ask hardware vendors to put tile-wise scaling inside the tensor cores, so every number measured around its absence is provisional.
