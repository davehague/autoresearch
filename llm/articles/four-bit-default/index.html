4-bit is the working default: the state of post-training quantization

Synthesized 2026-07-16 from post-training-quantization (status: consolidating). Three independent T1s; the defaults below rot in months — check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

Quantize open-weights model weights to 4 bits post-training and you keep the quality that matters. As of 2026-07 the vault holds this from three independent directions:

  • Inference, scaling family — AWQ (MIT Han Lab, MLSys 2024 Best Paper) keeps 4-bit quality by protecting ~1% of weight channels, identified from the activation distribution, via mathematically equivalent per-channel scaling. No backpropagation, no reconstruction — calibration stays cheap and doesn't overfit. [T1]
  • Inference, reconstruction family — GPTQ (IST Austria/ETH, ICLR 2023) spends the error budget optimally per layer: quantize column-by-column, compensate remaining weights using approximate second-order information from a small calibration set. A 175B model quantizes to 3–4 bits in ~4 GPU-hours with negligible degradation and fits one GPU — 3.25× generation speedup vs FP16 on A100 with their kernels. [T1]
  • Training substrate — QLoRA (UW, 2023) backprops through a frozen 4-bit base (NF4) into LoRA adapters and matches 16-bit fine-tune performance on the tasks tested — 65B fine-tuned on a single 48GB GPU. If 4-bit weights can carry gradients well enough to train against, they carry inference. [T1]

Same headline from opposite philosophies and a third from the training side. That convergence — not any single paper — is why the map sits at consolidating.

What to do

  • Default the deploy path to 4-bit weight-only quantization; treat FP16 serving of open-weights models as the thing that needs a stated reason. [T1, three groups]
  • Buying quality back: remember the error is concentrated — the activations say which ~1% of channels carry it. Any quantization decision made by staring only at weights is working blind. [T1]
  • Pick format and runtime as one decision: GPTQ/AWQ checkpoints run on GPU-serving stacks; GGUF quants run on llama.cpp/Ollama. The artifact menu is the local-inference map's territory. [T2]
  • Fine-tuning and quantization compose: 4-bit-base LoRA (QLoRA) is the single-card baseline every bigger training plan must beat (peft-methods). [T1]
  • The trade every method buys: bits vs quality is the visible axis, but the hidden axis is calibration — reconstruction (GPTQ) fits a calibration set and risks overfitting it (AWQ's critique); scaling (AWQ) avoids that but leans on its salience heuristic. [T1 each, no neutral referee]

Not settled

  • AWQ vs GPTQ head-to-head. Each family's numbers come from its own authors; AWQ's overfitting critique of reconstruction is a competitor's claim, not a neutral measurement. Which wins where — calibration sensitivity, domain transfer, modern architectures — is the map's sharpest open question.
  • Below 4 bits. GPTQ reports usable accuracy at 2-bit/ternary in restricted settings; the GGUF IQ-series lives down there. Where quality actually falls off a cliff has no graded in-vault comparison yet.
  • Weight-only is half the story. Activations and the KV cache stay high-precision in everything above. vLLM's PagedAttention (local-inference) attacks the KV memory wall by placement; shrinking the entries themselves (KV-cache quantization) is unserved.
  • Merge-then-quantize vs quantize-then-adapt. How a merged LoRA changes the weight distribution a quantizer sees is unmeasured — live for the fine-tune→ship-locally pipeline.
