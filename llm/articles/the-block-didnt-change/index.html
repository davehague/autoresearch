The block didn't change: what actually moved in transformer internals

Synthesized 2026-07-19, refreshed 2026-08-05 from transformer-architecture (status: consolidating). Seventeen dated findings landed between those two dates, and they did two things: they piled more evidence onto the original thesis, and — at the very top of the scale range — they started falsifying it. As of 2026-08 the architecture claims below are stable for anything you can run; the performance numbers are not, and every one of them is attached to a specific card, precision, and date.

The claim

The 2017 block is still the block, for every model you can fine-tune or serve yourself. Open-weights models are decoder-only stacks of scaled dot-product attention over Q/K/V, multi-head attention, a position-wise MLP, residuals, and normalization — the component set Vaswani et al. named. Everything a practitioner touches is named there too: the projections LoRA adapts, the K/V tensors the cache stores, the matrices the quantizer compresses.

What moved was mostly not the architecture. Three categories, and they age differently.

Two components genuinely changed form

Both are about a budget, not about capability.

  • Position stopped being additive. RoFormer's rotary embedding (Zhuiyi, 2021) multiplies the Q and K projections by a block-diagonal rotation scaled to the token's absolute index. Rotations compose — R_mᵀ R_n = R_{n-m} — so the attention score depends only on the offset. No position table sized to a maximum length, distance decay by construction, and compatibility with linear attention that additive relative schemes break. [T1]
  • The KV cache became a design parameter. GQA (Google Research, EMNLP 2023) shares g key/value heads across groups of query heads. On T5-XXL, GQA-8 scores 47.1 average against MHA's 47.2 at 0.28 s/sample vs 1.51 s — 5.4× decode speed for 0.1 points — and an existing multi-head checkpoint converts by uptraining at 5% of original pretraining compute. [T1]

That list has since grown a third entry from the frontier. DeepSeek-V3 attacks the same cache by factorizing rather than sharing: Multi-head Latent Attention compresses keys and values into a low-rank latent vector, caches that plus one small decoupled key, and reconstructs per-head K and V at use time. So there are now three ways to shrink one cache — fewer heads, fewer bits, fewer dimensions — and only the first is universal in models you can download today. [T1]

Everything else was systems work on unchanged math

This is the load-bearing observation, and it now has more independent support than any other claim on the map.

  • FlashAttention (Stanford/SUNY Buffalo, NeurIPS 2022) re-scheduled exact attention against the GPU memory hierarchy — tile Q/K/V into SRAM, never materialize the n×n matrix in HBM, recompute it backward from softmax statistics. Memory goes quadratic → linear, 3× on GPT-2 at seq 1K, output bit-identical. Attention's cost was memory movement, not arithmetic. [T1]
  • FlashAttention-2 (Dao, 2023) found a second bottleneck underneath the first: FlashAttention-1 still hit only 25–40% of an A100's peak while optimized GEMM reaches 80–90%, and the gap was work partitioning. Deferring the online-softmax rescale (non-matmul FLOPs cost ~16× matmul FLOPs on an A100 — 19.5 vs 312 TFLOPs/s), parallelizing over sequence-length blocks, and splitting Q across warps gets up to 73% of peak (230 TFLOPs/s). Still bit-exact. [T1]
  • FlashAttention-3 (2024) closed the loop this article opened in July as a prediction. The A100-tuned kernel reaches only 35% of an H100; a Hopper-native rebuild (warp-specialized producer/consumer asynchrony, matmul-softmax interleaving, FP8 with block quantization) restores 75% utilization / 740 TFLOPs/s at FP16 and ~1.2 PFLOPs/s at FP8, at 1.5–2.0×. The expiry was inferred on 2026-07-19 and measured on 2026-07-20. [T1]

And two structural facts about the block that nobody had used

New since the last refresh, and both are the kind of finding that pays without changing a weight.

  • The block has a continuous symmetry, and the coordinate system its activations live in carries no information. Because RMSNorm divides each row by its norm and rotations preserve norms, RMSNorm(X) = RMSNorm(XQᵀ)Q — multiply an input-side weight on the left by Q and its paired output-side weight on the right by Qᵀ and the output is unchanged. QuaRot uses that to rotate away the outlier channels that make activations hard to quantize, which is what makes end-to-end 4-bit inference reachable. Two caveats worth carrying: the symmetry holds for pre-norm RMSNorm specifically (a rescaling normalization breaks it until the per-channel scale is absorbed into neighbouring weights), and RoPE breaks fusion where LayerNorm does not — the positional rotation sits between W_k and the score, so it cannot be folded into the weights and must run online. [T1]
  • The stack divides labour unevenly across its own depth. Probing Qwen3 from 0.6B to 32B, semantic content is assembled by roughly 0.45L and that depth is close to scale-invariant, while upper layers progressively specialize for the current query. It is actionable: a rank-32 LoRA on a frozen backbone, trained only to match a full-depth teacher's top-64 logits on plain text with no task supervision, moves the usable readout split and takes a five-benchmark average from 28.32 to 53.38. [T1, correlational — one model family]

What to do

  • Read architecture claims and performance claims on different clocks. The block is stable enough to learn once. Any tok/s, TFLOPs/s, or speedup figure is a claim about a kernel, a card, a precision, and a date. [T1, three sources]
  • Never compare throughput benchmarked on different silicon. The FlashAttention line demonstrates the trap against itself twice — predicted in FA2, measured in FA3 at 35% of an H100 for an A100-tuned kernel. The tuning does not travel. [T1]
  • Assume GQA when reasoning about the KV budget. Nearly every open-weights model you would fine-tune already ships with it, so KV-quantization and paging gains measured on MHA models are upper bounds on what they add to what you actually run. [T1]
  • Expect the quantization decision to move inside the kernel. FlashAttention-3's FP8 path uses incoherent processing — multiplying by a random orthogonal matrix to spread outliers before quantizing — for 2.6× lower numerical error. That is the same move the weight-quantization literature applies offline, applied to activations at runtime. [T1]
  • The trade each lever buys: GQA spends a little quality for a lot of decode speed; FlashAttention spends recomputation for memory; FlashAttention-2 spends portability for occupancy; rotation-based quantization spends an online matmul for outlier-free 4-bit; rotary position spends nothing measurable and buys structure. Only the first is a quality trade, and it is small enough that the field took it universally.

Not settled

The headline claim now has a scale condition on it, and that is the biggest change since July. Through 2026-07 every arrival confirmed "systems progress on an unchanged architecture." Two things since have complicated it, and both sit above the hardware this vault is about.

  • Sparse routing is a real architecture change, not a systems one. Mixture-of-experts decouples parameter count from per-token compute — that is a different block, not a faster one. It earned its own map on 2026-07-21. The catch for anyone reading this on a consumer card is that the decoupling is a pretraining-FLOPs claim: on bandwidth-bound edge hardware, inference cost tracks total parameters, not active ones.
  • The open frontier is now changing several components at once, and none of it is reachable. Kimi K3 (2.78T total / 104.2B active) ships no position encoding at all on its global-attention layers, a linear-attention recurrence carrying position implicitly, attention across depth replacing plain residual accumulation, a bounded gated FFN replacing SwiGLU because SwiGLU is unbounded in both factors and produces low-precision overflow, and routed experts running at half model width. That is four component changes in one artifact. It is also ~1.4 TB of weights, with no consumer or single-node configuration reported anywhere, and a CC-BY-NC-ND licence. So the honest 2026-08 statement is narrower than the 2026-07 one: the block you can run didn't change; the block at the frontier is changing, and the two have not converged before. [T1, vendor report, no ablation of any architecture claim]

Still open, and mostly unchanged:

  • RMSNorm and SwiGLU still have no in-vault source of their own, nor do tokenization, generation and sampling, or where knowledge actually lives in the weights. Normalization placement is sourced twice and only suggestively — post-LN BERT degrades past a few hundred million parameters, and mixed image+text training diverges past ~7B without QK-normalization — so there is a stability layer beneath the headline components that gates whether a large model trains at all, re-tuned for each new stressor, with no isolating ablation in either source.
  • GQA's evidence is still encoder-decoder. T5-XXL, from Google. The decoder-only replication is universal in practice and unmeasured here.
  • Nobody has stacked the KV levers. Fewer heads (GQA), fewer bits (KV quantization), fewer dimensions (MLA), and less waste (paging) all attack one budget, and every in-vault number isolates one on its own hardware. A single-runtime, single-card measurement of the combination is the sharpest missing datum across this map, kv-cache-management and local-inference — and on 2026-07-29 the vault found the levers do not reliably stack, so the assumption they compose is now known to be unsafe rather than merely untested.
  • The position thread left this map on 2026-07-20 and is contested where it landed: cheap post-hoc window extension works, and whether a small cheaply-extended model gets usable rather than low-perplexity context is unresolved. See context-extension.
  • What FP8 attention costs in downstream task quality is unmeasured. FlashAttention-3 reports numerical error, not benchmark scores.
