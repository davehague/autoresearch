Measured on your own machine: the state of local inference

Synthesized 2026-07-24 from local-inference, the map's first article, written the run the map moved emerging → consolidating. It held at emerging through five strong sources for a specific reason: every mechanism in it had been measured on a datacenter GPU or a TPU, and the map is named for the machines that are neither. Two llama.cpp studies landed this run and changed that. The mechanism claims here are still better-sourced than the local measurements that now anchor them — read the map's through-line before trusting any number in 2027.

The claim

Running a model locally is four decisions — format, precision, runtime, hardware — and until this month the vault could only source three of them honestly. The format half settled early: GGUF won local distribution because it is a self-describing single file — typed key-value metadata, mmap loading, parseable without a library — so a fine-tuned model ships as a file copy and a conversion command. [T2]

The physics under everything else is one sentence, from FlashAttention: on modern accelerators arithmetic is nearly free and moving data is the bill. [T1] Every technique below is a way of moving fewer bytes, and the useful discipline when you read a speedup claim is to ask which currency it spends.

What to do

Start at Q4_K_M and only move if you measured a reason. The 13-format llama.cpp sweep on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct puts 4-, 5-, 6- and 8-bit all within roughly half an aggregate point of FP16's 69.47 across GSM8K, HellaSwag, IFEval, MMLU and TruthfulQA — Q4_K_M scores 69.15 at 69.4% smaller. Above 4-bit you are paying disk and bandwidth for nothing measurable. [T1]

Treat 3-bit as task-conditional, not as broken. The same sweep: at Q3_K_S, GSM8K falls 9.3 points (77.63 → 68.31) and MMLU 4.2, while HellaSwag falls 0.6. Precision is load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and instruction compliance and close to free for single-step commonsense — so a quant level that costs nothing on a classification or chat workload can cost a tenth of a math benchmark. Within 3-bit it is a slope, not a cliff: Q3_K_L recovers most of Q3_K_S's loss for four points of file size. [T1]

Do not pick a format from the perplexity column. The four 5-bit variants span 0.03 perplexity and 3.4 GSM8K points. Perplexity is a token-averaged likelihood, dominated by the many easy tokens and blind to the few hard ones — and multi-step reasoning fails on exactly the hard ones. Most community comparisons publish perplexity and nothing else. [T1]

Pick your quant for your prompt-to-generation ratio, not just your VRAM. This is the least intuitive result of the run. Decode is bandwidth-bound, so it speeds up monotonically as the file shrinks: 2.83 → 9.91 tok/s from F16 to Q3_K_S, a 3.5× span. Prefill is compute-bound and does the opposite — throughput peaks mid-ladder (Q4_0 at 97.35 tok/s against F16's 79.57) and then falls to 57.39 at Q3_K_S, 28% below full precision, because dequantizing packed weights costs more than the smaller read saves. A long-prefill RAG workload and a long-generation agent workload should not run the same file. [T1]

Budget for the KV cache separately, and know it has three levers. Once weights are quantized the cache is what fills the card, and it grows with context and concurrency rather than parameter count. Three independent levers act on it: architecture (GQA — 8 KV heads at roughly MHA quality), precision (KIVI — 2-bit KV), and placement (PagedAttention — near-zero waste, 2–4× serving throughput). Trade-off worth naming: the models you actually run already ship with GQA, so KV-quantization gains measured on MHA models are upper bounds on what they add. [T1]

If you need latency on one stream and have arithmetic to spare, speculate. Speculative decoding is the only lever in this map that costs no quality at all: a small draft model proposes tokens, the target verifies them in one parallel pass, and a modified rejection rule makes the output provably identical in distribution — 2.8–3.4× at batch size 1. The counter-intuitive tuning rule is that the best draft is a deliberately weak one; a closer draft's inference cost eats the winnings. [T1]

Do not assume a sparse model runs like its active parameter count on a small device. See below — this is the run's other new result, and it is the one most likely to change a purchase decision.

Where the evidence pushes back

On the edge, cost tracks total parameters, not active ones. The MoE pitch to a local user is that a 1.3B-active / 6.9B-total model costs what a 1.3B dense model costs. An empirical study on consumer and edge hardware ran that claim through llama.cpp at Q4_K_M and found it does not hold: OLMoE-1B-7B is 10% behind Llama-3.2-1B on an Apple M2 Pro (114.5 vs 127.4 tok/s) and 31% behind on a Jetson Orin Nano (22.9 vs 33.4), where it also loses to Qwen2.5-1.5B — a model with more active parameters — at 2.1× the energy per token (0.96 vs 0.45 J/tok) and 8.01 GiB peak resident memory against an 8 GB ceiling. [T1]

The instrumented half is what makes this durable rather than a benchmark artifact. Patching llama.cpp to wall-clock each node of the decode graph puts routing under 9% of MoE-block time on the backend whose instrumentation is trustworthy. It is not the router, so it is not a kernel that will improve: to serve 1.3B of compute you must keep 6.9B parameters resident, and residency is exactly what a bandwidth-bound device is short of. Sparsity is an answer denominated in FLOPs, and FLOPs are not what an SBC lacks. [T1]

Note what this does not say, because the headline invites over-reading. The MoE decoupling itself is a pretraining-FLOPs claim and is untouched — the Switch and Mixtral results stand. What fails is the inference corollary a local user would draw from them.

Every throughput number here was measured on different silicon, and they are not commensurable. FlashAttention-2 showed kernel tuning does not transfer across GPU generations, and FlashAttention-3 confirmed it by measurement: the A100-tuned kernel reaches 35% of an H100, while a Hopper-native rebuild restores 75%. So a tok/s figure is a claim about a kernel, a card, a precision — and, new this run, which phase was measured. Comparing two runtimes benchmarked on different machines is comparing nothing. [T1]

What is still unsettled

llama.cpp is now sourced as an instrument, not as a system. Two studies run it; neither describes it. The ggml graph, the CPU/Metal kernel split, threading and batching behaviour, mmap strategy, Ollama's serving layer — no graded source, and that gap is harder to close from papers because the documentation is the repository.

The quant ladder is one model on one CPU. Whether the task-shaped 3-bit damage holds at 70B (larger models are widely believed to tolerate aggressive quantization better, and nothing in-vault tests it), whether it holds on an MoE base, and where the prefill/decode crossover sits on Apple silicon rather than a 96-core Xeon are all open. The IQ series and MXFP4 rungs are unmeasured entirely. The provenance is also the weakest T1 in the vault — a single-author preprint with no venue.

The new ladder evidence is weight-only, at short context — which is to say it deliberately avoids the regime where this map says the KV budget binds. Nobody has measured llama.cpp's own KV-quantization options against KIVI's numbers, or the three KV levers together on one runtime.

Nobody has measured what an offload-aware runtime would recover on edge MoE. The edge study runs -ngl 99 — full residency, no expert offloading — and its decomposition stops at "routing is not the problem" without separating weight traffic from expert dispatch from KV pressure. Only the first is irreducible. Whether the measured route locality (~28% consecutive-token expert reuse, from DeepSeekMoE) is enough for an expert cache to close a 31% gap is the actionable unknown.

And the sharpest missing datum for anyone running this stack: acceptance rates for speculative decoding are measured on full-precision pairs. Whether a quantized draft and a quantized target still agree at the 0.6–0.8 rates the method needs — the only pairing a llama.cpp user would ever run — is unknown.

The trajectory

As of 2026-07, the honest summary is that this map spent its first six weeks reasoning about local deployment from datacenter evidence, and has just started replacing it. The mechanisms did not change when the local numbers arrived; the conclusions drawn from them did, in both directions — the 4-bit default survived translation into K-quants intact, and the MoE inference story did not survive contact with an 8 GB device at all. Expect more of the second kind. The gap between "this technique works" and "this technique works on the machine you own" is where this map's remaining open questions all live.
