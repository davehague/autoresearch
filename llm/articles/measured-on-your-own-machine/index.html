Measured on your own machine: the state of local inference

Synthesized 2026-07-24 from local-inference, the map's first article, written the run the map moved emerging → consolidating. Refreshed 2026-07-29 after nine dated bullets landed on the map in five days — the whole serving stack (scheduling, prefix reuse, chunked prefill, disaggregation) and, this run, the first measurement of two of these levers running at once. The refresh changes the article's spine. The original thesis was that mechanisms measured in a datacenter do not automatically hold on your machine. That still stands, and there is now a second-order version of it: the levers have also only ever been measured one at a time, and the single time anyone ran two together on a consumer card, they did not add. Read the map's through-line before trusting any number here in 2027.

The claim

Running a model locally is four decisions — format, precision, runtime, hardware — and the honest state of the evidence is that each is well sourced in isolation and almost nothing is sourced in combination. The format half settled early: GGUF won local distribution because it is a self-describing single file — typed key-value metadata, mmap loading, parseable without a library — so a fine-tuned model ships as a file copy and a conversion command. [T2]

The physics under everything else is one sentence, from FlashAttention: on modern accelerators arithmetic is nearly free and moving data is the bill. [T1] Every technique below is a way of moving fewer bytes, and most of them pay for it in arithmetic.

That last clause is the article's new organizing idea, so state it plainly. Ask what currency a speedup spends, and do not assume two techniques spending the same currency will add. A decode step has a finite amount of idle compute to sell. Quantization sells it. Speculation sells it. Sell it twice and the second sale fails — measurably, and worst on the smallest machine.

What to do

Start at Q4_K_M and only move if you measured a reason. The 13-format llama.cpp sweep on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct puts 4-, 5-, 6- and 8-bit all within roughly half an aggregate point of FP16's 69.47 across GSM8K, HellaSwag, IFEval, MMLU and TruthfulQA — Q4_K_M scores 69.15 at 69.4% smaller. Above 4-bit you are paying disk and bandwidth for nothing measurable. [T1]

Treat 3-bit as task-conditional, not as broken. The same sweep: at Q3_K_S, GSM8K falls 9.3 points (77.63 → 68.31) and MMLU 4.2, while HellaSwag falls 0.6. Precision is load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and instruction compliance and close to free for single-step commonsense — so a quant level that costs nothing on a classification or chat workload can cost a tenth of a math benchmark. Within 3-bit it is a slope, not a cliff: Q3_K_L recovers most of Q3_K_S's loss for four points of file size. [T1]

Do not pick a format from the perplexity column. The four 5-bit variants span 0.03 perplexity and 3.4 GSM8K points. New this run, an order of magnitude wider and from a different toolchain: a study of speculative decoding on quantized models reports one Llama-3-8B quantization ladder spanning 0.56 WikiText2 perplexity and 10.98 HumanEval points. Perplexity is a token-averaged likelihood, dominated by the many easy tokens and blind to the few hard ones — and multi-step reasoning and code fail on exactly the hard ones. Most community comparisons publish perplexity and nothing else. [T1]

Pick your quant for your prompt-to-generation ratio, not just your VRAM. Decode is bandwidth-bound, so it speeds up monotonically as the file shrinks: 2.83 → 9.91 tok/s from F16 to Q3_K_S, a 3.5× span. Prefill is compute-bound and does the opposite — throughput peaks mid-ladder (Q4_0 at 97.35 tok/s against F16's 79.57) and then falls to 57.39 at Q3_K_S, 28% below full precision, because dequantizing packed weights costs more than the smaller read saves. A long-prefill RAG workload and a long-generation agent workload should not run the same file. [T1]

Match the serving lever to your regime, because the three do not overlap. This is the section the original article was written too early to have. The stack has four layers and each was designed for a different situation. Iteration-level scheduling (continuous batching) reschedules after every token so early finishers return and arrivals join — 36.9× throughput on GPT-3 175B at matched median latency, and its one clear loss is max_bs=1. PagedAttention pages the KV cache like virtual memory — near-zero waste, 2–4× throughput. Prefix caching keeps a radix tree over cached KV blocks and reuses any exact shared prefix — 74.1% hit rate on Vicuna-33B across a month of production serving, cutting first-token latency 1.7×, and costing under 0.3% when there is no reuse to find. Speculative decoding attacks serial depth for one stream. So: many users → batch continuously; one user, single-shot → speculate; one user, multi-turn → cache the prefix. Prefix reuse is the only one of the four whose mechanism predicts a single-user win. [T1]

If you need latency on one stream, speculate — but not the way the defaults tell you to, if your weights are 4-bit. Speculative decoding remains the only lever here that costs no quality at all: a small draft proposes tokens, the target verifies them in one parallel pass, and a modified rejection rule makes the output provably identical in distribution — 2.8–3.4× at batch size 1. What changed this run is the tuning. On a 4-bit weight model, draft shallower and flatter than the defaults: halving a tree-shaped draft moves a 4-bit 8B from 2.7× up to 2.9× while moving the FP16 model 2.3× down to 2.1×, and cutting draft passes from six to three takes the 4-bit model to 3.4×. Taken to the limit, plain sequential speculation beats the tree-based state of the art on a 4-bit 70B (2.31× against 1.80×) despite drafting eleven times slower. [T1]

Budget for the KV cache separately, and know it has three levers. Once weights are quantized the cache is what fills the card, and it grows with context and concurrency rather than parameter count. Three independent levers act on it: architecture (GQA — 8 KV heads at roughly MHA quality), precision (KIVI — 2-bit KV, and new this run TurboQuant — 3.5 bits per channel at parity with a 16-bit cache on a 13-task long-context benchmark, with no calibration data at all), and placement (PagedAttention, above). Two trade-offs worth naming. The models you actually run already ship with GQA, so KV-quantization gains measured on MHA models are upper bounds on what they add. And the 3.5-bit result is not reachable from llama.cpp — its non-integer widths come from splitting channels into outlier and non-outlier sets (32 channels at 3 bits, 96 at 2), which the K-quant menu has no way to express. [T1]

Do not assume a sparse model runs like its active parameter count on a small device. See below.

Where the evidence pushes back

The two levers everyone stacks do not stack, and a consumer card is where it shows. This is the run's headline and it is a negative result. Speculative decoding and weight quantization both convert idle arithmetic into saved memory traffic. Quantization gets there first, because it changes the cost of the forward pass itself; speculation then finds a pass that is much cheaper in bytes and unchanged in FLOPs, and its verification step is now large relative to what it is measured against. Applying EAGLE-2 to a 4-bit Llama-3-8B yields a 1.3× relative speedup where the FP16 model gets 2.2× on an A100, because the target's verification-to-decoding time ratio — ideally about 1, since verifying n tokens and decoding one read the same weights — stays below 1.2 for FP16 and 8-bit, reaches 1.8 for 4-bit on an A100, and about 2.3 on an RTX 3090. Across the paper's whole grid of quantization schemes crossed with hardware, integration beats either technique alone in every cell but one: 4-bit weights on the 8B model on the RTX 3090 — precisely what a reader of this article would run. [T1]

Two things this does not say. It is not an agreement problem: average accepted length under quantization tracks full precision within noise, so the draft and the quantized target still agree fine. And it does not condemn the combination — the paper's own fix is a hierarchy, inserting a mid-sized model to absorb the tree-shaped draft and hand the quantized target a flat sequence, for 2.78× and 98.19 tok/s on a 4-bit 70B. But that fix keeps a draft head, an 8B and a 70B resident to buy latency, and nobody has priced that against simply running the target alone on a card where the target barely fits.

On the edge, cost tracks total parameters, not active ones. The MoE pitch to a local user is that a 1.3B-active / 6.9B-total model costs what a 1.3B dense model costs. An empirical study on consumer and edge hardware ran that claim through llama.cpp at Q4_K_M and found it does not hold: OLMoE-1B-7B is 10% behind Llama-3.2-1B on an Apple M2 Pro (114.5 vs 127.4 tok/s) and 31% behind on a Jetson Orin Nano (22.9 vs 33.4), where it also loses to Qwen2.5-1.5B — a model with more active parameters — at 2.1× the energy per token (0.96 vs 0.45 J/tok) and 8.01 GiB peak resident memory against an 8 GB ceiling. [T1]

The instrumented half is what makes this durable rather than a benchmark artifact. Patching llama.cpp to wall-clock each node of the decode graph puts routing under 9% of MoE-block time on the backend whose instrumentation is trustworthy. It is not the router, so it is not a kernel that will improve: to serve 1.3B of compute you must keep 6.9B parameters resident, and residency is exactly what a bandwidth-bound device is short of. Sparsity is an answer denominated in FLOPs, and FLOPs are not what an SBC lacks. [T1]

Note what this does not say, because the headline invites over-reading. The MoE decoupling itself is a pretraining-FLOPs claim and is untouched — the Switch and Mixtral results stand. What fails is the inference corollary a local user would draw from them.

Almost every serving number in this map is a datacenter number, and two of the four layers were designed for a problem you do not have. Chunked prefill exists to stop an arriving request's prefill from stalling incumbent decodes — a situation one user never creates — and disaggregated serving removes the same interference by putting prefill and decode on separate replicas and migrating the KV cache between them, which is a fleet-level design. Both are A100-class work. What chunked prefill does hand the local stack is a knob llama.cpp already exposes (the physical batch size) with two numbers attached and neither measured locally: the chunking penalty is about 25% at 512 tokens and near zero at 2048, and tile quantization makes a chunk of 257 cost 32% more than 256 — a cliff from a one-token config change. [T1]

Every throughput number here was measured on different silicon, and they are not commensurable. FlashAttention-2 showed kernel tuning does not transfer across GPU generations, and FlashAttention-3 confirmed it by measurement: the A100-tuned kernel reaches 35% of an H100, while a Hopper-native rebuild restores 75%. So a tok/s figure is a claim about a kernel, a card, a precision, and which phase was measured. Comparing two runtimes benchmarked on different machines is comparing nothing. [T1]

What is still unsettled

Only one pair of levers has ever been measured together, and it came back negative. That is the single most important sentence in this article's open-questions list. Prefix reuse × quantization (they push opposite ways — reuse cuts prefill, quantization slows prefill and speeds decode), KV quantization × weight quantization, speculation × KV quantization, chunked prefill × anything: every one of these is assumed to compose, on no evidence. The currency test above predicts which pairs are at risk. Nobody has run any of them.

The measurement this map actually wants remains unpublished, and it is cheap. llama.cpp or Ollama, one user, a multi-turn conversation, with and without prefix reuse, at a GGUF quant level, on one card, reporting first-token latency and decode throughput separately. Every ingredient is public and free.

The quantized-draft question is answered at a finer grain than it was asked, and replaced by a harder one. The article previously named acceptance rates on quantized pairs as the sharpest missing datum. Partial answer: a 4-bit 8B drafting for a 4-bit 70B reaches an average accepted length of 5.28, higher than the full-precision draft heads it replaces — so 4-bit agreement is fine for a real draft model, and the fragility reported elsewhere belongs to quantizing a single-transformer-layer draft head, which has no redundancy to lose. What replaces it: does a two-model speculative setup ever fit and pay on 12–24 GB? The hierarchy that fixes the compute wall costs memory on the device where memory binds, and nobody has priced it.

llama.cpp is sourced as an instrument, not as a system. Several studies run it; none describes it. The ggml graph, the CPU/Metal kernel split, threading and batching behaviour, mmap strategy, Ollama's serving layer — no graded source, and that gap is harder to close from papers because the documentation is the repository.

The quant ladder is one model on one CPU. Whether the task-shaped 3-bit damage holds at 70B, whether it holds on an MoE base, and where the prefill/decode crossover sits on Apple silicon rather than a 96-core Xeon are all open. The IQ series and MXFP4 rungs are unmeasured entirely. The provenance is also the weakest T1 in this article — a single-author preprint with no venue.

Neither KV-quantization source reports a single speed number. Both are footprint-and-quality only, which matters more here than for weight quantization because a KV quantizer sits on the critical path of every attention call and could plausibly slow decode while shrinking it. Both also sit on full-precision weights, which nobody deploys.

Nobody has measured what an offload-aware runtime would recover on edge MoE. The edge study runs -ngl 99 — full residency, no expert offloading — and its decomposition stops at "routing is not the problem" without separating weight traffic from expert dispatch from KV pressure. Only the first is irreducible. Whether the measured route locality (~28% consecutive-token expert reuse, from DeepSeekMoE) is enough for an expert cache to close a 31% gap is the actionable unknown.

The trajectory

As of 2026-07, this map spent its first six weeks reasoning about local deployment from datacenter evidence and has spent the last two replacing it — and the replacement keeps arriving as a subtraction. The 4-bit default survived translation into K-quants intact. The MoE inference story did not survive contact with an 8 GB device. Two of the four serving layers turned out to solve a multi-tenant problem a single user does not have. And now the assumption underneath all of it — that these techniques compose — has failed its first test, on the exact hardware this vault is about.

None of that makes the stack worse. It makes the arithmetic legible, which is the only thing that lets you predict a result instead of benchmarking your way to it. The rule to carry forward: name the currency each lever spends, and be suspicious of any two levers that spend the same one. The gap between "this technique works" and "this technique works on the machine you own, alongside the other thing you already turned on" is where every remaining open question here lives.
