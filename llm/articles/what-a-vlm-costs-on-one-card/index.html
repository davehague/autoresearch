What a VLM costs on one card: the state of VLMs on consumer hardware

Synthesized 2026-09-29 from vlm-on-device (status: contested), on the night it crossed the [article-due] threshold — eleven dated through-line findings and no article yet. This map forked out of vision-language-models on 2026-08-02: the parent argues architecture (early fusion vs. late fusion) on capability benchmarks; this map stopped asking that question and started asking what it costs to run or adapt a vision-language model on one consumer card, in milliseconds, megabytes and bits. It is contested, not consolidating — carry that tension rather than smoothing over it, per the vault's own rule that a contested map is more worth writing, not less.

The founding problem: everything here prices a component, and one VLM's whole answer still has a missing column

Two years into local LLM deployment being a solved-enough problem to have defaults, running a vision-language model on one card is still a scavenger hunt across separately-measured pieces: a CLIP encoder's precision budget, an inference engine's embedding cache, a serving stack's prefix reuse, and — finally, in 2026-08 — a shipped generative VLM at a stated size. None of the early sources measured the same thing, which is why this map stayed emerging for three weeks after it forked.

The perception front-end is cheap relative to the language model, and its precision budget should be allocated by modality, not set globally. A quantized CLIP ViT-B/16 adapting at test time without a backward pass runs at 196.73 MB / 26.04 ms per sample at 8 bits and 139.63 MB / 14.92 ms at 4 bits on one consumer GPU, batch size 1 (LQA, T1). The design rule this source argues for — vision at 4 bits, text at 8, with a projection/layer-norm layer or two kept at float16 — is the first of three independent partitioning arguments this map carries (after per-layer and per-tensor-class allocation on the text-only quantization map). Two boundaries travel with it: at 8 bits the allocation buys essentially nothing (four quantizers within 0.19 points of each other), and below 4 bits the descent is a cliff (60.59% / 42.54% / 3.02% at 3/2/1 bits) rather than the graded ladder an 8B language model shows at the same widths. Scope it hard: this prices CLIP, the encoder late fusion imports, not a generative VLM — it says nothing about the image-token KV load local-inference says binds first.

Small models quantize worse, and this map now has five directional supports for it across four architectures and two literatures. A CLIP encoder falling off a cliff below 4 bits; a 2B generative VLM losing 15 AUROC points of calibration where a 7B loses almost nothing (four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer, T1); a second CLIP-family encoder collapsing at Int4 under quantization-aware distillation on non-RGB edge sensing (56.3% vs. 67.9% RGB accuracy on EuroSAT, edge-VLM QAT, T2); and, most recently, the first monotonic curve rather than a two-point comparison — MiX (T1) tracks Qwen2.5-VL's accuracy gap against FP16 shrinking 8.0 → 4.1 → 2.4 → 2.3 points as the backbone scales 3B → 72B. Five agreements past the point of coincidence; still missing is the controlled test this map has asked for since 2026-08-01 — one model family, four sizes, one quantizer, one benchmark suite, so the effect size is comparable across the curve, not just the direction.

The contest this map is named for: which modality is the fragile one, and under what regime

This map went contested on 2026-08-28, and the tension is still live.

The incumbent rule, as carried since the fork: vision at 4 bits, text at 8 — the CLIP-encoder result above. The inversion: rfq-activation-outliers (T1) runs vision and language pathways in identical numerical formats under quantization-aware training on two multimodal models, and finds the vision pathway the fragile one on both — the reverse ordering, stated by the authors themselves against prior weight-only PTQ work. Three things are uncontrolled between the two results at once: PTQ vs. QAT (the authors' own proposed boundary), weight-only vs. activation-inclusive, and a contrastive discriminative encoder vs. a generative model. State both sides rather than picking one: the allocation framing survives (precision should be allocated by modality), the allocation direction does not (which modality gets the cheap bits is regime-dependent, not a universal rule).

The same source supplies this map's most transferable number, and it does not depend on which side of the contest is right: at four bits, the activations cost roughly nine times what the weights do. W4A8 costs 0.7–0.8% of training loss against a BF16 baseline; W4A4 costs 7.1–7.2% — same weight precision, an order of magnitude more damage from touching activations. MXFP8 is near-lossless (0.2–0.3%). A second rider: format outweighs bit-width at the same nominal width — HiF4's hierarchical scaling costs 2.7–3.8% where MXFP4's shared block exponent costs 7.1–7.2%, both nominally four bits. This is the closest thing this vault has to a measured justification for the weight-only regime its readers actually run: the cheapness of "4-bit" is a property of leaving activations alone, not of the bit count itself, and it is now priced at roughly a tenth of what touching activations costs.

What resolves the contest is nobody's to run yet. Every number on both sides of the modality-direction dispute costs a multi-billion-token quantization-aware fine-tune on a 5B–30B model, in formats (MXFP8, HiF4, MXFP4) consumer hardware does not support. Nobody can check either side on the hardware this map is written for.

A newer quantization method sidesteps the contest rather than settling it. SPHQuant (T1) decomposes weight vectors into sign, radius and direction rather than quantizing scalars directly, spending extra precision on the radius (where outlier magnitude concentrates) and a small shared codebook on direction. Applied uniformly across the model — not allocated per modality — it roughly matches the strongest prior vector-quantization baseline, QTIP, on three VLMs at 2–3 bits (a wash at 2-bit, a 1.3-point edge on one model at 3-bit) while decoding 30.3% faster via a codebook-friendly fused kernel (132.96 vs. 102.05 tok/s, RTX A6000). It neither confirms nor contests the modality-direction dispute; it is evidence that the outlier-isolation instinct behind this map's allocation arguments (spread or relocate outlier magnitude rather than accept its cost everywhere) keeps paying off when applied at a finer grain, inside a single weight vector instead of across a model.

Product & infrastructure: a VLM finally fits on a phone, and the artifact that proves it won't say at what precision

This map's founding open question — "does anyone ship a real number for a VLM at a stated precision on one card?" — is answered from below, not from the shipped-product side that first tried to answer it. Llama-Mobile (T1, Graphcore Research + Arm, full pipeline and Arm CPU inference implementation released) quantizes Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct to 3.73 GB at 2.68 bits per weight, INT8 activations, 0.083 average degradation across VQAv2/ChartQA/DocVQA/AI2D — decoding at a median 3.8 tok/s on a Pixel 8a and 36.8 tok/s on a Graviton4 (vs. INT8's 26.4). The honest comparison at the phone is not "2.7 bits vs. 8 bits" — at INT8 the 11B model does not fit in memory at all, so the alternative to aggressive quantization here is nothing running, not something worse running. Below three bits, procedure and format both outweigh bit-width: a rate-matched integer grid under GPTQ scores 0.018 at ~2.7 bits; the same rate under a vector format with quantization-aware training scores 0.661 — a 37× spread at one fixed bit budget. And the prefill/decode split this map inherited from local-inference replicates on Arm hardware at 2.68 bits: the aggressive format wins at batch-size-1 decode and loses slightly at compute-bound prefill, exactly the shape that map's local-inference findings predict.

A shipped 3.1B generative VLM answers most of the "does this run on a handset" question and leaves its deciding column blank. LFM2.5-VL-3B (T2, Liquid AI vendor post + model card) — a SigLIP2 NaFlex 400M vision encoder on a 2.6B text backbone, 32K context — fits in about 3 GB and decodes at 228 tok/s on an M5 Max, 116 on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395, 20 on a Galaxy S26 Ultra, with GGUF/ONNX/MLX-8bit checkpoints published day one. Twenty tokens per second is above reading speed — the first entry on this map saying a VLM on a handset is usable, not merely demonstrable. What is missing is the column this whole map is organized around: no precision is stated for any throughput or footprint number, and the four shipped precisions ship with zero quality-versus-precision comparison anywhere — the exact silence four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer says not to trust, given a 2B VLM elsewhere in this map lost fifteen AUROC points at 4-bit while its accuracy barely moved. The capability story reads as narrowing the job, not compressing a generalist: near-parity with a 4B baseline on grounding and screen tasks, flat on tool use, and the vendor states plainly the model "answers directly instead of reasoning" and is not recommended for long-context or reasoning-intensive work.

What makes local VLM use feel broken is the vision tower, and the fix is a cache, not a smaller model. On an Apple M4 Max, a multi-turn conversation about one 1024×1024 image on Qwen3-VL-8B costs 21.7 s on the first turn and 0.78 s by the third — 28× — and the ablation locates nearly all of it in caching the vision embeddings (7.8× on its own; the text KV cache adds only 1.2×) (vLLM MLX, T1, vendor-authored). Resolution is the cost dial on both axes: 224×224 gives 6.7× at 48 MB of cache, 1024×1024 gives 13.1× at 156 MB, and video is far worse (12.1 GB for 64 frames) — a local memory bill nobody has stated for the native-resolution direction the parent map credits recent VLMs with. The mechanism is mundane and portable (identity via SHA-256 over decoded pixels, not the URL), and it composes with the datacenter-scale version of the same idea: SGLang's RadixAttention (T1) keys a paged KV pool on the hash of the input image, turning a variable per-image token count into an ordinary cache-hit problem — 6.4× on LLaVA-v1.5-7B, a 52.4% production hit rate on Chatbot Arena. Scope both hard: one machine at 128 GB (roughly 4× a typical enthusiast's memory) for the MLX result, and all-datacenter-GPU for the serving result — neither has been run on the hardware this map is actually about.

What to take from this, stated plainly

Settled enough to act on: allocate a VLM's quantization budget by modality rather than setting one global precision (though which modality gets the cheap bits depends on your regime — see below); cache vision embeddings before you reach for a smaller model, since the tower, not the language model, is what makes local multi-turn VLM use feel slow; expect small VLMs and their encoders to degrade faster than large ones under compression, and budget accordingly. Still contested: whether vision or language is the fragile pathway under aggressive quantization — the answer flips between weight-only PTQ of a contrastive encoder and activation-inclusive QAT of a generative model, and nobody has run the controlled experiment that would decide it on hardware a practitioner owns. Still missing: a single VLM, one quantizer, four-plus sizes, one benchmark suite, to turn "small models quantize worse" from a five-way directional agreement into a measured curve; and the actual quality cost of the precisions Liquid AI shipped LFM2.5-VL-3B at, which is a one-afternoon experiment on hardware anyone reading this owns.

Open questions

  • Does the vision-embedding cache compose with anything? Caching spends storage to delete recomputation, which should be orthogonal to 4-bit weights, KV quantization, or speculative decoding on this map's own logic — but nobody has run it alongside any of them, and the one number this map has comes from an unquantized 8B model on a 128 GB machine.
  • What does llama.cpp actually do with a vision tower? The Llama-Mobile Arm pipeline and the LFM2.5-VL GGUF release both partially answer this empirically (there is a build, it runs), but whether the tower is quantized on the same ladder as the language model, whether embeddings are cached across turns, and what happens to native-resolution patch counts remain undocumented for the format most of this vault's readers use day to day.
  • The one experiment that would decide the modality-direction contest is still unrun: hold everything else fixed (same model, same PTQ-vs-QAT setting, weight-only) and vary only which modality gets the deeper cut, on hardware that fits in 24 GB.
