Pick by your data, not your loss: the state of post-training preference tuning

Synthesized 2026-07-21, refreshed 2026-08-19 from post-training-rl (status: consolidating since 2026-07-21, held deliberately twice since). Seven dated entries landed on the map in the four weeks after the first draft and three of them changed what this article should say. The original argued that the preference losses are one family and you pick by data shape; that survives. What it did not say, because the evidence had not arrived, is that three things you decide before you ever choose a loss now look like bigger levers than the choice itself — the fluency of the checkpoint you start from, the prompts you train on, and whether your adapter is actually receiving gradients. There is also a proof that the knob everyone tunes does not do what they think. New material is marked (new 2026-08). Every method below that isn't checker-verified is scored by GPT-4-family judges; read the numbers through evaluation-contamination. This field's defaults rot in months — check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

Post-training a base model into something useful runs on two reward sources, and they are not interchangeable:

  • Preference data → a classification loss. The KL-constrained RLHF objective has a closed-form optimum, and DPO (Rafailov et al., Stanford, NeurIPS 2023) reparameterizes it so preference pairs train with a single classification loss — no reward model, no sampling loop — matching or beating PPO-RLHF on the tasks tested. Alignment became a single-GPU operation. [T1]
  • A verifiable checker → pure RL. DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, Nature 2025, weights MIT) elicits reasoning with outcome-only rewards and no human traces — AIME 2024 pass@1 15.6% → 71.0% — but only reaches where an automatic checker exists. [T1]

The year since DPO has been successive deletion on the preference side, and it has a name. ORPO (Hong et al., KAIST, EMNLP 2024) deleted the reference model and the SFT/preference stage boundary, folding a small odds-ratio penalty into the SFT loss. SimPO (Meng et al., Princeton/UVA, NeurIPS 2024) deleted the reference model too, on a different argument — train the metric you actually decode with (length-normalized average log-prob), +6.4 over DPO on AlpacaEval 2. KTO (Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2024) deletes a fourth thing — the pairing constraint on the data — and supplies the account that unifies the rest: HALOs (Human-Aware Losses). Every one of these methods scores a completion by the value of its reward relative to a reference point, through a loss-averse value function borrowed from prospect theory. DPO, PPO, ORPO, SimPO, KTO are one object with different value functions. That framing — not any single new leaderboard row — is what moved the map to consolidating. [T1]

(new 2026-08) The deletion trajectory has since been confirmed from the other thread. DeepSeek-V3 (DeepSeek-AI, T1) runs GRPO, which drops the critic entirely and estimates the baseline from the spread of a sampled group. DPO deleted the reward model; ORPO deleted the reference model; GRPO deletes the critic. Stated as one claim across both threads: post-training is shedding every model that has to be resident during training. That is a memory argument as much as a method argument, and it is why these techniques keep landing on one or two cards. [T2 — vendor technical report, self-baselined]

Before you pick a loss

(new 2026-08) This section did not exist in the first draft and now leads the practical half, because three of the four weeks' arrivals are about the pipeline rather than the objective — and each of them can dominate the difference between any two losses in the section below.

  • Measure your SFT checkpoint's perplexity on the target corpus first. Above about 20, preference optimization does nothing for you. Across fifteen (model, corpus) configurations at 70M–500M, every run above PPL_SFT ≈ 20 shows negligible or negative gain and every gain above +0.2 sits below it — and the boundary is not parameter count: Pythia-410M improves markedly on TinyStories (win rate 59.9%) and regresses significantly on Wikitext-103 (39.9%) while a smaller SmolLM2-360M improves on both. The mechanism is that a fluent prior keeps rollouts inside the reward model's training distribution and a weak one produces a gradient indistinguishable from noise. This is a rare legitimate use of perplexity by this vault's standards — it predicts whether a training stage will work, rather than ranking finished models. Cost: minutes on one card. [T1, one study, 70M–500M] (rl-post-training-needs-a-fluent-prior-not-more-parameters)
  • Curate the prompts, not just the preferences. Holding the student (Qwen3-4B-Base), the algorithm (GRPO under rule-based verifiable rewards) and the evaluation fixed, and changing only which prompts the run consumes, a four-benchmark reasoning average moves from 44.6 (human-labelled s1k with R1 targets) and 47.5 (OpenMathReasoning) to 57.2 — with +4.2 of that gain coming from filtering the generated prompt pool while making it smaller (CoT-Self-Instruct, FAIR at Meta / NYU, T1). That is a larger swing than any loss-versus-loss margin in this article. It is also a warning about the whole comparison class: two methods evaluated on differently-curated prompt sets are not being compared. See data-curation and filtering-a-generated-corpus-outperforms-generating-more-of-it. [T1]
  • Verify your adapter is trainable before you trust a null result. In PPO loops assembled from PEFT and TRL, adapter parameters can be left silently non-trainable — the loss computes, rollouts generate, and the policy never moves. Fix: merge the SFT adapter into the base and attach a fresh zero-initialized one. A published "the adapter didn't help" on that pipeline is indistinguishable from a correct null. [T1] (small-scale-ppo-fails-three-ways-before-it-fails-at-learning)
  • Use float32 for the log-probability difference in any ratio-based objective. ρ_t = exp(log π_θ − log π_SFT) is catastrophic cancellation on bfloat16's 7-bit mantissa, producing ratios above 10⁶ and hardware exceptions in the first steps below 200M parameters. The paper scopes this to small models; the mechanism does not — it is a property of the computation, and what makes it bite below 200M is how fast a small policy leaves its reference. PPO, GRPO and every clipped-ratio variant perform the same subtraction, nobody has instrumented it at 7B, and bf16 is the assumed default in every recipe in this vault. [T1 at 70M–500M; unscoped above]
  • Budget accordingly. DeepSeek-V3 spent 5K of 2,788K GPU-hours on post-training — 0.2% of the bill. The expensive decision was made before this stage started.

What to do

  • Have preference pairs and want the cheap default? DPO — with the caveat below about β. It's operationally SFT-class, it's in TRL as DPOTrainer, and its reference model is a leash to the base you may want. [T1]
  • Only have thumbs-up / thumbs-down, or a badly imbalanced good:bad ratio? KTO. It aligns from an unpaired binary signal and matches DPO from 1B to 30B — and a deployed product's logs are already KTO data, no pairing step. Pairwise methods have nothing to pair when the classes are imbalanced; KTO reweights instead. [T1]
  • Want to drop the reference-model copy (half the resident memory, one fewer forward pass per step)? ORPO or SimPO both do it — SimPO if length exploitation is your failure mode, since length-normalization is its whole point. But hold the margin claims loosely (below). [T1]
  • Need reasoning and have a checker? That's the other reward source — pure RL (R1-style), which no amount of preference tuning substitutes for. Its product then compresses into SFT-able traces (distillation-small-models). [T1]
  • (new 2026-08) Split your reward by verifiability rather than picking one. DeepSeek-V3's production recipe uses a rule-based reward wherever a rule exists — answers in a box, LeetCode compilers, anything "resistant to manipulation" — and falls back to a learned reward model only for free-form answers. That reward model is trained from the policy's own SFT checkpoints, and the chain-of-thought leading to the reward is included in the preference data as an explicit anti-reward-hacking measure. This is the closest thing in-vault to an answer on reward-model failure, and it comes from a practitioner rather than a study. [T2]
  • (new 2026-08) Consider spending the first dollar on curation instead. LIMA (Meta AI et al., T1) takes LLaMa 65B, 1,000 curated supervised examples, no RL and no reward model, and is equal-or-preferred to RLHF-trained DaVinci003 in 65% of 300 human head-to-heads. It loses clearly to Claude (54%) and GPT-4 (57%), both RLHF-trained, and DaVinci003 is 2022-vintage. So the honest synthesis is a bound, not a refutation: the first large fraction of alignment is available from a small, well-curated supervised set, and preference optimization buys the remainder. (alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition) [T1, judged by 2023-vintage crowd workers with an acknowledged shared-prior leak between prompt authors and training-set authors]
  • The meta-rule: these are one loss family, so choose by the shape of the data you actually have (pairs vs binary, balanced vs skewed, checkable vs subjective), not by which loss has the glossiest paper. No HALO is universally superior — the authors say so themselves. [T1]
  • In open-weights practice all of this usually runs through LoRA adapters on a quantized base (peft-methods). As of 2026-08 there is still no in-vault evidence on a DPO or GRPO run through a LoRA adapter on a 4-bit base at 7B–8B against a full-precision control; what arrived instead was the defect list above. Treat that stack as unvalidated, and check the adapter is receiving gradients.

The knob that isn't a knob

(new 2026-08, and it is the sharpest single change to this article.) IPO / ΨPO (Azar, Rowland, Piot et al., Google DeepMind, AISTATS 2024, T1) proves something about DPO that no benchmark could see.

RLHF and DPO are the same point of a general objective max_π E[Ψ(p*(y ≻ y'))] − τ·D_KL(π ‖ π_ref), with Ψ(q) = log(q/(1−q)) — the Bradley-Terry logit. That transform is unbounded, so when an empirical preference probability reaches 1 the implied reward gap diverges and the optimal policy zeroes the dispreferred action for every value of τ. The KL term is not weakened by determinism; it is overridden.

The finite-sample version is what bites. A pair whose true preference is 0.8 but which was sampled three times the same way has p̂ = 1, and in a large action space with a small dataset — which is every real preference dataset — that is the modal case, not a corner case.

Two consequences to carry:

  • β is not a smooth dial from "stay near the reference" to "follow the data." Where your data is deterministic it is inert, and the early stopping the field treats as hygiene is performing the regularization the objective was supposed to provide.
  • It inverts the founding move of this whole thread. Under {0,1} preferences the optimal reward is infinite, so a fitted reward model underfits — and that underfitting was a regularizer DPO discarded along with the reward-modelling stage. Classical RLHF is more robust here precisely because its intermediate stage is bad at its job.

The fix is to bound Ψ: at Ψ = Identity (IPO) the loss becomes a regression of the log-likelihood-ratio gap onto a finite target.

Hold the scope honestly. This is proved, and demonstrated only on three-action bandits — there is no language model in the paper. It establishes a property of the objective, not a failure at the scale you work at. It is also, for the same reason, the only source in this vault's entire preference thread carrying zero judge risk, and the only one with zero language-model evidence. Freedom from the biased instrument was bought by proving things about bandits; hold both facts rather than treating a proof as strictly stronger. (bradley-terry-substitution-is-what-makes-dpo-overfit, bounding-the-preference-transform-restores-kl-regularization)

The practical reading, until someone runs this at 7B: if your DPO run needs aggressive early stopping to stay good, that is the mechanism, not your dataset.

Two frameworks, no reconciliation

(new 2026-08.) The zoo is now covered twice, by two groups who do not cite each other and slice along different axes.

  • HALOs (KTO) classify a loss by the implied human value function — what quirk of human judgment it encodes. Descriptive.
  • ΨPO (IPO) classifies a loss by which transform is applied to the preference probability — what the objective does to your policy under sparse data. Structural.

Two independent unifying accounts is a stronger framework layer than one, and it is the layer this map consolidated on. But nothing reconciles them, and a practitioner choosing a method needs both answers about the same method: what human quirk am I encoding, and what happens to my policy when a preference pair is unanimous? That is now the thread's sharpest theoretical gap.

Note also that the prospect-theory grounding under HALOs is a motivating analogy, not a fit — KTO's value-function constants are hyperparameters, not measured human parameters.

The one comparison with no judge in it

(new 2026-08.) The structural weakness of this article is that nearly every preference-side number is GPT-4-judged. One result compares the two threads with no annotator and no judge, because its metric is exact-match arithmetic.

GSM-Symbolic (Apple / Washington State University, T1) appends one grammatical, on-topic, irrelevant clause to a math word problem. Instruction-tuned open models lose 57 to 66 points; frontier chat models lose 32 to 40; reasoning-trained models lose 17.5 and 29.1. On a graded-difficulty ladder the same split appears — two added clauses take a 9B instruction-tuned model from 84.4 to 41.8 while a reasoning-trained model goes 94.9 to 89.1.

So whatever pure RL installs, it roughly halves distractor sensitivity relative to models of the same era trained by preference methods, measured on an instrument with no bias to argue about. It favours thread (2), and it is the first cross-thread comparison in-vault free of the instrument problem.

Two limits, stated with it. It does not eliminate the failure — a model sold on reasoning still loses ~18 points to a sentence that changes nothing. And the comparison is across model families, not an ablation, so scale, data and training recipe all vary alongside the RL. The clean experiment is one base model with and without the RL stage, and it does not exist in-vault. (an-irrelevant-clause-collapses-arithmetic-accuracy)

Not settled

  • The instrument is still the thread's structural weakness. Every preference-side win here except the two checker-verified results is ranked by a GPT-4-family judge, and judge bias is measured and directional — verbosity, position, self-enhancement (GPT-4 favors its own family by ~10% win rate), and these do not average out (llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias). SimPO's headline is length normalization measured on length-biased judges. Judge/human agreement does exceed 80%, so the instrument is noisy-and-biased, not worthless — but a fine-tune's "win" counts only on an instrument you've checked. (new 2026-08) The bias is now also visible being patched in the wild: the CoT-Self-Instruct authors report that synthetic instructions produce longer responses, that DPO under a length-favouring judge therefore risks length explosion, and that they apply a length-normalized reward with a hand-chosen coefficient of 0.2 to contain it.
  • No neutral bake-off, and the bar for one just went up. ORPO, SimPO, and KTO are each their authors' own evaluation of their own method. ORPO's apples-to-apples margin over DPO is a loss at 125M, a tie at 350M, a win at 1.3B — credible direction, unestablished size. The single most valuable missing source is a third-party head-to-head of the reference-free family at matched labeling budget (a thumbs-up is cheaper than a ranked pair, but you may need more of them — unmeasured). (new 2026-08) And it now has to hold the prompt distribution fixed too, since varying only that moved a reasoning average by 12.6 points — more than any loss-vs-loss margin claimed here.
  • Reference-free methods cut the anchor to the base, and nobody in-vault measures the cost. DPO's reference policy is a leash; ORPO and SimPO drop it. Off-distribution degradation is unmeasured, and it rhymes with the forgetting result in peft-methods (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting) — what a cheap update quietly costs elsewhere is the question this vault keeps finding under the headline.
  • Does the β failure bite at LLM scale? The derivation is airtight and the demonstration is a bandit. Nobody has run it at 7B on a real preference set: how much of DPO's known appetite for early stopping is this mechanism, how β should be set given that it is inert in the deterministic regime, and whether IPO's squared loss trains stably at that scale. The authors close by asking for exactly these experiments.
  • Does the bfloat16 importance-ratio failure exist at 7B? Cheap to answer — instrument ρ_t for overflow during one run — and it would either close the question or reveal a silent numerical failure in common practice.
  • The reward-modeling branch is still the named gap, and IPO made it more interesting: if a fitted reward model's underfitting is load-bearing regularization rather than a defect, the branch DPO deleted may have been doing useful work. DPO-vs-PPO-at-scale remains unsourced.
  • SFT itself has no dedicated in-vault source, which is awkward for an article whose new lead section says the SFT checkpoint's quality gates everything downstream.
