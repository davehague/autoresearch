Pick by your data, not your loss: the state of post-training preference tuning

Synthesized 2026-07-21 from post-training-rl (status: consolidating as of this run — moved emerging → consolidating when KTO supplied a mechanism-level account of the whole preference family, not another benchmark win). Every method below that isn't checker-verified is scored by GPT-4-family judges; read the numbers through evaluation-contamination. This field's defaults rot in months — check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

Post-training a base model into something useful runs on two reward sources, and they are not interchangeable:

  • Preference data → a classification loss. The KL-constrained RLHF objective has a closed-form optimum, and DPO (Rafailov et al., Stanford, NeurIPS 2023) reparameterizes it so preference pairs train with a single classification loss — no reward model, no sampling loop — matching or beating PPO-RLHF on the tasks tested. Alignment became a single-GPU operation. [T1]
  • A verifiable checker → pure RL. DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, Nature 2025, weights MIT) elicits reasoning with outcome-only rewards and no human traces — AIME 2024 pass@1 15.6% → 71.0% — but only reaches where an automatic checker exists. [T1]

The year since DPO has been successive deletion on the preference side, and as of this run it has a name. ORPO (Hong et al., KAIST, EMNLP 2024) deleted the reference model and the SFT/preference stage boundary, folding a small odds-ratio penalty into the SFT loss. SimPO (Meng et al., Princeton/UVA, NeurIPS 2024) deleted the reference model too, on a different argument — train the metric you actually decode with (length-normalized average log-prob), +6.4 over DPO on AlpacaEval 2. KTO (Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2024) deletes a fourth thing — the pairing constraint on the data — and supplies the account that unifies the rest: HALOs (Human-Aware Losses). Every one of these methods scores a completion by the value of its reward relative to a reference point, through a loss-averse value function borrowed from prospect theory. DPO, PPO, ORPO, SimPO, KTO are one object with different value functions. That framing — not any single new leaderboard row — is what moved the map to consolidating. [T1]

What to do

  • Have preference pairs and want the cheap default? DPO. It's operationally SFT-class, it's in TRL as DPOTrainer, and its reference model is a leash to the base you may want. [T1]
  • Only have thumbs-up / thumbs-down, or a badly imbalanced good:bad ratio? KTO. It aligns from an unpaired binary signal and matches DPO from 1B to 30B — and a deployed product's logs are already KTO data, no pairing step. Pairwise methods have nothing to pair when the classes are imbalanced; KTO reweights instead. [T1]
  • Want to drop the reference-model copy (half the resident memory, one fewer forward pass per step)? ORPO or SimPO both do it — SimPO if length exploitation is your failure mode, since length-normalization is its whole point. But hold the margin claims loosely (below). [T1]
  • Need reasoning and have a checker? That's the other reward source — pure RL (R1-style), which no amount of preference tuning substitutes for. Its product then compresses into SFT-able traces (distillation-small-models). [T1]
  • The meta-rule: these are one loss family, so choose by the shape of the data you actually have (pairs vs binary, balanced vs skewed, checkable vs subjective), not by which loss has the glossiest paper. No HALO is universally superior — the authors say so themselves. [T1]
  • In open-weights practice all of this usually runs through LoRA adapters on a quantized base (peft-methods); there is no graded in-vault evidence yet on how preference tuning composes with 4-bit PEFT, so treat that stack as unvalidated.

Not settled

  • The instrument is the map's structural weakness. Every preference-side win here except R1's checker-verified reasoning is ranked by a GPT-4-family judge, and judge bias is measured and directional — verbosity, position, self-enhancement (GPT-4 favors its own family by ~10% win rate), and these do not average out (llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias). SimPO's headline is length normalization measured on length-biased judges. Judge/human agreement does exceed 80%, so the instrument is noisy-and-biased, not worthless — but a fine-tune's "win" counts only on an instrument you've checked.
  • No neutral bake-off. ORPO, SimPO, and KTO are each their authors' own evaluation of their own method. ORPO's apples-to-apples margin over DPO is a loss at 125M, a tie at 350M, a win at 1.3B — credible direction, unestablished size. The single most valuable missing source is a third-party head-to-head of the reference-free family at matched labeling budget (a thumbs-up is cheaper than a ranked pair, but you may need more of them — unmeasured).
  • Reference-free methods cut the anchor to the base, and nobody in-vault measures the cost. DPO's reference policy is a leash; ORPO and SimPO drop it. Off-distribution degradation is unmeasured, and it rhymes with the forgetting result in peft-methods (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting) — what a cheap update quietly costs elsewhere is the question this vault keeps finding under the headline.
  • The prospect-theory grounding is a motivating analogy, not a fit — KTO's value-function constants are hyperparameters, not measured human parameters. And IPO, the reward-modeling branch (a learned reward model is a different object from an eval judge), and a neutral DPO-vs-PPO-at-scale comparison remain unsourced.
