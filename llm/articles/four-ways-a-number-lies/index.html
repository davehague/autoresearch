Seven ways a benchmark number lies: the state of honest evaluation

Refreshed 2026-08-07 from evaluation-contamination (status: consolidating). Twelve dated bullets landed on the map since the last refresh on 2026-07-27, and the count in the title moved — this piece was called "four ways" when it was written on 2026-07-20 and the checklist has grown by three. The candidate fifth mode was adopted: recipe leakage has been replicated twice and now carries a scope condition. Two more items arrived that are not properties of the evaluation's inputs at all — one about whether a clean benchmark predicts anything, one about the arithmetic that turns a suite into a headline. Mode one gained a measured dose. Mode two gained its sharpest instrument and its most expensive one. As of 2026-08 the modes are stable and the magnitudes are not — the judge-bias numbers are still 2023-vintage. Check the map's through-line before quoting percentages. The slug is unchanged.

The claim

This vault's premise is a comparison: open weights, plus your data and your compute, beating a frontier model on a specific task. That premise is a benchmark delta, so it is only as good as the measurement behind it.

The vault can now name seven independent ways that measurement fails. Independent is the operative word — clearing one says nothing about the rest, and the modal case here carries several at once. The first five are properties of how the number was produced. The last two are properties of what the number means once it is correct, which is a category this article did not have a year of vault-time ago.

The growth is itself the finding. Every refresh of this piece has added an item and removed none.

1. Leakage — the model already saw the items

The mode everyone knows about, the one whose standard defense does not work, and — as of 2026-07-30 — the one where the dose is finally measured.

The failure is real and measurable. GSM1k (Scale AI, NeurIPS 2024) rebuilt GSM8k from zero, difficulty-matched, and re-scored the field: several model families dropped up to 8%, and the drop correlated (Spearman's r² = 0.36) with how readily the model reproduced the original items verbatim. Contamination is unevenly distributed — frontier models mostly generalized to the novel items — so per-family checking is the honest posture. [T1]

The standard defense detects verbatim copies and nothing else. Essentially every corpus builder decontaminates by n-gram overlap — strip any training document sharing a 13-gram (GPT-3's convention) or a 50-character span (GPT-4's) with a test item. Rephrased samples (Yang, Chiang, Zheng, Gonzalez and Stoica) measured that defense against paraphrase and translation. It scores an F1 of exactly 0.000 — every subject, every language, against a random baseline of 0.500 — while scoring a perfect 1.000 on unrephrased items. It is not degraded. It has no signal on a reworded sentence. [T1]

The consequence is demonstrated rather than argued. Fine-tune Llama-2 13B on rephrased test items and MMLU goes 54.8 → 89.9 (GPT-4 scores 86.4); CodeLlama-13B takes HumanEval 36.0 → 81.1. Every one of those models passes decontamination clean. Pointing a stronger detector at corpora people actually train on returns, as a share of HumanEval: The Stack 18.9%, StarCoder-Data 15.9%, CodeAlpaca 12.8%, RedPajama-Data-1T 8.53%. The most instructive case is synthetic — CodeAlpaca's 12.8% was generated, by an LLM asked for instruction-following examples from seeds, because that is what "produce problems similar to these" does.

One copy is enough, and that is new. The dose question sat open on this map for two weeks: a corpus percentage cannot be converted into a discount on a published score without a response curve. Test-set contamination in generative evaluation (2026-07-30) fits one, sweeping replica counts {0, 1, 3, 10, 32, 100, 316, 1000, 3162}. The irreducible-error term falls from 3.594 clean to 0.0347 at 316 replicas, and a single replica already puts almost every model below the loss that infinite clean compute would asymptote to. So the curve saturates at the left edge: there is no safe low dose. [T1] Two discounts to apply: the sweep is run at 344M parameters, two orders of magnitude below anything this vault runs, and the memorization threshold falls with scale (32 → 10 → 1 replicas across 34M → 93M) — which points the alarming way for extrapolation, and the authors decline to extrapolate.

There are now three detector families, with three different failure modes. Min-K% Prob scores a passage by its lowest-probability tokens and recovers pretraining membership at AUC 0.72 from the checkpoint alone — a prior, not a verdict. N-gram overlap is available to everyone and scores F1 0.000 against paraphrase. GSM-Symbolic templates a benchmark and regenerates it, reading where the published score falls in its own distribution — up to 9.2 points of gap on GSM8K, though that gap is confounded with brittleness and nothing separates the two halves. A fourth screen is cheaper than all of them and nobody has placed it in a procedure: temperature sensitivity, two sampling runs, no corpus and no log-probs.

The two strong tools do not compose, and the gap is where you are standing. The reliable rephrasing detector needs the training corpus — available to a corpus builder and to nobody else. Min-K% Prob needs only the model, at the weaker number. So a model builder can use the strong tool and a model consumer cannot, and almost everyone fine-tuning open weights is a consumer auditing a corpus they cannot see, against a contamination class the builder's own pipeline probably missed.

Do: score on freshly-authored, held-out, or post-cutoff items. Screen with Min-K% Prob where you cannot. Treat "we decontaminated" as uninformative unless it says how. Open: the dose curve exists at 344M on verbatim insertions; the one you need is at 1B–7B on paraphrased ones.

2. Construct mismatch — the score is honest and still wrong

A number can be uncontaminated and measure a narrower surface than the claim it is quoted for. LoRA vs full fine-tuning found the two methods posting equal in-domain accuracy while having learned structurally different solutions, with the adapter generalizing worse out of distribution. Nothing about the score was dishonest. [T1]

This is the mode that has grown most, because five unrelated efficiency literatures have now walked into it independently.

Perplexity has failed in five of them. YaRN evidences a 128k context with perplexity at 131,072 tokens, which establishes the model is not confused and says nothing about retrieval across the window. A 13-format llama.cpp sweep found four 5-bit quantization formats spanning 0.03 perplexity and 3.4 GSM8K points. LIMA's authors abandoned perplexity-based early stopping. rsLoRA splits on it — a theorem-backed training-health claim (valid) and a model-selection claim resting on fine-tuning perplexity (discounted). And GEMQ swung GSM8K 31.77 → 42.30 on a calibration-corpus change while WikiText2 perplexity moved 0.15. The rule: perplexity is a training-health signal, not a model-selection criterion. [T1 × 5]

The sharpest instrument on this map is now an evidence check, and it is expensive. Does accuracy equal evidence? (UIUC, 2026-08-06) holds a reasoning trace byte-identical across eleven KV-cache compression methods on Qwen3-8B and asks whether the compressed cache still supports the answer it preserved. On AIME26, the Spearman correlation between a method's accuracy rank and its judged chain-validity rank is −0.95 — the leaderboard that produced that literature is inverted on the hardest task measured. Restricted to matched-correct answers, the derivation is invalid in 47.9–64.8% of cases for the token-eviction family against 4.2% for a 2-bit quantization control, with answer-first rationalization rising 58% → 94%. The general rule travels well past caches: a collapse proves damage; a preserved score proves nothing — unless the task removes the answer-recovery shortcut, which long-context retrieval does, and where accuracy duly collapses honestly. [T1]

And there is no cheap version of that check. Four probes over 30,000 judged chains (silent failures, 2026-08-02) top out at F1 0.530 against a stated 0.65 viability bar, and BERT embeddings (0.416) lose to TF-IDF, because the distinction is structural rather than semantic. Two human annotators reproduce that difficulty exactly: all 21 of 21 disagreements over 300 samples fall on that one boundary. This is the first item on the checklist whose honest instrument is a large judge on every output, with nothing to distil it into.

The same perturbation-preserves-the-score pattern is now six deep. Weight quantization (accuracy moves ≤3.1 points while the composition of wrong answers shifts sharply on 3B models), cross-model KV cache transfer (GSM8K retention 1.6% while HellaSwag holds above 58%), KV eviction, stated confidence (4-bit costs 15 points of error-detection AUROC at 1.6 points of accuracy), perplexity itself — and, new on 2026-08-07, approximate speculative decoding, where two decoding configurations produce over 95% hash-divergent output on GSM8K and MATH-500 with no measured accuracy change. Five different parts of the stack; one instrument failing the same way. The victim is almost always multi-step derivation, which is now a screening rule rather than a coincidence.

Do: ask what surface the metric covers before repeating the headline. On any reasoning claim from an efficiency paper, read a preserved score as no evidence rather than as evidence of no damage.

3. Infrastructure non-equivalence — same score, different computation

The quietest one, and it reads as a ladder — which makes it directly actionable, because it tells you which memory-saving levers to spend first.

Exact. Gradient checkpointing gives "equivalent weight gradient for training and can always be safely applied." It changes when a value is computed, never what it is. It never belongs in a results footnote, and it is the memory lever to reach for before any other. [T1]

Silently inexact. PyTorch FSDP (Meta, VLDB 2023) states plainly that sharded training "cannot ensure… the same mathematical equivalence as local training" for the optimizer step: the optimizer works over flattened, evenly-chunked parameters whose layout ignores parameter boundaries, so anything needing an unsharded value — weight or gradient norms, hence norm-based clipping — computes over the wrong object. No quality delta has ever been measured, which is why it belongs on this list. [T1]

Deliberately inexact. PipeDream's asynchronous pipelining is the worst case and the most honest about it: without weight stashing the update "is not a valid gradient of the loss function for any weight vector," and vertical sync — the mechanism that would restore data-parallel equivalence — ships off by default. [T1]

New rung, 2026-08-03, and it is the first with a practitioner-visible fix. Kimi K3 trains under quantization-aware training from SFT through the whole of RL, with rollout and gradient step sharing one quantization scheme, "eliminating the train–inference mismatch." State it as the general defect: an RL loop whose rollout policy is quantized and whose update is not is optimizing a policy it will never deploy, silently. [T1]

Do: rank your memory levers by exactness and spend the exact ones first. A table mixing a checkpointed run with an unsharded one is comparable; one mixing a ZeRO-3 run with local training is not, and neither run will say so.

4. Judge bias — the instrument has preferences

Judging LLM-as-a-Judge (LMSYS, NeurIPS 2023) cuts both ways, and the positive half matters as much as the negative.

The judge is legitimate. GPT-4's agreement with human preference exceeds 80% — the same rate humans agree with each other. That has a second group behind it (LIMA: crowd-versus-GPT-4 at 78–79% against crowd-versus-crowd at 82%) and, since 2026-08-06, a third and much tighter one: the KV-compression evidence check validated its judge against a human at 94.6% agreement, κ = 0.89, against a second judge family at κ = 0.88, and against a second human at κ = 0.92. That is the tightest judge validation in this vault, on a structural task rather than on open-ended chat. [T1 × 3]

The judge is also biased, in three named directions. Position bias, verbosity bias, and self-enhancement bias — GPT-4 favors its own family's outputs by ~10% win rate; Claude-v1 by ~25% — plus weak math and logic grading. These are systematic and directional, so sampling harder does not wash them out. Position bias is mitigable by swapping the order and requiring consistency; the other two are not cleanly fixable, and the paper says so. [T1]

The reach is the largest of any single source in this vault. Every method in the preference thread — DPO, ORPO, SimPO — is ranked by a GPT-4-family judge. SimPO is sharpest: its distinguishing mechanism is length normalization, and its headline win is scored on judges with a documented length preference.

Do: when a result is judge-ranked, check whether the judge shares a family with the model, the training data, or the benchmark author. Prefer verifiable checkers where they exist — this is why R1's AIME pass@1 is sturdier evidence than any judged suite average.

5. Recipe leakage — an undisclosed preparation step

Promoted from candidate to mode on 2026-08-02, and it now carries a scope condition. This article flagged it at n = 1 on 2026-07-27 and declined to adopt it. It has been replicated twice since, in two unrelated groups, and the effect got an order of magnitude larger.

All four modes above are properties of the evaluation. This one is a property of undisclosed model preparation: two people publish the same configuration, differ by double digits, and both are honest and reproducible, because a load-bearing step went unreported.

The ladder, on one intervention — hold model, quantizer and bit budget fixed, change only the calibration corpus:

  • AlphaQ, Mixtral-8×7B at 2.5 bits: HumanEval 29.3 / 13.7 / 25.1 across three corpora — a 2.1× swing — with no corrective rule (GitHub-Code scores worse on HumanEval than generic C4). [T1]
  • GEMQ (ICML 2026): GSM8K 31.77 → 42.30, with the competing method moving comparably — so this is a property of calibration-based quantization, not of one method — while perplexity moves 0.15. [T1]
  • ScaleQ-1.58 (Intel Labs China, 2026-08-06): Qwen3-4B at ternary, Math-500 0.00 → 58.40 and HumanEval+ 0.00 → 53.98, from generic web text to the model's own chain-of-thought traces. [T1]

And the scope condition is the useful part. The same intervention is worth +2.2 points at 4-bit weights, +58.8 at 2-bit and +58.4 at ternary. Recipe leakage is a low-bit hazard that grows as the format tightens, not a uniform one — so the four-bit defaults most readers run are comparatively robust, and the warning is specific.

Three unreported preparation steps are now on record across unrelated literatures: a quantization calibration corpus, an ablation's compute budget, and a baseline's learning rate. Do: treat a comparison without its preparation protocol as uninformative — and when you publish one, state the corpus, the budget and the step size.

Unresolved deliberately: whether this is a fifth mode or a meta-rule sitting above the other four. If it is a mode it should have its own detector, dose curve and positive exemplar like the others do. If it is a meta-rule, each of the four should have an undisclosed-preparation instance of its own — and three already do. The discriminating test is cheap and unrun: go looking for an undisclosed preparation step that swings a judge result. Judge temperature, system prompt and reference-answer formatting are unreported in most LLM-judge papers.

6. Criterion validity — the benchmark is clean and still predicts nothing

New 2026-08-05, and it lands on mode one from underneath. Leakage has been anchored since this article's first draft on a remedy as much as a diagnosis: rebuild the benchmark and watch the scores fall. Underneath sat an unexamined assumption — that reconstruction restores the instrument.

Commonsense benchmark predictive validity tests it on four benchmarks the field has already repaired (WinoGrande→WinoWhat and PIQA-RUP by paraphrase, GoldenSwag and Social IQA by filtering), across 23 open-weight models from six families at 1B–72B. The repaired versions preserve the originals' rankings and do not improve downstream predictive power — mean Fisher-z with downstream tasks: originals 1.565, reworked 0.982, Δ 0.584, 95% CI [0.326, 0.853]. Leakage and criterion validity are independent properties, and the standard remedy addresses one.

The validity itself is narrow and mislabelled. Under leave-one-family-out cross-validation, some tasks predict well (False Beliefs R² 0.483/0.695) while CEI and Sarcasm are negative for every model, and no positive correlation survives FDR correction for four of the tasks. "Transfer to particular tasks does not reliably follow the domains implied by benchmark labels" — a social benchmark does not preferentially predict social competence. Rankings were unstable to begin with: models move six to seven positions between benchmarks and top-three overlap never exceeds Jaccard 0.25. [T1, one study]

Scope it hard: commonsense benchmarks specifically, option-log-likelihood scoring rather than generation, and several downstream tasks small enough (n = 31, n = 60) to carry intervals the paper does not print. Nothing here licenses extending it to GSM8K, HumanEval or MMLU, which carry most of this vault's claims.

Do: nothing cheap, and that is the point. This is the item on the checklist a practitioner cannot clear at any budget — establishing criterion validity needs a downstream suite and a multi-family sweep, which is a research programme, not a check. What you can do is stop treating a rebuilt benchmark as a solved benchmark.

7. Aggregation — the summary operator is an undeclared level of optimism

New 2026-08-07, and it is the first defect on this list that is not in the inputs. Everything above is a property of what went into a score. This one survives clean, uncontaminated, correctly-scoped, honestly judged signals: aggregate them with the arithmetic mean and confidence can exceed the reliability of the weakest component.

Evaluation scores are perishable knowledge claims (GEM workshop at ACL 2026) re-ranks the public HELM boards under weakest-link aggregation instead of the mean:

  • HELM Capabilities (22 models, 5 benchmarks): Spearman 0.87, top-5 Jaccard 0.67, maximum displacement 8 positions.
  • HELM Lite (54 models, 10 scenarios): Spearman 0.89, maximum displacement 21 positions, top-5 Jaccard 0.000 — the two top-five sets are completely disjoint.

The rank correlation is the trap. It looks reassuring, and the disagreement is concentrated exactly where readers look. The worked case: Claude 3.5 Sonnet falls from rank 5 to rank 12 because a 0.28 Omni-MATH score is masked by strength elsewhere. And the effect grows with the number of dimensions averaged — more scenarios, more places for a weak one to hide.

What makes this a claim rather than a taste is the framing: the ordered weighted average family runs on a pessimism parameter where min is 1, max is 0, and the arithmetic mean is 0.5. Nobody argues for 0.5. It is the default. [T1 for the re-ranking]

This closes a question this article has carried since its first draft, and closes it worse than the question implied. The older instance still stands — Molmo-72B tops an 11-benchmark average at 81.2 while sitting 15 points below GPT-4o on MMMU — and so does Emu3's, where a 12-benchmark average of 62.1 against 61.8 for "surpasses LLaVA-1.6" is carried entirely by the five benchmarks the paper itself asterisks as "the images of related training datasets are observed during training," while it loses MMBench by 8.9 and MMMU by 3.5 on the unmarked ones. The authors disclosed the exposure honestly and the summary statistic erased the disclosure.

Do: when a claim rests on a suite average, ask for the minimum. It costs nothing, it is available in every published table, and it is the second independent discount you should be applying to a printed number — the first being that the number is one draw from a distribution nobody reports.

The paper's accompanying proposal — attach a formality tier, a scope declaration and an expiration date to every score — is argued rather than measured, and its constants should not be adopted wholesale (its 0.70 reliability ceiling for LLM-judge evidence is tighter than the κ = 0.89 validation above). The framing is worth adopting anyway: a benchmark result is a claim with a shelf life. This vault's own record agrees from three unrelated directions — an A100-tuned attention kernel reaching 35% of an H100's peak, a state-space-model study flagging every number as a claim about libraries in 2026-08, and DeepSeek-V3 asking hardware vendors to absorb the workaround its measurements were taken around.

What this adds up to

A pattern worth holding as a prior: where the eval is honest, the margin shrinks. ORPO looks weakest in its own apples-to-apples table. Molmo's human ratings and its benchmark ranking disagree, and it publishes the disagreement. Emu3 marks its own contaminated benchmarks and then wins on the average they carry. Five instances now, counting the nine-variant LoRA comparison that found every method within 1–2% once the learning rate was tuned per method.

The positive model exists. Molmo (Ai2, CVPR 2025) ranks models by over 325k human pairwise ratings (~450 matches per pair, Bradley-Terry), reports the automated suite separately, and publishes where the two rankings disagree rather than quoting whichever flatters it. [T1]

The second pattern, and it is newer: the honest instruments are getting more expensive. The cheap screens arrived first — n-gram overlap (broken), Min-K% Prob (AUC 0.72), temperature sensitivity (two sampling runs). What has arrived since needs a large judge on every output, a 23-model multi-family sweep, or a controlled replay protocol. Nothing on this checklist got cheaper in the last month, and two items got strictly harder.

What's still missing

The protocol. Seven failure modes are named; no source gives a practitioner an ordered procedure for clearing them at their own budget — which to run, in what order, at what cost, and what a positive licenses. That remains the single highest-value missing artifact here, and the item that stands between this map and consensus. The 2026-08-07 metadata proposal is the closest published thing and is not it: a schema for recording a result is not a procedure for earning one.

Whether they compound. A distilled model, evaluated by its teacher's own family, on a contaminated suite, trained under a sharded optimizer, is carrying four at once — plausibly the modal case here. Every source isolates one.

A demand-side detector for paraphrased contamination. Membership inference scores a passage you supply, and the whole problem with rephrasing is that you do not know which passage to supply.

A cheap structural probe for hollow reasoning. The four that failed at F1 0.530 were lexical by design, and the diagnosis is that the distinction is structural. Step count, whether stated quantities appear in the chain, whether each intermediate follows from a prior one — none of those has been tried, and one labelled dataset of 30,000 already exists.

Honest evaluation without a judge or an annotation budget. Molmo's 325k ratings are the lab-scale answer. The practitioner-scale answer does not exist in this vault, and the premise depends on it.

One dating caveat to carry throughout: the judge-bias magnitudes above come from GPT-4-0314 and Claude-v1, both retired, and the contamination survey is 2023-vintage corpora. The failure modes are structural; the percentages are old numbers being applied as a discount on 2026 results, and nobody has re-measured them. Treat them as direction, not size — which is, in this article's own new vocabulary, a validity window that expired some time ago.
