What the fine-tune is for: the state of parameter-efficient adaptation

Synthesized 2026-08-01 from peft-methods, which has been contested since 2026-07-18 and is written up now because contested is the state worth writing about, not the state to wait out. The map holds twenty-one dated entries and no article; this is the first. Read it as two pieces that have to stay separate. The settled piece is large and it is about configuration — what to type, what to skip, what the defaults get wrong. The contested piece is one question and everything turns on it: does a low-rank update have the capacity to install knowledge the base model does not have? Every number below carries a date. This field's defaults rot in months.

Ask what the fine-tune is for, before anything else

The single most useful sentence in this map is a conditional, and it was not a conditional until 2026-07-18.

Low-rank adaptation matches full fine-tuning on task adaptation and falls short on domain acquisition.

Task adaptation is teaching a model a format, a style, a tool interface, a classification boundary — work where the capability already exists in the base and the fine-tune selects which part of it to express. Domain acquisition is installing a body of knowledge the base does not have: a codebase, a legal corpus, a language, a specialist literature.

The original result stands in its own regime. LoRA (Microsoft, 2021) cut trainable parameters by roughly 10,000× and GPU memory by ~3× on GPT-3 175B at on-par quality across RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3, and the stated reason is that measured task-adaptation updates are rank-deficient — the useful change genuinely is low-rank, so a low-rank parameterization loses nothing. [T1]

The push-back is equally strong and lands somewhere else. A controlled comparison on Llama-2 7B and 13B over programming and mathematics — LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less (Databricks Mosaic / Columbia, TMLR 2024) — finds LoRA in standard low-rank settings substantially below full fine-tuning on the target domain, with the gap widening the further the domain sits from pretraining: code more than math, continued pretraining on ~20B tokens more than instruction tuning on ~100K pairs. The stated mechanism is that full fine-tuning learns perturbations of rank 10–100× greater than configured LoRA ranks. [T1, contested by T1]

The two results are not in conflict; they are measurements of different targets, and the field spent two years quoting the first as though it covered the second.

So: before choosing a method, decide which of the two you are doing. Format, style, task interface, tone, tool-calling → adapter, default holds, stop reading the method literature. New body of knowledge → the rest of this article is about how much trouble you are in.

Settled practice, as of 2026-08

These are the things to type. Each has a source and a trade-off.

Train on a 4-bit base. QLoRA (UW, 2023) backpropagates through a frozen 4-bit base (NF4, double quantization, paged optimizers) into full-precision adapters and preserves 16-bit fine-tune quality on the tasks tested — 65B on a single 48 GB GPU. Nothing in two weeks of ingest has pushed back on this. Trade: you give up the ability to update the base weights, which is exactly the thing the contested half says you may need. [T1]

Use vanilla LoRA and spend the saved compute on a learning-rate sweep. This replaces the advice this map gave a week earlier, and it is the cleanest result of the last month. Learning Rate Matters (National Taiwan University / MIT / IBM Research / Academia Sinica, 2026) ran nine representative LoRA variants plus vanilla LoRA on Qwen3-0.6B, Gemma-3-1B, Llama-2-7B and Llama-2-13B across math, code, commonsense and instruction following, with up to 16 learning rates from 1e-6 to 1e-3 crossed with six batch sizes and seven ranks. Tuned per method, every method lands within 1–2% of every other, and within 0.52% on the tightest configuration — where the best variant's advantage reads 0.22 points against a ±0.79 standard deviation. What the variants genuinely differ in is which learning rate they want: a 10× spread between PiSSA and LoRA, 18.2× for OLoRA and LoRA-GA. The mechanism is textbook rather than adapter-specific — these methods raise the largest Hessian eigenvalue of the loss surface (up to 100× for the worst offenders), and the optimal step size moves inversely with curvature. A clever initialization drops the optimizer into a sharper basin; a default learning rate overshoots it. The published gains were real measurements of an under-tuned baseline. Sixteen short runs at one rank fit on one card in a way that evaluating nine implementations never did. [T1]

Two named exceptions survive that null, and both are small. DoRA (NVIDIA/HKUST, ICML 2024 Oral) is worth about +1.1% on math at r=8 and washes out above it — which happens to be the rank a memory-bound local fine-tune actually uses. And LoftQ (Georgia Tech / Microsoft, ICLR 2024) was not among the nine tested, and its claim lives at bit-widths the sweep never visited: at 2.5-bit on LLaMA-2-13B, QLoRA reaches 19.39 WikiText-2 perplexity and LoftQ 5.22; at 2.25- and 2-bit QLoRA does not converge at all while LoftQ reaches 7.69. So "initialization doesn't matter" is established on full-precision bases only, and the one place the vault has evidence that it matters enormously is the place the sweep was not run. [T1 ×2]

Correct the scaling factor if you raise the rank. A LoRA submodule computes (W + γ_r·BA)x with γ_r = α/r. rsLoRA (Tenyx, 2023) proves the adapter preserves both forward moments and backward gradients at Θ_r(1) if and only if γ_r ∈ Θ_r(1/√r). Dividing by r is overly aggressive: it damps the adapter harder the more capacity you give it, so the symptom is not divergence but "extra rank does nothing" — gradient norms fall through two orders of magnitude across ranks 4→2048 on Llama-2 7B while loss curves collapse onto one line. use_rslora=True ships in Hugging Face PEFT. Note what this does to the historical record: LoRA's own Table 6, where rank 64 was no better than rank 4, was measured through the uncorrected divisor. [T1, single author, no venue]

Do not infer your memory footprint from target_modules and a rank — measure it. Twenty fine-tuning configurations under a hard 2 GB VRAM budget (adaptive checkpointing on a consumer GPU, 2026) rank peak memory backwards by trainable-parameter count on one architecture: BitFit, the smallest trainable set in the study, peaks at 3,307 MiB on TinyViT where LoRA, AdaLoRA and QLoRA sit at ~1,260, at matched batch size, reproduced on a second dataset. Bias terms live in the earliest layers, so the backward pass keeps the whole activation stack alive; an adapter concentrated later releases it. "PEFT reduces memory" is a property of a configuration, not of PEFT. Scope: 21–32M vision backbones, so the mechanism transfers by argument and the magnitude does not. [T1]

Assert your trainable-parameter count before believing any training run. New on 2026-08-01 and it is a hygiene item rather than a research finding. Robust RL for small-scale language model agents (2026) reports that in PPO loops assembled from PEFT and TRL in the ordinary way, adapter parameters end up flagged non-trainable: the loss computes, rollouts generate, the curve looks alive, and the policy distribution never moves. The fix is to merge the SFT adapter into the base and attach a fresh zero-initialized adapter for the second stage. This is the third case in two weeks where the tooling rather than the method produced a published result on this map, and it is the worst of the three, because a "the adapter didn't help" produced on that pipeline is indistinguishable from a correct null. Measured at 70M–500M; nobody has checked at 7B. [T1]

The four levers, and what each is worth now

A low-rank update is not one dial. It is four independent choices, and as of 2026-08 the vault has a graded source for each — with three of the four largely emptied out and one left standing.

Initialization — worth ~0 on a full-precision base. PiSSA (Peking University, NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight) starts the adapter on the base weight's principal singular components; LoftQ starts it on the quantization error. Both reported real gains against vanilla LoRA/QLoRA, and the 2026-02 sweep discounts them to a tuning effect wherever the base is full precision. On a quantized base the question is open and LoftQ's sub-3-bit numbers stand.

Parameterization — worth something, and unabsorbed. rsLoRA's correction is proven and shipping. It appears nowhere in Hugging Face PEFT's own quantization guide, whose worked example is r=16, lora_alpha=8, target_modules=["q_proj","k_proj","v_proj","o_proj"] — attention-only, alpha < r, essentially the 2021 configuration; and torchtune's memory guide recommends lora_alpha ≈ 2*lora_rank across ranks 8–256 with no mention of the correction. So the answer to "has the field absorbed this" is no, not in the two places a practitioner would first look. [T2, two official tool docs]

Rank — now the lever with the most arithmetic under it, and it cuts both ways. New on 2026-08-01: Tight Sample Complexity for Low-Rank Adaptation (2026) proves the excess risk of the empirical risk minimizer over rank-r LoRA is Θ̃(rd/n) — an upper bound by a localized Rademacher argument and a matching minimax lower bound by Fano packing, so the linear cost of declared rank belongs to the method class rather than to a loose proof. Excess risk therefore has two terms — an approximation floor for the singular directions you refused to model, and an estimation term for the parameters a finite dataset must fit — so validation loss is U-shaped in rank. All three measured fine-tunes show the U: optima at r=8, r=32 and r=32, rising to 0.519, 0.637 and 0.642 by r=128 (two of three significant at p=0.016).

The practitioner-facing consequence is a regime split, and it is this vault's reading rather than either author's. rd/n is negligible when n is a real corpus — which is rsLoRA's setting, and why that paper sees monotone gains with rank once the divisor is fixed — and dominant when n is 500 curated examples, which is this paper's most significant cell and roughly the scale LIMA (Meta AI et al., NeurIPS 2023) showed is enough to align a 65B base. Extra rank is nearly free on a large fine-tuning set and expensive on a curated one. If you have a thousand hand-written examples, r=8 is not timidity. [T1, single author, encoder-only empirics]

The same paper contains the more interesting theorem and it has no transformer evidence behind it: an estimator that regularizes by the nuclear norm and then truncates achieves Θ̃(r*d/n) for every declared r ≥ r*. Over-ranking becomes harmless and rank selection stops being a decision. The author's own reading — that the over-parameterization penalty is a property of unregularized empirical risk minimization rather than of the LoRA class — is the sharpest thing on this map, and no library implements it. Testing it is a rank sweep with one extra loss term on one card.

Placement — the only lever left with a large unexplained effect, and the one with the least evidence. AdaLoRA (Georgia Tech / Princeton / Microsoft Azure AI, ICLR 2023) pinned the trainable budget at 0.28M on a 183M encoder and varied only where it goes: the second FFN matrix scores 89.99 on MNLI against the query projection's 88.58, and layers 10–12 score 88.6 against layers 1–3's 77.87. A 10.7-point spread at identical budget, with no rank change anywhere. That is larger than most effects this map argues about, and it indicts both current defaults — the original paper's W_q/W_v targeting is the worst pair in that sweep, and "adapt every linear module," which tooling defaults to, avoids the decision rather than making it. Learned allocation recovers about one GLUE point of the ten. Scope it hard: one 183M encoder, task adaptation only, learning rate held fixed, and the same variable independently turns out to be a memory lever too. [T1]

The contested half: can a low-rank update install knowledge?

This is what contested means here, and it has not moved since 2026-07-18.

The case for "no" has two independent T1s and a mechanism. The Databricks/Columbia comparison measures the shortfall. The Illusion of Equivalence (MIT CSAIL, 2024) explains it: SVD of fine-tuned weights shows LoRA inserts new high-ranking singular vectors roughly orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum — intruder dimensions — which full fine-tuning does not produce. Scaling them down post-hoc recovers most pre-training-distribution performance at minimal downstream cost, so they cause the forgetting rather than accompany it. Two consequences worth carrying separately from the contest: LoRA generalizes worse out of distribution at matched in-domain accuracy, and intruder dimensions accumulate across sequential fine-tunes, which degrades the adapter-per-task workflow that is half of LoRA's appeal. [T1]

The case for "yes, if you adapt in the right subspace" is one paper and thin. MiCA (2026) confines the update to the weight's minor singular directions — the mirror of PiSSA — and reports a ~100,000-token German history book injected into Llama-2-7B-chat scoring 39.2% on a held-out 102-question quiz against LoRA's 29.4%, full fine-tuning's 30.4% and a 27.4% baseline, at flat retention. The subspace ablation is the load-bearing part: holding everything else fixed, minor 75.63 > major 74.21 > random 73.75 > untouched 72.91 — random beats major. Discount it heavily: two-author preprint, 300- and 102-item unreleased evals authored by the method's own authors, no variance reported on either arm, a single full-fine-tuning row trained 1 epoch against the adapters' 8, and a winning learning rate at the top of the swept range, which is the exact signature the learning-rate paper described. If it replicates it inverts this map's economics — 4M trainable parameters at r=16 beating a 67M r=128 LoRA is the opposite of "domain acquisition needs high rank." [T1, one study, suggestive]

The apparent collision between MiCA and the nine-variant null resolves cleanly and is worth understanding, because it is the whole shape of this map. The null tested MiLoRA — the same minor-component idea — and found it indistinguishable from vanilla LoRA once tuned. But every task there was task adaptation on benchmarks the base already knew, and MiCA's targets are content the base demonstrably lacks. So: among adapters, on task adaptation, initialization is a tuning artifact; on domain acquisition, one initialization has now been reported to matter a great deal, once. Scope condition, not contradiction.

And the contest may be mis-measured, twice over. Both push-back papers configure LoRA under γ_r = α/r, which rsLoRA proves damps adapters harder as rank rises — so "we raised the rank and the gap stayed" was measured through a suppressor. Both also run at fixed hyperparameters, which the learning-rate result establishes is the confound that manufactures apparent differences between adapter configurations. The map's status should therefore be read as unresolved and possibly mis-measured, not merely unresolved.

The recipe

As of 2026-08, for one or two consumer cards:

  1. Name the target. Task adaptation or domain acquisition. If you cannot tell, assume task adaptation and check whether the eval you care about is one the base already partly passes.
  2. 4-bit base, vanilla LoRA, alpha = r. Skip the variant literature.
  3. Set rank from your dataset size, not from a blog post. Small n → small r; the penalty for over-ranking scales as rd/n. Start at r=8–16 for a curated set.
  4. Sweep the learning rate — sixteen short runs beats any method choice available.
  5. Put the budget in the FFN and the upper layers, not W_q/W_v, and not "all-linear" by default. This is the one lever still worth real points and the evidence is one encoder, so measure on your own model.
  6. Set use_rslora=True if you go above r≈32.
  7. Measure peak VRAM. Do not infer it.
  8. Assert the trainable-parameter count before you believe a training curve.
  9. If it is domain acquisition: expect a gap, question the rank budget first, and know that full fine-tuning buys the rest at a quantified forgetting cost. A better initialization is not a substitute for the rank the domain actually needs.

What nobody has measured

Four holes, in rough order of how much they would change the advice above.

The whole comparison, re-run properly. No source varies more than one of the four levers. The wanted grid is small and does not exist: placement × initialization × parameterization × rank, each cell at its own tuned learning rate, on a decoder-only 7B, reporting downstream accuracy and peak VRAM together. Every claim in this article is one slice of that grid measured with the other axes pinned.

Does nuclear-norm regularization make rank selection free on a transformer? The theorem says yes and is verified only on synthetic trace regression. This is the cheapest item here and the only one whose payoff is a deleted decision.

Does the variant null survive on a quantized base? The nine-variant sweep runs on full-precision bases and excludes LoftQ, whose entire claim is that initializing on the quantization error is what makes 2- and 2.5-bit LoRA converge where QLoRA fails outright. So the vault holds "initialization is a tuning artifact" measured exactly where it has no counter-evidence, and "initialization is decisive" measured exactly where the sweep was not run. One grid settles it: PiSSA vs LoftQ vs plain init, on a 4-bit and a 2-bit base, each at its own tuned learning rate.

What an unmerged adapter costs at inference. Adapters merge into the base at deploy time for zero added latency — that is one of LoRA's cleanest wins, and it is a property of the base format rather than of the adapter. Across the nine quantization backends PEFT now supports, merging is what keeps breaking: AQLM cannot merge at all, torchao merges correctly only with LoRA and int8_weight_only and otherwise produces silently incorrect results, and both Intel Neural Compressor and Transformer Engine list merge/unmerge as unsupported. The reason is structural — merging requires adding a full-precision low-rank product back into the base and re-encoding it without loss, which is roughly true of int8 and NF4 and simply false of a codebook scheme. Quantization aggressiveness and adapter mergeability are in direct tension, so going below 3 bits means keeping a separate adapter forever, at whatever the unmerged forward pass costs — which nobody has measured. [T2]
