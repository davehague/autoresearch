What the fine-tune is for: the state of parameter-efficient adaptation

First synthesized 2026-08-01. Refreshed 2026-08-15 against seven dated entries the map added in two weeks, and the refresh is larger than a top-up: the lever this article named as the last one worth real points has been re-measured on decoder-only models and shrank by an order of magnitude, and the 4-bit default it recommended flatly now has a price tag in watt-hours. Both corrections are marked in place. peft-methods has been contested since 2026-07-18 and is still contested; it forked on 2026-08-10, so everything about serving adapters, multi-tenancy and adapters that are not stored matrices now lives in adapter-objects-and-serving and is out of scope here. Read this as two pieces that have to stay separate. The settled piece is large and it is about configuration — what to type, what to skip, what the defaults get wrong. The contested piece is one question and everything turns on it: does a low-rank update have the capacity to install knowledge the base model does not have? Every number below carries a date. This field's defaults rot in months, and two of them rotted while this article sat.

Ask what the fine-tune is for, before anything else

The single most useful sentence in this map is a conditional, and it was not a conditional until 2026-07-18.

Low-rank adaptation matches full fine-tuning on task adaptation and falls short on domain acquisition.

Task adaptation is teaching a model a format, a style, a tool interface, a classification boundary — work where the capability already exists in the base and the fine-tune selects which part of it to express. Domain acquisition is installing a body of knowledge the base does not have: a codebase, a legal corpus, a language, a specialist literature.

The original result stands in its own regime. LoRA (Microsoft, 2021) cut trainable parameters by roughly 10,000× and GPU memory by ~3× on GPT-3 175B at on-par quality across RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3, and the stated reason is that measured task-adaptation updates are rank-deficient — the useful change genuinely is low-rank, so a low-rank parameterization loses nothing. [T1]

The push-back is equally strong and lands somewhere else. A controlled comparison on Llama-2 7B and 13B over programming and mathematics — LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less (Databricks Mosaic / Columbia, TMLR 2024) — finds LoRA in standard low-rank settings substantially below full fine-tuning on the target domain, with the gap widening the further the domain sits from pretraining: code more than math, continued pretraining on ~20B tokens more than instruction tuning on ~100K pairs. The stated mechanism is that full fine-tuning learns perturbations of rank 10–100× greater than configured LoRA ranks. [T1, contested by T1]

The two results are not in conflict; they are measurements of different targets, and the field spent two years quoting the first as though it covered the second.

New on 2026-08-03, and it is the purest observed instance of what the low-rank bet is actually buying. A rank-32 LoRA trained only to match a full-depth teacher's logits on plain text — no task supervision at all — repairs a broken representation-readout interface well enough to move a five-benchmark average from 28.32 to 53.38 (CoMem, 2026). Nothing is being taught. An interface is being re-fitted, and twenty-five points fall out. That is task adaptation stripped of the task, and it is the shape the settled half of this article describes. [T1]

So: before choosing a method, decide which of the two you are doing. Format, style, task interface, tone, tool-calling, or re-fitting a readout → adapter, default holds, stop reading the method literature. New body of knowledge → the rest of this article is about how much trouble you are in.

Settled practice, as of 2026-08

These are the things to type. Each has a source and a trade-off.

Train on a 4-bit base — but only when the run will not otherwise start. Corrected 2026-08-06. QLoRA (UW, 2023) backpropagates through a frozen 4-bit base (NF4, double quantization, paged optimizers) and preserves 16-bit fine-tune quality on the tasks tested — 65B on a single 48 GB GPU. Nothing has pushed back on the quality claim. What arrived is the bill. Instrumented across 108 fine-tunes on one RTX 4090 (PEFT energy and memory on device, 2026), quantizing the base buys 3.9× peak fine-tuning VRAM (16,480 → 4,225 MB) and costs +62% wall-clock, +76% energy, and 76% higher inference latency than a merged adapter. It was selected in one of twelve transformer configurations on energy and five of twelve on memory. This article said "4-bit base" without qualification on 2026-08-01; the qualified version is quantize when the run will not otherwise fit, and not otherwise. The older trade still applies on top: you give up the ability to update the base weights, which is exactly the thing the contested half says you may need. [T1 ×2]

Use vanilla LoRA and spend the saved compute on a learning-rate sweep. This is the cleanest result on the map and two weeks of arrivals have only reinforced it. Learning Rate Matters (National Taiwan University / MIT / IBM Research / Academia Sinica, 2026) ran nine representative LoRA variants plus vanilla LoRA on Qwen3-0.6B, Gemma-3-1B, Llama-2-7B and Llama-2-13B across math, code, commonsense and instruction following, with up to 16 learning rates from 1e-6 to 1e-3 crossed with six batch sizes and seven ranks. Tuned per method, every method lands within 1–2% of every other, and within 0.52% on the tightest configuration — where the best variant's advantage reads 0.22 points against a ±0.79 standard deviation. What the variants genuinely differ in is which learning rate they want: a 10× spread between PiSSA and LoRA, 18.2× for OLoRA and LoRA-GA. The mechanism is textbook rather than adapter-specific — these methods raise the largest Hessian eigenvalue of the loss surface (up to 100× for the worst offenders), and the optimal step size moves inversely with curvature. The published gains were real measurements of an under-tuned baseline. [T1]

The uncomfortable corollary, and it has not improved: five consecutive adapter papers on this map since 2026-08-02 compare against an untuned LoRA at a fixed learning rate — 1e-4 in three of them. Any adapter result you read that does not report a sweep is reporting the gap between two default learning rates.

If a sweep is unaffordable, raise the B-matrix learning rate. New 2026-08-06. LoRA+ — identical adapter, lr_B = 16·lr_A, no parameter, FLOP or memory difference — took 18 of 24 selections in the energy study under a single fixed learning rate per benchmark. Read against the sweep result that is not a rival finding but the same one from the other side: it measures how far LoRA's shared default sits from its optimum. Use it as the fallback, not as a replacement for measuring. [T1]

Two named exceptions to the variant null survive, and both are small. DoRA (NVIDIA/HKUST, ICML 2024 Oral) is worth about +1.1% on math at r=8 and washes out above it — which happens to be the rank a memory-bound local fine-tune actually uses. And LoftQ (Georgia Tech / Microsoft, ICLR 2024) was not among the nine tested, and its claim lives at bit-widths the sweep never visited: at 2.5-bit on LLaMA-2-13B, QLoRA reaches 19.39 WikiText-2 perplexity and LoftQ 5.22; at 2.25- and 2-bit QLoRA does not converge at all while LoftQ reaches 7.69. So "initialization doesn't matter" is established on full-precision bases only, and the one place the vault has evidence that it matters enormously is the place the sweep was not run. [T1 ×2]

Correct the scaling factor if you raise the rank. A LoRA submodule computes (W + γ_r·BA)x with γ_r = α/r. rsLoRA (Tenyx, 2023) proves the adapter preserves both forward moments and backward gradients at Θ_r(1) if and only if γ_r ∈ Θ_r(1/√r). Dividing by r is overly aggressive: it damps the adapter harder the more capacity you give it, so the symptom is not divergence but "extra rank does nothing" — gradient norms fall through two orders of magnitude across ranks 4→2048 on Llama-2 7B while loss curves collapse onto one line. use_rslora=True ships in Hugging Face PEFT. Note what this does to the historical record: LoRA's own Table 6, where rank 64 was no better than rank 4, was measured through the uncorrected divisor. [T1, single author, no venue]

An unreplicated datum worth keeping, new 2026-08-02: on Qwen3-14B, doubling uniform LoRA's rank from 4 to 8 makes it worse (84.84 → 84.69) while a rank-allocated version improves — the rsLoRA symptom at ranks far below the ones rsLoRA studied.

Do not infer your memory footprint from target_modules and a rank — measure it. Twenty fine-tuning configurations under a hard 2 GB VRAM budget (adaptive checkpointing on a consumer GPU, 2026) rank peak memory backwards by trainable-parameter count on one architecture: BitFit, the smallest trainable set in the study, peaks at 3,307 MiB on TinyViT where LoRA, AdaLoRA and QLoRA sit at ~1,260, at matched batch size, reproduced on a second dataset. Bias terms live in the earliest layers, so the backward pass keeps the whole activation stack alive; an adapter concentrated later releases it. "PEFT reduces memory" is a property of a configuration, not of PEFT. Scope: 21–32M vision backbones, so the mechanism transfers by argument and the magnitude does not. [T1]

Assert your trainable-parameter count before believing any training run. Robust RL for small-scale language model agents (2026) reports that in PPO loops assembled from PEFT and TRL in the ordinary way, adapter parameters end up flagged non-trainable: the loss computes, rollouts generate, the curve looks alive, and the policy distribution never moves. The fix is to merge the SFT adapter into the base and attach a fresh zero-initialized adapter for the second stage. This is the third case in two weeks where the tooling rather than the method produced a published result on this map, and it is the worst of the three, because a "the adapter didn't help" produced on that pipeline is indistinguishable from a correct null. Measured at 70M–500M; nobody has checked at 7B. [T1]

The four levers, and what each is worth now

A low-rank update is not one dial. It is four independent choices — and as of 2026-08-15 all four have been measured, three came back small, and the fourth has a floor nobody had looked for.

Initialization — worth ~0 on a full-precision base, and now with a stated boundary. PiSSA (Peking University, NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight) starts the adapter on the base weight's principal singular components; LoftQ starts it on the quantization error. Both reported real gains, and the 2026-02 sweep discounts them to a tuning effect wherever the base is full precision. New on 2026-08-02, and it is the first initialization study run under the learning-rate discipline the sweep demanded: ULoRA (2026) collapses the initialization zoo into one two-parameter family, finds the best point usually interior and always task-dependent, maps a sharp failure region at negative whitening (−15 points) against a broad safe plateau above it, and — the part that matters — independently reproduces the null on T5 while bounding it. Saturation is the boundary: where tuned LoRA has already closed on full fine-tuning, initialization has nothing left to buy. That is a more useful statement than "initialization doesn't matter," because it tells you where to look for the exception. On a quantized base the question remains open and LoftQ's sub-3-bit numbers stand. [T1 ×2]

A mechanism arrived for initialization on 2026-08-12 and cuts against the method it explains. Marchenko–Pastur decomposition splits a pretrained attention projection into a random-like bulk and a thin set of outliers, validated causally: zeroing the 112,458 MP outliers in Mistral-7B lands HellaSwag, MMLU and PIQA at chance (0.814 → 0.256, 0.627 → 0.269, 0.823 → 0.508) while removing 75% of the bulk on LLaMA-2-7B costs 0.007 / 0.060 / 0.002 (spectral outliers, 2026). So the principal singular components PiSSA initializes on are, demonstrably, where the learned structure lives — and PiSSA's tuned advantage is still 0.22 points against a ±0.79 standard deviation. The resolution is not that one is wrong: knowing where the model's knowledge sits does not tell you where to add capacity. Read with the domain-acquisition result below, the pair says something neither says alone — the outliers are what the model already knows, and the bulk is where there is room to put something else. That predicts spectrum-aware adaptation should target the bulk, the opposite of PiSSA, and the experiment is a rank sweep on one card. Nobody has run it. [T1]

Parameterization — worth something, and still unabsorbed. rsLoRA's correction is proven and shipping. It appears nowhere in Hugging Face PEFT's own quantization guide, whose worked example is r=16, lora_alpha=8, target_modules=["q_proj","k_proj","v_proj","o_proj"] — attention-only, alpha < r, essentially the 2021 configuration; and torchtune's memory guide recommends lora_alpha ≈ 2*lora_rank across ranks 8–256 with no mention of the correction. So the answer to "has the field absorbed this" is no, not in the two places a practitioner would first look. [T2, two official tool docs]

Rank — the lever with the most arithmetic under it, and it now has both a ceiling and a floor. Tight Sample Complexity for Low-Rank Adaptation (2026) proves the excess risk of the empirical risk minimizer over rank-r LoRA is Θ̃(rd/n) — an upper bound by a localized Rademacher argument and a matching minimax lower bound by Fano packing, so the linear cost of declared rank belongs to the method class rather than to a loose proof. Excess risk therefore has two terms — an approximation floor for the singular directions you refused to model, and an estimation term for the parameters a finite dataset must fit — so validation loss is U-shaped in rank. All three measured fine-tunes show the U: optima at r=8, r=32 and r=32, rising to 0.519, 0.637 and 0.642 by r=128 (two of three significant at p=0.016).

The practitioner-facing consequence is a regime split, and it is this vault's reading rather than either author's. rd/n is negligible when n is a real corpus — which is rsLoRA's setting, and why that paper sees monotone gains with rank once the divisor is fixed — and dominant when n is 500 curated examples, which is this paper's most significant cell and roughly the scale LIMA (Meta AI et al., NeurIPS 2023) showed is enough to align a 65B base. Extra rank is nearly free on a large fine-tuning set and expensive on a curated one. [T1, single author, encoder-only empirics]

The floor is new on 2026-08-05 and nobody in-vault had noticed the sweeps were not testing it. This map spent two weeks deflating rank on an r=4→256 sweep that found 0.33–1.67% effects with sign flips. One rung lower, plain r=1 LoRA on Mistral-7B-v0.3 reads MultiArith 11.03%, SPIDER 30.34%, MBPP 15.75% (SALT, 2026, measured as that paper's baseline column). An adapter at rank 1 does not degrade — it stops functioning. So "rank barely matters" is a statement about the interval 4 ≤ r ≤ 256, and the sweep's floor was a choice that turned out to be load-bearing. [T1]

The same theory paper contains the more interesting theorem and it still has no transformer evidence: an estimator that regularizes by the nuclear norm and then truncates achieves Θ̃(r*d/n) for every declared r ≥ r*. Over-ranking becomes harmless and rank selection stops being a decision. The author's own reading — that the over-parameterization penalty is a property of unregularized empirical risk minimization rather than of the LoRA class — is the sharpest thing on this map, and no library implements it. Testing it is a rank sweep with one extra loss term on one card.

Placement — corrected 2026-08-02, and this is the article's largest revision. The 2026-08-01 version of this section called placement "the only lever left with a large unexplained effect." AdaLoRA (Georgia Tech / Princeton / Microsoft Azure AI, ICLR 2023) pinned the trainable budget at 0.28M on a 183M encoder and varied only where it goes: the second FFN matrix scored 89.99 on MNLI against the query projection's 88.58, and layers 10–12 scored 88.6 against layers 1–3's 77.87 — a 10.7-point spread at identical budget. The gap this article named was that no decoder-only model appeared anywhere in that literature.

Three decoder-only backbones later, the answer is +1.13 GSM8K points on average (IFCLoRA, 2026). Real, and an order of magnitude down. The two numbers are not measuring the same thing — best-versus-worst on a 183M encoder against good-versus-flat on an 8B decoder — and this article should stop citing the 10.7-point figure without that qualifier, which is what it did on 2026-08-01.

What survives intact is where the budget goes: mid-to-late FFN, shallow layers at the floor, replicated across two independent sources and against both tooling defaults. What is new and awkward is the ablation — random 81.27, uniform 80.97, gradient-only 81.13, full method 82.33 — which says a fifth of the gain is free from any non-uniform profile, and that a purely local sensitivity signal is worse than random. And there is a candidate mechanism that would demote the lever entirely: under α/r, varying rank across modules varies effective step size, so part of "placement" may be optimization rather than capacity routing. That is cheap to test and untested. [T1 ×2]

A fifth lever, and it only exists for systems of adapters

New since this article was first written, and it does not fit the four-lever frame because it has no meaning for a single adapter.

On 2026-08-03, FedWeave (UESTC / Zhejiang, 2026) found that a multi-adapter system has two learnable objects that want opposite training data. Experts need purity — pattern-coherent batches, because mixed patterns in one update destroy the coherence specialization depends on. Routers need contrast — mixed-pattern trajectories, because a selection rule requires observing alternatives side by side. The isolating ablation, backbone and data and capacity held fixed: both-coarse 0.5649, both-fine 0.5578, coarse-router-with-fine-experts 0.5872. Both symmetric configurations lose, in opposite directions — the asymmetry, not the resolution, is the lever. Scope it before using it: this is a mixture of LoRA adapters over a frozen dense backbone, not LoRA on a pretrained sparse model, every method runs at a fixed 1e-4, and the backbones are 2B–3B. One credit on a map whose sources mostly live on A100s: the whole study runs on one RTX 4090. [T1]

A related structural claim arrived 2026-08-05 and survives its own paper's discount: an adapter can be a function of the input rather than a stored object (MoEGen, 2026, generating the low-rank update from a per-expert vector via a shared hypernetwork). The headline numbers sit inside the band the learning-rate confound explains and the paper's own ablation puts its routing signal at 0.7 points, so take the idea and not the margin. [T1, discounted]

The contested half: can a low-rank update install knowledge?

This is what contested means here, and it has not moved since 2026-07-18. Fourteen sources arrived in the interim and not one ran a full fine-tuning arm on a domain-acquisition target. That is the single most conspicuous fact about this map.

The case for "no" has two independent T1s and a mechanism. The Databricks/Columbia comparison measures the shortfall. The Illusion of Equivalence (MIT CSAIL, 2024) explains it: SVD of fine-tuned weights shows LoRA inserts new high-ranking singular vectors roughly orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum — intruder dimensions — which full fine-tuning does not produce. Scaling them down post-hoc recovers most pre-training-distribution performance at minimal downstream cost, so they cause the forgetting rather than accompany it. Two consequences worth carrying separately from the contest: LoRA generalizes worse out of distribution at matched in-domain accuracy, and intruder dimensions accumulate across sequential fine-tunes, which degrades the adapter-per-task workflow that is half of LoRA's appeal. [T1]

The case for "yes, if you adapt in the right subspace" is one paper and thin. MiCA (2026) confines the update to the weight's minor singular directions — the mirror of PiSSA — and reports a ~100,000-token German history book injected into Llama-2-7B-chat scoring 39.2% on a held-out 102-question quiz against LoRA's 29.4%, full fine-tuning's 30.4% and a 27.4% baseline, at flat retention. The subspace ablation is the load-bearing part: holding everything else fixed, minor 75.63 > major 74.21 > random 73.75 > untouched 72.91 — random beats major. Discount it heavily: two-author preprint, 300- and 102-item unreleased evals authored by the method's own authors, no variance reported on either arm, a single full-fine-tuning row trained 1 epoch against the adapters' 8, and a winning learning rate at the top of the swept range, which is the exact signature the learning-rate paper described. If it replicates it inverts this map's economics — 4M trainable parameters at r=16 beating a 67M r=128 LoRA is the opposite of "domain acquisition needs high rank." [T1, one study, suggestive]

The 2026-08-12 spectral result is the first independent thing to make MiCA's direction plausible, and it did not set out to. If the outlier subspace is where the pretrained knowledge causally lives — zero it and three benchmarks go to chance — and the bulk is 75% removable at a cost of 0.007 accuracy, then "put new knowledge in the minor directions" is a claim about writing into the part of the weight matrix that is not already spoken for. That is a mechanism, not a replication: nobody has run domain acquisition against the MP decomposition, and MiCA's evidence is still one unreleased quiz.

The apparent collision between MiCA and the nine-variant null resolves cleanly and is worth understanding, because it is the whole shape of this map. The null tested MiLoRA — the same minor-component idea — and found it indistinguishable from vanilla LoRA once tuned. But every task there was task adaptation on benchmarks the base already knew, and MiCA's targets are content the base demonstrably lacks. So: among adapters, on task adaptation, initialization is a tuning artifact; on domain acquisition, one initialization has now been reported to matter a great deal, once. Scope condition, not contradiction. ULoRA's 2026-08-02 boundary — initialization leverage vanishes where tuned LoRA has saturated — says the same thing in the general form.

And the contest may be mis-measured, twice over. Both push-back papers configure LoRA under γ_r = α/r, which rsLoRA proves damps adapters harder as rank rises — so "we raised the rank and the gap stayed" was measured through a suppressor. Both also run at fixed hyperparameters, which the learning-rate result establishes is the confound that manufactures apparent differences between adapter configurations. The map's status should therefore be read as unresolved and possibly mis-measured, not merely unresolved.

One recalibration on how much of this literature is even about capacity. New 2026-08-14. A method that writes nothing into the weights — a logit-space correction transported from the author's own text, no backward pass — beats a LoRA-SFT arm on average on Qwen3-8B (0.335 vs 0.321) and on every task on Llama-3.1-8B (0.287 vs 0.259) (weightless fine-tuning, 2026). Do not read that as evidence that parameters are unnecessary. Read it the way the energy study taught this map to read bias-only tuning winning 18 of 24 pairs on 0.09M weights: as a statement about what personalization benchmarks ask for. Both results say short-form stylistic adaptation is a distribution shift that barely needs capacity, and both carry the same tell — bias-only then collapsed on the objective with the largest label space (0.043 accuracy), which is the shape of a method that moves a distribution but cannot install a capability. If your benchmark can be won without writing to the weights, it was never measuring the contested question. [T1 ×2]

The recipe

As of 2026-08-15, for one or two consumer cards:

  1. Name the target. Task adaptation or domain acquisition. If you cannot tell, assume task adaptation and check whether the eval you care about is one the base already partly passes.
  2. Vanilla LoRA, alpha = r. Skip the variant literature.
  3. Quantize the base only if the run will not otherwise fit. 4-bit buys 3.9× VRAM for +62% wall-clock, +76% energy and +76% inference latency. Changed 2026-08-06; this used to be step 2 unconditionally.
  4. Set rank from your dataset size, not from a blog post. Small n → small r; the penalty for over-ranking scales as rd/n. Start at r=8–16 for a curated set — and do not go to r=1, where an adapter stops working rather than degrading.
  5. Sweep the learning rate — sixteen short runs beats any method choice available. If you cannot afford it, set lr_B = 16·lr_A and move on.
  6. Put the budget in mid-to-late FFN, not W_q/W_v, and not "all-linear" by default. Worth ~1.1 points on a decoder-only 8B, not the 10.7 an encoder study suggested — and about a fifth of that is available from any non-uniform profile, so do not over-engineer the allocation.
  7. Set use_rslora=True if you go above r≈32.
  8. Measure peak VRAM. Do not infer it.
  9. Assert the trainable-parameter count before you believe a training curve.
  10. If it is domain acquisition: expect a gap, question the rank budget first, and know that full fine-tuning buys the rest at a quantified forgetting cost. A better initialization is not a substitute for the rank the domain actually needs.

What nobody has measured

Four holes, in rough order of how much they would change the advice above. Two from the 2026-08-01 version have been partly filled and are marked; the two that matter most are untouched.

The contested question itself, with a full fine-tuning arm. Fourteen sources in two weeks, not one of them running full fine-tuning against a domain-acquisition target on a decoder-only base at tuned learning rates. This is no longer a gap in the literature so much as a revealed preference: the comparison is expensive and every method paper has an incentive not to run it. It is the only experiment that would move this map's status.

Does nuclear-norm regularization make rank selection free on a transformer? The theorem says yes and is verified only on synthetic trace regression. This is the cheapest item here and the only one whose payoff is a deleted decision. Unchanged since 2026-08-01.

Does the variant null survive on a quantized base? The nine-variant sweep runs on full-precision bases and excludes LoftQ, whose entire claim is that initializing on the quantization error is what makes 2- and 2.5-bit LoRA converge where QLoRA fails outright. So the vault holds "initialization is a tuning artifact" measured exactly where it has no counter-evidence, and "initialization is decisive" measured exactly where the sweep was not run. Still open on 2026-08-15, and it got more pointed: ULoRA ran the discipline correctly and also on full precision, and neither it nor the four placement/rank/aggregation studies since 2026-08-02 tests a quantized base. One grid settles it: PiSSA vs LoftQ vs plain init, on a 4-bit and a 2-bit base, each at its own tuned learning rate.

Spectrum-aware adaptation targeting the bulk rather than the outliers. New 2026-08-12 and the cheapest live experiment on the map. The causal decomposition says the outliers carry what the model knows and the bulk is 75% removable; every spectrum-aware initialization in the literature targets the outliers. Running LoRA restricted to the MP bulk against PiSSA and vanilla, at tuned learning rates, on one 7B, is a weekend. The paper that supplied the decomposition outlines the idea and does not run it.

Out of scope since the 2026-08-10 fork, and moved rather than dropped: what an unmerged adapter costs at inference, multi-tenant adapter serving, shared-basis/private-residual splits, and adapters that are not stored matrices. Those are adapter-objects-and-serving's now. The one finding worth carrying across the boundary, because it constrains step 3 above: quantization aggressiveness and adapter mergeability are in direct tension — AQLM cannot merge at all, torchao merges correctly only with LoRA and int8_weight_only — so going below 3 bits means keeping a separate adapter forever.
