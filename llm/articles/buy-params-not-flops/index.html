Buy parameters, not FLOPs: the state of mixture-of-experts

Synthesized 2026-07-21 from mixture-of-experts (status: consolidating — coined this run, directly at consolidating, from five T1s across three lineages; the map's coining note explains why). As of 2026-07 the strongest open-weights models — the DeepSeek-V3/R1 line, Qwen3's large variants, Mixtral — are MoE, so this is no longer an exotic architecture; it is the architecture under the weights you are most likely to fine-tune, quantize, and serve. Check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

A mixture-of-experts layer replaces the transformer block's FFN with N expert FFNs behind a sparse router: each token is processed by k of them (k=1 or 2 in practice), so parameter count and per-token compute become separate dials. Quality tracks the parameter dial when training data is large; each token's cost tracks the compute dial. The lineage, briefly:

  • The sparsely-gated MoE (Shazeer et al., Google Brain, ICLR 2017) made conditional computation real — noisy top-k gating, >1000× capacity at minor efficiency loss, 137B parameters on 2017 hardware — and named the three problems everything since has been solving: routing collapse (favor is self-reinforcing; fixed by auxiliary balance losses), the shrinking batch, and the bandwidth ratio. [T1]
  • Switch Transformers (Fedus, Zoph & Shazeer, JMLR 2022) is the modern recipe: route each token to one expert, and expert count becomes a fourth scaling axis — 7× pretraining wall-clock to T5-Base quality at identical FLOPs per token, gains monotone from 2 to 256 experts, surviving fine-tuning and 101 languages. [T1]
  • ST-MoE (Zoph et al., 2022) is the owner's manual: most stability fixes trade quality away; the router z-loss doesn't (it fixes the actual failure — router exponentials amplifying bfloat16 roundoff). And sparse models fine-tune differently (below). [T1]
  • Mixtral 8x7B (Mistral AI, 2024, Apache 2.0) is the open-weights proof: 47B total / 13B active, matching or beating Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 (MMLU 70.6 vs 69.9 / 70.0), instruct variant above both on LMSys human Elo. [T1]
  • DeepSeekMoE (DeepSeek-AI, 2024) sets the current design point: slice the experts finer and pin a shared always-on expert — strictly better than conventional top-2 at matched cost, within noise of the dense upper bound (the same-total-params model with everything activated), and the substrate of the open flagships that followed. [T1]

What to do

  • Reading a model card: separate the two parameter counts. "8x7B = 56B" is wrong twice — Mixtral is 47B total (experts share attention) and 13B active. Compute, latency, and the pretraining bill track active; serving memory tracks total. The 5× headline win is per-FLOP, not per-byte. [T1]
  • Sizing hardware for a local MoE: budget RAM for the total count, then ask what compute you saved. Mixtral's own paper says SMoEs suit batched workloads — a single-user local server is the case where the trade bites. The counterexample that works: DeepSeekMoE 16B (~2.8B active, ≈LLaMA2-7B quality) on one 40GB GPU unquantized. Small-total MoE is local-friendly; big-total MoE wants offload machinery this map has no graded sources for yet. [T1]
  • Fine-tuning an MoE: change the protocol, not just the checkpoint. Sparse models overfit small tasks and want smaller batches + higher learning rates — the opposite of dense; with dense-tuned hyperparameters "virtually no fine-tuning gain over dense" survives. [T1]
  • Consider freezing the experts entirely. Updating only the non-expert dense skeleton matched updating all parameters (≈86 SuperGLUE both ways); updating only the experts — ~80% of the model — lost ~3.5 points. Cheaper, and nothing measured says you lose anything on task fine-tunes. Caveat: full-rank on the dense subset; nobody has published the LoRA version. [T1]
  • Training one from scratch (or debugging one): float32 the router, add the z-loss, init small. Pure bfloat16 diverges; float32 inside the router only costs ~nothing. Router z-loss (c_z=0.001) stabilized 3/3 seeds with a slight quality gain while every alternative (clipping, dropout, de-GEGLU-ing) paid quality for stability. Init scale ×0.1 cuts seed variance 0.68 → 0.01. Top-2, capacity factor 1.25, ≤1 expert per core is the published Pareto point. [T1]
  • Don't plan around a "domain expert." Two labs independently find routers learn syntax and position, not topics — near-uniform expert selection across content domains, no French expert, no math expert. Extracting "the code expert" for deployment, or fine-tuning one expert for your domain, has nothing in the routing to support it. [T1]
  • Need the quality but can't pay the memory? Distill to dense — and expect to keep ~30%. Initialize the dense student from the sparse teacher's non-expert weights, 0.75/0.25 hard/soft loss: ~30% of the sparse gain survives at 95% compression, 28% at 99%. An honest ceiling: most of what sparsity bought does not densify. [T1]

The trade, named

MoE buys pretraining speed and per-token serving compute with serving memory, fine-tuning fragility, and systems complexity (balance losses, capacity factors, dropped tokens, expert parallelism's load-balancing against measured route locality). It is the right trade for the labs pretraining flagships — which is why the open flagships are MoE — and a conditional trade for a practitioner: you inherit the quality-per-active-FLOP win at inference, but you pay the total-params RAM bill on your own hardware, and the fine-tuning craft on top of it is one graded source deep.

Hold loosely

  • The Google trio shares authors and codebases — lineage, not independent replication. The independent replications (Mistral, DeepSeek) don't disclose their training recipes; no neutral third party has trained or compared these architectures. [T1, provenance caveat]
  • Mixtral's benchmark table is Mistral's own re-runs; its Elo is a Dec 2023 snapshot; MT-Bench is GPT-4-judged (evaluation-contamination's biases apply).
  • Everything about MoE × LoRA, MoE × quantization (per-expert behavior, rare-expert calibration coverage, shared-expert precision), and MoE × llama.cpp/Ollama is unmeasured in-vault — and those three cells are precisely where this vault's premise (fine-tune, quantize, run locally) meets this architecture. The map flags them as its next sources.
  • The frontier recipe has already moved past what's graded here: aux-loss-free balancing and expert-choice routing (the DeepSeek-V3-generation refinements) are cited but not ingested — as of 2026-07 this article describes the established MoE craft, one generation behind the newest.
