Select, don't collect: the state of data curation

Synthesized 2026-07-25 from data-curation, the map's first article, written the run the map moved emerging → consolidating. It held at emerging since 2026-07-17 on a single source, because that source measured pretraining corpora and this vault's readers curate fine-tuning sets. The gap closed this run — and it closed with a result that agrees with the pretraining one closely enough to state as a single rule. Every number here has a date; this field's defaults rot in months.

The claim

Data curation is the half of the craft where a practitioner has the most leverage and the least published evidence. You cannot outspend a frontier lab on compute. You can out-select them on a narrow target, and as of 2026-07 there are two controlled studies — one at each end of the pipeline — that say what selection is worth and, more usefully, what it is not worth.

The through-line is a single sentence that now holds at 15 trillion pretraining tokens and at one thousand instruction examples: quality and diversity are the levers, and volume saturates.

That is worth stating carefully, because it is the opposite of the default instinct and because the two studies reached it independently. FineWeb (Hugging Face, NeurIPS 2024 D&B) and LIMA (Meta AI et al., NeurIPS 2023) share no authors, no scale, no pipeline stage, and no methodology beyond the one thing that matters: both trained the variants and measured, and both released the ablations. [T1, two groups]

What to do

Ablate your data choices instead of arguing about them. This is the method claim underneath everything else and it is the reason both sources are gradable at all. FineWeb's contribution is not a corpus, it is a corpus plus datatrove plus the proxy models that justify each filtering decision. The way to know whether a dedup setting or a quality filter helps is to train identical small models on each variant and read the downstream benchmark. That is affordable at practitioner scale in a way most of this vault is not. [T1]

Expect the intuitive move to invert — it has, twice now. FineWeb found that aggressive global cross-dump deduplication hurts: it strips so much that quality drops, and per-dump dedup wins because surviving across dumps is itself a weak quality signal. LIMA found that scaling quality-filtered examples 2K → 4K → 8K → 16K → 32K leaves generation quality flat at ~3.8 on a 1–6 scale — a 16-fold increase in data buying nothing measurable. Two defaults ("dedup as hard as possible," "more data is better") inverted by controlled evidence at opposite ends of the pipeline. [T1]

Spend a fixed curation budget on filtering and prompt diversity, not on volume. LIMA separates the three variables on a 7B model with matched hyperparameters. Quality is worth +0.50: 2,000 filtered Stack Exchange examples score 3.83 against 2,000 unfiltered at 3.33. Diversity is worth +0.34: filtered Stack Exchange (heterogeneous prompts) scores 3.83 against wikiHow at 3.49 — where wikiHow's responses are equally excellent and only the prompts are homogeneous, every one a "how to" question. Holding response quality fixed, prompt heterogeneity is independently valuable. [T1]

Steal the filters; they are unglamorous and they earn half a point. LIMA's Stack Exchange rules, in full: sample at temperature τ=3 across 75 STEM and 99 other exchanges for uniform domain coverage; take top-scoring self-contained questions; require an answer score ≥10; drop answers under 1,200 or over 4,096 characters, written in the first person, or referencing other answers; strip links, images and HTML while retaining code blocks and lists. Nothing there requires a research team. [T1]

On the pretraining side, a learned quality filter beats scale. A small classifier trained on LLM-rated "educational value" yields FineWeb-Edu at 1.3T tokens, whose models show large gains on MMLU and ARC against models trained on the unfiltered 15T. Selection over an order of magnitude more data. Caveat the filter honestly: "educational value" is an LLM-labeled proxy with its own biases, and what it systematically excludes — and whether that narrows the model — is unmeasured. [T1]

Assume alignment needs less data than you think, and structure it accordingly. LIMA's headline is the existence proof: LLaMa 65B, 1,000 curated examples (~750,000 tokens), plain supervised loss, no RLHF and no reward model. Against 300 test prompts in a human preference study it is equal-or-preferred to GPT-4 in 43% of cases, Bard in 58%, and RLHF-trained DaVinci003 in 65% — and it beats Alpaca-65B, the same base model trained on 52× more data. Its own absolute assessment is more sober than the win rates: 50% Excellent, 38% Pass, 12% outright Fail. [T1]

If you need multi-turn, 30 examples is the whole budget. Zero-shot, LIMA breaks within three turns in 6 of 10 conversations. Adding 30 hand-written dialogue chains takes excellent responses from 45.2% to 76.1% and failures from 1-per-42-turns to 1-per-46. The appendix reports that removing six examples of a task can break structured generation entirely. Treat capability coverage as a checklist with single-digit counts per item, not as a volume problem. [T1]

Before generating synthetic data, look for a channel where the ground truth already exists. This is Molmo's move and it generalizes past vision: annotators speak for 60–90 seconds per image and a language-only model that never sees the image transcribes and cleans the result (712k images, ~1.3M captions); PixMo-Docs generates charts from code and derives the Q&A from the generating code rather than the rendered image. A different modality, a reversed direction, or a human act that is cheap in the right format will often beat a teacher model. [T1]

Decontaminate on the supply side if you can, and screen on the demand side if you cannot. FineWeb removes benchmark n-grams before release, which is available to you for your own fine-tuning mix. For the base model you did not pretrain, Min-K% Prob recovers membership from the model alone at AUC 0.72 — a prior, not a verdict. The asymmetry is structural: almost every model this vault's readers fine-tune is one they did not build, so the applicable tool is the weaker one. The screen works markedly better on your own data (AUC 0.86 for deliberately inserted examples), which is exactly where you would use it. [T1]

Why it works, as far as anyone can say

LIMA's proposed mechanism is the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis: knowledge and capabilities are learned almost entirely during pretraining, and alignment teaches only which subdistribution of response formats to use. The multi-turn result is the cleanest evidence for it — a model trained on zero dialogue examples can converse at all, which is only explicable if the ability was already present and merely needed invoking.

That hypothesis is doing real work elsewhere in the vault, and it is the most useful thing this map exports. peft-methods has been contested since 2026-07-18 over a conditional it could state but not explain: low-rank adapters match full fine-tuning on task adaptation and underperform on genuine domain acquisition, so 4-bit LoRA is the right opening move and an explicit bet that your target is a task, not a body of knowledge. The Superficial Alignment Hypothesis is that bet restated as a claim about where knowledge lives. Two findings — one about parameters, one about data — arrive from opposite directions at the same boundary, and neither paper cites the other. [T1, two groups]

It also yields a prediction nobody has tested: the targets where LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning should be the targets where curated instruction data underperforms continued pretraining. If that correlation holds, it is the a-priori test for "does this target need a high-rank update" that the vault has called its sharpest open item.

What is contested or unresolved

Two sources disagree about where fine-tuning data should come from, and nobody has priced the choice. LLaVA manufactures a corpus with a blind teacher — language-only GPT-4 given captions and bounding boxes produced 158K multimodal instruction samples sufficient to train a working assistant. Molmo argues the whole teacher-distillation lineage costs the community its foundational knowledge and collects from humans instead, reaching the top of an 11-benchmark suite. No source compares them at matched cost, which is the only form the question takes for a practitioner. Molmo proves feasibility, not superiority, and its protocol needs an annotation operation most readers cannot run.

"More data doesn't help" is not yet established in the form you need it. LIMA's quantity ablation scales from 2K to 32K examples sampled from one source under one filter. What it shows is that more of the same distribution saturates. The move a practitioner actually makes is adding a different distribution — and this map's own diversity result predicts that would help. Those two findings are in tension at exactly the boundary where you would act on them, and the experiment that separates them is cheap: hold example count fixed, vary the number of distinct sources. Unrun.

Distillation and curation win on different targets, and the distinction is easy to lose. LIMA beats Alpaca-65B, a distilled set 52× its size. DeepSeek-R1 shows distilled reasoning traces beating direct RL decisively — 72.6% vs 47.0% AIME at 32B. Both are T1 and they are not in conflict: reasoning traces have a verifiable checker, so the teacher supplies something the student cannot generate and the ground truth is cheap. Alignment style has no checker, and the teacher's advantage is much thinner. Working rule, stated as a conditional because that is all the evidence supports: distill when the target has a checker; curate when it does not.

The synthetic-data ablation has never been run. LLaVA shows a corpus can be manufactured and reports only that it worked — no ablation of generation choices, no measurement of what share of the teacher's hallucinations the student inherits. The instrument now exists at both pipeline stages and has never been pointed at a generated corpus. That is the most obvious missing study in this map.

Watch the instruments, including this article's. LIMA's most load-bearing numbers — the +0.50 and +0.34 deltas — are graded by GPT-3.5-turbo on a 1–6 Likert scale, and evaluation-contamination's judge discount applies in full. Its main results run against 2023-vintage baselines (DaVinci003, Bard, Claude-v1, GPT-4-April), all since retired, so read the win rates as historical. 230 of its 300 test prompts were written by the same authors who wrote 200 training examples, and the paper concedes "significant contact between the groups" produced shared priors it does not quantify. FineWeb's ablations are validated at small proxy scale, and whether the ranking of curation choices holds at large-model training is assumed rather than shown. The two studies agree, and the agreement is the strongest thing on this map — but neither has been independently reproduced.

The shortest version

As of 2026-07: ablate, don't argue. Filter hard, diversify your prompts, and stop collecting. The evidence that volume saturates is now two studies at opposite ends of the pipeline, and the evidence that filtering and diversity pay is quantified at both. What nobody has measured is the thing you will actually want next — teacher synthesis against human collection at equal cost, and whether adding a genuinely new source escapes the saturation LIMA measured within one.
