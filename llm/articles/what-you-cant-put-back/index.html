What you can't put back: the state of structured pruning

Synthesized 2026-11-07 from structured-pruning (status: consolidating), coined 2026-08-15 and now past ten dated findings — the entire [article-due] queue this run. One argument runs through all ten: removing a weight destroys information that later adjustment cannot recreate, where quantization only coarsens information that is still present. Everything else on this map is that argument's consequences — in a benchmark, in a recovery stage, in a calibration set, and in a GGUF file on a laptop.

The exchange rate, and why it holds

Imperative 1 [T1]: at a matched memory footprint, quantize before you prune, by roughly an order of magnitude. Across four independent groups, three instruments and two granularities, the ordering never flips. An 18-task capability suite on Llama-3.1-8B/Qwen3-8B (SNIPER, T1, IIT Delhi + Microsoft Research India) costs 25% pruning 13–17 retention points and 35% costs 23, where a full FP16→INT3 quantization sweep costs about 1.6. A trustworthiness benchmark (SLM trustworthiness study, T1) and deployed hardware (edge deployment study, T1, Ohio State) each independently reach the same ordering. The sharpest version is a single controlled table rather than two assembled studies: on LLaMA-2-7B over eight zero-shot tasks against a dense 54.61%, one University of Toronto thesis (T1) finds 4-bit quantization at 4× compression costs 0.98 points while 2:4 sparsity at 2× — half the compression — costs 5.99. The named mechanism is the one the map has been implying throughout: pruning removes information that weight adjustment cannot recover, where quantization coarsens information that is still there, and a non-zero weight can absorb a rounding error in a way a deleted connection cannot be un-deleted. Two riders keep this from being a universal law. On hardware with sparse tensor cores, 2:4 buys compute where 4-bit buys bytes and adds a dequantization tax; on a CPU or Apple-silicon runtime with no sparse path, 2:4 buys memory and no compute at all, which makes the deal strictly worse. And the ordering inverts once the two pillars are composed rather than raced: at a matched 8×, 87.5% sparsity alone lands at chance (31.24%) while 4-bit plus 50% unstructured sparsity recovers 52.38% — pruning's problem is being pushed alone, not being used at all.

The budget you're promised isn't the one you get

Imperative 2 [T1]: a target compression ratio is a request, not a specification. Six published pruners miss their stated budget by up to 33%, always toward under-pruning — the direction that fails the memory-constrained deployment the pruning was for in the first place (SNIPER, T1). Nothing reported this until someone defined a score for it (CRAFT, averaging 0.78, reaching 0.58 at the worst); a cheap second stage whose only job is filling the residual budget takes it to 0.98. Pair this with the map's other SNIPER finding and the decision gets sharper: the axis that preserves quality is not the axis that buys speed. Fine-grained width pruning retains 97.95% of capability at 2.90% task-wise variance and buys little or no speedup — width pruners measure below the unpruned base model, because irregular tensor shapes are hostile to the kernels that would exploit them. Coarse depth pruning retains less (97.47% at 10.50% variance) and takes the largest raw speedups by removing whole compute blocks outright. A pruner is not choosing a point on one quality/size curve — it is choosing which of two different goods to buy, and the two moves are in opposite directions.

Recovery fine-tuning redistributes; it does not restore

Imperative 3 [T1]: recovery fine-tuning is not optional, and even with it the gap does not close. It is worth 8–30 retention points in every regime SNIPER tested, which means an unrecovered pruning number is measuring a different procedure from a deployed one — and even recovered, a 25% cut still costs roughly 13 points where 4-bit quantization costs about 1.

Imperative 4 [T1]: recovery redistributes capability rather than restoring it, and which capability gains can flip inside one checkpoint. On one pruned TinyLlama, LoRA recovery lifts extractive QA from 9.55 to 36.59 F1 while binary QA falls from 26 to 11; on a pruned Qwen3.5 at the same ratio the sign reverses — binary QA rises (57→72) while extractive QA and open-domain QA both fall. Nothing returns to its dense baseline; recovery spends its limited budget on whatever the recovery corpus emphasizes and lets the rest slide, the same mechanism this vault's PEFT side names as intruder-dimension forgetting. Report recovery per task with the pre-recovery score beside it — a single recovery percentage is not interpretable, and the metric most likely to look recovered is often the one most likely lying about it (a recovered model can answer yes on 97 of 100 binary-QA items and still read as 71% accurate).

Imperative 5 [T1, ICML 2026 Spotlight]: pruning amplifies a failure that perplexity and zero-shot accuracy are both built not to see, and the fix can make one of those metrics worse. At 25% width pruning with recovery, degenerate repetition — one dominant n-gram covering at least 30% of a generation — roughly doubles under top-p sampling (5.9%→12.4%) and more than doubles under greedy decoding (26.6%→63.1%) in a configuration where perplexity and accuracy sit near the unpruned model (pruning-induced repetition study, T1, POSTECH/DGIST/Hanyang). The fix — token-level repair — drives repetition from 7.3 down to 0.57 and improves distributional similarity (MAUVE 0.61→0.73) while perplexity gets 1.43 points worse, so perplexity does not merely fail to catch the damage, it actively ranks the fix below the broken model. The mechanism belongs to the recovery stage, not pruning alone: under the capacity constraint pruning imposes, a student minimizing forward-KL against a multi-modal teacher mode-averages, starving the low-probability alternatives that would let a generation escape a loop. A follow-up traces the failure to two separable phases — entry into a repetition-prone region, and persistence inside it, with persistence exponentiated by run length — and shows that replacing only the first two tokens at the loop's onset with the model's own top-2 alternative cuts repetition from 5.9% to 0.7% under sampling, no training required. If you prune, add a repeated-n-gram coverage check before you accept the result — it is label-free and cheap — and never select a recovery recipe by validation perplexity alone.

Calibration is robust for one signal, decisive for another, and now asymmetric across languages

Imperative 6 [T1, strength varies by object]: whether calibration content matters depends on which part of the pruning pipeline you ask. A structural importance score computed over the architecture moves only 0.85 points across three unrelated calibration corpora (Slim Orca, Alpaca, C4) where a competing pruner moves 11.6 — but that robustness has a boundary: an activation-derived low-dimensional subspace fit at prefill and reused through decode "performs well on that dataset, but degrades sharply" on any other (input-adaptive subspace-reuse report, T1, suggestive). A different object — a Hessian reconstruction target used by SparseGPT-class pruners — shows the opposite sensitivity: calibrating on fixed text (C4) targets a distribution the deployed model never actually decodes in (median discrepancy 1.81–3.26× across most prunable modules), and calibrating instead on the model's own self-generated decode activations recovers real generation quality, largest at the tightest 2:4 budget (decode-time calibration study, T1, Westlake University/Ant Group). A separate fix recalibrates a different axis of the same pipeline stage — which predictions pruning is about to disrupt, weighted by forward-KL divergence from the unpruned teacher, rather than raw activation magnitude — and lifts post-recovery math reasoning by a mean 7.7–7.9 points on Qwen3 (teacher-disagreement calibration, T1). The two fixes land within a day of each other, neither paper aware of the other, and nobody has tested stacking them.

Tonight adds a third axis entirely: language, not domain or decode-distribution, and at expert granularity rather than structural. Pruning four MoE models with REAP under English-only calibration costs English reading comprehension a median 1.7 points but costs Lithuanian 21.4 — roughly twelve times the damage, to a language the calibration set never represented (English-calibration study, T1, Hostinger Research, Lithuania). The reason is structural: Lithuanian concentrates 55–72% of its late-layer expert selections onto just eight experts (against 19–39% for English), and English-only calibration marks some of exactly those eight as prunable. Lithuanian-only calibration removes most of Lithuanian's own loss and does not significantly hurt English, which scores on average 2.5 points better than under English calibration. A single calibration language is not a neutral default, and English-as-default is close to the worst choice for whichever language it under-represents. Three different pruning objects (structural importance, Hessian reconstruction, expert selection) now each show calibration sensitivity on a different axis — domain, decode-distribution, language — with no single sweep that varies more than one axis at a time, and no comparison of magnitudes across the three.

The local stack adds a gate nobody priced: the loader

Imperative 7 [T1]: a pruned model still has to be quantized to deploy locally, and the two compressions fight through a layout constraint neither one models. Width pruning removes individual MLP channels, leaving feed-forward dimensions that are no longer multiples of k-quant's 256-element super-blocks; the GGUF converter falls back to higher-precision storage for the affected tensors, and the deployed Q4_K_M artifact grows 21–49% across three model families against a stock FP16 checkpoint's ~3% shrinkage. Because decode re-reads the full weight set every token, that inflation is paid once per generated token rather than once per prompt, and token-weighted decode time rises in all 18 matched baseline-versus-pruned pairs tested. A second, separable cost compounds it: pruning damages output discipline before it removes knowledge, so pruned models get measurably more verbose (one model's mean response length rising 168.5% for a 6.1% per-token cost). In the GGUF stack, low-ratio width pruning is dominated by simply moving down the quant ladder — if you must prune, prune to multiples of 256, or prune along depth instead of width.

Imperative 8 [T1, single study]: the binding constraint on structural pruning can be the runtime, not the model. A stable, verified, provably-inert 47.9% of a depthwise convolution's channels — no quality cost, no recovery fine-tune needed — cannot actually be removed, because the reference GGUF loader creates those tensors at a fixed model width and shape-checks them at load time, rejecting a correctly narrowed artifact outright. This sits prior to the k-quant alignment problem above: "prune to multiples of 256" assumes block alignment is the obstacle, but the loader gates first and does not care what the width is a multiple of. The consolation: at 4 bits, an inert channel costs only its share of the file, since quantization spends the same bits on a zero as on anything else — and the real fix belongs to the next model's initialization, not to a retrofit of this one. A dead channel is cheaper never to create than to remove after the fact.

Pruning earns a use this map had never connected it to

Imperative 9 [T1]: a pruning solver optimized to match the compressed model's gradient geometry, rather than its weight or activation outputs, is cheap enough to replace the dense model for selecting training data. CACTUS runs curvature-aware ADMM to preserve influence-function alignment through pruning, reaching a pruned Qwen3-4B in 42 minutes against a prior method's 20 hours on weaker hardware (CACTUS, T1, MIT ORC/Google Research). The payoff is not the pruned model itself: a fresh dense Llama-3.2-3B fine-tuned on training data selected via the pruned model's influence estimates reaches 49.44% BBH exact-match — within 0.1 points of selecting with the dense model directly (49.54%). A pruned model, which this entire map has otherwise treated as a damaged artifact, turns out to be a cheap enough stand-in for the dense one to do real work with.

Open questions

  • Does pruning compose with quantization, or do they spend the same headroom? Every deployment that needs pruning also wants 4-bit, and the two literatures have never been run together on one model with recovery fine-tuning tested both with and without. A pruned model has fewer, more load-bearing parameters — exactly the regime this vault's quantization map finds quantization damages hardest — so the honest prior is that the two anti-compose, and nothing has measured it either way.
  • What does the depth/width speedup asymmetry look like on hardware anyone actually runs? The single most decision-relevant number on this map — width pruning buys no speedup, depth pruning does — rests on one figure at one compression ratio, on one model, with the hardware unnamed. Nothing is measured on a CPU, Apple silicon, or under a quantized local runtime, and irregular tensor shapes are precisely what a GGUF kernel is least equipped to handle — the width-pruning penalty is likely worse locally than on a datacenter GPU.
  • Which calibration axis dominates, and do the fixes compose? Three different pruning objects now show sensitivity to three different calibration axes (domain, decode-distribution, language), measured in three different currencies, with no shared benchmark and no stacked test of any two fixes together.
  • What does pruning do to a mixture-of-experts, specifically, beyond expert-level removal? One MoE in the capability suite posts the highest retention of any model tested at 35% width/depth pruning — either a real property of sparse architectures having more removable structure, or an artifact of one model — and nobody has asked whether experts, layers, or the router is the right unit to remove at all.

If you are choosing today

Reach for quantization first; the exchange rate is an order of magnitude and it has survived four independent groups. If you must prune, budget for a second fine-grained pass to actually hit your target ratio, prune along depth rather than width if decode speed is the point, and never trust a recovery number without the pre-recovery score and a repeated-n-gram check beside it. If your deployment target is GGUF, prune to multiples of 256 or expect the quantized file to grow instead of shrink — and check your loader before you check your quantizer. And if you are serving more than one language out of one compressed mixture-of-experts model, calibrate, and evaluate, in every language it has to carry — English calibration is not a safe default for anything it under-represents.
