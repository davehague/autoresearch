The draft is free; the check is not: the state of speculative decoding

Synthesized 2026-08-12 from speculative-decoding (status: consolidating), four days after the map forked out of local-inference and on the night it crossed ten dated findings. The core bargain here is replicated and assumed; the arguments are all about what the bargain costs. As of 2026-08 every throughput number below is attached to a specific card, a specific attention backend, a specific sampling temperature and a specific context length — and the map's recurring lesson is that at least one of those four is missing from almost every published figure, including some quoted here.

The claim

Speculative decoding proposes several tokens cheaply, then checks them in one forward pass of the model you actually wanted to run, accepting the longest prefix that survives. The founding result (Leviathan, Kalman & Matias, Google, ICML 2023) is that the check can be arranged so the emitted text is drawn from exactly the target model's distribution. Not approximately — exactly. You spend arithmetic that was idle anyway on a memory-bound decode, and you get 2–3× with nothing traded away.

That property is why this is a separate map from every other efficiency lever in the vault. Quantization trades bits for quality, cache compression trades memory for fidelity, distillation trades a model for a smaller one. Speculation, done losslessly, trades nothing you can measure in the output.

Everything below is about the two words doing the work: cheaply and one forward pass. Both turn out to be assumptions rather than facts, and both have been falsified in the last three weeks — not the exactness guarantee, which holds, but the economics wrapped around it.

What is settled: seven imperatives

1. Check your speculation length against the inversion point, because there is one. Write the speedup in closed form (SparseSpec, T1): S = (αk+1)/(kγ+1) where α is acceptance rate, k the speculation length, and γ = C_d/C_v the ratio of draft cost to verify cost. Then ∂S/∂k = (α−γ)/(kγ+1)², so the derivative's sign is the sign of α − γ. Acceptance decays as the horizon grows, so every fixed speculation length is a bet that expires, and past the crossing point speculating further is slower than not speculating at all. Measured: MagicDec peaks at 1.74× at k=2 and reads 0.36× at k=16 as acceptance falls 33.5% → 6.3%. [T1]

2. Do not trust a downloaded drafter on your workload. This is the same finding's uncomfortable half, and it is the most practically important thing on the map. A trained drafter's acceptance rate is a property of the distribution it was trained on. The official EAGLE-3 checkpoint reads ~67.2% acceptance on the dataset in its own evaluation and 1.95% on long-context work, at 0.89× — measurably slower than plain autoregressive decoding. LayerSkip under its official configuration reads 0.80× even on normal-length inputs. Since practitioners download drafters rather than train them, the default position is structurally the failure regime. Measure acceptance on your own traffic before you believe any speedup. [T1]

3. Prefer a drafter with no training distribution to be out of. Self-speculation sidesteps imperative 2 entirely: draft with the target model itself over a sparsified but never-evicted KV cache, scored from attention statistics the previous verification pass already produced, and verify against the dense original — 72.9% acceptance, 11.26 accepted tokens per step, 2.79× in the cell where EAGLE-3 reads 0.89×, with an entropy controller picking k per step for under 0.5% of step latency. [T1] Bounded: one A40, batch size 1, contexts to 64K, generations capped at 128 tokens — short for the reasoning workloads that would amortize speculation best — a deliberately unfused dense verifier that inflates C_v for every arm, and no quantized base anywhere.

4. Run lossless, or report the verification rule with every number you publish. The "lossy verification" relaxations now shipping spend distributional fidelity: truncation schemes emit the draft's distribution restricted to an allowed set, collaborative ones emit a blend (Revisiting Lossy Verification, T1). Damage is under a point under plain single-draft speculation and 6.28 / 5.29 / 8.82 / 4.90 points (MATH / MBPP+ / INCLUDE / BFCL) under EAGLE-3 tree verification — the cost is set by a second, unrelated configuration choice. The trap is that block efficiency and tokens/second improve in every one of these configurations by construction, so self-benchmarking cannot see the failure: the fastest cell in that study (block efficiency 9.15) is its worst model, 50.26 Pass@1 against a lossless 75.84. [T1]

5. If you do relax exactness, meter it as a budget rather than a per-token tolerance. The one relaxation isolated as worth having is a cap on where the draft severely overestimates the target (≤0.7 Pass@1 for a small block-efficiency gain); the adaptive interpolation bundled with it in shipping implementations costs 25 points. The better-controlled version (approximate speculative decoding, T1) relaxes the greedy argmax rule under three simultaneous constraints — a regret gate normalized by remaining proposal length, a per-block exception cap, and a persistent request-level regret budget carried across rounds. B = 0 reproduces strict greedy verification exactly, so it is a continuum with the lossless case at one end. Price: +3.05% to +15.26% fixed-workload throughput (mean +7.78% on Qwen3-14B across seven tasks) for at most −1.52 accuracy points, with HumanEval (−0.61) and MT-Bench (−0.64) the two tasks that regress. The authors state the controls must be re-audited per target–drafter–workload combination: safety is not transferable. [T1]

6. Quote an acceptance rate with the sampling temperature you measured it at. Block drafters emit b positions as independent marginals while verification checks them sequentially conditioned on what was accepted. That is invisible under greedy decoding and degrades monotonically as the target's sampling entropy rises. Correlating the positions with a low-rank categorical mixture (DBLAST, T1) recovers +5.6–5.8% accepted length at T=0.7 and +9.3–12.1% at T=1.5, for 1.4–3.4% per-iteration latency, with the verification rule untouched. An acceptance rate quoted without its sampling configuration is underspecified. [T1]

7. The tree width and the verification budget both have interior optima, and your default is not at them. Re-scoring a semi-autoregressive drafter's refinement head per concrete parent turns a chain into a tree on the same checkpoint with no retraining and no extra backbone pass (PCTree, T1): mean accepted length 9.41 → 11.16, autoregressive speedup 6.14× → 6.60×, +3.1% to +29.5% across three sizes and nine benchmarks, with a three-way isolation attributing +9.1% specifically to per-parent conditioning rather than to having a tree. Speedup peaks at N=32 for a 7-token block and N=64 for 16, and the hardware-efficient width is 128 rows on an A100 against 256 on a GB10. [T1]

The load-bearing correction: verification is not one forward pass

This is the map's most consequential structural finding and it arrived on 2026-08-09.

"Verifying γ+1 candidates costs about one target forward pass" is not a property of speculation. It is a property of the KV cache — the only backbone memory structure that is append-only and branch-independent. Replace part of it and the assumption dies:

  • Hybrid attention. A recurrent linear-attention state is path-dependent, so each candidate branch implies a different one. On the hybrid stacks now shipping (three gated-DeltaNet layers per full-attention layer), a naive tree walk replays the recurrence per branch — serial recurrence climbing from 4% to 27% of the target forward as the tree grows 8 → 64 nodes — and snapshots a full state per proposal node, 36 GiB for eight 32-node requests on a 122B model (Bole, T1).
  • Offloaded mixture-of-experts. The same failure in a different currency: on an offloaded MoE the binding cost is expert movement, 87% of profiled decode time on an RTX 4090 and 49% on a Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU under llama.cpp Q4_0 (DraftExpert, T1, heavily discounted — single author, no affiliation, no venue, no code). Verifying K candidates touches the union of their experts, not one token's worth.

So the general form: the cost of verifying a draft is a property of the backbone's memory structure, not of the draft. Both architectures the field is moving to violate the assumption.

It is repairable, expensively. Bole derives an exact closed form for the gated delta rule over a tree, takes its inverse as a finite Neumann series because depth is bounded, and stores branches as token-scale factors instead of states: 3.4–7.7× on the verification kernel, 82–99× less transient state, up to 4.72× autoregressive decode throughput, at essentially unchanged accepted length — this is systems work, not drafting. Two riders matter more than the headline. The gains are largest on the slower machine (4.72× on a 273 GB/s GB10 against 3.62× on an A100), the first speculation result in-vault that improves as hardware becomes more bandwidth-bound. And the saved memory is paid to the prefix cache: hit rate 58–70% → 91–93% on an agent workload, TTFT down 67.6%, TPOT down 49.9%, with no change to the drafter. On the offloaded-MoE side, sizing the verifier's expert set rather than accepting the union is worth 2.06× throughput (AcceptMoE, T1).

Where the drafting side landed: two bounds from one night

2026-08-12 supplied both ends of the drafting axis, and read together they say the same thing.

Making the drafter perfect is worth little. A nested suite trains 500M ⊂ 1.5B ⊂ 3B end-to-end, so the draft model is literally contained in the verifier — trained jointly, on the same data, in the same run, with no alignment gap that can be improved on and no extra resident memory (Matryoshka LM Suites, T1). It is worth 14–26% throughput. Against 2–3× for ordinary lossless speculation, a structurally ideal drafter buys a modest increment.

Making the drafter free is worth a lot. A diffusion drafter emits a whole block in parallel, so the marginal cost of the twentieth draft token is roughly that of the fifth. Every dynamic-length method in this literature picks k by estimating how many tokens will be accepted — correct only while each extra token costs another drafter pass. When drafting is parallel the question becomes how many generated tokens are worth verifying, and the criterion is marginal: extend only while acceptance gain exceeds added verification cost (LibraSpec, T1). Training-free, provably convergent, and lossless: 2.1×–3.5× typical over autoregressive decoding on Qwen2.5-7B (MATH-500 3.54× at 5.11 mean accepted length; MT-Bench 2.39× at 3.43), up to 8.49× in the best cell, across six targets and three base methods.

Both point the same way, and it is the article's title. Drafting was not the constraint; checking is. Remove drafter–target mismatch entirely and you gain a quarter at most. Remove drafting cost entirely and the whole optimization becomes a verification-budget problem — which is exactly what the previous section found from the other direction.

There is a bonus finding worth carrying. A diffusion model used as a decoder pays for parallel emission in accuracy — −5.0 MMLU Pro but −19.2 AIME against the autoregressive parent it was converted from (DiffusionGemma, T2, Google DeepMind, open weights). Used as a drafter it pays nothing, because the target verifies. The reason is neat: committing many positions in parallel bets they do not depend on each other, which fails worst on long dependency chains — and those are precisely the tokens a verifier rejects. The failure mode and the rejection criterion select for the same thing, so the quality cost converts into a throughput cost.

What is contested, and stated as tension

Whether speculation and quantization stack, on hardware you own. Both trade arithmetic for memory traffic against the same decode step, so they contend for one headroom budget (SpecMQuant, Harbin Institute of Technology / Tsinghua, T1, code released). Worse, γ is a ratio: anything that makes verification cheaper — a fused kernel, quantized weights — raises γ and moves the inversion point closer, shrinking the useful horizon. Optimizing your verifier partially disarms your speculator, and nobody has measured the pair. One partial answer exists: the fragility of quantized drafting belongs to quantizing a single-layer draft head, not a real model — a W4A16 8B drafting for a W4A16 70B reaches τ = 5.28, above every FP16-headed EAGLE-2 configuration (3.25–3.81) — but that number is an A100 and was never run on the 3090 in the same paper.

Whether any of this survives to a consumer card. This is the map's largest unresolved gap and it is a gap in the evidence, not a disagreement in it. Nearly every number above is batch size 1 on a datacenter or workstation GPU: A100s at 1K context (LibraSpec), an L20 and eight H20s (regret budget), an H20 with an unquantized 4B (PCTree), one A40 with 128-token generations (SparseSpec). The two results that do reach edge hardware point in opposite directions — expert movement dominating decode on an RTX 4090 and a Snapdragon NPU, against 56.3% latency and 44.1% energy reduction from a hardware–software co-design (EdgeXpert, T1) that a reader cannot buy. The one encouraging signal is Bole's: gains grew on the slower machine, which is what the currency test predicts and what nobody else has checked.

Whether the exactness guarantee is worth defending in practice. The map's position is that it is — imperative 4 — but the honest tension is that lenient verification is shipping in real implementations, its cheap variant costs under a point, and its expensive variant costs 25. There is no principled way yet to know which one your stack is running short of reading the code, and safety is explicitly not transferable across target–drafter–workload combinations.

The unrun experiments, in priority order

  1. Speculation × quantization on one consumer card. A 4-bit target, a 4-bit drafter, one fused attention backend, batch size 1, acceptance and end-to-end tokens/s at two context lengths. Every ingredient is public. It would settle whether the vault's two most-recommended local levers compose or cancel, and the closed-form γ argument predicts they partly cancel.
  2. Acceptance rate on your own traffic, before anything else. Not an experiment so much as a habit. Given imperative 2, a downloaded drafter is a bet on someone else's workload, and the measurement costs one afternoon.
  3. The three-way parallel-emission comparison. Autoregressive, diffusion-sampled, and diffusion-drafted-with-verification, scored on one checkpoint at matched accuracy, reporting tokens per forward pass rather than tokens per second. DiffusionGemma makes this unusually cheap because the released weights still decode autoregressively.
  4. A nested drafter's memory footprint, and whether nesting survives quantization. The 14–26% throughput number is the less interesting half; a drafter that costs no extra VRAM is the half that decides whether speculation is attempted at all on a 12 GB card. Nobody has quantized a nested suite, and the sub-models share weights, so a scale fitted for the largest is imposed on the smallest.

The one-line version

Speculative decoding still buys 2–3× for free, and the guarantee is real. But the draft is the cheap part: your speculation length has an expiry, your downloaded drafter is probably mistuned for your workload, and your verification does not cost one forward pass the moment your backbone stops being a plain KV cache. Measure acceptance on your own traffic, run lossless, and quote every number with its card, backend, temperature and context length.
