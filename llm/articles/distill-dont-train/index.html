Distill, don't train: the state of small-model reasoning distillation

Synthesized 2026-07-17, refreshed 2026-07-31, 2026-08-13 and again 2026-08-27 from distillation-small-models (status: consolidating). Trigger: drift — eight more dated through-line entries. The headline still survives, and it now has a third instrument and a localization: distillation's advantage lands on the rare tail of the difficulty distribution, which is not where a mean can see it. Three things changed underneath. Somebody finally ran the arm this article argues against — training a small model from scratch — and it won, until you read its corpus table. The on-policy stage now has a leash and a measured usable range on both sides. And the multi-teacher case turns out to be worse than a mergekit command unless you fix a budget bug nobody plots. This field's defaults rot in months; check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

To make a small open-weights model good at reasoning, do not run the hard training recipe on the small model. Take a large model that already has the capability, and distill its traces (or logits) into the small one by supervised fine-tuning. As of 2026-07 the vault holds this from two independent directions:

  • Controlled ablation — DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, Nature 2025, weights MIT) fine-tunes a Qwen-32B base on ~800k reasoning traces from its R1 teacher and hits 72.6% AIME 2024 — versus 47.0% for the same base trained with reasoning RL directly. The distilled 7B student reaches 55.5% AIME (GPT-4o-0513: 9.3%) and 92.8% MATH-500, out-scoring a 32B reasoner (QwQ-32B-Preview: 50.0%). Same base, distillation vs direct RL, distillation wins. [T1]
  • Production replication — the Qwen3 Technical Report (Qwen Team, Apache-2.0) builds its whole small-dense lineup (0.6B–14B) by strong-to-weak distillation from fully-trained flagship teachers (235B-A22B, 32B): off-policy on teacher outputs across thinking/non-thinking modes, plus on-policy logit matching. It reports higher Pass@1 and Pass@64 than running the full four-stage recipe per model — at ~1/10 the GPU hours. A second lab, a different base, outside competition math. [T2]

That convergence — an ablation and a shipping pipeline pointing the same way — is why the map sits at consolidating, and nothing since has moved it.

One correction to the second bullet, made 2026-08-13 and important enough to sit here rather than in a footnote: Qwen3's "higher Pass@1 and Pass@64" is a pipeline number — off-policy cold start then on-policy logit matching — and the report never separates the two stages. Read below for why that distinction turned out to carry the whole argument.

The two stages do different jobs, and only one of them adds capability

This is the newest thing here and the one most likely to change what you do.

Towards Understanding On-Policy Distillation through the Lens of Test-Time Scaling (T1, 2026-08-12) does the boring, expensive thing nobody had: it sweeps the sampling budget out to K = 1024 instead of stopping at 8 or 64. Take a Qwen3-1.7B base, distil it on-policy from a 4B GRPO teacher, and score both on AIME2024:

BudgetBaseOn-policy distilledTeacher
pass@14.2%12.0%24.2%
pass@3228.2%30.5%58.2%
pass@6435.0%33.6%—
pass@102470.0%53.3%76.7%

The curves cross at pass@64, and by pass@1024 the distilled student is 16.7 points below the checkpoint it was trained from. Meanwhile every average improves at every budget (avg@1024: 12.0% against 4.2%). At the problem level, mean accuracy on problems the model could already solve roughly doubles — and across all three student–teacher pairs tested, more previously solvable problems became unsolvable than the reverse.

Two things stop this from being a retraction, and both matter.

Off-policy distillation was run as a control and behaved correctly — higher pass@K and higher avg@K than the base at small and large budgets alike. So what is bounded is a stage, not the play this article is about. R1's result, which is trace SFT, is off-policy and untouched.

And it converges with the mechanism this article already carried. The 2026-04 dynamics study concluded that transfer follows distributional novelty; this one measures, from perplexity, that on-policy trajectories become more likely under the teacher while staying closer to the base distribution under the base scorer. Two groups, two instruments, no cross-citation, one statement: the on-policy objective redistributes probability mass inside the student's existing support. It can make the student reliable at what it can already reach. It cannot widen what it reaches.

The operating consequence is an ordering constraint rather than a preference: the off-policy stage is where new capability enters, so a budget-constrained run spends there first and treats the on-policy stage as sharpening. Skipping the cold start is not a cheaper version of the recipe — it is a different intervention with a different result.

For anyone running this locally, the direction is double-edged rather than simply bad. Local deployment samples at K = 1, which is the whole point, so the sampling-efficiency gain is real value to you and the capability loss is invisible in your deployment — right up until the workload acquires a retry loop, a best-of-N sampler or a verifier, all of which are standard in agentic use, at which point the base model would have served you better. Bounded: competition mathematics only, three configurations, one decoding temperature, students at 1.5–1.7B with no scale control at the sizes a 24 GB card would actually target, and no code release. [T1]

Somebody ran the counter-arm, and the answer is in the data table

The whole article rests on don't train it small. As of 2026-08-15 that arm has been run properly and it won — and the qualifier that saves the claim is not in the results.

Mimir v1 (T2, Danish Foundation Models; open weights, training framework released, corpus enumerated to the token) is a 1B model trained from scratch, no distillation loss and no teacher logits, that beats every same-class open model by 18–32 points on its English suite and 20–28 on Danish. Against the identical architecture at identical scale, data mixture the only difference, it gains +36.7% on math and code.

Then the corpus table: 7.81B of its 70.48B tokens (11.08%) are synthetic data generated by a 31B model and audited, plus 1.59B translated-and-audited. That is distillation routed through a dataset instead of through logits. So the claim narrows rather than breaks: this build path is teacher-free at the gradient, not teacher-free in the information. What it genuinely buys is a licensing story, freedom from hosting a teacher, and freedom from the teacher's tokenizer and context limit.

Read it with a hard discount — one model, one unusual architecture whose scaling its own authors call unstudied, two target languages, self-reported, and no ablation dropping the 11.08% synthetic fraction, which is the single experiment that would say how much of the result it carries. [T2, single study]

Meanwhile the incumbent ordering picked up a third independent instrument on 2026-08-14, and this one is about the cheap version of the build path. Trustworthiness of compressed versus pretrained small models (T1, IJCNN 2026) puts a quantized 1.5B at 63.86% overall against a from-scratch 0.5B at 51.17% at comparable tokens per second, winning ethics (78.63 vs 65.71), privacy (40.38 vs 27.56), robustness (67.34 vs 62.42) and fairness (69.07 vs 48.99). Build path first, size second — now replicated on capability, on cost, and on trustworthiness. Bounded: one family for the head-to-head, no 2026-era from-scratch small model in the cohort, and a floor nobody states — sub-1B models absorb quantization far worse. [T1]

And the advantage now has a location, which is the most useful refinement in this refresh. Phantom gains (T1, primary home evaluation-contamination) scores per problem rather than per mean against a frozen control: external distillation expands 8–11 problems on the rare tail; three self-training recipes expand 0–2 (p < 10⁻⁶), with the "it just gained more overall" alternative rejected at p < 10⁻⁸. On the ten problems the base model never reaches at all, it is inconclusive (p = 0.35). So distillation's edge is about where in the difficulty distribution the gain lands — and a difference of means cannot see it, which is probably why this argument stayed contested as long as it did. Scope: every damaged arm is iterated on the model's own outputs, so this is not a result about checker-verified RL. [T1]

The on-policy stage now has a leash, and both edges of its usable range are measured

The 2026-08-13 refresh could say the on-policy stage sharpens rather than adds. As of 2026-08-17 it can say what goes wrong and what to do about it.

SimpleOPD (T1, Shanghai AI Lab; seven students across five model families, same- and cross-tokenizer, code + weights released) distils from a 30B-A3B reasoning teacher that sustains 100K+ tokens of proof reasoning into ordinary instruct students, and direct on-policy distillation degenerates: average response length, truncation rate and repetition rate rise together, one student passing 40% truncation on AIME25 and 60% on AnswerBench with its score falling. The case studies are a correct answer followed by 972 exact self-check repeats, and a trace collapsing into a one-token loop.

Two fixes, and the second is the one to take away. Masking the loss on </think> and <|im_end|> lifts ProofBench@4 from 21.70 → 38.10 and does not stop the length growth. A student-reference KL loss — penalizing drift from the student's own initial policy, coefficient 0.5 — drives truncation to near zero and reaches 38.50. On a third student the coefficient shows an interior optimum (0.5 too small, 1.0 best, 1.2 too large), so the usable range of on-policy distillation is bounded on both sides and both edges have been measured. With both fixes the flagship student goes 21.70 → 44.50 ProofBench against the teacher's 45.00, and two students end above the teacher on AnswerBench and AIME25. Distillation length matters (6K → 32K: 38.80 → 44.50), and a stronger teacher from an unrelated family beat the in-house one at matched length — a small point against the novelty framing, or a large one about tokenizer diversity, and the paper does not separate them.

Discount it on four axes the authors leave open: the teacher and flagship student are the authors' own models, ProofBench is judge-scored, no compute, wall-clock or hardware figure appears anywhere, and the paper reports @4 and @8 averages only — leaving this article's central pass@K question untouched by a lab that was set up to answer it. [T1]

A second constraint arrived 2026-08-22 and it is a scope condition on the whole on-policy literature. GC-OPD (T1, Tsinghua; code released) measures, on frozen responses before any training, that the dense signal on-policy distillation learns from is teacher preference, not verified correctness — and that the two diverge as input length grows. Every on-policy result in this article was established at ordinary lengths. Its proposed fix is honest to the point of self-injury: raw 29.08 → on-policy distillation 39.31 → GC-OPD 40.47, so the distillation step is worth ~9 points and the calibration on top is worth +1.16, dropping to +0.97 against the strongest competing method — sub-point, single seed, no intervals, and eight 80 GB GPUs per run. What survives is the ablation, argued under identical conditions: an extra distillation-derived term is worth +0.04 (nothing), the direct verifier reward +0.63, and the signed teacher–verifier disagreement +1.10. Adding the verifier's opinion helps; adding its disagreement with the teacher helps nearly twice as much. [T1]

Multi-teacher is currently losing to a merge command

New 2026-08-21, and it is the most uncomfortable result in this refresh.

Open-MOPD (T1, Tsinghua AIR + ByteDance Seed; recipe, trajectories and eval suites open-sourced) consolidates three domain-expert RL teachers (math, code, instruction-following) into one student with oracle routing by ground-truth domain label, so routing error is removed by construction. It recovers 35.6% of the gap to an oracle router. Distilling each domain into its own student reaches 88.0% — so teachers, initialization and objective are each sufficient, and the score collapses specifically when three sufficient signals are combined.

The obvious explanation is tested and rejected: token-level teacher disagreement is not the bottleneck. What is measured instead is arithmetic. 99% of gradient tokens go to math and code and ~1% to instruction-following, because reasoning traces are structurally longer than instruction-following answers and a per-batch token quota cannot tell the difference. IF ends 6.16 points below its reference — 3.3× the math gap — and is the only domain whose score falls mid-training. Pin the weighted token share at 33.33% and you get +1.17 points "almost entirely from IF", which is what a budget-starvation story predicts and a conflict story does not.

Generalize past the paper, because the same bug is everywhere: any multi-source training signal weighted by token count is implicitly weighted by verbosity, and it is invisible unless somebody plots the per-domain token share — which is not a standard diagnostic.

And then the row the paper reports without comment: training-free task-arithmetic weight merging recovers 71.7% — twice naive distillation — while the fully repaired three-teacher recipe beats it by 0.78 total points after 300 steps on eight A100s, on top of having trained the three experts first. So as of 2026-08 naive multi-teacher distillation is worse than a mergekit command, and the repaired version restores parity rather than winning. Any multi-teacher result published without a merge baseline is unevaluated. Bounded: one 3B student. [T1]

If you are chaining compressions, supervise every step from the original

New 2026-08-24, and it is a build-path rule this article did not have.

Quantization-aware healing (T1, Multiverse Computing; primary map post-training-quantization) runs a three-deep chain — structural compression 120B→60B, bfloat16 recovery, then 4-bit quantization — and finds that the intermediate recovered checkpoint must not become the teacher. It was never independently trained: its weights are derived by an operator and then tethered to the original's output distribution throughout recovery, so it carries the compressed architecture's ceiling by construction, and supervising from it caps the student there. Distilling the 4-bit student from the original instead — architectures differ, which distillation never required — yields a student matching or beating the bfloat16 checkpoint it was quantized from on 7 of 9 benchmarks.

General form: supervise every step of a compression chain from the original, or the chain compounds ceilings instead of capabilities. The load-bearing weakness is the authors' own first limitation — the head-to-head against distillation-from-the-recovered-checkpoint at matched configuration was never run, so the ceiling argument is well-motivated and undemonstrated. [T1]

The metric problem, restated by a review that ran nothing

New 2026-08-27, graded T3 and worth saying why. One Symptom, Three Levers is a critical review of on-policy self-distillation that states outright it reports no new experiments; every number in it is quoted from a primary this vault has not read. It is here because its instrument claim is checkable and none of the map's own primaries stated it.

Mean accuracy does not merely miss collapse — it moves the wrong way while collapse happens (pass@1 71.9 → 73.4 as pass@16 falls 83.6 → 78.5), and the obvious backstop fails too: the same collapsed model registers higher token entropy than its comparison at lower functional diversity. That is independent support, from a different literature, for this article's second imperative. It also splits the blame usefully: one cause family predates self-distillation entirely (the RL gradient erodes entropy; finite sampling eats distribution tails before modes), and one is specific — an over-informed teacher stops deliberating and the student inherits the missing uncertainty.

Its second claim is a decision procedure worth testing if you distil from a model conditioned on privileged information: rank the signal by whether the student could have reconstructed it, not by how much it tells them. Riskiest to safest — final answer, worked solution, chain of thought, plan, rubric, error feedback. Full demonstrations cost up to −17% avg@16 on thinking models; step-wise hints beat oracle answers 71.3 vs 59.5 on C-Eval.

Hold all of it loosely, and the review supplies the reason itself: AIME 2024 is partly in pretraining with models completing ~50% from memory, its 30-question format makes one flip worth >3 points, seed variation reaches ±15, checkpoint selection inflates a result from 41.1% at end of training to 57.2% best-over-checkpoints, and Qwen models gain from a random signal where Llama and OLMo do not — on a literature whose experiments are mostly Qwen at ≤8B. [T3]

What the last two weeks changed

The headline held. The reasons did not, and the most useful correction is that the obvious way to execute this advice is wrong.

Pick the teacher for what it knows, not for what it scores. Rethinking On-Policy Distillation (T1, 2026-04) runs the experiment nobody had: take a 1.5B student that reinforcement learning has already improved, and distill it toward a higher-scoring same-family 7B. It lands at nearly the identical level as one distilled toward its own weaker pre-RL checkpoint, because the two teachers are "distributionally indistinguishable from the student's perspective." Paired with the finding that post-trained teachers transfer far more than same-pipeline base ones, the operating statement becomes: what transfers is distributional novelty, not teacher rank. The failure is silent — a practitioner who upgraded their teacher and saw nothing would have no way to tell whether the recipe or the run was at fault. [T1]

That paper also supplies the mechanism, and it is cheap to instrument. The reward is carried by a token set the two models already share, holding 97–99% of both models' probability mass, with overlap climbing 72% → 91% in successful runs and flat from initialization in failing ones — and early mismatch "cannot be recovered later." So the overlap ratio is a free kill-the-run diagnostic, computable before you have spent anything. [T1]

The two-stage ordering is load-bearing, not conventional. The same study's recommended repair for a failing on-policy run — off-policy SFT on teacher rollouts first, worth 15–20 points of initial overlap and a higher final ceiling — is exactly the sequencing Qwen3 already ships. A third group derived the production recipe's shape from mechanism without setting out to. [T1 + T2]

Distill when the target has a checker; curate when it does not. LIMA (T1) is the clean loss: Alpaca-65B is the same LLaMa 65B base trained on 52,000 examples distilled from text-davinci-003, and LIMA's 1,000 human-curated examples beat it (53% win / 21% tie / 26% loss in human preference). Fifty-two times the data, from a teacher, beaten by curation. Set against R1's decisive win, the two are not contradictory — R1's teacher supplies traces a verifiable checker confirmed, where alignment style has no checker and the teacher's advantage is much thinner. [T1]

There is a horizon, and it arrives where reasoning traces are heading. The on-policy signal's per-token quality decays with depth: teacher continuation advantage +0.37 at a 1K-token prefix → +0.02 at 16K, usable window 3K–7K, plateau or decline by 10K–15K. The authors say the technique "may not extend cleanly to longer-horizon settings such as extended chain-of-thought or agentic multi-turn interaction," and flag their own explanation as unverified. One consolation for anyone reading this vault: 3K–7K is also roughly where a local fine-tune's activation memory is still tractable, so a practitioner on one or two cards is unlikely to meet the ceiling — and equally unable to observe it. [T1, suggestive]

The contamination audit this map has wanted since coining now costs two eval runs. Distillation sets are teacher generations, teachers have seen the benchmarks, and a student scoring well on a suite its teacher memorized measures the wrong thing. As of 2026-07-30 that check needs no corpus, no log-probs and no reference model: memorized answers are brittle to sampling temperature and honest ones are not — scores hold flat from greedy through τ ≤ 0.56 then fall sharply, and at high contamination going to τ = 1.0 costs ~40×, collapsing to roughly uncontaminated performance while clean models barely move. So: score the student and its teacher on the same benchmark at τ=0 and τ=1.0 and read the gap. The benchmark family that was measured is MATH, which is precisely what this article's headline rests on. [T1] (evaluation-contamination)

The bill, and two ways to cut it

Everything above is about quality. As of 2026-08 the vault also knows what the run costs and where the memory actually goes, which it did not in July.

The teacher does not need to be resident, and the thing that caps your context length is the loss, not the model. Offline Top-k Logit Distillation (T1) reports both halves. In the off-policy stage the teacher is a pure function of the input, so compute its logits once and cache the top-100 per token: peak memory 103 GB → 78 GB, iteration time 25.9s → 18.5s, throughput 237 → 331 TFLOP/s on a single H200 at 8K context, at "near-identical loss." Separately, computing the KL divergence naively materializes a [batch × sequence × vocabulary] tensor that peaks at ~250 GB at 32K — more than the card — while a fused chunked formulation that never builds it peaks at ~128 GB, buying 4× the training context on one GPU. Isolated, dense KL fails at 64K where the fused version peaks at 11.6 GiB.

Note what that second finding corrects. An 8B teacher and a 3.2B student are not what needs 103 GB — a vocabulary-sized logit tensor is. The fused loss is exact (an algebraic rewrite of the same gradient) and attacks a memory term the distributed-training literature never named, so it belongs at the front of the spend-the-exact-levers-first ordering. Two honest discounts: the caching win is confined to the off-policy stage — a cache cannot answer for states the student has not visited — and "near-identical loss" is a training-health proxy with no downstream benchmark behind it, which is the exact proxy this vault distrusts most. Every absolute number is 141 GB datacenter hardware; the mechanism travels, the figures do not. [T1, one study]

There is also a producer-side answer that deletes the teacher-residency question rather than optimizing it. Matryoshka Language Model Suites (T1) stacks the sizes inside each other — the 500M is a subset of the 1.5B, a subset of the 3B — trains end to end, and lets the largest distil into every smaller one at every step: parity with independently trained baselines at 36% less training compute, with no teacher to keep resident and no traces to generate. It requires training from scratch, which is the one move this vault's premise says its readers cannot make, so read it as a claim about what will be on the shelf to download, not about what you do afterwards. It is also an ambiguous test of the novelty account — a nested teacher is the low-novelty extreme and the suite still reaches parity, which is equally consistent with novelty failing at that end or with the nested teacher transferring nothing and shared compute doing all the work. The abstract reports no no-distillation nested arm. [T1, single study]

The same ordering keeps turning up outside distillation

Worth knowing because it is free corroboration of the article's least intuitive advice.

ScaleQ (T1, Intel Labs China) is a quantization calibration paper — no gradients, no student, a solver fitting a compressed model to reproduce a full-precision one's activations. Calibrating a 4B model with chain-of-thought traces from DeepSeek-R1-671B yields 20.06% mean accuracy across five reasoning and coding benchmarks; calibrating it with traces the 4B model wrote itself yields 45.60%. A 25.54-point gain for using the far weaker model's own distribution. Two literatures with no shared citations reach one rule: any procedure that fits one model against another's outputs wants the target's own distribution, not the best distribution available. The set of procedures with that shape — distillation, quantization calibration, draft-model training for speculative decoding, adapter initialization on synthetic data — is longer than either paper's scope, and exactly two have been checked. [T1]

And a fourth axis on the novelty framing from an unexpected place: RestoreKV (T1) self-distils a model against its own frozen weights, differing only in what the two are allowed to remember — teacher on the full KV cache, student on a compressed one — training eight restore tokens plus a rank-8 adapter (0.4% of parameters, ~2 hours on one card) for +35 points on RULER-4K at a 5% cache budget. The teacher is not better, only less compressed, so what transfers is information the student can no longer reach rather than skill or rank. It also repeats this article's recurring transfer limit: the module does not survive a change of compressor (trained on SnapKV, run on KVzip: 42.0 against 73.2). [T1]

What to do

  • Leash the on-policy stage to the student's own initial policy. Unconstrained on-policy distillation from a much longer-reasoning teacher degenerates — 40% truncation, 972 exact self-check repeats, one-token loops — and a student-reference KL term drives truncation to near zero and lifts ProofBench@4 21.70 → 44.50. The coefficient has an interior optimum (0.5 too small, 1.0 best, 1.2 too large), so sweep it rather than picking one. [T1]
  • Never run multi-teacher distillation without a weight-merge baseline, and plot your per-domain token share before blaming the teachers. Training-free task arithmetic recovers 71.7% of the oracle gap against naive multi-teacher distillation's 35.6%; the repaired recipe wins by 0.78 points after 300 steps on eight A100s. The bug is that 99% of gradient tokens went to the verbose domains, because a per-batch token quota is implicitly a verbosity weighting. [T1]
  • Supervise every step of a compression chain from the original model, not from the intermediate recovered checkpoint. The intermediate was never independently trained, so it carries the compressed architecture's ceiling and caps the student there. Architectures are allowed to differ; distillation never required otherwise. Well-motivated, and the matched head-to-head was not run. [T1]
  • If your teacher is conditioned on privileged information, rank the signal by reconstructibility. Final answer is the riskiest thing to show it, error feedback the safest; full demonstrations cost up to −17% avg@16 on thinking models. A decision procedure to test, not a settled result. [T3]
  • At long input lengths, stop treating teacher preference as correctness. The two are measurably different signals and they diverge as context grows — a scope condition on every on-policy result established at ordinary lengths. Adding a verifier's disagreement with the teacher is worth roughly twice adding the verifier's opinion alone (+1.10 vs +0.63 in a matched ablation). [T1]
  • Spend the off-policy stage first. It is where capability enters. On-policy logit matching sharpens what the student already reaches and, run alone, can leave it worse at large sampling budgets than the checkpoint you started from. [T1]
  • Report pass@K at a large K before you believe your own distillation result. pass@1 and averaged scores measure how reliably a model reaches what it can reach; only a large-budget pass rate measures what it can reach at all, and the two came apart by 16.7 points in the one study that looked. [T1]
  • When you need a small reasoning model, default to distilling a bigger one's traces via plain SFT before reaching for RL on the small model. Exploration is expensive and scale-dependent; imitation is cheap at any scale. [T1]
  • Choose the teacher by distributional distance from your student, not by leaderboard position. A stronger model from the same family and pipeline may transfer nothing. Cheap proxy nobody has validated as a ranking but which separates working from failing pairs at initialization: token overlap on a few hundred prompts. [T1]
  • Cold-start off-policy before going on-policy. SFT on teacher rollouts first, then logit matching — the ordering is worth 15–20 points of initial overlap and a better final ceiling, and it is what the one shipping pipeline in evidence does. [T1 + T2]
  • Watch the overlap ratio and kill flat runs early. Early thinking-pattern mismatch does not converge out. [T1]
  • Keep on-policy responses in the 3K–7K range. Shorter under-supervises; past ~10K the signal thins. [T1, suggestive]
  • Treat access to teacher traces or logits as the real asset — not the teacher's training run. This is why open releases of strong reasoning models move the craft more than closed capability jumps. [T1 / T2]
  • Run the expensive recipe once, on the teacher, then distill outward. Qwen3's ~10× GPU-hour saving is the shape of the argument, not a universal constant. [T2]
  • Cache the teacher's top-k logits offline instead of keeping it resident, and fuse the KL loss rather than materializing a vocabulary-sized tensor. The first is off-policy-only and bought ~29% iteration time and 25 GB on one datacenter card; the second is exact and bought 4× the training context. Neither has been run on a consumer card by anyone. [T1, one study]
  • Calibrate, initialize and draft against your target's own distribution, not against the strongest model you can access. Established now in two unrelated literatures. [T1 ×2]
  • Audit before you believe your own number: score student and teacher at τ=0 and τ=1.0 on the benchmark your claim rests on. Two eval runs. [T1]
  • Distillation SFT at these student sizes is commonly a PEFT job — 4-bit-base LoRA on one card (peft-methods) — and the merged student then feeds the quantize-and-run-locally pipeline (local-inference).

Not settled

  • Nobody has dropped the synthetic fraction. The one from-scratch small model that beats its distilled peers has 11.08% teacher-generated tokens in its corpus and no ablation removing them. Until that run exists, "trained from scratch" and "trained without a teacher" are not the same claim, and this article's headline is safe for a reason nobody has measured.
  • The multi-teacher repair restores parity, it does not win. A mergekit weight merge is currently the baseline to beat, and the fully repaired three-teacher recipe beats it by 0.78 points after training three experts and running 300 steps on eight A100s. Whether the ordering survives at other student sizes, other domain counts, or a merge baseline that has itself been tuned is unmeasured — and it is one 3B student.
  • The on-policy leash is one lab's fix on its own models. The student-reference KL result comes from a paper whose teacher and flagship student are the authors' own, scored by an LLM judge, with no compute or hardware figure reported anywhere — so the article gains a capability result and zero cost evidence. And that lab reports @4 and @8 only, leaving the pass@K question it was equipped to answer untouched.
  • The compression-chain rule is undemonstrated. Distilling from the original rather than from the intermediate recovered checkpoint is argued from how the intermediate was constructed; the head-to-head at matched configuration is the authors' own first stated limitation.
  • The stage split is measured at one scale. The pass@K crossover is students at 1.5–1.7B on competition mathematics at one decoding temperature, with no code released. Whether forgetting still exceeds learning at 7B or 14B — the sizes a local reader with a 24 GB card actually distils into — is untested and costs one training run per size. So is whether it holds on a non-mathematical, checker-verified task.
  • Nobody has run a matched-cost split of the two stages. The off-policy arm in the crossover study is a control, and its tuning and compute are not laid out to the standard of the headline. The run that would settle the ordering advice above: one student, one teacher, equal GPU-hours spent off-policy only, on-policy only, and split, all scored at pass@1 and pass@1024.
  • No neutral referee yet. R1 is T1 and Qwen3 is T2, and both evaluate their own students. The direction replicates across two labs; a third-party reproduction still does not exist in-vault. Note that Qwen3's Pass@64 figure — the one that made it a replication of capability transfer rather than of efficiency — sits exactly where the 2026-08-13 crossover appears, and because the report never separates its off-policy and on-policy stages, it was never evidence about on-policy distillation specifically.
  • Every result is a verifiable domain. Math and reasoning, where a checker filtered the teacher traces — including the 2026-04 dynamics study, which is AIME/AMC/DeepMath throughout and so sits entirely inside the easy half of this question. Whether the pattern holds for style, tool use or agentic behaviour is unmeasured, and the novelty framing predicts it should weaken exactly where the teacher's advantage is stylistic. The classic non-reasoning student-beats-generalist play (classification, extraction) predates all of this and has no in-vault source.
  • "Distributional novelty" is a description, not yet a quantity. The authors flag their own cross-family comparisons as confounded — data divergence, tokenizer mismatch and architecture vary together — so nobody can currently measure how much a candidate teacher has to offer before training.
  • The benchmarks are exactly the vulnerable kind. AIME/MATH deltas on fine-tuned models are where evaluation-contamination bites hardest, and as of 2026-07-30 that map holds that the standard decontamination method scores F1 0.000 against paraphrase, and that a single test-set replica can put a model below the loss infinite clean compute would reach. The temperature probe above is a detector, not a discount: nobody has converted a contamination rate into a correction on a published score.
  • A benchmark regression may mean the fine-tune is working. Above ~10 test-set replicas, supervised fine-tuning on the training split raises test loss — the model forgets what it memorized faster than it learns to generalize. A distillation run on a contaminated base can therefore show the opposite of what it means. [T1]
  • What the student loses. General-capability regression from narrow distillation — the catastrophic-forgetting question shared with peft-methods — is still unmeasured here, and there are now two mechanisms for it: narrow-distribution forgetting, and the fact that teacher traces are chosen-only data with no rejected side, so an SFT run on them has zero downward pressure on anything the traces do not contain.
  • Teacher quality is a ceiling on the skill, and no longer the useful variable for choosing one. The leverage is still the limit; it is just not the thing to optimize when picking.
