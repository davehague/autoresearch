Distill, don't train: the state of small-model reasoning distillation

Synthesized 2026-07-17, refreshed 2026-07-31 from distillation-small-models (status: consolidating). Trigger: drift — the map gained twelve dated through-line entries since this article was last written, including the first controlled study of when the technique fails. The headline survives; almost everything around it has been qualified. This field's defaults rot in months; check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

To make a small open-weights model good at reasoning, do not run the hard training recipe on the small model. Take a large model that already has the capability, and distill its traces (or logits) into the small one by supervised fine-tuning. As of 2026-07 the vault holds this from two independent directions:

  • Controlled ablation — DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, Nature 2025, weights MIT) fine-tunes a Qwen-32B base on ~800k reasoning traces from its R1 teacher and hits 72.6% AIME 2024 — versus 47.0% for the same base trained with reasoning RL directly. The distilled 7B student reaches 55.5% AIME (GPT-4o-0513: 9.3%) and 92.8% MATH-500, out-scoring a 32B reasoner (QwQ-32B-Preview: 50.0%). Same base, distillation vs direct RL, distillation wins. [T1]
  • Production replication — the Qwen3 Technical Report (Qwen Team, Apache-2.0) builds its whole small-dense lineup (0.6B–14B) by strong-to-weak distillation from fully-trained flagship teachers (235B-A22B, 32B): off-policy on teacher outputs across thinking/non-thinking modes, plus on-policy logit matching. It reports higher Pass@1 and Pass@64 than running the full four-stage recipe per model — at ~1/10 the GPU hours. A second lab, a different base, outside competition math. [T2]

That convergence — an ablation and a shipping pipeline pointing the same way — is why the map sits at consolidating, and nothing since has moved it.

What the last two weeks changed

The headline held. The reasons did not, and the most useful correction is that the obvious way to execute this advice is wrong.

Pick the teacher for what it knows, not for what it scores. Rethinking On-Policy Distillation (T1, 2026-04) runs the experiment nobody had: take a 1.5B student that reinforcement learning has already improved, and distill it toward a higher-scoring same-family 7B. It lands at nearly the identical level as one distilled toward its own weaker pre-RL checkpoint, because the two teachers are "distributionally indistinguishable from the student's perspective." Paired with the finding that post-trained teachers transfer far more than same-pipeline base ones, the operating statement becomes: what transfers is distributional novelty, not teacher rank. The failure is silent — a practitioner who upgraded their teacher and saw nothing would have no way to tell whether the recipe or the run was at fault. [T1]

That paper also supplies the mechanism, and it is cheap to instrument. The reward is carried by a token set the two models already share, holding 97–99% of both models' probability mass, with overlap climbing 72% → 91% in successful runs and flat from initialization in failing ones — and early mismatch "cannot be recovered later." So the overlap ratio is a free kill-the-run diagnostic, computable before you have spent anything. [T1]

The two-stage ordering is load-bearing, not conventional. The same study's recommended repair for a failing on-policy run — off-policy SFT on teacher rollouts first, worth 15–20 points of initial overlap and a higher final ceiling — is exactly the sequencing Qwen3 already ships. A third group derived the production recipe's shape from mechanism without setting out to. [T1 + T2]

Distill when the target has a checker; curate when it does not. LIMA (T1) is the clean loss: Alpaca-65B is the same LLaMa 65B base trained on 52,000 examples distilled from text-davinci-003, and LIMA's 1,000 human-curated examples beat it (53% win / 21% tie / 26% loss in human preference). Fifty-two times the data, from a teacher, beaten by curation. Set against R1's decisive win, the two are not contradictory — R1's teacher supplies traces a verifiable checker confirmed, where alignment style has no checker and the teacher's advantage is much thinner. [T1]

There is a horizon, and it arrives where reasoning traces are heading. The on-policy signal's per-token quality decays with depth: teacher continuation advantage +0.37 at a 1K-token prefix → +0.02 at 16K, usable window 3K–7K, plateau or decline by 10K–15K. The authors say the technique "may not extend cleanly to longer-horizon settings such as extended chain-of-thought or agentic multi-turn interaction," and flag their own explanation as unverified. One consolation for anyone reading this vault: 3K–7K is also roughly where a local fine-tune's activation memory is still tractable, so a practitioner on one or two cards is unlikely to meet the ceiling — and equally unable to observe it. [T1, suggestive]

The contamination audit this map has wanted since coining now costs two eval runs. Distillation sets are teacher generations, teachers have seen the benchmarks, and a student scoring well on a suite its teacher memorized measures the wrong thing. As of 2026-07-30 that check needs no corpus, no log-probs and no reference model: memorized answers are brittle to sampling temperature and honest ones are not — scores hold flat from greedy through τ ≤ 0.56 then fall sharply, and at high contamination going to τ = 1.0 costs ~40×, collapsing to roughly uncontaminated performance while clean models barely move. So: score the student and its teacher on the same benchmark at τ=0 and τ=1.0 and read the gap. The benchmark family that was measured is MATH, which is precisely what this article's headline rests on. [T1] (evaluation-contamination)

What to do

  • When you need a small reasoning model, default to distilling a bigger one's traces via plain SFT before reaching for RL on the small model. Exploration is expensive and scale-dependent; imitation is cheap at any scale. [T1]
  • Choose the teacher by distributional distance from your student, not by leaderboard position. A stronger model from the same family and pipeline may transfer nothing. Cheap proxy nobody has validated as a ranking but which separates working from failing pairs at initialization: token overlap on a few hundred prompts. [T1]
  • Cold-start off-policy before going on-policy. SFT on teacher rollouts first, then logit matching — the ordering is worth 15–20 points of initial overlap and a better final ceiling, and it is what the one shipping pipeline in evidence does. [T1 + T2]
  • Watch the overlap ratio and kill flat runs early. Early thinking-pattern mismatch does not converge out. [T1]
  • Keep on-policy responses in the 3K–7K range. Shorter under-supervises; past ~10K the signal thins. [T1, suggestive]
  • Treat access to teacher traces or logits as the real asset — not the teacher's training run. This is why open releases of strong reasoning models move the craft more than closed capability jumps. [T1 / T2]
  • Run the expensive recipe once, on the teacher, then distill outward. Qwen3's ~10× GPU-hour saving is the shape of the argument, not a universal constant. [T2]
  • Audit before you believe your own number: score student and teacher at τ=0 and τ=1.0 on the benchmark your claim rests on. Two eval runs. [T1]
  • Distillation SFT at these student sizes is commonly a PEFT job — 4-bit-base LoRA on one card (peft-methods) — and the merged student then feeds the quantize-and-run-locally pipeline (local-inference).

Not settled

  • No neutral referee yet. R1 is T1 and Qwen3 is T2, and both evaluate their own students. The direction replicates across two labs; a third-party reproduction still does not exist in-vault.
  • Every result is a verifiable domain. Math and reasoning, where a checker filtered the teacher traces — including the 2026-04 dynamics study, which is AIME/AMC/DeepMath throughout and so sits entirely inside the easy half of this question. Whether the pattern holds for style, tool use or agentic behaviour is unmeasured, and the novelty framing predicts it should weaken exactly where the teacher's advantage is stylistic. The classic non-reasoning student-beats-generalist play (classification, extraction) predates all of this and has no in-vault source.
  • "Distributional novelty" is a description, not yet a quantity. The authors flag their own cross-family comparisons as confounded — data divergence, tokenizer mismatch and architecture vary together — so nobody can currently measure how much a candidate teacher has to offer before training.
  • The benchmarks are exactly the vulnerable kind. AIME/MATH deltas on fine-tuned models are where evaluation-contamination bites hardest, and as of 2026-07-30 that map holds that the standard decontamination method scores F1 0.000 against paraphrase, and that a single test-set replica can put a model below the loss infinite clean compute would reach. The temperature probe above is a detector, not a discount: nobody has converted a contamination rate into a correction on a published score.
  • A benchmark regression may mean the fine-tune is working. Above ~10 test-set replicas, supervised fine-tuning on the training split raises test loss — the model forgets what it memorized faster than it learns to generalize. A distillation run on a contaminated base can therefore show the opposite of what it means. [T1]
  • What the student loses. General-capability regression from narrow distillation — the catastrophic-forgetting question shared with peft-methods — is still unmeasured here, and there are now two mechanisms for it: narrow-distribution forgetting, and the fact that teacher traces are chosen-only data with no rejected side, so an SFT run on them has zero downward pressure on anything the traces do not contain.
  • Teacher quality is a ceiling on the skill, and no longer the useful variable for choosing one. The leverage is still the limit; it is just not the thing to optimize when picking.
