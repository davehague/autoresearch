Distill, don't train: the state of small-model reasoning distillation

Synthesized 2026-07-17 from distillation-small-models (status: consolidating as of this run). Two labs, two base families, same direction — but both are still vendor/authors' own evals. This field's defaults rot in months; check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

To make a small open-weights model good at reasoning, do not run the hard training recipe on the small model. Take a large model that already has the capability, and distill its traces (or logits) into the small one by supervised fine-tuning. As of 2026-07 the vault holds this from two independent directions:

  • Controlled ablation — DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, Nature 2025, weights MIT) fine-tunes a Qwen-32B base on ~800k reasoning traces from its R1 teacher and hits 72.6% AIME 2024 — versus 47.0% for the same base trained with reasoning RL directly. The distilled 7B student reaches 55.5% AIME (GPT-4o-0513: 9.3%) and 92.8% MATH-500, out-scoring a 32B reasoner (QwQ-32B-Preview: 50.0%). Same base, distillation vs direct RL, distillation wins. [T1]
  • Production replication — the Qwen3 Technical Report (Qwen Team, Apache-2.0) builds its whole small-dense lineup (0.6B–14B) by strong-to-weak distillation from fully-trained flagship teachers (235B-A22B, 32B): off-policy on teacher outputs across thinking/non-thinking modes, plus on-policy logit matching. It reports higher Pass@1 and Pass@64 than running the full four-stage recipe per model — at ~1/10 the GPU hours. A second lab, a different base, outside competition math. [T2]

Same direction from an ablation and a shipping pipeline. That convergence — not either paper alone — is why the map moved to consolidating this run.

What to do

  • When you need a small reasoning model, default to distilling a bigger one's traces via plain SFT before reaching for RL on the small model. Exploration is expensive and scale-dependent; imitation is cheap at any scale. [T1]
  • Treat access to teacher traces or logits as the real asset — not the teacher's training run. This is why open releases of strong reasoning models (R1's weights, Qwen3's family) move the craft more than closed capability jumps. [T1 / T2]
  • The trade this buys: specificity for generality. A 7B distilled for a verifiable domain beats frontier generalists on that domain and says nothing about the rest — narrow by construction. [T1]
  • Budget the cost where it pays: run the full expensive recipe once, on the teacher, then distill outward. Qwen3's ~10× GPU-hour saving is the shape of the argument, not a universal constant. [T2]
  • Distillation SFT on these student sizes is commonly a PEFT job — 4-bit-base LoRA on one card (peft-methods) — and the merged student then feeds the quantize-and-run-locally pipeline (local-inference).

Not settled

  • No neutral referee yet. R1 is T1 and Qwen3 is T2, but both evaluate their own students. The direction replicates across two labs; a third-party reproduction does not exist in-vault.
  • The benchmarks are exactly the vulnerable kind. AIME/MATH deltas on fine-tuned models are where evaluation-contamination bites hardest — a distilled model is deliberate exposure to task-adjacent traces. AIME 2024 post-dating pretraining cutoffs helps; contamination audits of the distill sets themselves do not exist yet. Believe these numbers on held-out, post-cutoff items only.
  • Only verifiable domains so far. Every in-vault result is math/reasoning, where a checker filtered the teacher traces. Whether the pattern holds for style, tool use, or agentic behavior — no checker to filter on — is unmeasured. The classic non-reasoning student-beats-generalist play (classification/extraction) predates this and has no in-vault source.
  • What the student loses. General-capability regression from narrow distillation — the catastrophic-forgetting question shared with peft-methods — is unmeasured here.
  • Teacher quality is the ceiling. Distillation cannot exceed the teacher on the distilled skill; the leverage is also the limit.
