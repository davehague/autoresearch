Distill, don't train: the state of small-model reasoning distillation

Synthesized 2026-07-17, refreshed 2026-07-31, 2026-08-13, 2026-08-27, 2026-10-08, 2026-10-12, 2026-10-18, 2026-11-01 and again 2026-11-06 from distillation-small-models (status: consolidating). Trigger: drift — six dated through-line entries since 2026-11-01 (the deepest drift of five eligible candidates across the vault this run). The headline still survives. What changed: a black-box teacher-adaptation method (SPTA) and a sampler-side rollout-coupling method (CoRD) turn out to be mirror images of each other — one adapts the teacher to the student's own rollout prefixes, the other steers the student's rollouts toward the teacher — both targeting the same root problem (the teacher's signal not reaching every state that matters) from opposite ends of the pipeline, giving the map's standing teacher-selection/ switching composition question a fifth untested lever; a black-box method for teachers that hide their own chain-of-thought shows final-answer correctness and intermediate-step validity are separable supervision signals; shuffling the word order of the problem statement shared by student and teacher improves on-policy self-distillation, while shuffling the teacher-only privileged reference hurts it; an interpretability result locates where interpolated-teacher distillation's "thinking-like" behavior lives in the student's own LoRA-adapter subspace; and which privileged-information strategy a black-box teacher should supply turns out not to be fixed, but to shift with the student's own training state. This field's defaults rot in months; check the map's through-line before trusting this in 2027.

The claim

To make a small open-weights model good at reasoning, do not run the hard training recipe on the small model. Take a large model that already has the capability, and distill its traces (or logits) into the small one by supervised fine-tuning. As of 2026-07 the vault holds this from two independent directions:

  • Controlled ablation — DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, Nature 2025, weights MIT) fine-tunes a Qwen-32B base on ~800k reasoning traces from its R1 teacher and hits 72.6% AIME 2024 — versus 47.0% for the same base trained with reasoning RL directly. The distilled 7B student reaches 55.5% AIME (GPT-4o-0513: 9.3%) and 92.8% MATH-500, out-scoring a 32B reasoner (QwQ-32B-Preview: 50.0%). Same base, distillation vs direct RL, distillation wins. [T1]
  • Production replication — the Qwen3 Technical Report (Qwen Team, Apache-2.0) builds its whole small-dense lineup (0.6B–14B) by strong-to-weak distillation from fully-trained flagship teachers (235B-A22B, 32B): off-policy on teacher outputs across thinking/non-thinking modes, plus on-policy logit matching. It reports higher Pass@1 and Pass@64 than running the full four-stage recipe per model — at ~1/10 the GPU hours. A second lab, a different base, outside competition math. [T2]

That convergence — an ablation and a shipping pipeline pointing the same way — is why the map sits at consolidating, and nothing since has moved it.

One correction to the second bullet, made 2026-08-13 and important enough to sit here rather than in a footnote: Qwen3's "higher Pass@1 and Pass@64" is a pipeline number — off-policy cold start then on-policy logit matching — and the report never separates the two stages. Read below for why that distinction turned out to carry the whole argument.

The two stages do different jobs, and only one of them adds capability

This is the newest thing here and the one most likely to change what you do.

Towards Understanding On-Policy Distillation through the Lens of Test-Time Scaling (T1, 2026-08-12) does the boring, expensive thing nobody had: it sweeps the sampling budget out to K = 1024 instead of stopping at 8 or 64. Take a Qwen3-1.7B base, distil it on-policy from a 4B GRPO teacher, and score both on AIME2024:

pass@3228.2%30.5%58.2%
pass@6435.0%33.6%—
pass@102470.0%53.3%76.7%

The curves cross at pass@64, and by pass@1024 the distilled student is 16.7 points below the checkpoint it was trained from. Meanwhile every average improves at every budget (avg@1024: 12.0% against 4.2%). At the problem level, mean accuracy on problems the model could already solve roughly doubles — and across all three student–teacher pairs tested, more previously solvable problems became unsolvable than the reverse.

Two things stop this from being a retraction, and both matter.

Off-policy distillation was run as a control and behaved correctly — higher pass@K and higher avg@K than the base at small and large budgets alike. So what is bounded is a stage, not the play this article is about. R1's result, which is trace SFT, is off-policy and untouched.

And it converges with the mechanism this article already carried. The 2026-04 dynamics study concluded that transfer follows distributional novelty; this one measures, from perplexity, that on-policy trajectories become more likely under the teacher while staying closer to the base distribution under the base scorer. Two groups, two instruments, no cross-citation, one statement: the on-policy objective redistributes probability mass inside the student's existing support. It can make the student reliable at what it can already reach. It cannot widen what it reaches.

The operating consequence is an ordering constraint rather than a preference: the off-policy stage is where new capability enters, so a budget-constrained run spends there first and treats the on-policy stage as sharpening. Skipping the cold start is not a cheaper version of the recipe — it is a different intervention with a different result.

For anyone running this locally, the direction is double-edged rather than simply bad. Local deployment samples at K = 1, which is the whole point, so the sampling-efficiency gain is real value to you and the capability loss is invisible in your deployment — right up until the workload acquires a retry loop, a best-of-N sampler or a verifier, all of which are standard in agentic use, at which point the base model would have served you better. Bounded: competition mathematics only, three configurations, one decoding temperature, students at 1.5–1.7B with no scale control at the sizes a 24 GB card would actually target, and no code release. [T1]

As of 2026-10-08, the lever behind that split has a name, and it is not "on-policy versus off-policy." On-Policy Distillation Teaches New Skills but Not New Knowledge (T1, HKUST) builds a synthetic framework that separates "new facts" from "new compositional skill" as distillation targets and finds reverse-KL on-policy distillation — the field's default, and what every result above actually used — transfers compositional skill to unseen structures cleanly (79.10% mean accuracy vs. 0.72% for an undistilled control) while barely moving factual recall (13.12% vs. 0.03% control). Swap reverse KL for forward KL, changing nothing else about the recipe, and factual transfer recovers 3–5x (one model: 10.95% → 54.10%). A weight-space check corroborates the split structurally: the two kinds of update are nearly orthogonal (cosine similarity 0.004–0.046) and concentrate at different depths. This replicates on real factual-QA and competition-math benchmarks with a real teacher-student pair, not just the synthetic instrument, and it is the third independent account on this map to land on the same mechanism from a different angle — the pass@K crossover above measures reverse-KL OPD narrowing the reliability gap on problems the student can already reach; the off-policy control in that same study measures the stage that actually widens reach; this paper names why: KL direction, not sampling source, decides whether training reorganizes existing capability or adds to it. Practical reading: every result in this article trained under reverse-KL OPD (nearly all of them) should be read as skill/reliability transfer, regardless of what its benchmark nominally tested — a benchmark that rewards recomposing known facts will look like capability growth under reverse-KL OPD even where a benchmark requiring genuinely new factual recall would not move at all. Caveats: the main synthetic result uses arbitrary digit-lookup facts with no natural-language structure; the real-world replication is one model, one seed. [T1]

Is on-policy training worth its own cost at all? Only above a measured overlap threshold

New 2026-10-11 and 2026-10-10, and together they turn "the on-policy stage sharpens rather than adds" into a question with a number attached: when is running it worth the compute, and why does it sometimes barely move the loss regardless?

Semi-OPD (T1, NVIDIA / UCLA) tests a fixed-rollout alternative — sample once from the initial student before training, score once with the teacher, train on the fixed set, with rollout generation, teacher scoring and optimization each as one-time offline phases — against standard on-policy distillation across 17 teacher-student pairs (1.5B–235B, three model families). Semi-OPD beats standard OPD on 14 of 17 pairs, by up to 13.6% accuracy at up to 11.4x less training compute, and trails by only 0.1–2.6% on the rest. The deciding variable is the initial teacher-student output-token overlap: below high overlap, continually re-sampling from the evolving student drifts its rollouts further off-policy relative to the teacher as responses lengthen, while Semi-OPD's fixed, student-anchored rollouts keep exposing the model to more teacher-preferred tokens throughout training. Above high initial overlap, standard OPD earns its cost back. This splits "on-policy-ness" into two components this article has so far treated as one axis: Semi-OPD gets most of its advantage by being less on-policy with respect to the student while staying more on-policy with respect to the teacher. Requires an "adequately strong" (instruction-tuned, not base) initial student, or the advantage inverts. [T1]

On-Policy Distillation's Vanishing Learning Signals (T1, UPenn / Tsinghua / UC Riverside / Marquette) supplies a mechanism for a related but distinct question: not whether OPD is worth running, but why it sometimes barely trains at all even when run. Across seven student-teacher pairs on code and math, final loss reduction after 200 updates averages 96.2% when the teacher is the student's own family further RL-trained ("self-RL," close in parameter space) but only 25.1% when the teacher is a separately-scaled larger model. An ODE model of OPD's loss dynamics traces the gap to an early collapse in a gradient-based learning-signal proxy — dropping roughly an order of magnitude while 60%+ of the loss remains, a premature plateau rather than a reached floor — and a local theorem proves teacher proximity is sufficient for bounded recovery, without showing distant teachers must fail. This is a training-dynamics diagnosis orthogonal to the KL-direction and novelty findings elsewhere in this article: those ask what OPD transfers when it works; this asks why it sometimes doesn't train at all. Teacher parameter-distance (this paper) and initial output-token overlap (Semi-OPD, above) are two different alignment instruments measuring a related but currently untested-against-each- other notion of teacher-student fit — nobody has run both diagnostics on the same pool of pairs to see whether they predict the same failures. Bounded: idealized continuous-time dynamics measured against noisy real AdamW updates via a proxy gradient signal; one learning rate, no seeds. [T1]

Read together with the pass@K crossover above: that result already showed on-policy training can leave a student worse at large sampling budgets than the checkpoint it started from. These two results sharpen when that risk is live — low initial teacher-student overlap, or a teacher far away in parameter space — and supply a cheap pre-flight check (token overlap, or family/scale distance) before committing to a full on-policy run.

A new axis inside on-policy distillation: which tokens to train on, not which direction

New 2026-10-09, and it is a question nobody on this map had separated from KL direction until now.

DIAL-OPD (T1, EIT-NLP Ningbo / Hong Kong Polytechnic University / HKUST-GZ) finds that in sampled-token on-policy distillation, training on every token is not optimal — random retention of 40–90% of tokens beats full-token training on a 0.6B student — and that the obvious fix (select the highest-reward-magnitude tokens) does worse than random because it concentrates training on "low-low" tokens, where teacher and student both assign near-zero probability to everything. Weighting token selection by the logarithmic mean of student/teacher probability to a power beta, peaking at the theoretically-derived fixed point beta=1, beats full-token OPD by up to 5.25 points mean accuracy at 40% retention across four teacher-student pairs on seven math benchmarks — and a 4B teacher with this selection beats the strongest full-token 8B-teacher result by up to 1.76 points. This shares this article's on-policy-distillation object with the KL-direction finding but addresses a disjoint question — token selection within a rollout, not divergence direction between teacher and student — and the two levers are untested together. Bounded: evaluated only on mathematical reasoning; the motivating random-retention anomaly is reported by the authors to vanish when student and teacher are matched in size. [T1]

Cross-tokenizer distillation gets the credit-assignment fix its own alignment finding left open

New 2026-10-12. SimpleOPD, cited above for its student-reference-KL leash, separately established that cross-tokenizer distillation works by aligning only positions whose tokens cover identical text spans — but left open what happens inside an aligned chunk once it is known how much the chunk's advantage should move.

CAST (T1, Alibaba Taobao & Tmall Group / Singapore Management University) answers that: for aligned chunks, redistribute the chunk's advantage across student tokens by predictive uncertainty times local teacher-signal smoothness — weights that exactly solve a stated KL-regularized credit-allocation problem while preserving the chunk's total advantage. For unaligned positions — tokens with no teacher correspondence at all — anchor the student to its own SFT- initialization policy via a KL penalty. That anchoring move is structurally identical to SimpleOPD's student-reference-KL fix for a completely different symptom (SimpleOPD was stopping runaway length and repetition; CAST is bounding drift where there is no teacher signal at all) — two independent groups converging on the same stabilizer for two different proximate problems inside the same cross-tokenizer- OPD setting. Across six math/science/code benchmarks, CAST beats the strongest evaluated cross-tokenizer baseline by 2.45 points (Qwen3-8B teacher to Mistral-7B) and 1.32 points (to Llama-3.1-8B), and the same credit rule also wins the easier shared-tokenizer setting by 2.10 points over GKD/MiniLLM/DistiLLM — where GRPO notably collapses below the untrained SFT baseline on Mistral (30.97 vs. 44.33), one more entry in this article's running catalogue of on-policy training instability. Component ablations confirm both halves are load-bearing: dropping the smoothness term costs 1.62 points, dropping the anchoring KL entirely costs 3.22. Bounded: limited model families on mainly reasoning-oriented data; most comparisons are single runs; a teacher-scaling check found the effect is student-size-dependent rather than monotonic (a larger teacher helps a 4B student but hurts a 1.7B one). [T1]

A free weight merge challenges whether the on-policy stage needs to exist at all

New 2026-10-06, and it is a challenge to the premise underneath every fix in this article's on-policy sections, not another fix to the method.

Grafting (T1, Carnegie Mellon) asks whether on-policy training is actually necessary to avoid forgetting when a post-trained model absorbs new data, and answers no. Learn the update by fine-tuning an earlier pretraining checkpoint — never the model actually being deployed, so no rollout sampling against it happens at all — scale the resulting weight delta, optionally mask its most retention-sensitive directions by Fisher information, and merge it in. Across four continual-learning settings (teacher-trace distillation, STaR self-improvement, pedagogical RL, synthetic-knowledge injection) on Qwen3-1.7B/8B and OLMo-3-7B, this Pareto-dominates both plain SFT and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) on new-task performance and old-task retention simultaneously — one knowledge-injection setting: grafting ~99% injection / ~95% retention against SFT's ~99%/~30% and OPSD's ~85%/~95% — while training roughly 10x faster than OPSD, because OPSD spends about 90% of its step time sampling rollouts that grafting never generates.

This sits in direct tension with the leash section above: that fix manages OPSD's reasoning-collapse failure mode within a narrow coefficient range, which is real and replicated. Grafting's result raises the possibility that the failure mode the leash manages is a structural cost of training on-policy at all, not a tuning problem — a method that does zero on-policy sampling beats the best-regularized on-policy method on both the thing OPSD is supposed to protect (retention) and the thing it's supposed to deliver (new-task performance). It does not retroactively break anything measured above; it reframes what RC-OPD, the leash and this map's other OPSD-repair work are competing against: not just a better-tuned on-policy variant, but a free, 10x-cheaper alternative that was never on-policy to begin with.

Caveats that keep this from a rewrite: headline numbers are read off bar charts, not an exact results table (PDF not yet archived at ingest); the donor-checkpoint-age sweep (earlier pretraining checkpoints graft better, up to a point) is systematic only on OLMo-3, where the full pretraining lineage exists — Qwen3 uses whatever earlier checkpoint was on hand; the scale factor and mask fraction are set by grid search, with no automated selection rule; every evaluated model is a reasoning model. [T1]

Somebody ran the counter-arm, and the answer is in the data table

The whole article rests on don't train it small. As of 2026-08-15 that arm has been run properly and it won — and the qualifier that saves the claim is not in the results.

Mimir v1 (T2, Danish Foundation Models; open weights, training framework released, corpus enumerated to the token) is a 1B model trained from scratch, no distillation loss and no teacher logits, that beats every same-class open model by 18–32 points on its English suite and 20–28 on Danish. Against the identical architecture at identical scale, data mixture the only difference, it gains +36.7% on math and code.

Then the corpus table: 7.81B of its 70.48B tokens (11.08%) are synthetic data generated by a 31B model and audited, plus 1.59B translated-and-audited. That is distillation routed through a dataset instead of through logits. So the claim narrows rather than breaks: this build path is teacher-free at the gradient, not teacher-free in the information. What it genuinely buys is a licensing story, freedom from hosting a teacher, and freedom from the teacher's tokenizer and context limit.

Read it with a hard discount — one model, one unusual architecture whose scaling its own authors call unstudied, two target languages, self-reported, and no ablation dropping the 11.08% synthetic fraction, which is the single experiment that would say how much of the result it carries. [T2, single study]

Meanwhile the incumbent ordering picked up a third independent instrument on 2026-08-14, and this one is about the cheap version of the build path. Trustworthiness of compressed versus pretrained small models (T1, IJCNN 2026) puts a quantized 1.5B at 63.86% overall against a from-scratch 0.5B at 51.17% at comparable tokens per second, winning ethics (78.63 vs 65.71), privacy (40.38 vs 27.56), robustness (67.34 vs 62.42) and fairness (69.07 vs 48.99). Build path first, size second — now replicated on capability, on cost, and on trustworthiness. Bounded: one family for the head-to-head, no 2026-era from-scratch small model in the cohort, and a floor nobody states — sub-1B models absorb quantization far worse. [T1]

A second from-scratch family arrived 2026-09-22 and the shape of its caveat is a warning rather than a counter-example. K2-Horizon (T2, IFM/MBZUAI model-family release) pretrains four dense (0.9B–32B) and two sparse MoE (36B-A4B, 375B-A23B) models from scratch on ~20T tokens each, and its three smallest report size-class-leading agentic/coding scores (K2-Horizon-7B vs. Qwen3.5-9B: SWE-bench Verified 70.6% vs. 50.8%). The sharper fact for this article: the 375B-A23B flagship's own Terminal-Bench score needed correcting from 70.2% to 66.9% after the vendor's own reward-hacking audit removed 24 exploited trials — a disclosed benchmark-integrity failure on exactly the agentic evidence this craft leans on, and only the flagship's one score is stated to have been audited, so the same unaudited risk applies to every other number in the release. Read this alongside Mimir v1 below: both are from-scratch counter-arms to "distill, don't train," and both carry a discount that applies specifically to the evidence used to make the case. [T2]

And the advantage now has a location, which is the most useful refinement in this refresh. Phantom gains (T1, primary home evaluation-contamination) scores per problem rather than per mean against a frozen control: external distillation expands 8–11 problems on the rare tail; three self-training recipes expand 0–2 (p < 10⁻⁶), with the "it just gained more overall" alternative rejected at p < 10⁻⁸. On the ten problems the base model never reaches at all, it is inconclusive (p = 0.35). So distillation's edge is about where in the difficulty distribution the gain lands — and a difference of means cannot see it, which is probably why this argument stayed contested as long as it did. Scope: every damaged arm is iterated on the model's own outputs, so this is not a result about checker-verified RL. [T1]

The on-policy stage now has a leash, and both edges of its usable range are measured

The 2026-08-13 refresh could say the on-policy stage sharpens rather than adds. As of 2026-08-17 it can say what goes wrong and what to do about it.

SimpleOPD (T1, Shanghai AI Lab; seven students across five model families, same- and cross-tokenizer, code + weights released) distils from a 30B-A3B reasoning teacher that sustains 100K+ tokens of proof reasoning into ordinary instruct students, and direct on-policy distillation degenerates: average response length, truncation rate and repetition rate rise together, one student passing 40% truncation on AIME25 and 60% on AnswerBench with its score falling. The case studies are a correct answer followed by 972 exact self-check repeats, and a trace collapsing into a one-token loop.

Two fixes, and the second is the one to take away. Masking the loss on </think> and <|im_end|> lifts ProofBench@4 from 21.70 → 38.10 and does not stop the length growth. A student-reference KL loss — penalizing drift from the student's own initial policy, coefficient 0.5 — drives truncation to near zero and reaches 38.50. On a third student the coefficient shows an interior optimum (0.5 too small, 1.0 best, 1.2 too large), so the usable range of on-policy distillation is bounded on both sides and both edges have been measured. With both fixes the flagship student goes 21.70 → 44.50 ProofBench against the teacher's 45.00, and two students end above the teacher on AnswerBench and AIME25. Distillation length matters (6K → 32K: 38.80 → 44.50), and a stronger teacher from an unrelated family beat the in-house one at matched length — a small point against the novelty framing, or a large one about tokenizer diversity, and the paper does not separate them.

Discount it on four axes the authors leave open: the teacher and flagship student are the authors' own models, ProofBench is judge-scored, no compute, wall-clock or hardware figure appears anywhere, and the paper reports @4 and @8 averages only — leaving this article's central pass@K question untouched by a lab that was set up to answer it. [T1]

A second constraint arrived 2026-08-22 and it is a scope condition on the whole on-policy literature. GC-OPD (T1, Tsinghua; code released) measures, on frozen responses before any training, that the dense signal on-policy distillation learns from is teacher preference, not verified correctness — and that the two diverge as input length grows. Every on-policy result in this article was established at ordinary lengths. Its proposed fix is honest to the point of self-injury: raw 29.08 → on-policy distillation 39.31 → GC-OPD 40.47, so the distillation step is worth ~9 points and the calibration on top is worth +1.16, dropping to +0.97 against the strongest competing method — sub-point, single seed, no intervals, and eight 80 GB GPUs per run. What survives is the ablation, argued under identical conditions: an extra distillation-derived term is worth +0.04 (nothing), the direct verifier reward +0.63, and the signed teacher–verifier disagreement +1.10. Adding the verifier's opinion helps; adding its disagreement with the teacher helps nearly twice as much. [T1]

The teacher has a useful lifespan, and the signal it sends keeps getting decomposed further

Five sources landed on strand 2 between 2026-09-18 and 2026-10-05, none of them changing the headline, all of them narrowing what "the on-policy signal" actually is or how long it should run.

The teacher's supervision window has a measured stopping point. RetireOPD (T1, Zhejiang University + Alibaba, code released) tracks the teacher-student log-probability gap under joint GRPO+OPD training and finds it is not monotonic: it narrows early, then rebounds once reward optimization starts favoring actions the teacher itself would not take — so continued distillation past that point actively constrains the student (dropping OPD at the inflection point improves 76.6%→93.8% on one fork; continued joint training plateaus). Adaptively retiring the teacher — stop the first time the gap stops shrinking AND the student hits 90% of teacher performance, then continue with RL alone — beats plain GRPO by 14.1–18.8 ALFWorld points and 11.8–19.0 WebShop points across Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B, and the retired-teacher student surpasses its own teacher in every setting tested. A side finding sharpens the choose-a-teacher advice above: an unoptimized privileged-context teacher is not reliably better than the student at all (raw 7B teacher: 23.4% ALFWorld) until it is itself GRPO-trained first (79.7–90.6% after). [T1]

Reference-hindsight OPSD borrows the right answer without fixing the reasoning that got it wrong — and targeting the specific error instead of applying uniform hindsight fixes most of that. RC-OPD (T1, Renmin University of China / Peking University / UIBE) measures that hindsight pushes answer accuracy 71.7%→99.2%, but excluding responses with unresolved reference-borrowing drops that to 75.8% — nearly 24 of those points were the student reaching the right answer without correcting its own mistake. Root-Cause-Guided OPD locates the earliest substantive error in a failed attempt, generates a local repair, and supervises the erroneous segment and the valid prefix separately: +9.17pp over base on Qwen3-1.7B, +3.61pp over an OPSD baseline on 4B and 8B (Avg@4, AIME24/AIME25/HMMT-Feb2025). [T1]

On-policy's privileged-teacher signal bundles a correctness component with a behavioral one, and they can be separated. Contrastive self-distillation (T1, ETH Zurich + Max Planck) finds pulling a student toward a privileged "attractive" teacher imports its own behavioral drift (evaluation accuracy flattens), and pushing away from an unprivileged "repulsive" teacher imports a different one (unintended thinking-mode switches, length-truncation collapse) — contrasting the two (toward correct, away from incorrect) cancels the shared behavioral drift while compounding the correctness signal, stabilizing training on Qwen3-4B where either one-sided variant destabilizes. This sits alongside, unresolved against, this map's entropy-suppression account and RouteOPD's destination-specificity account of what the teacher signal does — a third description, not yet tested against the other two. [T1]

Reference-free self-distillation already captures most of the achievable gain, and most of what a reference buys is cross-mode access, not new information. AMPLE-Math (T1) runs the privileged-information reconstructibility ranking this article's "rank the signal" item called for, under one controlled protocol: on Qwen3-1.7B, reference-free self-distillation alone reaches most of the gain (+1.80 points at step 100), and the best reference adds only +1.30 more without surviving multiple-comparison correction. The paper's own read is that a reference mostly helps a thinking-capable student reach reasoning its own thinking mode already has while training on direct-response rollouts — cross-mode access, not new content. A second model (SmolLM3-3B) warns that a reference ranking read at one checkpoint need not hold at another: every configuration there falls below the frozen base by step 100. [T1]

Termination-token mismatch is a second, previously unattributed cause of the length-inflation failure this article already carries a fix for — and the new fix doesn't fully close it either. EOS-mismatch (T1) finds a student and teacher can declare identical EOS stopping sets and still place their stopping probability on different members of that set (one Qwen3 student: ~0.8 on <|endoftext|>; its teacher: ~10⁻¹¹ on the same token, preferring <|im_end|>) — so aligning the declared sets, which is what most pipelines check, does nothing. Treating equivalent EOS tokens as one shared "stop" action substantially cuts the inflation across Qwen3/Llama/Gemma, but a second, later-training re-inflation persists and the authors say outright they cannot fully explain it. [T1]

Multi-teacher is currently losing to a merge command

New 2026-08-21, and it is the most uncomfortable result in this refresh.

Open-MOPD (T1, Tsinghua AIR + ByteDance Seed; recipe, trajectories and eval suites open-sourced) consolidates three domain-expert RL teachers (math, code, instruction-following) into one student with oracle routing by ground-truth domain label, so routing error is removed by construction. It recovers 35.6% of the gap to an oracle router. Distilling each domain into its own student reaches 88.0% — so teachers, initialization and objective are each sufficient, and the score collapses specifically when three sufficient signals are combined.

The obvious explanation is tested and rejected: token-level teacher disagreement is not the bottleneck. What is measured instead is arithmetic. 99% of gradient tokens go to math and code and ~1% to instruction-following, because reasoning traces are structurally longer than instruction-following answers and a per-batch token quota cannot tell the difference. IF ends 6.16 points below its reference — 3.3× the math gap — and is the only domain whose score falls mid-training. Pin the weighted token share at 33.33% and you get +1.17 points "almost entirely from IF", which is what a budget-starvation story predicts and a conflict story does not.

Generalize past the paper, because the same bug is everywhere: any multi-source training signal weighted by token count is implicitly weighted by verbosity, and it is invisible unless somebody plots the per-domain token share — which is not a standard diagnostic.

And then the row the paper reports without comment: training-free task-arithmetic weight merging recovers 71.7% — twice naive distillation — while the fully repaired three-teacher recipe beats it by 0.78 total points after 300 steps on eight A100s, on top of having trained the three experts first. So as of 2026-08 naive multi-teacher distillation is worse than a mergekit command, and the repaired version restores parity rather than winning. Any multi-teacher result published without a merge baseline is unevaluated. Bounded: one 3B student. [T1]

Two more arrivals sharpen why combination keeps failing, and what "failing" has been hiding. Multi-teacher OPD mechanisms (T1), using four domain-specialist RL teachers on a Qwen3-1.7B student, finds the combination step is governed by implementation choices that silently reweight whose signal wins: global token-level loss averaging favors longer-response domains over per-domain averaging; Adam's momentum smooths updates into apparent cross-teacher agreement that isn't really there; and BF16 rounding hides most of the real change — ~97% of parameters move from initialization in FP32, but only 7–11% differ after BF16 rounding, meaning every single-teacher OPD result elsewhere on this map that was only checked at reduced precision has an unexamined blind spot. Momentum-free SGD with per-domain averaging is the best configuration found, lifting a four-task mean from 33.93% to 39.94%. AMD (T1, Harbin Institute of Technology) trains two differently-task-balanced models, mutually distills between them with probe-adjusted per-task weights, and merges the result — +2.91 points average over multi-task SFT across three backbones and six benchmarks. It is the mirror case of the open-MOPD finding above at the training-recipe level rather than the teacher-signal level: combining multiple sources again turns out to be governed by implementation choices (here, balancing-strategy pairing and distillation-weight schedule), and AMD reports no raw weight-merge-without-distillation control, so by this article's own standard — training-free merging alone already recovered 71.7% of a comparable gap above — its 2.91-point gain is not yet isolated from what merging might buy for free. [T1 x2]

If you are chaining compressions, supervise every step from the original

New 2026-08-24, and it is a build-path rule this article did not have.

Quantization-aware healing (T1, Multiverse Computing; primary map post-training-quantization) runs a three-deep chain — structural compression 120B→60B, bfloat16 recovery, then 4-bit quantization — and finds that the intermediate recovered checkpoint must not become the teacher. It was never independently trained: its weights are derived by an operator and then tethered to the original's output distribution throughout recovery, so it carries the compressed architecture's ceiling by construction, and supervising from it caps the student there. Distilling the 4-bit student from the original instead — architectures differ, which distillation never required — yields a student matching or beating the bfloat16 checkpoint it was quantized from on 7 of 9 benchmarks.

General form: supervise every step of a compression chain from the original, or the chain compounds ceilings instead of capabilities. The load-bearing weakness is the authors' own first limitation — the head-to-head against distillation-from-the-recovered-checkpoint at matched configuration was never run, so the ceiling argument is well-motivated and undemonstrated. [T1]

The metric problem, restated by a review that ran nothing

New 2026-08-27, graded T3 and worth saying why. One Symptom, Three Levers is a critical review of on-policy self-distillation that states outright it reports no new experiments; every number in it is quoted from a primary this vault has not read. It is here because its instrument claim is checkable and none of the map's own primaries stated it.

Mean accuracy does not merely miss collapse — it moves the wrong way while collapse happens (pass@1 71.9 → 73.4 as pass@16 falls 83.6 → 78.5), and the obvious backstop fails too: the same collapsed model registers higher token entropy than its comparison at lower functional diversity. That is independent support, from a different literature, for this article's second imperative. It also splits the blame usefully: one cause family predates self-distillation entirely (the RL gradient erodes entropy; finite sampling eats distribution tails before modes), and one is specific — an over-informed teacher stops deliberating and the student inherits the missing uncertainty.

Its second claim is a decision procedure worth testing if you distil from a model conditioned on privileged information: rank the signal by whether the student could have reconstructed it, not by how much it tells them. Riskiest to safest — final answer, worked solution, chain of thought, plan, rubric, error feedback. Full demonstrations cost up to −17% avg@16 on thinking models; step-wise hints beat oracle answers 71.3 vs 59.5 on C-Eval.

Hold all of it loosely, and the review supplies the reason itself: AIME 2024 is partly in pretraining with models completing ~50% from memory, its 30-question format makes one flip worth >3 points, seed variation reaches ±15, checkpoint selection inflates a result from 41.1% at end of training to 57.2% best-over-checkpoints, and Qwen models gain from a random signal where Llama and OLMo do not — on a literature whose experiments are mostly Qwen at ≤8B. [T3]

What the last two weeks changed

The headline held. The reasons did not, and the most useful correction is that the obvious way to execute this advice is wrong.

Pick the teacher for what it knows, not for what it scores. Rethinking On-Policy Distillation (T1, 2026-04) runs the experiment nobody had: take a 1.5B student that reinforcement learning has already improved, and distill it toward a higher-scoring same-family 7B. It lands at nearly the identical level as one distilled toward its own weaker pre-RL checkpoint, because the two teachers are "distributionally indistinguishable from the student's perspective." Paired with the finding that post-trained teachers transfer far more than same-pipeline base ones, the operating statement becomes: what transfers is distributional novelty, not teacher rank. The failure is silent — a practitioner who upgraded their teacher and saw nothing would have no way to tell whether the recipe or the run was at fault. [T1]

That paper also supplies the mechanism, and it is cheap to instrument. The reward is carried by a token set the two models already share, holding 97–99% of both models' probability mass, with overlap climbing 72% → 91% in successful runs and flat from initialization in failing ones — and early mismatch "cannot be recovered later." So the overlap ratio is a free kill-the-run diagnostic, computable before you have spent anything. [T1]

The two-stage ordering is load-bearing, not conventional. The same study's recommended repair for a failing on-policy run — off-policy SFT on teacher rollouts first, worth 15–20 points of initial overlap and a higher final ceiling — is exactly the sequencing Qwen3 already ships. A third group derived the production recipe's shape from mechanism without setting out to. [T1 + T2]

Distill when the target has a checker; curate when it does not. LIMA (T1) is the clean loss: Alpaca-65B is the same LLaMa 65B base trained on 52,000 examples distilled from text-davinci-003, and LIMA's 1,000 human-curated examples beat it (53% win / 21% tie / 26% loss in human preference). Fifty-two times the data, from a teacher, beaten by curation. Set against R1's decisive win, the two are not contradictory — R1's teacher supplies traces a verifiable checker confirmed, where alignment style has no checker and the teacher's advantage is much thinner. [T1]

There is a horizon, and it arrives where reasoning traces are heading. The on-policy signal's per-token quality decays with depth: teacher continuation advantage +0.37 at a 1K-token prefix → +0.02 at 16K, usable window 3K–7K, plateau or decline by 10K–15K. The authors say the technique "may not extend cleanly to longer-horizon settings such as extended chain-of-thought or agentic multi-turn interaction," and flag their own explanation as unverified. One consolation for anyone reading this vault: 3K–7K is also roughly where a local fine-tune's activation memory is still tractable, so a practitioner on one or two cards is unlikely to meet the ceiling — and equally unable to observe it. [T1, suggestive]

The contamination audit this map has wanted since coining now costs two eval runs. Distillation sets are teacher generations, teachers have seen the benchmarks, and a student scoring well on a suite its teacher memorized measures the wrong thing. As of 2026-07-30 that check needs no corpus, no log-probs and no reference model: memorized answers are brittle to sampling temperature and honest ones are not — scores hold flat from greedy through τ ≤ 0.56 then fall sharply, and at high contamination going to τ = 1.0 costs ~40×, collapsing to roughly uncontaminated performance while clean models barely move. So: score the student and its teacher on the same benchmark at τ=0 and τ=1.0 and read the gap. The benchmark family that was measured is MATH, which is precisely what this article's headline rests on. [T1] (evaluation-contamination)

The bill, and two ways to cut it

Everything above is about quality. As of 2026-08 the vault also knows what the run costs and where the memory actually goes, which it did not in July.

The teacher does not need to be resident, and the thing that caps your context length is the loss, not the model. Offline Top-k Logit Distillation (T1) reports both halves. In the off-policy stage the teacher is a pure function of the input, so compute its logits once and cache the top-100 per token: peak memory 103 GB → 78 GB, iteration time 25.9s → 18.5s, throughput 237 → 331 TFLOP/s on a single H200 at 8K context, at "near-identical loss." Separately, computing the KL divergence naively materializes a [batch × sequence × vocabulary] tensor that peaks at ~250 GB at 32K — more than the card — while a fused chunked formulation that never builds it peaks at ~128 GB, buying 4× the training context on one GPU. Isolated, dense KL fails at 64K where the fused version peaks at 11.6 GiB.

Note what that second finding corrects. An 8B teacher and a 3.2B student are not what needs 103 GB — a vocabulary-sized logit tensor is. The fused loss is exact (an algebraic rewrite of the same gradient) and attacks a memory term the distributed-training literature never named, so it belongs at the front of the spend-the-exact-levers-first ordering. Two honest discounts: the caching win is confined to the off-policy stage — a cache cannot answer for states the student has not visited — and "near-identical loss" is a training-health proxy with no downstream benchmark behind it, which is the exact proxy this vault distrusts most. Every absolute number is 141 GB datacenter hardware; the mechanism travels, the figures do not. [T1, one study]

There is also a producer-side answer that deletes the teacher-residency question rather than optimizing it. Matryoshka Language Model Suites (T1) stacks the sizes inside each other — the 500M is a subset of the 1.5B, a subset of the 3B — trains end to end, and lets the largest distil into every smaller one at every step: parity with independently trained baselines at 36% less training compute, with no teacher to keep resident and no traces to generate. It requires training from scratch, which is the one move this vault's premise says its readers cannot make, so read it as a claim about what will be on the shelf to download, not about what you do afterwards. It is also an ambiguous test of the novelty account — a nested teacher is the low-novelty extreme and the suite still reaches parity, which is equally consistent with novelty failing at that end or with the nested teacher transferring nothing and shared compute doing all the work. The abstract reports no no-distillation nested arm. [T1, single study]

A related recipe is now being re-purposed for a different problem entirely: training-time-quantization reports OnlineQAT sampling a quantized student's own rollouts for a reverse-KL recovery stage after quantization-aware training — the same on-policy mechanism this article tracks, aimed at a bit-width recovery problem rather than a capability-transfer one. It repeats the map's existing on-policy-recovery result cleanly at W3A16 (+2.90 avg over the best fixed-completion baseline) but the gain nearly vanishes at W2A16 (+0.44, trailing on 3 of 6 benchmarks) — and because it uses reverse KL specifically, the KL-direction finding above predicts exactly this kind of ceiling without yet being able to confirm it, since OnlineQAT runs no forward-KL ablation.

The same ordering keeps turning up outside distillation

Worth knowing because it is free corroboration of the article's least intuitive advice.

ScaleQ (T1, Intel Labs China) is a quantization calibration paper — no gradients, no student, a solver fitting a compressed model to reproduce a full-precision one's activations. Calibrating a 4B model with chain-of-thought traces from DeepSeek-R1-671B yields 20.06% mean accuracy across five reasoning and coding benchmarks; calibrating it with traces the 4B model wrote itself yields 45.60%. A 25.54-point gain for using the far weaker model's own distribution. Two literatures with no shared citations reach one rule: any procedure that fits one model against another's outputs wants the target's own distribution, not the best distribution available. The set of procedures with that shape — distillation, quantization calibration, draft-model training for speculative decoding, adapter initialization on synthetic data — is longer than either paper's scope, and exactly two have been checked. [T1]

And a fourth axis on the novelty framing from an unexpected place: RestoreKV (T1) self-distils a model against its own frozen weights, differing only in what the two are allowed to remember — teacher on the full KV cache, student on a compressed one — training eight restore tokens plus a rank-8 adapter (0.4% of parameters, ~2 hours on one card) for +35 points on RULER-4K at a 5% cache budget. The teacher is not better, only less compressed, so what transfers is information the student can no longer reach rather than skill or rank. It also repeats this article's recurring transfer limit: the module does not survive a change of compressor (trained on SnapKV, run on KVzip: 42.0 against 73.2). [T1]

Five more angles since mid-October: the stage split pushes one stage earlier, a selective guidance mechanism wins on tool use, a training-free fusion handles the common merge case, teacher reliability crosses into vision, and the cost lever this article has lacked finally arrives

New 2026-10-14 through 2026-10-18. The first three extend strand 2's transfer mechanism into territory this article hadn't reached (tool use, continued pretraining, same-base-model merging); the fourth names a failure mode this article's text-only entries had never separated out; the last two are the first sources on this map that spend the on-policy stage's own generation budget differently, rather than changing what it transfers.

A selective, token-level teacher-guidance mechanism beats SFT, GRPO, KD, and on-policy distillation itself — on tool use, not this article's usual math/reasoning target. SeTPO (T1, Nanjing Univ. of Aeronautics & Astronautics et al.) adds reverse-KL, token-level teacher feedback to a student's policy-gradient advantage specifically on the student's own low-reward trajectories, plus an auxiliary forward-KL term keeping the student near teacher-demonstrated behavior throughout. On BFCL-v4 and API-Bank tool-use benchmarks with Qwen2.5-Instruct students (0.5B/1.5B/3B), it beats SFT, GRPO, SFT+GRPO, KD, and OPD at every tested scale — the 0.5B student gains +8.32 points over GRPO and +2.82 over OPD on BFCL-v4, and the 3B student's API-Bank score (63.82) exceeds its own 14B teacher's (62.48). The selectivity (teacher guidance only where the student is already failing) is a different axis from DIAL-OPD's random-vs-weighted token retention above — one decides which trajectories get teacher help, the other decides which tokens within a rollout — and the two are untested together. [T1]

The stage-split finding (off-policy adds capability, on-policy sharpens it) now has an instance one stage earlier than anything else on this map: continued pretraining, not fine-tuning. PA1-OPD adds exactly one student-sampled branch token after selected pretraining-corpus prefixes, scores it against a frozen teacher via a differentiable tree-attention mask that shares the real-prefix KV cache (so the extra supervision costs roughly 10-100% more forward-state compute, not a full second rollout). On OLMo-2 (7B teacher → 1B student), continued pretraining to 10B tokens: selecting the 10% of branch roots with highest student surprisal beats plain corpus-path KD's held-out macro cross-entropy at every checkpoint — but the gain is 2.531810 → 2.531635 nats, real in direction and tiny in size, single-run, with no comparison to this article's existing full-rollout OPD entries and no downstream task measured. The authors themselves say their state-count accounting "does not establish compute-efficiency gains." [T1, single-study]

A training-free, closed-form fusion handles on-policy distillation's most common merge case — same-base-model specialists — and the paper's own headline finding is about data hygiene, not the solver. Training-Free OPD shows that when specialists being merged share a frozen base model, the on-policy-distillation merge objective has an exact per-layer least-squares solution from second-moment statistics of a few hundred forward-pass rollouts per specialist — no backward pass, no training loop. On Olmo 3 7B, a few GPU-hours of forward-only computation per specialist keeps most of each specialist's gains across seven math/code benchmarks. What actually moves the number from task-arithmetic level (plain weight averaging) up to the specialist's own AIME25 score is not the solver — it's filtering out rare, token-heavy, repetition-looped rollouts before computing the statistics. This is the article's clearest instance yet of the per-domain-token-share bug (Open-MOPD, above) having a cheap, mechanical fix once you know to look for it: a hygiene pass on the inputs to a method, rather than a better method. The restriction to same-base-model specialists is load-bearing — the exact solution does not claim to extend past it — and no head-to-head wall-clock exists yet against this article's training-based OPD entries. [T1]

On-policy distillation's teacher-reliability dependency — previously argued only as "is the teacher correct" — gets a second, independent axis: "does the teacher finish," and it crosses into vision-language models for the first time. On-policy distillation inherits the teacher in VLMs (T1, code released) runs the vanilla OPD-vs-SFT comparison on two VLM tasks with one teacher. Where the teacher reliably finishes its own chain-of-thought (ChartQA), OPD at 10% of the data lands within 1.4 points of full-data SFT — consistent with this article's existing small-sample OPD wins. Where the teacher frequently fails to finish (Geometry3K, 22% of its own solutions incomplete), OPD trails full-data SFT by 7.9 points, traced mostly to the incomplete responses specifically. The sharper finding: starting OPD from a student already fine-tuned by SFT — the natural-seeming warm start — lowers the student's own completion rate further, from 76% to 62%, so on-policy training on top of an already-adapted student compounds the teacher's incompleteness instead of correcting it. Filtering teacher demonstrations for completeness before training recovers most of SFT's advantage. [T1]

And the cost lever this article has lacked — a method that changes how much of the on-policy rollout itself gets generated, rather than what it transfers — finally arrives, from two directions that spend the same budget oppositely. STOPD samples a random early-stopping position per rollout before generation starts, corrects the resulting bias with blockwise inverse-probability weighting proven to preserve vanilla OPD's expected gradient exactly (with one-step update-error bounds for SGD, Adam, and Muon), and cuts generated tokens 25-48% for 1.3-2.2x training speedups at comparable accuracy, across multiple teacher-student pairs, task domains, and optimizers. It is a pure generation-cost reduction, orthogonal to the rollout-hygiene filtering that Training-Free OPD (above) found to matter more than the solver — nobody has tested whether randomly truncating a rollout before it reveals itself as a repetition loop interacts with a hygiene filter built to catch exactly that. Token-level teacher selection spends in the opposite direction: it names mid-rollout teacher-switching as an unexploited OPD design axis (the teacher only needs to score tokens the student already generated, so nothing requires one fixed teacher per rollout), builds a compatibility framework around three competing goals — reasoning-pattern alignment, teacher competence, and induced diversity — and its best routing scheme beats vanilla single-teacher OPD and, on some tasks, the standalone teacher experts themselves by up to 10 points pass@k across math, coding, kernel generation, and visual reasoning. Beating the teachers outright is the surprising half: it implies routing-induced diversity lets the student find solution paths no single teacher reliably reaches. The two papers are not tested against each other and the gain is reported as a best-case figure, not a uniform one — but between them, this map's on-policy stage now has a cheaper way to run and a more expensive way to run better, with no evidence yet on whether either composes with the other or with anything above. [T1 x2]

Six more angles since late October: OPD moves up a level to multi-agent teams, merging and multi-teacher OPD turn out to be the same objective, advantage-weighting replaces uniform imitation, per-token teacher selection beats per-response, consolidating specialists back into one MoE base is itself an OPD problem, and a pre-training metric finally predicts teacher choice

New 2026-10-19 through 2026-11-01. None of these move the headline; together they close out this article's lingering teacher-choice and multi-teacher questions and open one new structural setting (agent hierarchies) this article hadn't reached.

On-policy distillation moves one level up the agent hierarchy — a frozen Teacher trained on a simpler two-role workflow can supply token-level process supervision to Searcher agents inside a more complex Leader-Searcher-Answerer team, trained end to end by RL on top. MultiAgent-OPD (T1) gives Searcher agents inside a hierarchical multi-agent QA system their training signal from on-policy distillation against a frozen, task-trained Teacher (a simpler Searcher-Answerer policy), rather than from the diluted team-outcome reward or an unreliable LLM-judge score, while the Leader and Answerer still train on team-success and candidate-correctness rewards. Across seven open-domain QA benchmarks on Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, the full system reaches 38.68% macro avg@4, beating a prompted-critic baseline by 4.06 points and a trained cascaded multi-agent-RL baseline by 2.61 points; an ablation shows the gain splits roughly evenly between using a task-trained Teacher rather than the untrained base model (29.50% avg@4 if swapped) and giving Searchers direct distillation-based supervision at all (29.82% if removed). This is the mechanism this article already tracks (teacher scores a student's own rollouts token-by-token) applied one structural level up — one base model, one domain, no test of transfer to other agent roles or model families. [T1]

Model merging and multi-teacher on-policy distillation turn out to be the same objective at different orders of approximation, and the cheap second-order version already matches or beats the expensive training loop it approximates. A Fisher-Geometric View of Multi-Teacher OPD (T1) generalizes this article's 2026-10-16 same-base-model fusion result (an exact closed-form solve for merging specialists sharing one frozen base) from single-domain fusion to the full multi-teacher case: within a shared basin of related specialists, multi-teacher OPD is, to second order, the same objective as Fisher-weighted merging. RGBM (Residual-Governed Branch-and-Merge) restricts the Fisher merge to the task-vector subspace, solves per-domain mixing coefficients from unlabelled prompts in under 2 GPU-hours, then decides per domain whether to passivate, isolate, or admit a write-back with calibrated shrinkage. On Open-MOPD (SmolLM3-3B, three specialists), the cheap Phase-1 merge alone (32.52) already beats a fully-trained multi-teacher-OPD student released at 144 GPU-hours (30.93) — the closed-form solve outperforms the training loop — and the gated Phase-2 write-back adds a further +0.51 for zero extra joint-training steps; naive write-back variants (TIES, DARE, uniform distillation) all score below the Phase-1 merge, so gating is what makes write-back a net positive. This sharpens this article's existing "never run multi-teacher distillation without a weight-merge baseline" imperative into a stronger claim: for same-basin specialists, the merge may not just be a baseline to beat, it may already win. Scoped to specialists sharing a common base; tested on two model families, two-to-three specialists each; no independent replication of the central equivalence claim. [T1]

Standard on-policy distillation imitates the teacher at every state regardless of whether the teacher's choice actually helps the student — weighting each teacher action by its own counterfactual advantage under the student, estimated for free from the student's own rollouts, beats both OPD and RL baselines on most tested benchmarks. AOPD (T1) opens a new axis inside the same KL-direction/token-selection thread this article already tracks: not which tokens or which divergence, but whether the teacher should be trusted at a given state at all. The exact advantage would need extra rollouts per alternative teacher action; AOPD's estimator instead uses one ordinary student rollout and its terminal reward, proven unbiased, with no value function or extra sampling. Against an external teacher (Qwen3-1.7B student), AOPD beats forward-KL OPD by 2.9-5.2 points across AIME24/AMC/Open-Thoughts/HumanEval and beats GRPO and reverse-KL OPD on every tested math benchmark, though it loses the best HumanEval pass@10 to GRPO and the best MultiPL-E average to forward-KL OPD. In self-distillation on Qwen3-8B it beats OPSD by up to 10.4 points (AIME24). The authors disclose exceptions directly — it trails its own base model on one bio benchmark at one model size, and narrowly loses to OPSD on MMLU-Pro — so this is not reported as a uniform win. Untested against this article's other token-selection and KL-direction levers. [T1]

Which teacher helps most varies within a single rollout, not just response to response, and the teacher helping most at a given position tends to be the one farthest from the student in KL divergence, not the nearest. MTA-OPSD (T1, code released) directly intervenes to measure this: across seven same-weights/different-exposure teacher configurations, preferred-teacher overlap between positions in the same rollout is only ~48-52% (Qwen3-1.7B). A per-token majority-vote-then-farthest-KL selection rule beats standard OPSD, SDPO and Paint at every tested Qwen3 scale on math reasoning — while naive multi-teacher exposure without the selection rule (random assignment, loss-averaging) underperforms even single-teacher OPSD, confirming the selection mechanism itself does the work, not mere teacher multiplicity. This sharpens, rather than contradicts, this article's existing teacher-choice advice ("choose by distributional distance, not leaderboard position") into a per-token question the response-level framing couldn't see. Neither this paper nor token-level-teacher-selection-on-policy-distillation (2026-10-18, above) tests against the other — one routes between same-weights-different-exposure configurations, the other between separate standalone experts — so whether the two per-token selection mechanisms compose is untested. [T1]

Consolidating several already-trained domain specialists back into one shared, dynamically-routed MoE base is itself an on-policy-distillation problem, and the fix that matters most is calibrating each teacher's own loss scale, not the token-selection restriction around it. turing-llm-calibrated-distillation-dynamic-moe (T1, arXiv 2610.39876, XPeng Inc.) poses a question this article's multi-teacher strand had not previously carried: not which teacher trains a token, but how to merge several independently post-trained specialists (math, code, instruction-following, agent) into one shared MoE base without one domain's signal swamping another's. On-policy, student-generated-prefix supervision against each assigned teacher's local top-K support, with each token's loss calibrated by that teacher's own loss scale, retains 90-114% of each specialist's own gain — exceeding the standalone Code/Agent specialists on their own scores — and the calibration step is nearly as load-bearing as the top-K-support restriction itself (removing either costs close to the same). One in-house model, no code/weights released, no independent replication, math-only/Qwen3-only in this article's other multi-teacher entries' sense of scope. [T1]

A pre-training metric finally predicts which teacher will actually help a given OPD student — answering a question this article has carried unresolved since the 2026-04 "distributional novelty, not teacher rank" finding. opd-entropy-shape-correlation-teacher-selection (T1, code released) separates two things OPD transfers: which tokens look good (the existing overlap-based account this article already carries) and where the student should stay uncertain at all — a new "entropy shape" (the sequence of per-position next-token entropies). Measured as Entropy Shape Correlation (ESC) between teacher and student before training starts, the second property predicts which of several candidate teachers will help a given student with near-perfect rank agreement (Spearman ρ = 1.00 within each of two student families, zero selection regret) — where this article's existing token-overlap diagnostic, raw entropy gap, teacher accuracy, and teacher size are each inconsistent across the two families tested. A causal transplant (swapping entropy shape while holding token-ranking fixed) recovers roughly half a strong teacher's gain from shape alone. This is the first metric in this article's teacher-selection thread to beat the token-overlap diagnostic head-to-head rather than merely corroborate it — but it is untested against MTA-OPSD's per-token farthest-KL switching (above): ESC ranks a whole teacher before training; MTA-OPSD switches between teachers mid-rollout during training. Whether a high-ESC teacher pool still benefits from per-token switching on top is unmeasured. Math-reasoning-only, two student families, 10 total teacher-pairings — the authors' own word for the teacher-selection result is preliminary. [T1]

Six more angles since late October through early November: black-box teachers get a step-validity signal, teacher adaptation and rollout coupling turn out to be mirror images, shared-input word order matters more than anyone expected, thinking-like behavior gets located in a LoRA subspace, and the right privileged-information strategy turns out to be a moving target

New 2026-11-02 through 2026-11-06. None of these move the headline; together they add a fifth lever to the article's teacher-selection/switching composition question and open one genuinely new question (does the best teaching strategy change as the student itself changes?).

For teachers that hide their own reasoning entirely — only a final answer and a brief numbered solution, no logits, no internal chain-of-thought — querying the teacher once per claimed step to independently re-derive and validate it beats three existing black-box baselines, and step validity turns out to be separable from final-answer correctness as a supervision signal. stepwise-querying-black-box-reasoning-distillation (T1, arXiv 2610.41769, Beijing Normal University) targets a harder constraint than this article's other teacher-selection sources assume: a commercially realistic black-box teacher (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GLM-5.2) that never exposes its own chain-of-thought. The step-requerying method beats Trace Inversion, Answer-Conditioned CoT, and GLOW in 14 of 16 student-teacher-benchmark cells, by 3.7-16.3 points over the undistilled checkpoint. The sharper result, on GSM8K: training on trajectories with a wrong final answer but valid intermediate steps beats training on correct-answer-only data by 0.6-1.5 points, while heavily corrupted steps hurt even under a correct label — step validity and final-answer correctness are separable axes of supervisory value, a distinction this article's existing contamination-audit (score at τ=0 and τ=1.0) does not capture. Two teachers, two students, four benchmarks; untested against the ESC/farthest-KL teacher-selection levers below. [T1]

A teacher-adaptation method and a rollout-sampling method turn out to be mirror images of the same fix, attacking the same root problem — the teacher's signal not reaching every state that matters — from opposite ends of the pipeline. spta-student-prefix-teacher-adaptation (T1, arXiv 2611.03133, Beijing Jiaotong University/Weixin AI Tencent) adapts an OPD teacher via RLVR to continue from the student's own truncated rollout prefixes, then freezes it; this beats both plain OPD and a task-adapted (clean-prompt) teacher given the same RL training, isolating the gain specifically to prefix-matching — the untrained teacher's own accuracy collapses as a forced continuation prefix lengthens, which is exactly the failure prefix-adaptation targets. cord-coupled-rollouts-on-policy-distillation (T1, arXiv 2611.07959, Tsinghua/HIT/Nanjing/Huawei) measures the same underlying blind spot directly — a student starts 1.5-2.4x further from the teacher on frozen teacher-reached states than on its own states, and standard OPD closes only 15.0% of that gap in 40 steps — then fixes it without touching the teacher at all: CoRD changes only the rollout sampler, via the maximal coupling speculative decoding uses to keep a draft model's output exact, closing 21.7% of the same gap in the same 40 steps and reaching in 16 steps what plain OPD does not reach in 80. SPTA moves the teacher toward the student; CoRD moves the student's sampler toward the teacher. Neither paper cites or tests against the other, so this article's standing SPTA/ESC/MTA-OPSD/word-shuffling composition question now carries a fifth lever with no head-to-head against any of the other four. [T1 x2]

Shuffling the word order of the problem statement both the student and teacher see helps; shuffling the teacher-only privileged reference instead hurts — the two sides of the on-policy self-distillation training interface respond in opposite directions to the identical kind of disruption. shuffled-opsd-semantic-consistency (T1, arXiv 2611.04958, Duke University) finds word-level shuffling of the shared problem statement improves mean accuracy over standard OPSD at every tested scale (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B; largest gain +3.52 points at 1.7B), while shuffling the teacher-only privileged worked example reduces accuracy at every scale. A control that replaces rather than shuffles the teacher-only reference is roughly neutral — the privileged side cares about structure staying intact, the shared side cares about word order specifically, not content. A fourth, orthogonal lever on the composition question: input structure of the shared problem, still untested against teacher selection, teacher switching, or either of this refresh's two new teacher/sampler levers. Gains are modest (1.1-3.5 points), measured after a short fixed budget (50 steps), math-only. [T1]

Interpolated-teacher distillation's "thinking-like" behavior lives in a shared, low-dimensional subspace of the student's own LoRA adapter, and removing that subspace turns the behavior off. distilled-students-thinking-lora-subspace (T1, arXiv 2611.04141, Chosun University/Korea University Sejong, code released; cross-link peft-methods) compares LoRA adapters of three students trained on three interpolated-teacher pairs (a large reasoning model blended with a short-answer model at ratio λ) and finds a shared update subspace (eigenvalue ranks 5-8 of 16 directions, 47-72% of update energy) that tracks exactly where the teacher stops "thinking" — removing it turns thinking-like behavior off in all 9 tested cells. This is a second, methodologically distinct "where does distilled behavior live" instrument on this map, alongside 2026-11-02's KV-cache-patching result on kv-cache-management; the two are not tested against each other. LoRA-specific, 9 cells, math reasoning only. [T1]

Which privileged-information strategy a black-box teacher should supply is not fixed — it is a moving target that shifts as the student itself improves, which raises a scope question about every fixed-lever comparison in this article's teacher-selection literature. evoopd-evolving-privileged-information-opd (T1, arXiv 2611.06542, nine authors/seven institutions) works on black-box teachers whose logits are inaccessible, converting rich textual feedback into privileged information (PI) via a PI-conditioned privileged teacher. Different PI strategies (varying in focus, information type, granularity) have substantially different effects on the current student, and their relative ranking shifts as training progresses — so EvoOPD maintains a PI Strategy Bank that adapts using the measured repair utility of PI on the current student state, rather than committing to one strategy at the start. Across six model-task cells (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B × math/code) it wins every cell, beating the strongest baseline by 2.14 points average in math and 1.30 in code. This sits upstream of, rather than alongside, the SPTA/ESC/MTA-OPSD/word-shuffling composition question above: if PI effectiveness genuinely drifts with student state, the article's existing fixed-lever teacher-selection comparisons may be snapshotting a moving target — this vault's inference, not the source's claim. Modest, consistent gains (1.3-2.1 points), single-paper, no code release. [T1]

What to do

  • Before running on-policy distillation at all, check the initial teacher-student output-token overlap. Below a high-overlap threshold, sampling once from the initial student, scoring once with the teacher, and training on the fixed set beats continually re-sampling from the evolving student — by up to 13.6% accuracy at up to 11.4x less compute — because continual re-sampling drifts further off-policy relative to the teacher as responses lengthen. Above high overlap, standard on-policy training earns its cost back. [T1]
  • A teacher far away in parameter space can make on-policy distillation barely train at all, independent of how good the teacher is. Final loss reduction averages 96.2% against a close same- family RL-trained teacher versus 25.1% against a separately-scaled larger one — a training-dynamics failure, not a transfer-content one, so check teacher proximity before diagnosing a disappointing run as a data or recipe problem. [T1]
  • Not every token in an on-policy rollout is worth training on, and the obvious selection rule is wrong. Picking the highest-reward-magnitude tokens underperforms random retention; weighting by the logarithmic mean of student/teacher probability (peaking at a theoretically-derived fixed point) beats full-token training by up to 5.25 points and is untested in combination with the KL-direction lever above. [T1]
  • For cross-tokenizer distillation, align chunks by shared text spans, then solve credit assignment separately. Redistribute an aligned chunk's advantage across student tokens by predictive uncertainty and local teacher-signal smoothness; anchor unaligned tokens to the student's own initial policy via a KL penalty — the same anchoring mechanism that fixes runaway length and repetition elsewhere in this article, independently reinvented for a different failure mode. [T1]
  • Prefer forward KL, or an off-policy stage, when the goal is new facts; reverse-KL OPD mainly reorganizes what the student already knows. The split is 3-5x in the one controlled measurement, and three independent instruments now agree on the mechanism. [T1]
  • Before trying to fix on-policy training's forgetting, price a free weight merge against it. Grafting — learn the update on an earlier pretraining checkpoint, never the deployed model, merge it in — Pareto-dominates both SFT and OPSD on new-task performance and retention at ~1/10 OPSD's training cost, on the one paper that has tested it. [T1, single-study]
  • Retire the teacher once the student catches up, don't supervise indefinitely. The teacher-student gap under joint RL+OPD training rebounds rather than keeps shrinking; stopping OPD at that inflection point and continuing with RL alone beat continuous joint training by double digits and surpassed the trained teacher in every setting tested. [T1]
  • Any multi-teacher OPD comparison needs its optimizer, precision and loss-averaging scheme stated, not just its results. Momentum, BF16 rounding and token-level vs. per-domain averaging each silently reweight which teacher's signal wins, and a BF16-only check can hide ~90% of the real parameter movement. [T1]
  • Reference-free self-distillation is a reasonable default, not just a cheaper fallback — it already captures most of the achievable gain in the one controlled comparison, with a privileged reference adding a smaller, checkpoint-dependent increment on top. [T1]
  • Treat EOS tokens as a shared semantic action across models, not a set-membership check. A student and teacher can declare identical stop tokens and still disagree about which one to actually use, and that mismatch drives length inflation independent of the capability-vs-reliability split above. [T1]
  • Leash the on-policy stage to the student's own initial policy. Unconstrained on-policy distillation from a much longer-reasoning teacher degenerates — 40% truncation, 972 exact self-check repeats, one-token loops — and a student-reference KL term drives truncation to near zero and lifts ProofBench@4 21.70 → 44.50. The coefficient has an interior optimum (0.5 too small, 1.0 best, 1.2 too large), so sweep it rather than picking one. [T1]
  • Never run multi-teacher distillation without a weight-merge baseline, and plot your per-domain token share before blaming the teachers. Training-free task arithmetic recovers 71.7% of the oracle gap against naive multi-teacher distillation's 35.6%; the repaired recipe wins by 0.78 points after 300 steps on eight A100s. The bug is that 99% of gradient tokens went to the verbose domains, because a per-batch token quota is implicitly a verbosity weighting. [T1]
  • Supervise every step of a compression chain from the original model, not from the intermediate recovered checkpoint. The intermediate was never independently trained, so it carries the compressed architecture's ceiling and caps the student there. Architectures are allowed to differ; distillation never required otherwise. Well-motivated, and the matched head-to-head was not run. [T1]
  • If your teacher is conditioned on privileged information, rank the signal by reconstructibility. Final answer is the riskiest thing to show it, error feedback the safest; full demonstrations cost up to −17% avg@16 on thinking models. A decision procedure to test, not a settled result. [T3]
  • At long input lengths, stop treating teacher preference as correctness. The two are measurably different signals and they diverge as context grows — a scope condition on every on-policy result established at ordinary lengths. Adding a verifier's disagreement with the teacher is worth roughly twice adding the verifier's opinion alone (+1.10 vs +0.63 in a matched ablation). [T1]
  • Spend the off-policy stage first. It is where capability enters. On-policy logit matching sharpens what the student already reaches and, run alone, can leave it worse at large sampling budgets than the checkpoint you started from. [T1]
  • Report pass@K at a large K before you believe your own distillation result. pass@1 and averaged scores measure how reliably a model reaches what it can reach; only a large-budget pass rate measures what it can reach at all, and the two came apart by 16.7 points in the one study that looked. [T1]
  • When you need a small reasoning model, default to distilling a bigger one's traces via plain SFT before reaching for RL on the small model. Exploration is expensive and scale-dependent; imitation is cheap at any scale. [T1]
  • Choose the teacher by distributional distance from your student, not by leaderboard position. A stronger model from the same family and pipeline may transfer nothing. Cheap proxy nobody has validated as a ranking but which separates working from failing pairs at initialization: token overlap on a few hundred prompts. [T1]
  • Cold-start off-policy before going on-policy. SFT on teacher rollouts first, then logit matching — the ordering is worth 15–20 points of initial overlap and a better final ceiling, and it is what the one shipping pipeline in evidence does. [T1 + T2]
  • Watch the overlap ratio and kill flat runs early. Early thinking-pattern mismatch does not converge out. [T1]
  • Keep on-policy responses in the 3K–7K range. Shorter under-supervises; past ~10K the signal thins. [T1, suggestive]
  • Treat access to teacher traces or logits as the real asset — not the teacher's training run. This is why open releases of strong reasoning models move the craft more than closed capability jumps. [T1 / T2]
  • Run the expensive recipe once, on the teacher, then distill outward. Qwen3's ~10× GPU-hour saving is the shape of the argument, not a universal constant. [T2]
  • Cache the teacher's top-k logits offline instead of keeping it resident, and fuse the KL loss rather than materializing a vocabulary-sized tensor. The first is off-policy-only and bought ~29% iteration time and 25 GB on one datacenter card; the second is exact and bought 4× the training context. Neither has been run on a consumer card by anyone. [T1, one study]
  • Calibrate, initialize and draft against your target's own distribution, not against the strongest model you can access. Established now in two unrelated literatures. [T1 ×2]
  • Audit before you believe your own number: score student and teacher at τ=0 and τ=1.0 on the benchmark your claim rests on. Two eval runs. [T1]
  • Distillation SFT at these student sizes is commonly a PEFT job — 4-bit-base LoRA on one card (peft-methods) — and the merged student then feeds the quantize-and-run-locally pipeline (local-inference).
  • If you're merging several same-base-model specialists into one generalist, try the closed-form forward-pass solve before a training loop. It needs only second-moment statistics from a few hundred rollouts per specialist and no backward pass — but filter repetition-looped, truncated rollouts out of those statistics first; that hygiene step, not the solver, is what separates task-arithmetic-level fusion from matching the specialist directly. [T1]
  • If your teacher sometimes fails to finish its own reasoning, don't warm-start on-policy distillation from an already-SFT'd student — it lowers completion rate further rather than fixing it. Filter teacher demonstrations for completeness before training on them instead. [T1]
  • If the on-policy stage's generation cost itself is the bottleneck, truncate rollouts randomly rather than shortening them for everyone — a provably unbiased correction (blockwise inverse- probability weighting) buys 25-48% fewer generated tokens at comparable accuracy. If capability rather than cost is the bottleneck, consider routing between multiple teachers mid-rollout instead — it can beat the teachers themselves, at the price of needing every candidate teacher servable during training. [T1 x2]
  • Before training a multi-teacher OPD student, price a Fisher-weighted merge against it, not just a naive weight average. For specialists sharing a common base in one basin, a closed-form Fisher merge solved from unlabelled prompts in under 2 GPU-hours can already beat a fully-trained multi-teacher-OPD student; a gated write-back on top adds a small further gain for zero extra joint-training steps. Naive write-back (TIES, DARE, uniform distillation) scores below the merge alone — gate it or skip it. [T1]
  • Weight teacher imitation by whether it actually helps the student, not uniformly at every state. An advantage-weighted OPD objective, estimated for free from the student's own rollouts and proven unbiased, beats uniform-imitation OPD and GRPO on most tested math/coding/medical-QA benchmarks — though not every metric, and the authors disclose the exceptions. [T1]
  • If you have several same-weights-but-differently-exposed teacher configurations, select per token, not per response, and prefer the farthest-KL teacher at each position, not the nearest. Preferred- teacher identity varies substantially within a single rollout (~48-52% overlap between positions); naive multi-teacher exposure without a selection rule underperforms single-teacher distillation. [T1]
  • If you're consolidating several already-trained domain specialists into one shared, dynamically- routed MoE base, calibrate each teacher's own loss scale — it matters nearly as much as restricting supervision to each teacher's local top-K support. Removing either one costs close to as much as removing the other. [T1]
  • Before running on-policy distillation, compute Entropy Shape Correlation (ESC) between each candidate teacher and the student, not just token overlap. ESC — a shift/scale-invariant correlation between teacher and student per-position entropy sequences, measured before training starts — predicted the best teacher with zero selection regret in the one study that tested it, where token overlap, entropy gap, teacher accuracy and teacher size were each inconsistent. Preliminary: 10 total teacher-pairings, math-reasoning only. [T1]
  • If your teacher hides its own chain-of-thought, query it once per claimed step to re-derive and validate rather than reconstructing a trace from the final answer alone — and keep trajectories with a wrong final answer but valid intermediate steps rather than discarding them; step validity is a separable supervision signal from final-answer correctness. [T1]
  • Teacher adaptation and rollout coupling are two ends of the same fix — pick whichever side of the pipeline is cheaper to touch. If you can retrain the teacher, adapt it via RLVR to continue from the student's own rollout prefixes (SPTA). If you cannot, or want zero teacher changes, steer the rollout sampler toward teacher-preferred states instead via a speculative-decoding-style coupling (CoRD) — both close the same blind spot (teacher-preferred states the student's own rollouts rarely reach) from opposite directions, and neither has been tested against the other. [T1 x2]
  • If you're shuffling anything in on-policy self-distillation, shuffle the shared problem statement, never the teacher-only privileged reference. Word-level shuffling of what student and teacher both see improves accuracy at every tested scale; the identical disruption applied to the privileged-only side instead hurts. [T1]
  • Don't assume your privileged-information strategy is still the right one once the student has improved. Which PI strategy helps most shifts with student state in the one study that tracked it; an adaptive strategy bank beat every fixed strategy in every tested cell. Treat this as a caution on reading any fixed-lever teacher-selection comparison (including several above) as a permanent ranking. [T1]

Not settled

  • The teacher-selection/switching composition question now has five untested levers, no two of which have been run against each other. Teacher selection by Entropy Shape Correlation (ESC, static, pre-training), per-token farthest-KL teacher switching (MTA-OPSD, dynamic, during training), shared- input word shuffling (fourth lever), teacher-prefix adaptation (SPTA), and rollout-sampler coupling (CoRD) each independently beat their own baseline, and the fifth (CoRD) is structurally the mirror image of the fourth-from-last (SPTA) — but nobody has composed any two of the five, so whether they stack, cancel, or make each other redundant is entirely open. EvoOPD's finding that the right privileged-information strategy shifts with student state (above) raises, without testing, whether any of these five comparisons would still rank the same way at a different point in training.
  • The overlap threshold and the parameter-distance mechanism have never been tested on the same pool of teacher-student pairs. Semi-OPD's token-overlap diagnostic and the vanishing-learning-signals paper's parameter-distance diagnostic are two different instruments for a related but currently unverified notion of teacher-student fit; whether they predict the same failures, or different ones, is unmeasured.
  • The token-selection axis (DIAL-OPD) and the KL-direction axis are untested in combination. Both operate inside the same on-policy stage — one decides which tokens of a rollout to train on, the other decides which divergence to train against — and nobody has run both levers together.
  • CAST's teacher-scaling effect is non-monotonic in student size, on one paper's own data. A larger teacher helps a 4B student and hurts a 1.7B one; whether that reverses again at other scales, or is an artifact of this paper's specific model pairs, is unmeasured.
  • Grafting is one paper's own evaluation. Headline numbers are read off bar-chart figures, not an exact results table; the donor-checkpoint-age sweep (earlier is better) is systematic only on OLMo-3; Qwen3 runs use whatever earlier checkpoint was on hand without the same sweep. Whether it replicates outside this paper decides whether the leash-and-repair literature above is solving the right problem.
  • The KL-direction split is demonstrated on synthetic facts, not prose-embedded ones. The digit lookup tables are a clean instrument precisely because they have no natural-language structure a model could generalize from — untested whether real factual claims embedded in context behave the same way under reverse- vs. forward-KL OPD.
  • Three accounts of what the on-policy teacher signal is doing — entropy suppression, destination- specific probability transport, and behavioral/correctness decomposition — have never been tested against each other's manipulation. Each is measured on its own benchmark set; nobody has run all three diagnostics on one training run to see whether they are describing the same phenomenon three ways or three different ones.
  • The BF16-masking finding is a candidate confound for every single-teacher OPD result on this map evaluated at reduced precision, and none of them has re-checked its weight deltas in FP32. Whether any reported effect size would read differently is unknown until somebody reruns one.
  • The EOS-mismatch fix doesn't fully work and nobody knows why. A second, later-training re-inflation persists after the shared-stop-token fix, and the paper's own authors say they cannot fully explain it — an open mechanism, not just an open replication.
  • Nobody has dropped the synthetic fraction. The one from-scratch small model that beats its distilled peers has 11.08% teacher-generated tokens in its corpus and no ablation removing them. Until that run exists, "trained from scratch" and "trained without a teacher" are not the same claim, and this article's headline is safe for a reason nobody has measured.
  • The multi-teacher repair restores parity, it does not win. A mergekit weight merge is currently the baseline to beat, and the fully repaired three-teacher recipe beats it by 0.78 points after training three experts and running 300 steps on eight A100s. Whether the ordering survives at other student sizes, other domain counts, or a merge baseline that has itself been tuned is unmeasured — and it is one 3B student.
  • The on-policy leash is one lab's fix on its own models. The student-reference KL result comes from a paper whose teacher and flagship student are the authors' own, scored by an LLM judge, with no compute or hardware figure reported anywhere — so the article gains a capability result and zero cost evidence. And that lab reports @4 and @8 only, leaving the pass@K question it was equipped to answer untouched.
  • The compression-chain rule is undemonstrated. Distilling from the original rather than from the intermediate recovered checkpoint is argued from how the intermediate was constructed; the head-to-head at matched configuration is the authors' own first stated limitation.
  • The stage split is measured at one scale. The pass@K crossover is students at 1.5–1.7B on competition mathematics at one decoding temperature, with no code released. Whether forgetting still exceeds learning at 7B or 14B — the sizes a local reader with a 24 GB card actually distils into — is untested and costs one training run per size. So is whether it holds on a non-mathematical, checker-verified task.
  • Nobody has run a matched-cost split of the two stages. The off-policy arm in the crossover study is a control, and its tuning and compute are not laid out to the standard of the headline. The run that would settle the ordering advice above: one student, one teacher, equal GPU-hours spent off-policy only, on-policy only, and split, all scored at pass@1 and pass@1024.
  • No neutral referee yet. R1 is T1 and Qwen3 is T2, and both evaluate their own students. The direction replicates across two labs; a third-party reproduction still does not exist in-vault. Note that Qwen3's Pass@64 figure — the one that made it a replication of capability transfer rather than of efficiency — sits exactly where the 2026-08-13 crossover appears, and because the report never separates its off-policy and on-policy stages, it was never evidence about on-policy distillation specifically.
  • Every result is a verifiable domain. Math and reasoning, where a checker filtered the teacher traces — including the 2026-04 dynamics study, which is AIME/AMC/DeepMath throughout and so sits entirely inside the easy half of this question. Whether the pattern holds for style, tool use or agentic behaviour is unmeasured, and the novelty framing predicts it should weaken exactly where the teacher's advantage is stylistic. The classic non-reasoning student-beats-generalist play (classification, extraction) predates all of this and has no in-vault source.
  • "Distributional novelty" is a description, not yet a quantity. The authors flag their own cross-family comparisons as confounded — data divergence, tokenizer mismatch and architecture vary together — so nobody can currently measure how much a candidate teacher has to offer before training.
  • The benchmarks are exactly the vulnerable kind. AIME/MATH deltas on fine-tuned models are where evaluation-contamination bites hardest, and as of 2026-07-30 that map holds that the standard decontamination method scores F1 0.000 against paraphrase, and that a single test-set replica can put a model below the loss infinite clean compute would reach. The temperature probe above is a detector, not a discount: nobody has converted a contamination rate into a correction on a published score.
  • A benchmark regression may mean the fine-tune is working. Above ~10 test-set replicas, supervised fine-tuning on the training split raises test loss — the model forgets what it memorized faster than it learns to generalize. A distillation run on a contaminated base can therefore show the opposite of what it means. [T1]
  • What the student loses. General-capability regression from narrow distillation — the catastrophic-forgetting question shared with peft-methods — is still unmeasured here, and there are now two mechanisms for it: narrow-distribution forgetting, and the fact that teacher traces are chosen-only data with no rejected side, so an SFT run on them has zero downward pressure on anything the traces do not contain.
  • Teacher quality is a ceiling on the skill, and no longer the useful variable for choosing one. The leverage is still the limit; it is just not the thing to optimize when picking.
  • Rollout-truncation and multi-teacher routing have never been tested together, or against rollout- hygiene filtering. STOPD spends less per rollout at one teacher; token-level teacher selection spends more per rollout across several; Training-Free OPD's hygiene filter removes degenerate rollouts before they're counted. All three touch the same generation budget and nobody has run any pair of them together.
  • PA1-OPD's continued-pretraining result is one checkpoint range, one model pair, no downstream task. The held-out cross-entropy gain is real but tiny (2.531810 → 2.531635 nats) and the authors' own compute-efficiency accounting is explicitly not load-bearing; whether it holds at other scales, other tokenizers, or shows up on any task a practitioner would actually run is unmeasured.
  • SeTPO's selective teacher-guidance mechanism and DIAL-OPD's token-selection mechanism have never been combined. One decides which trajectories get teacher help; the other decides which tokens within a rollout to train on. Both beat full-coverage baselines independently; whether they compose or interfere is untested.
  • MultiAgent-OPD's agent-hierarchy result is one base model, one domain. English open-domain multi-hop QA with a fixed retriever, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 throughout every compared system. Whether the Teacher-scores-Searcher-rollouts mechanism transfers to a different agent role, a non-QA tool-use domain (code, web-browsing agents), or a different base model family is untested.
  • The Fisher-merge/multi-teacher-OPD equivalence is argued near a shared basin, tested on two model families with two-to-three specialists each. No independent group has reproduced the central equivalence claim, and whether the Phase-1-merge-beats-OPD result holds against a more heavily-trained OPD baseline (beyond the one 144-GPU-hour student used) is unmeasured.
  • AOPD's advantage-weighting is not a uniform win, by the authors' own disclosure. It trails its own base model on one bio benchmark at one model size and loses narrowly to OPSD on MMLU-Pro; whether advantage-weighting composes with this article's other token-selection or KL-direction levers is untested.
  • MTA-OPSD's per-token farthest-KL selection and 2026-10-18's token-level teacher-selection routing have never been tested against each other. One routes between same-weights-but-differently-exposed teacher configurations; the other routes between separate standalone experts — different kinds of teacher pools, both per-token, neither tested against the other.
  • The MoE-specialist-consolidation finding (calibrated local-top-K OPD) is one in-house model with no code or weights released and no independent replication. Whether the 90-114% gain-retention figure holds beyond math-heavy Qwen3-scale domains is unmeasured.
  • Entropy Shape Correlation is untested against MTA-OPSD's per-token switching. ESC ranks a whole candidate teacher before training starts; MTA-OPSD switches between teachers mid-rollout during training. Whether a high-ESC-ranked teacher pool still benefits from per-token switching on top, or whether ESC's single-teacher ranking is even well-defined once a per-token selection rule is in play, is unmeasured. The teacher-selection result itself rests on only 10 total pairings — the authors' own word is preliminary.
