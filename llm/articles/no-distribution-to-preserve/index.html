No distribution to preserve: the state of diffusion decoding

Synthesized 2026-10-30 from diffusion-decoding (status: emerging), on the night it crossed ten dated through-line bullets — the write-due threshold — and was the entire [article-due] queue. The map is emerging, not contested, so this piece is organized around its own founding distinction rather than around a dispute: whether the diffusion model emits the final text itself (the sampled regime, which has no target distribution to preserve and pays an accuracy cost) or merely proposes under an ordinary autoregressive verifier (the drafted regime, which inherits that verifier's exactness guarantee and pays nothing). Everything below sits on one side of that line or the other, and the two sides have almost nothing in common except the word "diffusion."

The claim

A discrete diffusion language model does not decode one token at a time. It iteratively denoises a block of masked positions in parallel and commits several of them together — a different generation algorithm, not a different deployment shape and not a different architecture. That is why this sits in its own map rather than inside speculative-decoding (organized around a draft-and-verify bargain with an exactness guarantee this literature does not universally have), local-inference (organized by deployment shape, which would file a sampler choice as a runtime detail), or kv-cache-management (which owns the cache but not what writes into it).

The single fact that organizes every finding below: committing several positions per forward pass is a bet that they do not depend on each other. When the diffusion model is the decoder — the sampled regime — nothing checks that bet, so it is spent as an accuracy cost, concentrated exactly where the bet is worst (long dependency chains, multi-step reasoning). When the diffusion model is a drafter behind an ordinary autoregressive verifier, the bet is checked every time, the target's exact distribution survives regardless of what the diffusion model guessed, and the same bet converts into a throughput cost instead — an accepted-length number, not an accuracy number. Two configurations, one mechanism, and the entire map is about which one a given source occupies and what that choice buys or costs.

What is settled, in the sampled regime

1. A decoding regime is a cheap post-hoc conversion of an existing checkpoint, not a pretraining commitment. DiffusionGemma (T2, Google DeepMind, open weights) is not a from-scratch diffusion model — it is the shipped autoregressive Gemma 4 26B-A4B mixture-of-experts checkpoint, converted by supervised fine-tuning for bidirectional denoising plus reinforcement learning combined with sampler distillation, for under 10% of the original training token budget, and it retains autoregressive decoding afterward with only minor degradation. That makes three structural properties this vault has now watched turn out to be fine-tunes rather than architecture: sparsity (mixture-of-experts's sparse-upcycling finding), window length (context-extension's YaRN finding), and now the sampler itself.

2. Committing many positions in parallel pays for itself at roughly 1,500 tok/s on one H100, and the accuracy bill lands unevenly, hardest on reasoning. At a 256-token canvas DiffusionGemma commits 15-20 tokens per forward pass — an order of magnitude past what acceptance-rate work on speculative decoding reports — but against the parent it was converted from: MMLU Pro 77.6 vs. 82.6 (-5.0), AIME 2026 69.1 vs. 88.3 (-19.2), BigBench Extra Hard 47.6 vs. 64.8 (-17.2), MMMU Pro 54.3 vs. 73.8 (-19.5). Committing 256 positions in parallel is structurally a bet that they do not depend on each other, and multi-step reasoning is where that bet is worst — the same damage profile aggressive quantization produces by an unrelated mechanism, pointing at sequential dependency as the shared cause. [T2]

3. The advertised speedup and the speedup at reproducible output are different numbers, by an order of magnitude. A content-drift measurement study (T1) on an 8B diffusion multimodal model runs 10-12x faster (72.9s to 6.1s) at its default setting and produces zero of fifty outputs identical to what the same model says unaccelerated (lexical agreement 0.420). Buying agreement back is monotone and expensive: 0.463 at 8.12s, 0.572 at 17.53s, 0.666 at 30.11s, and 0.987 (40 of 50 exact) at 55.29s — 1.3x the unaccelerated 71.90s. Half of fifty audited low-agreement pairs are genuine content substitution, not paraphrase. Any diffusion-decoding speedup quoted without the agreement it was measured at is underspecified — this map's reporting standard since the night it was coined.

4. The knob these systems expose is not the knob that controls their drift. Sweeping the same source's documented confidence threshold across its full range changes committed tokens per step from 1.055 to 1.254 and agreement by only +0.0056 (Wilcoxon p = 0.75) — a tight null. What actually controls drift is the KV-cache refresh interval, an internal constant: under the default schedule, swapping the input image returns a byte-identical description back ten times out of ten. Twelve cleverer schedules across five families — adaptive budgets, confidence decay, attention decay, learned gating — all fail to beat a fixed interval at matched compute. [T1]

5. The sampled regime has its first accelerator that doesn't touch quality by more than a point, and it targets the block boundary rather than the sampler. Early-Bird Decoding (T1, three dLLMs, four benchmarks) groups tokens into variable-length blocks by uncertainty — low-entropy tokens cluster and can be jointly decoded before any individually crosses the confidence threshold — rather than using a fixed chunk size, both components frozen-weight plug-ins on an already-trained checkpoint. 3.53x-18.76x over vanilla block-wise decoding, up to 1.58x over the strongest prior baseline, at roughly ±1-point accuracy (GSM8K-CoT on LLaDA-8B-Instruct: 79.07% to 78.24%, throughput 17.04 to 104.55 tok/s) — a smaller, more controlled quality cost than this map's other sampled-regime entries, closer to a serving optimization than to DiffusionGemma's 5-19-point trade for an order of magnitude in speed. The router does not transfer across model series (vocabulary differences), so this is a per-model trained artifact, not a universal drop-in, and every number is A100 datacenter hardware.

6. The cost of building a diffusion LM at all is architecture-dependent, not fixed — and a hybrid attention/recurrent backbone converts faster than a full-attention one. dQwen3.5 (T1, code + weights released) bidirectionalizes only the attention sub-layers (6 of 24 in the 2B model) of a hybrid Qwen3.5 backbone that interleaves attention with structurally-causal Gated DeltaNet layers, leaving the recurrent layers causal. Against a matched full-attention control, the hybrid reaches the same training loss in a median 2.21x fewer content tokens, and a 9B hybrid adapted on just 50B tokens matches or beats full-attention DLMs (Dream-7B, Dream-Coder-7B, LLaDA-8B) trained on 6-46x more adaptation tokens on several benchmarks — though it trails its own control on MMLU/GSM8K and leads on HumanEval/MBPP, so the efficiency gain is not a uniform benchmark win. This is the map's first training-side entry; every other sampled- regime finding here measures what parallel emission costs at inference.

7. A general mechanism exists for reward-fine-tuning a few-step diffusion sampler without its intractable sequence-level KL term — not yet shown on a named diffusion language model. IDRF (T1) proves an inverse-distillation loss upper-bounds the true KL-to-reference term a masked discrete diffusion model's intractable sequence likelihood rules out computing exactly, letting reward fine-tuning run via a clipped policy-gradient objective with no reference-model rollouts. Up to 32x fewer denoising steps than the reference while mitigating reward hacking, validated jointly across DNA, image, and text domains — but not on a named DLM (LLaDA, Dream, DiffusionGemma) at a stated scale, so this is the first plausible mechanism for DiffusionGemma's own unexplained "RL combined with sampler distillation" conversion recipe, argued here rather than demonstrated by either source.

8. An agent's own trajectories supply a learnable signal for two decoding-schedule knobs at once, and the trade it buys is backbone-dependent, not fixed by the method. A metacognitive decoding controller (T1, code released) runs a flux policy (how far to extend a response block) and a confidence-alignment policy (which tokens to finalize early, including group acceptance of recurring structural spans) jointly on a frozen diffusion backbone. On LLaDA-8B (SFT) across five agentic tool-use/multi-hop-QA benchmarks: 15.2-43.1% fewer FLOPs at accuracy within about a point of the uncontrolled baseline on four of five (net +4.44 on BFCL Live). On a second backbone (Dream-7B) the same two policies cut FLOPs 17.2-67.5%, but the pattern inverts — the largest cuts now pair with accuracy losses up to -2.20 points, not gains. Ablating either policy alone costs FLOPs without a consistent accuracy cost either way, so the two policies' savings and their accuracy headroom are not obviously coupled. FLOPs rather than wall-clock, agentic benchmarks only.

The drafted regime: the same bet, checked instead of trusted

Two days after this map was coined, its own founding frame got corrected rather than contradicted. The exactness trade the map opened with — parallel emission on a curve with an exchange rate, speculation at the zero-quality-cost end, diffusion decoding at the many-tokens-per-pass end — carried an unstated assumption: that the diffusion model produces the output. Put it in the drafter slot behind a verifying target instead, and the block-parallel generation survives while the output distribution becomes the target's, exactly. LibraSpec (T1) reaches 2.1x-3.5x typical over autoregressive decoding this way (up to 8.49x in the best cell, across six targets), losslessly — because the same mechanism that makes the sampled regime's bill so uneven (committing many positions in parallel bets they are independent, worst on long dependency chains) is exactly what a verifier rejects. The failure mode and the rejection criterion select for the same thing, so the accuracy cost converts cleanly into a throughput cost instead of surviving as damage.

So there are three configurations where the map once recorded two, and the honest restatement is that a decoder with no target distribution has none to preserve — but nothing obliges this literature to run the diffusion model as the decoder. Every sampled-regime number above stands; only the axis framing changed. Named failure modes specific to a block/diffusion drafter — a repetition trap from independent marginal per-position predictions, joint incoherence across a block, hidden states discarded after a partial rejection — are real and documented, but they belong to speculative-decoding's economics wholesale (backbone- dependent verification cost, the offloaded-MoE case included), not to this map's own open questions. This map records the drafted configuration as a boundary, not an argument: once verified, agreement with the target is 1.0 by construction, which makes the drafted configuration's speedup number the only one on this map that is self-interpreting.

What is still open

Four questions this map has carried since coining, dated where evidence has since narrowed one of them.

What does a diffusion sampler cost on consumer hardware? Nothing in this map is measured below an H100. DiffusionGemma ships GGUF builds at roughly 13 GB of 4-bit weights — over a 12 GB card, under a 24 GB one — so the offloaded case is reachable and untested, and this vault's own expert-union finding from the mixture-of-experts map predicts a canvas-wide expert union is exactly where the throughput claim would break first. A tokens-per-second and peak-memory measurement on one consumer card would be the single most useful number this map could acquire.

Diffusion versus speculation at matched quality. Unusually cheap to run, because DiffusionGemma's released weights still decode autoregressively — score the same task suite under a tuned speculative pipeline and under the diffusion sampler on the same base weights, reporting tokens per forward pass at equal accuracy rather than at equal effort. Nobody has run it.

Does the accuracy bill fall with more conversion compute, or is it structural? Under 10% of DiffusionGemma's original token budget bought a 5-point general-knowledge loss and a ~19-point reasoning loss. No source has reported a scaling curve for the conversion, so whether the reasoning gap is a training budget the lab chose not to spend, or a structural property of committing dependent tokens in parallel, is unknown.

Is there a consistency-preserving accelerator at all? Twelve schedule variants across five families lost to a fixed refresh interval at matched compute, and a learned gate reached cross-validated R² ≈ 0.04. Re- running the same input at the same average refresh rate with different timing moves agreement by 0.11 on average and up to 0.53, so the schedule's realization, not its rate, is doing the work, and nobody has explained why. Narrowed, not answered, 2026-10-05: IDRF's inverse-distillation mechanism is the first positive in-vault instance of buying speed without losing what the unaccelerated model would say, via a training-time rather than inference-time mechanism — but it is validated on DNA/image/text jointly, not a named diffusion language model, so the discriminating run (apply IDRF's objective to a named DLM and measure agreement the way this map's other sampled-regime sources do) is still unrun.

A fifth, newer question joins the list rather than answering any of the above:

What is the memory profile of a canvas under decoding, and does it behave like a plain KV cache at all? The encoder-decoder split builds the prompt cache autoregressively and appends each finished canvas, plus bidirectional attention over a canvas rewritten every step — no source reports a memory figure. Narrowed 2026-10-30: a KV-cache quantizer built for this substrate (diffusion-lm-kv-cache-quantization, T1) shows the cache is a moving target during generation — bidirectional states shift as masked positions resolve, so a reused cache already diverges from a full recomputation before any compression touches it, an assumption autoregressive KV-cache quantizers (KIVI, KVQuant) do not have to make. Its dLLM-specific fix beats both AR-adapted baselines on one model at every tested bit-width and cache mode, and on a second model's prefix-cache mode — but loses on that second model's dual-cache mode, so even a fix purpose-built for this substrate is not a clean win. Still no throughput or memory number anywhere on this map.

The one-line version

Diffusion decoding is one mechanism wearing two prices. As a decoder, it has no target distribution to preserve, so committing many positions per forward pass is an accuracy bet that lands hardest on long dependency chains, and every speedup number needs the agreement it was measured at attached to it — the knob that is exposed (a confidence threshold) is reliably not the knob that controls drift (a cache-refresh interval). As a drafter behind an ordinary verifier, the identical bet is checked instead of trusted, costs nothing, and the map's standing accuracy damage converts cleanly into a throughput number instead. The sampled regime is getting cheaper to improve without paying more quality — a block-grouping accelerator that costs under a point, a hybrid backbone that halves the tokens needed to build the model at all, and a trained agentic scheduling controller whose cost/accuracy trade flips sign across backbones, a reminder that none of this transfers for free. Nothing here has been measured on hardware this vault's reader could run today, no head-to-head against speculative decoding exists despite one being unusually cheap to run, and the newest arrival says the cache itself — not just its size — behaves differently under this substrate than it does under autoregressive decoding, with the actual memory and throughput numbers still unmeasured.
