Distillation & small models (students beating bigger generalists) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 (auto run) — first evidence in the "Distillation & small models" group, and the closest in-vault result to the vault's premise: a small open-weights student, fine-tuned on the right data, beating a frontier generalist on a specific domain. What we believe now:

  • A model suite can be one nested model trained once, which deletes the teacher-residency question entirely — and it is this map's first finding addressed to the producer rather than the consumer (new 2026-08-12). Stack the sizes inside each other — the 500M is a subset of the 1.5B, a subset of the 3B — train end-to-end, and the largest distills into every smaller one at every training step. Parity with independently trained baselines on benchmarks and on both validation and out-of-domain perplexity, at 36% less training compute, with the suite's total parameter count reduced because the small models are not stored separately. There is no teacher to keep resident and no traces to generate, which retires the cost structure this map priced on 2026-08-05 rather than optimizing it. Two things to hold carefully. It requires training from scratch, which is the one move this vault's premise says its readers cannot make — so this is a claim about how labs should build the small models we download, not about what we do to them afterwards; what changes for a reader is what to expect on the shelf, since nested family members stop being independent artifacts. And it is a genuine test of distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank whose result is ambiguous: a nested teacher is the low-novelty extreme (it shares parameters with the student), and the suite still reaches parity — which is equally consistent with the novelty account failing at this end or with the nested teacher contributing no transfer at all and the whole win being shared compute, since parity is exactly what a zero-transfer control would predict. The abstract does not report a no-distillation nested arm. [T1, single study] — one suite, nothing above 3B, two authors, no replication, no stated code or weight release. a-model-suite-can-be-one-nested-model-trained-once
  • Distillation's two dominant fixed costs are the teacher's residency and the loss tensor, and both are removable without touching the models (new 2026-08-05, and the first entry on this map that is a systems result rather than a quality one). The teacher need not be resident. In the off-policy case it is a pure function of the input, so compute its logits once and cache the top-100 per token: peak memory 103 GB → 78 GB, iteration time 25.9s → 18.5s (~29%), throughput 237 → 331 TFLOP/s (~41%) on a single H200 at 8K context, at "near-identical loss." And the thing that caps context length is the loss, not the model. Computing KL divergence naively materializes a [batch × sequence × vocabulary] float tensor, which at 32K peaks at ~250 GB — more than the card — while a fused chunked formulation that never builds it peaks at ~128 GB, making memory linear in sequence length and buying 4× the training context (32,768 tokens) on one GPU. The isolated kernel benchmark is starker: dense KL fails at 64K, forward-chunking peaks at 134.2 GiB, fused at 11.6 GiB. Two consequences this map should carry. The fused loss is a memory axis the distributed-training literature never named — it attacks neither parameters, gradients, optimizer states nor activations, and it is exact, an algebraic rewrite of the same gradient, so it belongs at the front of the spend-the-exact-levers-first ordering alongside recomputation-is-the-one-memory-technique-that-is-exact and arguably ahead of it, since it costs no extra forward pass. And the caching win is confined to the off-policy stage — a cache cannot answer for states the student has not visited — so it accelerates the first half of this map's two-stage recipe and cannot touch the second. Held at suggestive on the equivalence claim specifically: "near-identical loss" is a training-health signal with no downstream benchmark behind it, which is the proxy this vault distrusts most. One teacher–student pair, 141 GB datacenter hardware, and the cache's storage cost is never quantified — a conspicuous omission in a caching paper, and the number that decides whether any of this is affordable on a consumer SSD. [T1, one study] See caching-the-teachers-top-k-logits-removes-the-teacher-from-memory, the-kl-loss-not-the-model-caps-distillation-context-length.
  • What distillation transfers is distributional novelty, not teacher quality — and the recipe's two-stage ordering is load-bearing (new 2026-07-31, and it is this map's first mechanism-level account of its own play). A student already improved by RL, distilled toward a higher-scoring same-family 7B, lands at nearly the same place as one distilled toward its own weaker pre-RL checkpoint: the two teachers are "distributionally indistinguishable from the student's perspective." Post-trained teachers transfer far more than same-pipeline base ones, and a teacher whose thinking pattern mismatches the student's loses benefit that "cannot be recovered later." So the natural rule — pick the strongest teacher you can serve — is unsupported, and it fails silently. The mechanism is narrow and cheap to instrument: the reward is carried by a shared token set holding 97–99% of both models' probability mass, with overlap rising 72% → 91% in successful runs and flat from initialization in failing ones, which makes the overlap ratio a free early-kill diagnostic. And the repair — off-policy SFT cold start before on-policy distillation — is the ordering Qwen3 already ships, derived here independently from mechanism. Bounded: mathematics only, 1.5B–7B, and the novelty condition is confounded by construction. [T1, on-policy distillation specifically]
  • The on-policy signal has a horizon, and it arrives where reasoning traces are heading (new 2026-07-31). Teacher continuation advantage falls from +0.37 at a 1K-token prefix to +0.02 at 16K; the usable response-length window is 3K–7K, with performance plateauing or declining by 10K–15K and instability propagating back-to-front from the end of the response. The authors say the technique "may not extend cleanly to longer-horizon settings such as extended chain-of-thought or agentic multi-turn interaction." Their proposed explanation is explicitly unverified, so the decay is a measurement and its mechanism a conjecture. Note the direction for this vault's premise: 3K–7K is also roughly where a local fine-tune's activation memory is still tractable, so a practitioner distilling on one or two cards is unlikely to reach the ceiling — and equally unable to observe it. [T1, suggestive]
  • Reasoning distills: teacher traces → student SFT transfers capability that direct RL at small scale cannot reach. Same Qwen-32B base: R1-trace SFT hits 72.6% AIME 2024 vs 47.0% for the RL recipe run directly on it. Exploration is expensive and scale-dependent; imitation is cheap at any scale. [T1]
  • Independent replication, now at production scale: strong-to-weak distillation is how a shipping open-weights family builds its small models. Qwen3 distills fully-trained flagship teachers (235B-A22B, 32B) into its 0.6B–14B dense students — off-policy (teacher outputs across both thinking modes) + on-policy (logit matching) — reporting higher Pass@1 and Pass@64 than running the full four-stage recipe per size, at ~1/10 the GPU hours. Same direction as R1, a different lab and base, outside competition-math — this is what moves the map to consolidating. [T2, second group]
  • A distilled 7B beats frontier generalists on the verifiable domain it was distilled for — Distill-Qwen-7B: 55.5% AIME (GPT-4o-0513: 9.3%), 92.8% MATH-500 — and outruns a 32B reasoner (QwQ-32B-Preview: 50.0%). Specificity, not size, is doing the work. [T1]
  • Cost structure of the play: the leverage is access to teacher traces (~800k of them here), not reproducing the teacher's RL run — which is why open releases of reasoning models move this map more than closed capability jumps. [T1]

  • 2026-08-09 — edge from restorekv-learned-restoration-under-eviction (T1, primary map kv-cache-management): self-distillation used to repair a model's own damaged memory rather than to transfer a capability. Every distillation setup in this map has a teacher and a student that differ in parameters — a bigger model, a better model, or the same model on-policy. Here the teacher and student are the same frozen weights and differ only in what they are allowed to remember: the teacher runs on the full KV cache, the student on a compressed one, and a symmetric-KL objective over answer tokens trains eight restore-token embeddings plus a rank-8 adapter (0.4% of parameters, ~2 hours on one card) to close the gap. And the transfer limit is the same kind this map keeps finding: the trained module does not survive a change of compressor (trained on SnapKV, run on KVzip: 42.0 against 73.2), so what was distilled is a specific corruption, not a general repair.   • 2026-08-06 — edge from scaleq-158-reasoning-aware-ternary-calibration (T1, primary map post-training-quantization): this map's newest and least comfortable finding turns up in a literature that has never heard of it. distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank says transfer follows distributional novelty rather than teacher quality, so a better-scoring teacher can transfer nothing and the failure is silent — a claim this map has carried on one source since 2026-07-31. Quantization calibration is a different mechanism entirely: no gradients, no student, a solver fitting a compressed model to reproduce a full-precision one's activations on a small text sample. It reaches the same ordering. Calibrating a 4B model with chain-of-thought traces generated by DeepSeek-R1-671B yields 20.06% mean accuracy across five reasoning and coding benchmarks; calibrating it with traces the 4B model wrote itself yields 45.60% — a 25.54-point gain for using the far weaker model's own distribution, with the authors' explanation being that a ternary reconstruction of a 4B model cannot mimic a 671B model's reasoning regardless of how good that reasoning is. Two literatures, no shared citations, one conclusion: any procedure that fits one model against another's outputs wants the target's own distribution, not the best distribution available. The list of procedures with that shape is longer than either paper's scope — distillation, quantization calibration, draft-model training for speculative decoding, adapter initialization on synthetic data — and exactly two of them have been checked, which is the cheapest research direction this map currently has. Nothing on this map's status moves: this is an unrelated-mechanism corroboration of an existing bullet, not a second distillation study.   • 2026-07-31 — ingest of on-policy-distillation-dynamics (T1): the on-policy half of this map's production recipe finally has a controlled study behind it, and it is the first source here that measures when the technique fails rather than reporting that it worked. The map holds consolidating and the vault should say why it considered a downgrade. Three findings. (1) The teacher's benchmark score does not predict the student's gain. Reverse distillation: take JustRL-1.5B — a model RL has already improved — and distill it toward its own pre-RL checkpoint, and it regresses to roughly pre-RL performance; distill it toward a higher-scoring same-family 7B and it regresses to nearly the identical level, because "same-family 1.5B and 7B teachers are distributionally indistinguishable from the student's perspective." Paired with the condition that post-trained teachers transfer far more than same-pipeline base teachers, the unifying statement is that distillation's currency is distributional novelty, not teacher rank — which retro-explains R1's traces (a capability the family had never expressed), the 2026-07-25 LIMA scope edge (an only-aligned teacher has thin advantage to transfer), and this map's own suspicion about non-reasoning targets. (2) The dense reward is carried by a token set the two models already share — overlap climbing 72% → 91%, holding 97–99% of both models' probability mass, with optimization restricted to the overlap matching full on-policy distillation and the symmetric difference much weaker. Failing runs show overlap stagnant from initialization, and early mismatch "cannot be recovered later" — so the overlap ratio is a free kill-the-run diagnostic and the non-overlap tail is mostly wasted compute. (3) The dense reward decays with depth: teacher continuation advantage +0.37 at a 1K prefix → +0.02 at 16K, sweet spot 3K–7K, plateau or decline by 10K–15K, instability propagating back-to-front. Why not contested. The map's headlines rest on off-policy teacher-trace SFT (distilled-reasoners-beat-direct-rl), untouched here — and the paper's recommended repair, off-policy cold start before on-policy distillation (worth 15–20 points of initial overlap and a higher final ceiling), is the exact ordering strong-to-weak-distillation-is-the-small-model-build-path records Qwen3 as shipping. That is a third-party derivation of the production recipe's sequencing from mechanism, which is the opposite of a downgrade; what changes is that the ordering turns out to be load-bearing rather than conventional. What is a real narrowing is the practitioner rule this map implied and never stated: "pick the strongest teacher you can serve" is unsupported, and the failure is silent. Bounded: mathematics only by the authors' own scoping — so it sits entirely inside the easy half of this map's biggest open question and cannot help resolve it — 1.5B–7B, the novelty condition is confounded by construction (cross-family comparisons mix data, tokenizer and architecture), and the offered explanation for the depth decay is explicitly unverified by its authors. Every benchmark is competition math, so evaluation-contamination's standing discount applies unchanged.   • 2026-07-22 — reframing edge from speculative-decoding-fast-inference (T1, primary map local-inference): a small model has a second use, and in that use its quality gap is priced automatically. This map's whole play is "train a small model to replace a big one," which makes the student's residual gap a permanent quality cost. Speculative decoding uses the student as a draft model for the teacher instead: it proposes tokens, the teacher verifies them in one parallel pass, and the output distribution is provably the teacher's — so disagreement costs latency, not correctness. A student that agrees with its teacher 75% of the time is a 3× accelerator for the teacher. Two consequences worth carrying: the small-model artifact can be a component of the large model's serving stack rather than a substitute for it; and it implies a distillation objective this map has never optimized for — maximize α (expected per-token agreement with the teacher) rather than downstream benchmark score. Note the counter-intuitive corollary from that source's own table: a better student is not automatically a better draft, since its inference cost eats the winnings (T5-large, α=0.82, is the slowest configuration at 1.7×, while T5-small at α=0.75 gives 3.4×). Unmeasured in-vault: whether reasoning-distilled students (distilled-reasoners-beat-direct-rl) make good drafts, given that their long chains of thought diverge from a teacher's token-by-token. Note the asymmetry worth keeping — R1's headline is AIME pass@1, an automatically-checkable answer with no judge involved, while the vendor suite deltas (Qwen3) are judge-adjacent. The distillation claims resting on verifiable checkers are the sturdiest evidence in this map; the ones resting on judged suites now carry a named discount. See llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias.
  • 2026-07-19 — push-back edge from molmo-pixmo-open-vlm (T1, primary map vision-language-models): the first in-vault argument that teacher distillation carries a cost to the commons, not merely a quality ceiling. Molmo's charge is that the strongest "open" VLMs are proprietary models distilled into open weights, leaving the community "missing foundational knowledge about how to build performant VLMs from scratch" — capability transferred, understanding not. It then builds a top-of-suite model with zero teacher data. This does not contest this map's efficacy claim (distillation works, and works cheaply), but it sharpens what the leverage costs: a distilled artifact inherits its teacher's ceiling, its blind spots, and its license, and teaches you nothing about how to get there another way. Relevant to this vault's premise, where the goal is to understand models well enough to modify them. See open-vlms-are-mostly-distilled-closed-vlms.
  • 2026-07-19 — advisory edge from orpo-monolithic-preference-optimization (T1, primary map post-training-rl): an unasked question about this map's method. Cross-entropy SFT has no term that pushes anything down, and raises the likelihood of rejected responses alongside chosen ones (cross-entropy-sft-raises-the-odds-of-rejected-text). Distilled teacher traces are chosen-only data with no rejected side at all — so a distillation SFT run has, by construction, zero downward pressure on anything the traces do not contain. What that does to a student's refusal behaviour, format discipline, and general capability is unmeasured, and it is a second mechanism (beside narrow-distribution forgetting) for the regression this map already flags below.
  • 2026-07-18 — interaction edge from llava-visual-instruction-tuning (T1, primary map vision-language-models): the teacher-trace maneuver run sideways instead of down. Distillation in this map transfers capability from a big model to a small one in the same modality; LLaVA transfers a text-only teacher's reasoning into a student operating in a modality the teacher never had, by feeding the teacher symbolic proxies (captions, bounding boxes) for what it cannot see. Same leverage point this map names — access to teacher output, not the teacher's training run — generalized past model-size compression to capability bootstrapping where no corpus exists. See synthetic-instructions-from-a-blind-teacher-bootstrap-a-corpus.

The thinking-budget / mode-fusion mechanism is an adjacent test-time-compute lever (post-training-rl); its leaderboard deltas pass through the contamination lens (evaluation-contamination). See strong-to-weak-distillation-is-the-small-model-build-path.

Open questions:

  • Is there a measurable teacher-selection criterion, now that benchmark rank is ruled out? (new 2026-07-31.) distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank says the gain tracks distributional novelty and that a stronger same-family teacher buys nothing, but leaves novelty as a description rather than a quantity — the authors flag their cross-family comparisons as confounded by data, tokenizer and architecture all at once. The obvious candidate is sitting in the same paper: initial token-overlap ratio, which separates aligned from misaligned pairs at initialization and is computable before any training. Nobody has tested whether it ranks candidate teachers, which would turn a post-hoc explanation into a cheap pre-flight check — score three candidate teachers' overlap with the student on a few hundred prompts and pick by it. That is hours of inference, and it is the most decision-relevant unrun thing on this map.
  • Does the horizon ceiling bind before or after the memory ceiling? (new 2026-07-31.) On-policy distillation's reward decays to near-nothing by 16K tokens (dense-token-rewards-decay-with-trajectory-depth) and a 4-bit local fine-tune's activations get expensive in roughly the same range (distributed-training). Which binds first on one or two consumer cards is unmeasured and decides whether this is a limitation a local practitioner will ever meet. It also means the local case cannot observe the failure, so anyone extrapolating a small local result to a long-horizon run is extrapolating past where the evidence stops.
  • Both in-vault sources are still vendor/authors' own evals (R1 T1, Qwen3 T2); the direction now replicates across two labs, but a neutral third-party reproduction and contamination audits of the distill sets are the map's next sources.
  • Does the pattern hold outside verifiable domains — style, tool use, agentic behavior — where no checker filtered the teacher traces?
  • Task-specific distillation for non-reasoning targets (the classic student-beats-bigger-generalist play on classification/extraction/domain tasks) predates R1 and has no in-vault source yet.
  • What the student loses: general-capability regression from narrow distillation (the catastrophic-forgetting question, shared with peft-methods) is unmeasured here. Sharpened 2026-07-18: that map now carries quantified forgetting evidence (lora-regularizes-more-than-it-adapts) and a mechanism (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting) — neither applied to distillation students, which are trained on a narrow trace distribution and should be the most exposed case.
  • How is the distillation SFT actually run? peft-methods went contested 2026-07-18: low-rank adaptation underperforms full fine-tuning when the target domain is far from pretraining. Reasoning traces plausibly are such a domain — so whether these students were distilled with full fine-tuning or PEFT, and whether the rank budget binds here, is a question this map cannot currently answer about its own headline results.
