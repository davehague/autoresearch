Distillation & small models (students beating bigger generalists) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 (auto run) — first evidence in the "Distillation & small models" group, and the closest in-vault result to the vault's premise: a small open-weights student, fine-tuned on the right data, beating a frontier generalist on a specific domain. Auto-coined; flagged for David's review. Moved emerging → consolidating 2026-07-17 when Qwen3 independently replicated the distillation-beats-direct-training result at production scale (second lab, different base, outside competition-math).

Status: consolidating Status note (2026-08-17): holds consolidating, and the map's on-policy claim gets its mechanism confirmed as a training pathology by a group that was not testing it. simpleopd-tokenizer-agnostic-on-policy-distillation (T1, Shanghai AI Lab; seven students across five families, two tokenizer regimes, code and weights released) sets out to transfer proof reasoning from a 30B teacher that sustains 100K+ tokens of reasoning, and spends most of its length on why direct on-policy distillation does not work. The curves are the finding: response length, truncation rate and repetition rate rise together while task score falls — Qwen3.5-35B-A3B past 40% truncation on AIME25 and 60% on AnswerBench — with the appendix showing a correct answer followed by 972 exact self-check repeats. The diagnosis is that the student, scored on its own rollouts against a very long teacher, "gradually loses the ability to terminate properly." The fix is not a better objective: mask the loss on </think> and <|im_end|> (helps, insufficient) and add a student-reference KL loss penalizing drift from the student's own initial policy, which drives truncation to near zero. That is this map's 2026-08-13 claim — on-policy training reweights what the student already reaches and cannot introduce what it does not — enforced by hand as a hyperparameter, with an interior optimum measured on a third student (0.5 under-regularizes, 1.0 best, 1.2 over-constrains). With both fixes the results are large: Intern-S2-Preview 21.70 → 44.50 ProofBench against a teacher's 45.00, and 34.0 → 55.2 under the teacher's own Gemini-judged protocol; Qwen3-4B +12.30/+17.00/+19.58; students surpassing the teacher on AnswerBench and AIME25. Status does not move, for four reasons. The teacher and the flagship student are the authors' own models. ProofBench is judge-scored, so this map's newest numbers carry evaluation-contamination's judge discount. No compute or wall-clock figure appears anywhere, so the build-path claim gains capability evidence and no cost evidence. And the paper reports @4 and @8 averages only — this map's live contested question is whether on-policy distillation trades large-K capability for small-K efficiency, and an on-policy paper with this many students was one sampling sweep away from settling it. Two edges out. To tokenization-and-vocabulary: distillation across different tokenizers, done by aligning only tokens covering identical text spans, retains 90–98% of positions and rises during training — the vault's assumption that logit-level transfer needs a shared vocabulary is mostly an artifact of aligning token identities. To data-curation: adding the teacher's verifiable math data to proof-only training costs 6 ProofBench points (44.50 → 38.50). Status note (2026-08-14): holds consolidating, and the map's founding claim replicates on an axis it was never selected for — with a build path that needs no training run. slm-trustworthiness-compressed-vs-pretrained (T1, IJCNN 2026; four model families; TrustLLM's ethics / privacy / robustness / fairness dimensions) asks this map's question — derive a small model from a big one, or train one small — and scores it on trustworthiness rather than capability. Same answer. From-scratch SLMs cluster at 45–52% overall (h2o-danube3-500m 47.76, MobiLlama-500m 45.46, SmolLM2-360M 51.92, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct 51.17); a quantized larger model sits at 63–65%, and the head-to-head is Qwen2.5-1.5B-INT4-GPTQ 63.86 against Qwen2.5-0.5B's 51.17 — +12.69 points, winning all four dimensions, at roughly comparable ms/token on an RTX-4090 once INT4 has cut weight memory ~4×. Three instruments now rank derived-above-trained: competition-math pass@1 (distilled-reasoners-beat-direct-rl), a production build at 1/10 the GPU hours (strong-to-weak-distillation-is-the-small-model-build-path), and now a four-dimension safety benchmark. What is new is how cheap the cheapest version is. Both prior results need a teacher, a trace corpus and a training run — Qwen3 spent flagship GPU hours to make its 0.6B. This one needs pip install and a calibration set. For a vault whose premise is open weights on your own hardware, that reorders the practical advice: build path first, size second, and the build path with the best evidence-to-effort ratio is "quantize the next size up." The map's own distillation arm here is directional only — a single 7B→3B run on Alpaca, reaching 56.45% from 54.85% and landing above its own teacher on overall, robustness and fairness. Status does not move: one study, one family for the head-to-head, no 2026-era from-scratch SLM in the comparison cohort, and a floor the paper never states — Qwen2.5-0.5B loses 4.34 points under INT4-AWQ where the 7B loses 0.37, so the argument runs only while the model being compressed is big enough to absorb it.

Status note (2026-08-13): holds consolidating, and the map learns that one of its two load-bearing claims was measured on a metric that cannot see the thing it claims. on-policy-distillation-illusory-pass-at-k (T1, three student–teacher pairs, four competition-math benchmarks, sampling budgets to K = 1024) finds the pass@K curves of an on-policy distilled student and its own base checkpoint cross: pass@32 30.5% against 28.2%, pass@64 33.6% against 35.0%, pass@1024 53.3% against 70.0% on AIME2024 — sixteen points below where it started — while average accuracy improves at every budget (avg@1024 12.0% against 4.2%). At the problem level, more previously solvable problems become unsolvable than the reverse. Status does not move, for two reasons that matter more than the headline. The map's replication leg (Qwen3) is a pipeline result — off-policy cold start then on-policy logit matching — and this measures the on-policy stage alone, so the two are compatible and the map has simply been reading a pipeline number as evidence about a stage. And the paper's own control says off-policy distillation does not do this, beating the base at small and large K alike, which upgrades the two-stage ordering from a tip to a requirement and converges with distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank from an unrelated instrument. Bounded: mathematics only, three configurations, one decoding temperature, students at 1.5–1.7B, no code release, no stated limitations.

Status note (2026-08-22): holds consolidating, and the transfer-mechanism strand gains a scope condition that applies retroactively to every on-policy result already on this map. gc-opd-teacher-verifier-disagreement (T1, Tsinghua, code released) points out what on-policy distillation is actually optimising: the teacher-minus-student log-probability gap is a measure of teacher preference, used as a stand-in for task success. The paper then measures how good a stand-in it is as a function of input length — on frozen response sets, before any training — and finds trajectory-level distillation scores become progressively less aligned with verifier rewards across longer input ranges, with a follow-up sweep confirming the disagreement across four more task families while reporting its prevalence as task-dependent. The mechanism argued is specific and plausible: a correct long-context answer must aggregate evidence across distant positions, while dense token-level teacher support "can favor locally plausible responses that omit evidence distributed across the input." Every on-policy finding on this strand — on-policy-distillation-buys-sampling-efficiency-not-capability, on-policy-distillation-runs-on-a-shared-high-probability-token-set, on-policy-distillation-degenerates-without-a-leash-to-the-initial-policy — was established at ordinary context lengths, and this says the signal underneath them has a length-dependent validity range. Status holds consolidating: the strand's claim is unchanged, its domain is now bounded.

Sub-threads: (1) the build-path ordering — derive the small model from a large one rather than train it small, and how that derivation chains when there is more than one step · (2) the transfer mechanism — what actually moves from teacher to student (on-policy vs off-policy dynamics, distributional novelty over teacher rank, pass@K collapse), which has its own contested question, argued at fixed sampling budgets against a base checkpoint rather than against a bigger generalist, so it no longer reduces to a condition on strand 1 [reviewed 2026-08-24 @ 27 bullets]

What we believe now:

  • Under a measured null, the build-path advantage localizes: external distillation reaches problems the base model rarely solves and three separate self-training recipes do not — but on problems it never solves, the evidence is inconclusive (new 2026-08-23, and it is the first control-grade separation of "acquired" from "sharpened" on this map). Four arms matched in stream, volume and evaluation on Qwen3-8B: STaR +5.6 to +6.9 accuracy points, majority-vote SFT −2.8 to +4.0 (seed-unstable, CLR 0.55–1.53), a TTRL-style policy gradient that collapses into unbounded repetition on AIME across all three seeds, and external distillation from gpt-oss-120b +13 to +16. On the 22 problems with base rate ≤ 5/1,408 draws, distillation registers 8–11 expansions per seed under a per-problem exact test with FDR control; the three self-training arms register 0–2 (p < 10⁻⁶), and a regression rejects the asymmetry as a by-product of distillation's larger overall gain (p < 10⁻⁸). The bound the authors draw on themselves is the part this map must carry: on the 10 problems the base never reaches at all, p = 0.35 — not significant. So the licensed claim is about the rare tail, not the absent one, which is a narrower statement than "distillation teaches new capabilities" and the one the data supports. What strand 2 gains is an instrument as much as a result: the right way to argue about what transfers is a per-problem test against a pooled baseline denominator, not a difference of means, because the effects at issue live in a tail that means average away. The negative half is the stronger contribution — three separate self-improvement recipes, including an RL-flavoured one, all fail to expand the rare tail. [T1, Qwen3-8B, one benchmark] distillation-expands-the-rare-tail-and-self-training-sharpens-what-is-there
  • Multi-teacher consolidation fails on token accounting, not on teacher conflict — and a free weight merge is the baseline it has to beat. Under oracle routing that removes router error by construction, naive multi-teacher on-policy distillation recovers 35.6% of the available headroom where per-domain distillation reaches 88.0%; teacher disagreement is tested and rejected as the cause, and the measured culprit is that 99% of gradient tokens go to the two domains with longer responses, leaving the third at ~1% and 6.16 points short. Balancing the token share fixes almost exactly the starved domain (+1.17, "almost entirely from IF"), and three mechanisms reach 83.4%. But training-free task-arithmetic merging recovers 71.7% with no training at all, and the repaired recipe's margin over it is 0.78 total points — so the practical ordering is merge first, and treat any multi-teacher distillation result reported without a merge baseline as unevaluated. The general form of the diagnosis outlives the setting: any multi-source signal weighted by token count is implicitly weighted by verbosity. [T1, strong on the diagnosis; suggestive on the merge comparison — one 3B student, one three-domain split]
  • On-policy distillation's usable range is bounded by how far the student can be pulled from its own starting point, and the bound is a hyperparameter with a measured optimum (new 2026-08-17, and it turns strand 2's evaluation-side claim into a training-side mechanism). Point on-policy distillation at a teacher whose traces are an order of magnitude longer than the student's and the student degenerates rather than converging: length, truncation and repetition climb together while the score falls, and the failure transcripts are a correct answer followed by 972 exact self-check repeats and a trace collapsing into a one-token loop. Masking the loss on termination tokens is insufficient. What works is a KL penalty against the student's initial policy — a leash, not a better target — which drives truncation to near zero, and whose coefficient has an interior optimum (0.5 under-regularizes, 1.0 best, 1.2 over-constrains). Read with on-policy-distillation-buys-sampling-efficiency-not-capability, the picture is consistent: the on-policy stage moves the student around its own distribution, and both its benefit and its failure mode are functions of distance travelled. The same wall shows up from the other side the same week — a-small-student-cannot-absorb-supervision-outside-its-representable-subspace gets repetition collapse by handing a small student targets it cannot nearly produce — so the binding constraint on distillation is distance from the student, not teacher quality. The cheap consequence is an instrument nobody logs: repetition rate and truncation rate during training are the earliest visible symptom, free to record, and absent from every distillation source in this vault. [T1, vendor-self-evaluated] on-policy-distillation-degenerates-without-a-leash-to-the-initial-policy
  • The build path is teacher-free at the gradient, not teacher-free in the information (new 2026-08-15, and it narrows strand 1 without breaking it). A 1B model trained from scratch with no distillation loss and no teacher logits beats the open 1B field by 18–32 points on its target languages — and 11.08% of its corpus is data generated by a 31B model and audited. What "derive the small model from a large one" is actually asserting is a constraint on where the information comes from, not on whether a distillation objective moves it. Dropping the teacher from the training loop buys a licensing story, no teacher to host, and freedom from the teacher's tokenizer and context limit; it does not buy independence from a larger model's capabilities. [T2, single-study] a-from-scratch-small-model-still-needs-a-teachers-output, off-policy-distillation-transfers-what-on-policy-does-not
  • The on-policy stage sharpens what the student can already reach; the off-policy stage is where new capability enters (new 2026-08-13, and it retires an ambiguity this map has carried since its replication leg landed). Push the sampling budget past where distillation papers usually stop and the distilled student's advantage inverts: pass@64 is the crossover and by pass@1024 the on-policy student reads 53.3% against its own base checkpoint's 70.0% on AIME2024, having improved every average along the way (avg@1024 12.0% against 4.2%). The problem-level accounting says what happened — mean accuracy on retained problems roughly doubles while the forgotten fraction exceeds the learned fraction across all three student–teacher pairs. Run the off-policy variant instead and the curves do not cross at all. The mechanism is one line and it agrees with distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank's account arrived at from token-overlap rather than from evaluation: on-policy training scores the student's own samples, so it can reweight what the student already reaches and cannot introduce what it does not — measured directly as trajectories that become more likely under the teacher while staying closer to the base distribution under the base scorer. Two consequences. The recipe's ordering is an ordering constraint, not a preference: a budget-constrained run should spend on the off-policy stage first. And this map's Qwen3 replication — "higher Pass@1 and Pass@64" — was a pipeline number all along, never evidence about the on-policy stage, and has been read here as if it were. For a reader on one or two cards the direction is double-edged rather than bad: local deployment samples at K = 1, so the efficiency gain is real value and the capability loss is invisible — until the workload acquires a retry loop, a best-of-N sampler or a verifier. Bounded: competition mathematics only, three configurations, one decoding temperature, students at 1.5–1.7B, and the off-policy arm is a control whose tuning and compute are not laid out to the standard of the headline, so no matched-cost split is available. [T1] on-policy-distillation-buys-sampling-efficiency-not-capability, off-policy-distillation-transfers-what-on-policy-does-not
  • A model suite can be one nested model trained once, which deletes the teacher-residency question entirely — and it is this map's first finding addressed to the producer rather than the consumer (new 2026-08-12). Stack the sizes inside each other — the 500M is a subset of the 1.5B, a subset of the 3B — train end-to-end, and the largest distills into every smaller one at every training step. Parity with independently trained baselines on benchmarks and on both validation and out-of-domain perplexity, at 36% less training compute, with the suite's total parameter count reduced because the small models are not stored separately. There is no teacher to keep resident and no traces to generate, which retires the cost structure this map priced on 2026-08-05 rather than optimizing it. Two things to hold carefully. It requires training from scratch, which is the one move this vault's premise says its readers cannot make — so this is a claim about how labs should build the small models we download, not about what we do to them afterwards; what changes for a reader is what to expect on the shelf, since nested family members stop being independent artifacts. And it is a genuine test of distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank whose result is ambiguous: a nested teacher is the low-novelty extreme (it shares parameters with the student), and the suite still reaches parity — which is equally consistent with the novelty account failing at this end or with the nested teacher contributing no transfer at all and the whole win being shared compute, since parity is exactly what a zero-transfer control would predict. The abstract does not report a no-distillation nested arm. [T1, single study] — one suite, nothing above 3B, two authors, no replication, no stated code or weight release. a-model-suite-can-be-one-nested-model-trained-once
  • Distillation's two dominant fixed costs are the teacher's residency and the loss tensor, and both are removable without touching the models (new 2026-08-05, and the first entry on this map that is a systems result rather than a quality one). The teacher need not be resident. In the off-policy case it is a pure function of the input, so compute its logits once and cache the top-100 per token: peak memory 103 GB → 78 GB, iteration time 25.9s → 18.5s (~29%), throughput 237 → 331 TFLOP/s (~41%) on a single H200 at 8K context, at "near-identical loss." And the thing that caps context length is the loss, not the model. Computing KL divergence naively materializes a [batch × sequence × vocabulary] float tensor, which at 32K peaks at ~250 GB — more than the card — while a fused chunked formulation that never builds it peaks at ~128 GB, making memory linear in sequence length and buying 4× the training context (32,768 tokens) on one GPU. The isolated kernel benchmark is starker: dense KL fails at 64K, forward-chunking peaks at 134.2 GiB, fused at 11.6 GiB. Two consequences this map should carry. The fused loss is a memory axis the distributed-training literature never named — it attacks neither parameters, gradients, optimizer states nor activations, and it is exact, an algebraic rewrite of the same gradient, so it belongs at the front of the spend-the-exact-levers-first ordering alongside recomputation-is-the-one-memory-technique-that-is-exact and arguably ahead of it, since it costs no extra forward pass. And the caching win is confined to the off-policy stage — a cache cannot answer for states the student has not visited — so it accelerates the first half of this map's two-stage recipe and cannot touch the second. Held at suggestive on the equivalence claim specifically: "near-identical loss" is a training-health signal with no downstream benchmark behind it, which is the proxy this vault distrusts most. One teacher–student pair, 141 GB datacenter hardware, and the cache's storage cost is never quantified — a conspicuous omission in a caching paper, and the number that decides whether any of this is affordable on a consumer SSD. [T1, one study] See caching-the-teachers-top-k-logits-removes-the-teacher-from-memory, the-kl-loss-not-the-model-caps-distillation-context-length.
  • What distillation transfers is distributional novelty, not teacher quality — and the recipe's two-stage ordering is load-bearing (new 2026-07-31, and it is this map's first mechanism-level account of its own play). A student already improved by RL, distilled toward a higher-scoring same-family 7B, lands at nearly the same place as one distilled toward its own weaker pre-RL checkpoint: the two teachers are "distributionally indistinguishable from the student's perspective." Post-trained teachers transfer far more than same-pipeline base ones, and a teacher whose thinking pattern mismatches the student's loses benefit that "cannot be recovered later." So the natural rule — pick the strongest teacher you can serve — is unsupported, and it fails silently. The mechanism is narrow and cheap to instrument: the reward is carried by a shared token set holding 97–99% of both models' probability mass, with overlap rising 72% → 91% in successful runs and flat from initialization in failing ones, which makes the overlap ratio a free early-kill diagnostic. And the repair — off-policy SFT cold start before on-policy distillation — is the ordering Qwen3 already ships, derived here independently from mechanism. Bounded: mathematics only, 1.5B–7B, and the novelty condition is confounded by construction. [T1, on-policy distillation specifically]
  • The on-policy signal has a horizon, and it arrives where reasoning traces are heading (new 2026-07-31). Teacher continuation advantage falls from +0.37 at a 1K-token prefix to +0.02 at 16K; the usable response-length window is 3K–7K, with performance plateauing or declining by 10K–15K and instability propagating back-to-front from the end of the response. The authors say the technique "may not extend cleanly to longer-horizon settings such as extended chain-of-thought or agentic multi-turn interaction." Their proposed explanation is explicitly unverified, so the decay is a measurement and its mechanism a conjecture. Note the direction for this vault's premise: 3K–7K is also roughly where a local fine-tune's activation memory is still tractable, so a practitioner distilling on one or two cards is unlikely to reach the ceiling — and equally unable to observe it. [T1, suggestive]
  • Reasoning distills: teacher traces → student SFT transfers capability that direct RL at small scale cannot reach. Same Qwen-32B base: R1-trace SFT hits 72.6% AIME 2024 vs 47.0% for the RL recipe run directly on it. Exploration is expensive and scale-dependent; imitation is cheap at any scale. [T1]
  • Independent replication, now at production scale: strong-to-weak distillation is how a shipping open-weights family builds its small models. Qwen3 distills fully-trained flagship teachers (235B-A22B, 32B) into its 0.6B–14B dense students — off-policy (teacher outputs across both thinking modes) + on-policy (logit matching) — reporting higher Pass@1 and Pass@64 than running the full four-stage recipe per size, at ~1/10 the GPU hours. Same direction as R1, a different lab and base, outside competition-math — this is what moves the map to consolidating. [T2, second group]
  • A distilled 7B beats frontier generalists on the verifiable domain it was distilled for — Distill-Qwen-7B: 55.5% AIME (GPT-4o-0513: 9.3%), 92.8% MATH-500 — and outruns a 32B reasoner (QwQ-32B-Preview: 50.0%). Specificity, not size, is doing the work. [T1]
  • Cost structure of the play: the leverage is access to teacher traces (~800k of them here), not reproducing the teacher's RL run — which is why open releases of reasoning models move this map more than closed capability jumps. [T1]

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-08-24 — edges from two ingests, one on the build path and one on the transfer mechanism. quantization-aware-healing-4bit-recovery (T1, Multiverse Computing; primary map post-training-quantization) supplies the compound case strand (1) has been missing. This map's settled finding is to derive a small model from a large one rather than train it small; the unanswered form has been how many derivations you can chain and what each costs. Here the chain is three deep — structural compression (120B→60B), bfloat16 recovery, 4-bit quantization — and the rule is that the intermediate recovered checkpoint must not become the teacher. It was never independently trained: its weights are derived by an operator and then tethered to the original's output distribution throughout recovery, so it carries the compressed architecture's ceiling by construction and supervising from it caps the student there. Distilling the 4-bit student from the original instead — architectures differ, which distillation never required — yields a student matching or beating the bfloat16 checkpoint it was quantized from on 7 of 9 benchmarks and matching the 120B teacher on LiveCodeBench at half its parameters. General form: supervise every step of a compression chain from the original, or the chain compounds ceilings instead of capabilities. The load-bearing weakness is the authors' own first limitation — the head-to-head against distillation-from-the-recovered-checkpoint at matched configuration was never run, so the ceiling argument is well-motivated and undemonstrated. routing-divergence-not-behavioral-influence (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts) prices a strand-(2) worry that had been assumed rather than measured: in same-weight self-distillation, teacher and student share every parameter and still route the same token to different experts. Preregistered causal patches on three checkpoints put it at 3.1–4.0% of greedy tokens flipped, i.e. 40–45% of the natural context effect — real, not a null — but matched-norm random noise reproduces 58–74% of that same rate, and the routing term's premium over a random direction is 1.54–2.05× against the content term's 5.6–80×. So the mismatch is bounded and mostly generic to perturbation magnitude, while the direction-specific signal lives in the content term. Practical instruction: debug the content term. Status holds consolidating. See distil-from-the-original-not-from-the-recovered-checkpoint, same-weight-self-distillation-mismatch-is-mostly-magnitude-generic.
  • 2026-08-23 — interaction edge from phantom-gains-measured-null-self-improvement (T1; primary home evaluation-contamination, recorded here for the arm comparison): the map's oldest claim, run against a frozen control, and it survives with a narrower boundary than it has been stated at. This map has held since its first ingest that you derive a small model from a large one rather than train it small, and its transfer-mechanism strand argues about what actually moves. Here the argument is settled per problem rather than per mean: external distillation 8–11 expansions on the rare tail, three self-training recipes 0–2 (p < 10⁻⁶), with the "it just gained more overall" alternative rejected at p < 10⁻⁸ — and inconclusive (p = 0.35) on the ten problems the base model never reaches. Two things follow. The build-path ordering gains a localization: the advantage is about where in the difficulty distribution the gain lands, not merely its size. And the strand-2 methodology upgrades — a difference of means cannot see this, which is probably why the transfer argument has stayed contested. Scope worth stating: every damaged arm here is iterated on the model's own outputs; nothing tests a checker-verified loop of the R1-Zero/GRPO family, so this is not a result about verifiable-reward RL. Status holds consolidating.
  • 2026-08-22 — gc-opd-teacher-verifier-disagreement (T1, Tsinghua; Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B students, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 teacher, 9,527-prompt 32K-filtered GoLongRL subset over nine task families, five long-context benchmarks, four competing methods reimplemented under one shared setup, code released): the diagnostic outlives the method, and it bounds the strand rather than extending it. The dense signal on-policy distillation learns from measures teacher preference, not verified correctness — and measured on frozen responses before training, the two diverge as input length grows. That is a scope condition on this map's whole on-policy literature, which was established at ordinary lengths. See teacher-preference-diverges-from-verified-correctness-as-context-grows. The proposed fix normalizes verifier rewards and trajectory-level distillation scores separately within each rollout group and uses the signed difference as a disagreement residual, spread over tokens by relative advantage. It works, and the honest accounting is why it is worth reading: raw 29.08 → on-policy distillation 39.31 → GC-OPD 40.47 (Qwen3-4B) and 35.12 → 43.56 → 44.65 (Qwen3-8B). The distillation step is worth ~9 points; the calibration on top is worth +1.16 / +1.09, and against the strongest competing method (FiRe-OPD) only +0.97 / +0.64 — sub-point, single seed, no intervals, one model family with a same-family teacher, 100 steps. This map's standing discipline of reading the increment against the best alternative rather than the vanilla one applies, and what survives it is the ablation, which is argued under identical conditions: holding coefficient and allocation fixed, an extra distillation-derived term is worth +0.04 (nothing), the direct group-normalized verifier reward +0.63, and the signed teacher–verifier residual +1.10. Adding the verifier's opinion helps; adding the verifier's disagreement with the teacher helps nearly twice as much. Allocation matters too — absolute-advantage +0.38, uniform +0.72, relative-advantage +1.10, because discarding the advantage's sign hands large credit to strongly negative tokens. Edge to evaluation-contamination: the five-task mean is won while losing LongBench-v1 QA by 2.1 points to a method it outranks, disclosed by the authors. Out of reach as a recipe — eight 80 GB H800/H100 GPUs per run. Status holds consolidating.
  • 2026-08-21 — open-mopd-multi-teacher-capability-imbalance (T1, primary map; SIA-Lab of Tsinghua AIR + ByteDance Seed, recipe / trajectories / eval suites fully open-sourced, 8×A100-80GB): the map's first source with three teachers, and the failure is arithmetic rather than anything about distillation. Consolidating three domain-expert RL teachers (math, code, instruction-following) into one student by dense per-token on-policy reward, with oracle routing by ground-truth domain label so routing error is removed by construction, recovers only 35.6% of the gap between mixed-domain SFT and an oracle router. Distilling each domain into its own student reaches 88.0%, so teachers, initialisation and objective are each sufficient — the score drops specifically when three sufficient signals are combined. The obvious hypothesis is tested and rejected: token-level teacher disagreement is not the dominant bottleneck. What is measured instead is that 99% of gradient tokens go to math and code and ~1% to instruction-following, because reasoning traces are structurally longer than instruction-following answers and a per-batch token quota does not know the difference. IF ends 6.16 points below its reference (3.3× the math gap of 1.89), closes 26% of its remaining gap over steps 200–300 against math's 64%, and is the only domain whose score falls mid-training (−11% over steps 100–200). Speculative decoding uses the student as a draft model for the teacher instead: it proposes tokens, the teacher verifies them in one parallel pass, and the output distribution is provably the teacher's — so disagreement costs latency, not correctness. A student that agrees with its teacher 75% of the time is a 3× accelerator for the teacher. Two consequences worth carrying: the small-model artifact can be a component of the large model's serving stack rather than a substitute for it; and it implies a distillation objective this map has never optimized for — maximize α (expected per-token agreement with the teacher) rather than downstream benchmark score. Note the counter-intuitive corollary from that source's own table: a better student is not automatically a better draft, since its inference cost eats the winnings (T5-large, α=0.82, is the slowest configuration at 1.7×, while T5-small at α=0.75 gives 3.4×). Unmeasured in-vault: whether reasoning-distilled students (distilled-reasoners-beat-direct-rl) make good drafts, given that their long chains of thought diverge from a teacher's token-by-token. See speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution.
  • 2026-07-21 — interaction edge from switch-transformers-sparsity (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts, coined this run): the compress-the-architecture direction of this map's play, four years before reasoning traces. A sparse MoE teacher distills into a dense student — initialize the student from the teacher's non-expert weights, 0.75/0.25 hard/soft loss — keeping ~30% of the sparse quality gain at 95% compression (28% at 99%; 14.7B → 223M), and the trick works on fine-tuned teachers too (SuperGLUE 74.6 → 76.6 of the teacher's 81.3). Where this map's headline compresses a capability (reasoning traces → SFT), Switch compresses an architecture (routed experts → one dense FFN) — and the 30%-of-the-gain number is a usefully honest ceiling: most of what sparsity bought does not survive densification. Deploy-relevant because MoE serving memory tracks total params (mixture-of-experts): distill-to-dense is one exit from that bill. See one-expert-per-token-is-enough-to-scale.
  • 2026-07-20 — advisory edge from judging-llm-as-a-judge-mt-bench (T1, primary map evaluation-contamination): a specific, structural discount on this map's headline shape. The claim "the distilled student beats a bigger generalist" is usually established by a judge — and when the student was distilled from a teacher in the judge's own family, the self-enhancement bias (~10% win-rate inflation for GPT-4 on GPT-4-family outputs) points the wrong way for this map's comfort: it inflates teacher-family outputs, so a student carrying its teacher's style may inherit judge favour it did not earn on substance. Note the asymmetry worth keeping — R1's headline is AIME pass@1, an automatically-checkable answer with no judge involved, while the vendor suite deltas (Qwen3) are judge-adjacent. The distillation claims resting on verifiable checkers are the sturdiest evidence in this map; the ones resting on judged suites now carry a named discount. See llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias.
  • 2026-07-19 — push-back edge from molmo-pixmo-open-vlm (T1, primary map vision-language-models): the first in-vault argument that teacher distillation carries a cost to the commons, not merely a quality ceiling. Molmo's charge is that the strongest "open" VLMs are proprietary models distilled into open weights, leaving the community "missing foundational knowledge about how to build performant VLMs from scratch" — capability transferred, understanding not. It then builds a top-of-suite model with zero teacher data. This does not contest this map's efficacy claim (distillation works, and works cheaply), but it sharpens what the leverage costs: a distilled artifact inherits its teacher's ceiling, its blind spots, and its license, and teaches you nothing about how to get there another way. Relevant to this vault's premise, where the goal is to understand models well enough to modify them. See open-vlms-are-mostly-distilled-closed-vlms.
  • 2026-07-19 — advisory edge from orpo-monolithic-preference-optimization (T1, primary map post-training-rl): an unasked question about this map's method. Cross-entropy SFT has no term that pushes anything down, and raises the likelihood of rejected responses alongside chosen ones (cross-entropy-sft-raises-the-odds-of-rejected-text). Distilled teacher traces are chosen-only data with no rejected side at all — so a distillation SFT run has, by construction, zero downward pressure on anything the traces do not contain. What that does to a student's refusal behaviour, format discipline, and general capability is unmeasured, and it is a second mechanism (beside narrow-distribution forgetting) for the regression this map already flags below.
  • 2026-07-18 — interaction edge from llava-visual-instruction-tuning (T1, primary map vision-language-models): the teacher-trace maneuver run sideways instead of down. Distillation in this map transfers capability from a big model to a small one in the same modality; LLaVA transfers a text-only teacher's reasoning into a student operating in a modality the teacher never had, by feeding the teacher symbolic proxies (captions, bounding boxes) for what it cannot see. Same leverage point this map names — access to teacher output, not the teacher's training run — generalized past model-size compression to capability bootstrapping where no corpus exists. See synthetic-instructions-from-a-blind-teacher-bootstrap-a-corpus.
  • 2026-07-17 — ingest of qwen3-technical-report (Qwen Team, Alibaba, arXiv 2025-05-14, Apache-2.0, T2): this ingest moves the map emerging → consolidating — a second, independent, production-scale confirmation that strong-to-weak distillation is the small-model build path (~1/10 GPU-hours vs the per-model full recipe). The thinking-budget / mode-fusion mechanism is an adjacent test-time-compute lever (post-training-rl); its leaderboard deltas pass through the contamination lens (evaluation-contamination). See strong-to-weak-distillation-is-the-small-model-build-path.
  • 2026-07-16 — ingest of deepseek-r1-incentivizing-reasoning (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2025-01-22 / Nature 2025, weights MIT, T1): the distillation half — six distilled students (Qwen/Llama, 1.5B–70B) released; distillation-vs-direct-RL controlled at 32B. Open questions:

  • Is there a measurable teacher-selection criterion, now that benchmark rank is ruled out? (new 2026-07-31.) distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank says the gain tracks distributional novelty and that a stronger same-family teacher buys nothing, but leaves novelty as a description rather than a quantity — the authors flag their cross-family comparisons as confounded by data, tokenizer and architecture all at once. The obvious candidate is sitting in the same paper: initial token-overlap ratio, which separates aligned from misaligned pairs at initialization and is computable before any training. Nobody has tested whether it ranks candidate teachers, which would turn a post-hoc explanation into a cheap pre-flight check — score three candidate teachers' overlap with the student on a few hundred prompts and pick by it. That is hours of inference, and it is the most decision-relevant unrun thing on this map.
  • Does the horizon ceiling bind before or after the memory ceiling? (new 2026-07-31.) On-policy distillation's reward decays to near-nothing by 16K tokens (dense-token-rewards-decay-with-trajectory-depth) and a 4-bit local fine-tune's activations get expensive in roughly the same range (distributed-training). Which binds first on one or two consumer cards is unmeasured and decides whether this is a limitation a local practitioner will ever meet. It also means the local case cannot observe the failure, so anyone extrapolating a small local result to a long-horizon run is extrapolating past where the evidence stops.
  • Both in-vault sources are still vendor/authors' own evals (R1 T1, Qwen3 T2); the direction now replicates across two labs, but a neutral third-party reproduction and contamination audits of the distill sets are the map's next sources.
  • Does the pattern hold outside verifiable domains — style, tool use, agentic behavior — where no checker filtered the teacher traces?
  • Task-specific distillation for non-reasoning targets (the classic student-beats-bigger-generalist play on classification/extraction/domain tasks) predates R1 and has no in-vault source yet.
  • What the student loses: general-capability regression from narrow distillation (the catastrophic-forgetting question, shared with peft-methods) is unmeasured here. Sharpened 2026-07-18: that map now carries quantified forgetting evidence (lora-regularizes-more-than-it-adapts) and a mechanism (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting) — neither applied to distillation students, which are trained on a narrow trace distribution and should be the most exposed case.
  • How is the distillation SFT actually run? peft-methods went contested 2026-07-18: low-rank adaptation underperforms full fine-tuning when the target domain is far from pretraining. Reasoning traces plausibly are such a domain — so whether these students were distilled with full fine-tuning or PEFT, and whether the rank budget binds here, is a question this map cannot currently answer about its own headline results.
