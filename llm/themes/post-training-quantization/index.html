Post-training quantization (methods & formats) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 during the seed ingest at consolidating: two independent T1s from different groups and opposite directions — inference (AWQ) and training-substrate (QLoRA) — converge on "4-bit weight representations retain the quality that matters." Flagged for David's review.

Status: consolidating Status note (2026-08-02): holds consolidating, and the damage model gains a third axis on the same week it gained its second — with the caveat that the source cannot cleanly attribute it to four bits. Last night the map recorded that damage is signal-shaped as well as task-shaped. Tonight silent-failures-quantized-reasoning (T1, 30,000 judged chain-of-thought outputs, taxonomy validated at Cohen's κ = 0.906) reports it is also process-shaped: accuracy moves at most 3.1 points anywhere in a 5-model × 3-precision grid while the composition of wrong answers shifts sharply on the smallest models — LLaMA 3.2-3B under NF4 goes from 44% to 78% Shortcut Collapse and from 15.8% to 0.2% Confidence Snowballing, patterns that produce a wrong answer at similar rates so accuracy sees nothing. Models at 12B and above are statistically invariant, which is the same concentrate-at-the-small-end direction as at-a-fixed-footprint-take-the-larger-quantized-model. The discount is large and this map should apply it plainly: the biggest single distributional move in the paper is FP32 → FP16 (LLaMA 3.1-8B, 29.9 → 13.8, with NF4 adding 0.2 more), so the defensible claim is about precision reduction in general rather than about the four-bit floor this map is organized around, and the paper's own conclusion contradicts its tables on the direction of the effect. Separately, gemq-global-expert-mixed-precision-moe (T1, ICML 2026) lands two things here: the first throughput measurement for mixed-precision expert quantization — 1.4–1.6× batch-1 decode, Mixtral-8×7B at 12.0 GB / 88.6 tok/s — which closes the footprint-only bound this map has carried since 2026-07-25; and a second, independent measurement of the calibration confound, GSM8K moving 31.77 → 42.30 on a corpus swap alone, with perplexity barely moving under the same intervention. Status note (2026-08-01): holds consolidating, and the map gains a second axis of damage rather than a challenge to the first. Everything this map has measured about what quantization costs is an accuracy measurement, and the resulting rule — damage is task-shaped, load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and instruction compliance, near-free for single-step commonsense — survives untouched. vlm-quantization-uncertainty-signals (T1) measures a different construct on identical inputs and finds the damage is also signal-shaped: 4-bit NF4 on a 2B VLM costs 1.6 accuracy points and 15 points of error-detection AUROC, with the model's ability to emit a parseable stated confidence collapsing from 99% to 64%. The status does not move because nothing here contradicts an existing graded claim — it names a dimension no source in this map had ever evaluated. Single author, no venue, two scale points, one model family.

Sub-threads: none — one claim (4-bit is the default; damage is an allocation problem) run across weights/activations/KV cache via one recurring mechanism (rotation) and one recurring allocation framework, too interlinked to split [reviewed 2026-08-01 @ 21 bullets]

What we believe now:

  • The damage is process-shaped too — the answer survives and the derivation does not — and mixed- precision allocation is now measured to be faster as well as smaller (new 2026-08-02, two independent additions). Process. Across 30,000 chain-of-thought outputs from five instruction-tuned models (3B–14B) at FP32/FP16/NF4 on four reasoning benchmarks, judged into a six-category taxonomy validated against two human annotators at κ = 0.906, accuracy drops at most 3.1 points anywhere — "by any standard evaluation checklist, none of these five models would get flagged." Underneath, the kind of failure moves: LLaMA 3.2-3B's wrong answers go 44% → 78% Shortcut Collapse (skipping the chain) and 15.8% → 0.2% Confidence Snowballing (an early error propagating through a coherent chain), which reads as precision reduction degrading the capacity to sustain a chain rather than adding uniform noise. It is size-dependent (only the 3B and 8B models shift; 12B+ are invariant) and benchmark-dependent (GSM8K is near-immune at 0.4–2.6% because it forces explicit arithmetic; LogiQA and ARC-Challenge carry the largest rates and drops). Three discounts, and the third is the one to carry: judge cross-agreement is 80.6% for the rising category and 0.0% for the collapsing one; scope is BitsAndBytes NF4 only, and the authors note GPTQ/AWQ round against calibration data rather than onto a fixed NormalFloat grid; and the largest single move in the paper is FP32 → FP16, not FP16 → NF4 — so this is evidence about precision reduction generally, and only the 3B model shows the shift at the four-bit step. Speed. Mixed-precision expert quantization has a throughput number for the first time: batch-1 decode on one H100 with fused Triton kernels for 1/2/3-bit dequantization runs 1.4× to 1.6× faster than an FP16 baseline with torch.compile, and gets faster as the budget falls — the expected sign for a bandwidth-bound decode step, now measured on the routed case rather than inferred from four-bit-weights-put-llms-on-device. Mixtral-8×7B goes from over 90 GB (OOM on an H100) to 12.0 GB at 88.6 tok/s. Unreachable from llama.cpp or Ollama, whose menus cannot express per-expert bit assignment. [T1 ×2] See quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation, mixed-precision-moe-finally-has-a-throughput-number.
  • The damage is signal-shaped as well as task-shaped: 4-bit is nearly free for the answer and expensive for knowing whether the answer is right (new 2026-08-01, and it is a new axis rather than a revision). On identical inputs across 5,700 predictions, 4-bit NormalFloat on Qwen2-VL-2B costs 1.6 accuracy points (0.904 → 0.888) — comfortably inside the band this map calls the four-bit default — while the error-detection AUROC of the model's own mean token probability falls 0.95 → 0.80, and its rate of emitting a parseable natural-language confidence at all falls 99% → 64%. Translated into the decision a confidence signal exists to support: area under the risk–coverage curve nearly triples (0.017 → 0.048), and a deferral threshold calibrated on clean inputs stops holding under shift, admitting 12% of errors at the hardest degradation. The second half corroborates this map's older rule from an unexpected side — refusing to emit a well-formed confidence is an instruction-compliance failure, which is exactly what the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff found most precision-sensitive at 3-bit. Two studies, two model families, one conclusion: what 4-bit takes first is format discipline. Operating rule: evaluate a quantized model's confidence, not only its accuracy — nothing else in this vault ever has, for any model. Scope: one family, 2B and 7B, a 100-item four-option task, and the 7B fp16 arm was never run so the size interaction is inferred across arms. [T1, one study]
  • A precision budget should also be allocated by what the number is used for, and a quantity consumed as a ranking tolerates far more error than one consumed as a value (new 2026-08-01 — a fourth partitioning axis, and the first that is not about where the weight sits). The map's three existing partitionings are per-layer (AlphaQ), per-tensor-class (shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts) and per-modality (vlm-precision-should-be-allocated-by-modality). This one splits on the consumer. In a two-stage sparse feed-forward network, stage 1's numeric outputs are discarded and only their order survives into a top-k mask: INT4 proxy weights beat FP16 proxies by up to 9.0 points at 70% sparsity, which is not "adequate" but better, read by the authors as mild regularization of the ranking. Using those same proxy outputs as values instead of re-computing the selected channels exactly costs 18.1–24.3 points. Same weights, same bit-width, same model — more than sufficient as an ordering, catastrophic as a magnitude. That also explains an anomaly this map has half-noticed: quantization damage surfaces first on tasks with long dependency chains because those consume errors as values and propagate them. Practitioner form: for every place you spend precision, ask whether the consumer needs the number or only its order — routers, top-k selectors, draft models and eviction policies are all order-consumers, and none of them has been separately budgeted by anyone. [T1, one framework]
  • Rotation has become this map's general-purpose preprocessing primitive, and it now works on the KV cache too (new 2026-07-29, generalizing the 2026-07-28 bullet below). The vault has now seen the same operation do the same job in four independent places, and the unifying statement is that a rotation destroys the structure that makes a quantizer's job hard — outliers, channel concentration, coordinate dependence — at essentially no cost, because orthogonal transforms preserve the geometry everything downstream depends on. (i) On weights and activations, deleting outliers outright (QuaRot). (ii) Bolted onto an unchanged 4-bit GPTQ recipe by a third group as a drop-in step: HumanEval 53.05 → 57.93 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct while perplexity moves only 8.73 → 8.55. (iii) On the KV cache, where a random rotation induces a concentrated Beta distribution on the coordinates and makes them near-independent — at which point the optimal vector quantizer collapses into per-coordinate optimal scalar quantizers, which is what makes the method cheap, vectorizable and data-free. (iv) In the smoothing literature it replaced. The practitioner form: if you are quantizing anything below 8 bits, rotate first — it is close to free and it is the step that makes the rest of the machinery work. [T1, four sources, three groups]
  • The KV cache is a 3.5-bit object, with no calibration data required (new 2026-07-29). This map's KV-cache cell held one source (KIVI, 2-bit, asymmetric, modelling where the error concentrates — per channel for keys, per token for values). The second source attacks it from source-coding theory and lands better: rotate, quantize each coordinate optimally, then spend one bit on a Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss sketch of the residual to remove the inner-product bias that MSE-optimal quantizers introduce — which matters specifically because attention is an inner product, so an MSE-optimal cache carries systematic query-dependent error rather than noise. Provably within ≈2.7× of the information-theoretic distortion floor. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, LongBench-E average 50.06 at 3.5 bits, exactly the 16-bit full cache, and 49.44 at 2.5 bits against KIVI's 48.50 at 3 — achieved while also quantizing generated tokens, which KIVI and PolarQuant leave in full precision. Two qualifiers the map must carry: the damage is task-shaped, concentrating in summarization (24.80 vs 26.55 at 2.5 bits) while QA, few-shot and code stay flat; and ±0.2 here is noise — KIVI at 5 bits exceeds the full cache (50.16 vs 50.06). [T1]
  • 4-bit weight-only quantization retains the quality that matters — shown from two directions: AWQ preserves inference quality by protecting ~1% of activation-salient channels, and QLoRA fine-tunes through a frozen 4-bit base while matching 16-bit fine-tune performance. [T1, two groups]
  • Quantization error is concentrated, and the activations say where. Salient channels are found from the activation distribution, not weight magnitudes; per-channel equivalent scaling protects them without mixed precision. Any quantization decision made by staring only at weights is working blind. [T1]
  • …but the concentration is a property of the basis, and you can rotate it away (new 2026-07-28, and it reorders the two bullets above). Every outlier remedy in this map until now takes the outlier channels as given and works around them — protect the salient ~1% (AWQ), migrate their difficulty into the paired weight rows (SmoothQuant), or keep them in higher precision outright (QUIK's 256 retained features, Atom's 128). Computational invariance says the coordinate system is arbitrary: in a pre-norm RMSNorm architecture, multiplying an input-side weight matrix on the left by an orthogonal Q and the paired output-side matrix on the right by Qᵀ leaves the function unchanged, because RMSNorm divides by a norm and rotation preserves norms. Choose Q to be a randomized Hadamard and the rotation is also incoherence processing: every coordinate mixes into every other, so a spike in one channel becomes a small contribution to all of them and there are no outliers left to protect. Cost: 1.5 Hadamard transforms per layer at O(d log d), the rest fused into the weights. The evidence that removal beats accommodation is one table read across its rows, at 4-bit weights/activations/KV on LLaMA-2-7B against a 5.47 FP16 baseline — SmoothQuant 83.12 (total collapse; per-channel scaling was engineered for 8 bits and does not extend down), OmniQuant 14.26, QUIK-4B 8.87 while retaining 256 outlier channels, and rotation 6.10 while retaining none. General statement, which subsumes the two bullets above as basis-preserving special cases: the enemy is outlier concentration, and concentration is basis-dependent. This also retro-explains the 2026-07-20 FA3 coincidence — the identical random-rotation trick appearing inside an attention kernel is the same theorem applied transiently instead of fused. Preconditions are architectural, not incidental: pre-norm RMSNorm, and power-of-two head counts and head dimensions for the across-head construction. [T1]
  • 4 bits is no longer a weight-only claim, and the bill scales inversely with model size (new 2026-07-28). With the outliers rotated out, weights, activations and the KV cache all go to INT4 and the matmul itself runs on 4-bit tensor cores. Two consequences. Compute, not just memory: prefill runs 1.97–2.16× faster on a 7B at batch 1–64 and up to 3.33× on 70B, with 3.6–3.9× lower peak decode memory — measured on RTX 3090s, since the authors target consumer cards — which inverts quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill for the weight-and-activation case, because there is no dequantize step in the inner loop. And accuracy: the six-task zero-shot average falls 1.09 points at 70B, 2.80 at 13B and 4.18 at 7B. Every "retains 99%" framing of end-to-end 4-bit is the 70B number, and the gradient runs against this vault's premise — small models pay most. The same size-dependence appears in calibration: round-to-nearest with no calibration data at all is lossless at INT8 on both sizes, while at INT4 it costs 2.27 perplexity on 7B against 0.34 on 70B, so second-order reconstruction earns its two GPU-hours mainly at small scale. Read the 7B number as a floor on the damage, not a bound: finer groups recover it partly (per-column 6.10 → group-64 5.88 ppl), and the evaluation is six commonsense tasks with no GSM8K, no MMLU and no reasoning benchmark, the family this map established on 2026-07-24 to be least sensitive. [T1]
  • Concentration has a structural form too: protect the parameters every token passes through (new 2026-07-26). The salient-channel finding is a within-tensor statement. A crude 16-cell sweep on Mixtral-8×7B supplies the between-tensor one, and the asymmetry is severe. From an FP16 baseline of 70.51% 5-shot MMLU, taking the 96.6% of parameters that are experts down to 2 bits costs 3.85 points and saves 67 GB; taking the 3.4% that are not — attention, embeddings, the router — down to 2 bits costs 11.5 points and saves under 2 GB. Three times the damage from a thirtieth of the parameters. The mechanism generalizes past MoE: error in a weight on every token's forward path compounds through the residual stream, while error in a weight that fires for a routed fraction does not. This is the coarse, calibration-free counterpart to moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity's per-layer integer program — and it supplies the first measured basis for a llama.cpp convention this map has only ever seen asserted, that K-quant schemes hold attention and embedding tensors above the FFN. Note the quantizer here is HQQ, which is data-free, so this particular table is immune to the calibration confound below. Bounded: perplexity and MMLU only, one model, and the "2-bit" scheme is really 2.6 bits/param once scales and zero points are counted. [T1, venue-less]
  • Calibration-set overfitting is real, larger than anyone reported, and it is a hole in this map's evidence base (new 2026-07-25). AWQ's no-reconstruction design was sold on keeping calibration cheap and avoiding overfitting the calibration set — carried here since the seed ingest as one method's authors' assertion about their competitors, ungraded. There is now a measurement, and the effect is bigger than the rhetoric: holding model, method and bit budget fixed on Mixtral-8×7B at 2.5 bits and varying only the calibration corpus, HumanEval reads 29.3 (C4) / 13.7 (MATH) / 25.1 (GitHub-Code) — a 2.1× spread — with MMLU moving 10.8 points and GSM8K 11.4. And there is no tidy corrective rule: GitHub-Code calibration scores worse on HumanEval than generic C4, so "calibrate on your target domain" is not the fix. The operating consequence is uncomfortable for this map: a quantized model's benchmark score is only interpretable alongside its calibration corpus, and almost nobody reports it — which means every reconstruction-family comparison here carries an unquantified confound. Scope honestly: measured on MoE bit allocation, where input-dependent routing makes calibration coverage structurally harder than in a dense model, so this is an upper bound on the dense effect, and it does not vindicate AWQ over GPTQ (the source's own pipeline runs GPTQ underneath). [T1, one study]
  • NF4's premise travels: trained weights are approximately normally distributed, so a code matched to that distribution (4-bit NormalFloat) is information-theoretically efficient for them. [T1]
  • Both live PTQ method families are now in-vault, and they agree on the headline. Reconstruction (GPTQ: layer-wise second-order error compensation against calibration data — 175B in ~4 GPU-hours, 3–4 bits, negligible degradation) and scaling (AWQ: protect activation-salient channels, no reconstruction) reach 4-bit from opposite philosophies. That's a third independent T1 behind the central claim. The trade between them — calibration-set overfitting risk vs protection quality — has no neutral head-to-head yet. [T1, three groups]
  • Activations can be quantized too — by moving their difficulty into the weights — and that's what unlocks INT8 compute, not just a smaller footprint. Everything else in this map quantizes weights (or the KV cache) and leaves the activation path in FP16. SmoothQuant does W8A8: it exploits the fact that activation error concentrates in fixed outlier channels (the same dims spike ~100× across all tokens — naive per-tensor W8A8 drops OPT-175B to near-random), and migrates that difficulty into the paired weight rows via an offline, algebraically-identical per-channel rescale (sⱼ = max(|Xⱼ|)^α / max(|Wⱼ|)^(1−α), folded into the previous layer → zero inference overhead). Both sides then quantize to INT8: up to 1.56× speedup, 2× memory, negligible loss, 530B served in one node. Note the precision floor — this is the 8-bit throughput story (INT8 tensor cores), distinct from the map's 4-bit weight-only memory/on-device story; the map carries both without conflating them. [T1]
  • The activation-side memory wall now has a first source — and its error is directionally structured too. KIVI quantizes the KV cache to 2 bits tuning-free by matching granularity to structure — keys per-channel (outliers concentrate in a few channels), values per-token — near-lossless with 2.6× less peak memory, up to 4× batch, 2.35–3.47× throughput. Same lesson as weights: error concentrates and the distribution says where. This is orthogonal to weight quantization (shrinks activations, not params) and composes with PagedAttention's placement fix on the same wall (local-inference) — precision and paging stack. [T1]

  • 2026-08-03 — edge from kimi-k3-open-frontier-intelligence (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts), and it is a push-back on this map's framing rather than on any of its measurements. Every source here takes a finished checkpoint as input — that is what post-training quantization means. The strongest open-weights model released to date does not do that: its MoE expert weights are held in MXFP4 with MXFP8 activations, under quantization-aware training running from the SFT stage through the whole of RL, so the released weights are the 4-bit weights and the model never existed in full precision after pre-training. Map holds consolidating — there is no BF16 control arm anywhere in the report, so this supplies no effect size and cannot move a status. Three things it does supply. The tensor-class partition replicates from the largest instance available: experts quantized, attention projections / latent-MoE projections / shared experts / routers held higher, which is the same split GEMQ reached independently on 2026-08-02 and the same shape as the Mixtral offload sweep's 16-cell result (2-bit experts cost 3.85 MMLU points; 2-bit attention, embeddings and gates cost 11.5 from a thirtieth of the parameters). Three groups, three methods, one conclusion — experts are the cheap parameters and the shared path is not — which is now the best-replicated allocation finding on this map. The RL detail is genuinely new and belongs on evaluation-contamination's non-equivalence ladder: rollout and training share the same quantization scheme, "eliminating the train–inference mismatch." An RL loop whose rollout policy is quantized and whose gradient step is not is optimizing a policy it will never deploy, silently — and this is the ladder's first entry with a practitioner-visible fix. And the architecture was changed to suit the arithmetic: SiTU-GLU caps both multiplicative factors of the gated FFN specifically because SwiGLU is unbounded in both and coincident large coordinates "increase overflow risk in low-precision arithmetic" — this map has tracked activation outliers since SmoothQuant as something a quantizer must work around; here a pretraining team designed them out. Reachability caveat, and it is the third consecutive one: MXFP4 is a microscaling block format with datacenter hardware support, not a llama.cpp K-quant, so like AlphaQ's per-layer allocation and GEMQ's Triton kernels this is unreachable from the local stack.   • 2026-08-01 — co-primary ingest of vlm-quantization-uncertainty-signals (single author, arXiv 2026-07-27, T1; co-primary map vision-language-models): the first measurement in this vault of what quantization does to a model's metacognition, and it comes apart from accuracy. Three arms at a comparable memory footprint on identical inputs — Qwen2-VL-2B at fp16, the same model at 4-bit NF4 with double quantization, and Qwen2-VL-7B at 4-bit — over 5,700 predictions spanning a clean condition plus six photographic degradations (JPEG q=30/15/7, motion blur, low light, glare, rotation, resample) at three severities. Methodological edge to evaluation-contamination: the paper's ECE-versus-AUROC argument (verbalized confidence at ECE 0.011 and AUROC 0.56 on the same predictions) is a sixth instance of construct mismatch and the same shape as perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats. Caveats: single author, no venue, two scale points which the author says is not a scaling law, the 7B fp16 arm was never run, n ≈ 100 per condition with undefined AUROC at ceiling, a four-option Food101 classification rather than open-ended VQA, one model family, and synthetic rather than authentic camera degradations.   • 2026-07-31 — a third partitioning reaches the same allocation rule, and a different quantization cliff, from lqa-quantized-adaptive-vlm-edge (T1, primary map vision-language-models): this map now holds three independent measurements saying a quantization budget is an allocation problem, not a global setting, each partitioning the parameter space differently — per layer (AlphaQ's integer program over power-law tail exponents, Qwen1.5-MoE at 3.5 average bits matching BF16), per tensor class (the Mixtral 16-cell sweep: 2-bit experts cost 3.85 MMLU points from 96.6% of the parameters, the 2-bit shared path costs 11.5 from 3.4%), and now per modality (a VLM's vision tower at 4 bits, its text tower at 8, one or two projection/layer-norm layers per tower at float16 by Hessian trace — 63.98% averaged over seven datasets against 62.91 for uniform HQQ, 60.42 Quanto, 59.99 BitsAndBytes at the same 4 bits). Three partitionings, three groups, one rule. Weakest of the three, and the vault should say so: the authors never run the reversed assignment (text at 4, vision at 8), so the direction of the modality asymmetry rests on a mechanism story rather than an ablation. Two edges that matter more than the method. First, at 8 bits the allocation buys nothing — four quantizers within 0.19 points — so a CLIP-class vision encoder has an indifference width of 8 bits, twice the 4-bit floor this map carries for LLM weights, and every design decision only earns its keep below it. Second, the descent below that width is a cliff, not a ladder: 60.59 / 42.54 / 3.02% at 3 / 2 / 1 bits against a 65.56 reference, where the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff found the 8→3-bit descent on an 8B language model graded and task-shaped. One model, so it is a hypothesis with one measurement under it. Open questions:

  • Is the process shift a four-bit effect at all? (new 2026-08-02, and it is cheap.) The one source measuring reasoning-process damage reports its largest single distributional move at FP32 → FP16, a step nobody considers aggressive, with the NF4 step adding almost nothing on four of five models. Either half precision does something to small-model reasoning that nobody has looked for, or the measurement is picking up something other than precision. The disambiguating run is small: repeat the taxonomy classification on one 3B model at FP32, BF16, FP16, INT8 and NF4, which separates dynamic range from mantissa width and puts the four-bit step in a ladder rather than at the end of a two-point line. The authors' own scope note points the second way — GPTQ and AWQ round against calibration data rather than onto a fixed NormalFloat grid, so the same taxonomy under those methods is the other half of the experiment and would also say whether this is an artifact of BitsAndBytes.
  • Does a global expert-level allocation work without calibration data? (new 2026-08-02, shared with mixture-of-experts.) This map now holds two mixed-precision allocators with opposite cost profiles: a calibration-free spectral criterion that allocates per layer, and a calibration-dependent Fisher criterion that allocates per expert globally and wins on perplexity in every cell — while demonstrating, in its own tables, the 10-point calibration swing that makes calibration dependence expensive. The hybrid is unrun and both halves are published.
  • AWQ vs GPTQ head-to-head: both families are in-vault, each evaluated by its own authors (AWQ's overfitting critique of reconstruction included). A neutral comparison — calibration sensitivity, domain transfer, modern architectures, and where newer methods beat both — is the map's sharpest remaining gap, and it got sharper and better-specified on 2026-07-25. The critique's premise is now measured — calibration corpus choice swings a task benchmark 2.1× — but it was measured on MoE bit allocation with GPTQ underneath, so it establishes that the axis matters without ranking the two families on it. The experiment that would settle nine days of this: one dense model, one bit-width, AWQ vs GPTQ, three calibration corpora, evaluated on GSM8K and MMLU rather than commonsense tasks. Everything needed to run it is public.
  • How much of this map's evidence base is confounded by an unreported knob (new 2026-07-25). If calibration corpus moves task scores by double digits, then every published GPTQ-family number in this map — including its own 4-bit headline evidence — was produced with a specific corpus that was usually WikiText2 or C4 and rarely stated as a condition. Nobody has re-run the map's foundational comparisons under matched calibration, and until someone does, the cross-paper 4-bit agreement this map rests on is slightly weaker than it reads.
  • Below 4 bits: GPTQ reports usable 2-bit/ternary in restricted settings; the GGUF IQ-series lives there (local-inference carries the format menu) — where quality actually falls off a cliff needs graded head-to-head evidence.
  • Weight-only is no longer the whole story: KIVI lands the first KV-cache source (2-bit KV), and SmoothQuant (2026-07-21) lands the first activation-weight (W8A8) source — closing the "activation quantization for the compute-bound regime" half of this gap. The below-8-bits question resolved 2026-07-28 (quarot-outlier-free-4bit-rotated-llms) and resolved against the method that raised it: smoothing does not survive the trip, collapsing to perplexity 83 at 4 bits, while rotation reaches W4A4KV4 with no retained outliers. What replaces it is sharper and this vault should treat it as the map's new headline gap: all of the end-to-end 4-bit accuracy evidence is commonsense zero-shot, so the one number that would decide whether a 7B is usable at W4A4 — GSM8K or MMLU, where the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff says quantization damage actually concentrates — does not exist for any activation-quantized model in this vault. A 4.18-point drop on the easy family is a reason to expect worse on the hard one and nothing more. Still open alongside it: where 2-bit KV breaks vs the 4-bit setting others use; whether rotation composes with the mixed-precision allocation results (the rotation source names MoE as future work, and this map now holds two independent MoE bit-allocation findings it could be combined with); and the combined paging + precision + GQA measurement (KIVI's KV bits, PagedAttention's placement, GQA's head count are levers on one budget that no in-vault source measures together). vLLM's PagedAttention attacks the same wall from the placement side (local-inference).
  • Nine ways to train an adapter on a quantized base and zero published head-to-head (new 2026-07-29). This is the AWQ-vs-GPTQ hole one level up and wider: the reference PEFT implementation documents adapter training on bitsandbytes, GPTQ, AQLM, EETQ, HQQ, torchao, Intel Neural Compressor, Transformer Engine and AWQ-by-reference, and does not rank them on quality, memory or speed — nor does anyone else. "Supported" is not a uniform predicate across that list, spanning a maintained default path and a paragraph with a Colab link. The narrower and more decision-relevant version, for a reader who cannot merge: what does an unmerged adapter cost at inference on an aggressively quantized base, which is the price of the AQLM/torchao path and is unmeasured anywhere.
  • KV quantization has two sources and no speed number from either (new 2026-07-29). Both KIVI and the 3.5-bit result are footprint-and-quality only. That gap matters more here than for weight quantization, because a KV quantizer sits on the critical path of every attention call and quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill shows prefill and decode can move in opposite directions — so a compression ratio says nothing about whether the thing is faster. Also unmeasured: whether the 3.5-bit result holds with quantized weights underneath rather than a full-precision model, which is the only configuration anyone would actually deploy.
  • How merging a LoRA adapter changes the weight distribution a quantizer then sees — the merge-then-quantize vs quantize-then-adapt ordering question — is live for the local-deploy pipeline and unmeasured here. Sharpened 2026-07-18: if LoRA inserts high-ranking singular vectors orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting), then a merged adapter hands the quantizer a spectrally different matrix than the base — and every method in this map calibrates against distributional assumptions (NF4's normality premise, AWQ's salience channels, GPTQ's second-order reconstruction). Whether merged-adapter weights quantize as cleanly as base weights is now a specific, testable question rather than a vague one.
  • KV-cache claims need a GQA-adjusted restatement. See the 2026-07-18 through-line entry: this map's activation-side numbers were measured pre-GQA and overstate the marginal gain on current models.
