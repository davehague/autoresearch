Post-training quantization (methods & formats) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 during the seed ingest at consolidating: two independent T1s from different groups and opposite directions — inference (AWQ) and training-substrate (QLoRA) — converge on "4-bit weight representations retain the quality that matters." Flagged for David's review.

Status: consolidating

What we believe now:

  • 4-bit weight-only quantization retains the quality that matters — shown from two directions: AWQ preserves inference quality by protecting ~1% of activation-salient channels, and QLoRA fine-tunes through a frozen 4-bit base while matching 16-bit fine-tune performance. [T1, two groups]
  • Quantization error is concentrated, and the activations say where. Salient channels are found from the activation distribution, not weight magnitudes; per-channel equivalent scaling protects them without mixed precision. Any quantization decision made by staring only at weights is working blind. [T1]
  • Concentration has a structural form too: protect the parameters every token passes through (new 2026-07-26). The salient-channel finding is a within-tensor statement. A crude 16-cell sweep on Mixtral-8×7B supplies the between-tensor one, and the asymmetry is severe. From an FP16 baseline of 70.51% 5-shot MMLU, taking the 96.6% of parameters that are experts down to 2 bits costs 3.85 points and saves 67 GB; taking the 3.4% that are not — attention, embeddings, the router — down to 2 bits costs 11.5 points and saves under 2 GB. Three times the damage from a thirtieth of the parameters. The mechanism generalizes past MoE: error in a weight on every token's forward path compounds through the residual stream, while error in a weight that fires for a routed fraction does not. This is the coarse, calibration-free counterpart to moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity's per-layer integer program — and it supplies the first measured basis for a llama.cpp convention this map has only ever seen asserted, that K-quant schemes hold attention and embedding tensors above the FFN. Note the quantizer here is HQQ, which is data-free, so this particular table is immune to the calibration confound below. Bounded: perplexity and MMLU only, one model, and the "2-bit" scheme is really 2.6 bits/param once scales and zero points are counted. [T1, venue-less]
  • Calibration-set overfitting is real, larger than anyone reported, and it is a hole in this map's evidence base (new 2026-07-25). AWQ's no-reconstruction design was sold on keeping calibration cheap and avoiding overfitting the calibration set — carried here since the seed ingest as one method's authors' assertion about their competitors, ungraded. There is now a measurement, and the effect is bigger than the rhetoric: holding model, method and bit budget fixed on Mixtral-8×7B at 2.5 bits and varying only the calibration corpus, HumanEval reads 29.3 (C4) / 13.7 (MATH) / 25.1 (GitHub-Code) — a 2.1× spread — with MMLU moving 10.8 points and GSM8K 11.4. And there is no tidy corrective rule: GitHub-Code calibration scores worse on HumanEval than generic C4, so "calibrate on your target domain" is not the fix. The operating consequence is uncomfortable for this map: a quantized model's benchmark score is only interpretable alongside its calibration corpus, and almost nobody reports it — which means every reconstruction-family comparison here carries an unquantified confound. Scope honestly: measured on MoE bit allocation, where input-dependent routing makes calibration coverage structurally harder than in a dense model, so this is an upper bound on the dense effect, and it does not vindicate AWQ over GPTQ (the source's own pipeline runs GPTQ underneath). [T1, one study]
  • NF4's premise travels: trained weights are approximately normally distributed, so a code matched to that distribution (4-bit NormalFloat) is information-theoretically efficient for them. [T1]
  • Both live PTQ method families are now in-vault, and they agree on the headline. Reconstruction (GPTQ: layer-wise second-order error compensation against calibration data — 175B in ~4 GPU-hours, 3–4 bits, negligible degradation) and scaling (AWQ: protect activation-salient channels, no reconstruction) reach 4-bit from opposite philosophies. That's a third independent T1 behind the central claim. The trade between them — calibration-set overfitting risk vs protection quality — has no neutral head-to-head yet. [T1, three groups]
  • Activations can be quantized too — by moving their difficulty into the weights — and that's what unlocks INT8 compute, not just a smaller footprint. Everything else in this map quantizes weights (or the KV cache) and leaves the activation path in FP16. SmoothQuant does W8A8: it exploits the fact that activation error concentrates in fixed outlier channels (the same dims spike ~100× across all tokens — naive per-tensor W8A8 drops OPT-175B to near-random), and migrates that difficulty into the paired weight rows via an offline, algebraically-identical per-channel rescale (sⱼ = max(|Xⱼ|)^α / max(|Wⱼ|)^(1−α), folded into the previous layer → zero inference overhead). Both sides then quantize to INT8: up to 1.56× speedup, 2× memory, negligible loss, 530B served in one node. Note the precision floor — this is the 8-bit throughput story (INT8 tensor cores), distinct from the map's 4-bit weight-only memory/on-device story; the map carries both without conflating them. [T1]
  • The activation-side memory wall now has a first source — and its error is directionally structured too. KIVI quantizes the KV cache to 2 bits tuning-free by matching granularity to structure — keys per-channel (outliers concentrate in a few channels), values per-token — near-lossless with 2.6× less peak memory, up to 4× batch, 2.35–3.47× throughput. Same lesson as weights: error concentrates and the distribution says where. This is orthogonal to weight quantization (shrinks activations, not params) and composes with PagedAttention's placement fix on the same wall (local-inference) — precision and paging stack. [T1]

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-07-27 — a complementary ordering from the adaptation side, via adalora-adaptive-budget-allocation (T1, primary map peft-methods). This map gained its first between-tensor rule on 2026-07-26 — protect the parameters every token passes through, since 2-bit attention/embeddings/gates (3.4% of Mixtral's parameters) cost 11.5 MMLU points while 2-bit experts (96.6%, all feed-forward) cost 3.85. A controlled sweep from the PEFT literature runs the ordering the other way for a different resource: with the trainable budget pinned at 0.28M on DeBERTaV3-base, adapters on the feed-forward matrices score 89.99 on MNLI while adapters on the query projection score 88.58, and upper layers beat lower ones by 10.7 points at identical budget. These are not in conflict, and stating why is the useful part: the shared path is where error compounds through the residual stream, and the FFN is where task-specific capacity lives. The pair yields a rule cleaner than either finding alone — spend precision on the path everything traverses, spend adaptation on the part that specializes — which is also the first in-vault suggestion that a mixed-precision layout and an adapter layout should be designed together rather than chosen independently. Bounded hard: one comparison is Mixtral at ~2.6 bits/param, the other a 183M encoder on GLUE, and nothing tests the combination. See where-the-adapter-budget-goes-matters-more-than-how-much-there-is.
  • 2026-07-26 — structural edge from mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts): a mixed-precision result this map can use without adopting the MoE framing. The paper sweeps attention precision against expert precision on Mixtral-8×7B in a 16-cell table, and read across both axes it is the sharpest sensitivity asymmetry in-vault: 2-bit experts (96.6% of parameters) cost 3.85 MMLU points; 2-bit attention/embeddings/gates (3.4%) cost 11.5. Where AlphaQ arrived at non-uniform allocation from spectral theory and an integer program a day earlier, this arrives by brute force with no calibration data and no optimizer, which makes the coarse rule usable by anyone: spend precision on the shared path and compress what routes. Practically it also explains why the two chosen production configurations both hold attention at 4-bit — 4-bit attention with 3-bit experts is 21.37 GB at 68.47% MMLU against FP16's 70.51%, and with 2-bit experts 17.54 GB at 65.58%. Two qualifiers this map should carry: HQQ is data-free, so this table sidesteps rather than resolves the calibration confound landed 2026-07-25; and the quality evidence is perplexity plus MMLU at an effective 2.6 bits/param, well below the rung where local-inference measured damage to be task-shaped, with no reasoning benchmark run — so the asymmetry is well supported and the absolute usability of the 2-bit configuration is not. See shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts.
  • 2026-07-25 — ingest of alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation (Yang, Ma, Conzelmann, Zheng, Mahoney, Rusch, Liu — MPI-IS / ELLIS Tübingen / Xiamen / ICSI / LBNL / Berkeley / Liquid AI, arXiv 2026-06, T1; primary map mixture-of-experts): lands two things, and the smaller headline is the bigger result for this map. The stated contribution is mixed-precision allocation for MoE — rank layers by the power-law tail exponent of their weight spectra (HT-SR theory, no calibration data at all), solve a global bit budget as an integer program, and reach BF16-level accuracy on Qwen1.5-MoE at an average 3.5 bits, >4× compression. That extends this map's central claim in a specific direction: on an MoE the average bit-width can fall below 4 because the layers are not equally worth their bits, and the exploitable variance is larger on fine-grained architectures. The motivating experiment is what this map should absorb hardest, because it measures something the map has been asserting on one vendor's authority for nine days — calibration-set overfitting, at a 2.1× swing in HumanEval from the corpus choice alone. That belongs beside perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats as a second instance of the same discipline: the number you report depends on a knob you did not report, and perplexity would not have caught it either (WikiText2 barely moves across the calibration variants while the task scores swing by double digits). It is also a candidate fifth entry in evaluation-contamination's failure taxonomy, distinct from the four there because it is a property of model preparation rather than of the evaluation — flagged, not adopted, on one paper. Caveats: weight-only with no latency, throughput or energy measured anywhere; the five evaluation benchmarks are commonsense zero-shot tasks, which by this map's own 2026-07-24 finding are the family least sensitive to quantization damage, while GSM8K and MMLU appear only in the motivating figure; several method hyperparameters tuned by prior heuristics on a method sold as assumption-free; no head-to-head against AWQ; venue-less June 2026 preprint. See quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model, moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity.
  • 2026-07-24 — translation edge from llama-cpp-quantization-unified-evaluation (T1, primary map local-inference): this map's headline survives the trip into the format people actually download. Every 4-bit claim here comes from AWQ, GPTQ or QLoRA — GPU methods with bespoke kernels and their own calibration procedures. llama.cpp's K-quants are a different construction (mixed per-tensor bit allocation, block scales, no calibration set), and nobody in-vault had checked they land in the same place. They do: Q4_K_M is 0.32 aggregate points off FP16 at 69.4% smaller, and 5-, 6- and 8-bit are indistinguishable from full precision across five task benchmarks. Two refinements this map should absorb. (a) The 3-bit boundary is a slope, not a cliff, and it is task-shaped: at Q3_K_S, GSM8K drops 9.3 points and MMLU 4.2 while HellaSwag drops 0.6 — so "error is concentrated" is true across tasks as well as across channels, and the concentration lands on multi-step reasoning. Read with quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor, the two agree from opposite directions that the cliff is not where bit-counting puts it. (b) Every speedup claim in this map is a decode claim. Prefill is compute-bound and moves the other way — peaking mid-ladder at Q4_0 (97.35 tok/s vs F16's 79.57) then falling to 28% below full precision at Q3_K_S as dequantization outruns the smaller read. The map's throughput stamp (kernel, card, precision) needs a fourth field: which phase. Provenance caveat: single-author preprint, one 8B model, CPU-only. See the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff, quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill.
  • 2026-07-22 — push-back edge from loftq-quantization-aware-lora-init (T1, primary map peft-methods): part of the sub-3-bit cliff belonged to the fine-tuning method, not to the weights. This map's article is titled "4-bit is the working default," and one plank of that default is that quality collapses below 4 bits. LoftQ shows the collapse is initialization- dependent in the quantize-then-fine-tune setting: at 2.5-bit on LLaMA-2-13B the same base gives 19.39 WikiText-2 ppl under QLoRA's zero-initialized adapter and 5.22 under a jointly-solved one, and at 2.25-/2-bit QLoRA fails to converge while LoftQ reaches 7.69 ppl. Scope the correction carefully — it is about fine-tuning a quantized base, not about post-training quantization of a model you will merely serve, and LoftQ's own 2-bit numbers still degrade sharply (7.85 vs 5.24 ppl at 4-bit), so the 4-bit default holds. What changes is that the vault should stop citing "QLoRA fails below 3-bit" as evidence about bit-widths. Together with QPiSSA this is now a replicated claim from two groups: the quantization error is not a fixed tax on the base — it depends on which slice of the weight you keep in full precision. See quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor, quantizing-only-the-residual-shrinks-qlora-error.
  • 2026-07-21 — ingest of smoothquant-activation-quantization (Xiao et al., MIT Han Lab + NVIDIA, ICML 2023, T1): the map's first W8A8 / activation-quantization source, opening the lane flagged as its sharpest gap. Confirms from the activation side what AWQ found on the weight side — error concentrates in a small set of channels — and adds the general principle that this difficulty is transferable: divide the activation channel, multiply the weight row, output unchanged. Ties directly to the 2026-07-20 FA3 edge (both quantize activations by neutralizing outlier channels; FA3 does it transiently in-kernel with a random rotation, SmoothQuant offline with a per-channel scale) — the enemy is outlier concentration, not the weight/activation distinction. Precision caveat: 8-bit, not the map's 4-bit territory. See activation-outliers-are-the-barrier-to-w8a8, quantization-difficulty-is-transferable-between-weights-and-activations.
  • 2026-07-20 — interaction edge from flashattention-3-asynchrony-low-precision (T1, primary map transformer-architecture): precision is migrating from a storage decision into the kernel. Every method in this map quantizes weights, once, ahead of time. FA3 quantizes activations, transiently, inside the attention kernel at runtime — and to do it, reaches for the same trick this map's weight literature uses: incoherent processing, multiplying by a random orthogonal matrix to spread outliers before quantizing, worth 2.6× lower numerical error than baseline FP8 attention. That the identical outlier remedy shows up on both sides is the useful signal: the enemy is outlier concentration, not the weight/activation distinction, which is the general form of this map's salient-channel finding (salient-channels-carry-quantization-error). Opens a lane this map has flagged as absent — activation quantization — from an unexpected direction. See kernel-performance-expires-with-the-gpu-generation.
  • 2026-07-18 — interaction edge from gqa-grouped-query-attention (T1, primary map transformer-architecture): an advisory on this map's KV numbers — GQA shrinks the KV cache architecturally by storing far fewer key/value heads, and it is standard in every modern open-weights model. KIVI's 2.6×-peak-memory and 4×-batch figures are measured against an un-GQA'd baseline, so they are an upper bound on what KV quantization adds to a model practitioners would actually quantize. The levers compose (fewer heads × fewer bits × less waste) but they are not additive, and no in-vault source measures the stack. See sharing-kv-heads-buys-decode-speed-at-near-zero-quality-cost.
  • 2026-07-17 — ingest of kivi-2bit-kv-cache-quantization (Liu et al., Rice/UIUC/CMU/Stevens/JHU, ICML 2024, T1): the KV-cache/activation half the seed run flagged missing — 2-bit KV, keys-per-channel/values-per-token asymmetry; interaction edge with local-inference (paging vs precision, orthogonal levers that stack). See kv-cache-error-is-directionally-structured.
  • 2026-07-16 — ingest of gptq-post-training-quantization (Frantar et al., IST Austria/ETH, ICLR 2023, T1): the loudest absence resolved — the reconstruction family lands; 2-bit/ternary reported viable in restricted settings (first sub-4-bit datum). See second-order-reconstruction-quantizes-at-scale.

Open questions:

  • AWQ vs GPTQ head-to-head: both families are in-vault, each evaluated by its own authors (AWQ's overfitting critique of reconstruction included). A neutral comparison — calibration sensitivity, domain transfer, modern architectures, and where newer methods beat both — is the map's sharpest remaining gap, and it got sharper and better-specified on 2026-07-25. The critique's premise is now measured — calibration corpus choice swings a task benchmark 2.1× — but it was measured on MoE bit allocation with GPTQ underneath, so it establishes that the axis matters without ranking the two families on it. The experiment that would settle nine days of this: one dense model, one bit-width, AWQ vs GPTQ, three calibration corpora, evaluated on GSM8K and MMLU rather than commonsense tasks. Everything needed to run it is public.
  • How much of this map's evidence base is confounded by an unreported knob (new 2026-07-25). If calibration corpus moves task scores by double digits, then every published GPTQ-family number in this map — including its own 4-bit headline evidence — was produced with a specific corpus that was usually WikiText2 or C4 and rarely stated as a condition. Nobody has re-run the map's foundational comparisons under matched calibration, and until someone does, the cross-paper 4-bit agreement this map rests on is slightly weaker than it reads.
  • Below 4 bits: GPTQ reports usable 2-bit/ternary in restricted settings; the GGUF IQ-series lives there (local-inference carries the format menu) — where quality actually falls off a cliff needs graded head-to-head evidence.
  • Weight-only is no longer the whole story: KIVI lands the first KV-cache source (2-bit KV), and SmoothQuant (2026-07-21) lands the first activation-weight (W8A8) source — closing the "activation quantization for the compute-bound regime" half of this gap. Still open — where 2-bit KV breaks vs the 4-bit setting others use; whether SmoothQuant's smoothing survives below 8 bits (it is an 8-bit method, and the map's other claims live at 4); and the combined paging+precision+GQA measurement (SmoothQuant's α tuning, KIVI's KV bits, PagedAttention's placement, GQA's head count are four levers on one budget that no in-vault source measures together). vLLM's PagedAttention attacks the same wall from the placement side (local-inference).
  • How merging a LoRA adapter changes the weight distribution a quantizer then sees — the merge-then-quantize vs quantize-then-adapt ordering question — is live for the local-deploy pipeline and unmeasured here. Sharpened 2026-07-18: if LoRA inserts high-ranking singular vectors orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting), then a merged adapter hands the quantizer a spectrally different matrix than the base — and every method in this map calibrates against distributional assumptions (NF4's normality premise, AWQ's salience channels, GPTQ's second-order reconstruction). Whether merged-adapter weights quantize as cleanly as base weights is now a specific, testable question rather than a vague one.
  • KV-cache claims need a GQA-adjusted restatement. See the 2026-07-18 through-line entry: this map's activation-side numbers were measured pre-GQA and overstate the marginal gain on current models.
