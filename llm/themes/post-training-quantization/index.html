Status note (2026-09-02): holds consolidating, and the allocation strand gets a second, independent argument for the same direction — from dense models instead of MoE experts. quantization-damage-should-be-spent-globally (T1, 9 models across 4 architecture families) localizes where post-training quantization damage concentrates — task circuits, compute location, weight statistics — and finds it diffuse rather than concentrated in identifiable layers. Given that, spending a marginal bit on finer global granularity beats locally repairing the most-recoverable layers, for 8 of 9 group-128-compatible models, by 21–52 points. This is not a replication of mixture-of-experts's expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer (GEMQ) — different substrate (dense vs. MoE experts), different lever (granularity vs. per-unit bit-width) — but it is the same directional conclusion (treat the model as one allocation problem, not a set of locally-repairable units) arriving independently, which is recorded here as convergent evidence rather than asserted as settled: the mechanisms differ enough that this strengthens rather than duplicates the claim. Status does not move — this adds a second line of support to an allocation debate already argued from the MoE side, it does not resolve a contradiction. Bounded: no code/weights indicated, one of nine models excluded from the headline comparison for group-128 incompatibility, no wall-clock/throughput cost reported for finer global granularity (which carries real scale/zero-point metadata overhead). Status note (2026-08-27): holds consolidating, and the map's most-repeated technique finally gets a reason rather than a track record — rotation is a workaround for a feature the kernel deleted. transform-quantization-great-inversion-survey (T1, single author; formal results plus 200 works indexed to a June 2026 cutoff, 43 transform methods classified; no code, no new experiments) is the first arrival to explain the shelf this map has accumulated since the seed ingest — rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free, rotation-recovers-the-code-loss-of-four-bit-weights, a-rotations-value-is-its-mixing-support-not-its-structure, an-exactly-optimal-rotation-can-be-worse-than-no-rotation — none of which could say why rotation is the right kind of operation. The answer is that classical transform coding (1963: decorrelate, allocate bits, quantize) optimizes for concentration because it can spend different bits per coordinate, while a deployed matrix instruction carries one absolute-maximum scale per group and equal bits everywhere with no allocation at all, which makes the group peak the cost and flattening the optimum — every doubling of crest factor costing 6 dB whatever the width, and Hadamard incoherence taking crest factor from O(√d) to O(√log d). The two prescriptions are proved strictly opposed under within-group majorization (Thm 4.3), so each is optimal against its own surrogate and neither is evidence for the other. The consequence this map should carry: rotation is a workaround for a missing feature, and the entire allocation strand is an attempt to recover by construction what fixed-rate hardware removed from the format (rotation-is-right-because-the-kernel-cannot-allocate-bits). It also retires two of the map's own oddities — mixing-support-not-structure is exactly what a flattening objective predicts, and an exactly-optimal rotation underperforming is unsurprising once the target is the group max rather than the spectrum — and bounds the learned-rotation family with one line: no rotation can supply whitening beyond what the covariance sees. The second axis is the decision-relevant one. Transform benefit is a joint function of allocation regime and number format: strongest on uniform INT4; weaker on MXFP4, where a block-confined rotation helps while a global one fails (recorded and explicitly unexplained); largely absent on NVFP4, whose mantissa-carrying block scale removes the pull; and on a floating-point grid the prescription can reverse, a rotation costing by destroying a favourable weight–activation anti-alignment. The deployment guide is blunter than the literature's tone: for W4A16 — weight-only, memory-bound, the entire local-inference default — a transform is optional, and diagonal scaling or none is what shipped models use; it is essential in W4A4, a compute-bound prefill/batch regime. So a substantial fraction of the rotation literature answers a question a single-card practitioner does not have, which is local-inference's standing complaint arriving on the quantization side (whether-a-rotation-helps-depends-on-the-number-format-not-the-bit-width). Status does not move, and it should not: there are no new measurements here. The inversion is proved against surrogate objectives (geometric mean, group max) rather than the extreme-value cost a model actually pays, the author states it "is not on the published record," and it is illustrated at single-layer scale only. The Bennett high-resolution model underpinning it is called by its own author "merely good at four bits" — the width everything here targets. The dropped cross-product term at aggressive W4A4 is unquantified. Single author, no code, literature frozen at June 2026. A strong organizing claim on a thin empirical floor.

Status note (2026-08-26): holds consolidating, and the map gets the level-up experiment it has never run — what happens if you spend the compression budget somewhere other than the bits. compression-trinity-joint-sparsity-quant-lowrank (T1, University of Toronto doctoral thesis whose chapters are separately published papers, SLiM at ICML 2025) frames every question this map argues — per channel, per layer, per expert, per rounding decision — as allocation inside one pillar, and then holds the compression ratio fixed to compare across pillars. At a matched 8× on LLaMA-2-7B over eight zero-shot tasks, both single-pillar routes collapse to chance: 2-bit quantization 31.81%, 87.5% unstructured sparsity 31.24%, against a dense 54.61%. The hybrid at the identical ratio recovers 52.38% (4-bit + 50% unstructured), within 2.2 points of dense. Nothing about the footprint distinguishes those rows; the compression ratio does not predict the damage, and how it was spent does. For a map whose sub-3-bit strand is its least reliable territory, that is an escape route rather than a deeper entry: staying at 4 bits and taking the rest out of structure beats going to 2 bits by more than twenty points, with no new format and no new kernel. The framing table beside it prices the other direction and confirms an existing result from an unrelated lineage — 2:4 sparsity at 2× costs 5.99 points where 4-bit at 4× costs 0.98, so the pillar that compresses least damages most (see structured-pruning). And the thesis's culmination contributes a mechanism this map's repair notes lack: choose a saliency function that is invertible and additive and the optimal low-rank correction to joint sparsity-plus-quantization error becomes analytically computable in one shot, removing the retraining step the QLoRA-derived repair line depends on — up to +5.66% over state-of-the-art at 2:4 + 4-bit, and 3.78× layer-wise on an RTX 3060. Status does not move, and the qualifications are the usual ones for a thesis: the two framing tables are one model, one metric family, no seeds and no confidence intervals, every row is a best-of-a-selected-field, the "beats dense at equal parameter budgets" headline compares a compressed large model against a dense small one, the speedups are layer-wise rather than end-to-end (see a-memory-saving-and-a-decode-speedup-are-different-quantities, ingested the same night from an unrelated group), and the 8× accounting treats a removed weight and a dropped bit as interchangeable currency — true for weight memory, false for compute on any runtime without a sparse path, which is most consumer runtimes. The author's own future work names the hole: SLiM's pipeline is sequential, and a joint quantized-sparse-plus-low-rank formulation is unsolved. Status note (2026-08-08): holds consolidating, and the map gets its first entry that changes nothing about allocation and everything about what an allocation costs to compute. bakron-kronecker-factored-hessian-quantization (T1, UC San Diego) is a solver, not an objective. GPTQ's curvature model is one-sided — H ≈ E[xxᵀ] ⊗ I_m, read off input activations, output coordinates assumed independent — and second-order-reconstruction-quantizes-at-scale made it this map's default because it was cheap enough to run at 175B, not because it was the most faithful. Two-sided Kronecker-factored approximations (BoA, YAQA) additionally capture cross-output correlations and cost O(m²n²), quartic where GPTQ is cubic, which is why nobody ships them. Anti-diagonal parallelism (entries on one anti-diagonal carry no mutual dependency, so sequential depth falls from O(mn) to O(m+n)) plus a recursive divide-and-conquer construction brings total work to O(mn(m+n)) — cubic — measured at 60× on an 8192×8192 matrix and 45.6× on 14336×4096, on a single RTX PRO 6000. Separately, and this is the part a single-card reader should read twice, the Hessian accumulation drops from YAQA's O(ℓ) accelerator- resident storage to O(log ℓ) with activations streamed from host RAM — the authors say plainly that YAQA's requirement was impractical without many GPUs. Status cannot move and the map should not yet recommend anything: the quality comparison that would justify switching — two-sided against one-sided at matched bit-width, at 4 bits and below — is appendix material rather than the paper's claim, there is no code link, and the authors state that at today's calibration sizes the excluded terms (Cholesky, Hessian accumulation, calibration propagation) dominate end-to-end wall-clock, so the 60× speeds up what is not currently the bottleneck at 8B. The correct entry is the barrier moved and nobody has walked through it. Status note (2026-08-07): holds consolidating, and the map finds a gigabyte it never allocated. This map's four partitionings of a bit budget all assume the tensor under discussion is being quantized. archead-lm-head-residual-compression (T1, aiXplain, code released) opens the checkpoints and shows that in practice one large tensor is not: under bitsandbytes NF4 and under AWQ alike, lm_head remains a torch.nn.Linear holding BF16 weights of shape [151936, 4096] — 1,188 MB on Qwen3-8B-Base, 1,280 MB on Gemma-4-E4B, and after 4-bit block quantization one of the largest dense tensors left in the file. Vocabularies are growing while hidden sizes are not, so the head's share of a quantized model is rising. A head-specific compressor recovers 3.7–3.9× of it at relative perplexity 1.007, where storage-matched naive INT4 reads 1.151, composing with AWQ and NF4 blocks for +0.006/+0.007 cross-entropy at under 2% throughput change. Status does not move — this is a new place to spend the budget, not a revision of what four bits costs. The more durable half is the mechanism: an LM-head error is followed by no normalization, no residual and no further learned transformation, so the correction is fitted in an activation-derived metric rather than in weight space, buying top-1 agreement 76.95% → 93.05% and logit MSE 107,599 → 7,167 at matched storage. That is the third unrelated literature in this vault to conclude the loss should be measured where the consumer stands. Bounded: venue-less preprint, one hardware platform, three downstream tasks the authors call a sanity check, no calibration-corpus swap, and on Gemma-4-E4B naive INT4 is already inside the reporting precision. Status note (2026-08-06): holds consolidating, and the calibration confound this map has recorded twice gets a third measurement, an order of magnitude larger — and, for the first time, a boundary. scaleq-158-reasoning-aware-ternary-calibration (T1, Intel Labs China, nine models from 1.7B to 235B across dense and MoE) fixes the model, the quantizer, the bit budget and the token count and changes only what the calibration text contains. Qwen3-4B at W1.58A16: generic web text gives Math-500 0.00 and HumanEval+ 0.00; domain-relevant question-answer pairs give 24.20; the same questions with the target model's own chain-of-thought traces give 58.40 / 53.98. The step that matters is not topical relevance — it is whether the calibration text contains the multi-step derivation the model produces at inference, which is exactly the capability quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation says precision loss attacks first. Two further results carry. Self-generated beats stronger-teacher-generated by 25.54 points of mean accuracy (45.60% against traces from DeepSeek-R1-671B at 20.06%), which is distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank arrived at in an unrelated mechanism by a group that does not cite it. And the effect is bit-width-indexed: the same intervention under a second quantizer is worth +2.2 points at W4A16, +58.8 at W2A16 and +58.4 at ternary — so this map's four-bit default is comparatively robust to the confound, and recipe leakage is a low-bit phenomenon that grows as the format tightens. Status does not move: this is a third leg under an already-recorded confound plus a scope condition, not a rebuttal. Status note (2026-08-02): holds consolidating, and the damage model gains a third axis on the same week it gained its second — with the caveat that the source cannot cleanly attribute it to four bits. Last night the map recorded that damage is signal-shaped as well as task-shaped. Tonight silent-failures-quantized-reasoning (T1, 30,000 judged chain-of-thought outputs, taxonomy validated at Cohen's κ = 0.906) reports it is also process-shaped: accuracy moves at most 3.1 points anywhere in a 5-model × 3-precision grid while the composition of wrong answers shifts sharply on the smallest models — LLaMA 3.2-3B under NF4 goes from 44% to 78% Shortcut Collapse and from 15.8% to 0.2% Confidence Snowballing, patterns that produce a wrong answer at similar rates so accuracy sees nothing. Models at 12B and above are statistically invariant, which is the same concentrate-at-the-small-end direction as at-a-fixed-footprint-take-the-larger-quantized-model. The discount is large and this map should apply it plainly: the biggest single distributional move in the paper is FP32 → FP16 (LLaMA 3.1-8B, 29.9 → 13.8, with NF4 adding 0.2 more), so the defensible claim is about precision reduction in general rather than about the four-bit floor this map is organized around, and the paper's own conclusion contradicts its tables on the direction of the effect. Separately, gemq-global-expert-mixed-precision-moe (T1, ICML 2026) lands two things here: the first throughput measurement for mixed-precision expert quantization — 1.4–1.6× batch-1 decode, Mixtral-8×7B at 12.0 GB / 88.6 tok/s — which closes the footprint-only bound this map has carried since 2026-07-25; and a second, independent measurement of the calibration confound, GSM8K moving 31.77 → 42.30 on a corpus swap alone, with perplexity barely moving under the same intervention. Status note (2026-08-01): holds consolidating, and the map gains a second axis of damage rather than a challenge to the first. Everything this map has measured about what quantization costs is an accuracy measurement, and the resulting rule — damage is task-shaped, load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and instruction compliance, near-free for single-step commonsense — survives untouched. vlm-quantization-uncertainty-signals (T1) measures a different construct on identical inputs and finds the damage is also signal-shaped: 4-bit NF4 on a 2B VLM costs 1.6 accuracy points and 15 points of error-detection AUROC, with the model's ability to emit a parseable stated confidence collapsing from 99% to 64%. The status does not move because nothing here contradicts an existing graded claim — it names a dimension no source in this map had ever evaluated. Status note (2026-08-13): holds consolidating, and the map's fourth calibration finding is the first one that is not about topic — it is about language, and it is a scope condition on everything else here. language-conditional-dequantization (T1, two 3B families, eight non-English languages, anonymized code) quantizes with GPTQ W3A16 calibrated on 128 English C4 samples — which is the field's unstated default — and measures what that costs elsewhere: English perplexity degrades 1.35×, Arabic 4.37×, Korean 3.49×, Japanese 3.04×, with the penalty ordered by script distance and holding across two tokenizers. That is exactly what this map's newest belief predicts (S and N are activation-space, and a language is a workload) from a group that does not cite it, and it means every quantization number in this vault is an English number whose transfer is untested rather than assumed. The repair is cheap and belongs to adapter-objects-and-serving — per-language rank-2 corrections at 0.12% of parameters and 20 GPU- minutes recover 70–83% of the non-Latin perplexity gap and 17–28% of the GlobalMMLU gap. The finding this map should carry hardest is an accident of the author's own ablation: on Llama, the placement that wins perplexity recovery by 43 points loses on accuracy recovery (9.5% against uniform's 17%), which converts the map's standing "is reconstruction error the wrong objective?" question from a suspicion into an observed inversion. Status does not move — single author, one quantizer at one bit-width, 3B only, and the benefit collapses to 8.5% at 7B. Two edges land the same night. Per-role bit allocation (rotaryquant-fused-compressed-space-attention, T1) adds a third allocation unit — 4-bit dense, 2-bit routed expert, 8-bit shared expert on a kurtosis gap of 10.1 against 0.41 — and it is the first one coarse enough for the local stack to express. And route-flip-damage-quantized-moe (T1, pre-registered pilot) supplies a second negative repair sweep from an unrelated mechanism: about a third of the damage from a 4-bit cache travels through changed MoE routing, flips are detectable at AUC 0.772, their sign is not learnable from any router statistic tested, and an oracle-gated repair beats a rate-matched random one by +0.4% [−1.8, +2.8].

Status note (2026-08-19): holds consolidating, and the downgrade question was asked. schurquant-groupwise-discrete-ptq (T1) pushes back on a claim this map has been treating as settled — that compression damage falls with model size — with the cross-family matched comparison the claim never had, and two of three families run the other way. Three reasons the map does not move to contested. The pushback is suggestive, not strong: the confound it implicates (training-token density) is untested, the instrument is the multiple-choice family least sensitive to compression damage, and a floor correction is required before the families separate at all. It contradicts a secondary claim about where on the scale ladder to spend compression, not the map's central one — the 4-bit default is untouched, and at 4 bits this same source's own margin over GPTQ is +0.68 points. And it narrows rather than reverses: the amplification law survives as a within-family statement and the practitioner prescription attached to ultra-low-bit-is-a-large-model-technique is unchanged. What has changed is that model size is no longer usable as a predictor of a 2-bit run's cost on a modern checkpoint, which is recorded in "What we believe now" rather than in the status.

Status note (2026-08-20): holds consolidating, and the map's damage catalogue gets its sixth axis — the first one measured with plain accuracy, on the task the model was deployed for. compress-and-forget-quantized-proactive-interference (T1, 23,075 trial-level-paired trials, code released) holds a retrieval task fixed and varies only bitsandbytes precision. Pooled over all conditions, FP16 → INT4 costs 1.1–2.4 points; at each model's high-interference operating point it costs 7–13 (Qwen 81.0% → 68.3% at 64 prior overwrites, McNemar OR 20.00, p = 4.1 × 10⁻¹⁰). Every previous entry in this map's damage catalogue needed a different instrument — the derivation, the confidence signal, the agency, the trustworthiness suite. This one needed only a better-chosen operating point, which makes it the cheapest of the six to act on and the most damning of aggregate reporting. The specificity is what turns it into a mechanism: on a numeric control the same interaction term reverses sign (β = +0.053 against word-type's −0.046), and 99.8–99.9% of all errors at every precision are same-key intrusions whose rate rises 21.5% → 24.6% while their recency does not move — the model is not getting noisier, it is getting worse at separating two near neighbours, which is what quantization-multiplies-the-decision-margin predicted and nothing had yet observed. Status does not move, and the reason is the same one applied to SchurQuant the night before: this is bitsandbytes NF4 and LLM.int8() only, on three models at 3.8–7B, greedy, weight-only, with prompts of a few hundred tokens — GPTQ, AWQ and the GGUF K-quants the local stack actually ships are untested, and the authors cite independent work already singling out NF4 as among the worst 4-bit schemes for retrieval-heavy work, which both corroborates the direction and narrows the scope. One recorded belief does move, and it is a default: INT8 is not the free step this map called it (eight-bit-is-not-a-free-precision-step) — the trial-matched paired test finds a real penalty in two of three models (OR 1.84 and 2.50) that the unpaired test on the identical cells cannot see. The ordering survives intact (INT4's odds ratios run 2.58–20.00), so this is a scope condition on a rung, not a reversal of the ladder. And the same paper independently reproduces the-lm-head-is-the-tensor-four-bit-quantization-skips by checkpoint inspection in a second lab, then adds the behavioural half: forcing lm_head to four bits on top of the backbone costs nothing detectable (all p = 1.0, ≤2.5% of the gap) — evidence for compressing the head, from a paper not trying to give it. A second edge lands the same night from flashattention-v-scalable-vector-architectures (T1, primary map local-inference) and it is the first time this map has convicted a format of something other than accuracy-per-bit: Q8_0's interleaved scale-and-weights layout structurally prevents long-vector execution, costing 28% of cycles in packing alone and 60% in packing plus masked reduction at 2048-bit vector length, reproduced on Arm SVE as well as RISC-V (the-quantized-weight-layout-caps-usable-vector-width).

Status note (2026-08-22): holds consolidating, and the map's 4-bit default is asked a question no metric in this vault has ever put to it — not "how many points does it cost" but "how many of the same answers come back." asymmetric-harms-llm-compression (T1, Rice + UNC + Rutgers; 11 compression methods across quantization and pruning, three 8–9B instruction-tuned models, one shared protocol, isotonic-calibrated confidence on a held-out split) measures a knowledge-loss rate — of the questions the full-precision model answered correctly, how many are now wrong. At 4-bit GPTQ, 30% WANDA and 5% layer dropping — the settings this map calls nearly free — it is 22% to 35%, offset by newly-correct answers so the aggregate barely moves. Nothing this map has recorded is refuted: the score really is preserved. What is newly bounded is what that licenses. "Retains the quality that matters" is a claim about the score, not about the answer set, and the answer set is substantially replaced. For a benchmark mean, or wherever the specific facts are fungible, quantize as before. Wherever which facts survive is the point — a domain assistant on a known question set, a retrieval-free lookup, an item-by-item comparison against a full-precision run — 22–35% churn at the recommended setting is a different proposition, and no instrument this map carries would have shown it. Two further results land on the damage-measurement strand. The received claim that compression preferentially erases rare knowledge is backwards on the relative axis: normalized to each group's own full-precision accuracy, tail retention runs 22.7–39.5 points above the overall rate and head retention 3.6–14.4 below, most extremely under the same semi-structured N:M patterns this map already flagged as the expensive lever — while the absolute ordering is unchanged, so this corrects the distribution of damage rather than declaring rare facts safe. And the model stays confident about what it broke: median calibrated confidence 0.4–0.6 on now-wrong answers, highest on the popular facts users actually ask about. Tonight's second arrival cuts the other way and keeps the map where it is — int8-bert-server-cpu-pytorch-native (T2-grade engineering at T1 rigour, Intel, upstreamed to PyTorch/TorchAO) shows INT8 costing under 1% and usually 0.0% on task accuracy while delivering 2.2–5.8× over FP32. Status holds consolidating: two strong sources, one deepening the damage catalogue and one confirming the method, with the pushback aimed at the instrument rather than at the claim. Status note (2026-08-25): holds consolidating, and the map's most-quoted unit — bits per weight — is shown to be a scope statement rather than a size statement, in a source that reports it honestly enough to be the example rather than the warning. awsrc-seeded-residual-repair-low-bit (T1, Huawei Boole RC; byte-audited, five model families, three-seed repeats, a load-from-disk round trip with hash equality) attaches a detachable sidecar to a finished quantized checkpoint and codes its residual W − W₀ with a basis regenerated from a seed rather than stored, fitted under an activation-weighted objective and allocated by error reduction per serialized byte including index and padding. At +0.162 scope-bits/weight — a 49.25 MB sidecar, ~0.8% of the BF16 weight payload — it closes 88.2% / 78.9% / 71.3% of the perplexity, KL and accuracy gaps between an INT4 round-to-nearest backbone and BF16, and at byte-identical sidecar size beats sparse, low-rank and learned vector-quantized correction on perplexity and mean accuracy. Three things outrank the headline for this map. The advertised bit-width is scoped: the same artifact is 4.23 bpw over the 108 MLP projections it repairs and 6.71 bpw model-wide, because attention, embeddings, norms and the head stayed BF16 — a 2.48-bit gap, printed in adjacent columns on every row, and the second independent instance after the-lm-head-is-the-tensor-four-bit-quantization-skips of a "4-bit" checkpoint whose file is nothing of the kind. Repair is a property of the parent, not the codec: it rescues round-to-nearest and a clean-room QAM-W and does approximately nothing for official GPTQ or AWQ checkpoints (AWQ "negligible despite a larger sidecar"), which makes a post-hoc corrector and a calibrated quantizer substitutes competing for the same activation-visible error rather than complements. And omitting the codebook is worth real bytes — at 49,245,876 bytes exactly, the records a seeded basis buys instead of a dictionary are what win the four-codec comparison. Status does not move: no code release is stated, the strong-parent arm is a clean-room reimplementation whose absolute numbers differ from the published ones, the repair scope is MLP-only against all-linear references, every number is WikiText-2-calibrated and WikiText-2-measured (this map's four-times-recorded confound), and the paper claims compressed quality, not runtime — all evaluated artifacts are dense BF16 reconstructions with no packed kernel, memory-traffic or load-time measurement.

What we believe now:

  • Quantization is moving from a conversion-time step into something the serving engine does online, at production tensor-parallel scale, and that shift creates a correctness axis this map hasn't had to carry before (new 2026-08-31). vLLM v0.28.0 ships online MXFP4 with weight scales shared across tensor-parallel ranks — sharing prevents ranks from independently computing drifting scales for what should be an identical layer — plus NVFP4 batch-invariant MoE via CUTLASS, meaning the quantized MoE's numerics do not shift with batch composition, a reproducibility property distinct from accuracy that only matters once quantization happens live, under variable concurrent load, rather than once at conversion time. No accuracy numbers reported — purely a systems/correctness capability. [T2] (vllm-ships-online-mxfp4-and-batch-invariant-nvfp4-moe-quantization)

  • The damage has a task term and a scale term, and neither is a property of the weight you quantized (new 2026-08-12, and it is the layer underneath the margin account). Decompose a quantized module's signal-to-noise ratio and it splits three ways: SNR = M + S − N, where M is the weight's magnitude relative to its own quantization error, S is the energy of task-specific activations along the full-precision weight direction, and N is the activation energy along the quantization-error direction. Only M is weight-space. Two modules with identical weight-space error can differ arbitrarily in what they cost, because S and N are computed against activations and activations belong to the workload. This does not collide with the multiplicative margin result even though it looks like it should: the decomposition is additive in log-SNR, which is multiplicative in the ratio — same shape, two levels of description, no shared citation. What it adds is the composition of the surviving fraction c, which turns the map's most annoying practical finding (the constants do not transfer — 18–33pp at 3 bits) from a surprise into a prediction, and makes the paired-margin prescription under-strict: re-measure per model, per bit-width, and per task family. It also explains the repair sweep's negative result — importance scored in weight space moves M alone, while an extra bit moves M everywhere including wherever N is large, which a weight-space scorer cannot see.   • Quantization does not add noise to a decision — it multiplies the margin, so being confident buys no protection (new 2026-08-10, and it is the mechanism this map has wanted for three weeks). Define a two-way decision's margin as the model's score for the option it picks minus the score of its best alternative. Fitted on the same items before and after compression across 16 models from 8 families and three quantization paths — round-to-nearest, GPTQ/AutoRound, and llama.cpp's own released GGUF builds — the relation is m′ = c·m + b + ε with a surviving fraction that collapses: median 0.86 at 4 bits, 0.33 at 3, 0.00 at 2. Against three additive alternatives by BIC the multiplicative account wins 85% of damaged cells and every cell in the tool-calling and safety families (per-cell BIC differences +28.8 to +1031.2); additive never wins where the damage is. It predicts flips it was never fitted on — within a median 1.8 percentage points on 868 held-out cells, calibrated to an expected calibration error of 0.004 over 131,758 per-decision predictions. Three things follow. The ordering is the last thing to go, which is why an argmax-scored benchmark stays quiet while confidence signals, long dependency chains and threshold decisions degrade continuously — the map's three separate observations of silent damage (quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation, four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer, the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff) are one phenomenon. The failure is directional and deterministic: at 3 bits an agent stops calling tools (should-call flips at 0.963 against should-not-call at 0.200, a 4.8× asymmetry) while which tool is untouched, and the same items flip identically under two different quantizers (77 of 77, 16 of 16). And the constants are yours — borrowing another model's fitted c costs 0.2–8.5pp at 4 bits and 18–33pp at 3.   • The cheapest honest test of a quantized model is a few hundred paired decisions, and the cheapest repair is one more bit (new 2026-08-10).   • GPTQ won on affordability rather than fidelity, and the thing it was more affordable than just got cheaper by an order of magnitude — plus a memory term this map has never tracked (new 2026-08-08). Adaptive rounding needs a curvature model. GPTQ uses a one-sided Hessian, H ≈ E[xxᵀ] ⊗ I_m: geometry from the input activations only, every output coordinate treated as independent. Two-sided Kronecker-factored approximations relax that and quantize better, and were priced out at O(m²n²). Two structural facts remove the price. Entries on one anti-diagonal of the weight matrix carry no mutual dependency under the two-sided update, so they round in parallel and sequential depth falls to O(m+n); and a recursive divide-and-conquer — quantize the first half, propagate its induced error into the second, quantize that — brings total work to O(mn(m+n)), the same cubic order as GPTQ. Measured against the best prior arrangement on a single workstation card: 3.7× at 1024², 26.6× at 4096², 60.0× at 8192², 45.6× at 14336×4096. The second finding is the one this map is structurally blind to. Every entry here reports what the quantized model costs and none reports what running the quantizer costs, because the papers assume a machine where that is uninteresting. For the two-sided family it is decisive: Hessian accumulation drops from O(ℓ) accelerator-resident to O(log ℓ) at O(ℓ log ℓ) compute, with activations streamed from host RAM — the same structural move as frozen-weights-can-be-streamed-which-is-a-peft-only-memory-axis and nvme-offload-removes-the-model-scale-ceiling, applied to the quantizer's own working set. The practitioner form is a question to ask of any quantization method before adopting it: what does the quantization pass itself need resident, and for how long? Round-to-nearest: nothing. GPTQ: one layer's Hessian. The two-sided family, until now: more than a consumer machine has. Nothing here is yet a recommendation — the two-sided-versus-one-sided quality comparison at 4 bits and below is in an appendix and is not the paper's claim, no code is linked, and the authors state the excluded terms dominate end-to-end time at current calibration sizes.   • The damage is process-shaped too — the answer survives and the derivation does not — and mixed- precision allocation is now measured to be faster as well as smaller (new 2026-08-02, two independent additions). Process. Across 30,000 chain-of-thought outputs from five instruction-tuned models (3B–14B) at FP32/FP16/NF4 on four reasoning benchmarks, judged into a six-category taxonomy validated against two human annotators at κ = 0.906, accuracy drops at most 3.1 points anywhere — "by any standard evaluation checklist, none of these five models would get flagged."   • A precision budget should also be allocated by what the number is used for, and a quantity consumed as a ranking tolerates far more error than one consumed as a value (new 2026-08-01 — a fourth partitioning axis, and the first that is not about where the weight sits). The map's three existing partitionings are per-layer (AlphaQ), per-tensor-class (shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts) and per-modality (vlm-precision-should-be-allocated-by-modality). This one splits on the consumer. In a two-stage sparse feed-forward network, stage 1's numeric outputs are discarded and only their order survives into a top-k mask: INT4 proxy weights beat FP16 proxies by up to 9.0 points at 70% sparsity, which is not "adequate" but better, read by the authors as mild regularization of the ranking.   • Rotation has become this map's general-purpose preprocessing primitive, and it now works on the KV cache too (new 2026-07-29, generalizing the 2026-07-28 bullet below). The vault has now seen the same operation do the same job in four independent places, and the unifying statement is that a rotation destroys the structure that makes a quantizer's job hard — outliers, channel concentration, coordinate dependence — at essentially no cost, because orthogonal transforms preserve the geometry everything downstream depends on. (i) On weights and activations, deleting outliers outright (QuaRot). (ii) Bolted onto an unchanged 4-bit GPTQ recipe by a third group as a drop-in step: HumanEval 53.05 → 57.93 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct while perplexity moves only 8.73 → 8.55. (iii) On the KV cache, where a random rotation induces a concentrated Beta distribution on the coordinates and makes them near-independent — at which point the optimal vector quantizer collapses into per-coordinate optimal scalar quantizers, which is what makes the method cheap, vectorizable and data-free. (iv) In the smoothing literature it replaced. The practitioner form: if you are quantizing anything below 8 bits, rotate first — it is close to free and it is the step that makes the rest of the machinery work. [T1, four sources, three groups]

  • …but the concentration is a property of the basis, and you can rotate it away (new 2026-07-28, and it reorders the two bullets above). Every outlier remedy in this map until now takes the outlier channels as given and works around them — protect the salient ~1% (AWQ), migrate their difficulty into the paired weight rows (SmoothQuant), or keep them in higher precision outright (QUIK's 256 retained features, Atom's 128). Computational invariance says the coordinate system is arbitrary: in a pre-norm RMSNorm architecture, multiplying an input-side weight matrix on the left by an orthogonal Q and the paired output-side matrix on the right by Qᵀ leaves the function unchanged, because RMSNorm divides by a norm and rotation preserves norms. Choose Q to be a randomized Hadamard and the rotation is also incoherence processing: every coordinate mixes into every other, so a spike in one channel becomes a small contribution to all of them and there are no outliers left to protect. Cost: 1.5 Hadamard transforms per layer at O(d log d), the rest fused into the weights. The evidence that removal beats accommodation is one table read across its rows, at 4-bit weights/activations/KV on LLaMA-2-7B against a 5.47 FP16 baseline — SmoothQuant 83.12 (total collapse; per-channel scaling was engineered for 8 bits and does not extend down), OmniQuant 14.26, QUIK-4B 8.87 while retaining 256 outlier channels, and rotation 6.10 while retaining none. General statement, which subsumes the two bullets above as basis-preserving special cases: the enemy is outlier concentration, and concentration is basis-dependent. This also retro-explains the 2026-07-20 FA3 coincidence — the identical random-rotation trick appearing inside an attention kernel is the same theorem applied transiently instead of fused. Preconditions are architectural, not incidental: pre-norm RMSNorm, and power-of-two head counts and head dimensions for the across-head construction. [T1]

  • A residual codec need not ship its dictionary. Scope the correction carefully — it is about fine-tuning a quantized base, not about post-training quantization of a model you will merely serve, and LoftQ's own 2-bit numbers still degrade sharply (7.85 vs 5.24 ppl at 4-bit), so the 4-bit default holds. What changes is that the vault should stop citing "QLoRA fails below 3-bit" as evidence about bit-widths. Together with QPiSSA this is now a replicated claim from two groups: the quantization error is not a fixed tax on the base — it depends on which slice of the weight you keep in full precision. See quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor, quantizing-only-the-residual-shrinks-qlora-error.

  • 2026-07-21 — ingest of smoothquant-activation-quantization (Xiao et al., MIT Han Lab + NVIDIA, ICML 2023, T1): the map's first W8A8 / activation-quantization source, opening the lane flagged as its sharpest gap. Confirms from the activation side what AWQ found on the weight side — error concentrates in a small set of channels — and adds the general principle that this difficulty is transferable: divide the activation channel, multiply the weight row, output unchanged. Ties directly to the 2026-07-20 FA3 edge (both quantize activations by neutralizing outlier channels; FA3 does it transiently in-kernel with a random rotation, SmoothQuant offline with a per-channel scale) — the enemy is outlier concentration, not the weight/activation distinction. Precision caveat: 8-bit, not the map's 4-bit territory. See activation-outliers-are-the-barrier-to-w8a8, quantization-difficulty-is-transferable-between-weights-and-activations.

  • 2026-07-20 — interaction edge from flashattention-3-asynchrony-low-precision (T1, primary map transformer-architecture): precision is migrating from a storage decision into the kernel. Every method in this map quantizes weights, once, ahead of time. FA3 quantizes activations, transiently, inside the attention kernel at runtime — and to do it, reaches for the same trick this map's weight literature uses: incoherent processing, multiplying by a random orthogonal matrix to spread outliers before quantizing, worth 2.6× lower numerical error than baseline FP8 attention. That the identical outlier remedy shows up on both sides is the useful signal: the enemy is outlier concentration, not the weight/activation distinction, which is the general form of this map's salient-channel finding (salient-channels-carry-quantization-error). Opens a lane this map has flagged as absent — activation quantization — from an unexpected direction. See kernel-performance-expires-with-the-gpu-generation.

  • 2026-07-18 — interaction edge from gqa-grouped-query-attention (T1, primary map transformer-architecture): an advisory on this map's KV numbers — GQA shrinks the KV cache architecturally by storing far fewer key/value heads, and it is standard in every modern open-weights model. KIVI's 2.6×-peak-memory and 4×-batch figures are measured against an un-GQA'd baseline, so they are an upper bound on what KV quantization adds to a model practitioners would actually quantize. The levers compose (fewer heads × fewer bits × less waste) but they are not additive, and no in-vault source measures the stack. See sharing-kv-heads-buys-decode-speed-at-near-zero-quality-cost.

Open questions:

  • What does a second compression cost on top of the first, at four bits? (new 2026-08-16, and it is the most consequential gap this map has ever had, because the whole vault's local stack rests on the assumption it closes.) Every result in this vault is measured with one lever pulled. The only direct composition measurement anywhere — attention-side arithmetic reduction on an INT8 model — cost 4.0 points on a benchmark where the same lever cost a dense model nothing (stacking-reduction-on-a-quantized-model-costs-four-points), which is what quantization-multiplies-the-decision-margin predicts and what nobody has verified at the width this map actually recommends. The run is a 2×2 and it is small: one 8B model, dense and INT4, each with and without a second lever (KV quantization at 4 bits is the obvious choice, since kv-cache-management holds an entire ladder of them), scored on a task with enough items to carry an interval. If the interaction is superadditive at four bits, every "these compose" sentence in this vault needs a caveat; if it is not, the local stack's headroom story is finally measured instead of assumed.
  • Does the rounding gain survive above 3B, and does it survive a generative task? (new 2026-08-16.) The rounding-revision result is positive on eight models and every one of them is under 3B, on four zero-shot multiple-choice tasks. Both restrictions point the same way and both are cheap to lift. Scale matters because small-models-amplify-quantization-error-large-ones-attenuate-it says the small end is where quantization error is worst, so a repair that works there may simply be repairing an amplification larger models do not have — which would make this a small-model technique rather than a method. Task family matters because quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation says precision loss attacks multi-step derivation first, and multiple-choice accuracy is precisely the instrument that misses it. One 7–8B model, the same pipeline, scored on GSM8K and a code benchmark would settle both, and the authors released code.
  • Does the language penalty survive at four bits, and does it exist under AWQ or GGUF? (new 2026-08-13, and it is the cheapest run this map has carried in a week.) The measurement is W3A16 GPTQ only. This map's entire practitioner-facing position is built at four bits, where damage is comparatively robust to the calibration confound (+2.2 points at W4A16 against +58.8 at W2A16 on the content axis) — so the 4-bit default may absorb the language penalty almost entirely, or may not, and the difference decides whether this is a scope condition on the whole map or a footnote about aggressive formats. The run is a matrix somebody could do in a day on one card: one 3B model, English and Arabic evaluation sets, GPTQ and AWQ and a GGUF K-quant, at 4 and 3 bits. Add the obvious second question the paper opens and never asks: does calibrating in the target language do most of what the adapter does, for free?
  • Is the perplexity/accuracy placement inversion a property of one family? (new 2026-08-13.) A repair placement that wins by 43 points on perplexity recovery loses on accuracy recovery — on Llama only; Qwen, whose errors sit late, shows no disagreement. If it replicates on a second early-error family, every perplexity-tuned or reconstruction-tuned allocation in this map is exposed, including its two founding methods. The controlled version is one experiment and it subsumes the older question below: one model, one bit budget, the same allocator fitted under a perplexity objective and under a downstream-accuracy objective, both scored on both.
  • Is weight-space reconstruction error the wrong objective for everything, not just for the head? (new 2026-08-07, and it is the most consequential cheap run this map has ever carried.) Three unrelated literatures now say the loss should be measured in the geometry the consumer reads: the output head (activation-derived metric, top-1 agreement 76.95% → 93.05% at matched storage), cross-model KV transfer (attention-output cosine predicts downstream retention at +0.57 where regression R² predicts it at −0.20), and feed-forward sparsity (INT4 proxies better as a ranking, catastrophic as a value). Both of this map's founding methods minimize a layer-output error in the model's own basis, and expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer separately found per-layer reconstruction objectives are not comparable across layers. The controlled version is one experiment: one model, one bit budget, the same quantizer fitted under a plain reconstruction metric and under an activation-derived one, scored on GSM8K and MMLU rather than on perplexity. Nobody has run it, and every method in this map would move if the answer is yes.
  • What else is not being quantized? (new 2026-08-07; partly answered 2026-08-10.) The LM head was found by loading a checkpoint and looking at the module types, not by reading a paper. Embeddings, the router in an MoE, layer norms and the tied-vs-untied embedding decision are all in the same category — things this map reasons about as parameters and has never audited as bytes on disk in an actual 4-bit artifact. A per-tensor byte census of one popular 4-bit checkpoint from each of bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ and GGUF is an afternoon of work and would say where the remaining footprint actually is. The embedding half now has an answer from an unexpected direction: for a single-language or single-domain deployment most vocabulary rows are unreachable and can be deleted rather than compressed (an-unused-vocabulary-is-deletable-weight), which composes with the head's bit-side compression and has never been tried on a decoder-only model. The router, the norms and the census are still open.
  • Does a 2-bit KV cache preserve decision margins, or only aggregate retrieval accuracy? (new 2026-08-10.) The margin instrument found that taking the cache from 16 to 8 bits is free at 4-bit weights — but kv-cache-management now carries anchor-residual and learned-restoration methods operating at an effective 2 bits and below, all of them scored on RULER-style retrieval and perplexity. Retrieval accuracy is an argmax; a margin is a magnitude, and this map's newest finding is precisely that the two come apart under compression. Running a-paired-margin-set-prices-quantization-damage-without-generation against an aggressive cache compressor is a few GPU-minutes and would tell both maps whether the cache budget and the weight budget damage a model in the same currency.
  • Is the process shift a four-bit effect at all? (new 2026-08-02, and it is cheap.) The one source measuring reasoning-process damage reports its largest single distributional move at FP32 → FP16, a step nobody considers aggressive, with the NF4 step adding almost nothing on four of five models. Either half precision does something to small-model reasoning that nobody has looked for, or the measurement is picking up something other than precision. The disambiguating run is small: repeat the taxonomy classification on one 3B model at FP32, BF16, FP16, INT8 and NF4, which separates dynamic range from mantissa width and puts the four-bit step in a ladder rather than at the end of a two-point line. The authors' own scope note points the second way — GPTQ and AWQ round against calibration data rather than onto a fixed NormalFloat grid, so the same taxonomy under those methods is the other half of the experiment and would also say whether this is an artifact of BitsAndBytes.
  • Does a global expert-level allocation work without calibration data? (new 2026-08-02, shared with mixture-of-experts.) This map now holds two mixed-precision allocators with opposite cost profiles: a calibration-free spectral criterion that allocates per layer, and a calibration-dependent Fisher criterion that allocates per expert globally and wins on perplexity in every cell — while demonstrating, in its own tables, the 10-point calibration swing that makes calibration dependence expensive. The hybrid is unrun and both halves are published.
  • AWQ vs GPTQ head-to-head: both families are in-vault, each evaluated by its own authors (AWQ's overfitting critique of reconstruction included). A neutral comparison — calibration sensitivity, domain transfer, modern architectures, and where newer methods beat both — is the map's sharpest remaining gap, and it got sharper and better-specified on 2026-07-25. The critique's premise is now measured — calibration corpus choice swings a task benchmark 2.1× — but it was measured on MoE bit allocation with GPTQ underneath, so it establishes that the axis matters without ranking the two families on it. The experiment that would settle nine days of this: one dense model, one bit-width, AWQ vs GPTQ, three calibration corpora, evaluated on GSM8K and MMLU rather than commonsense tasks. Everything needed to run it is public.
  • How much of this map's evidence base is confounded by an unreported knob (new 2026-07-25). If calibration corpus moves task scores by double digits, then every published GPTQ-family number in this map — including its own 4-bit headline evidence — was produced with a specific corpus that was usually WikiText2 or C4 and rarely stated as a condition. Nobody has re-run the map's foundational comparisons under matched calibration, and until someone does, the cross-paper 4-bit agreement this map rests on is slightly weaker than it reads.
  • Below 4 bits: GPTQ reports usable 2-bit/ternary in restricted settings; the GGUF IQ-series lives there (local-inference carries the format menu) — where quality actually falls off a cliff needs graded head-to-head evidence.
  • Weight-only is no longer the whole story: KIVI lands the first KV-cache source (2-bit KV), and SmoothQuant (2026-07-21) lands the first activation-weight (W8A8) source — closing the "activation quantization for the compute-bound regime" half of this gap. The below-8-bits question resolved 2026-07-28 (quarot-outlier-free-4bit-rotated-llms) and resolved against the method that raised it: smoothing does not survive the trip, collapsing to perplexity 83 at 4 bits, while rotation reaches W4A4KV4 with no retained outliers. What replaces it is sharper and this vault should treat it as the map's new headline gap: all of the end-to-end 4-bit accuracy evidence is commonsense zero-shot, so the one number that would decide whether a 7B is usable at W4A4 — GSM8K or MMLU, where the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff says quantization damage actually concentrates — does not exist for any activation-quantized model in this vault. A 4.18-point drop on the easy family is a reason to expect worse on the hard one and nothing more. Still open alongside it: where 2-bit KV breaks vs the 4-bit setting others use; whether rotation composes with the mixed-precision allocation results (the rotation source names MoE as future work, and this map now holds two independent MoE bit-allocation findings it could be combined with); and the combined paging + precision + GQA measurement (KIVI's KV bits, PagedAttention's placement, GQA's head count are levers on one budget that no in-vault source measures together). vLLM's PagedAttention attacks the same wall from the placement side (local-inference).
  • Nine ways to train an adapter on a quantized base and zero published head-to-head (new 2026-07-29). This is the AWQ-vs-GPTQ hole one level up and wider: the reference PEFT implementation documents adapter training on bitsandbytes, GPTQ, AQLM, EETQ, HQQ, torchao, Intel Neural Compressor, Transformer Engine and AWQ-by-reference, and does not rank them on quality, memory or speed — nor does anyone else. "Supported" is not a uniform predicate across that list, spanning a maintained default path and a paragraph with a Colab link. The narrower and more decision-relevant version, for a reader who cannot merge: what does an unmerged adapter cost at inference on an aggressively quantized base, which is the price of the AQLM/torchao path and is unmeasured anywhere.
  • KV quantization has two sources and no speed number from either (new 2026-07-29). Both KIVI and the 3.5-bit result are footprint-and-quality only. That gap matters more here than for weight quantization, because a KV quantizer sits on the critical path of every attention call and quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill shows prefill and decode can move in opposite directions — so a compression ratio says nothing about whether the thing is faster. Also unmeasured: whether the 3.5-bit result holds with quantized weights underneath rather than a full-precision model, which is the only configuration anyone would actually deploy.
  • How merging a LoRA adapter changes the weight distribution a quantizer then sees — the merge-then-quantize vs quantize-then-adapt ordering question — is live for the local-deploy pipeline and unmeasured here. Sharpened 2026-07-18: if LoRA inserts high-ranking singular vectors orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting), then a merged adapter hands the quantizer a spectrally different matrix than the base — and every method in this map calibrates against distributional assumptions (NF4's normality premise, AWQ's salience channels, GPTQ's second-order reconstruction). Whether merged-adapter weights quantize as cleanly as base weights is now a specific, testable question rather than a vague one.
  • KV-cache claims need a GQA-adjusted restatement. See the 2026-07-18 through-line entry: this map's activation-side numbers were measured pre-GQA and overstate the marginal gain on current models.
  • "Compression buys speed because decode is memory-bound" is a fact about the kernel, and ultra-low-bit is a large-model technique. A lookup-table kernel that computes products from a table instead of dequantizing to FP16 reports >99.8% GPU busy time with low bandwidth utilization and a speedup that persists at large batch rather than fading — so the batch-size expiry date this map has assumed belongs to dequantize-then-GEMM kernels, not to weight compression as such. On the same evidence, at fixed method / bits / calibration / kernel, the cost of a ~2.75-bit representation runs 7.92 points at 7B, 6.21 at 13B, 4.20 at 70B — a clean within-experiment confirmation of small-models-amplify-quantization-error-large-ones-attenuate-it and a blunt rule: two bits is for making a large model fit, never for making a small one smaller. The 7B configuration spends eight points to land below where this map's four-bit default already sits. Also worth carrying: that method is pure PTQ with 256 calibration samples and still catches the fine-tuning-based two-bit methods, so the recovery pass is not what separates them — and its calibration is non-monotone, peaking at 256 and degrading past 512, which is worse than fragile because a practitioner tuning upward passes silently through the optimum. [T1, single study]
  • The coarse per-role split is cheap and, uniquely on this map, expressible in a format people run. Four bits on a mixture-of-experts model's routed expert projections and full precision on everything a token unconditionally passes through — a partition of tensor names, not layers or experts — costs −0.546 aggregate points across 12,450 paired questions and removes seven-eighths of the resident footprint. It agrees constructively with the sensitivity ablation (shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts) that reached the same allocation by destruction. The damage is concentrated, not spread — null on ARC-Easy and PIQA, −0.71 at p = 0.0027 on the task with the most routing decisions per item — which is a reminder that a retained aggregate hides where the loss lives. [T1, single study]
