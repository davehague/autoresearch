Status note (2026-08-08): holds consolidating, and the map gets its first entry that changes nothing about allocation and everything about what an allocation costs to compute. bakron-kronecker-factored-hessian-quantization (T1, UC San Diego) is a solver, not an objective. GPTQ's curvature model is one-sided — H ≈ E[xxᵀ] ⊗ I_m, read off input activations, output coordinates assumed independent — and second-order-reconstruction-quantizes-at-scale made it this map's default because it was cheap enough to run at 175B, not because it was the most faithful. Two-sided Kronecker-factored approximations (BoA, YAQA) additionally capture cross-output correlations and cost O(m²n²), quartic where GPTQ is cubic, which is why nobody ships them. Anti-diagonal parallelism (entries on one anti-diagonal carry no mutual dependency, so sequential depth falls from O(mn) to O(m+n)) plus a recursive divide-and-conquer construction brings total work to O(mn(m+n)) — cubic — measured at 60× on an 8192×8192 matrix and 45.6× on 14336×4096, on a single RTX PRO 6000. Separately, and this is the part a single-card reader should read twice, the Hessian accumulation drops from YAQA's O(ℓ) accelerator- resident storage to O(log ℓ) with activations streamed from host RAM — the authors say plainly that YAQA's requirement was impractical without many GPUs. Status cannot move and the map should not yet recommend anything: the quality comparison that would justify switching — two-sided against one-sided at matched bit-width, at 4 bits and below — is appendix material rather than the paper's claim, there is no code link, and the authors state that at today's calibration sizes the excluded terms (Cholesky, Hessian accumulation, calibration propagation) dominate end-to-end wall-clock, so the 60× speeds up what is not currently the bottleneck at 8B. What we believe now:

  • What a rotation buys is its mixing support, not its structure — and a rotation optimised against the wrong statistic can be worse than no rotation at all (new 2026-08-15, and it narrows the map's most dependable mechanism from the inside). rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free is usually read as "the outlier basis is arbitrary, so change it." The sharper reading is that an outlier is a peak concentrated in few coordinates, and a transform helps in proportion to how many coordinates it can spread that peak over: |x|/√b is the floor, and along a controlled interpolation the K range, the relative quantisation error and the end-to-end perplexity gap all fall monotonically with b, reaching parity with full-head mixing only at b = 64–128 of 128. The corollary is a negative result the map should carry as a warning: the only orthogonal family that commutes with RoPE — per-pair planar rotations, b = 2 — is worse than the identity transform even when its angle is derived in closed form and verified to attain its own analytic optimum (10.6499 vs 10.5764 PPL on Llama-3.2-3B), because that optimum minimises a position-averaged second moment while the quantiser reads a tokenwise group range. Align a transform with the statistic the quantiser reads, not with the architecture's own decomposition — and read off any proposed transform's mixing support before anything else. This map has recorded the calibration confound three times and every one varied what the calibration text was about. Domain is a choice somebody made; language is a default nobody recorded, which is why this is a fourth leg rather than a fourth instance.   • The damage has a task term and a scale term, and neither is a property of the weight you quantized (new 2026-08-12, and it is the layer underneath the margin account). Decompose a quantized module's signal-to-noise ratio and it splits three ways: SNR = M + S − N, where M is the weight's magnitude relative to its own quantization error, S is the energy of task-specific activations along the full-precision weight direction, and N is the activation energy along the quantization-error direction. Only M is weight-space. Two modules with identical weight-space error can differ arbitrarily in what they cost, because S and N are computed against activations and activations belong to the workload. This does not collide with the multiplicative margin result even though it looks like it should: the decomposition is additive in log-SNR, which is multiplicative in the ratio — same shape, two levels of description, no shared citation. What it adds is the composition of the surviving fraction c, which turns the map's most annoying practical finding (the constants do not transfer — 18–33pp at 3 bits) from a surprise into a prediction, and makes the paired-margin prescription under-strict: re-measure per model, per bit-width, and per task family. It also explains the repair sweep's negative result — importance scored in weight space moves M alone, while an extra bit moves M everywhere including wherever N is large, which a weight-space scorer cannot see. Then the errors propagate, and scale sets the gain: across Qwen3 0.6B–14B, larger models show "consistently lower source error and accumulated error," with smaller models in "sustained amplification" where larger ones "largely preserve or mildly attenuate." Two compounding disadvantages at the small end, which is the end this vault's readers are forced to use — so the quant ladder's rungs should be model-size-dependent, a claim no in-vault source has priced. Bounded hard: the paper proposes no method and measures no repair, the scale result is one family, and the authors decline extrapolation to activation quantization, QAT or combined compression.   • Quantization does not add noise to a decision — it multiplies the margin, so being confident buys no protection (new 2026-08-10, and it is the mechanism this map has wanted for three weeks). Define a two-way decision's margin as the model's score for the option it picks minus the score of its best alternative. Fitted on the same items before and after compression across 16 models from 8 families and three quantization paths — round-to-nearest, GPTQ/AutoRound, and llama.cpp's own released GGUF builds — the relation is m′ = c·m + b + ε with a surviving fraction that collapses: median 0.86 at 4 bits, 0.33 at 3, 0.00 at 2. Against three additive alternatives by BIC the multiplicative account wins 85% of damaged cells and every cell in the tool-calling and safety families (per-cell BIC differences +28.8 to +1031.2); additive never wins where the damage is. It predicts flips it was never fitted on — within a median 1.8 percentage points on 868 held-out cells, calibrated to an expected calibration error of 0.004 over 131,758 per-decision predictions. Three things follow. The ordering is the last thing to go, which is why an argmax-scored benchmark stays quiet while confidence signals, long dependency chains and threshold decisions degrade continuously — the map's three separate observations of silent damage (quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation, four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer, the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff) are one phenomenon. The failure is directional and deterministic: at 3 bits an agent stops calling tools (should-call flips at 0.963 against should-not-call at 0.200, a 4.8× asymmetry) while which tool is untouched, and the same items flip identically under two different quantizers (77 of 77, 16 of 16). And the constants are yours — borrowing another model's fitted c costs 0.2–8.5pp at 4 bits and 18–33pp at 3.   • The cheapest honest test of a quantized model is a few hundred paired decisions, and the cheapest repair is one more bit (new 2026-08-10). A paired margin set — the same two-way items scored at the first token under full precision and under the candidate build, no generation, no judge, no harness — estimates which decisions break, and it has to be re-measured per model and bit-width because the constants do not transfer. The repair sweep is a negative result with a control arm: over 501 severely damaged conditions one additional bit recovers a median 0.305 of flipped decisions, and five plausible alternatives fail to beat it at matched cost — importance-based weight protection in four variants including a random-weight control, full-precision trailing blocks, margin-gradient rounding, dithering, and attention-only protection. Only three help, all narrowly: an activation-aware quantizer at the collapse point and nowhere else, non-uniform per-layer allocation, and taking the KV cache from 16 to 8 bits, which is free at 4-bit weights — the reallocation shrink-the-cache-by-bits-before-you-shrink-it-by-tokens already argued for, now with a decision-level number behind it.   • GPTQ won on affordability rather than fidelity, and the thing it was more affordable than just got cheaper by an order of magnitude — plus a memory term this map has never tracked (new 2026-08-08). Adaptive rounding needs a curvature model. GPTQ uses a one-sided Hessian, H ≈ E[xxᵀ] ⊗ I_m: geometry from the input activations only, every output coordinate treated as independent. Two-sided Kronecker-factored approximations relax that and quantize better, and were priced out at O(m²n²). Two structural facts remove the price. Entries on one anti-diagonal of the weight matrix carry no mutual dependency under the two-sided update, so they round in parallel and sequential depth falls to O(m+n); and a recursive divide-and-conquer — quantize the first half, propagate its induced error into the second, quantize that — brings total work to O(mn(m+n)), the same cubic order as GPTQ. Measured against the best prior arrangement on a single workstation card: 3.7× at 1024², 26.6× at 4096², 60.0× at 8192², 45.6× at 14336×4096. The second finding is the one this map is structurally blind to. Every entry here reports what the quantized model costs and none reports what running the quantizer costs, because the papers assume a machine where that is uninteresting. For the two-sided family it is decisive: Hessian accumulation drops from O(ℓ) accelerator-resident to O(log ℓ) at O(ℓ log ℓ) compute, with activations streamed from host RAM — the same structural move as frozen-weights-can-be-streamed-which-is-a-peft-only-memory-axis and nvme-offload-removes-the-model-scale-ceiling, applied to the quantizer's own working set. The practitioner form is a question to ask of any quantization method before adopting it: what does the quantization pass itself need resident, and for how long? Round-to-nearest: nothing. GPTQ: one layer's Hessian. The two-sided family, until now: more than a consumer machine has. Nothing here is yet a recommendation — the two-sided-versus-one-sided quality comparison at 4 bits and below is in an appendix and is not the paper's claim, no code is linked, and the authors state the excluded terms dominate end-to-end time at current calibration sizes.   • Rotation has become this map's general-purpose preprocessing primitive, and it now works on the KV cache too (new 2026-07-29, generalizing the 2026-07-28 bullet below). The vault has now seen the same operation do the same job in four independent places, and the unifying statement is that a rotation destroys the structure that makes a quantizer's job hard — outliers, channel concentration, coordinate dependence — at essentially no cost, because orthogonal transforms preserve the geometry everything downstream depends on. (i) On weights and activations, deleting outliers outright (QuaRot). (ii) Bolted onto an unchanged 4-bit GPTQ recipe by a third group as a drop-in step: HumanEval 53.05 → 57.93 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct while perplexity moves only 8.73 → 8.55.   • …but the concentration is a property of the basis, and you can rotate it away (new 2026-07-28, and it reorders the two bullets above). Every outlier remedy in this map until now takes the outlier channels as given and works around them — protect the salient ~1% (AWQ), migrate their difficulty into the paired weight rows (SmoothQuant), or keep them in higher precision outright (QUIK's 256 retained features, Atom's 128). Computational invariance says the coordinate system is arbitrary: in a pre-norm RMSNorm architecture, multiplying an input-side weight matrix on the left by an orthogonal Q and the paired output-side matrix on the right by Qᵀ leaves the function unchanged, because RMSNorm divides by a norm and rotation preserves norms. Choose Q to be a randomized Hadamard and the rotation is also incoherence processing: every coordinate mixes into every other, so a spike in one channel becomes a small contribution to all of them and there are no outliers left to protect. Cost: 1.5 Hadamard transforms per layer at O(d log d), the rest fused into the weights. The evidence that removal beats accommodation is one table read across its rows, at 4-bit weights/activations/KV on LLaMA-2-7B against a 5.47 FP16 baseline — SmoothQuant 83.12 (total collapse; per-channel scaling was engineered for 8 bits and does not extend down), OmniQuant 14.26, QUIK-4B 8.87 while retaining 256 outlier channels, and rotation 6.10 while retaining none. General statement, which subsumes the two bullets above as basis-preserving special cases: the enemy is outlier concentration, and concentration is basis-dependent. This also retro-explains the 2026-07-20 FA3 coincidence — the identical random-rotation trick appearing inside an attention kernel is the same theorem applied transiently instead of fused. (a) The outlier doctrine gains its general form: concentration is basis-dependent, which makes salient-channels-carry-quantization-error and quantization-difficulty-is-transferable-between-weights-and-activations the two basis-preserving responses to a problem that has a basis-changing one. (b) It retro-explains the FA3 edge of 2026-07-20 — the map recorded as a coincidence that an attention kernel reached for the same random rotation for 2.6× lower FP8 error; it is the same theorem, applied transiently. The vault now holds this trick in three places (weight quantization, activation quantization, attention kernels), which makes it one of the few ideas here that crosses the storage/compute boundary. (c) The 4-bit headline is size-dependent in the direction that hurts: −1.09 zero-shot average at 70B, −4.18 at 7B, and the abstract's "99% retention" is the 70B row. Ripple to local-inference: prefill speeds up 1.97–3.33× on RTX 3090s, which reverses that map's weight-only rule and is its first compute-bound result on consumer hardware. Caveats: LLaMA-2 only in the main tables; speed and memory measured on a single transformer block, not end-to-end, because the models did not fit at large batch; evaluation is perplexity plus six commonsense zero-shot tasks with nothing from the reasoning family where this map knows the damage lands; the default pipeline runs GPTQ underneath on 128 WikiText-2 sequences with no calibration-sensitivity study, so it inherits the 2026-07-25 confound — though the calibration-free RTN variant is lossless at 8 bits, which is a real escape hatch; and the kernels are compared against the authors' own unfused FP16 baseline. See rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free, the-four-bit-floor-moved-from-the-weights-to-the-whole-forward-pass.

Open questions:

  • Does the language penalty survive at four bits, and does it exist under AWQ or GGUF? (new 2026-08-13, and it is the cheapest run this map has carried in a week.) The measurement is W3A16 GPTQ only. This map's entire practitioner-facing position is built at four bits, where damage is comparatively robust to the calibration confound (+2.2 points at W4A16 against +58.8 at W2A16 on the content axis) — so the 4-bit default may absorb the language penalty almost entirely, or may not, and the difference decides whether this is a scope condition on the whole map or a footnote about aggressive formats. The run is a matrix somebody could do in a day on one card: one 3B model, English and Arabic evaluation sets, GPTQ and AWQ and a GGUF K-quant, at 4 and 3 bits. Add the obvious second question the paper opens and never asks: does calibrating in the target language do most of what the adapter does, for free?
  • Is the perplexity/accuracy placement inversion a property of one family? (new 2026-08-13.) A repair placement that wins by 43 points on perplexity recovery loses on accuracy recovery — on Llama only; Qwen, whose errors sit late, shows no disagreement. If it replicates on a second early-error family, every perplexity-tuned or reconstruction-tuned allocation in this map is exposed, including its two founding methods. The controlled version is one experiment and it subsumes the older question below: one model, one bit budget, the same allocator fitted under a perplexity objective and under a downstream-accuracy objective, both scored on both.
  • Is weight-space reconstruction error the wrong objective for everything, not just for the head? (new 2026-08-07, and it is the most consequential cheap run this map has ever carried.) Three unrelated literatures now say the loss should be measured in the geometry the consumer reads: the output head (activation-derived metric, top-1 agreement 76.95% → 93.05% at matched storage), cross-model KV transfer (attention-output cosine predicts downstream retention at +0.57 where regression R² predicts it at −0.20), and feed-forward sparsity (INT4 proxies better as a ranking, catastrophic as a value). Both of this map's founding methods minimize a layer-output error in the model's own basis, and expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer separately found per-layer reconstruction objectives are not comparable across layers. The controlled version is one experiment: one model, one bit budget, the same quantizer fitted under a plain reconstruction metric and under an activation-derived one, scored on GSM8K and MMLU rather than on perplexity. Nobody has run it, and every method in this map would move if the answer is yes.
  • What else is not being quantized? (new 2026-08-07; partly answered 2026-08-10.) The LM head was found by loading a checkpoint and looking at the module types, not by reading a paper. Embeddings, the router in an MoE, layer norms and the tied-vs-untied embedding decision are all in the same category — things this map reasons about as parameters and has never audited as bytes on disk in an actual 4-bit artifact. A per-tensor byte census of one popular 4-bit checkpoint from each of bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ and GGUF is an afternoon of work and would say where the remaining footprint actually is. The embedding half now has an answer from an unexpected direction: for a single-language or single-domain deployment most vocabulary rows are unreachable and can be deleted rather than compressed (an-unused-vocabulary-is-deletable-weight), which composes with the head's bit-side compression and has never been tried on a decoder-only model. The router, the norms and the census are still open.
  • Does a 2-bit KV cache preserve decision margins, or only aggregate retrieval accuracy? (new 2026-08-10.) The margin instrument found that taking the cache from 16 to 8 bits is free at 4-bit weights — but kv-cache-management now carries anchor-residual and learned-restoration methods operating at an effective 2 bits and below, all of them scored on RULER-style retrieval and perplexity. Retrieval accuracy is an argmax; a margin is a magnitude, and this map's newest finding is precisely that the two come apart under compression. Running a-paired-margin-set-prices-quantization-damage-without-generation against an aggressive cache compressor is a few GPU-minutes and would tell both maps whether the cache budget and the weight budget damage a model in the same currency.
  • Is the process shift a four-bit effect at all? (new 2026-08-02, and it is cheap.) The one source measuring reasoning-process damage reports its largest single distributional move at FP32 → FP16, a step nobody considers aggressive, with the NF4 step adding almost nothing on four of five models. Either half precision does something to small-model reasoning that nobody has looked for, or the measurement is picking up something other than precision. The disambiguating run is small: repeat the taxonomy classification on one 3B model at FP32, BF16, FP16, INT8 and NF4, which separates dynamic range from mantissa width and puts the four-bit step in a ladder rather than at the end of a two-point line. The authors' own scope note points the second way — GPTQ and AWQ round against calibration data rather than onto a fixed NormalFloat grid, so the same taxonomy under those methods is the other half of the experiment and would also say whether this is an artifact of BitsAndBytes.
  • Does a global expert-level allocation work without calibration data? (new 2026-08-02, shared with mixture-of-experts.) This map now holds two mixed-precision allocators with opposite cost profiles: a calibration-free spectral criterion that allocates per layer, and a calibration-dependent Fisher criterion that allocates per expert globally and wins on perplexity in every cell — while demonstrating, in its own tables, the 10-point calibration swing that makes calibration dependence expensive. The hybrid is unrun and both halves are published.
  • AWQ vs GPTQ head-to-head: both families are in-vault, each evaluated by its own authors (AWQ's overfitting critique of reconstruction included). A neutral comparison — calibration sensitivity, domain transfer, modern architectures, and where newer methods beat both — is the map's sharpest remaining gap, and it got sharper and better-specified on 2026-07-25. The critique's premise is now measured — calibration corpus choice swings a task benchmark 2.1× — but it was measured on MoE bit allocation with GPTQ underneath, so it establishes that the axis matters without ranking the two families on it. The experiment that would settle nine days of this: one dense model, one bit-width, AWQ vs GPTQ, three calibration corpora, evaluated on GSM8K and MMLU rather than commonsense tasks. Everything needed to run it is public.
  • How much of this map's evidence base is confounded by an unreported knob (new 2026-07-25). If calibration corpus moves task scores by double digits, then every published GPTQ-family number in this map — including its own 4-bit headline evidence — was produced with a specific corpus that was usually WikiText2 or C4 and rarely stated as a condition. Nobody has re-run the map's foundational comparisons under matched calibration, and until someone does, the cross-paper 4-bit agreement this map rests on is slightly weaker than it reads.
  • Below 4 bits: GPTQ reports usable 2-bit/ternary in restricted settings; the GGUF IQ-series lives there (local-inference carries the format menu) — where quality actually falls off a cliff needs graded head-to-head evidence.
  • Weight-only is no longer the whole story: KIVI lands the first KV-cache source (2-bit KV), and SmoothQuant (2026-07-21) lands the first activation-weight (W8A8) source — closing the "activation quantization for the compute-bound regime" half of this gap. The below-8-bits question resolved 2026-07-28 (quarot-outlier-free-4bit-rotated-llms) and resolved against the method that raised it: smoothing does not survive the trip, collapsing to perplexity 83 at 4 bits, while rotation reaches W4A4KV4 with no retained outliers. What replaces it is sharper and this vault should treat it as the map's new headline gap: all of the end-to-end 4-bit accuracy evidence is commonsense zero-shot, so the one number that would decide whether a 7B is usable at W4A4 — GSM8K or MMLU, where the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff says quantization damage actually concentrates — does not exist for any activation-quantized model in this vault. A 4.18-point drop on the easy family is a reason to expect worse on the hard one and nothing more. Still open alongside it: where 2-bit KV breaks vs the 4-bit setting others use; whether rotation composes with the mixed-precision allocation results (the rotation source names MoE as future work, and this map now holds two independent MoE bit-allocation findings it could be combined with); and the combined paging + precision + GQA measurement (KIVI's KV bits, PagedAttention's placement, GQA's head count are levers on one budget that no in-vault source measures together). vLLM's PagedAttention attacks the same wall from the placement side (local-inference).
  • Nine ways to train an adapter on a quantized base and zero published head-to-head (new 2026-07-29). This is the AWQ-vs-GPTQ hole one level up and wider: the reference PEFT implementation documents adapter training on bitsandbytes, GPTQ, AQLM, EETQ, HQQ, torchao, Intel Neural Compressor, Transformer Engine and AWQ-by-reference, and does not rank them on quality, memory or speed — nor does anyone else. "Supported" is not a uniform predicate across that list, spanning a maintained default path and a paragraph with a Colab link. The narrower and more decision-relevant version, for a reader who cannot merge: what does an unmerged adapter cost at inference on an aggressively quantized base, which is the price of the AQLM/torchao path and is unmeasured anywhere.
  • KV quantization has two sources and no speed number from either (new 2026-07-29). Both KIVI and the 3.5-bit result are footprint-and-quality only. That gap matters more here than for weight quantization, because a KV quantizer sits on the critical path of every attention call and quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill shows prefill and decode can move in opposite directions — so a compression ratio says nothing about whether the thing is faster. Also unmeasured: whether the 3.5-bit result holds with quantized weights underneath rather than a full-precision model, which is the only configuration anyone would actually deploy.
  • How merging a LoRA adapter changes the weight distribution a quantizer then sees — the merge-then-quantize vs quantize-then-adapt ordering question — is live for the local-deploy pipeline and unmeasured here. Sharpened 2026-07-18: if LoRA inserts high-ranking singular vectors orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting), then a merged adapter hands the quantizer a spectrally different matrix than the base — and every method in this map calibrates against distributional assumptions (NF4's normality premise, AWQ's salience channels, GPTQ's second-order reconstruction). Whether merged-adapter weights quantize as cleanly as base weights is now a specific, testable question rather than a vague one.
