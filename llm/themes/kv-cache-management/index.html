KV cache management (eviction, reconstruction, depth, reuse) — map

Coined 2026-08-03 (auto run) — three sources landed in one night on the same object, and the object had no home. The KV cache has been the vault's most-referenced un-mapped thing: it appears as a component on transformer-architecture (grouped-query attention shrinks it), as a format on post-training-quantization (2-bit KV, data-free rotation), as a serving structure on local-inference (paged allocation, prefix reuse, eviction economics), and as the bill on context-extension (extend the window and the cache grows by the same factor). Four maps hold pieces of one budget, and none of them can state the trade between the pieces. Auto-coined at emerging; flagged for David's review — the alternative was folding tonight's arrivals into local-inference, which is already 1,000 lines and whose own sub-thread declaration names "KV-cache budget" as a sub-item of on-device inference. The case for a map is that the question is not device-shaped: an eviction policy, a residual reconstruction and a depth-partitioned store are answers to the same question — given a fixed budget, what do you keep and in what form? — and they trade against each other in ways no serving map is organized to compare.

Status note (2026-09-02): holds contested, and the map's damage instrument gets a use-case-specific construct that accuracy cannot see. faithfulness-is-not-free-kv-cache-quantization-rag (T1, "Grounding Language Models" workshop @ EMNLP 2026) quantizes the KV cache of retrieved-document context in a RAG pipeline (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, RGB + HotpotQA, INT8/INT4) and scores two constructs: answer accuracy and answer faithfulness (grounding in the retrieved evidence rather than parametric memory). Status note (2026-08-26): holds contested, and the map's scoring rule turns out to have been measuring two different resources under one name. geodesia-kv-monotonic-bit-demotion (T1, Geodesia.ai + University of Bari; native vLLM 0.26.0 plugin released) is the first arrival to attack the denominator of this map's founding question rather than the numerator. Two conflations, both inflating the number in the reporting paper's favour. Chunked teacher-forcing: in real decode the model appends one token and issues one query, but a harness appending 64 or 1024 target tokens at once lets the cache policy treat the whole chunk as a current, unquantized region — measured on one KIVI implementation, the resident rate moves from 5.03 bits/value at chunk 64 to 5.69 at chunk 1024, a 13% swing from a setting that has nothing to do with the method, and almost no paper on this map reports its chunk size. Resident bits versus read bits: query-sparse methods report the selected bytes as if they were resident capacity, and under separated accounting Quest's exact K/V plus min/max page summaries cost +1.6% VRAM over storing the cache uncompressed — its traffic efficiency buys no capacity at all. The uncomfortable consequence, and the map should carry it plainly: a meaningful fraction of the retention-versus-bytes comparisons already here were scored on one or both, and the rule needs three numbers — resident bits, read bits, and the query length they were measured at. This joins cache-compression-must-be-scored-query-agnostic, which found the same failure one level down at the policy rather than the harness. The paper's own method results are then honest to the point of self-defeat: 5-bit perplexity margins of 0.04% at 3B and 0.39% at 8B, and on the frozen full eleven-book PG-19 set the paired 95% cluster-bootstrap CI on the perplexity ratio includes one, with an interval half-length an order of magnitude larger than the effect — "the perplexity difference is not measurable at 4.989 against 5.031 bits/value." What survives is the narrow true statement: the same perplexity at a lower resident rate. It also publishes a long list of variants that helped on calibration windows and failed the frozen holdout — spectral K–V states, a signed second-order cumulant correction, Born gating, Hadamard rotation, VQ, static protection, layer profiles — which is a cleaner statement of this map's learned-policy fragility than any of its dedicated notes. And the capacity table gives the map its sharpest structural result: capacity headroom is set first by the attention architecture and only then by the cache policy, with a graded-precision ladder and window eviction landing on exactly the same single-sequence maximum (590.3k / 590.3k on Qwen3-8B) while splitting 100% to 0% on retrieval. Status note (2026-08-17): holds contested, and the map finally learns what its levers are — from a theorem, not from the composition it keeps asking for. aatc-attention-aware-transform-coding-kv (T1, TU Munich/Darmstadt + MIT + Red Hat AI + Harvard; two backbones, five benchmark suites, three reimplemented baselines given a fairness allowance) proves that under a white-noise quantization model the expected error at the attention output — not in the cache — decomposes into additive key and value terms that factor across tokens and channels, weighted by quantities the cache cannot see (query energy per channel; the output projection's column norm). Run the classical transform-coding + reverse-water-filling solution on that objective and you get an offline per-layer bit allocation that is statistically indistinguishable from FP16 in all 18 cells at 5.82× (Llama's KV budget 1.07 GB → 184 MB), where KIVI, KVQuant and PALU each break somewhere. The durable half is the by-product: eviction zeroes the per-token factor, low-rank projection zeroes the per-channel factor, and per-channel scaling attacks the third — the families this map treats as rivals are assignments under one budget, and low-rank projection falls out of the derivation for free because reverse water-filling gives sub-water-level channels zero bits. That converts the founding question from which lever wins into which factor is cheapest to spend, and turns the composition question into a prediction nobody has tested: two levers on the same factor should interfere, two on different factors should be closer to additive. Status does not move, and three things hold it. Quality is task accuracy throughout — the instrument the 2026-08-06 downgrade holds to be inverted on reasoning workloads. There is no timing of any kind anywhere in the paper: no TTFT, no TPOT, no throughput, no hardware, and the authors state the implementation "lacks a dedicated CUDA implementation," so an online whitening projection plus per-channel bit-width lookup plus reconstruction on every attention call is entirely unpriced — on a map whose sharpest methodological finding is that exactly that class of per-step reconstruction cost QEvict 59% of throughput under a fused kernel. And the theory assumes tokens are independent. Two findings carry independently of the method. The authors' own var-only ablation drops the attention-aware weights and ties the full method on every Llama benchmark at 5.82×, diverging only at a 2-bit budget — the derived importance signal is redundant until the budget is tight, which narrows post-training-quantization's standing rule by scoping it rather than by refuting it. And the table contains a number the paper spends one clause on: the same quantizer at the same ratio is near-lossless on Llama-3.1-8B and catastrophic on Qwen-2.5-7B (LongBench 0.564 vs 0.351, GSM8K 0.730 vs 0.165), which makes the backbone a first-order experimental variable and most of this map's single-backbone quality claims one draw. Status note (2026-08-14): holds contested, and the map gets its first source that answers the founding question by refusing it. Every arrival so far accepts a fixed budget and spends quality to fit inside it — evict, quantize, anchor-plus-residual, partition by depth, restore. vllm-decode-context-parallelism (T2, NVIDIA + Red Hat AI, the team shipping the runtime) says the budget was an artifact of the sharding. Tensor parallelism splits the KV cache by attention head, which works only up to num_key_value_heads; past that the cache duplicates on every rank, and multi-head latent attention — one shared latent, no heads to split by — replicates in full always. Decode Context Parallelism shards along sequence position instead: AllGather Q, attend on the local slice, AllGather + ReduceScatter the partials merged by online-softmax reweighting, which makes the result arithmetically identical to full-cache attention rather than an approximation. On 8×B200 with Kimi K2.6 in NVFP4: baseline TP saturates KV memory at concurrency 64 and plateaus at 1,863 tok/s/GPU; DCP reaches 6,091 tok/s/GPU at concurrency 512 on 82% KV memory — 3.27× per GPU, no quality term. The map should now carry both framings with the boundary stated: compression is the answer when the memory is genuinely absent (one card); sharding is the answer when it is present but replicated (multi-GPU, few KV heads) — and note that nearly every compression result here was measured in the first regime and none of them says so, which makes their baselines systematically the harder case. Status does not move, and two omissions are why. Throughput is measured at high concurrency, the exact regime where two extra collectives per decode step per layer amortize; per-token latency at low concurrency is unmeasured, and this map's own currency test predicts that is where the bill lands — the mirror image of cache-compression-charges-throughput-and-pays-only-under-pressure. And the post is silent on prefix caching and chunked prefill, which is precisely where re-partitioning a cache is most likely to have consequences (kv-reuse-must-be-exact-prefix-to-stay-lossless is the constraint that would need checking). One hardware configuration, one model, one chart.

Status note (2026-08-09): holds contested, and the map's central question — given a fixed budget, what do you keep — got its first two answers of the form "keep everything, badly." Two T1 preprints from the same week, from groups who do not cite each other, both decline to select. anchorkv-anchor-residual-kv-compression (Technion, three models to 70B, byte-matched baselines) stores a few exact anchors per KV head and expresses every other token as a projection plus a 2-bit Hadamard-rotated residual, retaining 99.3% of full-cache RULER-32K at 20× on Llama-3.1-70B and beating every eviction baseline at 10× — the same quality for half the memory — with the gap widening to 0.94 against AdaKV's 0.18 on needle retrieval, where a deleted token is unrecoverable and a coarse one is not. restorekv-learned-restoration-under-eviction (Pusan National University, five base evictors, four backbones) leaves the selection alone and spends part of the same budget on a manufactured summary: eight learned restore tokens make one LoRA-adapted pass over the full pre-eviction cache, lifting KVzip on Qwen3-4B from 38.2 to 73.2 at a 5% budget and improving 59 of 60 budget-matched settings, for 0.4% of parameters and about two hours of training on one card. Status note (2026-08-08): holds contested, and the map discovers it has been treating two budgets as one. binarypc-training-free-hashing-sparse-attention (T1, five models across three families including a 70B, four long-context suites, code released) is the first method here that does not make the cache smaller. It grows it by 1.56% — a 64-bit hash code per key, 8 bytes against 512 — and uses the codes to decide which states to read, attending to a 2% budget at LongBench 49.50 against full attention's 49.64 and decoding 3.56× faster than FlashAttention-2 at 128K/batch 1, 5.04× at batch 16, and up to 2.4× on a consumer GPU at 512K. Status cannot move on it: quality is task accuracy throughout, which is the instrument the 2026-08-06 downgrade holds to be inverted on reasoning workloads, and the headline is an eight-GPU batch-1 configuration. What it changes is the map's founding question, which asked what to keep on a fixed budget and never said which budget: memory binds on a 24 GB card, bandwidth binds on a long decode, and a method that relieves one can make the other worse. It also closes the 2026-08-07 backend warning into a design rule, from the opposite direction — BinaryPC never materializes attention probabilities, so it has no SDPA fallback to pay for and beats the fused kernel outright, which means a cache method's throughput is decided by whether its selection rule needs the attention matrix. Third finding, and it is the map's cheapest transferable one: data-aware binary codes need 64 bits where MagicPIG's random-projection LSH needs >1000, and match Spotlight's gradient-learned hashing (59.31 vs 58.97 at a 5% budget) with no training at all — the third instance in this vault of a structure-aware linear map recovering what a learned one buys. Status note (2026-08-07): holds contested, and the map's founding complaint gets its first partial answer — from a method rather than from the head-to-head it asked for. qevict-recoverable-quantized-kv-eviction (T1, three model families, six eviction and four quantization baselines) is the first source here to put quantization and eviction inside one policy on one budget. The design point is that eviction's defining property is not deletion but irreversibility, and that the irreversibility is separable from the saving: keep the top windows in full precision, hold the middle band in an INT2 tier attention can still read, delete only the bottom, and promote a quantized window back when its cumulative attention score recovers. It beats the best pure-eviction baseline at every budget on Llama (RULER 32K at 20%: 87.6 vs 86.4; LongBench at 5%: 33.6 vs 27.4) and lands within 1.2–1.8 LongBench points of KIVI-2b at half its memory. Status does not move, for the reason the 2026-08-06 downgrade established: every number here is task accuracy, which is the instrument this map now holds to be asymmetric on reasoning workloads, and the GSM8K sweep reports final answers only. Two things it adds beyond the method. It supplies a measured rate for the drift the whole eviction family assumes away — about 6% of long-inactive windows revive against an oracle's 0.98%, and token-granularity scoring churns fourteen times more than window-granularity scoring, so part of what reads as importance drift is estimator noise at the wrong unit. And its efficiency table is the sharpest methodological finding on this map since coining: the same method is 9.8% faster under eager SDPA and 59% slower under FlashAttention-2, where it is also the only configuration that cuts peak memory (29.54 → 20.78 GB). Status note (2026-08-25): holds contested, and the map acquires a question that sits underneath every entry it has: whether the model was built to be evicted from. sigmoid-attention-learned-kv-eviction (T1, ICML 2026 workshop, University of Pittsburgh, code released for all eight cells) runs a controlled 2×2×2 over attention type × learned gating × positional encoding and finds that the attention normalization decides how cleanly a training-time soft gate transfers to inference-time hard deletion. Softmax rows sum to one, so suppressing a token forcibly redistributes its mass to the rest of the row — training's soft operation and inference's hard deletion are genuinely different operations. Sigmoid attention has no such constraint (measured row-sum coefficient of variation 0.44–0.48 against exactly 0 for softmax), so a gate mostly attenuates its own token. The result inverts the dense ranking, which is what makes it worth recording: sigmoid is the worse language model (0.36 PPL behind with RoPE, 1.21 without) and the better substrate for learned eviction — Sig+G RoPE deletes 19.8% of KV entries at 22.424 against its own 22.440 no-eviction reference and beats the paper's H₂O and KeyDiff implementations, while the softmax gate loses to H₂O at matched compression. The author's own probe refutes the easy reading: sigmoid is not generically deletion-tolerant, and at 50% random K/V removal its attention output is perturbed 3.2× more than softmax's (3.666 vs 1.131). The advantage exists only for the learned pattern — which is the same lesson a-learned-cache-repair-is-tied-to-the-evictor-it-trained-against carries from the repair side, arriving from inside the mechanism. Status cannot move on it and the note is graded single-study: 123M parameters, 512-token contexts, one corpus, one threshold per model from a coarse grid, self-implemented baselines the author labels "matched-cache reference points rather than fully optimized SOTA benchmarking," and perplexity as the only quality metric — with no fused kernel and no wall-clock, so nothing here is a byte saved. What it contributes is a co-design hypothesis with a mechanism and a self-refuted alternative explanation, at a scale where nobody has checked.

What we believe now:

  • Attention magnitude is not just beatable as a ranking rule — it is uncorrelated with the thing eviction is supposed to protect (new 2026-08-31). TwinKV measures token attention magnitude against actual causal contribution to the model's answer — a different ground truth than the full-attention- fidelity objective keeping-the-largest-attention-weights-is-already-near-optimal measured near-optimality against — and finds essentially no relationship: Spearman ρ = −0.004. This does not contradict that near-optimality result (a signal can be near-optimal for reproducing full attention while uninformative about causal contribution), but it removes the implicit license to trust attention-weight ranking as causally meaningful, which is the assumption the whole eviction family's design rests on. [T1, back-edge added to the rebutted note] (attention-magnitude-does-not-predict-a-tokens-causal-contribution)

  • A training-free, attention-free redundancy signal repairs whatever eviction policy is already running, rather than competing with it (new 2026-08-31). TwinKV's fix for the above does not propose a better importance score — it scores redundancy: orphan keys (no duplicate, protect) versus redundant donor keys (a near-duplicate survives, safe to swap). The repair pass composes with any existing eviction policy's decisions. Mixed by model and policy — strongest on Llama-3.2-1B/RULER, policy-dependent on Qwen3-4B, no benefit on few-shot classification, where the eviction decision it patches was never the bottleneck. Joins this map's small "repair after eviction" family (an-adapter-can-repair-what-eviction-deleted, what-eviction-deletes-is-two-sums-not-a-set-of-tokens) as the cheapest member: no training, no attention scores at all. [T1] (pairwise-key-redundancy-is-a-training-free-repair-that-composes-with-existing-eviction-policies)

  • vLLM adds disk as a managed KV-cache tier, alongside CPU (new 2026-08-31). Production serving-engine support (out-of-tree secondary-tier managers, partial-load results, tiering metrics) for a capacity lever this map's sub-thread (2) already names — the cache relocated rather than absent, which composes differently with eviction/quantization than either does with each other since tiering does not lose information. See memory-hierarchy-offload, which carries the full entry — this map notes the interaction only: a disk-tiered cache is a slower fetch, not a smaller budget, and belongs on the "where the budget comes from" side of this map's founding question rather than the "what to keep" side. [T2] (vllm-adds-a-disk-tier-to-managed-kv-cache-offloading)

  • Selection is close to exhausted; form is not. Measured against an oracle best subset over 168,192 attention rows, keeping the largest attention weights already closes all but a median 2.1–4.7% of the gap to full attention, and computing the exact optimum is NP-complete. So the levers with real headroom are the ones that change what is scored rather than how well: restoring the deleted softmax mass algebraically (what-eviction-deletes-is-two-sums-not-a-set-of-tokens), repairing with an adapter (an-adapter-can-repair-what-eviction-deleted), lowering precision instead of dropping entries, or enlarging the budget outright. Bounded: the oracle is computed per sampled attention row rather than per generated answer, and the panel is mostly 1–2B. (keeping-the-largest-attention-weights-is-already-near-optimal, T1, 2026-08-27.)

  • A per-head mask is not a freed byte, and this is the third place the same number was inflated. The strongest query-agnostic methods hold the full cache because their selections are masks over one equal-slot tensor per layer; only ragged per-head storage frees memory. Enforcing the nominal budget on the same selection costs 14.1–62.2 benchmark points. Together with the query-visibility gap (now replicated at 87.6 points and decomposed to rankings computed while the question was visible) and the harness chunk-size artifact, the rule this map adopted on 2026-08-26 needs a fourth column: resident bits, read bits, the query length they were measured at, and whether the selection was materialized or masked. All four are properties of the measurement, all four move the number more than the method does, none is routinely reported. (a-per-head-mask-is-not-a-freed-byte, T1, 2026-08-27.)

  • A cache policy's bits-per-value is partly an artifact of the harness that measured it, and "bytes retained" has been two resources under one name. Chunked teacher-forcing lets a policy treat an appended chunk as a current, uncompressed region: the same KIVI implementation reports 5.03 bits/value at chunk 64 and 5.69 at chunk 1024, so comparisons at different query lengths are not rate-matched — and this map's sources overwhelmingly do not state their chunk size. Separately, query-sparse methods report selected bytes as resident capacity, and under separated accounting Quest costs +1.6% VRAM more than uncompressed storage while reporting a low read rate.   • The families on this map are not rivals; they are assignments to different factors of one attention-output distortion — and which model you run changes the answer more than which family you pick (new 2026-08-17).   • At a fixed byte budget, approximating every token beats keeping a few exactly — and the reason is the shape of the failure, not the mean (new 2026-08-09).   • What eviction actually costs is the irreversibility, not the deletion — and the irreversibility is separable from the saving (new 2026-08-07). Every eviction policy on this map scores a state once and drops it forever; the policies differ in the scoring function and agree on the permanence. Two measurements say the permanence is the expensive half. Importance drifts: about 6.18% of long-inactive windows re-enter the important set later in decoding, against an oracle's 0.98% — small, but it is the fraction a one-shot decision gets wrong by construction. And a large part of the apparent drift is estimator noise at the wrong unit: selection churn (Jaccard distance between retained sets at consecutive routing events) reads 0.017 at token granularity and 0.0012 at eight-window granularity. So the fix is not a better scorer but a cheaper way to be wrong. Partition the budget three ways — full precision for the top band, an INT2 tier attention can still read for the middle, deletion only for the bottom — re-rank every 8 tokens on cumulative attention, and dequantize and promote a window whose score recovers, reusing the codes minted on its first demotion so oscillation does not compound requantization error. The INT2 tier tracks full-cache attention scores at 0.9824 cosine, and the attention-mass accounting shifts from 33.3% exact / 64.8% gone to 19.7% exact / 42.6% quantized / 35.7% gone.   • How much eviction a model tolerates may have been decided at pretraining time, by the attention normalization. Every entry on this map takes the trained model as given and asks what to do with its cache. This is the first evidence that the substrate is a variable. Learned eviction trains a differentiable gate and deploys a hard deletion, and the gap between those two operations is what the policy must survive — under softmax it is large, because row normalization forces mass onto the surviving tokens; under sigmoid it is small, because rows are free to vary (row-sum coefficient of variation 0.44–0.48 vs 0 by construction). The same learned rule therefore loses to post-hoc H₂O under softmax and beats it under sigmoid, at 19.8–32.2% deletion with sub-0.04 perplexity cost against each model's own reference. Two guards against over-reading it. Sigmoid is the worse dense model, so "better model evicts better" is not the mechanism. And sigmoid is not generically deletion-tolerant — at 50% random removal it is perturbed 3.2× more than softmax — so this is a property of learned deletion, and a learned policy remains a bet on the deletion pattern staying learned. Open questions:

  • Does the factor decomposition predict which compositions interfere? (new 2026-08-17, and it is the first version of this map's founding question that is falsifiable.) If eviction, low-rank projection and per-channel quantization zero or coarsen different factors of one attention-output distortion, then stacking two that act on different factors should be closer to additive than stacking two that act on the same one. The map's evidence so far is consistent and accidental: QEvict partitions the budget between exact, INT2 and deleted bands — different tokens, so different per-token weights, and no interference appeared. The decisive run is the one the map has asked for since coining, now with a stated prediction to test against: an attention-aware bit allocation applied on top of a token-evicted cache, where eviction has already concentrated the per-token weights onto the surviving states and the allocation is solving for a distribution that eviction changed.
  • Is checkpoint compressibility readable in advance, and does the ordering hold across method families? (new 2026-08-17.) The same quantizer at the same ratio reads 0.730 GSM8K on Llama-3.1-8B and 0.165 on Qwen-2.5-7B. Qwen has half the KV heads, which is the obvious hypothesis and is untested. The cheap version is a table nobody has published: one quantizer, one ratio, six open checkpoints, the spread reported. The interesting version is whether it is diagnosable from the weights the way head-function-is-readable-from-the-frozen-query-key-spectrum diagnoses head function — because if it is, "will this cache compress" becomes a seconds-long check on a checkpoint rather than an evaluation run.
  • Is the head budget searchable, and does the head axis compose with the token axis? (new 2026-08-10.) A uniform, hand-set 50% head split beats four tuned token-side baselines. Nobody has swept it per layer, and nobody has run a head partition and an anchor-residual token compressor together — which is the obvious composition, since one decides which heads need a full cache and the other decides what a full cache costs. Both are cheap; neither has a published number.
  • Do this map's compression methods preserve decision margins or only aggregate accuracy? (new 2026-08-10.) Every quality number here is an argmax-scored aggregate — RULER retention, LongBench average, needle accuracy. quantization-multiplies-the-decision-margin shows those survive compression while the margin behind them contracts, and supplies a few-hundred-item paired instrument that would answer this in GPU-minutes. Until somebody runs it, "99.3% of full-cache RULER at 20×" is a claim about rankings and is silent about everything downstream of the gap's size.

The regime this vault's premise cares about — a 12–24 GB consumer card, where the cache is the term that actually forces a decision — has no number in any of these sources, and the 2026-07-31 eviction ingest already showed why that matters: at a small cache on a 7B model, eviction's measured benefit on an RTX 4090 was a 4% speedup at higher peak memory, because the cache was a rounding error beside the weights.   • Is the selector finding real outside synthetic retrieval? BM25 tying an oracle is the most disruptive result on this map and the most fragile: needle-in-a-haystack inserts literal text, so surface-form matching is near-optimal by construction, and the source's own limitations flag exactly that. The experiment that would settle it is cheap — use a lexical or dense retriever as the scoring signal for token-axis eviction, against the attention scores every method uses, on a benchmark where the query and the evidence do not share vocabulary.
  • Nobody refreshes. Both compression methods here construct once after prefill and never update: the residual entries are fixed, and the depth-partitioned store is written per chunk and read thereafter. That matches reusable prefix-cache serving and mismatches generation-heavy workloads — reasoning traces, agentic loops — which are exactly the workloads long context is being bought for. The sparse-view drafter is the one entry that rebuilds every step, and it does so only because it never evicted anything.
  • What does the budget look like as a curve? Every result here is reported at a handful of retention ratios against a full-cache reference, and the honest reading of the best numbers is sobering — at 10% retained on 32K, the best query-agnostic score is 32.74 against an uncompressed 88.57. Nobody plots quality against cache bytes across methods on one axis, which is the single figure a practitioner choosing a budget needs and which every one of these papers has the data to draw.
