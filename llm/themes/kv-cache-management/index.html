KV cache management (eviction, reconstruction, depth, reuse) — map

Coined 2026-08-03 (auto run) — three sources landed in one night on the same object, and the object had no home. The KV cache has been the vault's most-referenced un-mapped thing: it appears as a component on transformer-architecture (grouped-query attention shrinks it), as a format on post-training-quantization (2-bit KV, data-free rotation), as a serving structure on local-inference (paged allocation, prefix reuse, eviction economics), and as the bill on context-extension (extend the window and the cache grows by the same factor). Four maps hold pieces of one budget, and none of them can state the trade between the pieces. Auto-coined at emerging; flagged for David's review — the alternative was folding tonight's arrivals into local-inference, which is already 1,000 lines and whose own sub-thread declaration names "KV-cache budget" as a sub-item of on-device inference. The case for a map is that the question is not device-shaped: an eviction policy, a residual reconstruction and a depth-partitioned store are answers to the same question — given a fixed budget, what do you keep and in what form? — and they trade against each other in ways no serving map is organized to compare.

What we believe now:

  • Before compressing a cache, check whether it is replicated — the cheapest budget increase is not a compression at all (new 2026-08-14, and it is the first entry here with no quality term). Tensor parallelism partitions the KV cache by attention head, so once tensor_parallel_size exceeds num_key_value_heads the cache duplicates across ranks, and latent attention (effectively one KV head) replicates in full at any TP size. Sharding along sequence position instead recovers it exactly — the partial-attention merge carries each rank's running softmax max and denominator, so the recombination is the full-cache result rather than an approximation. Measured: 1,863 → 6,091 tok/s/GPU (concurrency 64 → 512, 8×B200, Kimi K2.6 NVFP4), at 82% KV memory instead of a saturated 100%. The map's standing question — given a fixed budget, what do you keep — is the right question only when the memory is genuinely absent. On a single card it always is; on a multi-GPU box with a low KV-head count it may not be, and every compression number this map holds was measured without asking. What is not established: per-token latency at low concurrency (two extra collectives per step per layer, measured only where they amortize), and any interaction with prefix caching or chunked prefill. [T2, one configuration] See a-replicated-cache-can-be-sharded-instead-of-compressed.
  • The budget can also be spent per head instead of per token, and which heads need the whole cache is written in the frozen weights (new 2026-08-10, and it is the first answer on this map that reads nothing at runtime). Every method here so far decides what to keep from something observed while the model runs — attention scores, an observation window, calibration prompts, or gates trained on data. Form a head's frozen query-key matching operator M_h = W_K^{h⊤}W_Q^h, normalize its singular values, take the exponentiated Shannon entropy eff_rank(h) = exp(−Σ σ̂_k log σ̂_k): a concentrated spectrum means a few dominant matching directions and marks a retrieval head that needs the full cache, a diffuse one marks a streaming head servable from a 128-token sink plus a 256-token recent window. At 50% head sparsity that retains 96.5% of full-attention LongBench on average across four models including one MoE (Qwen3-8B 38.78 vs 39.10; Llama-3.1-8B 47.55 vs 48.65) and halves KV memory at 256K (34.362 GB → 17.206 GB). Two facts make it more than another selector. It beats gates trained on data at the identical budget (DuoAttention 45.81, QUEST 45.85, SnapKV 45.80, H2O 43.39), and its two controls are brutal — random head selection reads 30.89 and inverting the criterion reads 23.31, so the spectrum is not merely correlated with head function. By Sylvester's determinant theorem the whole diagnosis costs O(d_head²·d_model) — about 4.6×10⁷ operations on Qwen2.5-7B against 5.9×10¹⁰ for the naive form — i.e. seconds on the checkpoint, once, forever.   • At a fixed byte budget, approximating every token beats keeping a few exactly — and the reason is the shape of the failure, not the mean (new 2026-08-09). Store a small set of anchors exactly per KV head; express every other token as a scalar projection onto its nearest anchor plus, for the tokens whose error most moves the attention output, a 2-bit Hadamard-rotated residual. At 20× compression that retains 99.3% of full-cache RULER-32K on Llama-3.1-70B (98.4% LongBench, 94.4 vs 95.0 at RULER-64K), 93.5% at 8B and 95.3% at 24B — and beats every eviction baseline at 10×, i.e. the same quality for half the memory, against byte-matched SnapKV, PyramidKV, AdaKV and a 2-bit rotation quantizer. The decisive number is not the average: on needle-in-a-haystack at 5×, this reads 0.94 against an uncompressed 0.99 while AdaKV reads 0.18. Eviction fails hard — a discarded needle is unrecoverable — and approximation fails soft, because an under-valued position is stored coarsely and is still there. That is the map's 2026-08-06 mechanism (coverage of the trace beats fidelity of any part of it) turned from a diagnosis into a design principle, so this is the first method here with a specific reason to survive the evidence check nobody has run. Two costs a reader should carry: decode latency is 1.3× higher at 32K and only reaches parity near 64K (reconstruction is fused into the attention kernel), and a badly reconstructed key can attract attention mass its exact counterpart would not — a failure eviction avoids by construction. Code is promised on acceptance, not released. [T1] See approximate-every-token-instead-of-keeping-a-few, rank-cached-tokens-by-their-effect-on-the-attention-output, kv-cache-redundancy-grows-with-model-size.
  • Part of the budget is better spent on a summary you manufacture than on more tokens you kept — and the cheapest way to manufacture it is a LoRA adapter (new 2026-08-09). After prefill and before eviction, eight learned restore tokens make one LoRA-adapted pass over the full cache and emit a compact restore cache that occupies part of the same total budget. On Qwen3-4B, RULER-4K: KVzip 38.2 → 73.2 at a 5% budget, 80.1 → 88.8 at 10%, 91.4 → 93.5 at 20% — the gain concentrating exactly where a memory-bound reader operates — and 59 of 60 paired budget-matched settings improve across five different base evictors. Cost: 0.4% of a 4B backbone's parameters (rank-8 LoRA plus the restore embeddings, ≈16.5M), about two hours on one card, and ~0.5% one-time construction overhead with zero query-time cost, because the adapters are switched off after the restore pass. Two ablations say what it is: fixed embeddings with attention-side LoRA reach 96% of the gain, so the restore cache is a learned read rather than memorised content; and building it from the evicted cache instead of the full one costs 8.8 points, so it genuinely needs to see what is about to be thrown away. Mechanistically it restores what what-eviction-deletes-is-two-sums-not-a-set-of-tokens identified as the loss — attention mass, not tokens: eviction drops context attention from 2.73% to 0.56% and restoration returns it to 1.30%, with output-distribution KL falling 7.3 → 3.8. The limitation is the map-level one: restoration trained on SnapKV and run on KVzip reads 42.0 against 73.2, so it is a per-compressor artifact and composition costs a training run per pair. [T1] See an-adapter-can-repair-what-eviction-deleted, a-learned-cache-repair-is-tied-to-the-evictor-it-trained-against.
  • This map has two budgets and has only ever spent one; the first question about a cache method is which one it relieves (new 2026-08-08). Eviction, quantization, depth partitioning and the three-tier recoverable cache all reduce bytes, and all pay in quality. Hashing-based sparse attention reduces reads: keep every state at full precision, add a 64-bit binary hash code per key (8 bytes against 512 of BF16, so the cache gets 1.56% bigger), score all keys with XOR/AND/popcount, attend to the top 2%. Quality is close to free — LongBench 49.50 vs 49.64 full attention on Llama-3.1-8B, InfiniteBench 43.21 vs 43.30, RULER 8K–128K 85.57 vs 87.41 — against sparse baselines at 48.15–49.01, and short-context LM-Eval holds 71.16 vs 72.74 at a 5% budget where Quest at 64 tokens collapses to 36.53. Throughput 3.56× over FlashAttention-2 at 128K/batch 1, 5.04× at batch 16, 2.4× on a consumer GPU at 512K, with a per-layer breakdown of 295 μs (search 90 + select 120 + gather 85) against FlashAttention's 1,814. Memory binds first for the reader this vault is written for, which makes this the less immediately useful family and the one with the cleaner quality story. Two mechanisms are worth keeping independently of the method. The codes are built by a greedy rank-1 binary decomposition of the key matrix — u = sign(Rv*ᵀ), v = uR/N, deflate, repeat — no gradients, and 64 bits suffices where data-independent LSH needs >1000 and learned hashing needs ~8 GPU-hours per model variant. And an Error-Aware Safeguard retaining the ~10% of tokens with the largest reconstruction error is load-bearing: without it the online variant's passkey retrieval falls to 64%. Scored on task accuracy throughout, so "near-lossless" is near-lossless as measured. [T1] See sparse-attention-buys-bandwidth-and-spends-memory, data-aware-hashing-needs-a-tenth-the-bits-of-random-projections.
  • What eviction actually costs is the irreversibility, not the deletion — and the irreversibility is separable from the saving (new 2026-08-07). Every eviction policy on this map scores a state once and drops it forever; the policies differ in the scoring function and agree on the permanence. Two measurements say the permanence is the expensive half. Importance drifts: about 6.18% of long-inactive windows re-enter the important set later in decoding, against an oracle's 0.98% — small, but it is the fraction a one-shot decision gets wrong by construction. And a large part of the apparent drift is estimator noise at the wrong unit: selection churn (Jaccard distance between retained sets at consecutive routing events) reads 0.017 at token granularity and 0.0012 at eight-window granularity. So the fix is not a better scorer but a cheaper way to be wrong. Partition the budget three ways — full precision for the top band, an INT2 tier attention can still read for the middle, deletion only for the bottom — re-rank every 8 tokens on cumulative attention, and dequantize and promote a window whose score recovers, reusing the codes minted on its first demotion so oscillation does not compound requantization error. The INT2 tier tracks full-cache attention scores at 0.9824 cosine, and the attention-mass accounting shifts from 33.3% exact / 64.8% gone to 19.7% exact / 42.6% quantized / 35.7% gone. Beats the best pure-eviction baseline at every budget on Llama-3.1-8B (RULER 32K at 20% retained: 87.6 vs 86.4, full cache 90.0; LongBench at 5%: 33.6 vs 27.4), with the margin widest at the tightest budgets, and comes within 1.2–1.8 LongBench points of KIVI-2b at half its memory. One cell loses (Mistral at 10%, 31.4 vs 31.6) and the paper prints it. This is the closest thing the map has to the composition it was coined to want — with the honest qualifier that the two levers act on disjoint window sets rather than stacking on the same states, so what is shown is that a budget can be partitioned between precision and deletion, not that quantizing an evicted cache is safe. [T1] See eviction-can-be-recoverable-instead-of-permanent, a-quiet-cache-window-comes-back-six-percent-of-the-time.
  • A cache-compression result without its attention backend is two deployment answers reported as one (new 2026-08-07, and it is a methodological rule of the same kind as the query-agnostic one below). The same method, model and workload measured under two attention implementations: under eager SDPA, +9.8% throughput (416.4 vs 379.3 tok/s) at flat peak memory; under FlashAttention-2, −59% throughput (218.1 vs 535.1) and −29.7% peak memory (29.54 → 20.78 GB). The compressed FA2 configuration is slower than the uncompressed eager baseline. The mechanism is structural rather than an implementation slip: a policy that scores tokens by attention needs the attention probabilities materialized, and the fused kernel family exists precisely to avoid writing them down — so importance- scored compression and optimized attention kernels are optimizing against each other, and the better the kernel, the more the fallback costs. The memory saving and the speed saving live in different configurations. No source on this map states its backend, which makes every efficiency claim here conditional in a way none of them says. Same shape as the-cpu-inference-bottleneck-was-the-memory-allocator on local-inference — an unreported runtime term larger than every method term in the comparison.   • PUSHES BACK — the metric this whole map was graded on is asymmetric, and on reasoning workloads it is inverted (new 2026-08-06, and it is the reason the status moved down). A compressed cache can keep the answer and lose the reason for it. Under a controlled fixed-trace replay protocol — an uncompressed model writes the reasoning trace once, then every method replays the same text through its own compressed cache, so what varies is whether the representation is still usable rather than what the model said — eleven methods on Qwen3-8B across mathematics, science QA, clinical calculation and long-context retrieval separate cleanly on two axes that were assumed to move together. Spearman ρ between accuracy rank and reasoning-quality rank: AIME26 −0.95, GPQA-Diamond −0.20, MedCalc +0.31, RULER +0.61. On matched-correct cases the chain becomes invalid for SnapKV 64.8%, AdaKV 63.2%, HeadKV 60.2%, R-KV 57.7%, TOVA 50.6%, LagKV 47.9% against KIVI-2bit 4.2%. The operating rule is the asymmetry: a collapse proves damage; a preserved score proves nothing — except on RULER, where exact distant evidence removes the answer-recovery shortcut and accuracy becomes informative again, which the authors correctly read as a control rather than a separate mode. Three further findings. Coverage beats precision: the gap is tied to losing access to parts of the trace, not to reducing KV memory, which is why the quantization control is barely affected. More budget fixes the answer first — accuracy recovers across 512→8,192 retained tokens while AIME chain consistency stays degraded at all of them. And salience is not trust: sink/window policies over-preserve early claims (StreamingLLM fidelity 94.0 → 28.9 under wrong-chain span replacement) while attention- and head-scored policies over-preserve late conclusion-shaped tokens (SnapKV 53.0), so each family has a position-shaped vulnerability rather than a uniform loss.   • Given a fixed budget on a reasoning workload, spend bits before you spend tokens (new 2026-08-06). This map was coined to compare levers acting on one budget and has complained since that nobody runs two together. This still does not compose them — but it is the first time the two families have been measured on one axis under identical conditions, and it is not close: 2-bit KV quantization matches the uncompressed model exactly on AIME (67.4 vs 67.4) where the best eviction method reaches 50.0, and degrades a matched-correct chain in 4.2% of cases against 47.9–64.8%. Read with a-data-free-rotation-puts-the-kv-cache-at-three-and-a-half-bits — 3.5 bits, no calibration data — and kv-eviction-is-a-quality-spending-lever-and-its-own-numbers-say-so — a 4% speedup at higher peak memory on an RTX 4090 — the ordering follows. Two honest limits keep it a rule of thumb rather than a result: the comparison is not byte-matched (a 2-bit cache over all tokens against a 16-bit cache over 256 of them are different footprints, and the paper does not equalize), and composing the two remains the run nobody has done, now with a reason to expect interference rather than addition. [T1] See shrink-the-cache-by-bits-before-you-shrink-it-by-tokens.
  • The cache is portable, and the error that matters is measured after attention rather than in the cache (new 2026-08-05, and it is the map's first finding about reuse rather than budget). Every other entry here asks what to keep of one model's cache. This asks whether the cache belongs to the model at all, and finds substantial linear structure across matched-KV pairs within a family: on Qwen3 14B→32B one source layer explains 56% of the target's key variance and 32% of its values, rising to 79% and 65% with several source layers. A closed-form per-head ridge regression (λ=0.01, calibrated on 500 FineWeb-Edu sequences of 1,024 tokens, RoPE stripped so the fit is position-free and length- reusable) retains 73–98% of the receiver's standalone-prefill accuracy on four of six pairs and runs 2.7–25× faster than re-prefilling. Two pairs collapse to 41–44%. Three structural readings. Cross-layer selection is the method — collapsing to one source layer costs 35.89 HellaSwag points against 5.31 for dropping inference-time RoPE, so a target layer is a function of several source layers, not of its counterpart. The damage is task-shaped in the vault's most familiar pattern: GSM8K retention runs 95.6% → 1.6% across the six pairs while HellaSwag holds 58.7–97.6%, and one pair posts 94.4% HellaSwag against 18.2% GSM8K — the same signature silent-failures-quantized-reasoning found for weight quantization, from an unrelated perturbation of an unrelated tensor. And fidelity is the wrong objective: regression R² correlates with downstream retention at r = −0.20 while attention- output cosine correlates at +0.57, which is why swapping ridge for an MLP buys +24.3 and +36.8 pp on the two failures at −0.3/−1.5 on two that worked — it redistributes error away from the subspaces attention reads rather than fitting the tensor better. That gives this map the selection criterion its other four levers have been chosen without.   • What eviction deletes is a pair of sums, not a set of tokens — and a few slots of the same budget can keep them. Write attention with the normalization opened up: o = N_S(q)/Z_S(q), with Z_T = Σ_{p∈T} e^{a_p} and N_T = Σ_{p∈T} e^{a_p} v_p. Hard eviction drops (N_E, Z_E) for the evicted set E and renormalizes over what remains. So the loss is algebraic, and sums are approximable even when their terms are not storable: partition E in key space and keep, per group, a mean key, a mean value and a population count c_j, entering the same softmax through ā_j = ⟨q,k̄_j⟩/√d + log c_j + log g(q). Improves all 32 displayed LongBench configurations (+1.02 avg) and 63 of 64 RULER configurations (+3.38 avg) at identical retained budget, with the largest gains at the tightest budgets (+3.47 and +3.66 at 10% and 20% retained). The shared softmax is load-bearing — separate normalizations cost 3.00 points on RepoBench-P — and hard eviction is recovered exactly at zero residual budget, so the method strictly generalizes the family it beats.   • The cache has a second axis, and it is cheaper than the one everyone compresses. The cache is tokens × layers, and every method above operates on tokens. Cache instead one intermediate residual tensor per token at a mid-depth split, retrieve a bounded set of chunks per query, and recompute only the upper layers over the retrieved pack. On Qwen3-8B at j=12 of 36 the stored tensor is 1/18 the bytes of full bf16 KV (8 KB vs 144 KB), and — the structural claim — at fixed retrieval breadth the model-side read is independent of stored-context length, roughly 6.7k tokens whether the store holds 8k or 128k. Measured at 128k: 18.26 GB against a full context's 89.36 GB, 7.83× prefill, with decode roughly a wash (0.89–1.07× of dense). Three controls make it non-obvious: retrieved chunks must attend to each other (block-diagonal reuse costs 36/62 points on multi-key retrieval), more retrieval is not better (a 17k pack loses to a 6.7k one from 16k onward), and depth is a quality-versus-query-cost knob rather than a free win.   • A cache you sparsify but never evict from turns the model into its own drafter, for free. Score historical positions per layer and head from the attention statistics the previous verification pass already materialized — no extra forward pass — draft over the sparse view, then verify over the complete cache, which preserves the output distribution exactly. 72.9% acceptance, 11.26 accepted tokens per step, 2.79× on long-context work where a downloaded trained drafter reads 1.95% and 0.89× — slower than not speculating. If they do, this map's practitioner ordering stops being "prefer bits" and becomes "prefer coverage, in whatever currency buys it." The replay protocol is released and the run is a day's work — it is now the third method on this map with a reason to pass it and the third that has not been tested.
  • The two levers that should be composed are now the two the vault can rank, and the 2026-08-07 ingest composed them the easy way (revised 2026-08-07). QEvict partitions the budget between an exact band, an INT2 band and a deleted band, which is a disjoint composition: no state is both evicted and quantized, so the interference this question was posed about never gets a chance to appear. The sharp version therefore survives intact — quantize the states a token-eviction policy has already selected, where eviction has concentrated attention onto fewer retained states and a coarse quantizer's error is least tolerable. What is now also worth running, and is cheaper, is the partition sweep: QEvict fixes the quantized-tier fraction at q = 0.70 with no ablation, so the shape of the quality curve as the budget moves between exact, quantized and deleted bands is unmeasured — and that curve is the practitioner's actual dial.
  • Does the recoverable tier survive an evidence check? (new 2026-08-07.) The map's newest and strongest finding is that eviction destroys a valid derivation in 47.9–64.8% of matched-correct cases while a 2-bit quantization control loses 4.2%, and the stated mechanism is coverage of the trace. A three-tier cache keeps 42.6% of full-cache attention mass in INT2 that binary eviction would have deleted outright, so it should sit much closer to the quantization end of that spectrum — and if it does, this map's ordering becomes "partition the budget" rather than "prefer bits." The protocol is released, the method is described, and the run is a day's work. It is currently the highest-value cheap experiment on this map.
  • Does the evidence check change the ranking of anything other than eviction? Eleven methods were scored; four of the levers on this map — residual reconstruction, depth partitioning, sparse-view drafting, cross-model transfer — were not among them. Residual reconstruction is the interesting case because its mechanism restores aggregate softmax mass rather than selecting salient tokens, which is closer to preserving trace coverage than anything in the eviction family, so it is the one method here with a reason to survive the check. Running the fixed-trace replay protocol against it is a day's work and the code is released.
  • Is attention-output cosine a general instrument, or an explanation of one paper's results? The finding that regression R² is anti-correlated with downstream retention (−0.20) while attention- output cosine is positively correlated (+0.57) is the cheapest thing this map has been handed: it needs a short calibration pass, no benchmark run, and it applies in principle to every lever here. But it was introduced to explain a cross-model mapper's own behaviour, measured over 12 evaluations on one benchmark. Pointing it at KV quantization — does a 2-bit cache's attention-output cosine predict which tasks it breaks? — would either give practitioners a fast safety screen or kill the idea, and it is a day's work on hardware anyone in this vault's audience owns.
  • Nothing here composes with anything, and composition is the entire point of the map. Five levers now act on one budget — eviction, residual reconstruction, KV quantization, depth partitioning, and sparse-view drafting — and no source measures any two of them together. The two obvious pairs are cheap and decision-relevant: residual reconstruction on top of a 2-bit cache (does the population count survive quantization? do the two errors add or cancel?), and depth-axis caching combined with token-axis eviction, which attack orthogonal dimensions of the same tensor and should multiply. Until someone runs a pair, this map is a list rather than a budget.
  • Every measurement is on a datacenter card, and until 2026-08-07 all of them were at batch size 1. An A100-40GB, an A40-48GB, a 96 GB H20 — and now one run at batch 32 whose peak memory actually falls (29.54 → 20.78 GB), which is the first entry here to move the number a 24 GB card owner watches. It is still a datacenter-class workload, and it buys that reduction by giving up FlashAttention-2. The batch-1 consumer-card crossover remains undemonstrated. The regime this vault's premise cares about — a 12–24 GB consumer card, where the cache is the term that actually forces a decision — has no number in any of these sources, and the 2026-07-31 eviction ingest already showed why that matters: at a small cache on a 7B model, eviction's measured benefit on an RTX 4090 was a 4% speedup at higher peak memory, because the cache was a rounding error beside the weights. The crossover where the cache dominates has still never been demonstrated on consumer hardware.
  • Is the selector finding real outside synthetic retrieval? BM25 tying an oracle is the most disruptive result on this map and the most fragile: needle-in-a-haystack inserts literal text, so surface-form matching is near-optimal by construction, and the source's own limitations flag exactly that. The experiment that would settle it is cheap — use a lexical or dense retriever as the scoring signal for token-axis eviction, against the attention scores every method uses, on a benchmark where the query and the evidence do not share vocabulary.
  • Nobody refreshes. Both compression methods here construct once after prefill and never update: the residual entries are fixed, and the depth-partitioned store is written per chunk and read thereafter. That matches reusable prefix-cache serving and mismatches generation-heavy workloads — reasoning traces, agentic loops — which are exactly the workloads long context is being bought for. The sparse-view drafter is the one entry that rebuilds every step, and it does so only because it never evicted anything.
  • What does the budget look like as a curve? Every result here is reported at a handful of retention ratios against a full-cache reference, and the honest reading of the best numbers is sobering — at 10% retained on 32K, the best query-agnostic score is 32.74 against an uncompressed 88.57. Nobody plots quality against cache bytes across methods on one axis, which is the single figure a practitioner choosing a budget needs and which every one of these papers has the data to draw.
