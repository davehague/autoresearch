What we believe now:

  • A suite average is an undeclared level of optimism, and on a real leaderboard the declaration decides who is in the top five (new 2026-08-07, and it is a defect in the arithmetic rather than in any input). Every mode below is a property of what was fed in. This one survives clean, uncontaminated, correctly-scoped, honestly-judged signals: aggregate them with the arithmetic mean and confidence can exceed the reliability of the weakest component. Measured by re-ranking the public HELM boards under weakest-link aggregation: HELM Capabilities (22 models, 5 benchmarks) — Spearman 0.87, top-5 Jaccard 0.67, max displacement 8 positions; HELM Lite (54 models, 10 scenarios) — Spearman 0.89, max displacement 21 positions, top-5 Jaccard 0.000. The rank correlation is the trap: it looks fine, and the disagreement is concentrated exactly where readers look. Worked case: Claude 3.5 Sonnet falls from rank 5 to rank 12 because a 0.28 Omni-MATH score is masked by strength elsewhere. And the effect grows with the number of dimensions averaged — more scenarios, more places for a weak one to hide. The framing is what makes it actionable: the ordered weighted average family runs on a pessimism parameter ρ where ρ=1 is min, ρ=0 is max, and ρ=0.5 is the arithmetic mean — nobody argues for 0.5, it is simply the default. Practitioner form, cheap and immediately applicable to every citation in this vault: when a claim rests on a suite average, ask for the minimum. Two independent discounts now apply to the same printed number — this one and a-benchmark-score-is-one-draw-from-an-unreported-distribution — and no source in this vault reports either. Carried beside the four modes, like criterion validity, not inside one. The paper's accompanying proposal (formality tiers, scope declarations, validity windows) is argued rather than measured and its LLM-judge reliability ceiling of 0.70 is tighter than this vault's own best judge validation, so the schema is worth adopting and the constants are not. [T1 for the re-ranking; the framework is a position] See averaging-a-suite-hides-its-weakest-dimension, a-benchmark-score-should-carry-an-expiration-date.
  • A metric can be asymmetric — informative when it falls and uninformative when it holds — and the metrics this vault's efficiency literatures are ranked by are exactly that shape (new 2026-08-06, and it is construct mismatch's fifth leg on a substrate this map had not considered). Every mode on this checklist so far is a property of the benchmark, the judge, the data or the training infrastructure. This one is a property of the serving configuration: a compressed KV cache can preserve the answer and destroy the reasoning that supports it. Across eleven compression methods on Qwen3-8B under a controlled fixed-trace replay — the reasoning text held byte-identical, only the cached representation varying — accuracy rank and judged chain-validity rank correlate at −0.95 on AIME26, and among matched-correct answers the derivation is invalid for 47.9–64.8% of the eviction family against 4.2% for a 2-bit quantization control, with answer-first rationalization rising 58% → 94%. The general operating rule, which applies well past KV caches: a collapse proves damage; a preserved score proves nothing — unless the task removes the answer-recovery shortcut, which the long-context retrieval arm does, and where accuracy duly collapses honestly. Two things this map should carry beyond the finding. It is the fifth in-vault appearance of damage concentrating on multi-step derivation while single-step tasks hold — weight quantization, cache transfer, eviction, and now eviction measured inside the chain — four unrelated perturbations with one victim, which is now strong enough to be a screening rule rather than a coincidence. And the judge validation here is the tightest this vault holds (human–judge κ = 0.89, a second judge family at 0.88, a second human at 0.92), which strengthens llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias from a third independent group while making the cost problem worse: the honest check runs a large judge on every output, and no cheap probe has been found to replace it.   • A benchmark can be clean, uncontaminated, freshly rebuilt — and still not predict the capability it is named after (new 2026-08-05, and it lands on this map's oldest mode from underneath). Leakage has been anchored since coining on a remedy as much as a diagnosis: rebuild the benchmark and watch the scores fall (benchmark-scores-can-be-memorization, GSM1k, up to 8%). Underneath sat an unexamined assumption — that reconstruction restores the instrument. Tested on four commonsense benchmarks the field has already repaired (WinoGrande→WinoWhat and Physical IQA→PIQA-RUP by paraphrase, HellaSwag→GoldenSwag and Social IQA by filtering), across 23 open-weight models from six families at 1B–72B, the repaired versions preserve the originals' rankings and do not improve downstream predictive power (mean Fisher-z with downstream: originals 1.565, reworked .982, Δ .584, 95% CI [.326, .853]). Leakage and criterion validity are independent properties, and the standard remedy addresses one. The validity itself is narrow and mislabelled: under leave-one-family-out cross-validation, False Beliefs reaches R² .483/.695 and TRIP .451/.397, while CEI and Sarcasm are negative for every model and no positive correlation survives FDR correction for CEI, Presupposition, TimeDial or Indirect Requests — and "transfer to particular tasks does not reliably follow the domains implied by benchmark labels," so a social benchmark does not preferentially predict social competence. Rankings were unstable to begin with: models move six to seven positions between benchmarks and top-three overlap never exceeds Jaccard .25. This belongs beside the four modes rather than inside them — leakage is about items having been seen, construct mismatch about two models reaching one score by different means, and this about the instrument's relationship to the world, surviving fixes to both. It is also the item on the checklist a practitioner cannot clear at any budget: establishing criterion validity needs a downstream suite and a multi-family sweep, which is a research programme, not a check. Scope it hard — commonsense benchmarks specifically, option-log-likelihood scoring rather than generation, and several downstream tasks small enough (CEI n=31, TimeDial n=60) to carry intervals the paper does not print. Nothing here licenses extending it to GSM8K, HumanEval or MMLU, which carry most of this vault's claims. [T1, one study] See rebuilding-a-benchmark-does-not-buy-predictive-validity, a-commonsense-benchmark-predicts-a-narrow-slice-of-commonsense.
  • Recipe leakage is now a replicated failure mode, and the checklist's newest item has no affordable instrument (new 2026-08-02). The replication. Fix Mixtral-8×7B, the quantizer, and the bit budget; change only the calibration corpus from 128 C4 sequences to 128 C4 plus 128 MATH: GSM8K moves 31.77 → 42.30 at 2.5 bits per expert and 12.89 → 23.12 at 2.0, with the competing allocator moving comparably (33.06 → 41.93). That is a second group, at a peer-reviewed venue, on the same axis the 2026-07-25 flag was raised on and larger than the original 2.1× HumanEval swing. Two details raise its weight: the authors publish the column in which their own method loses to the baseline, and perplexity barely moves under the same swap (WikiText2 5.10 → 5.20 baseline, 5.03 → 5.18 theirs) — so the two instruments disagree by an order of magnitude about the same intervention, which is this vault's fifth independent failure of perplexity as a selection criterion and the first against a preparation choice rather than a format choice. The operating rule stated on 2026-07-27 — "a decontamination claim absent its method is uninformative" — now has a sibling with two legs under it: a quantized model's benchmark score is uninterpretable without its calibration corpus, and almost nobody reports one, which covers every GPTQ-family number in post-training-quantization. Still open, and now more pressing: whether this is a fifth mode alongside leakage, construct mismatch, infrastructure non-equivalence and judge bias, or a meta-rule above all four. The instrument problem. Construct mismatch has been anchored on weight-space evidence — equal in-domain scores over structurally different solutions. Measured in the outputs, where a practitioner would have to catch it, it is expensive: four probes trained on 30,000 judged chain-of-thought outputs top out at F1 0.530 on detecting a correct answer reached without derivation, against a 0.65 viability threshold, and frozen BERT embeddings (0.416) underperform TF-IDF (0.530) because a sound chain and a hollow one are about the same thing in the same register. The human data says the same: 21 of 21 inter-annotator disagreements fall on that single boundary (κ = 0.723) while four of six categories score κ = 1.000. So the checklist gains an item that currently requires a 70B-class judge over every output. Stated generally, and consistently with the perplexity rule: every cheap aggregate this vault trusts is a projection, and each new failure mode lives in a dimension the projection discards — perplexity discards task shape, accuracy discards process.   • The dose saturates at one copy, and three of the handles are on the demand side (new 2026-07-30). Five model sizes (34M–344M) pretrained from scratch on web text mixed with a swept number of MATH test set replicas. One replica is enough to break through the clean corpus's irreducible-error floor — so the hope implicit in "RedPajama contains 8.53% of HumanEval" (that a low dose might be harmless) is gone, and it composes badly with the bullet below: the detector is blind to paraphrase and the effect saturates after the first instance, meaning the quantity a practitioner can neither measure nor bound is also the quantity that stopped mattering. What is new and useful is that contamination behaves like a dose with handles a model consumer can reach. Fresh tokens dilute it — above ~10 replicas, overtraining on clean data raises test loss, with the threshold falling as models grow (32 replicas at 34M, 10 at 63M, 1 at 93M). Temperature exposes it — scores are flat from greedy through τ ≤ 0.56 then fall sharply, and at 1000 replicas going to τ=1.0 costs ~40×, collapsing to roughly uncontaminated performance while clean models barely move. That is a probe needing no corpus, no log-probs and no reference model — you sample the same benchmark twice — which matters against this map's structural complaint that the strong detector needs the training set and pretraining-membership-leaves-a-detectable-tail is the weak demand-side alternative at AUC 0.72. This is a third demand-side signal, cheaper than Min-K% Prob and orthogonal to it (memorization brittleness, not a log-prob tail). Long solutions resist memorization exponentially — three regimes from P(T) ~ e^(−E·T) decoherence in clean models through stretched-exponential "brittle memorization" to "deterministic lock-in", with α_eff(τ) ≈ α/τ, so low-temperature decoding makes brittle memorization look like lock-in. That is this map's first positive benchmark-design result: prefer long-form generative evaluations, and the reason is a mechanism rather than a heuristic — which also explains why the multiple-choice literature could not have found it, since a two-token answer has no length to decay over. [T1]
  • A fifth entry on the non-equivalence ladder: post-training can lower a contaminated model's score (new 2026-07-30). Supervised fine-tuning on the MATH training split lowers test loss below ~10 replicas, does nothing at ~10, and raises test loss above it — the model both learns to generalize and forgets what it memorized, and past the threshold the forgetting costs more. Operationally: a practitioner fine-tuning a downloaded base and watching the benchmark fall would blame their own recipe, when the correct inference may be that the base was contaminated and the fine-tune is working. Nothing else on this map predicts a regression that means the opposite of what it looks like. [T1]
  • Benchmark scores can be memorization, and the parallel-benchmark design measures how much. GSM1k (from-scratch, difficulty-matched to GSM8k): several model families drop up to 8%, and the drop correlates (Spearman's r² = 0.36) with how readily the model generates the original benchmark items verbatim — leakage, mechanistically tied. [T1]
  • The standard defense against leakage detects verbatim copies and nothing else (new 2026-07-27). Every corpus builder in this vault decontaminates by n-gram overlap — strip training documents sharing a 13-gram (GPT-3's convention) or a 50-character span (GPT-4's) with a test item. Measured against paraphrase and translation, that detector scores an F1 of exactly 0.000, in every subject and every language tested, against a random baseline of 0.500 — while scoring a perfect 1.000 on unrephrased items, so it is working as designed and simply has no signal on a reworded sentence. The consequence is demonstrated, not argued: Llama-2 13B fine-tuned on rephrased test items takes MMLU from 54.8 to 89.9 (GPT-4 scores 86.4) and GSM-8K from 28.7 to 95.3, and CodeLlama-13B takes HumanEval from 36.0 to 81.1 (GPT-4: 67.0) — all passing n-gram decontamination clean. The obvious upgrade does not hold either: embedding similarity works within a language (0.903–0.985) and collapses across one (0.179 on a Chinese translation, 0.438 on the same problem in C), and no single threshold works across MMLU subjects because their within-subject similarity distributions differ too much. Only an LLM-judged detector (retrieve top-k by embedding, then ask a strong model pairwise "is this a rephrasing?") is reliable everywhere — and it needs the training corpus, so it is unavailable to anyone evaluating a checkpoint they did not build. Operating rule: a decontamination claim without a stated method is uninformative, and since the standard method is n-gram, the modal claim in the field covers verbatim copies only. [T1]
  • It is not an attack — the corpora already contain rephrasings (new 2026-07-27). As a share of HumanEval, found by the strong detector: The Stack 18.9%, StarCoder-Data 15.9%, CodeAlpaca 12.8%, RedPajama-Data-1T 8.53%. These are the open-data substrate this vault's premise depends on, and the ability to inspect them was supposed to be the advantage of open weights. The MATH training set contains 79 rephrasings of its own test items (1.58% of the test set) with nobody at fault. And the most instructive case is synthetic: CodeAlpaca, generated by prompting an LLM for instruction-following examples from seeds, produced 12.8% of HumanEval as paraphrases because that is what "generate problems similar to these" does. Read these as a floor — they sit on top of whatever verbatim contamination the same corpora carry, which the Llama-2 report puts at >10% of MMLU and the GPT-4 report at 25% of HumanEval. [T1, corpus figures from samples rather than full scans]
  • A benchmark score is a point estimate of a distribution, and the field reports the point (new 2026-07-28). Turn a benchmark's questions into symbolic templates — names, entities and numeric values become variables with sampling domains and correctness conditions — and regenerate them, and a model can be scored many times on the same reasoning problems with different surface content. Across 50 draws of 100 templated GSM8K items, one 9B model spans more than 12 points and another about 15. Two papers can honestly report very different numbers for the same model on the same task, and no source in this vault reports a spread. The contamination signal falls out of the same construction: the original items are just one more sample, so their score should sit near the centre — and for 21 of 25 models it sits on the right, often beyond one standard deviation. Drops from the published score run −9.2 to −0.3, largest in the Mistral, Gemma and Mathstral families and smallest for the frontier models, which is the same uneven-distribution shape benchmark-scores-can-be-memorization found by authoring a parallel benchmark — arrived at far more cheaply, by templating an existing one. This is the third rung of the detection ladder and the first a model consumer can climb: it needs neither the training corpus (which the strong rephrasing detector requires) nor a model-internal likelihood (which the membership screen requires) — only the benchmark and the ability to rewrite it. Two hard limits. The resolution is population-level, not per-item: it prices a reported number, it does not identify which items leaked. And it cannot separate leakage from brittleness — see the next bullet, which is why. [T1]
  • Two of the four failure modes produce the same observable, which the checklist did not anticipate (new 2026-07-28). Append one grammatical, on-topic, operationally irrelevant clause to each question and every model tested collapses: −57 to −66 points for the small open models, −32 to −40 for the frontier ones, −17.5 to −29.1 for the reasoning-trained ones. Eight-shot prompting with the same question's correct reasoning chain does not repair it (one model reads 82.3 normal → 29.4 perturbed → 20.2 with same-question shots), so this is not a prompting deficiency. The consequence for this map is structural: a brittle model loses points when you rewrite a benchmark for reasons unrelated to having seen it, so a perturbation gap measures leakage and brittleness superimposed and is a prior rather than a verdict — the same over-reading risk this map already flags for contamination-detection-is-a-prior-not-a-verdict at AUC 0.72. The four modes were framed as independent, with clearing one saying nothing about the others; here are two that are not separable by the instrument that detects either. The closest thing to a discriminator is in the same source: changing only proper nouns moves the distribution less than changing only numbers (86.6 / 83.1 / 79.1 for names / numbers / both on one model), and neither a memorizing model nor a reasoning one should care about a name.   • Contamination is unevenly distributed — frontier models showed minimal overfitting and generalized to the novel items. "Everyone's cheating" and "leaderboards are fine" are both wrong; per-family checking is the honest posture. [T1]
  • Operating rule, therefore: a fine-tune is deliberate exposure to task-adjacent data, so a fine-tuned/distilled model's win only counts on items it demonstrably hasn't seen — freshly-authored, held-out, or post-cutoff evals. [T1-derived practice]
  • An LLM judge is a legitimate instrument with three biases that do not average out. GPT-4's agreement with human preference exceeds 80% — the same rate humans agree with each other — which is the finding that stops "LLM judges are unreliable" from being the right conclusion. There is no clean ground truth here, only a noisy human signal at the same noise floor. But the judge also carries position bias (which answer is shown first), verbosity bias (longer preferred regardless of quality), and self-enhancement bias (GPT-4 favors its own outputs by ~10% win rate; Claude-v1 by ~25%), plus weak math/logic grading. These are systematic and directional, so sampling more does not wash them out. Position bias is mitigable — swap the order and require consistency; the other two are not cleanly fixable. [T1]
  • The four failure modes, as a checklist. Any benchmark delta in this vault can be wrong by leakage (the model saw the items), construct mismatch (the score is honest but measures a narrower surface than the claim), infrastructure non-equivalence (two runs post identical scores having optimized differently), or judge bias (the ranking instrument has a preference of its own). They are independent — clearing one says nothing about the others — and each now has a graded T1 behind it. [T1, four sources]
  • Infrastructure non-equivalence now has a scale, and one end of it is safe (new 2026-07-26). The mode has been carried as a uniform warning; it is better read as a ladder, because the training systems in this vault sit at very different points on it. Exact: activation recomputation — "all the memory optimizations proposed in this paper give equivalent weight gradient for training and can always be safely applied." It changes when a value is computed, never what it is, and is the only lever here that never belongs in a results footnote. Silently inexact: sharded optimizer math, which the FSDP authors state is not guaranteed equivalent to local training and measure nothing about. Deliberately inexact: asynchronous pipelining, where without weight stashing "the weight update is not a valid gradient of the loss function for any weight vector," where stashing restores only intra-stage consistency, and where the mechanism that would restore data-parallel equivalence is measured as negligible and ships disabled by default. That last one is the sharpest case in-vault precisely because it is documented and defensible — a model trained by an optimizer nobody would write down, whose weights get compared against models that were. Operating rule: the training system is part of the experimental configuration, almost no result reports it, and the honest ordering when you are out of memory is to exhaust the exact levers first. [T1, three sources]
  • A candidate fifth: recipe leakage — and it happens before the evaluation starts (flagged 2026-07-25, not yet adopted). All four modes above are properties of the evaluation. This one is a property of an undisclosed model-preparation choice. Holding model, quantization method and bit budget fixed on Mixtral-8×7B and varying only the calibration corpus, HumanEval reads 29.3 / 13.7 / 25.1 — a 2.1× swing — with MMLU moving 10.8 points. Two people can publish "Mixtral-8×7B, 2.5-bit," differ by 16 HumanEval points, and both be honest and reproducible. It is distinct from construct mismatch (the score measures what it claims; it just depends on a hidden knob) and it compounds badly, because perplexity would not have caught it — WikiText2 barely moves across the variants. Held as flagged rather than a fifth pillar on one paper, in one setting (MoE bit allocation, where routing makes calibration coverage structurally harder than in a dense model). What would promote it: the same measurement on a dense model, or a second instance from any other preparation stage. [T1, one study]
  • Perplexity is the vault's most-repeated bad proxy, and it now fails in three unrelated literatures (new 2026-07-25). Quantization-format selection (four 5-bit llama.cpp formats spanning 0.03 PPL and 3.4 GSM8K points), long-context capability claims (the caveat context-extension was coined with), and now fine-tuning checkpoint selection — LIMA's authors report that validation perplexity does not correlate with generation quality and abandon perplexity-based early stopping for hand-selection against a 50-example dev set. One mechanism behind all three: a token-averaged likelihood is dominated by many easy tokens and blind to the few hard ones, and every capability in question lives in the hard ones. The rule this hardens into: perplexity is a training-health signal, not a model-selection criterion. The uncomfortable corollary is that the honest alternative — a small held-out set, graded — reintroduces either judge bias or an annotation budget. There is no free instrument. A fourth setting landed 2026-07-26 — PEFT hyperparameter selection, where rsLoRA's entire case for higher adapter rank is fine-tuning perplexity with no task benchmark — and it is the first time the rule has been applied within one paper rather than across papers, splitting a training-health claim (gradient collapse: valid) from a model-selection claim (higher rank is better: discounted). No corpus access, no reference model, no training. Open questions:

  • Fifth mode or meta-rule — and the test is now runnable (revised 2026-08-02). Recipe leakage has four instances across four literatures and, as of tonight, one direct replication, which is what it needed to stop being a candidate. The remaining question is structural: is "a comparison absent its preparation protocol is uninformative" a fifth item on the checklist, or a statement that sits above leakage, construct mismatch, infrastructure non-equivalence and judge bias? There is a discriminating test and it is cheap. If it is a mode, it should have its own detector, its own dose curve and its own positive exemplar, like the other four. If it is a meta-rule, then each of the four should have an undisclosed-preparation instance of its own — and three already do (a quantization calibration corpus, an ablation's compute budget, a baseline's learning rate). Go looking for the fourth: an undisclosed preparation step that changes a judge result. Judge temperature, system prompt and reference-answer formatting are all unreported in most LLM-judge papers, and if one of them swings agreement the way a calibration corpus swings GSM8K, the meta-rule reading wins.
  • Can a structural probe catch hollow reasoning where a lexical one cannot? (new 2026-08-02.) The four probes that fail at F1 0.530 are simple by design — eight hand-crafted surface features, TF-IDF, and a frozen BERT — and the paper's own diagnosis is that the distinction is structural rather than semantic, which none of the four represents. The obvious next attempt is a probe over features the diagnosis implies: step count, presence of arithmetic operations, whether the quantities given in the question appear in the chain, whether each stated intermediate follows from a prior one. If a cheap structural classifier clears 0.65, this map's newest failure mode becomes affordable to check and the cost asymmetry noted above disappears; if it does not, "you need a large judge" is established rather than assumed. One labelled dataset of 30,000 already exists.
  • The dose is measured at 344M and the trend points the wrong way for extrapolation (new 2026-07-30). The saturate-at-one-copy result is established two orders of magnitude below any model this vault runs, and the authors decline to extrapolate. The vault cannot either — but the direction is the alarming one, since the memorization threshold falls with scale (32 → 10 → 1 replicas across 34M → 93M). The missing measurement is the one that would let a percentage become a discount: the same replica sweep at 1B–7B, on HumanEval rather than MATH, with paraphrased rather than verbatim insertions — since paraphrase is the map's actual live problem and is presumably a weaker dose per copy. Until then "The Stack contains 18.9% of HumanEval" still cannot be priced.
  • A fourth detector exists and no protocol places it (new 2026-07-30). Temperature sensitivity is now the cheapest demand-side probe on this map — two sampling runs, no corpus, no log-probs — which makes the ordered-protocol gap below more embarrassing rather than less: there are now four instruments with four different failure modes and still no published procedure saying which to run, in what order, at what budget, and what a positive licenses. Concretely unrun and cheap: score a distilled small model and its teacher on the same benchmark at τ=0 and τ=1.0 and read the gap — the audit distillation-small-models has wanted since coining, now costing two eval runs.
  • Is the fifth mode one mode or a family? (sharpened 2026-07-30.) Recipe leakage was flagged 2026-07-25 on one paper in one setting (the quantization calibration corpus). It now has three instances in three unrelated literatures — calibration corpus, ablation compute budget, baseline learning rate — which is enough to state the general form (a comparison absent its preparation protocol is uninformative) and not enough to know whether the map should adopt it as a fifth failure mode alongside the four, or as a meta-rule that sits above all of them. It is a property of model preparation rather than of evaluation, which is the argument for the latter. Unresolved deliberately; the article carries it as its own flagged section.
  • Detection without rebuilding — all three families now sourced, as of 2026-07-28. Membership inference landed 2026-07-24 (min-k-prob-detecting-pretraining-data, AUC 0.72 — a prior, not a verdict). N-gram / substring overlap landed 2026-07-27 and landed negative (F1 of zero against paraphrase). Dynamic / perturbed evals landed 2026-07-28 (gsm-symbolic-limitations-mathematical-reasoning) — template the benchmark, regenerate it, and read where the published score falls in its own distribution. The branch is no longer a list of absences; it is a set of three instruments with known and different failure modes, which is what the ordered protocol below has been waiting for. What replaces the gap is the composition problem, restated with the third tool in hand: the corpus-side detector is reliable and unavailable to model consumers, the model-side screen is available and weak, and the benchmark-side perturbation is available and confounded with brittleness. Nobody has written the procedure that says which to run, in what order, at what budget, and what a positive on each licenses you to conclude.
  • The two contamination tools in-vault do not compose, and the gap between them is where every reader of this vault stands (new 2026-07-27). The reliable rephrasing detector needs the training corpus; the membership-inference screen needs only the model and runs at AUC 0.72. A model builder can use the strong one and does not need the weak one; a model consumer — which is everyone fine-tuning an open-weights checkpoint they did not pretrain — can only use the weak one, on a corpus they cannot see, against a contamination class the builder's own pipeline probably missed. Nobody has published a demand-side detector for paraphrased contamination specifically, and it is not obvious one is possible: membership inference scores a passage you supply, and the whole problem is that you do not know which passage to supply.
  • Nobody has measured the dose (revised 2026-07-28). The rephrasing result trains for 16 epochs on nothing but rephrased test items — a maximal-overfit demonstration that establishes the failure exists and says nothing about its size at realistic exposure. The number a practitioner needs is a response curve: given x% of a fine-tuning set as paraphrased benchmark items at n epochs, how many points of inflation? Without it, "8–18% of HumanEval is in RedPajama" cannot be converted into a discount on any published HumanEval score, which is exactly the operation this map exists to support. 2026-07-28 supplies the other end of the same missing curve and shows why it is hard to close. Perturbation gives an observed inflation at realistic exposure — up to 9.2 points between a published GSM8K score and its templated variants — which is the outcome measurement the dose-response curve was wanted for. But it is unattributed: some of those 9.2 points are leakage and some are brittleness, and nothing in-vault separates them. So the vault now has a controlled-exposure experiment with an unrealistic dose and an uncontrolled-exposure measurement with an unattributed effect, and the missing artifact is the one that would join them — contaminate a known fraction of a fine-tuning set with paraphrased items, then measure both the raw score and the perturbation gap.
  • Does the detector survive the models it would be used on? (new 2026-07-24). Every model in the membership-inference evidence is 2022–2023 vintage and ≤65B, trained before the deduplication and decontamination pipelines that data-curation now documents as standard. A modern 15T-token run with aggressive dedup plausibly has a weaker memorization tail — which would make detection harder without making contamination rarer. Untested, and it is the same 2023-vintage-numbers problem this map already flags for judge bias.
  • Nobody has measured whether the four failure modes compound, though the modal in-vault case carries all four at once — and the vault still has no source giving a practitioner an ordered, budgeted procedure for clearing them. Leakage now has its first two rungs; construct mismatch, infrastructure non-equivalence, and judge bias have named modes and no protocol.
  • Domain reach: GSM1k is grade-school math — whether code, reasoning, and instruction-following suites show the same overfitting pattern is unmeasured in-vault.
  • LLM-as-judge has failure modes orthogonal to contamination and needs its own thread. RESOLVED 2026-07-20 after two escalations (2026-07-18, 2026-07-19) — the biases are now graded. What replaces it is narrower and sharper: the in-vault magnitudes are 2023-vintage. GPT-4-0314 and Claude-v1 are retired models; the failure modes are structural but nobody has re-measured their size on the judges scoring today's papers, and the vault is applying 2023 numbers as a discount on 2024–2026 results. Also unresolved: judge reliability on technical, long-form, and domain-specific outputs — the 80% agreement figure is on open-ended chat, which is not the distribution this vault's premise targets. And reward-model failure (a different object from an eval judge) is still ungraded; see post-training-rl.
  • What replaces the judge, when you can afford it (new 2026-07-19): Molmo demonstrates human pairwise preference at 325k ratings cross-checked against automated benchmarks. That is a lab-scale answer. The practitioner-scale version — how to evaluate a fine-tune honestly without a judge model or an annotation budget — has no source, and it is the question this vault's premise most needs answered.
  • Suite averages hide shape (new 2026-07-19): Molmo-72B tops an 11-benchmark average while trailing GPT-4o by 15 points on MMMU. RESOLVED 2026-08-07 — and worse than the question implied. Re-ranking HELM Lite (54 models, 10 scenarios) by weakest-link instead of the mean leaves the two top-five sets completely disjoint, with displacements up to 21 positions, while the overall Spearman stays at 0.89; the effect grows with the number of scenarios averaged. Any "beats frontier on task X" claim — the vault's own premise — needs the per-task breakdown, and the operating form is now sharper: ask for the minimum, not the mean. What replaces the gap is narrower and unrun: nobody has re-ranked a domain-specific leaderboard this way, and nobody has checked whether the disjointness survives when the scenarios are correlated rather than deliberately diverse — HELM is built for coverage, which is the case most favourable to the effect. Also unrun and cheaper: apply the same re-ranking to this vault's own cited suite averages, several of which are the sole support for a graded claim.
  • Construct validity is now a named second failure mode (2026-07-18): a score can be uncontaminated and still misleading if the benchmark surface is narrower than the claim. Detecting that is a different problem from detecting leakage — OOD generalization gaps and spectral checks on the fine-tuned weights are the two probes in-vault, neither of them a workflow. What an honest fine-tune evaluation protocol looks like, covering both failure modes, is unsourced.
  • All four failure modes are named; the protocol that covers them is still not written (sharpened 2026-07-20). The vault can now enumerate leakage, construct mismatch, infrastructure non-equivalence, and judge bias, each with a T1 — but no in-vault source gives a practitioner an ordered procedure for clearing all four on their own fine-tune, at their own budget. That synthesis is now the map's highest-value missing artifact, and it is the one thing standing between this map and consensus.
  • Nobody has measured whether the failure modes compound (new 2026-07-20). A distilled model evaluated by the teacher's own family on a contaminated suite trained under a sharded optimizer is carrying all four at once — plausibly the modal case in this vault — and every source isolates one.
  • The practical decontamination workflow for the craft loop (dedup your fine-tuning set against your eval set, prove it) has no tool-doc source yet — though FineWeb now shows the supply-side version at pretraining scale (benchmark-n-gram removal in the pipeline; data-curation).
