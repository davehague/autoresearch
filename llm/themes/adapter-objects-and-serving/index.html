Adapter objects & serving (what the added object is, and who serves it) — map

Forked out of peft-methods on 2026-08-10 (auto run, bounded auto-fork; flagged for David's review). This is the third strand, and it is the cleanest carve of the three because it is the only one that is not an answer to the parent's contested question. The parent asks whether a low-rank update matches full fine-tuning and which knob explains the gap; its second strand asks what adaptation costs on one card. Both take the adapter as given and argue about its configuration. The sources collected here kept changing the ontology instead — task knowledge that lives outside both the weights and the prompt, an adapter that is a function of the input rather than a stored matrix, an adapter split across two owners, an adapter used to repair a KV cache — and they are scored against serving throughput and per-tenant memory rather than against task accuracy on a held-out set. That is a different question asked on different hardware against different baselines, which is the signature of a separable argument rather than a facet. Status: consolidating (inherited from peft-methods at the fork; mergeability-as-linearity is replicated from two directions and the serving economics are measured, while every substrate claim rests on one study) Status note (2026-08-14): holds consolidating, and the map acquires a fifth place for task knowledge to live — the one that touches nothing the model owns. weightless-fine-tuning-logit-space-transport (T1, Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, three LaMP personalization tasks, four baselines, a budget-controlled arm, an ablation and a human study) computes the ordinary supervised residual on an author's own writing and transports it to the current prompt through a cross-prefix operator estimated from dropout-induced cross-covariance, applying the result directly to the logits. No weight update, no prompt positions, no backward pass anywhere in the method. It beats the LoRA-SFT arm on average on both bases (Qwen3 R-1 0.335 vs 0.321; Llama 0.287 vs 0.259, winning every individual task) at a stated <7% of SFT's effective compute. Status does not move, and the reason is the same one this map has now applied five times: the substrate claim rests on one study, and the comparison arm is a fixed-recipe LoRA — rank 8, 40 epochs, no learning-rate sweep — which a-differential-learning-rate-is-the-free-version-of-the-sweep prices at up to 0.25 absolute on this very benchmark family. A 0.014 average margin does not clear that. What does survive independently is stronger than the benchmark: the logit shift WFT induces has cosine 0.875 with SFT's over the top-10 tokens carrying >95% of the mass, so the claim "this is the same adaptation, applied elsewhere" is measured rather than asserted; and the ablation says the operator, not the residual, is the method (0.335 → 0.277 without it, a bigger loss than halving compute or halving data). Two riders the map should carry as prerequisites rather than caveats. The method requires dropout at inference on a frozen model, which is a checkable property of a checkpoint in the same way weight tying is. And its serving cost is an O(V)-scale operation per token per personalized request, against a merged LoRA's exact zero — a bill only tokenization-and-vocabulary (coined tonight) is organized to read, and one the paper never states, reporting no wall-clock, memory or throughput number anywhere. WFT buys the training budget and spends it at decode.

Status note (2026-08-20): holds consolidating, and the map's merge instinct gets a boundary it did not have. slaat-switching-lora-adapters-as-a-tool (T1 by source class, single-study by strength; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, two synthetic languages with disjoint corpora, adapters and harness released) gives an agent a tool to swap its own LoRA adapter mid-trajectory and finds the two results this map most needed and least expected. The fused adapter — one adapter trained on both corpora, the obvious answer to "I need two skills" — is best of every condition on isolated per-skill knowledge (44/50 and 360/500, beating both specialists) and scores 0/200 on the composed agentic tasks, because the trajectory also needs the ordinary Python and JSON that specialization erased; the capability panel prices it at HumanEval+ 7.3% against a base the routed conditions match. Model-driven switching scores 116/200 against a hand-written file-extension heuristic's 101, subagent delegation's 59, the untouched base model's 64, token-level Arrow routing's 43, and a frontier reference's 75 — the last of which is a measurement of the task construction, not of the models, and should not be quoted otherwise. So this map's existing merge finding (adapters-can-merge-away-inference-latency) needs a clause: merge for latency, do not merge for capability, and the mechanism is already in the vault (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting — orthogonal intruder directions that accumulate across sequential fine-tunes, here compressed into one run). The cost comparison against subagents is the widest-reaching part and the least emphasized: 46.1× fewer output tokens on the median failure (797 vs 36,731), because a failing subagent retries inside a context the host cannot see. Status does not move — one base model, one seed, one sample per task, a two-adapter library, synthetic skills — and the number a local reader actually needs is absent: no adapter-swap latency, no KV-cache invalidation policy, no throughput anywhere. That gap collides with the same night's speculative-decoding arrival, which is incompatible with LoRA: the serving stack that would make swapping fast and the one that makes decoding fast are currently not the same stack.

What we believe now:

  • Merge an adapter to remove latency; do not merge two adapters' training data to gain capability (new 2026-08-20). Training one adapter on two skills maximizes exactly what it is designed to maximize — isolated per-skill knowledge, where the fused adapter beats both specialists — and scores zero on 200 composed agentic tasks, because composed work also needs the general capability specialization erodes. Keeping the adapters separate and letting the agent swap between them mid-trajectory solves 116/200, against 101 for a hand-written switching rule and 64 for the untouched base model. Two honest limits sit inside the same result: model-chosen routing beats the rule partly by inventing an unwritten policy, and it collapses on the hardest binding (4/50 against the 22/50 its own single-binding rates predict) where the hand-written rule composes near-multiplicatively — so the routing is good enough to beat a heuristic and not good enough to hold a two-skill trajectory together. single-study: one base model, one seed, one sample per task, two adapters, synthetic skills. [T1] a-swappable-adapter-beats-a-fused-one-when-the-trajectory-needs-both
  • An adapter swap dominates a subagent call on both quality and cost, and the cost gap is concentrated in failures (new 2026-08-20). Same task suite: swapping solves 116/200 against delegation's 59, and uses 1.3× fewer output tokens on the median success and 46.1× fewer on the median failure (797 against 36,731). The mechanism is structural — a subagent that cannot do the task retries inside a context the host cannot observe and returns only a failure, while a swap keeps one trajectory, one context and one budget, so a bad swap is visible on the next turn. This is a-prompt-resident-skill-is-a-per-query-tax one level out: routing around a missing skill re-imports its cost somewhere less visible. Caveat the source does not test and which would close much of the gap in one line: the subagent baseline has no token ceiling. [T1] an-adapter-swap-costs-less-than-a-subagent-call
  • Task knowledge can live in the logits, and getting it there needs no gradient at all (new 2026-08-14, the map's fifth substrate and the first with nothing stored on the model). The four this map already tracks — merged weights, an unmergeable nonlinear branch, an external positionless operator, the prompt — all put something somewhere. Weightless Fine-Tuning puts nothing anywhere: a supervised residual computed offline, carried to the current prompt by a cross-prefix transport operator, added to the logits at decode. The mechanism evidence is the durable part (cosine 0.875 against SFT's own logit shift over

    95% of the probability mass; removing the operator costs 0.335 → 0.277, more than any compute or data reduction tested). The benchmark win over LoRA-SFT is not, because the LoRA was never tuned. The trade the paper declines to state is the one this map exists to state: zero training cost, non-zero and vocabulary-scaled serving cost, on every token of every request, against a merged adapter's zero. And it is only available on checkpoints that run dropout at inference. [T1, one study, heavily discounted on the head-to-head] See a-supervised-update-can-be-applied-in-logit-space, the-transport-operator-is-the-method-not-the-residual.

  • Mergeability is a property of the adapter being linear, not only of the base format being well-behaved — so expressivity and zero-cost serving are a hard trade (new 2026-08-08). adapters-can-merge-away-inference-latency has been narrowed once already, by every-quantization-backend-now-accepts-an-adapter: merging breaks on codebook formats, silently and incorrectly in one shipped backend. This is the same property failing from a direction no base format can fix. An adapter path computing B·φ(Ax) has no weight-matrix equivalent, for any φ that is not the identity, at any precision. So the four levers this map tracks gain a fifth axis with a price nailed to it, and the paper that crossed the line does not pay it: AuroOFT reports no memory, latency or throughput number anywhere, in a study whose entire premise is a 4-bit base on constrained hardware — against a measured 76% higher inference latency for the unmerged path in four-bit-fine-tuning-buys-vram-with-time-and-energy, for a 1.30–2.70-point accuracy gain. Practitioner form: decide whether you will merge before choosing an adapter architecture. Fine-tuning to produce a checkpoint you quantize and ship disqualifies a nonlinear adapter outright; serving hot-swappable per-task artifacts off one frozen base makes the trade free. And the paper's other number is a lesson about reading this literature rather than about the method: a 15.5-point margin over QLoRA with no learning-rate sweep is an order of magnitude beyond anything a parameterization has bought in this vault's controlled comparisons (0.43–1.75% tuned, per adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact), so carry the matched-branch delta and discard the cross-family one. Fourth consecutive adapter paper here to run without a sweep. [T1, heavily discounted] See a-nonlinear-adapter-branch-can-never-merge, a-fifteen-point-baseline-gap-is-a-claim-about-the-baseline.
  • An adapter's zero-inference-cost property belongs to the base format, not to the adapter (new 2026-07-29). adapters-can-merge-away-inference-latency is one of LoRA's cleanest practical wins. Across the nine backends, merging is the capability that keeps breaking: AQLM cannot merge at all, torchao merges correctly "only with LoRA and int8_weight_only" and otherwise produces silently incorrect results, and both Intel Neural Compressor and Transformer Engine list merge()/unmerge() as unsupported. The reason is structural rather than incidental — merging requires adding a full-precision low-rank product back into the base and re-encoding it without loss, which is roughly true of int8 and NF4 and simply false of a codebook scheme where the "weight" is an index into learned vector codes. Quantization aggressiveness and adapter mergeability are in direct tension. For a local practitioner that converts a settled advantage into a format-selection question: go to 2 bits and you keep a separate adapter forever, at whatever the unmerged forward pass costs — which nobody has measured. No paper in this map or in post-training-quantization states this, because each paper works with exactly one base format. [T2]
  • Adapters still add no inference tax: ΔW merges into W at deploy time (zero added latency), or stays separate as small hot-swappable task artifacts — the merged form is what feeds the quantize-and-ship-locally pipeline. The intruder-dimension result adds a caveat to stacking those artifacts, not to merging one. [T1]
  • Task knowledge has three places to live, not two — and on the tasks where an adapter is weakest, the third one wins at a fifth of the storage (new 2026-08-08). This map has always been a search inside one option: knowledge goes in the weights, and the question is how cheaply. The other option was in the prompt, which nobody here studied because it is not fine-tuning. The third is an external factorized operator — M_s = W_s U_sᵀ at each injection layer, read as (1/√m_s)·W_s(U_sᵀq) with q a normalized function of the current hidden state, injected back through a shared interface with per-layer gates, over a frozen backbone, at zero prompt positions. Under matched ~1.3M-parameter budgets on Qwen3.5-4B it reads SearchQA EM 80.0 / 80.1 against soft prefix 75.6 / 73.8, prefix-KV 69.4 / 70.7 and LoRA 70.1; LiveMath 75.6 / 79.0 against LoRA's 80.7 — and on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B it takes both (SearchQA 82.8 vs 79.9; LiveMath 72.6 vs 52.4). Seven of eight matched settings. The properties that make it a substrate rather than a variant come apart from the accuracy: the per-task artifact is 20 KiB at rank 1 (67 KiB compact) against LoRA's 10 MiB, a cached swap is 0 ms, and three skills register sequentially into one interface with no measurable forgetting (80.9 / 78.2 / 40.6 against single-skill 78.3 / 78.2 / 40.3, with mixed replay load-bearing — without it SearchQA drops 2.4 points) — which is the property intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting says the adapter-per-task workflow lacks. The costs are real and one of them is unpriced: 6.5% of decode and 1.0–1.8% of prefill on an H100, a 13.3M-parameter interface per backbone that must be trained before any skill loads and whose training cost the paper never states, operators built in one model's residual space with cross-backbone transfer unevaluated, and a sparse-reward failure on long-horizon agentic tasks (ALFWorld 42.3 vs SkillOpt's 81.3). The suggestive and untested reading of the LoRA split: the operator wins where the knowledge is a procedure for reading and the adapter wins where it is closer to a learned function. [T1, one study] See task-knowledge-has-a-third-place-to-live, a-registered-skill-compresses-to-one-direction-per-layer.
  • Distilling a prompt into a positionless operator beats letting the model re-read the prompt, and on a large enough model the prompt was making things worse (new 2026-08-08). Registration prefills an authored text skill once through the frozen model, takes the normalized residual states as the operator's factors, and then trains only the interface against KL(p_text ‖ p_KV) + ½·CE(p_KV, y) — teacher and student are the same weights, one with the text in the prompt and one without. Nothing is taught; a capability is relocated out of a representation the model must re-derive every query. Same words, same author, same procedure: LiveMath 23.4 → 77.2 on Qwen3.5-4B, SearchQA EM 67.4 → 79.3, STaRK-Prime 39.8 → 49.7, gpt-oss-20b LiveMath 22.6 → 65.3. On Qwen3.6-35B-A3B the authored text skill scores below the unskilled base (26.6 against 31.2) while consuming its positions, and conversion recovers 46 points. The shape is the same as semantic-content-is-assembled-by-mid-depth — a low-rank object trained purely to match a teacher's logits, re-fitting an interface rather than adding knowledge — which is this map's task-not-knowledge bet in its purest observed form, and the far side of the axis the map is contested about. The cost side belongs on context-extension: a prompt-resident skill inflates prompts 65× (LongHealth 184 → 12,040) and 39× (QuALITY 150 → 5,848), re-encoded every request, where the operator's overhead is flat from 128 to 8,192 tokens because it is not in the attention cache. [T1] See converting-a-prompt-into-an-operator-beats-reading-the-prompt, a-prompt-resident-skill-is-a-per-query-tax.
  • An adapter can be factored across two owners, and an adapter can be a function instead of a stored object — two independent departures from "an adapter is a small matrix you train and keep" (new 2026-08-05). The factorization: train a high-capacity domain basis once on public data, pin it in VRAM, and let each user train an r ≤ 2 residual on private data over it, swapping only the residual per request — 13.63 MB resident against 0.85 MB in flight, a 16× cut in the per-tenant term, and vLLM throughput up to +51% under PCIe pressure and +28% under VRAM pressure, with gains that grow with tenant count (+9.5% → +25.4% from 128 to 512 adapters) exactly as amortizing a fixed cost predicts. The generation: represent each expert of an MoE-style adapter as a small conditioning vector and have one shared hypernetwork emit the low-rank update row by row, per input — adapter storage becomes independent of expert count and the adaptation becomes instance-specific rather than task-specific. Both are structural additions to the four levers this map tracks (placement, initialization, parameterization, rank), and both come with the same two holes: no quantized base in either, and no latency or memory measurement for the generated-weights path, which for a vault premised on one or two consumer cards is the number that decides whether it is usable at all. The factorization is also a two-party protocol — it needs someone else serving a basis for your domain — so it belongs to local-inference's multi-tenant strand and buys a single-card practitioner nothing. [T1, two studies, both single-group] See an-adapter-splits-into-a-shared-basis-and-a-private-residual, expert-capacity-can-be-decoupled-from-adapter-storage.

  • 2026-08-10 — map carved from peft-methods on that map's fork-due advisory (27 dated bullets, 3 declared sub-threads). Two dated bullets and six belief bullets moved. What the strand had accumulated, stated as one argument: an adapter is not necessarily a small matrix you train and keep. It can be a factorized operator read from the residual stream that occupies no prompt positions and does not grow with context; it can be emitted per input by a hypernetwork instead of stored; it can be split into a provider-trained basis pinned in VRAM plus a per-user rank-1 residual; and it can be pointed at a problem that is not model adaptation at all, as a learned repair for a compressed KV cache. Running underneath all four is the property that decides whether any of them is deployable, and it is the strand's one replicated finding: mergeability is a property of the adapter being linear and of the base format being invertible, and both halves break — a nonlinear branch B·φ(Ax) has no weight-matrix equivalent at any precision, and codebook quantization formats cannot re-encode a merged low-rank product (AQLM cannot merge at all; torchao is silently wrong outside LoRA with int8_weight_only). So the founding claim that adapters add no inference tax survives only as a conditional, and the practitioner form is a decision made before choosing an architecture: if you will merge and ship a quantized checkpoint, a nonlinear adapter and an aggressive quantizer are both disqualified; if you serve hot-swappable artifacts off one frozen base, the trade is free and the per-tenant economics become the thing to optimize. The strand's standing defect, inherited and unfixed: not one source here reports a latency or memory number for the unmerged path on a consumer card, against a measured 76% higher inference latency for an unmerged adapter in the one place the vault has measured it.

Read against rank-one-lora-fails-where-rank-four-works, where a plain r=1 LoRA scored 11.03% on MultiArith, this map's rank floor looks more and more like a property of the initialization than of the capacity — the same conclusion an-adapter-splits-into-a-shared-basis-and-a-private-residual reached from the serving side. AuroOFT is the cost. It bolts a gated nonlinear low-rank residual onto a quantized orthogonal rotation over NF4 and gains 1.30–2.70 Macro-6 points over matched qoft — and in doing so establishes that mergeability requires the adapter to be linear, on every base format, since no matrix reproduces B·φ(Ax). adapters-can-merge-away-inference-latency now carries two independent narrowings. The paper prices none of it — no memory, no latency, no throughput — and its headline 15.5-point margin over QLoRA, produced without a learning-rate sweep, is exactly the artifact adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact described; the vault carries the matched-branch 1.30–2.70 and discards the rest. One credit: it is the first adapter paper here in five to run only on a quantized base, which was this map's standing complaint — and it then omits the memory numbers that were the reason to want it. Ripples to context-extension (the 39–65× prompt inflation a system-prompt skill costs per query), distillation-small-models (a KL objective whose teacher and student share weights), local-inference (a 20 KiB hot-swappable task artifact at 0 ms, against 10 MiB adapters) and post-training-quantization (an unmergeable branch over NF4). Open questions:

  • What does an unmerged adapter actually cost on one consumer card? The whole strand turns on this number and nobody reports it. four-bit-fine-tuning-buys-vram-with-time-and-energy measured 76% higher inference latency for an unmerged path in one setting; every source here that crosses the mergeability line — a nonlinear branch, a generated adapter, a swapped residual — declines to measure it. One benchmark, one 7B base at 4 bits, merged versus unmerged, tokens/second and peak memory, would price the entire trade.
  • Is the external-operator result a substrate or a single study? task-knowledge-has-a-third-place-to-live is the strand's most consequential claim and rests on one paper, whose interface must be trained per backbone at a cost it never states, and whose operators are built in one model's residual space with cross-backbone transfer unevaluated. A replication on a different model family — or a straight failure to reproduce the seven-of-eight matched-budget result — would move this map more than any new method.
  • Does the adapter-as-cache-repair economics generalize past its evictor? A rank-8 adapter trained for two hours on one card moves a benchmark by tens of points where nine tuned adapter variants move task quality by 1–2%. That contrast is the strand's sharpest argument that the target matters more than the method — but the repair does not transfer between evictors (42.0 against 73.2 when trained on one and run on another), so it may be a per-compressor artifact rather than a general use of adapters. Pointing the same recipe at a third problem that is not model adaptation would settle which.
  • Do any of these objects survive a quantized base? None of the five sources in this strand tests one, which is the same hole the parent map carries, and it is worse here: mergeability is defined by the base format, so a strand about serving economics that never quantizes the base is missing its own binding constraint.
  • Is a two-party protocol reachable for this vault's reader? The basis-plus-residual factorization needs somebody else serving a domain basis, and its gains grow with tenant count (+9.5% → +25.4% from 128 to 512 adapters). On one card with one user it buys nothing. Whether a single practitioner can self-host both halves and still net a win is unasked.
