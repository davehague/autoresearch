Status note (2026-09-02): holds consolidating, and the allocation-unit debate (AlphaQ: per-layer → GEMQ: global-over-every-expert) gets a third position instead of a resolution. q-strata-hierarchical-moe-bit-allocation (T1, Seoul National University) reframes "global vs. per-layer" as a bi-level program: a cheap proxy ranks within-block (per-expert-layer) assignments and caches a Pareto frontier per block, while a model-level objective evaluated on the assembled quantized model sets one budget per block across the whole model. Reported to beat uniform-bitwidth baselines on perplexity. This reads as a plausible answer to the cost problem GEMQ's own authors flagged — 191 GB peak GPU memory for the full global program, "potentially exceeding single-GPU capacity for larger MoEs" — by only paying the expensive model-level evaluation at block granularity rather than per-expert. But the abstract does not state whether Q-Strata's authors frame it that way or report a comparable memory figure, so this is an open question for the map, not a confirmed resolution, and needs a full-text read before grading Q-Strata's numbers against GEMQ's twelve-cell table. Graded extends, not pushes-back: it refines the granularity of "global" rather than contradicting GEMQ's directional claim. Status does not move. Separately, and from an unrelated substrate: post-training-quantization's new quantization-damage-should-be-spent-globally (dense models, granularity rather than per-expert bit-width) reaches the same directional conclusion as GEMQ — spend the marginal budget globally rather than via local/per-unit repair — independently, which this map should read as convergent support for "global" over "local" as the right scope, even while "what the allocation unit is within global scope" (expert? block? layer?) stays open. See moe-bit-allocation-can-be-solved-bi-level-within-and-across-blocks.

Status note (2026-08-26): holds consolidating, and the map's compression cells stop being answered one lever at a time. moexbench-composable-moe-compression (T1, University of Virginia + Sony AI; normalized module scores, compressed artifacts and reproducible scripts released) is the first systematic measurement of what happens when a practitioner does what deployment actually requires — prune experts, then quantize weights, then compress the KV cache — rather than measuring each in isolation. Ten MoE models from 30B to 235B total parameters across standard-attention, hybrid linear-attention and sliding-window architectures; seven workloads; 20–50% expert pruning, 1–16 bit weight quantization, multiple KV precisions, individually and combined; H100 and Apple M1 Max. The headline is a non-result about a shortcut: composite quality cannot be predicted from the standalone techniques, and the compression rate does not predict the loss — two configurations at the same ~75–80% compression land in different places. The ordering is stable and useful: expert pruning dominates degradation, low-bit weight precision is second, KV compression contributes mainly memory — which gives a spending order (cache first, then precision, then experts last) that is the reverse of the order the levers are usually reached for, since idle experts look free. Two findings push against assumptions this map held implicitly. Pruning tolerance is architecture-governed, not scale-governed: a 235B model takes a 54% perplexity increase where one 6.71× smaller takes 31%, and hybrid linear-attention MoEs are systematically more robust than standard-attention ones (72.15 vs 65.13 aggregate under the harshest setting) — a checkpoint-selection criterion available before any compression work, and the sparse-model version of at-two-bits-model-family-outranks-model-size. And average accuracy hides the damage: prune+quant scores 96.35 on average retention and 88.91 on workload robustness, with the drops concentrating on MMLU and HumanEval while GSM8K, MuSR and RULER stay stable, and coder-oriented variants degrading more than instruction-tuned ones. Two method riders join existing threads: the calibration dataset materially changes which capabilities survive pruning (the dependence quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model established for quantization, now measured for expert selection), and a pruning score that knows the packed MoE layout it will be composed with beats a module-level one. Status does not move. Perplexity plus seven benchmarks is the whole quality instrument, with none of the generation-length or reasoning-trace measurement this vault has repeatedly found to invert compression rankings; expert pruning is REAP throughout, so "pruning dominates" is one method's damage profile; the efficiency sweeps are batch size 1 on one H100 and one M1 Max with estimated rather than measured memory on the latter; and the smallest configuration measured, at 17.1 GB peak, only just fits a 24 GB card — nothing here is measured at a size that fits 8–16 GB, which is the standing complaint this map inherits from local-inference. The speculation/routing incompatibility reaches three independent groups in six days, and the third one moves it off the offload premise. edgexpert-moe-speculative-decoding-accelerator (T1, KAIST, four MoE models) measures the same effect on a 28nm accelerator with a 16 GB/s LPDDR4X channel and 512 KB of on-chip SRAM, where nothing is offloaded in the usual sense because nothing was ever resident — and it appears anyway. Qwen3 with an EAGLE-3 drafter: ~4.3 accepted tokens per stage costs 52.1 activated experts against a top-8 route, 6.5× the per-stage expert memory traffic, and 24.3% more energy than plain autoregressive decoding. So the claim should now be stated as a property of sparse routing under any bandwidth boundary rather than of offloading, and the energy figure is a currency neither prior source reported. Its own contribution is a lever the map did not have: both prior fixes manage the consequences of per-token routing (a cheaper drafter, a smaller eligible verifier set), while this one changes what routing is a function of — the prompt in prefill (a MiniLM encoder scores token importance, the important tokens define a shared expert set, the rest route into it at a reduced budget: 55.7% less prefill expert traffic at 1.0–3.1% accuracy) and the depth position in decode (same-depth candidates co-activate 3+ of 8 experts 88.4% of the time and are mutually exclusive, so load the union of salient channels once and let every candidate compute on all of it — spending arithmetic, which is free here, to buy traffic, which is not). Status does not move: a third replication plus an engineering result, no new claim about sparsity, and the paper is an ASIC design evaluated by simulator plus synthesis rather than on silicon. The prefill half needs no custom hardware and has never been tried on a GPU or under llama.cpp. Status note (2026-08-06): holds consolidating, and the map's newest finding is now replicated by an independent group on a production serving stack. acceptmoe-verifier-expert-set-sizing (T1, Imperial College London / Tile-AI) reaches the 2026-08-02 conclusion — that speculative decoding's "verify a block in one forward pass" is a dense-model property — by a different route and with the wall-time decomposition that explains it: standard speculative decoding on an offloaded MoE moves 2.3–2.8× more expert data per output token than not speculating at all, spends 65–78% of wall time stalled on expert fetches, and is slower than plain autoregressive decoding on seven of eight Qwen model–task pairs. Its own contribution is a fix from the opposite side of the pipeline: where the earlier source made the drafter cheaper (a resident distilled draft expert), this one shrinks what the verifier is allowed to touch, sizing the eligible expert set per verification block from the effective rank of commitment-weighted router demand and then pruning it against the current expert cache rather than a predicted route — 2.06× over standard speculative decoding under offloading on an RTX 5090, 1.290× fully resident, host-to-device traffic −73.6% to −77.1%, cache hit rate +10.8 to +14.2 points, at 0.27 points of mean accuracy. Two things it adds that the map should carry. It is not exact — masking the target's router logits changes the model distribution, and unlike lossy verification the alteration is in the target's own forward pass, so no acceptance rule can correct it. And the number the authors do not headline is the most decision-relevant one here: standard speculative decoding wins on all four GPT-OSS pairs, whose offloaded experts are packed MXFP4, and loses on seven of eight Qwen pairs, whose experts are BF16 — which, if it survives a controlled test, means quantization is the precondition that makes speculation profitable on an offloaded MoE rather than a competitor for the same headroom. Status note (2026-08-02): holds consolidating, and the map's two open cells — MoE × quantization's speed half and MoE × speculative decoding — both closed on one night, from sources that do not cite each other and that reach the same architectural conclusion by opposite routes. gemq-global-expert-mixed-precision-moe (T1, ICML 2026, code released) supplies the throughput number alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation could not — 1.4–1.6× faster decode at batch size 1, Mixtral-8×7B at 12.0 GB and 88.6 tok/s where FP16 does not fit an H100 at all — and pushes back on that source's structural claim: expert sensitivity varies within a layer as well as across, so a global program over experts beats a layer-local one in all twelve cells. What we believe now:

  • A frozen controller can pick the per-layer expert budget at inference time, and the allocation function transfers to a task it was never trained on (new 2026-08-31). MetaNet keeps the trained router and backbone frozen and trains a small external controller that reads a task's support set and predicts, per layer, an expert-retention threshold and a bounded routing bias, applied at inference. On DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Chat this cuts average active experts 40% (conservative: 3.61 experts) at 0.489 MMLU accuracy vs 0.474 uncompressed — not a quality-compute trade in this cell — or 62% (aggressive: 2.28 experts) at ~3.7 points lower accuracy. Trained only on MMLU support sets, the controller transfers zero-shot to C-Eval, still cutting active experts 52% (2.90 experts vs a fixed k=6 baseline) at 0.386 accuracy — the allocation function generalizes across tasks even though every prior compression entry on this map commits to one static budget at conversion time. Missing: a comparison against a static per-layer threshold matched for the same reduction (so task-conditioning's own contribution is unisolated), and any controller-overhead number.   • Shared experts get sharded across GPUs too, not just replicated (new 2026-08-31). vLLM v0.28.0 ships shared-expert sharding — splitting an MoE's always-active shared expert's weights across devices instead of holding a full copy on every GPU — saving ~17 GiB/GPU on multi-device deployments. Pure memory accounting, no reported throughput trade, and a datacenter-scale finding: it needs multiple GPUs to shard across, so a single-consumer-card reader gets nothing from it directly. Included as a second, independent instance of this map's residency-over-compute theme. [T2] (shared-expert-sharding-saves-17gib-per-gpu-in-vllm)
  • Composable MoE compression is not the sum of its levers, and the compression rate does not predict the loss (new 2026-08-26). Stacking expert pruning, weight quantization and KV-cache compression — the sequence deployment actually requires — produces quality outcomes unpredictable from any of them measured alone: at ~75–80% compression one weight format stays near the quantization-only perplexity while a more aggressive one inflates it sharply at similar or larger size reduction. The ordering of damage is stable: expert pruning dominates, low-bit precision is second, KV compression contributes mainly memory, which gives a spending order of cache → precision → experts, the reverse of how the levers are usually reached for. KV precision has a knee at 8 bits — q8 preserves short-context accuracy in every configuration (score 93.0–94.4) while q4 and TurboQuant variants cost more, and the same cache compression is more damaging on an already-pruned model, a composition effect invisible to kv-cache-management because everything there is measured on dense checkpoints. And average accuracy hides it: 96.35 average retention against 88.91 workload robustness, with the loss concentrating on MMLU and HumanEval while GSM8K, MuSR and RULER hold. Perplexity plus seven benchmarks, REAP as the only pruner, models ≥30B total parameters. (composable-moe-compression-is-not-the-sum-of-its-levers, T1, 2026-08-26.)
  • A bigger expert pool does not buy more slack to prune; the architecture does (new 2026-08-26). Under identical prune-plus-quantize settings a 235B model takes a 54% perplexity increase while one 6.71× smaller takes 31%, and one large model stays fragile across the whole pruning sweep while two smaller ones degrade gradually. Hybrid linear-attention MoEs are systematically more robust than standard-attention MoEs under extreme compression (72.15 vs 65.13 aggregate), which is a checkpoint-selection criterion a practitioner can apply before doing any compression work. Coder-oriented variants inflate perplexity more than instruction-tuned ones under comparable pruning. This is the sparse-model instance of at-two-bits-model-family-outranks-model-size, and it sits beside a result from the cache side of the same question — cache-capacity-is-set-by-the-attention-architecture-before-the-cache-policy — to make one point twice: the attention design of the checkpoint constrains what compression can do to it more than the compression method does. REAP is the only pruner tested and the 235B row is single-configuration. (pruning-tolerance-is-architecture-governed-not-scale-governed, T1, 2026-08-26.)
  • Cacheability is trainable, it costs quality, and its real payoff is that it makes a second, training-free lever almost free (new 2026-08-23, and it is the first pre-registered negative result this map has carried). Every locality technique here so far has been an inference-time edit to somebody else's router — prefetch (expert-routing-is-predictable-one-layer-ahead), condition on residency (ask-the-cache-not-the-router-when-experts-are-offloaded), nudge draft tokens onto experts in flight (the-expert-union-is-a-decision-variable-not-a-cost-to-schedule-around). Training the router to be cache-friendly works mechanically — an auxiliary loss asking adjacent tokens to route alike cuts LRU misses 59–60% on both seeds, and a domain-confinement loss reaches a 99.1% static-pin hit rate — and fails a quality gate the author wrote down in advance: +2.1–3.1% perplexity against a ≤1% bar, +3.1% against a ≤2% bar. The frontier "trades at a rate that excludes the {≥30%, ≤1%} corner." The scale hypothesis inverts: at 340M the tax rises, +2.0% → +2.5%. But the composition is the finding — running training-free cache-aware rerouting on the locality-trained model costs +0.5–0.9% perplexity for 80–82% miss reduction, against +0.8–5.8% for 30–50% on the baseline, a 5–8× cheaper exchange rate attributed to experts co-adapting into substitutable neighbourhoods. Together: ~80% miss reduction at ≤3.4% perplexity, cheaper than either alone. So trained locality failed as a product and succeeded as a substrate. The reconciliation with concurrent StickyMoE (identical loss, reported near-free at −4.1% perplexity on a 22M single-domain model) is a scope condition this map should keep: routing stickiness is cheap within one domain and expensive across several, and the same study measures code's expert set as nearly disjoint from the others (Jaccard 0.11–0.16). Bounded hard — 137M and 340M, 200M tokens, one author, one consumer GPU.   • The expert-union tax this map has measured four times is partly self-inflicted, because routing is a knob and not a given (new 2026-08-19; the finding lives on speculative-decoding and this map owns the routing half of it). Every prior arrival here — offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass, speculation-multiplies-the-expert-union-not-just-the-tokens, verified-tokens-and-activated-experts-are-different-costs — treated the union of experts a batch of draft tokens touches as a property of the model to be scheduled around. It is not. Two of this map's own standing facts make it editable: expert importance is skewed, so beyond the top-ranked few many candidates carry comparable routing scores and are functionally interchangeable (fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy, fewer-active-experts-means-less-tolerance-for-expert-substitution for the limit), and draft-time routing predicts target routing well enough to serve as a pre-verification cost proxy — the same predictability expert-routing-is-predictable-one-layer-ahead and ask-the-cache-not-the-router-when-experts-are-offloaded exploit for prefetching, used here for pricing instead. Nudging borderline experts toward ones already in flight cuts the reuse ratio 0.6017 → 0.4453 on HumanEval and 0.5893 → 0.3943 on GSM8K, and turns edge configurations that ran slower than autoregressive decoding into 1.3–5.3× speedups. The price is that the router is no longer the model's: top-1 output agreement against the unmodified target is 89.1–93.0%. So this map now holds a second instance of its recurring pattern — a routing edit made for systems reasons changes which tokens the model produces — alongside quantizing-a-moe-reroutes-its-tokens-and-the-router-can-be-retrained and a-route-flip-is-detectable-and-its-sign-is-not.   • (12) How much a model tolerates inexact routing scales with how many experts it activates — so the sparsest models are the ones every corner-cutting technique here damages most (new 2026-08-17). Serve with whatever experts happen to be resident instead of stalling for the routed ones, at one confidence threshold: Granite-1B (k=8) loses 0.5 average points across ARC/MMLU/WinoGrande/TruthfulQA, Granite-3B (k=8) 0.8, DeepSeek-V2-Lite-16B (k=6 + 2 shared) 0.8 — and Phi-mini-MoE-7B (k=2) loses 4.2, with 1.2 perplexity, rising to 6.0 points at a looser threshold. The distinguishing variable is not size, family or expert count (32/40/64/16 experts, no ordering) but k. The mechanism is arithmetic: a layer's output is a weighted combination of k experts, so a wrong one perturbs roughly 1/k of it, and a well-ordered predictor puts its mistakes on the lowest-weighted members — which helps when k is 8 and cannot when k is 2. That is a prediction about every approximate-routing entry on this map, testable with the instrument the map already has (a-route-flip-is-detectable-and-its-sign-is-not): run one approximation across a k sweep and check whether damage goes as 1/k. The deployment reading is uncomfortable for this vault's premise — low-k sparsity is what makes a MoE attractive on one card, and it is exactly what removes the slack that makes serving it fast. The source's own guidance agrees: exact routing is the default for low-k models, approximation is opt-in. Bounded: four models, one point each rather than a sweep, and every number is from a synthesis-calibrated simulator, not hardware. [T1, fully simulated] See fewer-active-experts-means-less-tolerance-for-expert-substitution.
  • (11) Routing is cheap in seconds and expensive in nats, and the damage it carries is detectable but not repairable (new 2026-08-13, and it is this map's first entry about damage rather than cost). Since 2026-07-24 the map has held, on node-level decode-graph timing, that routing is under 9% of MoE-block time — which is why the edge deficit reads as structural rather than as a kernel awaiting optimization. That is a statement about where the seconds go. Under compression, a causal four-run decomposition says roughly a third of the added loss goes through the routing decision: route-mediated fraction 0.31 [0.20, 0.41] on OLMoE-1B-7B under a 4-bit KV cache with routers held in BF16, five-process replication 0.313 ± 0.020, pre-registered held-out read 0.429 [0.330, 0.519], with an INT8 near-null control at −0.00006. The two paths do not separate cleanly — the compute×route interaction is +0.0083 nats [+0.0039, +0.0124] — so this is a decomposition at one dose rather than a transferable budget. The scope condition it exposes is the sharpest thing here: every MoE-quantization result on this map compresses expert weights and leaves the router in full precision, so all of them measure a system whose routing is intact; quantizing the cache perturbs the hidden states the router reads, which is the shortest path to a flipped route, and nobody has run the two literatures together. Then the paper pre-registers the obvious exploit and kills it. A flip is easy to detect — router margin AUC 0.772 — and its sign is not learnable: margin 0.490, logistic 0.522, MLP 0.504, gradient-boosted trees 0.515, route history 0.522, a cross-layer 80-dimensional router vector 0.512, all inside a falsification band declared before measurement, with P(harmful | flip) = 0.572. Good and bad flips cancel, so an oracle gate recovers 8.9% [6.4, 11.4] against a rate-matched random gate's 8.4% [5.7, 11.1] and the deployable gate scores below random. That matches post-training-quantization's cost-matched sweep, where five weight-space repairs including a random control lost to one more bit — two mechanisms, one rule: spend the bit, don't build the detector. Discounted throughout: simulated primary quantizer (real int4 anchor 0.194 [−0.111, 0.394], underpowered), NLL only with no downstream benchmark, pilot scale, single author, no released artifact, and the one model in the set with norm_topk_prob=True reads a null.   • (10) The speculation/routing incompatibility is a property of any bandwidth boundary, not of offload — and the fix nobody had tried is to stop routing per token (new 2026-08-07, generalizing (8) and (9)). The map recorded the incompatibility twice under expert offload, where the natural reading was that the problem belonged to PCIe. A third measurement lands it on a 28nm accelerator with a 16 GB/s LPDDR4X channel and 512 KB of SRAM — too small to hold even one 4–8 MB expert, so nothing is "offloaded" and everything streams. The effect is unchanged: ~4.3 accepted tokens per stage activate 52.1 experts against a top-8 route, 6.5× the per-stage expert traffic, and 24.3% more energy than not speculating. The arithmetic shows the two terms are coupled by construction — weight traffic per accepted token goes as (P_total − S × P_expert) / N_A, and raising the accepted count N_A disperses the routes, driving the untouched-expert count S down. Three groups, three cost models (PCIe transfer time, wall-clock fetch stall, DRAM picojoules), one conclusion; the energy figure is new and matters most, since moe-inference-cost-tracks-total-not-active-params-on-edge already had an MoE paying 2.1× energy per token against a same-active dense model on a Jetson. The new lever is orthogonal to both earlier fixes, which take routing as given: route the prompt, not the token. A small bidirectional encoder scores prompt tokens by CLS attention (bidirectional deliberately — the target's own causal mask is a worse importance estimator), the major tokens define a shared expert set, and minor and trivial tokens get more candidates but fewer activations, preferring experts already in the set: 55.7% less prefill expert traffic at under 1% accuracy loss, worst case 1.0–3.1% across five benchmarks, acceptance length unchanged. In decode the same idea is indexed by depth: same-depth candidates co-activate 3+ of 8 experts 88.4% of the time and only one survives, so load the union of salient channels once and let every candidate compute on all of it — extra arithmetic, no extra memory. Two things to carry. The prefill half is pure software (an encoder, a three-way token split, a shared set) and has never been run on a GPU or under llama.cpp, where expert loading is 87% of profiled decode time on an RTX 4090. And this sits awkwardly with experts-specialize-by-syntax-not-semantics: a shared set derived from semantically important tokens has no obvious reason to cover the syntactic routes the rest need, and yet it does at 1–3% cost — either routes are far more concentrated within one prompt than that finding implies, or the reduced budget is doing the work. Unseparated. Reachability caveat, and it is the strongest yet on this map: the latency and energy headlines (up to 56.3% and 44.1% against MoE-Pruner, EdgeMoE and SMoLPU at a common 28nm/800 MHz/16 GB/s normalization) come from an instruction-level simulator plus synthesis of a chip that has not been fabricated.   • (9) Under offloading, "how many tokens" and "how many experts" and "how many bytes" are three different costs, and the third one is a function of the cache rather than of the model (new 2026-08-06, replicating and extending (8)). Draft-side pruning optimizes the first: a prior method removes 74.7% of verified tokens and only 32.5% of activated experts, because each draft-tree node routes independently and verification must load their union. Measured host-to-device traffic per output token makes the consequence concrete — vanilla autoregressive decoding 888 / 721 / 439 MiB, standard speculative decoding 2394 / 2025 / 1243 for Qwen3-Instruct / Qwen3-Coder / GPT-OSS-120B, i.e. speculating moves 2.3–2.8× more expert data than not speculating, and 65–78% of wall time is fetch stall. The fix that pays is not a better predictor but a cheaper question: rank eligible experts by router demand weighted by the offline-estimated probability that a node at that draft position reaches the accepted output, size the set from the effective rank of that demand (so concentrated demand yields a small set and diffuse demand a large one, with the root token's natural top-k always anchored), then prune non-resident members against the current LRU cache. Traffic −73.6% to −77.1%, hit rate +10.8 to +14.2 points, fetch stall 65–78% → 27–35%, 2.06× over standard speculative decoding offloaded and 1.290× resident (up to 257 tok/s), at 0.27 points of mean accuracy over 12 model–task pairs. Three readings this map should keep. Residency conditioning is the complement of prefetching, not a rival: expert-routing-is-predictable-one-layer-ahead answers "what will I need, so I can start early" and needs lead time; this answers "of what I could use, what is free" and needs none — and it is available only because a verifier has slack about which experts it uses, which a decode step does not. Self-sizing beats a borrowed sweep by an order of magnitude: the closed-form size lands 0.97 points below the best measured fixed budget, while transferring one model's swept budget to another costs 9.1 points. And it is not exact — this belongs with lossy-verification-silently-replaces-the-target-distribution, and is worse in kind, because the target's own forward pass is altered. Bounded: batch size 1, three targets at 30B and 120B, one offloading configuration, byte counts not comparable across the two families (BF16 vs packed MXFP4 experts), no composition with the resident-draft-expert approach that attacks the same stall from the drafter side.   • (8) When the experts are offloaded, the accounting unit is the expert and not the FLOP — which inverts two standard levers and adds one that pays in a different currency (new 2026-08-02). The parameter asymmetry that makes offload the sane deployment is stark: on DeepSeek-V2-Lite routed experts are 91.6% of the weights (14.4B of 15.7B, 26.8 GiB in BF16) against 2.4 GiB for everything exercised on every forward pass, and each token touches top-6 of 64. Keep the small part resident, stream the rest, and expert movement becomes 87% of profiled decode time on an RTX 4090 and 49% on a Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU. Open questions:

  • Does expert-weight quantization move the routes too? (new 2026-08-13, and it is the gap the night's own scope condition opens.) Route-mediated damage was measured with the compression on the cache and the router in BF16. Every mixed-precision allocator on this map does the reverse — compresses expert weights, leaves the router and the cache alone — so none of them could have observed a routing effect, and each reports quality as though the route were fixed. Running the same four-run apparatus with the disturbance moved to 2-bit routed experts would say whether the map's allocation literature has been measuring a partly different failure mode from the one it names. The apparatus is described in full and the models are open; what is missing is the artifact, which the author says is coming.
  • Does the route-flip result survive norm_topk_prob=True? (new 2026-08-13.) Reference recovery is strongly positive on the two models with the flag False and null on Qwen3-30B-A3B with it True. The headline was therefore measured on a routing-normalization convention that the newest and largest model in the set does not share, and whether route-mediated damage is a property of quantized MoEs or of a normalization choice is unresolved at n = 3. It is a config value, which makes this the cheapest follow-up on the map.
  • Does prompt-wise expert reuse work on hardware anyone owns? (new 2026-08-07, and it is the cheapest premise-relevant run this map has ever carried.) The 2026-08-07 ingest's prefill fix — score prompt tokens with a small bidirectional encoder, let the important ones define a shared expert set, route the rest into it at a reduced budget — is ordinary software: a MiniLM encoder, a three-way token split, and a modified routing call. It bought 55.7% less prefill expert traffic at 1.0–3.1% accuracy on a custom accelerator, and no in-vault source has run it on a GPU or under llama.cpp, where local-inference measured expert loading at 87% of profiled decode time on an RTX 4090 and 49% on a Snapdragon NPU. The version worth running first is the offload case: does a prompt-level shared expert set raise the LRU hit rate the way ask-the-cache-not-the-router-when-experts-are-offloaded raised it from the verifier side? One card, one afternoon, and it is the first fix on this thread that does not need trained weights or a fabricated chip.
  • Does quantizing the experts make speculative decoding profitable on an offloaded MoE? (new 2026-08-06, and it is the cleanest cheap experiment this map has ever carried.) The 2026-08-06 ingest reports, without pursuing it, that standard speculative decoding loses to plain autoregressive decoding on seven of eight Qwen pairs, whose offloaded experts are BF16, and wins on all four GPT-OSS pairs, whose experts are packed MXFP4. The causal story is immediate — verification's cost under offload is bytes over PCIe, and halving the bytes halves the penalty — and it would invert speculation-and-quantization-compete-for-the-same-headroom for exactly the regime this vault's readers are in. As published the precision is confounded with the model, and the fix is one variable: take a single MoE target, run standard speculative decoding under offload with BF16 experts and again with 4-bit experts, and report the fetch-stall share. One card, one afternoon, and it decides whether "quantize the experts" is a footprint lever or the thing that unlocks the latency lever.
  • Quantized MoE that fits versus dense model that fits, on one 12 GB card (new 2026-08-02, and it is now the sharpest premise-relevant question on this map). mixed-precision-moe-finally-has-a-throughput-number puts Mixtral-8×7B at 12.0 GB and 88.6 tok/s, which is the exact card mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference used to demonstrate that Mixtral could be run at 2–3 tok/s by offloading and caching experts. If an aggressively quantized build fits outright, the entire offload apparatus is unnecessary for that model, and the choice a 12 GB owner faces changes shape. The unrun comparison is 1.5-bit Mixtral against a dense 7B–8B at 4 bits on the same card, on GSM8K and MMLU, at matched footprint — the head-to-head the offloading paper also failed to run, now with a second contender. Everything needed is public except the kernels, which is the catch below.
  • Does the global expert allocation work from a calibration-free signal? (new 2026-08-02.) The two MoE-quantization sources disagree about the allocation unit and differ on cost: moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity scores layers from weight spectra with no data at all, sidestepping the calibration confound; expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer scores experts from 128 calibration sequences and demonstrates that dependence biting (GSM8K moves 10.5 points on a calibration swap). The obvious hybrid — a global integer program over experts, scored by the calibration-free spectral criterion — has not been tried, and both halves are published with code or full method descriptions. It would say whether the win comes from the unit, the scope, or the data.
  • Nothing in either result is reachable from the local stack (new 2026-08-02, and this is now the standing complaint on two maps at once). The quantization throughput numbers depend on custom fused Triton kernels for 1/2/3-bit dequantization plus grouped GEMM; the offloaded-speculation result depends on a trained per-layer draft expert with no released weights or code. llama.cpp's K-quant menu still cannot express per-expert bit assignment, and no local runtime ships an expert-level speculative decoder. So this map now holds two demonstrations that the speed exists and no path for a reader to run either. What would close it is not a paper: it is a GGUF-side representation for per-tensor mixed expert precision, which is a tooling question this vault can only track.
  • Does the verification-expansion curve hold on a coarse-grained MoE? (new 2026-08-02.) offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass is measured on two models with 64 routed experts and top-6 routing. The expansion rate depends on how widely a block's tokens disperse across experts, so an 8-expert top-2 model (Mixtral — this vault's other offload substrate) should expand far more slowly, and speculative decoding under offload might still pay there without a trained drafter. Unmeasured, and it is a one-afternoon profiling run on hardware the vault already has evidence about.
  • MoE × PEFT is the tutor rung's sharpest unanswered question. ST-MoE's freeze-the-experts result says the dense skeleton is where fine-tuning wants to act — but it used full-rank updates, never adapters. Whether LoRA on the dense subset (or on experts) behaves, whether the peft-methods rank findings transfer, and how routing shifts under fine-tuning are all unmeasured in-vault.
  • MoE × quantization: RESOLVED 2026-07-25 for footprint, open for everything else (alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation). Answered: layers within an MoE do differ in quantization sensitivity, the difference is readable from the weights alone, and uneven allocation buys real accuracy at a fixed budget — most on fine-grained architectures. What replaces the gap is sharper. Speed is entirely unmeasured — the source reports no latency, throughput or energy, and quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill establishes that footprint and speed move independently, so "4× smaller" says nothing yet about tokens per second on the devices local-inference cares about. The shared expert question is still open: DeepSeekMoE's lesion test shows disabling the shared expert is catastrophic (1.808 → 2.414), which predicts it deserves protection, and no source allocates bits by that structural role — though 2026-07-26 lands the coarse version of exactly that: holding the shared path (attention, embeddings, gates) at 4-bit while crushing experts to 2–3 bits costs 3.85 MMLU points, whereas crushing the shared path instead costs 11.5 from a thirtieth of the parameters (shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts). Quantized and offloaded: RESOLVED 2026-07-26 (mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference), with the honest qualifier that the quality evidence there is perplexity and MMLU only. And what the GGUF path does with routed FFNs at mixed precision remains unserved — llama.cpp's K-quant menu does not express per-layer allocation of this kind, so the most useful result here is currently unreachable from the local stack.
  • MoE × local inference: RESOLVED 2026-07-26, and the two answers disagree in an instructive way (revised 2026-07-26). What llama.cpp does with routed FFNs when everything is resident is answered — it runs them, slower per token than the active count promises, the deficit widening as the device tightens. Expert offload/caching is now also answered (mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference: LRU expert cache plus residual-stream speculative prefetch, 2–3 tok/s for Mixtral-8×7B on a free-tier T4, 3.2× over naive offloading), and it reaches the opposite practical conclusion because a different term binds. What replaces the gap: nobody has run the two configurations against each other on one device — same card, same model, resident-at-low-precision versus offloaded-at-higher-precision — which is the actual choice a 12 GB owner faces and which neither paper poses. Sub-questions the resident study raises and does not answer remain open: Two sub-questions the edge study raises and does not answer: the 91% of MoE-block time that is not routing is never decomposed into weight traffic vs expert-dispatch gather/scatter vs KV pressure — only the first is irreducible — and the whole result rests on one total-to-active ratio (6.9B/1.3B), so whether a fine-grained + shared-expert design (fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy), which exists partly to change this trade, lands differently is untested (local-inference).
  • The Google trio is lineage-related (shared authors, shared codebase) — like the FSDP/ZeRO pair in distributed-training, what replicates across them is engineering practice, not independent derivation. The independent replications are Mistral and DeepSeek — both of which keep their training recipes closed (Mixtral discloses no data or recipe; DeepSeek's baselines are self-reimplemented).   • Expert-choice routing is now used-but-not-studied in-vault (2026-07-22, revised 2026-07-28): sparse upcycling routes by Expert Choice in its encoders, so the vault has a source that depends on it without one that evaluates it against top-k. Aux-loss-free balancing closed 2026-07-28 (deepseek-v3-technical-report), and the frontier recipe is no longer a generation ahead of this map. What it opens is more interesting than what it closed. Nobody has isolated the specialization claim: the domain-peaked heat maps are qualitative, and the experiment that would settle it is the one DeepSeekMoE already ran for shared experts — lesion the domain-peaked experts and measure the loss on that domain. Nobody has connected specialization to caching, which is the cell shared with local-inference: expert-routing-is-predictable-one-layer-ahead and the offloading result rest on route locality that was assumed syntactic and short-range, whereas domain-keyed routes should be far more stable across a single user's conversation — so is an aux-loss-free-trained MoE easier to cache than an aux-loss-trained one is cheap, unrun, and directly premise-relevant. And the batch-wise finding has never been tested below cluster scale, where neither of its two named remedies (large expert-parallel micro-batches, redundant-expert redeployment) is available. Still fully unserved: hash routing.
  • Upcycling is sourced (2026-07-22) but only in pretraining currency. Nothing tests it at fine-tuning-scale extra compute (1–5% rather than 20–100%), on an instruction-tuned rather than pretrained checkpoint, or with adapters instead of full updates — and upcycle-then-LoRA-the- dense-skeleton is now the sharpest untested cell shared with peft-methods. The evidence is also T5-XL (~3B) on a 2020 encoder-decoder, predating every decoder-only MoE this map tracks, so transfer to a fine-grained + shared-expert modern base is assumed rather than shown. And this is the fourth Google MoE paper in-vault — the lineage caveat below now covers most of the map.
  • What does upcycling do to specialization? Experts that begin as identical copies of one MLP must break symmetry through a randomly initialized router alone. Whether they end up specializing the way from-scratch experts do (experts-specialize-by-syntax-not-semantics) — or merely diverge enough to avoid collapse — is unmeasured, and it is the mechanism question underneath the whole result. It also bears on local-inference: expert offload/caching depends on route locality, and upcycled routers may not have the same locality profile.
  • The residency ceiling is a variable, so the conditional offload rule now has a step before it. The 2026-07-26 rule — if it fits, prefer dense at matched active params; if it does not, an MoE is the easier thing to offload — took the footprint as given. But 93% of a sparse model's parameters sit in the routed expert bank, which shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts identified as its least quantization-sensitive tissue, so a role-based split takes almost all the footprint and almost none of the damage: OLMoE-1B-7B goes fully resident at 4.061 GiB and 47.4% faster than its own BF16 baseline, for −0.546 aggregate points across 12,450 paired questions. Shrink the bank before you build a streaming system — the resident W4 point beats every offloaded configuration of the same model on both throughput and latency. Where it still will not fit, the offload literature stands and gains a systems finding: a matched-cache ablation says roughly half of an offloaded W4 runtime's throughput is grouped fused execution, not the bits (1.97×, identical weights, identical cache).
