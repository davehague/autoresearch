Mixture of experts (sparse architectures & routing) — map

Coined 2026-07-21 (auto run) directly at consolidating — the vault's first consumer-driven map: David's atlas-tutor session ai-eng-repo-gap-check queued it 2026-07-21 when the tutor's l22-moe rung came up empty, and the index named five candidate sources; all five were ingested in one run. Coining at consolidating rather than emerging is unusual and flagged for David's review — the justification is deterministic under the evidence model: five T1s from three independent lineages (Google Brain 2017/2021/2022 — lineage-related among themselves; Mistral 2024; DeepSeek 2024) converge on the core claim across LSTM and transformer substrates, encoder-decoder and decoder-only forms, and vendor-paper and open-weights-artifact evidence. This is a mature literature ingested in batch, not a fresh thread — the map's status reflects that.

Status: consolidating

What we believe now:

  • (1) The core decoupling: parameter count and per-token compute are separate dials. A routed set of expert FFNs behind a sparse top-k gate lets capacity scale without scaling what each token costs — >1000× capacity at minor efficiency loss (2017, LSTMs, 137B params), then the transformer form: at fixed FLOPs per token, more experts = faster pretraining (7× wall-clock to T5-Base quality; gains monotone 2→256 experts; 1.6T params trained stably). Quality tracks the parameter dial when data is large; too much sparsity degrades (131k experts < 65k experts). [T1, three groups]
  • (2) Routing is simpler than intuition suggests — and stays dumb at every scale. Top-1 suffices (Switch, falsifying the k≥2 conjecture); top-2 is the common production point (Mixtral); gains from top-(n+1) over top-n are ~1/20th of a dense-model tripling (ST-MoE). What routers learn is syntactic and positional, not semantic — no math expert, no French expert, near- uniform selection across content domains, replicated in two labs (Mixtral on Pile domains; ST-MoE token-type analysis). Consecutive tokens repeat experts far above chance (~28% vs 12.5% at depth) — exploitable for caching, hazardous for expert-parallel load balance. [T1, two groups independently]
  • (3) The failure modes are named and each has a standard fix. Routing collapse (favor is self-reinforcing) → auxiliary balance losses, kept deliberately small at the expert level and larger at the device level, because computation balance is what matters and over-constraining expert balance costs quality (DeepSeek). Numeric instability (routers add exponentials; bfloat16 roundoff flips top-k decisions) → float32 router (Switch) and the router z-loss, the one stabilizer measured to cost nothing (ST-MoE: 3/3 seeds stable, quality −1.755 → −1.741, vs update clipping's catastrophic −4.206). Token overflow (static expert-capacity buffers drop tokens through the residual) → capacity factor ~1.25 + the balance loss keeps drops <1%, and fine-tuning tolerates 10–15% drops anyway. [T1, three groups]
  • (4) Expert granularity and shared capacity are design dials, and the fine-grained end wins at matched cost. "8 experts pick 2" (Mixtral) and "1 shared + 7-of-63 quarter-size" (DeepSeekMoE) bracket the space; DeepSeek's ablations say segment finer + pin always-on shared experts → strictly better at constant params/FLOPs (Pile 1.808 vs GShard's 1.867), approaching the dense upper bound — the dense model with the same total params all activated (1.806) — within noise. Lesion-tested: routed experts become less redundant; the shared expert is irreplaceable (disable it: 1.808 → 2.414). [T1]
  • (5) MoE ships as open weights, and the wins survive contact. Mixtral 8x7B (47B total / 13B active, Apache 2.0): matches/beats Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 at ~5× fewer active params (MMLU 70.6 vs 69.9/70.0; GSM8K 74.4 vs 69.6), margins largest on math/code/multilingual; Instruct (SFT+DPO) above GPT-3.5/Claude-2.1/Gemini Pro on human LMSys Elo. DeepSeekMoE 16B ≈ LLaMA2 7B at ~40% FLOPs, released, runs on one 40GB GPU unquantized — and this architecture scaled is the substrate under DeepSeek-V2/V3/R1 (deepseek-r1-incentivizing-reasoning). [T1, two groups]
  • (6) The bill is memory and the fine-tuning protocol. Compute tracks the active count; serving memory tracks the total count (Mixtral's own accounting: 13B-active compute, 47B-param RAM; "more suitable for batched workloads") — for single-user local serving the trade bites hardest. And sparse models fine-tune differently: they overfit small tasks, want smaller batches + higher LR (opposite of dense), and updating only the non-expert dense skeleton matches updating everything while expert-only updates (~80% of params) drop ~3.5 SuperGLUE points (ST-MoE). Get the protocol wrong and the pretraining speedup evaporates downstream. [T1, two groups]

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-07-24 — ingest of moe-on-consumer-and-edge-hardware (Alfarizy et al., UNB / NRC Canada, arXiv preprint v3, T1): the map's first source that is not a lab result, and the first to test an MoE claim on the hardware this vault's premise is about. It refutes nothing in this map and measures a bill the map already recorded — a distinction worth holding, because the headline invites over-reading. Belief (1)'s decoupling is a pretraining-FLOPs claim and is untouched; what is tested is the inference corollary a local user would draw from it. On an Apple M2 Pro, OLMoE-1B-7B at Q4_K_M is 10% behind Llama-3.2-1B (same active count); on a Jetson Orin Nano it is 31% behind, also behind Qwen2.5-1.5B, at 2.1× energy per token and 8.01 GiB peak RSS against an 8 GB ceiling. The map already carried Mixtral's accounting — compute like the active count, RAM like the total — and already flagged that "more suitable for batched workloads" is exactly what single-user local serving is not; what is new is that on a bandwidth-bound device the total-parameter footprint does not merely cost RAM, it eats the throughput advantage too. The instrumented half is what makes this durable rather than a benchmark run: patching llama.cpp to wall-clock each decode-graph node puts routing under 9% of MoE-block time on the trustworthy backend, so the deficit is structural, not a kernel awaiting optimization. Status holds at consolidating — this is a boundary condition on where the sparsity dial pays, not a challenge to the dial. Closes the MoE × local inference gap below and co-moves local-inference to consolidating. Bounded hard: one MoE model at one total-to-active ratio, two devices, one llama.cpp tag, and naive full-residency serving with no expert offload — the remedy this map names is precisely what was not tested. See moe-inference-cost-tracks-total-not-active-params-on-edge, routing-is-not-what-makes-moe-slow-on-device.
  • 2026-07-22 — ingest of sparse-upcycling-moe-from-dense-checkpoints (Komatsuzaki, Puigcerver et al., Google Brain, ICLR 2023, T1): closes the map's last budget-shaped gap, named at coining as "the budget-relevant path for this vault's premise and has no source," and partially serves the Expert Choice routing item (it is the routing algorithm used throughout the encoders here). Every other source in this map is a pretraining result and therefore addressed to a lab; this is the first addressed to someone holding a checkpoint and a modest budget, which makes it the map's most premise-relevant entry. Three findings beyond the recipe: copying the MLP into the experts is load-bearing (randomly initialized experts never catch up, and partial upcycling also underperforms — the reused sunk cost is the MLP's learned weights specifically); there is an initial quality dip below the source checkpoint, deeper with more experts or more converted layers, which is why the defaults are tuned to the +20–100% extra-compute regime rather than the asymptote; and the crossover is measured — from-scratch MoE needs ~120% of the dense budget to catch up, so the rule is budget-conditional. Ripples to peft-methods (a third capacity move, priced in pretraining currency) and distributed-training (a mid-training capacity upgrade has a transient regression a naive checkpoint policy would misread as failure). See dense-checkpoints-can-be-upcycled-into-moe.

Open questions:

  • MoE × PEFT is the tutor rung's sharpest unanswered question. ST-MoE's freeze-the-experts result says the dense skeleton is where fine-tuning wants to act — but it used full-rank updates, never adapters. Whether LoRA on the dense subset (or on experts) behaves, whether the peft-methods rank findings transfer, and how routing shifts under fine-tuning are all unmeasured in-vault.
  • MoE × quantization has no source at all. Per-expert weight distributions (do rarely-routed experts quantize worse? does the shared expert deserve more bits?), whether AWQ/GPTQ calibration even sees rare experts' activations, and what the GGUF path does with routed FFNs — entirely open. Mixtral is the obvious test artifact (post-training-quantization).
  • MoE × local inference: the naive case is now measured, the interesting case is not (revised 2026-07-24). What llama.cpp does with routed FFNs on consumer hardware is answered — it runs them, and the model is slower per token than its active count promises, with the deficit widening as the device tightens. Expert offload/caching against the measured route locality (~28% consecutive-token repetition) is still unserved, and it is now the sharper question rather than the softer one, because the measured configuration deliberately holds everything resident. Two sub-questions the edge study raises and does not answer: the 91% of MoE-block time that is not routing is never decomposed into weight traffic vs expert-dispatch gather/scatter vs KV pressure — only the first is irreducible — and the whole result rests on one total-to-active ratio (6.9B/1.3B), so whether a fine-grained + shared-expert design (fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy), which exists partly to change this trade, lands differently is untested (local-inference).
  • The Google trio is lineage-related (shared authors, shared codebase) — like the FSDP/ZeRO pair in distributed-training, what replicates across them is engineering practice, not independent derivation. The independent replications are Mistral and DeepSeek — both of which keep their training recipes closed (Mixtral discloses no data or recipe; DeepSeek's baselines are self-reimplemented). No neutral third party has trained or compared these architectures.
  • Expert-choice routing is now used-but-not-studied in-vault (2026-07-22): sparse upcycling routes by Expert Choice in its encoders, so the vault has a source that depends on it without one that evaluates it against top-k. Still fully unserved: hash routing and DeepSeek-V3's aux-loss-free balancing; the modern frontier recipe (fine-grained + shared + no aux loss) remains one generation past what this map holds.
  • Upcycling is sourced (2026-07-22) but only in pretraining currency. Nothing tests it at fine-tuning-scale extra compute (1–5% rather than 20–100%), on an instruction-tuned rather than pretrained checkpoint, or with adapters instead of full updates — and upcycle-then-LoRA-the- dense-skeleton is now the sharpest untested cell shared with peft-methods. The evidence is also T5-XL (~3B) on a 2020 encoder-decoder, predating every decoder-only MoE this map tracks, so transfer to a fine-grained + shared-expert modern base is assumed rather than shown. And this is the fourth Google MoE paper in-vault — the lineage caveat below now covers most of the map.
  • What does upcycling do to specialization? Experts that begin as identical copies of one MLP must break symmetry through a randomly initialized router alone. Whether they end up specializing the way from-scratch experts do (experts-specialize-by-syntax-not-semantics) — or merely diverge enough to avoid collapse — is unmeasured, and it is the mechanism question underneath the whole result. It also bears on local-inference: expert offload/caching depends on route locality, and upcycled routers may not have the same locality profile.
