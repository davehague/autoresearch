Status: consolidating Status note (2026-08-02): holds consolidating, and the map's two open cells — MoE × quantization's speed half and MoE × speculative decoding — both closed on one night, from sources that do not cite each other and that reach the same architectural conclusion by opposite routes. gemq-global-expert-mixed-precision-moe (T1, ICML 2026, code released) supplies the throughput number alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation could not — 1.4–1.6× faster decode at batch size 1, Mixtral-8×7B at 12.0 GB and 88.6 tok/s where FP16 does not fit an H100 at all — and pushes back on that source's structural claim: expert sensitivity varies within a layer as well as across, so a global program over experts beats a layer-local one in all twelve cells. It also names a mechanism nobody in this vault had measured: quantization moves the router, over 40% of tokens rerouting at 1.5 bits, and re-fitting the router — under 0.04% of parameters, 3.5% of quantization time — is the largest single lever in the paper. draftexpert-self-speculative-moe-offload (T1, discounted: single author, no venue, no code) finds that under expert offload, speculative decoding's verification step stops costing one forward pass — 5.8× a token-decode at block length 16, against a flat dense control — and that the obvious training-free MoE self-draft is slower than not speculating at all (1.19 vs 2.19 tok/s). The status does not move because neither source contradicts a graded claim on this map's core decoupling; they close named gaps. The common thread is worth stating: in an offloaded or memory-bound MoE, the accounting unit is the expert, not the FLOP — GEMQ makes each expert cheaper to hold, DraftExpert makes each transfer buy more tokens, and both are levers the FLOPs-based framing of sparsity cannot see.

What we believe now:

  • (8) When the experts are offloaded, the accounting unit is the expert and not the FLOP — which inverts two standard levers and adds one that pays in a different currency (new 2026-08-02). The parameter asymmetry that makes offload the sane deployment is stark: on DeepSeek-V2-Lite routed experts are 91.6% of the weights (14.4B of 15.7B, 26.8 GiB in BF16) against 2.4 GiB for everything exercised on every forward pass, and each token touches top-6 of 64. Keep the small part resident, stream the rest, and expert movement becomes 87% of profiled decode time on an RTX 4090 and 49% on a Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU. Under that cost model: (a) longer speculation windows are counterproductive. A dense transformer verifies a K-token block in about one forward pass because it reuses the same weights; an offloaded MoE must load the union of every expert any drafted token routes to, so verification costs 2.4× at K=4 and 5.8× at K=16 against a flat dense control, touching 59% of all routed layer-experts. (b) The free version of MoE self-drafting is a net loss. Shared expert plus router top-r raises acceptance (22% → 31% → 42% for r=1,2,3) by loading more experts, so draft speedup collapses 4.7× → 2.7× → 2.0× and even top-3 stays under 50% acceptance — end to end 1.19 tok/s against an unspeculated 2.19 on a 4090. (c) What works is buying the accuracy in resident parameters instead of in transfers. One small self-distilled draft expert per layer, trained to reproduce the residual the top-1 path omits, reaches 85.0% acceptance while touching 88 unique experts against top-1's 84 — 1.45× average decode speedup, output exact, with the phone case (15.47 tok/s) landing past its own resident-memory reference (~14.36). And (d) router agreement is a separate training objective from token agreement: adding a router-KL term leaves acceptance unchanged (87.1 → 87.0) and moves prefetch hit rate 73% → 88%, so a better drafter is not automatically a better prefetcher. Bounded hard — single author, no venue, no code, two models of the same DeepSeek MoE shape, one GPU and one phone at different precisions, and the draft experts' own memory footprint is never reported. [T1, discounted] See offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass, a-distilled-draft-expert-buys-acceptance-without-buying-transfers, router-agreement-and-token-agreement-are-different-objectives.
  • (9) Mixed-precision expert quantization is global, not per-layer; it is fast as well as small; and it drags the router out of alignment on the way (new 2026-08-02, and it revises (7)). Three moves. Allocation unit: moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity concluded the natural unit is the layer because projections inside one expert score differently. Measuring sensitivity as the trace of the empirical Fisher instead, an ICML 2026 result finds it varies substantially within a layer too, and that a single global integer program over every expert — using a loss-increase estimate taken on the MoE layer output, which makes scores comparable across layers as per-layer reconstruction objectives are not — beats the layer-local formulation in all twelve model-budget cells on perplexity (Qwen3-30B-A3B at 1.5 bits: 12.16 vs 20.85). The two sources agree the scope must be global and disagree about the unit, and the global method pays for it by needing 128 calibration sequences where the spectral criterion needed none. Router shift: over 40% of tokens are routed to different experts after 1.5-bit quantization. Routers are under 0.04% of parameters; re-fitting them against task loss (128 sequences, one epoch, under a minute, 3.5% of the GPTQ time) takes Mixtral from 9.66 to 7.69 WikiText2 perplexity — while forcing the quantized router to imitate the full-precision router is worth almost nothing (9.29), because after the experts change the original routing has stopped being correct. Speed: batch-1 decode on one H100 is 1.4–1.6× faster than FP16, and Mixtral-8×7B goes from over 90 GB (OOM on that card) to 12.0 GB at 88.6 tok/s — which lands inside the 12 GB card mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference used to demonstrate offloading, making "aggressively quantized MoE that fits" a live alternative to "offloaded MoE." Bounded: custom Triton kernels unreachable from llama.cpp, seven commonsense zero-shot tasks (the least quantization-sensitive family), attention pinned at 4 bits, no offload configuration anywhere, and on Mixtral at 2.5 bits the uniform baseline still posts the best zero-shot accuracy of the three. [T1] See expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer, quantizing-a-moe-reroutes-its-tokens-and-the-router-can-be-retrained, mixed-precision-moe-finally-has-a-throughput-number.
  • (1) The core decoupling: parameter count and per-token compute are separate dials. A routed set of expert FFNs behind a sparse top-k gate lets capacity scale without scaling what each token costs — >1000× capacity at minor efficiency loss (2017, LSTMs, 137B params), then the transformer form: at fixed FLOPs per token, more experts = faster pretraining (7× wall-clock to T5-Base quality; gains monotone 2→256 experts; 1.6T params trained stably). Quality tracks the parameter dial when data is large; too much sparsity degrades (131k experts < 65k experts). [T1, three groups]
  • (2) Routing is simpler than intuition suggests — and what it learns depends on how you balance it (revised 2026-07-28). Top-1 suffices (Switch, falsifying the k≥2 conjecture); top-2 is the common production point (Mixtral); gains from top-(n+1) over top-n are ~1/20th of a dense-model tripling (ST-MoE). Consecutive tokens repeat experts far above chance (~28% vs 12.5% at depth) — exploitable for caching, hazardous for expert-parallel load balance. The semantic half of this belief is now conditional. It was: routers learn syntax and position, not semantics — no math expert, no French expert, near-uniform selection across content domains, replicated in two labs. Both of those labs trained with sequence-wise auxiliary balance losses, which require each sequence to spread its tokens evenly across experts — and since a sequence is usually one domain, that constraint forbids the specialization they went looking for. Balance over the batch instead and domain structure appears: expert-load heat maps over Wikipedia, Github and DM Mathematics are near-flat for the aux-loss-based model and show distinct per-domain peaks at up to ~10× the balanced load for the batch-balanced one, sharpest on maths. Reframe rather than retire: the new evidence is qualitative heat maps at two layers over three domains from a vendor report, with no lesion test showing the specialized experts are individually necessary — the standard this map applies elsewhere via fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy — against two prior quantitative analyses. What the map can state: the degree of semantic specialization is a property of the balancing scheme, and the labs that measured its absence measured models trained under the constraint that suppresses it. [T1, two groups for the syntax finding; one for the conditionality]
  • (3) The failure modes are named and each has a standard fix — and for load balancing the fix is about scope, not about the loss (revised 2026-07-28). Routing collapse (favor is self-reinforcing) → auxiliary balance losses, kept deliberately small at the expert level and larger at the device level, because computation balance is what matters and over-constraining expert balance costs quality (DeepSeek). The frontier recipe replaces that loss with a per-expert bias term added to the affinity score for the top-K selection only and excluded from the gating weight that scales the expert's output, nudged down for overloaded experts and up for underloaded ones each step — balanced well enough that no tokens are dropped in training or inference, which retires the overflow mode outright. It is named "auxiliary-loss-free," and its own ablation says the name misattributes the mechanism: a batch-wise auxiliary loss matches it exactly (validation loss 2.253 vs 2.253 at 1B, 2.080 vs 2.080 at 3B) while the sequence-wise version costs 2.258 and 2.085. So auxiliary losses do not corrupt the gradient; a per-sequence balance constraint is simply too strong, and the bias method's real contribution is that it balances over a batch by construction. Portable rule for anyone training an MoE by any mechanism: balance over the largest scope your parallelism allows. The two costs of batch scope are named and both are handled at cluster scale only — imbalance inside small batches (absorbed by large expert-parallel micro-batches) and domain-shift imbalance at inference (absorbed by duplicating high-load experts and re-planning the redundant set every ~10 minutes). Numeric instability (routers add exponentials; bfloat16 roundoff flips top-k decisions) → float32 router (Switch) and the router z-loss, the one stabilizer measured to cost nothing (ST-MoE: 3/3 seeds stable, quality −1.755 → −1.741, vs update clipping's catastrophic −4.206). Token overflow (static expert-capacity buffers drop tokens through the residual) → capacity factor ~1.25 + the balance loss keeps drops <1%, and fine-tuning tolerates 10–15% drops anyway. [T1, three groups]
  • (4) Expert granularity and shared capacity are design dials, and the fine-grained end wins at matched cost. "8 experts pick 2" (Mixtral) and "1 shared + 7-of-63 quarter-size" (DeepSeekMoE) bracket the space; DeepSeek's ablations say segment finer + pin always-on shared experts → strictly better at constant params/FLOPs (Pile 1.808 vs GShard's 1.867), approaching the dense upper bound — the dense model with the same total params all activated (1.806) — within noise. Lesion-tested: routed experts become less redundant; the shared expert is irreplaceable (disable it: 1.808 → 2.414). [T1]
  • (5) MoE ships as open weights, and the wins survive contact. Mixtral 8x7B (47B total / 13B active, Apache 2.0): matches/beats Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 at ~5× fewer active params (MMLU 70.6 vs 69.9/70.0; GSM8K 74.4 vs 69.6), margins largest on math/code/multilingual; Instruct (SFT+DPO) above GPT-3.5/Claude-2.1/Gemini Pro on human LMSys Elo. DeepSeekMoE 16B ≈ LLaMA2 7B at ~40% FLOPs, released, runs on one 40GB GPU unquantized — and this architecture scaled is the substrate under DeepSeek-V2/V3/R1 (deepseek-r1-incentivizing-reasoning). [T1, two groups]
  • (6) The bill is memory and the fine-tuning protocol. Compute tracks the active count; serving memory tracks the total count (Mixtral's own accounting: 13B-active compute, 47B-param RAM; "more suitable for batched workloads") — for single-user local serving the trade bites hardest. And sparse models fine-tune differently: they overfit small tasks, want smaller batches + higher LR (opposite of dense), and updating only the non-expert dense skeleton matches updating everything while expert-only updates (~80% of params) drop ~3.5 SuperGLUE points (ST-MoE). Get the protocol wrong and the pretraining speedup evaporates downstream. [T1, two groups]
  • (7) On small hardware, the currency sparsity pays in depends on whether the weights fit (new 2026-07-26). The map's 2026-07-24 edge measured MoE against dense with everything resident and found cost tracks total parameters — sparsity buys FLOPs, and FLOPs are not what a bandwidth-bound device is short of. Measure the same class of hardware with the weights offloaded and the conclusion inverts, because a different term binds. Mixtral-8×7B is 96.6% expert parameters; decode touches two of eight experts per layer, so an offloaded system moves a small fraction of the model per token — and an LRU cache of the k least-recently-used experts (k=2 at 12 GB, k=4 at 16 GB, hit rate ~0.2–0.3 at k=1 rising to ~0.8 at k=4) moves less than that. Result: 2–3 tok/s at batch size 1 on a free-tier Colab T4, an RTX 3060 and an RTX 3080 Mobile, against 0.58–1.06 for naive layer-wise offloading. A dense model of the same 46.7B total has no idle 75% to leave in RAM. So the two findings bracket the real decision rather than contradicting each other: if it fits, prefer dense at matched active params; if it does not fit, the MoE is the easier thing to offload. A third of the win comes from speculative expert prefetch — run layer L+1's gating function on layer L's hidden states, which works because a residual stream changes slowly (recall ~0.8–0.9 prefetching 3 experts one layer ahead) and which needs no verification, since a wrong guess about what you will need costs only the load you owed anyway. Both consumer cards are bottlenecked by host-to-device PCIe, not the GPU: the 12 GB 3060 nearly matches the 16 GB 3080 Mobile. [T1, venue-less, one model family]

Headline: Qwen1.5-MoE at an average 3.5 bits reaches 68.04 against BF16's 67.93, at over 4× weight-memory compression; at an aggressive 2.5 bits it holds within 2.8–6.6% of full precision across four models. Three structural findings the map should keep. (a) The right allocation unit is the layer, not the expert — up/gate/down projections inside one expert score differently — which is a quiet echo of experts-specialize-by-syntax-not-semantics: there is no math expert to extract and none to protect. (b) Fine-grained MoEs have far more exploitable variance than vanilla ones, so mixed precision pays most exactly where fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy says the architecture is heading — confirmed by Qwen1.5-MoE gaining 13.2 points over uniform at 2.5 bits against Mixtral's 2.9. (c) The map's specific coining questions get partial answers: rarely-routed experts are treated differently, but by a data-free signal rather than by routing frequency, which sidesteps the "does calibration even see rare experts" worry rather than resolving it. That worry is separately vindicated — see the calibration-bias result now carried in post-training-quantization, a 2.1× HumanEval swing from calibration corpus alone. Caveats: weight-only and footprint-only — no latency, throughput or energy anywhere, so MoE × quantization for speed stays open; evaluation is five commonsense zero-shot benchmarks, the family least sensitive to quantization damage, with GSM8K/MMLU appearing only in the motivating figure; no AWQ head-to-head; venue-less 2026 preprint. See moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity, quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model.

Open questions:

  • Quantized MoE that fits versus dense model that fits, on one 12 GB card (new 2026-08-02, and it is now the sharpest premise-relevant question on this map). mixed-precision-moe-finally-has-a-throughput-number puts Mixtral-8×7B at 12.0 GB and 88.6 tok/s, which is the exact card mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference used to demonstrate that Mixtral could be run at 2–3 tok/s by offloading and caching experts. If an aggressively quantized build fits outright, the entire offload apparatus is unnecessary for that model, and the choice a 12 GB owner faces changes shape. The unrun comparison is 1.5-bit Mixtral against a dense 7B–8B at 4 bits on the same card, on GSM8K and MMLU, at matched footprint — the head-to-head the offloading paper also failed to run, now with a second contender. Everything needed is public except the kernels, which is the catch below.
  • Does the global expert allocation work from a calibration-free signal? (new 2026-08-02.) The two MoE-quantization sources disagree about the allocation unit and differ on cost: moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity scores layers from weight spectra with no data at all, sidestepping the calibration confound; expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer scores experts from 128 calibration sequences and demonstrates that dependence biting (GSM8K moves 10.5 points on a calibration swap). The obvious hybrid — a global integer program over experts, scored by the calibration-free spectral criterion — has not been tried, and both halves are published with code or full method descriptions. It would say whether the win comes from the unit, the scope, or the data.
  • Nothing in either result is reachable from the local stack (new 2026-08-02, and this is now the standing complaint on two maps at once). The quantization throughput numbers depend on custom fused Triton kernels for 1/2/3-bit dequantization plus grouped GEMM; the offloaded-speculation result depends on a trained per-layer draft expert with no released weights or code. llama.cpp's K-quant menu still cannot express per-expert bit assignment, and no local runtime ships an expert-level speculative decoder. So this map now holds two demonstrations that the speed exists and no path for a reader to run either. What would close it is not a paper: it is a GGUF-side representation for per-tensor mixed expert precision, which is a tooling question this vault can only track.
  • Does the verification-expansion curve hold on a coarse-grained MoE? (new 2026-08-02.) offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass is measured on two models with 64 routed experts and top-6 routing. The expansion rate depends on how widely a block's tokens disperse across experts, so an 8-expert top-2 model (Mixtral — this vault's other offload substrate) should expand far more slowly, and speculative decoding under offload might still pay there without a trained drafter. Unmeasured, and it is a one-afternoon profiling run on hardware the vault already has evidence about.
  • MoE × PEFT is the tutor rung's sharpest unanswered question. ST-MoE's freeze-the-experts result says the dense skeleton is where fine-tuning wants to act — but it used full-rank updates, never adapters. Whether LoRA on the dense subset (or on experts) behaves, whether the peft-methods rank findings transfer, and how routing shifts under fine-tuning are all unmeasured in-vault.
  • MoE × quantization: RESOLVED 2026-07-25 for footprint, open for everything else (alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation). Answered: layers within an MoE do differ in quantization sensitivity, the difference is readable from the weights alone, and uneven allocation buys real accuracy at a fixed budget — most on fine-grained architectures. What replaces the gap is sharper. Speed is entirely unmeasured — the source reports no latency, throughput or energy, and quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill establishes that footprint and speed move independently, so "4× smaller" says nothing yet about tokens per second on the devices local-inference cares about. The shared expert question is still open: DeepSeekMoE's lesion test shows disabling the shared expert is catastrophic (1.808 → 2.414), which predicts it deserves protection, and no source allocates bits by that structural role — though 2026-07-26 lands the coarse version of exactly that: holding the shared path (attention, embeddings, gates) at 4-bit while crushing experts to 2–3 bits costs 3.85 MMLU points, whereas crushing the shared path instead costs 11.5 from a thirtieth of the parameters (shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts). Quantized and offloaded: RESOLVED 2026-07-26 (mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference), with the honest qualifier that the quality evidence there is perplexity and MMLU only. And what the GGUF path does with routed FFNs at mixed precision remains unserved — llama.cpp's K-quant menu does not express per-layer allocation of this kind, so the most useful result here is currently unreachable from the local stack.
  • MoE × local inference: RESOLVED 2026-07-26, and the two answers disagree in an instructive way (revised 2026-07-26). What llama.cpp does with routed FFNs when everything is resident is answered — it runs them, slower per token than the active count promises, the deficit widening as the device tightens. Expert offload/caching is now also answered (mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference: LRU expert cache plus residual-stream speculative prefetch, 2–3 tok/s for Mixtral-8×7B on a free-tier T4, 3.2× over naive offloading), and it reaches the opposite practical conclusion because a different term binds. What replaces the gap: nobody has run the two configurations against each other on one device — same card, same model, resident-at-low-precision versus offloaded-at-higher-precision — which is the actual choice a 12 GB owner faces and which neither paper poses. Sub-questions the resident study raises and does not answer remain open: Two sub-questions the edge study raises and does not answer: the 91% of MoE-block time that is not routing is never decomposed into weight traffic vs expert-dispatch gather/scatter vs KV pressure — only the first is irreducible — and the whole result rests on one total-to-active ratio (6.9B/1.3B), so whether a fine-grained + shared-expert design (fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy), which exists partly to change this trade, lands differently is untested (local-inference).
  • The Google trio is lineage-related (shared authors, shared codebase) — like the FSDP/ZeRO pair in distributed-training, what replicates across them is engineering practice, not independent derivation. The independent replications are Mistral and DeepSeek — both of which keep their training recipes closed (Mixtral discloses no data or recipe; DeepSeek's baselines are self-reimplemented). No neutral third party has trained or compared these architectures.
  • Expert-choice routing is now used-but-not-studied in-vault (2026-07-22, revised 2026-07-28): sparse upcycling routes by Expert Choice in its encoders, so the vault has a source that depends on it without one that evaluates it against top-k. Aux-loss-free balancing closed 2026-07-28 (deepseek-v3-technical-report), and the frontier recipe is no longer a generation ahead of this map. What it opens is more interesting than what it closed. Nobody has isolated the specialization claim: the domain-peaked heat maps are qualitative, and the experiment that would settle it is the one DeepSeekMoE already ran for shared experts — lesion the domain-peaked experts and measure the loss on that domain. Nobody has connected specialization to caching, which is the cell shared with local-inference: expert-routing-is-predictable-one-layer-ahead and the offloading result rest on route locality that was assumed syntactic and short-range, whereas domain-keyed routes should be far more stable across a single user's conversation — so is an aux-loss-free-trained MoE easier to cache than an aux-loss-trained one is cheap, unrun, and directly premise-relevant. And the batch-wise finding has never been tested below cluster scale, where neither of its two named remedies (large expert-parallel micro-batches, redundant-expert redeployment) is available. Still fully unserved: hash routing.
  • Upcycling is sourced (2026-07-22) but only in pretraining currency. Nothing tests it at fine-tuning-scale extra compute (1–5% rather than 20–100%), on an instruction-tuned rather than pretrained checkpoint, or with adapters instead of full updates — and upcycle-then-LoRA-the- dense-skeleton is now the sharpest untested cell shared with peft-methods. The evidence is also T5-XL (~3B) on a 2020 encoder-decoder, predating every decoder-only MoE this map tracks, so transfer to a fine-grained + shared-expert modern base is assumed rather than shown. And this is the fourth Google MoE paper in-vault — the lineage caveat below now covers most of the map.
  • What does upcycling do to specialization? Experts that begin as identical copies of one MLP must break symmetry through a randomly initialized router alone. Whether they end up specializing the way from-scratch experts do (experts-specialize-by-syntax-not-semantics) — or merely diverge enough to avoid collapse — is unmeasured, and it is the mechanism question underneath the whole result. It also bears on local-inference: expert offload/caching depends on route locality, and upcycled routers may not have the same locality profile.
