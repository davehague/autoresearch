Pipeline parallelism (depth splitting, the bubble, staleness) — map

Forked 2026-08-06 from distributed-training (auto run — flagged for David's review). The parent hit the fork condition for a third time in five days (20 dated bullets, 4 declared sub-threads, after the 2026-08-02 activation-memory fork and the 2026-08-03 offload fork), and this is the strand that was never doing the same job as the others. Every remaining axis on the parent partitions a tensor across devices inside one synchronous step — sharding splits the state, tensor parallelism splits a layer's matmul, sequence parallelism splits the input — and they compose multiplicatively because none of them changes when anything runs. This one splits the schedule. That is why it is the only axis with a bubble, the only one that offers to trade numerical exactness for throughput, the only one whose original motivation was network bandwidth rather than memory, and the only one that reappears intact in serving, where the same bubble analysis applies for an entirely different reason. Four sources, three of them already load-bearing on other maps, and one live internal tension that no other parallelism axis has.

Status: consolidating Status note (2026-08-06, at forking): consolidating, carried from the parent rather than re-derived. Two T1 systems papers from unrelated groups (Google Brain 2019, Microsoft/CMU/Stanford 2019) establish the two branches; a third T1 vendor report (DeepSeek-AI 2024) ships the synthesis at 671B on 2,048 H800s; and a fourth T1 (Orca, OSDI 2022) reproduces the bubble analysis in inference from a different cause. What holds it below consensus is that the internal tension is live and unresolved — flush-and-stay-exact versus never-flush-and-go-stale is a choice with no published head-to-head at modern scale, PipeDream's staleness has never been evaluated on a decoder-only language model, and the newest answer (overlap the communication into the bubble) comes from a vendor report with no ablation isolating it. Also: nothing on this map has been measured below a multi-node cluster, and unlike the parent's other forks it has no single-card story at all.

Status note (2026-08-25): holds consolidating, and the map's cheapest open question — "is the bubble ever the binding constraint below a multi-node cluster?" — is closed by the first source here measured on hardware a household owns. precompiled-pipeline-shards-intel-ai-pc-fleet (T1, Not Community Labs + Intel; every table row backed by a committed script and raw log in a public reproduction directory) splits Llama 3.1 8B INT4 by layer across two consumer laptops over WiFi and Llama 3.1 70B INT4 across four Lunar Lake nodes. The bubble is not only binding at that scale, it is half the machine: with balanced 25 ms stages a 2-stage single-request pipeline runs at roughly 50% utilization, and interleaving a second user's request through independent stateful InferRequests recovers 1.80× (16.33 → 29.34 tok/s). That is Orca's iteration-granularity answer arriving on two laptops rather than a cluster, and it is the first micro-batch sweep this map has for a sub-cluster deployment. Two findings that were not in the map's model at all. First, splitting a compiled graph can cost you the compiler: a naive per-stage export runs 13–23% below monolithic and the gap widens with stage count, because the shards no longer trigger the backend's KV-cache indirection fusion; re-injecting a beam_idx Parameter and Gather restores parity to within 4%, and the post-fusion smaller per-stage GEMMs are also what make micro-batching pay at 1.80×. The map's bubble arithmetic has always treated stage time as a property of the partition; it is a property of the partition as compiled. Second, on a LAN the network is the smaller term — of a 61 ms per-token budget, ~6 ms is one TCP round trip and ~14 ms is host-side Python and dispatch overhead. Status does not move: one vendor's toolchain, one silicon family, several headline rows are single paired sessions, and the WAN characterization is largely sleep-simulated with the paper's own three injection methods disagreeing by up to 5× at nominally equal hop latency.

What we believe now:

  • Splitting depth is the axis that splits the schedule, and the bill is idle silicon. Partition the layer stack into K stages, one per device, and split each minibatch into M micro-batches so stages overlap. Peak activation memory drops toward one stage's worth, and the cost is the bubble — O((K−1)/(M+K−1)) of the step spent filling and draining the pipe, negligible only when M ≫ K. Micro-batching is therefore not free: raising M shrinks the bubble and shrinks the per-device batch that kernels and normalization layers want. [T1] See pipeline-parallelism-splits-depth-and-pays-a-bubble.

  • The axis has two branches with opposite trades, and the memory framing is the later one. Pipelining's original motivation was communication, not memory: data-parallel bulk-synchronous training all-reduces every parameter every step, measured at 70–90% of total training time on the models PipeDream targets, and stage partitioning ships one layer's activations instead — up to 95% less traffic and up to 5× faster time-to-target-accuracy. That branch spends memory rather than saving it (a stashed weight version plus intermediate state per in-flight minibatch). So: flush and stay exact (GPipe — pay the bubble, keep the gradient correct) versus never flush and go stale (PipeDream — 1F1B scheduling, weight stashing, a gradient that is deliberately not the gradient of the weights that produced the activations). The driver worsens with hardware: across five models, moving K80 → Titan X → V100 raises communication's share, and PipeDream's edge over bulk-synchronous training grew 2.99× → 5.12× on newer cards behind a slower network. [T1 ×2] See asynchronous-pipelining-buys-bandwidth-with-gradient-freshness.

  • There is a third answer to the bubble, and it is to put work in it. DeepSeek-V3's DualPipe neither pays the bubble nor accepts staleness: it runs bidirectional micro-batch flows and schedules the cross-node expert-parallel all-to-all inside the pipeline gaps, so a communication pattern that would otherwise impose a roughly 1:1 computation-to-communication ratio is hidden entirely — 16-way pipeline parallelism composed with 64-way expert parallelism over 8 nodes, 671B parameters, 2,048 H800s. The bubble stops being a loss when the cluster has an unrelated stall to overlap into it, which is a property of the workload (sparse routing's all-to-all) rather than of the schedule. [T1, vendor report, no ablation isolating DualPipe]

  • The same analysis reappears in serving, with a different cause and the same fix. In training the bubble comes from filling and draining a fixed pipeline. In inference the stages are never idle for that reason, and bubbles are worse, because micro-batch runtimes vary wildly with their prefill/decode mix. Orca's answer is the structural analogue of DualPipe's: schedule at iteration granularity over a continuous stream of independent requests, so the gap is filled by the next request rather than the next micro-batch — and the batching-efficiency-versus-bubble trade that micro-batching forces simply stops existing. That makes depth splitting a viable serving layout on commodity networking rather than a last resort. [T1]

  • The right partition is a search, not a rule. PipeDream profiles the model for 1,000 minibatches and runs an O(N²M²) dynamic program over stage partition, replication factor and in-flight count. In all but one experiment the optimum was a mixture of data, model and pipeline parallelism; in the exception the search correctly returned pure data parallelism (Inception-v3, 5% communication overhead, 1.00× over bulk-synchronous). Same model, different cluster, different answer: 25 Gbps says data-parallel, 10 Gbps says a 7-1 pipeline. The transferable rule is the premise rather than the algorithm — the optimum is a property of the deployment, so profile the specific model on the specific hardware. [T1] See the-parallelism-configuration-is-searchable-not-a-dial.

  • Splitting a compiled model at a layer boundary can silently deoptimize it, and the penalty grows with stage count. Per-stage shards are individually valid and individually slower: on OpenVINO they run 13–23% below the monolithic export because they no longer carry the input that triggers the GPU plugin's IndirectKVCache fusion. A graph-surgery pass that re-injects a beam_idx Parameter and a Gather(ReadValue, beam_idx, axis=0) restores it — 21.26 → 24.45 tok/s, within 4% of monolithic, and parity survives 2- and 3-way splits at a cost of ~1 tok/s. Two consequences. Any distributed-inference study that partitions, measures, and reports has measured the partition and an unintended compiler regression with no way to separate them — and would naturally mis-attribute it to the network, which on a LAN is the smaller term (~6 ms of TCP against ~14 ms of host dispatch in a 61 ms per-token budget). And the fix changes the bubble as well as the throughput: post-fusion per-stage GEMMs are smaller, leaving more idle time for a second stream to fill. [T1] See pipeline-shards-reach-monolithic-parity-only-after-a-graph-fusion-unlock.

  • Below a cluster, the bubble is roughly half the machine, and micro-batching is what recovers it. A 2-stage pipeline with balanced 25 ms stages leaves stage 0 idle while stage 1 computes. Independent stateful inference requests, each carrying its own KV cache, interleave two users' decode steps and recover 1.80× aggregate (2.03× on an earlier, slower single-stream baseline — the ratio compresses as the baseline improves, because a faster stage leaves less idle time to absorb). Scaling is bounded by min(N_users, N_stages) pipeline fill and by per-stream compiled-graph memory (~6 GB of integrated-GPU memory per stream on a 16 GB machine): 3 streams on a 3-stage testbed add +14.1 tok/s aggregate but drop per-stream throughput from 25.3 to 21.6 tok/s. This is Orca's iteration-granularity result at fleet scale, and it composes multiplicatively with speculative decoding rather than contending with it. [T1]

  • Depth splitting is what raises the model-size ceiling on hardware you own. Four 32 GB Intel laptops, 20 layers each, serve Llama 3.1 70B INT4 (~36 GB) at 5.42 tok/s, bit-exact against the same topology's non-speculative path — while a monolithic 70B INT4 export was OOM-killed at layer 71 of 80 on a 133 GB machine. Nothing is compressed further; the footprint is simply partitioned, which makes this the only lever on the sibling maps that raises the ceiling without trading quality. The honest boundary is that 5.42 tok/s clears the paper's own 5 tok/s interactive floor by 8%, and a second concurrent stream pushes per-user throughput under it. [T1] See a-fleet-of-consumer-laptops-raises-the-ceiling-to-the-interactive-floor.

Open questions:

  • Nobody has run flush-versus-stale on a decoder-only language model. PipeDream's staleness results are on VGG16, AlexNet, S2VT, GNMT and Inception-v3 — vision and translation workloads from 2019 — and every modern pipeline deployment this vault holds (GPipe's own successors, DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K3) is synchronous. Whether a stale gradient is tolerable for a transformer trained to convergence on trillions of tokens is unmeasured, and it matters because the communication driver PipeDream identified has only grown.
  • DualPipe's benefit is entangled with sparse routing and nobody has separated them. Overlapping communication into the bubble works because expert parallelism supplies a large, regular all-to-all to hide. On a dense model at the same scale there may be nothing of comparable size to overlap, in which case the third answer to the bubble is not general — it is a mixture-of-experts affordance. The vendor report runs no ablation, so this is inference from the mechanism.
  • Is the bubble ever the binding constraint below a multi-node cluster? Answered 2026-08-25 — yes, and it is about half the machine. A balanced 2-stage pipeline on two consumer laptops runs at roughly 50% utilization for a single request, and micro-batching recovers 1.80×. What remains open is the single-machine multi-GPU version of the question — two discrete cards under device_map="auto" — which is what most local practitioners actually run, and for which this vault still has no measured bubble, micro-batch sweep or throughput number. The fleet result suggests the answer transfers, since the binding term there was host-side dispatch rather than interconnect, but nobody has measured it over PCIe or NVLink.
  • Nothing here composes stage-chained pipelines with continuous batching. The fleet source measures both — micro-batching across stages, and OpenVINO GenAI's ContinuousBatchingPipeline (paged attention, split-fuse scheduling, block-level prefix caching) reaching 4.9× aggregate at sixteen concurrent requests on one node — but explicitly notes the continuous-batching mode "does not yet compose with the stage chain," and that every measured configuration of it loses 8–15% at concurrency 1. So the two answers to the inference bubble, request-level scheduling and depth splitting, have not been run together on the same deployment, and the request-level one has a cost at exactly the concurrency a local user runs at.
  • Exactness ordering, restated for this axis. activation-memory holds that recomputation is the one memory lever that is provably exact and should therefore be spent first. Asynchronous pipelining sits at the opposite end of that ladder — deliberately inexact, by its authors' own account. Nobody has measured what the staleness costs at a fixed final quality target, so the ladder has a labelled bottom rung with no number on it.
