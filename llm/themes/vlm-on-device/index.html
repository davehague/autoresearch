What we believe now:

  • A generative vision-language model now fits on a phone, and the artifact that proves it will not say at what precision (new 2026-08-16, and it answers most of this map's founding open question while leaving its deciding column blank). A shipped 3.1B VLM — a SigLIP2 NaFlex 400M vision encoder on a 2.6B text backbone, 32K context, BF16 weights under a custom vendor licence — fits in about 3 GB and decodes at 228 tok/s on an M5 Max, 116 on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395 and 20 tok/s on a Galaxy S26 Ultra, with GGUF, ONNX and MLX-8bit checkpoints published on release day alongside llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM and SGLang support. Twenty tokens per second is above reading speed, which makes this the first entry here saying a VLM on a handset is usable rather than demonstrable — and the day-one GGUF build answers this map's second standing question ("what does llama.cpp actually do with a vision tower?") in the plainest way available. What is missing is the column this map is organized around. The precision of every throughput and footprint number is unstated; 3.1B parameters is ~6.2 GB at BF16 and ~3.1 GB at 8 bits, so "about 3 GB" implies roughly 8-bit, but that is arithmetic rather than a vendor claim, and the four shipped precisions come with no quality-versus-precision comparison at all — which is exactly the silence four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer says to distrust, having found a 2B VLM losing 1.6 accuracy points and 15 AUROC points at 4-bit. The capability story has a matching shape: near-parity with a 4B open baseline on grounding and screen understanding (RefCOCO 87.9 vs 88.8, MMBench 81.0 vs 81.1, ScreenSpot-v2 80.7), essentially flat on tool use (BFCL V4 32.5 vs a 2B baseline's 33.2), and the vendor's own scoping says why — the model "answers directly instead of reasoning," is recommended for single-turn OCR, grounding and translation, and is explicitly not recommended for long-context or reasoning-intensive work. So on-device VLM capability is currently bought by narrowing the job, not by compressing a generalist — the practical form of quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation, arrived at from the product side. Graded low: vendor engineering post plus its own model card, self-reported benchmarks with vendor-chosen baselines, no independent replication. [T2, single-study] a-three-billion-parameter-vlm-runs-on-a-phone-at-twenty-tokens-a-second, an-edge-vlm-buys-grounding-by-declining-to-reason
  • At a fixed memory budget, take the larger quantized VLM over the smaller full-precision one. Three arms sized to a comparable footprint, on identical inputs, over 5,700 predictions: Qwen2-VL-2B at fp16 reads 0.904 accuracy / 0.95 error-detection AUROC / 0.017 risk–coverage area; the same model at 4-bit NF4 reads 0.888 / 0.80 / 0.048; Qwen2-VL-7B at 4-bit reads 0.935 / 0.98 / 0.008. Not a trade — a dominance: the larger quantized model answers better, knows better when it has failed, and is the only arm whose clean-calibrated deferral threshold survives image degradation (selective accuracy 1.00 against the 2B-4bit arm admitting 12% of its errors at the hardest glare condition). The reframing is the durable part: model size and precision are one budget, not two, where every other quantization claim in this vault measures quality at a fixed model rather than at a fixed footprint. Two riders. The cost is paid in the confidence signal, not the answer — 4-bit costs the 2B arm 1.6 accuracy points and 15 AUROC points, so an accuracy-only evaluation would have called it free. And it is the second in-vault support for small models quantize worse. Scope: two scale points (not a scaling law, the author says so), the 7B fp16 arm was never run, a 100-item four-option Food101 task with n ≈ 100 per condition, one model family, single author, no venue. [T1, one study] See at-a-fixed-footprint-take-the-larger-quantized-model, four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer.
  • The perception front-end is not the expensive part on one card, and its precision budget should be allocated by modality. A quantized CLIP ViT-B/16 adapting at test time without any backward pass runs at 196.73 MB / 26.04 ms per sample at 8 bits and 139.63 MB / 14.92 ms with the vision tower at 4 bits, on one consumer GPU at batch size 1. The design rule: vision at 4 bits, text at 8, one or two projection/layer-norm layers per tower kept at float16 — the third independent partitioning in this vault (after per-layer and per-tensor-class) to conclude that a quantization budget is an allocation problem, not a global setting. Two boundaries stated plainly. At 8 bits the allocation buys nothing — four quantizers within 0.19 points — so the whole design earns its keep only below a width that is twice the 4-bit floor this vault carries for language-model weights; and below it the descent is a cliff (60.59 / 42.54 / 3.02% at 3 / 2 / 1 bits) rather than the graded task-shaped ladder an 8B language model shows. Scope is the headline caveat: this is CLIP, the encoder late fusion imports, not a generative VLM — so it prices the perception stage and says nothing about the image-token KV load that local-inference says binds first. [T1, one model] See vlm-precision-should-be-allocated-by-modality, a-vlm-adapts-on-one-consumer-card-without-a-backward-pass.
  • What makes local VLM use feel broken is the vision tower, and the fix is a cache rather than a model change. On an Apple M4 Max, a multi-turn conversation about a single 1024×1024 image on Qwen3-VL-8B costs 21.7 s on the first turn and 0.78 s by the third — 28× — and the ablation locates it: caching the vision embeddings is worth 7.8× on its own; the text KV cache 1.2×. Two consequences. Resolution is the cost dial on both axes: 224×224 gives 6.7× at 48 MB of cache, 1024×1024 gives 13.1× at 156 MB, and video is far worse (12.1 GB for 64 frames) — so the native-resolution direction the parent map credits Qwen2.5-VL with has a local memory bill nobody has stated. And the mechanism is mundane and portable: identity is established by SHA-256 over decoded pixels rather than over the URL or blob, which is the whole trick and is implementable in any runtime. Scope it hard — one engine, one machine at 128 GB (roughly 4× a typical enthusiast's memory), no quality evaluation anywhere in the source, the author is the engine's own vendor, and it is MLX rather than llama.cpp. [T1, vendor-authored] See caching-vision-embeddings-is-the-multi-turn-vlm-lever.
  • The image-token serving problem is solved by not preallocating, and it is solved in the datacenter rather than on a laptop. Put image tokens in the same paged pool as text and key the cache on the hash of the input image, and a variable per-image token count stops being a special case: 0.18 → 1.15 images/s on LLaVA-v1.5-7B (6.4×) and 0.02 → 0.10 frame/s on LLaVA-NeXT-34B, against the model authors' own reference implementations. A month of production corroboration on Chatbot Arena gives a 52.4% cache hit rate on LLaVA-NeXT-34B, below text-only Vicuna-33B's 74.1% but substantial. The structural point worth keeping: the interface both VLM families share — images become a token sequence — is exactly what lets the existing serving machinery apply unchanged, which is a rare case of the parent's architecture contest not mattering. Scope: all of it is datacenter GPU, and it is about serving cost rather than architecture. [T1] See prefix-reuse-attacks-prefill-and-needs-no-concurrency.

Open questions:

  • Nobody has measured a generative VLM's language half on one card (partly answered 2026-08-16). Everything here prices a component: a CLIP encoder's precision budget, an MLX engine's embedding cache, a datacenter serving stack's prefix reuse, and one generative model's accuracy-versus-calibration at 4 bits on a 100-item task. What is missing is the obvious thing — prefill and decode throughput, peak VRAM, and a real benchmark for a 3B–8B VLM at 4 bits on a 12–24 GB card, with and without vision-embedding caching. That single table would let this map say something a practitioner could act on, and every component it needs is public. A shipped edge VLM (a-three-billion-parameter-vlm-runs-on-a-phone-at-twenty-tokens-a-second) now supplies decode throughput on three devices and a ~3 GB footprint, which is most of it. Four things it does not supply, and they are what remains open: the precision every number was measured at; prefill throughput and time-to-first-token, which the embedding-cache finding says dominates the felt experience; peak VRAM on a discrete consumer card rather than on unified memory; and any measurement with and without vision-embedding caching, which is the one axis this map has independent evidence for. The residual question is now much narrower and one afternoon of work on hardware anyone owns: the same shipped 3B model, GGUF Q4 against Q8 against BF16, prefill and decode and peak VRAM on a 12–24 GB card, with a benchmark scored at each.
  • Four precisions shipped and no quality delta measured (new 2026-08-16). This map's third open question asks whether "small models quantize worse" is a real law, and it has been waiting for a controlled test. A 3B VLM released simultaneously at BF16, GGUF, ONNX and MLX-8bit, by a vendor with every incentive to publish a favourable number, published none — no benchmark score for any quantized checkpoint. Given that four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer found a 2B VLM losing ten times more error-detection AUROC than accuracy at 4-bit, an absent number is a weak negative signal and should be read as one. The check is cheap and does not need the vendor: run the published GGUF and MLX-8bit checkpoints against the BF16 weights on the model's own reported benchmarks, plus one calibration measure. Nobody has, and until somebody does, "runs in 3 GB" and "scores 87.9 on RefCOCO" are two statements about two different artifacts.
  • What does llama.cpp actually do with a vision tower? (inherited from the parent, carried since 2026-07-22.) The two local measurements this strand holds are MLX. GGUF's handling of a vision encoder — whether the tower is quantized on the same ladder as the language model, whether embeddings are cached across turns, what happens to native-resolution patch counts — is undocumented in-vault and is the format most of this vault's readers use. The parent's architecture contest also depends on it: whichever family is cheaper to run at 4 bits on one card is the one that matters here, and that axis has no published measurement for either family.
  • Is "small models quantize worse" a real law or two coincidences? The strand has two supports pointing the same way — a CLIP encoder falling off a cliff below 4 bits, and a 2B generative VLM losing 15 AUROC points where a 7B loses almost nothing — and they are different architectures, different tasks and different currencies. post-training-quantization now holds a third from a text-only literature (reasoning-process shifts confined to 3B and 8B models). Three directional agreements across three literatures is the point at which this deserves a controlled test: one model family, four sizes, one quantizer, one benchmark suite. Nobody has run it, and it decides whether the edge deployment story is "quantize a big model" or "quantization is for big models."
  • Does the vision-embedding cache compose with anything? local-inference's standing finding is that local decode levers contend for the same headroom. Embedding caching spends storage to delete recomputation, which should be orthogonal to everything on that map — but nobody has run it alongside 4-bit weights, KV quantization or speculative decoding, and this strand's own numbers come from an unquantized 8B model on a 128 GB machine.
