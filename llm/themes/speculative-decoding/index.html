Status note (2026-10-27): the map's "speculation fights sparse routing" thread gets a performance model for exactly why, and a usable answer for what draft length to pick under it, rather than another measurement that the fight happens. specdecode-moe-expert-bytes-draft-length (T1, arXiv 2610.34440) models MoE verification-pass step time as bytes-over-effective-bandwidth plus fixed overhead, applied to the specific question of whether to deduplicate repeated expert reads across a verification pass's tokens. On every tested engine (llama.cpp/Metal and MLX-LM on an Apple M4 Max, llama.cpp/CUDA on an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER) the deduplicating kernel has 32-75% of the per-slot kernel's effective bandwidth, so fewer bytes only wins at roughly 5-8 drafted tokens per pass, and the model — fit from a single layer's bandwidth, under an hour — predicts both that crossover and the acceptance-rate-dependent best draft length within one token in most tested configurations (DeepSeek- V2-Lite/CUDA: within one token at all 22 tested acceptance values). This doesn't resolve this map's open "why does speculation fight sparse routing, and is the fix portable?" question — it is about kernel bandwidth under a fixed draft length policy, not the activated-expert-union mechanism that question names — but it supplies the first in-vault instrument for choosing a draft length given that fight, measured on two consumer-class engines/cards (M4 Max, RTX 4070 Ti SUPER) rather than a datacenter card. Status does not move: single author, no end-to-end speedup number, no code/weights release confirmed. specdecode-moe-dedup-only-wins-at-5-8-tokens-and-bandwidth-not-bytes-decides-draft-length.

Status note (2026-10-23): holds consolidating, and the map's KV-budget-for-drafters territory — thin relative to kv-cache-management's target-cache literature — gets its first named mechanism for why a draft model's own compressed cache degrades specifically as generation proceeds, plus three progressively better fixes. rift-kv-cache-compression-for-drafters (T1, arXiv 2610.31098) measures "decode staleness": a prefill-based cache selection (SnapKV-class) captures 67.0% of full-cache attention mass early in generation and falls to 25.4% by the end of a long output, while a pure sliding window's share rises 43.5%→68.7% over the same span — the two standing baseline mechanisms fail in opposite directions. RIFT-stat (static recency/prefill split), RIFT-dyn (periodic decode-time rescore), and RIFT-ada (adaptive rescore timing) progressively close the gap: at an 8K budget, Qwen3.5-4B drafting for Qwen3.5-122B retains 86.9%/94.6%/95.8% of full-cache acceptance length at 32K input (vs. SnapKV's 63.0%), with RIFT-ada needing roughly half the rescores of RIFT-dyn at matched quality. A compressibility-driven drafter training phase (sparse-mask fine-tuning) adds up to a further +16% acceptance length at tight (2K) budgets, shrinking as budget grows. rift-decode-staleness-and-recency-split-fix-drafter-kv-compression, rift-compressibility-training-closes-remaining-gap. Cross-linked to kv-cache-management (the budget-allocation mechanism is a close cousin of that map's target-cache compression literature, applied here to the drafter's cache specifically). Status does not move: acceptance length, not throughput, is the reported metric throughout — this map's own precedent (DCD, DPara) is that accepted length and wall-clock speed can disagree when the drafter's own cost changes, and no such control is run here; the paper's abstract/introduction/Table 7 report three different percentages for overlapping claims, not yet reconciled; no independent replication.

Status note (2026-10-21): holds consolidating; drafter-training objective is a lever this map had not separated from drafter architecture, and distribution-matching beats point-mass imitation — plus a direct mechanism-level interaction with training-time-quantization. d-pal-acceptance-derived-speculative-decoding (T1, arXiv 2610.28355, code+weights released) replaces cross-entropy-against-the-target's-one-token with a divergence (forward KL or Rényi-1/2) between the draft's and target's full distributions as the per-position training loss; Rényi-1/2 additionally bounds the true acceptance rate rather than merely correlating with it. D-PAL beats the prior position- weighted point-mass loss (D-PACE) on mean accepted length and throughput at both T=0 and T=1 across six benchmarks with a Qwen3-4B target. distributional-acceptance-loss-beats-point-mass-draft-training. Separately, bitmtp-low-bit-multi-token-prediction (T1, arXiv 2610.28500, NeurIPS 2026, code released) finds that an ultra-low-bit (~1.58-bit) backbone and multi-token-prediction drafting do not compose for free — the backbone's output distribution interacts with drafting's acceptance mechanics in a way that erases the naive expected speedup — and a co-designed single model with a hybrid decoding mode is needed to recover it: five-task mean 2.2-2.4x on an H20, up to 2.0x on an RTX 3090, 3.3x on MBPP+ on a Jetson Orin. bitmtp-hybrid-decoding-composes-ultra-low-bit-with-mtp. This is the first in-vault source crossing this map and training-time-quantization at the mechanism level — flagged in both maps. Status does not move on either finding: D-PAL extends this map's drafter-objective thread without contradicting prior drafter-provenance findings; BitMTP is a new composition result, not a reversal of either map's standing claims.

Status note (2026-10-20): holds consolidating, and the drafter-shape strand gets a third, structurally distinct fix for a block-diffusion drafter — not the repetition trap (D-Loop) or joint incoherence (LiLiCorr/DSpark), but the single anchor per block throwing away hidden states the target already paid for. reanchor-diffusion-drafting-speculative-decoding (T1, arXiv 2610.26531, KAIST/SNU, code released) finds 60-66% of the draft tokens discarded past a verification rejection still match the target's own top-1 prediction, so the hidden states behind them remain useful conditioning even though the tokens that produced them were rejected. ReAnchor (training-free, no retraining) decodes a shifted anchor from each retained discarded state, reruns the same frozen drafter from it in parallel, and verifies the merged trie in one target pass — exactly as lossless as the baseline. Averaged over four targets and eight benchmarks at T=0: +15.2% acceptance length, +13.4% decode speed over plain DFlash; largest on Gemma 4-12B (+19.4%/+17.9%). reanchor-recycles-discarded-verifier-states-for-free-draft-breadth. Cross-linked to diffusion-decoding as the drafted configuration that map's 2026-08-12 boundary ruling assigns here. Status note (2026-10-19): holds consolidating, and two sources sharpen two different open questions — one narrows "does the exactness dial survive an evidence check," the other adds a sixth drafter-provenance answer built specifically for the one workload shape this map had never tested. lossy-speculative-decoding-length-inflation (T1, arXiv 2610.23818) measures a cost this map's lossy-verification evidence had not metered: completion length itself. Across 360 target-drafter-benchmark cells (12 pairs, 6 benchmarks, 5 published training-free relaxed-verification rules), relaxed verification accepts 1.34x as many tokens per round on average but produces completions 1.54x longer — the length growth outruns the acceptance gain that is supposed to justify the method, mainly by extending reasoning traces with tokens the target model itself rates unlikely. Forcing a relaxed run's trace to close near the generation budget recovers most of the lost accuracy (126 → 439 correct answers across 1,132 affected runs) without recovering the speed. This narrows the map's exactness-dial open question with a mechanism independent of the one already on file (per-token distribution substitution); it does not close it, since the fixed-trace-replay check named below is still unrun. length-inflation-undermines-accepted-tokens-as-a-speed-metric. dlite-history-free-speculative-decoding (T1, arXiv 2610.23787, code + weights released) targets agentic workloads specifically — long, continually-evolving multi-turn contexts — where this map has never had a measurement, and where existing dedicated drafters lose up to 80% of acceptance length while their own historical KV cache adds 10-20% of end-to-end latency. Its fix is structural rather than incremental: a history-free drafter that reads the target's own contextual hidden states (which already encode the full history) and predicts only a local block, restoring intra-block order with a lightweight sequential head instead of tracking growing context at all. Reaches 2-6 acceptance length and beats the prior state-of-the-art drafter (DSpark) by up to 70%, largest on agentic benchmarks (SWE-Bench: 2.68-2.74x vs. DSpark's 1.60-1.64x). This is a sixth drafter-provenance option, not evaluated against this map's other five on one backbone — the provenance-comparison open question still stands, now with one more named entry addressed to a workload the other five were never tested on. history-free-drafting-fixes-agentic-speculative-decodings-growing-context-problem. Status note (2026-10-17): holds consolidating, and the map's standing "where should a drafter come from" question gets a new, orthogonal axis — context length — that turns out not to need a different drafter at all. frozen-drafters-scale-speculative-decoding-2m-context (T1, arXiv 2610.20865) answers the long-standing assumption that speculation's speedup erodes with context length because a drafter must either be trained for long context or inherit the target's own multi-token-prediction head (this map's hyco/AdaSpark-style fixes). The paper's reframe: a short-context drafter already benefits from the target's hidden states, which already encode distant context, and verification corrects what the drafter gets wrong regardless — so what actually erodes the speedup is mechanical, not representational: an unbounded attention cache, and RoPE position indices running outside the drafter's trained range. Fixing both at inference time only (bound the cache the drafter reads; remap its rotary indices back into its training range) — no retraining, no weight changes — preserves a frozen short-context drafter's speedup from 4K out to 2M tokens across 3 target-drafter pairs and 5 corpora, with at most a 0.16x worst-case cost at the shortest context, provided per-token speculative cost stays below running the target alone. This adds a third, cheaper option to the map's drafter-provenance comparison (downloaded small model, self-speculative pass, trained MTP head, sparse-cache view, resident distilled expert) without resolving which of those wins on one backbone — it instead shows the comparison no longer needs to control for context length as a confound, since an off-the-shelf short-context drafter now travels with the model regardless. Status does not move: this is a mechanism fix removing a cost that was assumed structural, not a new exactness or verification-cost claim. frozen-short-context-drafters-scale-to-2m-tokens-via-cache-bounding-and-rope-remap.

Status note (2026-10-16): holds consolidating, and the map's phone-NPU strand gets a second instance — this time hosting half a draft tree rather than the whole workload. hyco-mobile-cloud-speculative-decoding (T1) relocates the server model's own multi-token-prediction (MTP) head to the device: the server sends a hidden state, input embedding, and the KV entries the head needs for one query, and the phone runs the head itself to grow draft depth, avoiding both server-side head execution and the many sequential small- model passes a phone-only drafter needs to reach the same depth. A separate single pass of a small on- device model supplies branch alternatives; quantization, a restricted vocabulary, and a 1K-token window fit both drafters on a phone NPU. On a Galaxy S26 Ultra over live LTE/5G, this cuts server GPU time per output token 38.6% (Spec-Bench) / 29.2% (32K-token summarization) vs. server-side MTP self-speculation, and beats an SLM-only on-device drafter by 1.9x output speed and 9.0 more percentage points of server-time savings, at judged-quality parity (+0.2, 95% CI -0.9 to +1.3). An explicit, named epsilon-threshold relaxation trades a bounded amount of exact target-distribution fidelity for the headline numbers; without it, savings are smaller (27.7% / 11.9%) but still positive — the cost of insisting on exactness is reported, not hidden. Cross-linked to local-inference's on-device strand, where the standing finding is that a phone NPU never wins on throughput for the whole inference workload (GPU < CPU < NPU, roughly 4x the cost per token of an iGPU): HyCo doesn't contest that — it finds the NPU a role outside that comparison, hosting draft- tree growth rather than full decode, alongside this map's own 2026-08-02 DraftExpert phone result (Snapdragon 8 Elite, offloaded-MoE drafting). Status does not move: all numbers are single-client, this map's standing complaint about speculative-decoding serving numbers, and no absolute wall-clock latency is given, only relative savings. hyco-runs-the-servers-mtp-head-on-device-to-grow-draft-depth-for-free.

Status note (2026-10-08): holds consolidating, and the drafter-training literature gets its first exact (not block-local-proxy) training objective for parallel drafters, with a small but consistent win on the map's own existing DSpark drafter. edr-objective-parallel-speculative-draft-training (T1, arXiv 2610.10411, University of Michigan) models speculative decoding as a Markov reward process and derives an Expected Decoding Rounds (EDR) objective proven exactly equal (Theorem 1) to the expected number of decoding rounds, with an exact temporal-difference gradient for unbiased training from target-model rollouts and an offline evaluator that compares drafters on round count without running live speculative decoding. Fine-tuning this map's own DSpark drafter plus a new drafter (DFly) with EDR ties or beats the existing E2E training objective on mean accepted length across all nine math/code/chat benchmarks tested, though margins are small (0.01-0.04 MAL per benchmark). edr-objective-trains-parallel-speculative-drafters-on-exact-expected-rounds. This is a training-stage lever on the same quantity — accepted length — this map's the-best-proposal-length-is-not-a-constant already treats as the central currency, rather than a new strand. Status does not move: one baseline objective (E2E) benchmarked against named alternatives (CE, KL, TV, LK, D-PACE) left untested; two drafter-target pairs; EDR's own training cost is higher than block-local objectives, unquantified.

Status note (2026-10-07): holds consolidating, and the lossy-verification thread's standing "distributional fidelity is spent, not just traded away qualitatively" observation becomes an exact, provable equality. nucleus-speculative-decoding-plausibility-verification (T1, arXiv 2610.07822, Eastern Institute of Technology Ningbo / Hong Kong Polytechnic University) measures that standard speculative-decoding verification rejects draft tokens the target model still rates highly plausible purely as a ratio artifact: 81.5% of rejected tokens still rank in the target's top-10, 52.6% still sit in its own top-p=0.95 nucleus. Nucleus Speculative Decoding (NSD) adds a second acceptance path — accept if standard rule passes OR token is in the target's nucleus — and proves the resulting single- step distributional deviation is exactly the draft model's excess probability mass inside that nucleus (an equality, not a bound), with a derived sequence-level fidelity bound for how it compounds. Mean speedups 3.70-4.63x across six backbones/five benchmarks (up to 5.16x over AR, 3.15x over standard SD headline), accepted length up ~1.5-2.5x; task accuracy is a genuine mixed bag (wins 15/30, ties 1/30, loses 14/30 of model-benchmark comparisons versus standard SD, net +0.40 points average). nucleus-acceptance-recovers-plausible-rejected-draft-tokens-with-exact-fidelity-bound. Status does not move: this is the map's clearest confirmation yet that relaxed verification is a real, now provably- quantified speed/fidelity knob rather than a free win — genuine accuracy loss on nearly half the tested comparisons — and no test of composability with tree-structured drafting or this map's other relaxations.

Status note (2026-09-26): holds consolidating — a serving-systems answer to the draft/target hardware question that the map has so far treated as a placement choice. nebulasd-many-for-many-speculative-decoding (T1, arXiv 2609.29364, cs.NI) reframes draft/target GPU allocation as a per-round scheduling problem: instead of co-locating draft and target on the same GPU (forcing one shared batch size) or physically disaggregating them onto fixed GPU sets (freezing the draft:target ratio at deploy time), it organizes both into independently, dynamically resizable resource pools drawn from one shared physical pool, reconstructing each stage's batch from a shared request queue every round. Status note (2026-08-14): holds consolidating, and the map's 2026-08-12 belief gets its cleanest possible confirmation — a drafter made deliberately worse at the metric everyone reports, winning anyway. dcd-decoupled-contrastive-decoding (T1, three 8B expert/amateur pairs across two families plus a 70B extension, four benchmarks, two purpose-built diagnostics, code released) asks whether a drafter speculating contrastive decoding should be trained on the contrastive distribution it will be verified against or on the expert alone, and answers with arithmetic rather than a leaderboard: reconstructing π_CD ∝ π_e^{1+α}·π_f^{−α} re-enters the expert-side approximation error at exponent (1+α), and the correction being installed is usually smaller than the error being amplified — 81.1% of positions carry contrastive signal below 1.0 while 48.7% carry expert-side proposal KL ≥ 2.0.   • Accepted length is not a speed metric, and when the drafter's cost is the variable it points the wrong way (new 2026-08-14 — the sharpest form of the belief below, and a correction to how this map has been reading its own sources).   • When the verification target is composite, draft the dominant term rather than the composite (new 2026-08-14). Approximating a reweighted product of two distributions means each part's error re-enters at the exponent that combines it — expert-side error at (1+α) — while the correction being reconstructed is typically smaller than that error (81.1% of positions below signal 1.0 against 48.7% above proposal KL 2.0).   • Drafting cost, not just draft quality, is a design variable — and once a drafter generates in parallel the entire optimization is about verification (new 2026-08-12, and it closes the drafting side of the verification-cost argument this map has been building since 2026-08-09).   • "Verification costs about one target forward pass" is a property of the KV cache, not of speculation — and the two architectures the field is moving to both break it (new 2026-08-09, and it is the map's first structural claim about where the assumption came from). Verifying γ+1 candidates in one pass works because attention reads an append-only, branch-independent cache. Replace part of that with a recurrent linear-attention state — every hybrid stack now shipping, three gated-DeltaNet layers per full-attention layer — and the state is path-dependent, so each candidate branch implies a different one. A naive tree walk replays the recurrence per branch (serial recurrence climbing from 4% to 27% of the target forward as the tree grows 8 → 64 nodes) and snapshots a full state per proposal node (36 GiB for eight 32-node requests on a 122B model). This is the same failure offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass found on a sparse backbone in a different currency (weight traffic, 5.8× a token-decode at K=16), so the general form is: the cost of verifying a draft is a property of the backbone's memory structure, not of the draft. It is repairable, expensively: an exact closed form for the gated delta rule over a tree, its inverse taken as a finite Neumann series because depth is bounded, plus branches stored as token-scale factors instead of states — 3.4–7.7× on the verification kernel, 82–99× less transient state, up to 4.72× autoregressive decode throughput and 2.03× the strongest tree-speculative baseline, with mean accepted tokens essentially unchanged (this is systems, not drafting).   • The draft's shape is a third lever alongside its size and the verification rule, and both of its knobs are set wrong by default (new 2026-08-09). Two results, one cheap and one cheaper. Shape by decoding regime: block drafters emit b positions as independent marginals while verification checks them sequentially conditioned on what was accepted — invisible under greedy decoding, and degrading monotonically as the target's sampling entropy rises.   • Speculation's exactness flag now has a dial on it, and the third relaxation mechanism is the affordable one (new 2026-08-07). The map's 2026-07-31 finding was that lossy verification abandons the exactness guarantee and that the damage is set by an unrelated configuration choice; the ablation behind it split one lenience knob into a nearly-free cap and a ruinous interpolation (1.67× block efficiency for 25 Pass@1 points). A third mechanism relaxes the greedy argmax rule instead of the sampling rule, and controls it with three simultaneous constraints — a local target-logit regret gate normalized by remaining proposal length (r_i / q_i ≤ g, so an early mismatch is tolerated more readily because more suffix is recoverable), a per-block exception cap, and a persistent request-level regret budget carried across rounds.   • Speculative decoding's exactness is a flag, not a property — and no speed metric can tell you which side of it you are on (new 2026-07-31, and it revises the map's own yardstick rather than extending it).   • A rewindable KV cache is not a precondition — masking is bit-identical to eviction.   • A parallel block drafter's proposals are individually right and jointly wrong, and the fix is cheap. Block drafters train on per-slot cross-entropy, so nothing couples slot i to slot i+1 — the worst possible shape under prefix acceptance. Acceptance length rises 12–19% on every benchmark tested.
