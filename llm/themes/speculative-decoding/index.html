Speculative decoding (draft-verify, exactness, drafter provenance) — map

Forked out of local-inference on 2026-08-08 (auto run, bounded auto-fork; flagged for David's review). The parent declared two sub-threads on 2026-08-01 and listed "speculative decoding" as an item inside its on-device strand. Six of the parent's 33 dated bullets and two of its belief bullets were about draft-and-verify and nothing else, and the strand had stopped being device-shaped: it now spans a Google research paper, a datacenter quantization study, an offloaded-MoE consumer setup, an edge ASIC and a request-level throughput dial. That is the signature of a separable argument rather than a facet — the questions this literature asks (does the output distribution survive? where does the drafter come from? what does verification cost when the verifier is not one forward pass?) are the same questions on every substrate, and the parent map is organized by deployment shape, which is exactly the axis they do not vary along. Status: consolidating (inherited from local-inference at the fork; the exactness guarantee is replicated and assumed, the relaxations of it are the live argument) Status note (2026-08-14): holds consolidating, and the map's 2026-08-12 belief gets its cleanest possible confirmation — a drafter made deliberately worse at the metric everyone reports, winning anyway. dcd-decoupled-contrastive-decoding (T1, three 8B expert/amateur pairs across two families plus a 70B extension, four benchmarks, two purpose-built diagnostics, code released) asks whether a drafter speculating contrastive decoding should be trained on the contrastive distribution it will be verified against or on the expert alone, and answers with arithmetic rather than a leaderboard: reconstructing π_CD ∝ π_e^{1+α}·π_f^{−α} re-enters the expert-side approximation error at exponent (1+α), and the correction being installed is usually smaller than the error being amplified — 81.1% of positions carry contrastive signal below 1.0 while 48.7% carry expert-side proposal KL ≥ 2.0. So draft the expert, verify the composite, and exactness is preserved structurally because only the proposer changed. 1.65–1.95× over vanilla CD at 8B, 1.96–2.02× at 70B, proposal path 5–12× cheaper. Two things this map should take out of it, neither of which is the speedup. First, accepted length is retired as a cross-study metric: DCD accepts 1.44–1.48 tokens per cycle against the aligned baseline's 2.45–2.64 — about 40% fewer — and is faster on all three pairs (1.50 vs 1.34, 1.61 vs 1.14, 1.79 vs 1.20), because its proposal costs 0.6 ms against 3.5–7.4 ms. A comparison in accepted tokens between drafters of different cost is not a speed comparison; that pushes back on train-the-drafter-on-accepted-length-not-likelihood, whose training argument survives intact and whose metric does not. Second, the (1+α) result generalizes past contrastive decoding to any composite verification target — guidance-style decoding, reward-tilted sampling, layer contrast, safety steering — as draft the dominant term, verify the composite. Held at consolidating, and the honest baseline is in the paper: expert-only speculative decoding still reaches 2.55× where DCD reaches 1.75×, so contrastive decoding is now affordable rather than free.

Status note (2026-08-20): holds consolidating, and the map's verification-budget question gets a systems answer that inverts its own prescription. This map established analytically that the budget has an interior optimum which moves with block size and with the hardware, and prescribed a search (the-verification-budget-has-an-interior-optimum, speculation-length-has-a-closed-form-inversion-point). vllm-dspark-adaptive-verification (T2, official vLLM engineering post, merged in PR #47808) observes that if the optimum depends on batch composition then no offline value survives a scheduler step, and makes the budget a per-step decision: a confidence head gives per-position survival probabilities, a running product gives per-sequence survival, and a global top-B selection across all in-flight requests allocates a shared budget to the drafts most likely to be accepted, scored against a startup-profiled cost table rather than an analytic model. One policy then reproduces long-block behaviour at low concurrency and short-block at high. Status does not move, and the reason is that the published claim is Pareto-frontier optimality with no table behind it — no tokens per second, no baseline ratio, no accepted-length figures beyond "first drafted token >70%, seventh <10%", and no quality measurement at all, on one model on 8×B300. Two things transfer to a single card anyway. Batch size one is the low-concurrency limit where this policy's own logic says draft long, so the local win is available today by raising num_speculative_tokens — the shipped default is a compromise struck for concurrency a local user does not have. And the feature is incompatible with LoRA, pipeline parallelism, --enforce-eager and output logprobs, which is the third recorded case of speculative decoding failing to compose with something the local stack wants, after speculation-and-quantization-compete-for-the-same-headroom and speculations-transient-memory-evicts-the-prefix-cache — and it collides directly with the same night's adapter-switching result on adapter-objects-and-serving.

Sub-threads: none — the apparent strands (exactness and its relaxations, verification cost, drafter shape and provenance) are the three terms of one equation — accepted length against draft cost plus verify cost, subject to preserving the target distribution — and they move each other rather than standing alone: making drafting free relocates the whole optimization to verification, and the backbone's memory structure sets verification cost. Status note (2026-08-25): holds consolidating, and two sources land on the same evening from opposite ends of the hardware range, agreeing that the headline multiplier is the least portable thing in a speculative-decoding paper. lilicorr-correlated-parallel-drafts (T1, NVIDIA; nine benchmarks × two targets × two decoding regimes, three seeds at T=1, a 90-point throughput sweep) proposes a correlation head over a parallel block drafter: DFlash-class drafters are trained on per-position marginals, so their proposals are individually plausible and jointly incoherent — the exact failure prefix acceptance punishes. Give each of the top-K candidates per slot an in and an out vector, score adjacent pairs by cosine similarity, and the block's joint structure falls out of one batched matmul with only a greedy walk left sequential. Acceptance length +12–19% over vanilla DFlash for a head costing ~2.8% of per-block latency; highest throughput in 70 of 72 settings. The number this map should keep is underneath: the same system runs 3.49× at concurrency 1 and 1.69× at concurrency 32, and 3.57× → 1.33× as output entropy rises, against a 0.1–0.3× spread between the four competing methods anywhere in that grid. A speculative speedup quoted without its concurrency and its output entropy is quoted at one unstated point on two axes that move it further than the choice of drafter does — and the favourable corollary for this vault is that concurrency 1 is where speculation pays best, which reverses nearly every other lever on these maps. The same paper supplies the sharper methodology finding: its competitor's head costs 1.97 ms unoptimized and 0.67 ms after the proposing authors apply their own fusion and CUDA-graph treatment to it — a 2.9× swing from engineering, larger than every algorithmic difference reported, published as a before/after table rather than asserted. Separately, precompiled-pipeline-shards-intel-ai-pc-fleet (T1) removes a precondition this map never wrote down: the canonical implementation assumes a rewindable cache, and on a runtime with opaque device state a physical trim costs ~48 ms per step — enough to make speculation a net loss. Leaving the rejected drafts resident and zeroing their attention-mask positions is bit-identical at essentially zero cost, which is what makes 1.33× single-node and a 3.1× four-node 70B result possible on machines with no paged attention at all. Status does not move: LiLiCorr is single-H100 with no stated code release and its margins over the nearest baselines are 2–5%, and the fleet result is one vendor's toolchain on one silicon family.

What we believe now:

  • A fixed speculative token budget was itself an unpriced cost, and adaptive budgeting targets the latency metric this map has under-covered (new 2026-08-31). vLLM v0.28.0 ships adaptive per-step speculative token budgeting — draft count set per step rather than fixed — delivering ~60% better DSpark time-to-first-token (TTFT). TTFT is a prefill/scheduling-latency metric, not the steady-state tokens/s figure most entries here report, and it matters specifically for interactive single-turn latency (a chat response starting to stream) rather than batch throughput. Single named configuration (DSpark); no steady-state throughput trade reported alongside the TTFT win, so whether adaptive budgeting costs anything in this map's usual metric is unknown from this digest.   • The draft-and-verify win is algorithmic, not kernel-local — it survives a vendor change intact — and there is still no shippable default draft length (new 2026-08-23). Run on AMD Instinct MI300X and MI355X under ROCm across five drafter types (native MTP, Gemma 4 MTP, EAGLE-3, DFlash, DSpark) and several model families, speedups land 1.15×–2.87× with the same qualitative ordering the NVIDIA results here report: Gemma 4-26B-A4B with DFlash 2.87× / 2.79× (MATH500 / HumanEval), Gemma 4-31B 2.34× / 2.05×, Kimi-K2.5 2.68×, Qwen3-8B with DSpark 1.15×–1.63×. Absolute: Gemma 4-26B 2,181 → 6,161 tok/s, Qwen3.5-122B 1,446 → 3,183 tok/s. That portability is worth naming because it cuts against this vault's standing lesson that systems progress expires with the silicon (kernel-performance-expires-with-the-gpu-generation) — speculation exploits a structural imbalance (memory-bound decode against compute-bound verification) that both vendors' parts share, not a tuned kernel. The published per-position acceptance curve is the more durable artifact: 88 / 77 / 67 / 59 / 52 / 45 / 39% at positions 1–7 (Gemma 4-26B, DFlash, MATH500, N=7). A decaying series means each drafted token is worth less than the last at the same verification cost, so the optimum sits where marginal acceptance stops covering marginal verify — and all three terms move. Measured, native MTP's optimum landed N=4 to N=7 by model, and the maintainers state the throughput-maximizing length "was not constant." This confirms the 2026-08-12 finding from the tuning side: make drafting free and the whole optimization moves to verification — the parallel drafters are exactly the ones whose N is bounded by acceptance alone. The standing complaint applies undiminished: every number is datacenter silicon at serving concurrency, and nothing here measures batch-one on a consumer card.   • The verification budget is better scheduled per step than configured per deployment (new 2026-08-20). If the optimum depends on batch composition, and batch composition changes every scheduler step, then no offline value is right for long. vLLM's shipped implementation turns the budget into a decision variable with three parts: a confidence head scoring each draft position (survival decays hard — first token

    70%, seventh <10%), a global top-B selection across all in-flight requests so slots go to the drafts most likely to be used rather than being split evenly, and a cost table profiled at engine startup, which absorbs the hardware dependence this map identified analytically and could not close. One policy then behaves as a long block at low concurrency and a short one at high, and the operator-facing payoff is the deletion of a tuning knob rather than a speedup number — none is published. For a single-card reader the deduction is not the scheduler: batch size one is the low-concurrency limit where the same logic says draft long, so raising num_speculative_tokens captures most of it today. Held suggestive: no throughput table, one model, 8×B300, no quality measurement. [T2] the-speculation-budget-is-a-scheduling-variable-not-a-configuration-value

  • The expert union that makes speculation lose on a sparse model is a decision variable, not a cost to schedule around — and the first method to treat it that way runs in llama.cpp on hardware this vault actually cares about, at the price of the exactness guarantee (new 2026-08-19, and the map's first edge-native measurement). Prior work on this collision has been policy: decide whether to speculate, or how long. Two of the three mechanisms here re-aim decisions the system is already making. Expansion prices each candidate by U_i = p_i·T_AR / (|E_i \ ∪_{j∈S} E_j|·T_exp + C_draft) and admits only U_i ≥ 1, so a candidate is judged by the experts it adds rather than by confidence — which matters because draft confidence piles up near 1.0 for retained tokens and barely discriminates, while equally-confident candidates differ several-fold in expert cost; the resulting cost model tracks measured verification latency at R² = 0.943–0.999, MAPE 0.8–4.5% across twelve model-by-budget cells. Gating then biases borderline experts toward ones already in flight, cutting the reuse ratio 0.6017 → 0.4453 (HumanEval) and 0.5893 → 0.3943 (GSM8K). The baselines are the confirmation: naive expert- and layer-sparsity self-speculation run at 0.12×–0.99× of plain autoregressive decoding on a Jetson Orin NX 16 GB with experts on SSD — speculating actively hurts — against 1.3–5.3× (≈2.0× average) for the combined method, and 1.2–2.9× on an RTX 4090. The exactness break is the map's cost and must be carried: the gating bias is applied to the target model's routing logits during verification, so certified tokens come from a model whose routing was edited — top-1 agreement 89.1–93.0%, mean KL 0.026–0.065, perplexity ratio 1.012–1.013, i.e. roughly one token in ten. Task accuracy nonetheless moves up on all four models, on the multiple-choice-and-pass@1 instrument that is least sensitive to this damage and with no long-form evaluation anywhere. The third mechanism is free and portable: share the target's KV cache with the draft, roll back draft writes before verification, commit only accepted tokens — +23% to +79% acceptance, distribution-preserving, and not MoE-specific at all. The practitioner split is clean: take the first and third mechanisms for an exact ~2× at tight memory, add the second only if you can afford a different model.   • A draft-tree search that looks inherently sequential usually is not, and the serialization — not the search quality — is what it costs (new 2026-08-15). Path-dependent scoring appears to force node-wise best-first expansion, but with a non-positive depth bonus the globally top-B prefixes are prefix-closed and exactly the heap's output, so the search collapses into one sort after a depth-batched pass. Measured against the exact sequential alternative at identical acceptance, that is worth 2.2× in round time and turns 4.38× into 9.77×. Two riders: coverage and per-branch causal correction are one quantity (chain-correct- then-build-a-tree costs up to 16.4% τ at the same latency), and because correction batches cheaply, the right shape is a supertree wider than the tree you verify. [T1] a-best-first-draft-tree-can-be-built-by-one-sort, every-branch-needs-its-own-correction-not-the-chains

  • Accepted length is not a speed metric, and when the drafter's cost is the variable it points the wrong way (new 2026-08-14 — the sharpest form of the belief below, and a correction to how this map has been reading its own sources). Across three expert/amateur pairs the configuration accepting ~40% fewer tokens per cycle is uniformly faster: 1.44 vs 2.45 accepted at 1.50× vs 1.34×; 1.48 vs 2.49 at 1.61× vs 1.14×; 1.46 vs 2.64 at 1.79× vs 1.20×. The entire difference is proposal latency, 0.6 ms against 3.5–7.4 ms. Accepted length prices one of the two terms in the cycle, so it ranks drafters correctly only when their cost is held fixed — which is exactly what a single paper's ablation does and what reading across papers does not. Report accepted length divided by cycle cost, or report wall-clock; nothing else is a speed number. This narrows train-the-drafter-on-accepted-length-not-likelihood rather than overturning it: acceptance remains the right training objective, and stops being the right comparison metric. A second-order finding rides along — the cheap misaligned drafter also degrades more gracefully as the target moves (retaining 77.4% of acceptance at α=0.5 against 62.9%, and 81.9% against 65.8%), because it approximates a fixed distribution rather than a shifting one. [T1, one study, one benchmark for the decomposition] See accepted-length-can-be-anti-correlated-with-speedup.

  • When the verification target is composite, draft the dominant term rather than the composite (new 2026-08-14). Approximating a reweighted product of two distributions means each part's error re-enters at the exponent that combines it — expert-side error at (1+α) — while the correction being reconstructed is typically smaller than that error (81.1% of positions below signal 1.0 against 48.7% above proposal KL 2.0). Aligning the proposal with the verification target, the default instinct everywhere else in this literature, is therefore net-negative here, confirmed twice under controlled conditions: matched cross-α training prefers α=0 in every configuration but one, and an explicit dual-drafter decomposition loses on accepted length, draft-to-target KL and top-1 match simultaneously. Exactness survives because only the proposer changes — the accept/reject guarantee is indifferent to where drafts come from. The rule generalizes to any reweighted target (guidance, reward tilting, layer contrast, safety steering), which is untested outside contrastive decoding and is the map's cheapest available follow-up. [T1, three model pairs + 70B] See draft-the-dominant-term-verify-the-composite.

  • Drafting cost, not just draft quality, is a design variable — and once a drafter generates in parallel the entire optimization is about verification (new 2026-08-12, and it closes the drafting side of the verification-cost argument this map has been building since 2026-08-09). Every dynamic speculative-length method picks γ by estimating how many tokens will be accepted, which is the right question only while each extra draft token costs another drafter pass — the cost model behind speculation-length-has-a-closed-form-inversion-point. A diffusion drafter emits the whole block in parallel at near-zero marginal cost, so the question becomes "how many generated tokens are worth verifying," and the criterion that falls out is marginal: extend only while the acceptance gain exceeds the added verification cost. Training-free, plug-and-play, provably convergent, and lossless — the target still verifies, preserving the property speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution makes load-bearing here. 2.1×–3.5× typical over autoregressive decoding on Qwen2.5-7B (MATH-500 3.54× at 5.11 mean accepted length; MT-Bench 2.39× at 3.43), up to 8.49× in the best cell, across six targets to a 30B-A3B MoE and three independent diffusion-speculation base methods. The complementary bound arrives the same night from the opposite direction: a nested drafter — a sub-model literally contained in the verifier, trained jointly with it — has no target-alignment problem that can be improved on, and is worth 14–26%. Read together they localize the bottleneck. Removing drafter–target mismatch entirely buys a quarter at most; making drafting free buys 2–3×. Drafting was not the constraint; checking is — which is what this map's three newest structural findings already said from the verification side. Bounded: A100 at 1K context only, and the nested result is one 3B-max suite quoting throughput with neither its sampling temperature nor its attention backend. [T1] a-diffusion-drafter-moves-the-question-from-generation-to-verification, nesting-the-drafter-inside-the-verifier-removes-drafter-alignment

  • "Verification costs about one target forward pass" is a property of the KV cache, not of speculation — and the two architectures the field is moving to both break it (new 2026-08-09, and it is the map's first structural claim about where the assumption came from). Verifying γ+1 candidates in one pass works because attention reads an append-only, branch-independent cache. Replace part of that with a recurrent linear-attention state — every hybrid stack now shipping, three gated-DeltaNet layers per full-attention layer — and the state is path-dependent, so each candidate branch implies a different one. A naive tree walk replays the recurrence per branch (serial recurrence climbing from 4% to 27% of the target forward as the tree grows 8 → 64 nodes) and snapshots a full state per proposal node (36 GiB for eight 32-node requests on a 122B model). This is the same failure offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass found on a sparse backbone in a different currency (weight traffic, 5.8× a token-decode at K=16), so the general form is: the cost of verifying a draft is a property of the backbone's memory structure, not of the draft. It is repairable, expensively: an exact closed form for the gated delta rule over a tree, its inverse taken as a finite Neumann series because depth is bounded, plus branches stored as token-scale factors instead of states — 3.4–7.7× on the verification kernel, 82–99× less transient state, up to 4.72× autoregressive decode throughput and 2.03× the strongest tree-speculative baseline, with mean accepted tokens essentially unchanged (this is systems, not drafting). Two riders. The gains are largest on the slower machine (4.72× on a 273 GB/s GB10 against 3.62× on an A100), which is what local-inference's currency test predicts and the first speculation result in-vault that improves as hardware gets more bandwidth-bound. And the memory saved is paid to the prefix cache: hit rate 58–70% → 91–93% on an agent workload, TTFT down 67.6%, TPOT down 49.9%, with no change to the drafter. [T1] See verification-cost-is-a-property-of-the-backbones-memory-structure, speculations-transient-memory-evicts-the-prefix-cache.

  • The draft's shape is a third lever alongside its size and the verification rule, and both of its knobs are set wrong by default (new 2026-08-09). Two results, one cheap and one cheaper. Shape by decoding regime: block drafters emit b positions as independent marginals while verification checks them sequentially conditioned on what was accepted — invisible under greedy decoding, and degrading monotonically as the target's sampling entropy rises. Correlating the positions with a low-rank categorical mixture recovers +5.6–5.8% accepted length at T=0.7 and +9.3–12.1% at T=1.5, for 1.4–3.4% per-iteration latency, with the verification rule untouched. So an acceptance rate quoted without its sampling configuration is underspecified — the third unreported-configuration finding on this axis in two weeks, after the attention backend and the query-agnostic setting, and the first about the workload rather than the runtime. Shape by policy: a semi-autoregressive drafter's refinement head is a conditional that greedy decoding evaluates at one parent; re-scoring it per concrete parent turns the chain into a tree on the same checkpoint, with no retraining and no extra backbone pass — mean accepted length 9.41 → 11.16, autoregressive speedup 6.14× → 6.60×, gains of +3.1% to +29.5% across three sizes and nine benchmarks, with a three-way isolation showing +9.1% comes specifically from the per-parent conditioning rather than from having a tree. The bill is that draft-side latency goes 1.09 ms → 5.24 ms against a 3.55 ms backbone: under speculation-length-has-a-closed-form-inversion-point that is γ rising sharply, and it wins only because acceptance rises faster — on an H20 running an unquantized 4B. Both the tree width and the verification budget have interior optima (speedup peaks at N=32 for a 7-token block, N=64 for 16, and the hardware-efficient width is 128 rows on an A100 against 256 on a GB10). [T1] See a-drafters-acceptance-rate-depends-on-the-temperature-you-decode-at, a-greedy-draft-head-is-an-under-queried-conditional, the-verification-budget-has-an-interior-optimum, train-the-drafter-on-accepted-length-not-likelihood.

  • Speculation's exactness flag now has a dial on it, and the third relaxation mechanism is the affordable one (new 2026-08-07). The map's 2026-07-31 finding was that lossy verification abandons the exactness guarantee and that the damage is set by an unrelated configuration choice; the ablation behind it split one lenience knob into a nearly-free cap and a ruinous interpolation (1.67× block efficiency for 25 Pass@1 points). A third mechanism relaxes the greedy argmax rule instead of the sampling rule, and controls it with three simultaneous constraints — a local target-logit regret gate normalized by remaining proposal length (r_i / q_i ≤ g, so an early mismatch is tolerated more readily because more suffix is recoverable), a per-block exception cap, and a persistent request-level regret budget carried across rounds. That last one is new in kind: every other lenient scheme in this vault sets a per-acceptance tolerance and lets consequences accumulate wherever they fall. B = 0 reproduces strict greedy verification exactly, so this is a continuum with the lossless case at one end rather than a separate mode — which preserves the property speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution made load-bearing for every stacking argument here. Price: +3.05% to +15.26% fixed-workload throughput (mean +7.78% on Qwen3-14B across seven tasks, accepted length +0.49 to +0.67 tokens) for at most −1.52 accuracy points anywhere, with HumanEval (−0.61) and MT-Bench (−0.64) the two tasks that regress — the same instruction-compliance-and-code signature the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff found most precision-sensitive. Two deflations. The gains are single digits on datacenter cards (an L20, eight H20s), an order of magnitude below the 2–3× lossless speculation buys on a bandwidth-bound single-stream decode, so this refines a lever rather than adding one. And the authors state that the controls must be re-audited per target–drafter–workload combination — safety is not transferable, which in evaluation-contamination's vocabulary makes a serving configuration an undisclosed preparation step. [T1] See lenient-verification-can-be-metered-as-a-request-budget, the-trajectory-diverges-completely-and-the-score-does-not-move.

  • Speculative decoding's exactness is a flag, not a property — and no speed metric can tell you which side of it you are on (new 2026-07-31, and it revises the map's own yardstick rather than extending it). This map has treated speculation as the one lever that spends nothing but idle arithmetic. The "lossy verification" relaxations now shipping spend distributional fidelity: truncation-based schemes emit the draft's distribution restricted to an allowed set, collaborative ones emit a blend. The damage is under a point under plain single-draft speculation and 6.28 / 5.29 / 8.82 / 4.90 points (MATH / MBPP+ / INCLUDE / BFCL) under EAGLE-3 tree verification, average gap widening −0.32 → −6.32 — so the cost is set by a second, unrelated configuration choice. Block efficiency and tokens/second improve in every one of these configurations by construction, which makes self-benchmarking blind to the failure: the fastest cell in the study (BE 9.15) is its worst model (50.26 Pass@1 vs a lossless 75.84). Operating rule: run lossless, or report the verification rule with every acceptance-rate and throughput number. The one relaxation worth having is isolated by ablation — cap the draft only where it severely overestimates the target (≤0.7 Pass@1 for a small block-efficiency gain); the adaptive interpolation bundled with it in shipping implementations costs 25 points. [T1]

  • The speedup is a serving-regime measurement, and the regime moves it further than the drafter does. One system, one H100, one target: 3.49× at concurrency 1 → 1.69× at 32, and 3.57× → 1.33× as output entropy rises. The spread between LiLiCorr, Domino, DSpark and vanilla DFlash at any fixed cell is 0.1–0.3×. The mechanism is the one that gives speculation its win in the first place — decode at batch one is bandwidth-bound so the arithmetic is free, and raising concurrency lets other requests occupy exactly that free capacity. Two readings follow. Comparing a 4.5× from one paper against a 2.1× from another is, with high probability, comparing two concurrencies. And the local single-user case is the regime where speculation is worth most, which inverts the usual direction of this vault's complaints. [T1] See a-speculative-speedup-is-quoted-at-an-unstated-concurrency.

  • A rewindable KV cache is not a precondition — masking is bit-identical to eviction. Canonical implementations assume paged attention or a trim API, which quietly excluded every runtime with opaque device state, disproportionately the on-device ones. A physical trim through host state costs ~48 ms per decode step with a 72-token cache; setting attention_mask[j] = 0 at rejected-draft positions is bit-exact and free, yielding 1.33× ± 0.13 across eight prompt types at 70.7% acceptance and K=3. The bill is that live and allocated cache size diverge until compaction, which matters where capacity binds. The same host state round-trip that is fatal per step is negligible once per generation, where it buys whole-sequence prefix caching — a cost that is prohibitive at one frequency and cheap at another. [T1] See a-rejected-draft-can-be-masked-instead-of-evicted.

  • A parallel block drafter's proposals are individually right and jointly wrong, and the fix is cheap. Block drafters train on per-slot cross-entropy, so nothing couples slot i to slot i+1 — the worst possible shape under prefix acceptance. Assigning each top-K candidate an out and an in vector and scoring adjacent pairs by cosine similarity captures the joint structure as batched matrix products without ever materializing a distribution exponential in block length; the head is ~36M parameters and 0.28 ms of a 10.15 ms block. Acceptance length rises 12–19% on every benchmark tested. Two limits: the reranker only reorders tokens the drafter already proposed, so acceptance is bounded by top-K coverage, and whether couplings beyond adjacent pairs repay their cost is open — adjacency is what makes it a matmul. [T1] See correlating-marginals-recovers-a-block-drafters-joint-structure.

  • Proposal length tracks the cost of a round trip, whatever the round trip is made of. This map established the interior optimum against batch size and hardware. On a network-distributed pipeline the same law appears with hops as the cost term: K = 7 on a LAN (compute-bound), K = 10 at ≥50 ms/hop, and a four-stage 70B topology paying three worker round trips per verify also lands on K = 10. Amortization grows with pipeline depth — a K = 10 verify emits ~7.5 tokens per target round trip, so each token pays ~13% of the latency it would at K = 1. Different substrate, same equation. [T1]

The leading open-weights model is 2.78T parameters with 104.2B active, shipping with MXFP4 expert weights — roughly 1.4 TB of weights before any cache — and the report contains no VRAM figure, no batch-size-1 tokens/s, no offload configuration and no single-node deployment anywhere; its serving section is about scheduling across clusters (prefix-cache affinity with consistent hashing to a primary and secondary cluster, and budget-based admission control because per-request cost spans three orders of magnitude). "Open weights" and "runnable on hardware you own" have come apart.   • 2026-07-31 — ingest of lossy-verification-speculative-decoding (T1, arXiv 2026-07-29, code released): the stack's one exact lever stopped being exact, and the metric a local user would benchmark with cannot detect it. This map treats speculative decoding as uniquely safe to stack because the rejection rule provably reproduces the target's distribution (speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution) — every stacking argument the map has made about speculation assumes that. The "lossy verification" schemes now shipping reduce to two mechanisms: truncation-based (SpecCascade/min-p, Medusa's typical acceptance/η-sampling) accepts a token if it lands in an allowed set and rejects with no resampling, so what is emitted is "the renormalized draft distribution restricted to 𝒜_Θ" — a 0.5B model wearing a 72B's name; and collaborative (CoS, lenience) which blends draft and target. The measured cost depends on an unrelated configuration choice. Under plain single-draft speculation the gaps against matched truncation-sampling baselines run −0.88 to +0.76 across MATH, MBPP+, INCLUDE and BFCL — indistinguishable from free. Under EAGLE-3 tree verification typical acceptance loses 6.28 / 5.29 / 8.82 / 4.90 points on the same four, with the average gap widening −0.32 → −6.32. The trap is that block efficiency and tokens/second improve in every lossy configuration: the best-looking cell in the study by block efficiency (9.15, 1.67× over lossless) is the worst model in it (50.26 Pass@1 against 75.84). Someone benchmarking their own machine — which is what this map tells people to do — sees the speedup and gets no signal the distribution moved. The constructive half is cheap and specific. Lenience ships as one knob fusing two mechanisms, and the ablation separates them: the overshoot ceiling alone costs at most 0.7 Pass@1 (75.13–75.93 vs a lossless 75.84) at block efficiency 5.54–5.59 vs 5.47, while the adaptive interpolation it is bundled with buys 1.67× for 25 points. So: cap the draft where it severely overestimates the target, and leave the undershoot region alone. One consequence for this map's own standing question — whether a quantized draft still agrees with a quantized target at usable acceptance rates now carries a precondition: run it under lossless verification, or report the verification rule alongside the acceptance rate, because under a lossless rule a worse draft costs only acceptance while under a lossy one it costs output quality, and neither side currently reports which was used. Bounded: two model families at a fixed draft/target pairing, no open-ended generation, nothing measured on consumer hardware, and the EAGLE-3 rows are Llama-3.1-8B where the standard-SD rows are Qwen2.5-72B, so the widening is confounded with a change of model and scale. See lossy-verification-silently-replaces-the-target-distribution, the-overshoot-ceiling-is-the-whole-of-lenience.

Open questions:

  • What does speculation cost on the machine this vault is about? The strand's measurements run on datacenter cards, a simulated ASIC, and one offloaded-MoE consumer setup. The plain case — a 7–8B model with a small drafter on one 24 GB card at batch 1 — has no end-to-end number here, and the currency-test argument inherited from local-inference predicts speculation should pay there better than in a batched server, because the idle arithmetic it spends is exactly what a single-user decode has spare.
  • Does the exactness dial survive an evidence check? Lenient verification is metered as a per-request budget and reported to cost at most 1.52 accuracy points — measured as task accuracy, while over 95% of generated trajectories differ by hash. kv-cache-management's fixed-trace replay protocol is released and would answer whether the surviving derivations are still valid.
  • Where should a drafter come from, and what shape should its output be? (revised 2026-08-09.) The provenance options in this vault — a downloaded small model, a self-speculative pass, a multi-token-prediction head trained anyway, a sparse-cache view of the model itself, a resident distilled draft expert — have never been compared on one backbone at one budget. As of tonight the question has a second axis that is cheaper to vary and was never separated from the first: the shape of the draft (chain, block, tree), which turns out to be partly a policy rather than an architecture — the same checkpoint yields a chain or a tree depending on how you query its refinement head, for +9.1% accepted length. A single-backbone comparison should now cross provenance with shape, and both are inference-time choices for at least some drafters.
  • Nobody reports the temperature, and the effect is the size of the method differences (new 2026-08-09.) Accepted length degrades monotonically as target sampling entropy rises, and correcting for it is worth +5.6% at T=0.7 and +12.1% at T=1.5 on the same drafter. Every acceptance rate and speedup on this map is quoted without its sampling configuration, and most workloads a local user runs are not greedy. Re-reporting even two existing results at two temperatures would establish whether the map's comparisons are between methods or between decoding regimes — the same question cache-compression-must-be-scored-query-agnostic settled for the neighbouring map, and it took one table.
  • Why does speculation fight sparse routing, and is the fix portable? Three independent measurements now say drafting inflates the activated-expert union. The one proposed remedy — route per prompt rather than per token — is pure software and has never been tried on a GPU.
