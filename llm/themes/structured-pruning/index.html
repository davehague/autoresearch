Structured pruning (depth, width, budget adherence, what removal costs) — map

Coined 2026-08-15 (auto run) — auto-coined at emerging; flagged for David's review. Pruning has been homeless in this vault since the seed. Its one dedicated finding, pruning-costs-trustworthiness-where-quantization-does-not, sits under post-training-quantization because that was the only map that could hold a compression comparison; vocabulary pruning lives under tokenization-and-vocabulary; attention sparsity lives under transformer-architecture. None of those can state what pruning actually trades. The case for a map is that removing parameters is a different object from re-encoding them, and it has its own economics on three axes the quantization map has no slot for: budget adherence (a pruner is asked for a size and does not deliver it), the depth/width split (the axis that preserves quality is not the axis that buys throughput), and recovery fine-tuning (a mandatory second training stage that no quantization method needs). The alternative considered and rejected was folding this into post-training-quantization, which would have put a lever with a measured order-of-magnitude worse exchange rate inside a map whose founding claim is that 4-bit is nearly free — and would have buried the one comparison a practitioner most needs to see. Coined at emerging and deliberately low: one T1 source, however thorough, with a second (the trustworthiness study) contributing from the quantization map's side rather than from here.

Status: emerging (coined at emerging 2026-08-15; one T1 with six baselines, four architectures and 18 tasks, plus one cross-map T1 agreeing on the exchange rate — but no independent replication of any number here, and the throughput claim rests on a single figure)

What we believe now:

  • At matched footprint, quantize rather than prune, and the margin is roughly an order of magnitude. Removing 25% of parameters costs 13–17 points of average retention across an 18-task suite after recovery fine-tuning; 35% costs 23. An entire FP16→INT3 sweep costs about 1.6. Two independent instruments now agree — a capability suite here, a trustworthiness benchmark from the quantization side — across unstructured, N:M and structured depth+width sparsity. [T1] a-quarter-of-the-parameters-costs-an-eighth-of-the-capability
  • The pruning axis that preserves quality is not the axis that buys speed, and they move in opposite directions. Fine-grained width pruning retains 97.95% at 2.90% task-wise variance and yields little or no speedup — width pruners measure below the unpruned base model, because irregular tensor shapes are hostile to the kernels. Coarse depth pruning retains 97.47% at 10.50% variance and takes the largest raw speedups by removing whole compute blocks. A pruner is not choosing a point on a quality/size curve; it is choosing which of two goods to buy. [T1] the-pruning-axis-that-preserves-quality-is-not-the-one-that-buys-speed
  • A target compression ratio is a request, not a specification. Six published pruners miss it by up to 33%, always toward under-pruning — the direction that fails the memory-constrained deployment the pruning was for — averaging a CRAFT of 0.78 and reaching 0.58. A fine-grained second stage whose only job is to fill the residual budget takes it to 0.98 across five target ratios and four architectures. Nothing reported this until someone defined a score for it. [T1] a-pruner-misses-the-compression-ratio-you-asked-for
  • Recovery fine-tuning is not optional and it does not close the gap. It is worth 8–30 points of retention in every regime tested, which means an unrecovered pruning number is measuring a different procedure — and even with it, a 25% cut still costs 13 points where 4-bit quantization costs ~1. [T1] a-quarter-of-the-parameters-costs-an-eighth-of-the-capability
  • Averages hide the damage; report the spread. Task-wise standard deviation runs 8.8% to 30.4% across methods, and a pruner at ~50% average retention with 30% spread has not degraded uniformly — it has destroyed particular capabilities. Every baseline tested has at least one configuration in which it disintegrates, with mean ranks spreading 1.25 to 6.50, so a pruning result quoted from one model at one ratio predicts little about the next. [T1] a-quarter-of-the-parameters-costs-an-eighth-of-the-capability, averaging-a-suite-hides-its-weakest-dimension
  • Importance estimation is the cost, and it amortizes. It is ~96% of a ~27-minute pruning run — the pruning step itself takes 56 seconds — and scores computed at one compression ratio transfer to another at a cost of 0.17 retention points. So the hit-and-trial search the budget-adherence defect forces is cheaper than it looks: reuse the scores, re-solve the allocation. [T1] the-pruning-axis-that-preserves-quality-is-not-the-one-that-buys-speed
  • Structural importance is a robust signal where activation statistics are not, and its shape is recognisable. Varying calibration corpus across Slim Orca, Alpaca and C4 moves the knapsack pruner 0.85 points where a baseline moves 11.6; layer importance is high at the boundaries and low in the middle, stable across budgets — the same profile semantic-content-is-assembled-by-mid-depth found from the interpretability side, arrived at by a dynamic-programming solver. Whether this is a genuine property of structural signals or just a gentler dose than 4-bit is unresolved. [T1] a-pruner-misses-the-compression-ratio-you-asked-for Scope condition, added 2026-08-16, and it is the first boundary this claim has: the robustness measured here belongs to a structural signal computed over the architecture, and does not extend to an activation-derived feature subspace. A second T1 tried the systems-friendly version — fix a low-dimensional subspace at prefill, reuse it through decode — and found it does not transfer across tasks at all, with a subspace derived on one corpus degrading sharply on any other and "unimportant" head dimensions unstable across tasks, layers and decoding steps. Read the 0.85 points as a property of layer-level importance, not of importance estimation in general. [T1, suggestive] no-fixed-low-dimensional-subspace-survives-a-task-change
  • The static/dynamic boundary is worth more than any refinement inside the static family (new 2026-08-16). At matched retention 0.8, an input-adaptive reduction matches the dense baseline on summarization at every scale from 1B to 70B, while static and random pruning at the same retention "degrade severely." That is a larger gap than anything separating the six published pruners this map already compares — so before choosing among static importance criteria, the question is whether the decision has to be static at all. The catch is that dynamic selection is an inference-time technique and buys no footprint reduction: the weights all stay. The two are not substitutes, and this map should hold them as different products — pruning shrinks the artifact, dynamic reduction shrinks the work. [T1, suggestive] no-fixed-low-dimensional-subspace-survives-a-task-change, a-twenty-percent-compute-cut-buys-five-percent-of-the-time

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-08-16 — edge from rmm-input-adaptive-matrix-product-reduction (T1, primary map local-inference): the day-old calibration-robustness finding gets its first boundary, from an appendix describing an experiment that failed. Yesterday this map recorded that structural importance is calibration-insensitive — 0.85 points across Slim Orca, Alpaca and C4 where a competing pruner moves 11.6 — and flagged as a live anomaly that it sits badly beside the quantization map's four separate calibration findings. Part of that anomaly now resolves into a distinction rather than a contradiction. These authors began where a systems engineer would: identify a fixed low-dimensional subspace during prefill, reuse it through decode, and collect a smaller KV cache and less memory traffic along the way. It failed. A subspace derived from WikiText "performs well on that dataset, but degrades sharply when evaluated on other tasks or distributions," and head dimensions that look reliably inactive for one task are "not stable across tasks, layers, or inputs" — their conclusion being that hidden states admit no single globally valid low-dimensional subspace, because information migrates across dimensions with the input, the layer and the decoding step. So a structural importance score over the architecture transfers across corpora, and an activation-derived coordinate subspace does not transfer across tasks at all. A practitioner reading "importance estimation is robust" and applying it to a channel- or head-dimension selector is crossing a boundary the evidence does not. It also supplies a mechanism the quantization map's calibration thread never had: there was no stable subspace to calibrate against. The constructive half is the static/dynamic gap above, and the honest grade is suggestive — this is prose about unpublished failed experiments with no table, no numbers and no ablation. The controlled version is a day's work: one model, one selector, subspaces fitted on three corpora, all three evaluated on all three tasks. Status holds emerging: the map still rests on one dedicated source, and this is an edge from another map's primary.
  • 2026-08-15 — map coined on sniper-knapsack-depth-width-pruning (T1, IIT Delhi + Microsoft Research India; six baselines, four architectures including a fused-MLP model and an MoE, 18 tasks across five domains, code released). Coined at emerging and deliberately low: one source, self-contained but unreplicated. Three findings the vault could not have guessed, and one it half-knew. The interface defect is new and embarrassing for the field — structured pruners miss their target compression ratio by up to 33%, and the deviation runs toward under-pruning, so the artifact does not fit the budget it was produced for. The depth/width split is the deployment-relevant one: fine-grained pruning wins on both retention (97.95 vs 97.47) and consistency (2.90% vs 10.50% variance) and delivers no speedup at all, with width pruners measuring slower than the base model, while depth pruning takes the throughput and pays in instability. And the exchange rate confirms, on a completely different instrument, what pruning-costs-trustworthiness-where-quantization-does-not measured on TrustLLM three days ago: pruning costs roughly an order of magnitude more per unit of footprint than quantization, and recovery fine-tuning — the variable that study could not test — narrows the gap without closing it (87.34% retention at a 25% cut on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 82.64% on Qwen3-8B). Three edges out. Against post-training-quantization: the two levers do not sit on one footprint curve and should not be compared as if they did; that map keeps the trustworthiness note, which is where its safety argument lives, and this map carries the capability accounting. Against local-inference: this is the currency test in its starkest form, because one pruning axis moves footprint and latency in opposite directions, and the field reports only the first. Against data-curation and the quantization map's calibration thread: the calibration insensitivity is a live anomaly — structural importance moves 0.85 points across three unrelated corpora where calibration-corpus-choice-swings-a-quantized-models-math-score-again and quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model establish calibration content as load-bearing below 4 bits, and nobody has run both levers against the same sweep. Bounded honestly: no venue, no independent replication, full results at only two compression ratios (25% and 35%), the throughput comparison is one figure at one ratio on one model with the hardware unstated, and the recovery-fine-tuning recipe is not compared across methods — so some of the spread at the top may be RFT sensitivity rather than pruning quality.

Open questions:

  • Pruning versus quantization at matched footprint on one suite, run by one group. This is the map's founding claim and it is assembled from two papers that share no benchmark, no model and no metric. The "order of magnitude" is a comparison of relative damage at comparable footprint reduction, not a controlled experiment. One study — same models, same tasks, prune to 4× smaller against quantize to 4 bits, both with and without recovery fine-tuning — would settle the single most actionable question in this map, and nothing about it is expensive.
  • Does pruning compose with quantization, or do they spend the same headroom? Every deployment that needs pruning also wants 4-bit. speculation-and-quantization-compete-for-the-same-headroom is this vault's standing warning that levers assumed to stack do not, and the pruning literature and the quantization literature have never been run together. A pruned model has fewer, more load-bearing parameters, which is exactly the regime small-models-amplify-quantization-error-large-ones-attenuate-it says quantization damages most — so the prior should be that they anti-compose, and there is no measurement either way.
  • What does the throughput claim look like on hardware anyone runs? The depth/width speedup asymmetry is the map's most decision-relevant finding and it rests on a single figure at one compression ratio, on one model, with the hardware unnamed. Nothing here is measured on a CPU, on Apple silicon, under llama.cpp or under any quantized runtime — and irregular tensor shapes are precisely the thing a GGUF kernel is least equipped to handle, so the width-pruning penalty is likely worse locally than it is on a datacentre GPU.
  • Is calibration insensitivity real or is 25% just a gentle dose? Structural importance moves 0.85 points across three unrelated calibration corpora where quantization's activation statistics swing hard. Either the signal is genuinely more robust — which would be a useful asymmetry — or the compression ratio tested is simply not aggressive enough to expose it. Running the same calibration sweep at 50% and 75% would separate the two, and the 50% arm already reported (53.30 retention at 28.60% spread) suggests the regime exists.
  • What does pruning do to an MoE, specifically? GPT-OSS-20B is in the evaluation and posts the highest retention of any model tested (86.99% at 35%), which is either a real property of sparse architectures having more removable structure or an artifact of one model. mixture-of-experts holds that serving memory tracks total parameters while compute tracks active ones — pruning attacks exactly the term that binds on edge hardware, and no one has asked whether experts, layers or the router is the right unit to remove.
