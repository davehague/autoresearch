Consumer-hardware pretraining cost (from-scratch, not fine-tuning) — map

_Coined 2026-08-29 (auto run) — this vault's premise is "your own compute" outperforming or matching frontier models, and every from-scratch pretraining number the vault has recorded so far assumes a datacenter unit of account: data-curation's FineWeb-class scaling laws and its bitter-lesson filtering crossover (~1e30 FLOPs), tokenization-and-vocabulary's 1.27B nanochat models, a 1B model trained on 8 B200s in under three weeks. None of those is a number this vault's actual reader — one or two consumer cards — can reproduce. Tonight's source is the first to make the dollar cost of from-scratch pretraining on consumer GPU hardware its own subject and derive a predictive scaling law for it, rather than reporting it as an incidental footnote to an architecture or data result. What we believe now:

  • A 1.5B-class model trained from scratch on consumer GPUs costs a few thousand dollars, not a few hundred thousand, and the number comes with a predictive curve rather than standing alone as an anecdote (new 2026-08-29). Puro-2B trains from scratch on RTX 5090 (the desktop workstation/gaming card) at FP8 precision, up to 1.4 trillion tokens, and its best checkpoint approaches Qwen2.5-1.5B for under $6.9K total spend — against an estimated >$1.5M for Llama-3.2-3B's training run and >$700K for SmolLM3-3B's public reproduction. The more load-bearing claim is the fitted Puro Cost Scaling Law, which reads off ~$4.4K as already sufficient to match Qwen2-1.5B under the paper's protocol — meaning the achieved $6.9K result overshot the law's own predicted floor, and the curve itself, not the single point on it, is what a second team's run would test. Recipe, data, code and weights are all released, which is why this is graded strong on a single source: the claim is reproducible, not merely reported. Open and unresolved from this digest: GPU count and wall-clock time behind the dollar figure, whether the FP8 recipe needed a stability/healing fallback (which would also implicate training-time-quantization), and the exact evaluation suite. [T1] (a-cost-scaling-law-predicts-1-5b-class-quality-from-consumer-gpu-spend)

Open questions:

  • Does the Puro Cost Scaling Law hold at a second team's hands, on different consumer hardware? The law is fitted on one team's own RTX-5090 runs. The cheapest falsification test is someone else running the released recipe on a different consumer card (an RTX 4090, an RTX 3090, an Apple Silicon unified- memory machine) and checking whether the predicted $/quality curve holds, drifts, or breaks — everything needed (recipe, code, data) is already public.
  • Does the curve extend past 1.5B-3B parameters, and where does it cross the point where consumer hardware simply cannot hold the model? This map has no data yet on whether the same FP8-on-consumer-GPU approach scales its cost-efficiency argument to 7B-8B class targets, or whether memory capacity (not compute cost) becomes the binding constraint first — which would make this a memory-hierarchy-offload question rather than a pure cost one.
  • What does the FP8 training recipe cost in stability, and is any of it transferable to fine-tuning workflows? training-time-quantization has recorded that training into a low-precision format beats converting into it post-hoc, for fine-tuning. Whether Puro-2B's from-scratch FP8 recipe (healing steps, loss-scaling, any mixed-precision fallback) generalizes to that map's QAT thread is unexamined — the source digest this map was coined from does not yet extract that detail.
