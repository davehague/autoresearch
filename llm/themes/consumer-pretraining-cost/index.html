Consumer-GPU pretraining cost (from-scratch training priced in a consumer unit of account) — map

Coined 2026-08-31 (auto run) — flagged for David's review. This vault's premise is using data and your own compute to make open-weights models outperform frontier models on specific tasks, then quantize and run them locally — "your own compute" being the operative phrase. Every from-scratch pretraining result the vault has carried until now (Chinchilla-optimal scaling, sparse upcycling's ~50%-of-sunk-cost accounting) is priced in cluster-node-hours or an A100/H100-fleet compute budget — useful for comparing recipes, useless for answering "could I actually do this." This map exists to hold the founding question a fine-tuning-only vault never had to ask: what does training a model from scratch, rather than adapting one, cost on the hardware a practitioner can actually own?

Status: emerging Status note (2026-08-31, at coining): emerging. One source anchors the map — a single T1 with a fitted scaling law and full recipe/data/code/weight release, no independent replication yet attempted. The map moves to consolidating the first time a second, independent group reproduces either the cost point or the scaling law's predicted floor on different hardware or a different base recipe.

Sub-threads: none — one question (what does from-scratch pretraining cost on consumer hardware) with one source and one fitted law answering it; no separable strands yet at this bullet count [reviewed 2026-08-31 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now:

  • A 1.5B-class model trained from scratch on consumer GPUs, at FP8, can approach a same-class open model's quality for a few thousand dollars — and a fitted scaling law predicts an even lower floor (new 2026-08-31). Puro-2B trains up to 1.4 trillion tokens on consumer RTX 5090 GPUs in FP8 and its best checkpoint approaches Qwen2.5-1.5B for under $6.9K, against the paper's own estimated >$1.5M to reproduce Llama-3.2-3B's training run and >$700K for SmolLM3-3B. The more durable artifact is the fitted Puro Cost Scaling Law, whose own predicted floor is ~$4.4K to match Qwen2-1.5B — the $6.9K headline result overshot what the law says was necessary, and a law that predicts a floor is falsifiable in a way a single run is not. One paper, one lab, no independent replication of either the run or the law yet; full recipe, data, code and weights released (Apache 2.0), so replication is possible but has not happened. [T1] (a-cost-scaling-law-predicts-1-5b-class-quality-from-consumer-gpu-spend)

Open questions:

  • Does the Puro Cost Scaling Law's predicted floor hold under independent replication? The paper's own $6.9K run overshot the law's fitted $4.4K floor for a comparable target; nobody outside this one lab has yet tried to land on or below that floor with a different recipe or GPU generation. Corroboration here — not a bigger number, an independent one at any cost — is what moves this map to consolidating.
  • What happens off the RTX 5090? FP8-capable consumer parts vary generation to generation (4090 lacks native FP8 tensor-core support at the same throughput, 5090-class cards are new and expensive relative to the used-market cards most of this vault's premise assumes). Whether the recipe and the scaling law's constants transfer to older or cheaper consumer silicon is untested.
  • How does this interact with the rest of the vault's compute-substitution results? mixture-of-experts's sparse-upcycling result (convert a dense checkpoint to MoE for ~50% of sunk pretraining cost) and this map's from-scratch consumer-GPU number are two different answers to "how do I avoid paying frontier-scale compute" — upcycling assumes you already have a dense checkpoint and datacenter compute to continue training it; Puro assumes neither. Nobody has stacked them (upcycle a Puro-class from-scratch base) or compared their end costs on the same target quality bar.
