What we believe now:

  • This map has been written as though the policy sampled from and the policy updated are one object; they are the same parameters and a different function, and the disagreement reaches five nats at the worst token of a typical step (new 2026-08-23). RL post-training runs generation in an inference engine (vLLM) and the update in a training engine (Megatron). Floating-point addition is not associative and reduction order is fixed by the kernel, the batch shape and the parallelism layout — all of which differ between the two by design — so the log-probabilities the trainer computes are not the ones the sampler used, and the importance ratio at the centre of every policy-gradient update is formed against a policy that never generated anything. Measured on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B over DAPO-Math-17k: rollout-vs-training logprob difference mean ~10⁻³, std ~2×10⁻³, average per-step maximum 5.073. The documented failure mode, cited rather than reproduced, is a Fireworks GLM-5.2 run where clipping discarded ~45% of tokens and reward collapsed around step 20 under mismatched execution. Not all of it is kernels: for gated-DeltaNet linear attention, training's chunkwise-parallel and inference's recurrent formulation are algebraically identical and differ by mean ~10⁻⁵, max ~0.25, which no amount of kernel pinning would fix. But the fix as measured is a cost against an unmeasured benefit and belongs on the map that way. Unifying execution by contract plus batch-invariant kernels takes the tail gap to <10⁻⁴ and costs 25.3% end-to-end throughput (+31.3% generation, +18.6% training) — and over the 50-step window the authors report no meaningful reward improvement. Fifty steps on one model is far short of where the cited instabilities occur, so the null is close to uninformative about the risk case; the missing arm is obvious and cheap — take a configuration known to collapse under mismatch and show it surviving. Until then: carry the precondition as a named risk with a documented failure mode, not a recommended default. Practical read: a low clip fraction means this buys reproducibility for a quarter of your throughput; a high one makes mismatch a live suspect and the contract worth its cost as a diagnostic. [T2, self-reported, 8×H100, 50 steps] rollout-and-training-are-two-different-policies, bitwise-alignment-costs-a-quarter-of-throughput-and-has-not-yet-bought-a-measured-gain
  • Self-training on your own outputs corrupts what it already solved at 11–13× the noise floor, and the accuracy number conceals it (new 2026-08-23). On a curated 1,163-problem band, STaR corrupts 106 previously-solved problems and majority-vote SFT 88, against a design-matched frozen control's median of 10 and 8 over 120 orderings — and 58% of STaR's corruptions exceed 0.234, the largest solve-rate shift a frozen model produces across 20 pairs, so more than half the damage is outside the entire range re-evaluation noise can produce. Over the same runs STaR's mean accuracy rises 5.6–6.9 points. A method winning on the headline metric is destroying capability on more than a hundred problems it used to solve, and nothing in the average says so — the same failure of aggregates that post-training-quantization keeps rediscovering under compression, arriving here from post-training. Scope, carefully: all three damaged arms are iterated on the model's own outputs (SFT on own rationales, SFT on own majority votes, a TTRL-style policy gradient whose reward is the model's own agreement — that one collapses into unbounded repetition on AIME across all three seeds). Nothing here tests a checker-verified R1-Zero/GRPO loop, where the reward comes from outside the model, so this is a claim about self-sourced training signal rather than about strand 2. It gives peft-methods a rate where intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting gave a mechanism, at a rank and round count a practitioner would actually run. Bounded: the band is MATH-derived and likely in pretraining, so this is not a forgetting rate for a natural distribution — arguably the easier case, which makes it the more alarming result. [T1, Qwen3-8B, rank-32 LoRA, 3 rounds] self-training-corrupts-what-it-already-solved
  • A rollout batch reused for K inner updates carries a dense reward whose student term is stale by up to K−1 updates, and refreshing it is free. PPO's actor forward already computes current-student log-probabilities, so reading the reward's student-dependent term from there instead of from rollout-time values costs 0.5 s in a 1298 s outer step (inside step-to-step variation) and is worth +0.81 points on the one benchmark that has measured it. The argument mentions no teachers, domains or routing, so it applies to any on-policy loop with K > 1 whose reward depends on the policy's own probabilities. [T1, suggestive — one 3B student, one value of K]
  • (1b) Whether preference RL helps is a property of the prior you start from, not of the model's size — and PPO's instability at small scale is three engineering bugs, not a law (new 2026-08-01, and it is the map's first evidence about running these methods rather than about their objectives). Fifteen (model, corpus) configurations at 70M–500M in ~16 GPU-hours on two A6000s, the cheapest complete grid in this vault. The three failures. Adapter parameters left silently non-trainable in PPO loops assembled from PEFT and TRL — loss computes, rollouts generate, the policy never moves — fixed by merging the SFT adapter into the base and attaching a fresh zero-initialized one. bfloat16 destroys the importance ratio: ρ_t = exp(log π_θ − log π_SFT) is catastrophic cancellation on a 7-bit mantissa, producing ratios above 10⁶ and hardware exceptions in the first steps below 200M parameters — fixed by float32 for policy, reference, value head and reward model. And policy collapse under long-tailed rewards with unclipped KL, fixed by reward whitening at 3σ, an importance-ratio guard at ρ̄ > 5, and weight rollback on NaN/Inf. The boundary that survives the fixes is the interesting part and it is not parameter count: Pythia-410M improves markedly on TinyStories (Δ +1.355, win rate 59.9%) and regresses significantly on Wikitext-103 (Δ −1.043, 39.9%), while a smaller SmolLM2-360M improves on both. What separates them is the SFT prior's perplexity on that corpus — every configuration above PPL_SFT ≈ 20 shows negligible or negative gain, every gain above +0.2 sits below it. Mechanism: a fluent prior keeps rollouts inside the reward model's training distribution; a weak one produces a gradient indistinguishable from noise. That is alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition measured from the RL side — a stage that selects among behaviours gets nothing from a base that has none — and it is a legitimate use of perplexity by this vault's own rule, since it predicts whether a training stage will work (training health) rather than ranking models. Practitioner form, minutes on one card: measure your SFT checkpoint's perplexity on the target corpus before spending anything on preference optimization. [T1, one study, 70M–500M]
  • (1a) The preference thread's regularization knob is not the knob you think it is (new 2026-07-24). RLHF and DPO are the same point — Ψ(q) = log(q/(1−q)), the Bradley-Terry logit — of a general objective max_π E[Ψ(p*(y ≻ y'))] − τ·D_KL(π ‖ π_ref). That transform is unbounded, so when a preference probability reaches 1 the implied reward gap diverges and the optimal policy zeroes the dispreferred action for every value of τ. The KL term is not weakened by determinism; it is overridden. The finite-sample version is what bites: a pair whose true preference is 0.8 but which was sampled three times the same way has p̂ = 1, and in a large action space with a small dataset — every real preference dataset — that is the modal case, not a corner case. Two consequences. Practical: β is not a smooth dial from "stay near the reference" to "follow the data," and early stopping is performing the regularization. Historical, and it inverts this map's founding move: under {0,1} preferences the optimal reward is infinite, so a fitted reward model underfits, and that underfitting was a regularizer DPO discarded along with the reward-modelling stage — classical RLHF is more robust here precisely because its intermediate stage is bad at its job. The fix is to bound Ψ: at Ψ = Identity (IPO) the loss becomes a regression of the log-likelihood-ratio gap onto the finite target τ⁻¹/2. [T1 — proved; demonstrated only on 3-action bandits]
  • (1) Preference thread — preference tuning reduces to classification. The KL-constrained RLHF objective has a closed-form optimal policy; DPO reparameterizes it so preference pairs train with a single classification loss — no reward model, no sampling loop — matching or exceeding PPO-based RLHF on sentiment control, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. The affordable alignment lever: operationally the same class as SFT. [T1]
  • (2) Verifiable-reward thread — pure RL elicits reasoning where a checker exists. R1-Zero: no SFT, no human traces, outcome-only rewards → AIME 2024 pass@1 15.6% → 71.0%, with self-reflection and verification behaviors emerging from the incentive. Reach is bounded by what can be automatically verified. [T1]
  • (1a) The preference thread's trajectory is successive deletion — and the reason the stage exists at all is a property of the SFT loss. Cross-entropy has no term that pushes anything down, so fine-tuning on chosen responses alone raises log P(chosen) and log P(rejected) together (OPT-350M on HH-RLHF). Preference tuning is not a polish pass on an already-good model; it supplies the only downward pressure in the pipeline. That explains a common practitioner failure: curating clean good examples reliably underdelivers at suppressing a behaviour, because "absent from the training data" is not a signal cross-entropy can act on. RLHF deleted nothing; DPO deleted the reward model and the sampling loop; ORPO deletes the reference model and the stage boundary as well, folding a small odds-ratio penalty into the SFT loss itself (L_SFT + λ·L_OR, λ ≈ 0.1–0.25) for half the forward passes per batch. [T1]
  • (1b) Two independent groups now agree the reference model is optional — and SimPO names a mismatch none of the others did. DPO's implicit reward is a summed log-ratio against a frozen reference; decoding is governed by average token log-probability. Those are different quantities, and SimPO shows the DPO reward often disagrees with the actual likelihood ranking of the pair it trained on. Making the training reward be the decoding metric — length-normalized average log-prob, no reference model, plus a target margin γ — yields +6.4 over DPO on AlpacaEval 2 and +7.5 on Arena-Hard (Gemma-2-9B-it: 72.4% LC win rate, 59.1% Arena-Hard, 1st on Chatbot Arena under 10B). ORPO (2026-07-19) and SimPO (2026-07-20) reached "drop π_ref" by different arguments within months — that convergence is what firms the successive-deletion trajectory, even though neither validates the other's specific objective. [T1]
  • Hold ORPO's margin well below DPO's, though — the direction is credible, the win is not established. Where the comparison is apples-to-apples (same bases, same data, small scale), ORPO's win rate against DPO is 41.7% at OPT-125M, 49.4% at 350M, 70.9% at 1.3B — a loss, a tie, then a win. The headline 7B results (Mistral-ORPO-β: AlpacaEval 2.0 12.20%, MT-Bench 7.32, vs Zephyr-β 10.99) rest on one dataset for one epoch, nothing above 7B, and three of four headline benchmarks GPT-4-family judged, with the comparison models' numbers lifted from leaderboards rather than re-run. [T1, single-study]
  • (1c) The variant zoo is one family — Human-Aware Losses — and the framing is what consolidates the map. KTO names the account the map was missing: an alignment loss that scores a completion by its reward relative to a reference point (the KL-anchored reference policy) through a loss-averse, reference-dependent value function. DPO and PPO-RLHF are already HALOs; ORPO and SimPO are too. So the successive-deletion trajectory (reward model → reference model → pairing constraint) is not a pile of tricks but one mechanism, several value functions. The paper's own result — no HALO is universally superior, best choice depends on data form and noise — is why this unifies without crowning a default. This is a claim about why the methods work, and it does not depend on the biased judge. [T1]
  • (1d) Preference tuning does not need pairs. Because a HALO scores individual completions, KTO aligns from an unpaired binary signal (desirable / not) instead of chosen-vs-rejected pairs — matching or exceeding DPO from 1B to 30B on a strictly cheaper, more abundant data shape (a product's thumbs-up/down is already KTO data), and staying stable under class imbalance that pairwise methods cannot represent. It deletes a fourth thing the others kept: the pairing constraint on the data itself. Caveat: the empirical wins are still GPT-4-family-judged, and "cheaper per item" is not yet "cheaper at matched labeling budget." [T1]
  • The two threads differ in what they buy: preference tuning fits subjective judgments cheaply; verifiable-reward RL needs no labeler but needs a checker — and its product compresses into SFT-able traces (distillation-small-models). [T1, two groups]

  • 2026-08-21 — open-mopd-multi-teacher-capability-imbalance (T1, cross-entry; primary map distillation-small-models; SIA-Lab of Tsinghua AIR + ByteDance Seed, fully open-sourced, 8×A100-80GB): a free correctness fix for any policy-gradient loop that reuses a rollout batch and scores it with a student-dependent dense reward. The standard PPO throughput arrangement generates rollouts once and takes K inner updates on them, computing rewards at rollout time. When the reward is dense and token-level it has two terms: a teacher term that is genuinely constant across the K updates, and a student term that is not — after the first inner update the student has moved, so by the K-th update the reward being optimised was computed against a policy that no longer exists, stale by K−1 steps. The fix requires no extra forward pass of any kind: PPO's actor forward already computes current-student log-probabilities because the ratio needs them, so the dense reward's student term is read from there rather than from saved rollout-time values, while teacher log-probs stay prefilled once per rollout batch. The measured cost is consistent with that account rather than asserted — dense-reward computation is 2.2% of one outer step, taking 27.3 s with refresh against 27.8 s without, with the full outer step 1298 s against 1313 s, i.e. inside step-to-step variation and nominally faster with the fix. Worth +0.81 total points on the paper's benchmark. The argument makes no reference to teachers, domains or routing, so it applies to single-teacher on-policy distillation and to any reward-model setup whose reward depends on the policy's own probabilities — this vault has read several such recipes and none of them mention it. Graded suggestive: isolated in one cumulative ablation on one 3B student at one value of K, with the free claim much better supported than the size claim. The asymmetry is the point — a fix costing nothing needs far less evidence to justify adopting than one costing a training run. New: a-reused-rollout-makes-the-student-half-of-a-dense-reward-stale.

And LIMA loses clearly to Claude (54%) and GPT-4 (57%), both RLHF-trained, while DaVinci003 is 2022-vintage RLHF. So the synthesis this map should carry is: the first large fraction of alignment is available from a small, well-curated supervised set, and preference optimization buys the remainder — which is a more useful statement for this vault's premise than either literature makes alone, since it says where a practitioner's first dollar goes. It also lands a quiet cost argument in the same direction as preference-alignment-collapses-into-a-single-stage: the trajectory of this whole thread has been successive deletion of pipeline stages, and LIMA is the limit case — delete the preference stage entirely and pay for curation instead. Bound: judged by 2023-vintage crowd workers and GPT-4-April against retired baselines, on 300 prompts of which 230 were written by authors who also wrote 200 training examples, with an acknowledged shared-prior leak between the groups. See alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition.

Open questions:

  • Does DPO's simplicity cost capability at the top end? Frontier labs kept PPO-family pipelines; DPO's wins here are narrow tasks with GPT-4-judged evals. A neutral DPO-vs-PPO-at-scale source remains the thread's missing piece — and note that the same gap now applies one level down: ORPO's DPO comparison is the authors' own, at ≤1.3B where it is APIs-and-all a wash. The judged-eval problem is now this map's structural weakness, not an incidental caveat: every method in thread (1) is ranked by a GPT-4-family judge.
  • Two unifying frameworks, no reconciliation (new 2026-07-24). The zoo is now covered twice — HALOs (KTO, 2026-07-21) and ΨPO (IPO, 2026-07-24) — by groups that do not cite each other and classify along different axes. Whether they are compatible views of one object or competing accounts is unresolved, and it matters practically: HALOs say what human quirk a loss encodes, ΨPO says what a loss does to your policy under sparse data, and a practitioner choosing a method needs both answers about the same method. This is now the thread's sharpest theoretical gap.
  • Does the DPO overfitting failure actually bite at LLM scale? (new 2026-07-24). The derivation is airtight and the demonstration is a 3-action bandit. The empirical question is untouched: at 7B on a real preference set, how much of DPO's known appetite for early stopping is this mechanism, how should β be set given that it is inert in the deterministic regime, and does IPO's squared loss train stably at that scale? The authors close by asking for exactly these experiments and, as far as this vault has ingested, nobody has run them head-to-head. Practice has partly routed around the problem independently — β-tuning, label smoothing, conservative DPO variants — none compared here.
  • The variant zoo is now mostly mapped, and named as one family. ORPO (2026-07-19), SimPO (2026-07-20), KTO (2026-07-21, with the HALO taxonomy) and IPO (2026-07-24, with ΨPO) are in; the reward-modeling branch is the remaining named gap — and it is now more interesting than it was, since IPO's argument implies a fitted reward model's underfitting is load-bearing regularization rather than a defect. The bigger absence is unchanged: no neutral head-to-head of the family at a scale where differences would show — every in-vault variant is its authors' own evaluation on a GPT-4-judged instrument. A third-party bake-off (ideally KTO-vs-DPO at matched labeling budget, not matched item count) is now the thread's single most valuable missing source.
  • Reference-free methods drop the only anchor to the base model, and nobody in-vault measures the cost (new 2026-07-20). DPO's π_ref is a leash; ORPO and SimPO both cut it. Off-distribution degradation, and whether the gains are narrower than the judged benchmarks suggest, is unmeasured — and it rhymes with the forgetting result in peft-methods (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting): what a cheap update quietly costs elsewhere is the question this vault keeps finding under the headline.
  • SFT itself — the stage both threads sit on top of — has no dedicated in-vault source, though this run put a sharp dent in the gap: cross-entropy-sft-raises-the-odds-of-rejected-text is the vault's first graded claim about what the SFT objective actually does. (QLoRA's data-quality finding gestures at the data side; see peft-methods.) It also raises a question for distillation-small-models that nobody in-vault has asked: distilled teacher traces are chosen-only data with no rejected side at all — so what does a distillation SFT run do to the behaviours it was never shown?
  • How post-training RL composes with PEFT — PARTLY SERVED 2026-08-01, and the answer so far is a defect list rather than a method result. small-scale-ppo-fails-three-ways-before-it-fails-at-learning supplies the first graded evidence: adapters silently non-trainable in standard PEFT/TRL PPO loops, and bfloat16 breaking the importance ratio. Both are pipeline findings at 70M–500M. What is still entirely unsourced is the thing practitioners actually do — a DPO or GRPO run through a LoRA adapter on a 4-bit base at 7B–8B, with the quality of the result compared against full-precision full fine-tuning. Nobody in-vault has measured it, and this map cannot currently say whether preference optimization through an adapter loses anything.
  • Does the bfloat16 importance-ratio failure exist at 7B? (new 2026-08-01, and it is cheap to answer.) The mechanism — exp(log π_θ − log π_SFT) losing its significant digits to a 7-bit mantissa — is a property of the computation, not of model size; what makes it bite below 200M is how fast the policy leaves its reference. Mixed-precision bf16 is the assumed default in every PEFT and distributed-training recipe this vault holds, and every ratio-based objective in this map (PPO, GRPO, and any clipped-ratio variant) performs the same subtraction. Instrumenting ρ_t for overflow during one 7B run would either close this or reveal a silent numerical failure in common practice.
  • Reward hacking and eval-judge bias: where those judges fail is unmeasured here. Half-resolved 2026-07-20 — llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias measures where the eval judge fails (position, verbosity, self-enhancement). Still open: reward hacking proper, and where a learned reward model fails, which is a different object from an eval judge. Also unmeasured — whether those 2023-vintage bias magnitudes still hold for the judges scoring today's papers.
  • Test-time compute as a controllable lever: Qwen3 (distillation-small-models) fuses the verifiable-reward reasoning product and a fast non-thinking mode into one model with an adaptive "thinking budget" — reasoning RL's output made a runtime dial. Unmapped in-vault; a thread to open as sources land.
