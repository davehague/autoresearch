Post-training RL (preference tuning & reasoning RL) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 (auto run) — first evidence in "The training pipeline" group. Two reward sources land on day one as distinct sub-threads: human preference pairs (DPO) and verifiable outcome checkers (R1-Zero). Auto-coined; flagged for David's review. 2026-07-20 (auto run): held at emerging, deliberately. SimPO is a third T1 in thread (1) and on method grounds would have firmed this map. It is offset the same run by judging-llm-as-a-judge-mt-bench, which supplies measured bias in the only instrument thread (1) has ever been scored on. The methods are not contested; their measurement is. Status should not rise on evidence whose evaluation the vault just learned to distrust — this is the status-moves-both-ways rule applied to an instrument rather than a claim. Upgraded emerging → consolidating 2026-07-21 (auto run): kto-prospect-theoretic-optimization (Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2024, T1) tips it — but not on another judged benchmark win, which is exactly what the 2026-07-20 hold said would not be sufficient. KTO supplies two things the map was missing: (a) a mechanism-level account — the HALO family — under which DPO, PPO, ORPO, SimPO, and KTO are one object (a loss-averse value function over a KL reference point), so the map finally has a story rather than a variant list; (b) a second reward modality, unpaired binary feedback, matching DPO 1B–30B. The map never rested solely on judged evidence — thread (2) is checker-verified (AIME) — and the consolidation is of the framework, which does not ride the biased instrument. The judge caveat stays loud on every thread-(1) benchmark number; what firmed is the account of why the family works, plus a fifth T1 spanning both reward sources.

2026-07-24 (auto run): held at consolidating, deliberately — and the reason is the interesting part. ipo-general-theoretical-paradigm-preferences (Azar et al., Google DeepMind, AISTATS 2024, T1) is a proven push-back on this map's anchor: DPO's KL coefficient is structurally inert whenever empirical preference probabilities hit 0 or 1. Under status-moves-both-ways that reads like a downgrade, and it is not, for two reasons. First, its empirical support is three-action bandits — there is no language model in the paper — so it establishes a property of the objective, not a failure at the scale this map's claims live at. Second, and more importantly, what tipped this map on 2026-07-21 was the arrival of a mechanism-level account, and ΨPO is a second, independent one. HALOs classify these losses by the implied human value function; ΨPO classifies them by the transform applied to the preference probability. Two unifying frameworks from two groups, neither citing the other, is a stronger framework layer than one — which is precisely the layer the consolidation was of. What the map now owes its readers is the defect carried loudly in "What we believe now" rather than a status change: β/τ does not do what practitioners think it does, and the early stopping the field treats as hygiene is doing the regularization the objective was supposed to provide. A move to contested waits on an LM-scale demonstration of the failure — which the authors themselves ask for.

Status: consolidating

What we believe now:

  • (1a) The preference thread's regularization knob is not the knob you think it is (new 2026-07-24). RLHF and DPO are the same point — Ψ(q) = log(q/(1−q)), the Bradley-Terry logit — of a general objective max_π E[Ψ(p*(y ≻ y'))] − τ·D_KL(π ‖ π_ref). That transform is unbounded, so when a preference probability reaches 1 the implied reward gap diverges and the optimal policy zeroes the dispreferred action for every value of τ. The KL term is not weakened by determinism; it is overridden. The finite-sample version is what bites: a pair whose true preference is 0.8 but which was sampled three times the same way has p̂ = 1, and in a large action space with a small dataset — every real preference dataset — that is the modal case, not a corner case. Two consequences. Practical: β is not a smooth dial from "stay near the reference" to "follow the data," and early stopping is performing the regularization. Historical, and it inverts this map's founding move: under {0,1} preferences the optimal reward is infinite, so a fitted reward model underfits, and that underfitting was a regularizer DPO discarded along with the reward-modelling stage — classical RLHF is more robust here precisely because its intermediate stage is bad at its job. The fix is to bound Ψ: at Ψ = Identity (IPO) the loss becomes a regression of the log-likelihood-ratio gap onto the finite target τ⁻¹/2. [T1 — proved; demonstrated only on 3-action bandits]
  • (1) Preference thread — preference tuning reduces to classification. The KL-constrained RLHF objective has a closed-form optimal policy; DPO reparameterizes it so preference pairs train with a single classification loss — no reward model, no sampling loop — matching or exceeding PPO-based RLHF on sentiment control, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. The affordable alignment lever: operationally the same class as SFT. [T1]
  • (2) Verifiable-reward thread — pure RL elicits reasoning where a checker exists. R1-Zero: no SFT, no human traces, outcome-only rewards → AIME 2024 pass@1 15.6% → 71.0%, with self-reflection and verification behaviors emerging from the incentive. Reach is bounded by what can be automatically verified. [T1]
  • (1a) The preference thread's trajectory is successive deletion — and the reason the stage exists at all is a property of the SFT loss. Cross-entropy has no term that pushes anything down, so fine-tuning on chosen responses alone raises log P(chosen) and log P(rejected) together (OPT-350M on HH-RLHF). Preference tuning is not a polish pass on an already-good model; it supplies the only downward pressure in the pipeline. That explains a common practitioner failure: curating clean good examples reliably underdelivers at suppressing a behaviour, because "absent from the training data" is not a signal cross-entropy can act on. RLHF deleted nothing; DPO deleted the reward model and the sampling loop; ORPO deletes the reference model and the stage boundary as well, folding a small odds-ratio penalty into the SFT loss itself (L_SFT + λ·L_OR, λ ≈ 0.1–0.25) for half the forward passes per batch. [T1]
  • (1b) Two independent groups now agree the reference model is optional — and SimPO names a mismatch none of the others did. DPO's implicit reward is a summed log-ratio against a frozen reference; decoding is governed by average token log-probability. Those are different quantities, and SimPO shows the DPO reward often disagrees with the actual likelihood ranking of the pair it trained on. Making the training reward be the decoding metric — length-normalized average log-prob, no reference model, plus a target margin γ — yields +6.4 over DPO on AlpacaEval 2 and +7.5 on Arena-Hard (Gemma-2-9B-it: 72.4% LC win rate, 59.1% Arena-Hard, 1st on Chatbot Arena under 10B). ORPO (2026-07-19) and SimPO (2026-07-20) reached "drop π_ref" by different arguments within months — that convergence is what firms the successive-deletion trajectory, even though neither validates the other's specific objective. [T1]
  • Hold ORPO's margin well below DPO's, though — the direction is credible, the win is not established. Where the comparison is apples-to-apples (same bases, same data, small scale), ORPO's win rate against DPO is 41.7% at OPT-125M, 49.4% at 350M, 70.9% at 1.3B — a loss, a tie, then a win. The headline 7B results (Mistral-ORPO-β: AlpacaEval 2.0 12.20%, MT-Bench 7.32, vs Zephyr-β 10.99) rest on one dataset for one epoch, nothing above 7B, and three of four headline benchmarks GPT-4-family judged, with the comparison models' numbers lifted from leaderboards rather than re-run. [T1, single-study]
  • (1c) The variant zoo is one family — Human-Aware Losses — and the framing is what consolidates the map. KTO names the account the map was missing: an alignment loss that scores a completion by its reward relative to a reference point (the KL-anchored reference policy) through a loss-averse, reference-dependent value function. DPO and PPO-RLHF are already HALOs; ORPO and SimPO are too. So the successive-deletion trajectory (reward model → reference model → pairing constraint) is not a pile of tricks but one mechanism, several value functions. The paper's own result — no HALO is universally superior, best choice depends on data form and noise — is why this unifies without crowning a default. This is a claim about why the methods work, and it does not depend on the biased judge. [T1]
  • (1d) Preference tuning does not need pairs. Because a HALO scores individual completions, KTO aligns from an unpaired binary signal (desirable / not) instead of chosen-vs-rejected pairs — matching or exceeding DPO from 1B to 30B on a strictly cheaper, more abundant data shape (a product's thumbs-up/down is already KTO data), and staying stable under class imbalance that pairwise methods cannot represent. It deletes a fourth thing the others kept: the pairing constraint on the data itself. Caveat: the empirical wins are still GPT-4-family-judged, and "cheaper per item" is not yet "cheaper at matched labeling budget." [T1]
  • The two threads differ in what they buy: preference tuning fits subjective judgments cheaply; verifiable-reward RL needs no labeler but needs a checker — and its product compresses into SFT-able traces (distillation-small-models). [T1, two groups]

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-07-27 — the input side of this map gets its first source, from cot-self-instruct-synthetic-prompt-curation (T1, primary map data-curation): every source here argues about the objective — which loss, which reference model, which reward modality — and every one of them treats the prompt distribution as given. This one varies it and nothing else. Holding the student (Qwen3-4B-Base), the algorithm (GRPO under rule-based verifiable rewards) and the evaluation fixed, and changing only which prompts the run consumes, a four-benchmark reasoning average moves from 44.6 (human-labelled s1k with DeepSeek-R1 targets) and 47.5 (OpenMathReasoning) to 57.2 — with +4.2 of that coming from filtering the generated prompt pool while making it smaller. For a map whose sharpest open item is a third-party bake-off at matched labelling budget, that is a warning about the whole comparison class: two preference or RL methods evaluated on differently-curated prompt sets are not being compared. Two smaller items. It is the map's first use of the reward-modeling branch it names as missing — though not as a training signal: a reward model scores K responses and the worst of them stands for the instruction's quality, which is reward modeling as a data filter rather than as an objective, and it works (56.2 on the same axis). And it is a direct sighting of the failure mode the map's judged numbers all carry: the authors report that synthetic instructions produce longer responses, that DPO under a length-favouring judge therefore risks length explosion, and that they apply a length-normalized reward with a coefficient of 0.2 to contain it — verbosity bias observed, named, and patched with a hand-chosen constant. See filtering-a-generated-corpus-outperforms-generating-more-of-it.
  • 2026-07-25 — premise-level push-back from lima-less-is-more-for-alignment (T1, primary map data-curation): every source in this map argues about which preference loss to use; this one asks whether you need preference data at all. LLaMa 65B, 1,000 curated supervised examples, no RL and no reward model, is equal-or-preferred to RLHF-trained DaVinci003 in 65% of 300 human head-to-heads and to GPT-4 in 43%. The map should record this without overstating it, because the honest reading is a bound rather than a refutation. Nothing here touches the mechanism layer that actually tipped this map to consolidating — HALOs and ΨPO are claims about what a family of losses is, and LIMA neither tests nor contradicts them. And LIMA loses clearly to Claude (54%) and GPT-4 (57%), both RLHF-trained, while DaVinci003 is 2022-vintage RLHF. So the synthesis this map should carry is: the first large fraction of alignment is available from a small, well-curated supervised set, and preference optimization buys the remainder — which is a more useful statement for this vault's premise than either literature makes alone, since it says where a practitioner's first dollar goes. It also lands a quiet cost argument in the same direction as preference-alignment-collapses-into-a-single-stage: the trajectory of this whole thread has been successive deletion of pipeline stages, and LIMA is the limit case — delete the preference stage entirely and pay for curation instead. Bound: judged by 2023-vintage crowd workers and GPT-4-April against retired baselines, on 300 prompts of which 230 were written by authors who also wrote 200 training examples, with an acknowledged shared-prior leak between the groups. See alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition.

Open questions:

  • Does DPO's simplicity cost capability at the top end? Frontier labs kept PPO-family pipelines; DPO's wins here are narrow tasks with GPT-4-judged evals. A neutral DPO-vs-PPO-at-scale source remains the thread's missing piece — and note that the same gap now applies one level down: ORPO's DPO comparison is the authors' own, at ≤1.3B where it is APIs-and-all a wash. The judged-eval problem is now this map's structural weakness, not an incidental caveat: every method in thread (1) is ranked by a GPT-4-family judge.
  • Two unifying frameworks, no reconciliation (new 2026-07-24). The zoo is now covered twice — HALOs (KTO, 2026-07-21) and ΨPO (IPO, 2026-07-24) — by groups that do not cite each other and classify along different axes. Whether they are compatible views of one object or competing accounts is unresolved, and it matters practically: HALOs say what human quirk a loss encodes, ΨPO says what a loss does to your policy under sparse data, and a practitioner choosing a method needs both answers about the same method. This is now the thread's sharpest theoretical gap.
  • Does the DPO overfitting failure actually bite at LLM scale? (new 2026-07-24). The derivation is airtight and the demonstration is a 3-action bandit. The empirical question is untouched: at 7B on a real preference set, how much of DPO's known appetite for early stopping is this mechanism, how should β be set given that it is inert in the deterministic regime, and does IPO's squared loss train stably at that scale? The authors close by asking for exactly these experiments and, as far as this vault has ingested, nobody has run them head-to-head. Practice has partly routed around the problem independently — β-tuning, label smoothing, conservative DPO variants — none compared here.
  • The variant zoo is now mostly mapped, and named as one family. ORPO (2026-07-19), SimPO (2026-07-20), KTO (2026-07-21, with the HALO taxonomy) and IPO (2026-07-24, with ΨPO) are in; the reward-modeling branch is the remaining named gap — and it is now more interesting than it was, since IPO's argument implies a fitted reward model's underfitting is load-bearing regularization rather than a defect. The bigger absence is unchanged: no neutral head-to-head of the family at a scale where differences would show — every in-vault variant is its authors' own evaluation on a GPT-4-judged instrument. A third-party bake-off (ideally KTO-vs-DPO at matched labeling budget, not matched item count) is now the thread's single most valuable missing source.
  • Reference-free methods drop the only anchor to the base model, and nobody in-vault measures the cost (new 2026-07-20). DPO's π_ref is a leash; ORPO and SimPO both cut it. Off-distribution degradation, and whether the gains are narrower than the judged benchmarks suggest, is unmeasured — and it rhymes with the forgetting result in peft-methods (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting): what a cheap update quietly costs elsewhere is the question this vault keeps finding under the headline.
  • SFT itself — the stage both threads sit on top of — has no dedicated in-vault source, though this run put a sharp dent in the gap: cross-entropy-sft-raises-the-odds-of-rejected-text is the vault's first graded claim about what the SFT objective actually does. (QLoRA's data-quality finding gestures at the data side; see peft-methods.) It also raises a question for distillation-small-models that nobody in-vault has asked: distilled teacher traces are chosen-only data with no rejected side at all — so what does a distillation SFT run do to the behaviours it was never shown?
  • How post-training RL composes with PEFT: DPO/GRPO-class runs through LoRA adapters on quantized bases is common practice with no graded in-vault evidence yet.
  • Reward hacking and eval-judge bias: where those judges fail is unmeasured here. Half-resolved 2026-07-20 — llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias measures where the eval judge fails (position, verbosity, self-enhancement). Still open: reward hacking proper, and where a learned reward model fails, which is a different object from an eval judge. Also unmeasured — whether those 2023-vintage bias magnitudes still hold for the judges scoring today's papers.
  • Test-time compute as a controllable lever: Qwen3 (distillation-small-models) fuses the verifiable-reward reasoning product and a fast non-thinking mode into one model with an adaptive "thinking budget" — reasoning RL's output made a runtime dial. Unmapped in-vault; a thread to open as sources land.
