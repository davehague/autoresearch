What we believe now:

  • The cost half of this map now lives in vlm-on-device (forked 2026-08-02). What moved there, in one line each so this map does not have to be re-read to know it: at a fixed memory budget take the larger quantized model — Qwen2-VL-7B at 4-bit dominates Qwen2-VL-2B at fp16 on accuracy, error-detection AUROC and selective risk, so size and precision are one budget rather than two; a quantization budget should be allocated by modality — vision at 4 bits, text at 8, with a CLIP encoder falling off a cliff below 4 where an 8B language model degrades gradually; the vision tower is what makes local VLM use feel broken — 21.7 s to 0.78 s across three turns on one image, with embedding caching worth 7.8× of that; and image tokens need no special serving machinery once the cache is keyed on a hash of the pixels. The one place the two maps still meet is the open question below about pricing early fusion, which belongs to both.
  • Early fusion's structural advantage is now demonstrated, by a second group, and it extends to video (new 2026-07-27). The trade the early-fusion family offers — give up the CLIP prior and accept much harder training, get native generation from the same weights that do perception — had a measured cost and an unmeasured benefit until this run. One 8B decoder-only transformer, trained from scratch on interleaved text, image and video tokens under a single cross-entropy loss, generates images preferred over SDXL by human voters (70.0 vs 66.9), scores 0.66 on GenEval against Chameleon's 0.39, SDXL's 0.55 and DALL-E 3's 0.67, reaches 81.60 on DPG-Bench (long dense prompts; SDXL 74.65), and tops VBench at 80.96 against OpenSora 1.2's 79.76 — by predicting the next token in a video sequence, and extending a clip by continuing it out to 8 seconds. No diffusion, no CLIP, no projection, and no external text encoder, because conditioning is just earlier tokens. The video result is the sharper one: the late-fusion stack has no story for generating a temporal modality, and this needs no new mechanism to get one. [T1]
  • The whole modality interface is a discrete tokenizer plus a vocabulary extension — and the bill is sequence length (new 2026-07-27). Structurally, the model above is a Llama-2 block: 32 layers, grouped-query attention at 32 query / 8 KV heads, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE at base 1,000,000, biases removed. The only architectural difference from a text model is a 184,622-entry vocabulary — text tokens plus a 32,768-entry vision codebook plus five special tokens. Everything modality-specific lives outside the transformer, in a vector-quantized autoencoder (4× temporal, 8×8 spatial compression) and in a document format: [BOS] caption [SOV] meta text [SOT] vision tokens [EOV], with resolution and frame rate written in as plain text, and moving the caption after [EOV] converts a generation example into an understanding one. So the price is not paid in architecture. One 512×512 image is 4,096 tokens, which is why pretraining's second stage runs at a 131,072-token context with tensor, context and data parallelism composed. Late fusion spends compute in an encoder once and hands the LLM a short sequence; early fusion spends nothing on an encoder and pays in context forever after — through the KV cache, through attention, through every cost context-extension and local-inference track. Two smaller taxes are visible: vision tokens need an explicit loss weight of 0.5 to stop them dominating a mixed corpus, plus dropout for stability — a second group finding that one loss over two modalities needs a thumb on the scale. [T1]
  • CONTESTED — the interface. There are two VLM families, not one settled recipe. The late-fusion adapter family (LLaVA, Molmo) connects a frozen CLIP encoder to an open LLM through a trainable projection — the image becomes a token sequence the model reads; cheap (~1 node-day), reuses a pretrained vision prior, understanding-only. The early-fusion token family (Chameleon) deletes the encoder and the projection: an image is tokenized into discrete codebook tokens sharing the text vocabulary, and one transformer processes the interleaved stream from layer one. Early fusion buys native image generation the adapter stack structurally cannot have, and stays competitive on understanding (7B/34B: SOTA captioning, beats Llama-2 on text, competitive with Mixtral 8×7B / Gemini-Pro, matches GPT-4V on human-judged mixed-modal generation) — but pays by discarding the CLIP prior and being much harder to train. Which family is "right" depends on whether you need generation and whether you're pretraining or fine-tuning; the vault's budget-fine-tune premise still points at late fusion. [T1 vs T1 — interface unsettled]
  • Within late fusion, the replicated claim is the INTERFACE — not the frozen encoder. These were one bullet until 2026-07-22, and they had to be split. The interface — an image becomes a token sequence the LLM reads, through a small trainable bridge, with no cross-attention surgery and no joint pretraining — is now n=3 across three independent groups: LLaVA (single linear projection onto Vicuna), Molmo (MLP-projection-plus-pooling onto OLMo/OLMoE/Qwen2), Qwen2.5-VL (MLP vision-language merger onto Qwen2.5). That is a strong replication and the durable finding. The encoder is not replicated. LLaVA and Molmo both import a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 at a fixed 336px; Qwen2.5-VL trains its own ViT from scratch at native resolution and aspect ratio (resize only to a multiple of 28, 14×14 patches, so token count varies with the image rather than the image being distorted to fit the tokens), made affordable by window attention in all but 4 of its layers (112×112 window) so encoder cost is linear, not quadratic, in patch count — architecturally the same play as GQA in local-inference. The practitioner rule is therefore conditional, and it does not favor a small budget: the frozen CLIP is what makes VLM adaptation cost ~1 node-day, and training a tower from scratch is lab-scale — but the gains concentrate exactly where a fixed 336px grid binds, on documents, charts, and OCR (Qwen2.5-VL-72B InfoVQA 87.3 vs GPT-4o's 74.3, DocVQA 96.4). If your target is documents at native resolution, no projection will fix the encoder; otherwise the cheap recipe still holds. The middle option — unfreezing an off-the-shelf CLIP rather than replacing it — is measured by nobody in-vault. [T1 ×2 + T2]
  • The recipe is short, and the freezing schedule is not load-bearing. LLaVA uses two staged unfreezes (align the projection with encoder and LLM frozen; then instruction-tune projection + LLM). Molmo uses two stages as well — caption pretraining, then SFT on a data mixture — but updates all parameters in both, with no frozen components and no RLHF at all. Two different freezing policies, both working, suggests the staging is a resource convenience rather than a requirement. [T1, two groups]
  • The cost lands inside fine-tuning economics, not cluster economics. LLaVA-1.5 reports full training on ~1.2M public pairs in ~1 day on a single 8×A100 node — the same order of resource as the text fine-tunes in peft-methods, which is what makes VLM adaptation reachable for a practitioner rather than a lab. [T1]
  • CONTESTED — where the supervision comes from. The two sources solved "there is no multimodal instruction corpus" in opposite ways, and disagree about whether one of them was legitimate. LLaVA fed symbolic proxies (COCO captions + bounding boxes, never pixels) to a language-only GPT-4, which wrote 158K conversations — a teacher that could not perceive the modality it was supervising. Molmo names that entire lineage as the field's blind spot: the strongest open-weight VLMs "rely heavily on synthetic data from proprietary VLMs… effectively distilling these closed models into open ones," leaving the community "missing foundational knowledge about how to build performant VLMs from scratch." Its answer uses zero VLM-generated data — annotators speak for 60–90 seconds per image and the audio is transcribed and cleaned by a language-only LLM that never sees the image (PixMo-Cap: 712k images, ~1.3M captions), plus 2.3M human question-point pairs, doc QA derived from generating code, and text-only QA synthesis. [T1 vs T1 — unresolved]
  • The teacher was a convenience, not a necessity — but the alternative costs a human operation. Molmo-72B tops its 11-benchmark academic table at 81.2 average, above Qwen2-VL-72B (79.4), GPT-4o (78.5), Gemini 1.5 Pro (78.3), and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (76.7), and ranks 2nd on human- preference Elo behind only GPT-4o. That settles feasibility. It does not make the method portable: most practitioners cannot run a 712k-image annotation operation, so the transferable content is the question — is there a channel where ground truth already exists? — rather than the protocol. [T1]
  • Discount headline numbers in this map by their eval method, which now differ sharply between the two sources. LLaVA's 85.1% relative-to-GPT-4 and 92.53% ScienceQA come from GPT-4 judging a model trained on GPT-4-authored data against a GPT-4-authored benchmark — a closed loop. Molmo's headline rests on >325k human pairwise ratings (~450 matches per model pair, Bradley-Terry), reports the automated suite separately, and publishes where the two rankings disagree (Qwen2-VL scores well academically, ranks lower with humans). Read LLaVA as "the pipeline works"; read Molmo's ranking as an actual measurement — while still noting that a suite average hides shape: Molmo wins on perception and counting (Flickr Count 85.2 vs GPT-4o's 59.6) and trails badly on knowledge (MMMU 54.1 vs 69.1). [T1, differentiated read]

  • 2026-08-03 — the two-family framing has been conflating two independent choices, and a frontier lab took one without the other. Map holds contested, but the contest is now better specified. kimi-k3-open-frontier-intelligence (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts) is architecturally late fusion — a 401M vision encoder, MoonViT-V2, with a lightweight MLP projector into the shared embedding space — and procedurally early fusion: "language and vision are jointly optimized from the start of training, rather than grafting a vision encoder onto a pre-trained language model through a post-hoc alignment stage," visual and textual tokens interleaved inside one next-token objective. This map's dichotomy has treated where the modality boundary sits in the architecture and whether it exists in the training schedule as one choice; they are two, and this is the existence proof. The specific result: MoonViT-V2 is trained entirely from scratch with next-token prediction, departing from prior practice including this team's own previous model, which initialized from contrastively pretrained SigLIP. It matches the SigLIP-initialized baseline on vision evaluations, and the reason they switched is stability — under joint optimization the SigLIP-initialized tower shows persistently higher gradient norms with frequent spikes while the from-scratch tower stays smooth. Their reading: a language-modelling objective shapes the encoder's representations directly, "rather than by a contrastive loss that favors global semantics over fine-grained textual and structural cues." That pushes back on this map's cheapest and most-replicated assumption. The frozen CLIP prior is what makes late-fusion adaptation cost about a node-day — the economics this map credits with putting VLM work within a practitioner's reach — and if contrastive pretraining is unnecessary at scale, the prior was buying convenience rather than capability. The practitioner rule needs a scale condition attached, not a reversal: import CLIP because you cannot afford to train a tower, not because the contrastive objective contributes something a language objective cannot. Two limits keep this suggestive. There is no table comparing the two encoders — "matches the baseline" is asserted with a gradient-norm plot as the visible evidence. And "at scale" is load-bearing: a 401M encoder trained jointly with a 2.78T backbone on a corpus that includes programmatically generated multimodal data (SVG, 3D assets, webpages, games, CAD schematics paired with the code that produced them) says nothing about whether a 7B late-fusion model fine-tuned on one node should unfreeze its CLIP. One portable detail regardless of scale: a 2×2 pixel-shuffle downsample before projection cuts the visual token count fourfold, which is what keeps 3584×3584 inputs affordable — the same lever this map credited to Qwen2.5-VL's window attention, landing on the bill vlm-on-device tracks. Vision results for the record: OmniDocBench 91.1 (best in the comparison set), Video-MME 90.0, MMVU 82.1, Math-Vision 94.3 → 97.8 with Python tools, WorldVQA 51.0 (second, behind Claude Fable 5). See a-vision-encoder-does-not-need-contrastive-pretraining.

  • 2026-08-01 — co-primary ingest of vlm-quantization-uncertainty-signals (single author, arXiv 2026-07-27, T1; co-primary map post-training-quantization): the map's oldest named gap — "quantizing and deploying a VLM has no sources" — closes on the generative side, and the answer is good news for this vault's premise and bad news for its instruments. On 2026-07-31 LQA priced the encoder and the map recorded that it said nothing about a generative VLM. This is a generative VLM: Qwen2-VL-2B and 7B, 4-bit NormalFloat with double quantization and an fp16 compute dtype, across a clean condition plus six photographic degradations at three severities (JPEG, motion blur, low light, glare, rotation, resample), 5,700 predictions. Map holds contested — nothing here touches the fusion interface, which is what this map is contested about. Three things it adds. (a) The fixed-budget ordering, in the new first belief: 7B-4bit dominates 2B-fp16 on accuracy, error detection, selective risk and threshold robustness, so the practitioner rule is buy parameters, then pay for them in bits. (b) The cost is invisible to accuracy. 4-bit costs the 2B arm 1.6 points of accuracy and 15 points of error-detection AUROC, and collapses its ability to emit a parseable stated confidence from 99% to 64% — a format-compliance failure that rhymes exactly with the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff's finding that instruction compliance is the precision-sensitive capability. (c) The small-models-quantize-worse hypothesis gains its second support, in a different currency from LQA's: the 2B loses a great deal to 4-bit and the 7B loses nothing detectable. That is now a real finding in this vault and it is uncomfortable, because the edge story assumes you can shrink twice — smaller model and fewer bits — and two sources now say the two shrinks interact badly. What survives is that you can shrink once, in the right direction. Methodological edge to evaluation-contamination: ECE 0.011 at AUROC 0.56 on the same predictions is a construct mismatch as clean as any in that map. Caveats: single author, no venue, two scale points, the 7B fp16 arm was never run so the size×precision interaction is inferred across arms rather than ablated, n ≈ 100 per condition with undefined AUROC wherever accuracy hits ceiling, a closed-set four-option classification rather than open-ended VQA (which structurally prevents observing refusal, the behaviour a selective-prediction study most wants), one model family, and synthetic degradations. See at-a-fixed-footprint-take-the-larger-quantized-model, four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer, stated-confidence-does-not-improve-with-scale.
  • 2026-07-31 — ingest of lqa-quantized-adaptive-vlm-edge (T1, arXiv 2026-02-08): the map's loudest empty cell — nothing here has ever been priced on consumer hardware — is now partly filled, and the honest headline is that it prices the encoder, not the model. CLIP ViT-B/16 on a single RTX 4070 Ti SUPER at batch size 1: 196.73 MB / 26.04 ms per sample at 8 bits, 139.63 MB / 14.92 ms with the vision tower at 4 bits, against 355.71 MB / ~30 ms for the strongest comparable baseline and 2,700 MB / ~88 ms for the gradient-based one the advertised 19.9× is measured against (against the strong baseline it is 2.5× memory, 2× latency — the number the vault carries). Adaptation is gradient-free — two FIFO caches of high-confidence and high-entropy exemplars, cosine-similarity voting, the whole loop in INT8/INT4 — reaching 65.56% averaged over seven datasets against unadapted CLIP's 61.05, though the margin over the strongest baseline is 0.59 points. Two structural findings outlast the system. (1) Precision should be allocated across the modality boundary: vision at 4 bits, text at 8, with one or two projection/layer-norm layers per tower held at float16 by Hessian trace. That is the third independent partitioning of the same parameter space to reach the same rule — per-layer (AlphaQ), per-tensor-class (the Mixtral 16-cell sweep, "protect the parameters every token passes through"), and now per-modality — so post-training-quantization's a quantization budget is an allocation problem, not a global setting now replicates across three partitionings. Weakest of the three: the authors never run the reversed assignment, so the direction of the modality asymmetry is argued rather than ablated. (2) The indifference width for a CLIP-class encoder is 8 bits, and below it the descent is a cliff. At 8 bits four quantizers land within 0.19 points of each other — the quantizer choice stops mattering entirely — while with both encoders quantized HQQ reads 60.59 / 42.54 / 3.02% at 3 / 2 / 1 bits against a 65.56 reference. That is a different shape from the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff's graded 8→3-bit descent on an 8B language model, and the natural hypothesis is that a ~150M encoder has far less redundancy to surrender — i.e. small models quantize worse, which is exactly the wrong direction for edge deployment and a tension this vault's premise should carry rather than assume away. Scope governs all of it: this is CLIP, a dual-encoder contrastive classifier — the perception front-end late fusion imports (the-vision-encoder-is-a-design-choice-not-a-frozen-import), not a generative VLM. No language model, no KV cache, no autoregressive decode, so early fusion's 4,096-image-tokens-per-image KV load is untouched and the map's complaint about the generative half stays open. Also: the "edge" hardware is a 16 GB consumer desktop GPU with batch size 1 standing in for a device, so no throughput number exists, and the quantization is data-free — the third source in-vault to duck post-training-quantization's calibration confound by construction rather than resolve it. Map holds contested — nothing here speaks to the fusion interface. See vlm-precision-should-be-allocated-by-modality, a-vlm-adapts-on-one-consumer-card-without-a-backward-pass.
  • 2026-07-31 — fine-tuning-side prediction from adaptive-checkpointing-peft-consumer-gpu (T1, primary map distributed-training): a memory-budget-aware gradient-checkpointing policy that works on pure Transformer and pure Mamba backbones breaks on the one hybrid architecture tested — TinyViT, whose MBConv stem allocates more than several attention layers combined, so the cascade triggers at the front and the authors fall back to checkpointing everything. The precondition they name is a near-uniform per-layer activation footprint, and a vision tower bolted onto a language model is the canonical violation of it. So the prediction this map should carry, untested by anyone: adaptive or selective checkpointing is the memory lever most likely to underperform on a VLM specifically, and a VLM fine-tune on one card may need stage-aware granularity that no framework exposes. Same source also measures the fine-tuning energy of small autoregressive VLMs used as classifiers and finds them "uncompetitive" at 15–35 Wh of inference energy per image, where a frozen self-supervised backbone with kNN reads 91.7% on CIFAR-100 — a data point on whether to fine-tune at all that this map has no slot for and the index's "when to fine-tune vs prompt/RAG" gap does. See adaptive-checkpointing-beats-the-boolean-at-a-measured-price.
  • 2026-07-30 — the first local serving number this map has ever held, from vllm-mlx-apple-silicon-inference (T1, primary map local-inference): this map has said since Qwen2.5-VL that "nothing yet says what GGUF/llama.cpp does with a vision tower," and its edge half contained an edge-sized model with no measurement attached. Here is a measurement, on an Apple M4 Max: a multi-turn conversation about a single 1024×1024 image on Qwen3-VL-8B costs 21.7 s on the first turn and 0.78 s by the third — 28× — and the ablation says where it comes from: caching the vision embeddings is worth 7.8× on its own, the text KV cache 1.2×. Three things this map should carry. The encoder, not the language model, is what makes local VLM use feel broken — 21.7 seconds is the tower, and every architecture debate on this map (late fusion's projection versus early fusion's tokenizer versus Qwen's native-resolution ViT) is a debate about the component that dominates first-turn latency, which no source in this map has ever priced. Resolution is the cost dial, on both axes: 224×224 gives 6.7× at 48 MB of cache, 1024×1024 gives 13.1× at 156 MB — so the native-resolution direction this map credited Qwen2.5-VL with (linear encoder cost in patch count, gains landing on documents and charts where a fixed 336px grid binds) has a local memory bill attached to it that nobody has stated, and video is worse (12.1 GB for 64 frames). And the fix is a caching problem, not a modelling one: identity is established by SHA-256 over decoded pixels rather than over the URL or blob, which is the whole trick. Scope it hard — one engine, one machine at 128 GB (roughly 4× a typical enthusiast's memory), no quality evaluation anywhere in the source, the author is the engine's own vendor, and it is MLX rather than llama.cpp, so the specific question this map asked is still open. What is no longer open is whether the tower is a serving problem worth attention. See caching-vision-embeddings-is-the-multi-turn-vlm-lever.

  • 2026-07-26 — serving edge from sglang-radixattention-prefix-caching (T1, primary map local-inference): the vault's first source about serving a VLM at all, and it answers — obliquely but concretely — the question this map has asked since 2026-07-22 about "a variable per-image token count against a preallocated KV cache." The answer is don't preallocate: put image tokens in the same paged pool as text, key the cache on the hash of the input image, and a variable token count stops being a special case. Measured at 0.18 → 1.15 images/s on LLaVA-v1.5-7B (6.4×) and 0.02 → 0.10 frame/s on LLaVA-NeXT-34B (video), against the model authors' own Hugging Face implementations — a large multiple, which says the image tokens were the dominant recomputed cost whenever a conversation returns to the same image. Production corroboration over a month on Chatbot Arena: 52.4% cache hit rate on LLaVA-NeXT-34B, lower than the text-only Vicuna-33B's 74.1% but substantial. Scope: this is about serving cost, not architecture — it says nothing about the encoder fork this map is contested over, and all of it is datacenter GPU, so the GGUF/llama.cpp vision-tower question stays open. What it does establish is that the interface both VLM families share — images become a token sequence — is what makes the serving stack's existing machinery apply unchanged. See prefix-reuse-attacks-prefill-and-needs-no-concurrency.

It does not simply add a third data point — it splits a claim the map had been carrying as one thing, taking the projection interface to n=3 while showing the frozen CLIP encoder was never the replicated part. Ripples: transformer-architecture (RoPE now appears in-vault in three forms — 1D over text, 2D over image patches, and MRoPE keyed to absolute wall-clock time over video — a stronger claim for the rotation mechanism than position-can-be-a-rotation-not-an-addition's original paper made; the ViT also imports SwiGLU + RMSNorm from the LLM block); context-extension (dynamic FPS sampling is a content-side answer to long input — spend fewer tokens per second of video — as opposed to YaRN's position-side interpolation: hours of video fit by sampling, not by extending the window); local-inference (the 3B variant is the first in-vault model sized for the edge half of this group). Graded T2, same as qwen3-technical-report — vendor report, open weights, standard benchmarks, no external review, no human-preference measurement, and no ablation separating window attention or native resolution from a 3.4× data increase. See the-vision-encoder-is-a-design-choice-not-a-frozen-import.
  • 2026-07-21 — ingest of chameleon-early-fusion-mixed-modal (Chameleon Team, FAIR/Meta, arXiv 2024-05, T1): the source that downgrades this map to contested by reopening the architecture question the two-source replication had appeared to settle. Open questions:

  • The data question is now the map's live tension, and it is not settled by one counterexample. Molmo shows a VLM-free pipeline can reach the top of a benchmark suite; it does not show that teacher-distilled data is worse at matched cost, because no source compares them under controlled conditions. What a practitioner needs — given a fixed budget, is human collection or teacher synthesis the better spend? — has no in-vault answer.
  • The architecture space is 3-vs-2 and still unresolved (revised 2026-07-27). Early fusion is no longer n=1 and its generative claim is no longer partly reproducible — Emu3 demonstrates image and video generation from the same weights that do perception, from a second group. Late fusion remains n=3 on the interface; InternVL and Llama-Vision are still un-ingested. What is unchanged is the thing that matters: no source compares the two families at matched cost. A count is not a comparison, and now it is a closer count. Two sub-questions have sharpened rather than closed. Does early fusion's understanding hold up outside its own authors' evaluation — the two clean cross-family cells in-vault are one paper's unmarked benchmarks, on which early fusion wins two and loses three. And is the CLIP prior's absence what costs it, or the tokenizer's lossiness? A 32,768-entry codebook reconstructing at LPIPS ~0.1 puts a fine-detail ceiling on perception before any model sees anything, and no benchmark in-vault isolates that from the architecture choice.
  • Nobody has priced early fusion, and the price is the whole question for this vault (new 2026-07-27). 4,096 tokens per image, an 8B model, a 131,072-token context — and no latency, no throughput, no memory footprint, no quantized variant anywhere in the source. The late-fusion side is barely better off: the map's only edge-sized data point is Qwen2.5-VL's 3B, and nothing says what GGUF or llama.cpp does with a vision tower either way. So the map has two competing architectures and zero consumer-hardware measurements of either. Whichever is cheaper to run at 4-bit on one card is the one that matters for this vault's premise, and that is not the axis anyone has published on. Sharpest concrete form: an early-fusion model's KV cache is dominated by image tokens, which is exactly the regime local-inference says binds first.
  • The encoder question is now the map's live architectural fork, and it has three branches with two measured. Frozen off-the-shelf CLIP at fixed resolution (LLaVA, Molmo — cheap, node-day economics); trained-from-scratch native-resolution ViT (Qwen2.5-VL — lab-scale, wins on document/OCR); and unfreezing an existing CLIP, which nobody in-vault has tried and which is the only one of the three a practitioner could actually afford. Nor does any source ablate native resolution against the data scale-up that shipped alongside it, so how much of Qwen's document advantage is the encoder rather than 4.1T tokens is asserted, not shown.
  • The vault now has a directional prior against this map's newest source's own numbers. Molmo published where academic suites and human raters disagree and named Qwen2-VL as a model that scores better on benchmarks than with humans (open-vlms-are-mostly-distilled-closed-vlms). Qwen2.5-VL reports an 11-benchmark vendor table with no human-preference measurement and no data or decontamination disclosure. Discount accordingly (benchmark-scores-can-be-memorization); read its architecture as the durable content and its ranking as a vendor claim.
  • Does PEFT transfer cleanly to the vision half? weight-decomposition-recovers-lora-gap validated DoRA on LLaVA, so adapters work on the LLM side — but whether the projection and the vision encoder want the same treatment, and whether the contested rank findings in peft-methods apply to cross-modal alignment (plausibly a high-rank update), is unmeasured. Molmo's all-parameters-unfrozen recipe is weak evidence that the update is not low-rank, but it never tested an adapter.
  • The knowledge gap Molmo shows is unexplained. A model can top an 11-benchmark average while sitting 15 points below GPT-4o on MMMU. Whether that reflects the backbone LLM, the absence of teacher-distilled reasoning traces, or the benchmark, is exactly the kind of question a suite average is designed not to answer.
  • The edge half of this group is PARTLY SERVED as of 2026-08-01, and what remains missing is now specific. The gap was: quantizing and deploying a VLM (a vision encoder plus an LLM, two very different weight distributions), on-device constraints, and the compress→convert→deploy pipeline have no sources. Two sources now sit in it — LQA prices the encoder on an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, and at-a-fixed-footprint-take-the-larger-quantized-model measures a generative VLM at 4-bit and gives the fixed-budget ordering. What is still missing is everything between them. Nobody has measured the two weight distributions separately in a generative VLM: the 4-bit arms here quantize the whole model, so whether the encoder or the language model is what loses the confidence signal is unknown, and LQA's per-modality allocation rule (vision at 4 bits, text at 8) has never been applied to a model that generates. No throughput or latency number exists for a quantized generative VLM on any consumer card — this source reports accuracy and AUROC and no wall-clock at all. And whether GGUF/llama.cpp handles the vision tower well is still unknown, since every VLM measurement in this map is PyTorch, MLX or a bespoke pipeline.
  • Does the confidence collapse belong to the vision tower or the language model? (new 2026-08-01 — the cheapest follow-up this map has.) A 2B VLM at 4-bit loses 15 points of error-detection AUROC while losing 1.6 points of accuracy, and its stated-confidence parse rate falls from 99% to 64%. Both are language-model behaviours (token probabilities, format compliance) measured on a model whose encoder was quantized too. LQA already argues precision should be allocated across the modality boundary; the ablation that would settle this is one grid — vision tower at 4 bits with the language model at 8, and the reverse — on the same degraded-image benchmark. If the signal loss is a language-model effect, it is a general quantization finding this map merely happened to observe first, and post-training-quantization should own it outright.
