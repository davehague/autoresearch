Status note (2026-08-28): holds contested, and the map finally gets an account of why its empirical rank answers keep refusing to generalize. lora-rank-error-bounds-attention (T1, solo arXiv preprint, formal results only — no experiments of any kind) proves, under stated assumptions, that the best attention KL achievable at rank r is bracketed by an explicit function of the downstream-weighted tail energy T_r: the residual spectrum of the target update after it has been reweighted by the queries and keys the task actually produces. So the rank an adapter needs is a property of ΔW crossed with the task, not of ΔW — a large singular direction the task never activates costs nothing, a small one that repeatedly reorders attention costs a great deal. That is the reason a swept rank does not transfer, stated as a theorem rather than as an observation, and it is the first structural explanation this map has for the saturation adapter-rank-is-a-dial-between-what-you-learn-and-what-you-keep measured last night: past the rank covering the task-activated directions there is nothing left to represent, and the retention curve says the extra capacity gets spent on damage instead. Two further results with practical shape. Attention KL is quadratic in small score errors and only linear in large ones (ψ(t)=min{t²,t}, with an explicit obstruction to any global quadratic lower bound), which is an argument for the low-rank regime being safe in the way this map's empirical sources keep finding. And softmax saturation separates the rank needed to match the attention function from the rank needed to match the logits — for an explicit Walsh family, k becomes k − ⌊k/3⌋, with a second family reaching a 4/7 ratio — but the same construction's lower bound forbids collapsing to o(k), so it is a constant-factor saving and not an escape hatch. The extensions touch real configuration choices: fused multi-head LoRA is analyzed with a shared rank budget, and joint query/key adaptation is priced for the extra error of factoring one effective score update into two separately-budgeted factors — the first statement here of what widening target_modules costs beyond what it buys. Status does not move, and the discounts are why it is graded suggestive despite being a proof: there are no experiments, the bounds are on the attention KL of one head and explicitly not on head output or task loss, they are population statements whose lower-bound constants are theorem assumptions rather than anything a calibration set certifies, the author states outright that no constant has been checked for tightness on a trained head, and the whole framework describes representation rather than optimization — the best candidate in a rank-constrained class, not what SGD finds, which is precisely the confound this map's rank sweeps also cannot separate. It also needs a trained high-rank target first, so it compresses an adapter you have and cannot prescribe one you do not. One architectural caveat reaches outside the map: RoPE couples the query and key factors across relative positions, so the joint-Q/K spectral specialization does not apply directly in a rotary model — which is every model this vault's readers fine-tune.

Status note (2026-08-26): holds contested, and the map's third independent measurement of the same shape lands — a per-unit importance score earning less than the decision to be non-uniform at all. aqlora-zero-search-quantized-lora (T1, Kennesaw State; code and raw per-run artifacts released, every table recomputable without a GPU) sets out to buy back the time QLoRA spends dequantizing every NF4 block on every forward and backward pass — a cost independent studies put at 0.7–0.8× fp16 LoRA throughput, and one this map has almost never priced, having measured quantized adaptation in memory and energy. Keeping the top-K layers in fp16 lets them skip dequantization, which over nine independent timing sessions on six models across four families is +11.1 ± 2.7% over well-tuned QLoRA for the speed setting (costing about one accuracy point) and +4.8 ± 2.4% for the quality setting at parity, for 0.2 GiB. The scale arithmetic is this map's own premise stated in memory: at 7B fp16 LoRA peaks at 19.1 GiB, already crowding a 24 GB card, against AQLoRA-q's 10.6 GiB at identical accuracy (89.0 both); at 14B fp16 LoRA's weights alone are 27.5 GiB, past the card before a single activation, while every quantized method loads the same base in 9–11 GiB. Downstream the three selections are indistinguishable. The speed result is untouched (any K fp16 layers skip the same dequantization) and so is accuracy parity; what dies is the explanation. That makes three objects on this map — adapter rank (a-random-non-uniform-rank-profile-already-beats-a-flat-one), bit-width (bit-width-can-be-assigned-by-architectural-role), and now dequantization protection — where the ranking adds nothing over being non-uniform, with the boundary condition supplied by an-importance-weighted-allocation-pays-only-under-a-tight-budget: an importance signal earns its keep only once the budget is tight enough that the unweighted version has begun to fail, and this budget is loose by construction. Status note (2026-08-08): holds contested, and the map acquires a substrate rather than another variant. Under matched ~1.3M trainable parameters it beats soft prefixes, prefix tuning and LoRA in 7 of 8 settings (Qwen3.5-4B SearchQA EM 80.0 vs LoRA's 70.1; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B LiveMath 72.6 vs 52.4), while the per-task artifact is 20 KiB at rank 1 against LoRA's 10 MiB and a cached swap costs 0 ms. What we believe now:

  • A basis shared across layers, not a per-layer rank, is a new lever for PEFT memory (new 2026-08-31). FrameFT represents every layer's ΔW as a sparse coefficient vector against one algorithmically-generated Fusion Frame basis shared across all adapted layers, instead of giving each layer its own low-rank factor pair the way LoRA, DoRA and AdaLoRA all do. Because the basis itself is not a per-layer cost, the paper's claim is that per-layer trainable-parameter cost drops below LoRA's O(dr) at accuracy "on par with or exceeding" SOTA PEFT baselines, via dual sparsity — sparse coefficients and sparse basis-element usage — with formal convergence results for the fitting procedure. Evaluated on SFT language benchmarks plus vision-model applications. Discounted to suggestive: this digest is graded from an abstract-level read, with no baseline methods named and no parameter-count deltas at matched accuracy yet pulled from the full PDF. If the numbers hold on a follow-up read, this map's cost sub-thread (2) gets a point on the cost curve genuinely decoupled from a per-layer rank choice, rather than an incremental rank truncation. [T1] (a-shared-basis-across-layers-cuts-peft-parameters-below-loras-per-layer-cost)
  • The rank a LoRA needs is set by the part of the update the task activates, not by the update's spectrum (new 2026-08-28). Under stated assumptions the best attention KL at rank r is bracketed above and below by an explicit function of the downstream-weighted tail energy — the residual spectrum of the target update after reweighting by the queries and keys the downstream task produces. A direction the task never uses contributes nothing; a small one that repeatedly reorders attention contributes a lot. This is the first account here of why rank answers are task-specific rather than method-specific, and it explains three things the map had recorded as puzzles: swept ranks do not transfer (the weighting matrix changed), singular-value heuristics fail (they omit the weighting), and acquisition saturates (the task-activated tail has already gone to zero). Two riders with practical shape. Attention KL is quadratic in small score errors and only linear in large ones, so the damage from an under-ranked adapter flattens rather than compounding — an argument for the low-rank regime being safe in the way this map's empirical sources keep finding. And joint query/key adaptation carries an extra, separable error from factoring one effective score update into two independently-budgeted low-rank factors, the first statement here of what widening target_modules costs beyond what it buys. Graded suggestive and the discounts are load-bearing: no experiments at all, bounds on one head's attention distribution rather than task loss, population statements with no finite-sample guarantee, constants never checked for tightness on a trained head, representation rather than optimization, and it needs an existing high-rank target so it compresses a rank rather than prescribing one. Also: RoPE breaks the joint-Q/K specialization, and RoPE is what everyone here fine-tunes. [T1, theory only] (the-rank-a-lora-needs-is-set-by-the-part-of-the-update-the-task-activates)
  • Softmax saturation makes the rank needed for the attention function smaller than the rank needed for the logits — by about a third, and not more (new 2026-08-28). softmax(T,0) and softmax(2T,0) converge to the same distribution while their logits diverge; formalized as a relative closure rank over the score space a query-only LoRA can actually reach, an explicit Walsh family whose every exact finite-logit realization has rank k has limiting-attention rank k − ⌊k/3⌋, with a second construction reaching a 4/7 ratio. The same construction's lower bound forces every limiting block to keep rank ≥ 2, so saturation cannot collapse the requirement to o(k) — a constant-factor saving with an explicit counter-family against the argument that attention's nonlinearity makes low rank always sufficient. It is a second, independent reason a swept rank does not transfer, operating even at fixed task: how peaked the target attention is decides how much of the logit spectrum has to be reproduced at all. These are designed constructions, not models of language data, and the author says so. [T1, theory only] (softmax-saturation-lowers-the-rank-needed-for-the-function-below-the-rank-needed-for-the-logits)
  • "PEFT is safe because it touches few parameters" is a claim about rank, and it fails outside the low range. On a controlled fact-injection task with matched acquired knowledge, rank 8 and full fine-tuning both retain ~98% of out-of-domain capability; rank 32 retains 79.8% and rank 64 retains 36.7%, with instruction-following collapsing 80.2 → 14.6 and code 79.2 → 23.2 while TruthfulQA barely moves. Acquisition saturates at rank 16 and reverses, so rank 64 is dominated on both axes. Reproduced without quantization on a second model, so it binds LoRA as much as QLoRA. What this does not settle is how much belongs to rank and how much to 100 epochs over 1,938 examples — no epoch ablation was run. Practical form: at low rank the old safety argument still holds; past about rank 16 an out-of-domain arm is mandatory, not optional. (adapter-rank-is-a-dial-between-what-you-learn-and-what-you-keep, T1, 2026-08-27.)
  • Ask what kind of fine-tune you are running before you pick a rank — the cliff is domain-conditional. The same model, ranks and retention benchmarks show no frontier at all on a math adaptation (FFT 42.50, r=16 42.60, r=32 42.03 pass@1; OOD drops of 1.6–2.2 points that do not order in rank). The trade-off bites when adaptation must install new factual associations and largely vanishes when it reinforces a skill pretraining already supports — the same axis, distance from the pretraining distribution, that makes low rank a real capacity ceiling in low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new. Which is also the cleanest available explanation of why the LoRA-matches-full-FT literature, run overwhelmingly on task adaptation, kept finding equivalence. Graded suggestive: the two arms differ in epochs, dataset size and rank coverage, so domain type and training regime are confounded. (the-forgetting-frontier-appears-only-when-the-adaptation-installs-new-facts, T1, 2026-08-27.)
  • The forgetting mechanism now has a dose-response curve and a label-free instrument. Symmetric KL from the base model, dense RMS drift of the merged (α/r)BA update, and SVD intruder excess over the full-fine-tuning rate all rise monotonically r=8 → r=64 and track the behavioural collapse. Symmetric KL needs no benchmark, no held-out set and no labels — two forward passes over any corpus — which on a map whose evidence almost always requires an eval harness is a genuinely local check. Correlational only: the paper reports the association and offers no causal test at rank, and gives no coefficients. (spectral-departure-from-the-pretrained-basis-is-dose-dependent-in-rank, T1, 2026-08-27.)
  • QLoRA's memory saving is charged in wall-clock, and a small fraction of it is refundable at parity. The 4-bit backbone dequantizes every NF4 block on every forward and backward pass, running at 0.7–0.8× fp16 LoRA throughput — so quantizing to fit costs twice. Keeping a small set of layers in fp16 lets them skip dequantization: pooled over nine independent sessions across six models and four families, +4.8 ± 2.4% faster than well-tuned QLoRA at accuracy level with fp16 LoRA for +0.2 GiB, or +11.1 ± 2.7% if the backward pass also stops early, costing 0.5–1.5 accuracy points. Faster in every session run. About 6 of those 11 points are the known backward-stopping trick rather than the protection, and with gradient checkpointing off the gap widens to 42% at 23% less memory. Measured entirely on one pre-Hopper V100; the consumer-card case is a projection, not a measurement. What generalizes without the method is the framing — the time axis of quantized adaptation is a real cost with real levers on it, and this map had been scoring PEFT in memory and energy alone. (protecting-layers-from-dequantization-buys-training-speed-not-quality, T1, 2026-08-26.)
  • The ranking that spends a protection budget adds nothing over the decision to spend one — third object, same answer. At a fixed K=29 protected layers with random draws matched on parameter count so peak memory is byte-identical (1.177 GiB), eight of ten random selections beat the top-K-by-NF4-reconstruction-error rule; an average random draw recovers about twice the perplexity, and downstream the three selections (top-K, bottom-K, random) are indistinguishable against a seed spread near 0.3. With ten draws this is no evidence better than chance, not evidence of worse. A second control in the same paper finds choosing which layers to adapt by weight density is also worse than random, last of three in all four cells. The quantized variant is the sharper result: QPiSSA beats QLoRA (LLaMA-3-70B GSM8K 86.05% vs 81.73%, +4.32) because pulling the high-energy components into a full-precision adapter leaves the residual with smaller spectral range, so the 4-bit error is smaller from step one. Critically, this is measured on task-adaptation benchmarks — the exact regime where LoRA already matched full FT — with no forgetting or new-domain test, so it raises the low-rank ceiling without resolving the domain-acquisition contest. Net: if you use LoRA, initialize with PiSSA (QPiSSA over QLoRA); it's free — but it's still rank-r and can't manufacture the high-rank update a new domain needs. [T1]
  • Adapter initialization is now a two-source, two-group finding — and it moves the bit-width floor, not just the accuracy. PiSSA and LoftQ solve the same objective from opposite ends. QPiSSA takes the SVD of W first, moves the principal components into the full-precision adapter, and quantizes the low-energy residual. LoftQ minimizes ‖W − Q − ABᵀ‖_F by alternating quantization and SVD, so the adapter is initialized on the quantization error itself. Two independent groups (Peking Univ.; Georgia Tech/Microsoft), months apart, converge on the load-bearing claim: the quantization error is not a fixed tax on the base — it depends on which slice of the weight you keep in full precision, and the adapter is that slice. Read that as replicated. The sharper consequence is LoftQ's sub-3-bit result: at 2.5-bit on LLaMA-2-13B, QLoRA reaches 19.39 WikiText-2 ppl and LoftQ 5.22; at 2.25- and 2-bit QLoRA does not converge at all while LoftQ reaches 7.69 ppl / 25.4% GSM8K. So "QLoRA fails below 3-bit" was evidence about QLoRA's zero-init, not about bit-widths — though LoftQ's own 2-bit numbers degrade sharply (7.85 vs 5.24 ppl at 4-bit), so sub-3-bit remains a real loss and the 4-bit default holds. What the two sources do not agree on is which slice belongs in the adapter (hardest to quantize, or actually mis-quantized — these coincide only if quantization error concentrates in the top singular directions, which neither establishes), and their gains sit on different bases, tasks, and scales, so +4.32 and +8.1 are not comparable numbers. The single measurement worth carrying even if the method is never used: with the trainable budget pinned at 0.28M on DeBERTaV3-base, MNLI reads 89.99 for adapters on the second FFN matrix against 88.58 for the query projection, and 88.6 for layers 10–12 against 77.87 for layers 1–3 — a 10.7-point spread from nothing but where an identical budget was spent. That is larger than most effects this map argues about, and it indicts the two defaults in current tooling: the original paper's W_q/W_v targeting picks the worst two cells here, and "adapt every linear submodule" avoids the choice rather than making it. Learned allocation — write the update as a pseudo-SVD PΛQ, score each singular triplet by a smoothed sensitivity-times-uncertainty signal, prune to a cubically decaying global budget, masking singular values rather than deleting adapter pairs so a misjudged component can revive — recovers about a GLUE point at matched budget (89.31 vs LoRA r=8's 88.34) and +1.2 SQuADv2.0 F1 at 0.08% of full-fine-tuning parameters, where it also exceeds full fine-tuning outright. Note the honest gap between 10.7 points available and ~1 point recovered: the sensitivity signal finds some of the structure, not most of it. Map holds contested — this does not touch the domain-acquisition axis at all. The reason it doesn't is the ingest's most consequential caveat: there is no decoder-only model anywhere in it. A 183M encoder and an encoder-decoder, on GLUE, SQuAD and XSum — task adaptation only, no instruction tuning, no new knowledge, no quantized base, and no wall-clock or memory accounting for a method that computes per-entry sensitivity plus two moving averages every step. One interaction the vault records as its own inference, not either author's claim: AdaLoRA states outright that it inherits LoRA's alpha/r scaling, and its mechanism is per-matrix, time-varying rank reallocation — so under the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank every matrix the allocator promotes has its contribution damped by the promotion. rsLoRA's author names AdaLoRA as a conjectured beneficiary without testing it; this source supplies the reason the conjecture is stronger than it looks, and makes the reported gains a floor rather than an estimate. Two edges out: a structural pairing with post-training-quantization that reads like a contradiction and is not (adapter budget pays in the FFN; precision pays on the shared path every token traverses — add capacity where the model specializes, preserve precision where error compounds), and a note to evaluation-contamination on a method whose cost side is entirely unreported. See where-the-adapter-budget-goes-matters-more-than-how-much-there-is, masking-singular-values-makes-rank-allocation-reversible.

  • 2026-07-26 — ingest of rslora-rank-stabilization-scaling (Kalajdzievski, Tenyx, arXiv 2023, T1): the first source to attack this map's contest from under it, by questioning the parameterization rather than the method. The rank ceiling the map has reasoned about since 2026-07-18 rests partly on the field's belief that raising LoRA's rank does not help — and that belief was measured under gamma_r = alpha/r, which the paper proves is the wrong exponent: adapters are rank-stabilized (forward moments and backward gradients both Theta_r(1)) iff gamma_r ∈ Theta_r(1/sqrt(r)). The empirics land on both sides of the theorem — gradient norms collapsing two orders of magnitude across ranks 4→2048 under LoRA and staying banded under rsLoRA, monotone gains with rank only in the corrected version, replicated on GPT-J 6B / GSM8k / Adafactor and under SGD, with 1/r^(1/4) unstable and 1/r^2 collapsing harder as predicted. Two controls matter: an lr sweep proves it is capacity rather than a step-size boost, and Appendix B.3 proves initialization cannot substitute — which closes a door on the map's own init thread, since adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling and quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor both locate the lever in where the adapter starts, and this shows parameterization is a different lever with a different failure mode. Status holds contested, and deliberately: this does not favour either side, it says the measurement instrument was miscalibrated for one of them. Also worth flagging as a rare clean case of the vault's own rule biting both ways — perplexity is the right evidence for the gradient-collapse claim (training health) and the wrong evidence for the better-model claim (model selection), and the paper offers only perplexity for both. Caveats: single author, industry lab, no venue, ~600 steps per run, no downstream benchmark of any kind, nothing over a quantized base, and rank 2048 is far outside practice while the 8–64 range people actually use is not separately reported.   • 2026-07-26 — interaction edge from sublinear-memory-gradient-checkpointing (T1, primary map distributed-training): the axis this map's readers cannot avoid finally has a primary source and a price. The cross-cutting argument has been that PEFT deletes most of the memory ceiling — sharding, tensor parallelism and CPU offload all attack terms proportional to trainable parameters, and LoRA on a 4-bit base has almost none — but activations do not scale that way, so recomputation is the one technique PEFT cannot render moot (rematerialization-trades-compute-for-the-activation-memory-nothing-else-shrinks). The price is now graded: O(n/k) + O(k) memory, one extra forward pass at k = sqrt(n), ~30% runtime, and — the part that matters for anyone comparing fine-tunes — the gradient is unchanged, so unlike sharded optimizer math it never enters the experimental configuration. See activation-memory-is-a-tunable-continuum-not-a-fixed-cost, recomputation-is-the-one-memory-technique-that-is-exact.

  • 2026-07-25 — mechanism edge from lima-less-is-more-for-alignment (T1, primary map data-curation): this map's central contest gets its missing "why", from the data side and without a single adapter in sight. Since 2026-07-18 the map has held a conditional it could state but not explain — low-rank adaptation matches full fine-tuning on task adaptation and underperforms on domain acquisition (low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new) — and a revised default that reads as a wager: 4-bit LoRA is the right opening move and an explicit bet that your target is a task, not a body of knowledge. The Superficial Alignment Hypothesis is that wager restated as a claim about where things live: capabilities and knowledge are acquired almost entirely in pretraining, and alignment only selects which subdistribution of response formats to use. If that is right, then a low-rank update is exactly the right shape of intervention for a selection problem, and no amount of adapter rank substitutes for pretraining when the target genuinely needs new knowledge. The evidence is that 1,000 curated examples on a 65B base — no RLHF, no reward model — reach 43% equal-or-better against GPT-4 and beat the same base trained on 52× more distilled data. Two findings, one about parameters and one about data, arriving from opposite directions at the same boundary, neither citing the other. That yields a testable prediction this map should carry: the targets where LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning ought to be the same targets where curated instruction data underperforms continued pretraining — and if that correlation holds, it is the a-priori test for "does this target need a high-rank update" that the index has called the sharpest open item across the vault. Nobody has checked, and checking is cheap. Bound: LIMA runs full fine-tuning, so this is an argument about the shape of the problem rather than a PEFT measurement; its baselines are 2023-vintage and retired. See alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition.

  • 2026-07-24 — interaction edge from gpipe-micro-batch-pipeline-parallelism (T1, primary map distributed-training): there is one memory term LoRA does not shrink, and nobody in-vault has priced it. The vault's memory-wall comparison lists four axes — shard it, split the layer, demote it to CPU, shrink what you train — and has flagged that PEFT may render offload moot, since LoRA on a 4-bit base has almost no fp32 optimizer state left to demote. That argument generalizes: parameters, gradients and optimizer states all scale with the number of trainable parameters, so freezing 99.99% of the model shrinks all three at once. Activations do not. The forward activations of a 70B model are a function of batch size, sequence length and depth — identical whether you are updating every weight or none of them. So re-materialization / gradient checkpointing is the one memory technique in this vault that PEFT cannot make redundant, and by elimination the one most likely to bind in the LoRA-on-one-card regime this map's premise assumes. GPipe's isolated measurement of the lever — 3.9× more parameters on one unchanged accelerator on a 2018 8GB card, before any distribution — shows the size of the effect, though not at this map's scale or precision. This is the vault's inference, not the paper's, and it names the cheapest unrun experiment shared between the two maps: peak memory for a 4-bit LoRA fine-tune with and without gradient checkpointing at a realistic sequence length — which matters more now that context-length-is-a-cheap-post-hoc-edit-not-a-pretraining-property makes long sequences cheap to acquire and expensive to train through. Open questions:

  • Is part of the placement effect an alpha/r artifact? (new 2026-08-02, and it is the cheapest run on this map.) a-random-non-uniform-rank-profile-already-beats-a-flat-one measures that a randomly scattered rank profile beats a flat one at matched budget. Under the standard alpha/r divisor a module's adapter is damped in proportion to its own rank, so a random rank profile is also a random per-module effective-step-size profile — which would make some of what this map calls "placement" an optimization effect rather than a claim about where adaptation capacity belongs. Repeat the nine-way ablation under alpha/sqrt(r), where the coupling disappears: if random no longer beats uniform, the free fifth was step-size scatter. use_rslora ships in Hugging Face PEFT, the ablation is nine short runs on one card, and it also re-tests the same question the map has had open since 2026-07-27 about AdaLoRA.
  • Does placement survive a per-method learning-rate sweep? (new 2026-08-02.) The decoder-only placement result runs every arm at a fixed 1e-4. adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact established that a shared default is the confound that manufactured the previous round of adapter wins, and that different parameterizations want optima up to 18× apart. Until someone sweeps, +1.13 points is an upper bound on a possibly smaller number — and the missing control is the other one: a-random-non-uniform-rank-profile-already-beats-a-flat-one shows every adaptive-rank paper should be quoting itself against random allocation rather than against uniform, and none of them do.
  • Does the saturation boundary predict where a variant can still win? (new 2026-08-02.) initialization-leverage-vanishes-where-tuned-lora-saturates converts this map's 2026-07-30 null into a scope condition: initialization has leverage only where tuned LoRA has not closed on full fine-tuning. Every task in both studies is one where it had closed. The prediction is therefore sharp and untested — the setting where an initialization scheme should still pay is domain acquisition, which is exactly the axis this map is contested on and the one neither source runs. Pair the ULoRA family with the MiCA-style injected-corpus quiz and there is a real experiment, on one card, that would speak to the contest rather than around it.
  • Does nuclear-norm regularization make rank selection free on a transformer? (new 2026-08-01 — the cheapest untried item on this map, and the only one whose payoff is a deleted decision rather than a better number.) over-ranking-is-an-estimator-defect-not-a-lora-defect proves that a nuclear-norm-then-truncate estimator's rate saturates at Θ̃(r*d/n) regardless of the rank declared, so the U-shape flattens and "what rank?" stops mattering. The theorem is verified on synthetic trace regression only; all three real fine-tunes in the source run the unregularized ERM everyone ships. The run is a rank sweep with one extra loss term, on one card, and it decides whether this map's longest-running configuration question was ever a question about LoRA at all. Pair it with the second half of the same result: since rd/n sets the penalty, the sweep should vary dataset size alongside rank — the theory says the U deepens as n shrinks, and nobody has drawn that surface.
  • Do the estimation-cost and subspace stories make opposite predictions about corpus size? (new 2026-08-01.) Θ(rd/n) says the cost of meeting a large r* falls as the corpus grows, so a domain-acquisition gap driven by insufficient rank should narrow with more target-domain data. the-minor-singular-directions-are-where-new-knowledge-fits measured the opposite — its advantage over LoRA widened from ~3 points on 30 blog texts to ~10 on a 100,000-token book, which its authors read as a capacity story. Both cannot be the whole account. The discriminating experiment is one corpus-size sweep at fixed rank on a single domain-acquisition target, and it would also test whether the vault's regime split (cheap rank at corpus scale, expensive at curated scale) survives contact with a decoder-only model.
  • Was the adapter trainable? (new 2026-08-01, and it is a hygiene item rather than a research question.) small-scale-ppo-fails-three-ways-before-it-fails-at-learning reports adapters silently flagged non-trainable inside standard PEFT/TRL PPO pipelines. This map now holds three separate cases where the tooling rather than the method produced the published result, and no source in it reports a trainable-parameter count. The unanswerable-as-things-stand question: how much of the adapter-vs-full-fine-tuning literature was run on a pipeline that trained what it thought it was training? Nothing can be reconstructed retroactively; what can be done is to require the assertion going forward, and to note that the failure was found at 70M–500M with no check at 7B anywhere.
  • Does the subspace effect survive a real domain-acquisition benchmark, and does it show up in the spectrum? (new 2026-07-31 — the cheapest status-moving run on this map.) the-minor-singular-directions-are-where-new-knowledge-fits reports minor-subspace adaptation beating both LoRA and full fine-tuning on injected-corpus quizzes, on evidence too thin to move the status: 102 and 300 unreleased items, one scale, one full-fine-tuning row. The run that would settle it is exact and public — MiCA against LoRA against full fine-tuning on lora-vs-full-ft-illusion-of-equivalence's domain-acquisition setup, with the intruder-dimension SVD check on all three arms. The prediction the vault would be testing is specific: if dominant-subspace adaptation is what manufactures intruder dimensions, a minor-subspace adapter should show fewer of them at equal or better acquisition, which would convert two separate findings into one mechanism. Two riders. Nobody has run minor-subspace initialization on a quantized base, where LoftQ argues the adapter should hold the quantization error instead — three claimants to the same slot (principal components, quantization error, minor components) and no head-to-head. And the cross-check against data-curation's 2026-07-25 prediction is now runnable from both sides: if the targets where LoRA underperforms full FT are the targets where curated instruction data underperforms continued pretraining, a method that fixes the first should be measurable on the second.
  • Placement is the last lever standing and it has never been measured with memory attached (new 2026-07-31). The 2026-07-30 null emptied the initialization and architecture columns, leaving placement as the only one of the four choices with a large unexplained effect — 10.7 accuracy points at a fixed 0.28M budget, on a 183M encoder, with the learning rate held fixed. Tonight adds a second axis to the same variable from an unrelated study: a 2.6× peak-memory inversion at matched batch size driven purely by where the trainable parameters sit (peft-does-not-reliably-reduce-peak-memory). Neither group cites the other and neither measured the other's axis. The wanted table is small, cheap and does not exist anywhere: one adapter budget, several placements, on a decoder-only 7B, reporting downstream accuracy and peak VRAM together, each cell at its own tuned learning rate.
  • Does the variant null survive on a quantized base — the one place initialization demonstrably matters? (new 2026-07-30, and it is the sharpest hole tonight's null leaves.) The nine-variant sweep runs on full-precision bases and does not include LoftQ, whose entire claim is that initializing on the quantization error is what makes 2- and 2.5-bit LoRA converge where QLoRA fails outright (13B @2.5-bit: 5.22 vs 19.39 ppl, quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor). So the vault now holds "initialization is a tuning artifact" measured exactly where it has no counter-evidence, and "initialization is decisive" measured exactly where the sweep was not run. The experiment is one grid: PiSSA vs LoftQ vs plain init, on a 4-bit and a 2-bit base, each at its own tuned learning rate. If the null holds there too, this map loses a claim it currently records as shipped; if it does not, the null is a full-precision-only result and should be labelled as one.
  • Was every adapter comparison in this literature measured at one learning rate? (new 2026-07-30.) Tonight's null implies the field's baseline hygiene is the confound, not any method. The two push-back sources that make this map contested were themselves run at fixed hyperparameters — which means the domain-acquisition gap now carries two candidate artifacts, alpha/r and an untuned step size, and the item below has grown a second axis. This is not a reason to doubt the contest; it is a reason the resolving experiment must sweep both.
  • The contest's central comparison may need re-running under a corrected scaling factor (new 2026-07-26). This is now the cheapest experiment that could move this map's status, and it is cheaper than any other item here. Both push-back sources — low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new and intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting — configure LoRA under gamma_r = alpha/r, which the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank shows damps adapters harder as rank rises. So the two load-bearing questions are: does the domain-acquisition gap close, narrow, or hold when LoRA is run at high rank under alpha/sqrt(r); and do intruder dimensions appear at all in a rank-stabilized high-rank adapter, or are they a signature of an under-driven low-rank one? Nobody has checked either, use_rslora ships in Hugging Face PEFT, and both are a rank sweep on one base model. Until they are run, the map's contested status should be read as "unresolved and possibly mis-measured," not merely unresolved. Escalated 2026-07-29: the correction is not merely unabsorbed by the two push-back papers, it is absent from the reference library's quantization guide and from torchtune's memory guide, both of which document rank ranges up to 256 and recommend alpha values under the uncorrected scheme. So if the domain-acquisition gap is a scaling artifact, the artifact is currently the documented default for anyone following the two most likely starting points — which means a meaningful share of published LoRA-vs-full-fine-tuning comparisons may inherit it, and the experiment's value is now about the literature rather than about this map alone.
  • Nine ways to train an adapter on a quantized base, no head-to-head, and no price on the unmerged path (new 2026-07-29). Two sub-questions, both unpublished by anyone. Which of the nine backends actually preserves fine-tune quality — the QLoRA result is one backend at one bit-width, and AQLM at 2 bits is a very different proposition that nobody has evaluated with an adapter on top. And what an unmerged adapter costs at inference, which is the standing price of the AQLM and torchao paths and the thing that decides whether aggressive quantization is reachable for a local deployment at all.
  • The four levers have never been varied together, and one pair is actively self-cancelling (new 2026-07-27). Placement, initialization, parameterization and rank each have a source; no source varies more than one. Two specific holes follow. First, the map's most-wanted comparison grows another dimension: it was PiSSA vs LoftQ vs plain init under alpha/r and alpha/sqrt(r) across a rank sweep, and placement is now a fifth axis on the same grid — nobody knows whether the initialization methods' differences survive being pointed at the right matrices. Second, and cheaper: AdaLoRA reallocates rank per matrix while inheriting alpha/r, which damps a matrix exactly in proportion to the capacity the allocator just gave it. That is this vault's inference rather than either paper's claim, and it is the single place in the PEFT literature where the scaling bug should bite hardest — which makes "AdaLoRA under alpha/sqrt(r)" the most informative cheap run available, since both pieces already ship in Hugging Face PEFT. rsLoRA's author conjectures the improvement and tests nothing.
  • Every placement result in-vault is on a 183M encoder (new 2026-07-27). The 10.7-point spread above says FFN matrices and upper layers are where adapter budget pays — on DeBERTaV3-base, on GLUE. Whether that ordering survives a decoder-only 7B–70B, an instruction-tuning target, or a domain-acquisition target is untested, and it is exactly the regime where the answer would change what a practitioner types. Worth pairing with the observation from shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts that the precision-sensitivity ordering runs the other way — attention, embeddings and gates cost far more per parameter when degraded than FFN weights do. Both can hold: the shared path is where error compounds, the FFN is where specialization lives. If that pairing survives at scale it is a usable rule — precision on the shared path, adaptation on the specializing part — and nobody has tested it as one.
  • Where exactly is the task-adaptation / domain-acquisition boundary? The map now has both sides but no crisp diagnostic. What a practitioner needs is an a priori test — given a target dataset, predict whether the required update is low-rank — rather than discovering it from a disappointing eval. Unsourced.
  • Does DoRA's decomposition change the contested picture? DoRA recovers part of the LoRA→full-FT gap on commonsense reasoning (weight-decomposition-recovers-lora-gap), but was never tested on the distant-domain / continued-pretraining regime where the gap is now known to be largest, nor checked for intruder dimensions. Two unanswered questions that would move this map's status.
  • Does the push-back survive quantization and scale? Both contested T1s run at 7B/13B (and RoBERTa-scale for the spectral analysis) over full-precision bases. QPiSSA (2026-07-21) is a data point near the operating point — a 4-bit 70B base, fine-tuned and beating QLoRA — but on task benchmarks, so it says the low-rank ceiling can be raised at 4-bit/70B, not whether the domain-acquisition gap or intruder dimensions persist there. The 65B-on-one-card × new-domain × 4-bit combination is still the map's most decision-relevant untested cell.
  • Is the spectral fix practical? Post-hoc scaling of intruder dimensions is demonstrated but productized nowhere; no library ships it, and nobody has checked it over merged or quantized adapters.
  • Rank/placement/hyperparameter practice has moved since 2021 (which modules, what rank) — and rank is now a first-order decision rather than a tuning detail. The variant zoo is four-sourced (revised 2026-07-26): DoRA (decomposition), PiSSA and LoftQ (init), and rsLoRA landed 2026-07-26 (parameterization). Still unsourced: AdaLoRA — which the rsLoRA author flags as inheriting LoRA's scaling factor and conjectures would improve when rebuilt on alpha/sqrt(r), untested — and OLoRA. The most-wanted missing source is now specific and one dimension larger than it was: not "a neutral head-to-head of the init family" but PiSSA vs LoftQ vs plain init, each under alpha/r and alpha/sqrt(r), on one base at one bit-width across a rank sweep. All three init papers are author-evaluated against vanilla LoRA/QLoRA only, they disagree about which subspace the adapter should hold, their reported gains sit on non-overlapping scales (LoftQ tops out at 13B, QPiSSA runs to 70B), none cites the others — and whether their differences grow or vanish in the high-rank regime rsLoRA unlocks is the part that would actually settle which lever matters.
  • Nobody has looked for intruder dimensions in an init-method spectrum. PiSSA seeds the adapter on the base's principal singular directions; LoftQ seeds it on the quantization-error subspace. Both are exactly the kind of intervention that would plant or prevent intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting — and neither paper runs the SVD analysis that would show it. Since the whole contest in this map turns on what LoRA does outside the target task, a spectral check on a LoftQ- or PiSSA-initialized fine-tune is the cheapest experiment that could move the status, and it is unrun. LoftQ's 2-bit recovery is also reported at rank 64, so some of it may be rank rather than initialization.
  • Eval-hygiene ripple (2026-07-16, sharpened 2026-07-18): this map's claims are benchmark deltas, and lora-vs-full-ft-illusion-of-equivalence now shows a second way that class of number misleads — not contamination this time but construct mismatch, equal in-domain scores over materially different solutions. Both failure modes get graded through evaluation-contamination.
