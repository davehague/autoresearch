PEFT methods (LoRA/QLoRA vs full fine-tuning) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 during the seed ingest, directly at consolidating: two independent T1s from different groups (Microsoft 2021; UW 2023) converge on "low-rank adaptation preserves fine-tune quality," and the second extends it through a 4-bit base. Downgraded to contested 2026-07-18 (auto run): two further independent T1s (Databricks/Columbia empirically; MIT CSAIL mechanistically) show the equivalence does not hold outside task adaptation. Flagged for David's review.

Status: contested

What we believe now:

  • The headline is now a conditional, not a claim: LoRA matches full fine-tuning on task adaptation, and does not on domain acquisition. The 2021 result stands in its own regime (trainable params ↓ ~10,000×, GPU memory ↓ ~3× on GPT-3 175B, on-par quality across RoBERTa/DeBERTa/GPT-2/GPT-3, because measured task-adaptation updates are rank-deficient). But a controlled comparison on Llama-2 7B/13B over programming and mathematics finds LoRA in standard low-rank settings substantially below full FT on the target domain, with the gap widening the farther the domain sits from pretraining — code more than math, continued pretraining (~20B tokens) more than instruction tuning (~100K pairs). The stated mechanism: full FT learns perturbations of rank 10–100× greater than configured LoRA ranks. The vault previously carried only task-adaptation evidence and was over-generalizing it. [T1, contested by T1]
  • A low-rank update is four choices, not one dial — and only one of them has ever been varied in this map's central comparison (new 2026-07-27). The vault now holds a graded source for each. Rank — how much capacity. Initialization — where the adapter starts (PiSSA on the base's principal components, LoftQ on the quantization error; they disagree about which subspace and agree it is load-bearing). Parameterization — the divisor, provably wrong at alpha/r and increasingly so with rank. Placement — which weight matrices get the budget at all, which is the new one and which turns out to be worth more than the others individually. Pinning the trainable budget at 0.28M on a 183M encoder and varying only where it goes: the second FFN matrix scores 89.99 on MNLI and the query projection 88.58; layers 10–12 score 88.6 and layers 1–3 77.87. A 10.7-point spread at identical budget, with no rank change anywhere. Two consequences. The configurations in common use are not neutral defaults — the original paper's W_q/W_v targeting is the worst pair in that sweep, and "apply adapters to every linear module," which current tooling defaults to, avoids the decision rather than making it. And automatic allocation recovers about a tenth of what placement is worth: an importance-scored, budget-scheduled allocator gains ~1 GLUE point over uniform LoRA at matched parameters and +1.2 SQuADv2.0 F1 at 0.08% of full-fine-tuning parameters (above full fine-tuning outright at that budget), which is real and is not 10.7. The design detail that generalizes past this method: when an allocator runs on a noisy signal — first-order sensitivity estimated on a sampled minibatch — make its decisions reversible. Masking a singular value keeps its vectors trainable so a misjudged component can revive; deleting an adapter row-column pair is permanent, and the controlled comparison favours masking in every cell at every budget. Scope it hard: encoder-scale, task-adaptation only, no decoder-only model, no quantized base, no cost accounting. [T1]
  • The contest has a confound underneath it, and it is in the denominator (new 2026-07-26). The natural response to "full FT learns perturbations of rank 10–100× greater" is use a higher rank, and the standing answer — from LoRA's own Table 6, where rank 64 was no better than rank 4 — was measured through a scaling factor that provably suppresses high-rank adapters. A LoRA submodule computes (W + gamma_r·BA)x with gamma_r = alpha/r; the rsLoRA theorem shows the adapter preserves both forward moments and backward gradients at Theta_r(1) if and only if gamma_r is in Theta_r(1/sqrt(r)). Dividing by r is overly aggressive — it damps the adapter harder the more capacity you give it, so the symptom is not divergence but "extra rank does nothing." Measured on Llama 2 7B over ranks 4→2048: LoRA's gradient norms fall through two orders of magnitude while its loss curves collapse onto one line; under alpha/sqrt(r) the norms stay in one band and each higher rank ends lower. It is not a disguised learning-rate change — a two-decade lr sweep for rank-4 LoRA bottoms out at 1.863 perplexity and rsLoRA at rank 2048 reaches 1.836 on the default lr — and it is not an initialization effect, since rank-correcting the init instead is unstable at high rank and reproduces LoRA exactly when combined with 1/r. This does not resolve the contest; it confounds it. Every LoRA-vs-full-FT comparison in this map ran under alpha/r, so the map cannot currently say whether the rank ceiling it has been reasoning about is a property of low-rank adaptation or of a denominator. Hold the mechanism as strong and the performance claim as suggestive: the paper's entire empirical case is fine-tuning perplexity with no downstream task benchmark anywhere, which is valid evidence for training health and is precisely the proxy this vault has watched fail three times for model selection. [T1, single author, no venue]
  • The capacity ceiling and the forgetting brake are one property. LoRA preserves base-model performance on out-of-domain tasks better than full FT, mitigates forgetting more effectively than weight decay or dropout, and keeps generations more diverse — the authors read it as a regularizer. The method choice is therefore an explicit trade with a measured direction: how much target-domain capability you buy with how much general capability. [T1]
  • Equal benchmark scores concealed structurally different solutions. SVD of fine-tuned weights shows LoRA inserts new high-ranking singular vectors approximately orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum — intruder dimensions — which full FT does not produce. Scaling them down post-hoc recovers most pre-training-distribution performance at minimal downstream cost, so they cause the forgetting rather than accompany it. Consequences: LoRA generalizes worse out of distribution at matched in-domain accuracy, and intruder dimensions accumulate across sequential fine-tunes, degrading the adapter-per-task workflow. [T1]
  • The base still doesn't need to be 16-bit. QLoRA backprops through a frozen 4-bit base (NF4 + double quantization + paged optimizers) into LoRA adapters and preserves 16-bit fine-tune performance on the tasks tested — 65B on a single 48GB GPU, Guanaco at 99.3% of ChatGPT's level (LLM-judged) after 24h on one card. Untouched by this run's push-backs, which are about rank, not precision. [T1]
  • Adapters still add no inference tax: ΔW merges into W at deploy time (zero added latency), or stays separate as small hot-swappable task artifacts — the merged form is what feeds the quantize-and-ship-locally pipeline. The intruder-dimension result adds a caveat to stacking those artifacts, not to merging one. [T1]
  • Part of LoRA's gap is a warm-start artifact, removable for free — but it doesn't touch the contest. LoRA starts its adapter as a no-op (A Gaussian, B zero) and must discover a useful subspace by descent. PiSSA keeps the identical architecture, param count, and mergeability but initializes A,B on the weight's principal singular components (SVD) and freezes the low-energy residual instead of the whole weight — recovering several points at zero added cost (Mistral-7B GSM8K 72.86% vs LoRA 67.7%, +5.16, consistent across 12 models 184M–70B, faster convergence). The quantized variant is the sharper result: QPiSSA beats QLoRA (LLaMA-3-70B GSM8K 86.05% vs 81.73%, +4.32) because pulling the high-energy components into a full-precision adapter leaves the residual with smaller spectral range, so the 4-bit error is smaller from step one. Critically, this is measured on task-adaptation benchmarks — the exact regime where LoRA already matched full FT — with no forgetting or new-domain test, so it raises the low-rank ceiling without resolving the domain-acquisition contest. Net: if you use LoRA, initialize with PiSSA (QPiSSA over QLoRA); it's free — but it's still rank-r and can't manufacture the high-rank update a new domain needs. [T1]
  • Adapter initialization is now a two-source, two-group finding — and it moves the bit-width floor, not just the accuracy. PiSSA and LoftQ solve the same objective from opposite ends. QPiSSA takes the SVD of W first, moves the principal components into the full-precision adapter, and quantizes the low-energy residual. LoftQ minimizes ‖W − Q − ABᵀ‖_F by alternating quantization and SVD, so the adapter is initialized on the quantization error itself. Two independent groups (Peking Univ.; Georgia Tech/Microsoft), months apart, converge on the load-bearing claim: the quantization error is not a fixed tax on the base — it depends on which slice of the weight you keep in full precision, and the adapter is that slice. Read that as replicated. The sharper consequence is LoftQ's sub-3-bit result: at 2.5-bit on LLaMA-2-13B, QLoRA reaches 19.39 WikiText-2 ppl and LoftQ 5.22; at 2.25- and 2-bit QLoRA does not converge at all while LoftQ reaches 7.69 ppl / 25.4% GSM8K. So "QLoRA fails below 3-bit" was evidence about QLoRA's zero-init, not about bit-widths — though LoftQ's own 2-bit numbers degrade sharply (7.85 vs 5.24 ppl at 4-bit), so sub-3-bit remains a real loss and the 4-bit default holds. What the two sources do not agree on is which slice belongs in the adapter (hardest to quantize, or actually mis-quantized — these coincide only if quantization error concentrates in the top singular directions, which neither establishes), and their gains sit on different bases, tasks, and scales, so +4.32 and +8.1 are not comparable numbers. [T1, two groups]
  • Practical shape, therefore, revised: 4-bit-base LoRA on one card remains the right opening move, and is now explicitly a bet that the target is a task, not a body of knowledge — initialized with PiSSA/QPiSSA, which is a strict improvement at the same budget. Ask what the fine-tune is for before choosing. Format/style/task-interface → LoRA, default holds. New domain knowledge → question the rank budget first; higher rank recovers part of the gap, full FT recovers the rest at a now-quantified forgetting cost — and a better init is not a substitute for the rank the domain actually needs. [T1, five groups]

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-07-27 — ingest of adalora-adaptive-budget-allocation (Zhang, Chen, Bukharin, Karampatziakis, He, Cheng, Chen, Zhao — Georgia Tech / Princeton / Microsoft Azure AI, ICLR 2023, T1): closes the AdaLoRA gap the index has named since the LoRA-variant zoo was assembled, and supplies the frame the zoo has been missing. A low-rank update is not one dial with a rank on it; it is four independent choices, and the vault now has a graded source for each — placement (this one), initialization (adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling, quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor), parameterization (the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank) and rank itself. Only the last has been varied in any in-vault comparison of adapters against full fine-tuning. The single measurement worth carrying even if the method is never used: with the trainable budget pinned at 0.28M on DeBERTaV3-base, MNLI reads 89.99 for adapters on the second FFN matrix against 88.58 for the query projection, and 88.6 for layers 10–12 against 77.87 for layers 1–3 — a 10.7-point spread from nothing but where an identical budget was spent. That is larger than most effects this map argues about, and it indicts the two defaults in current tooling: the original paper's W_q/W_v targeting picks the worst two cells here, and "adapt every linear submodule" avoids the choice rather than making it. Learned allocation — write the update as a pseudo-SVD PΛQ, score each singular triplet by a smoothed sensitivity-times-uncertainty signal, prune to a cubically decaying global budget, masking singular values rather than deleting adapter pairs so a misjudged component can revive — recovers about a GLUE point at matched budget (89.31 vs LoRA r=8's 88.34) and +1.2 SQuADv2.0 F1 at 0.08% of full-fine-tuning parameters, where it also exceeds full fine-tuning outright. Note the honest gap between 10.7 points available and ~1 point recovered: the sensitivity signal finds some of the structure, not most of it. Map holds contested — this does not touch the domain-acquisition axis at all. The reason it doesn't is the ingest's most consequential caveat: there is no decoder-only model anywhere in it. A 183M encoder and an encoder-decoder, on GLUE, SQuAD and XSum — task adaptation only, no instruction tuning, no new knowledge, no quantized base, and no wall-clock or memory accounting for a method that computes per-entry sensitivity plus two moving averages every step. One interaction the vault records as its own inference, not either author's claim: AdaLoRA states outright that it inherits LoRA's alpha/r scaling, and its mechanism is per-matrix, time-varying rank reallocation — so under the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank every matrix the allocator promotes has its contribution damped by the promotion. rsLoRA's author names AdaLoRA as a conjectured beneficiary without testing it; this source supplies the reason the conjecture is stronger than it looks, and makes the reported gains a floor rather than an estimate. Two edges out: a structural pairing with post-training-quantization that reads like a contradiction and is not (adapter budget pays in the FFN; precision pays on the shared path every token traverses — add capacity where the model specializes, preserve precision where error compounds), and a note to evaluation-contamination on a method whose cost side is entirely unreported. See where-the-adapter-budget-goes-matters-more-than-how-much-there-is, masking-singular-values-makes-rank-allocation-reversible.
  • 2026-07-26 — ingest of rslora-rank-stabilization-scaling (Kalajdzievski, Tenyx, arXiv 2023, T1): the first source to attack this map's contest from under it, by questioning the parameterization rather than the method. The rank ceiling the map has reasoned about since 2026-07-18 rests partly on the field's belief that raising LoRA's rank does not help — and that belief was measured under gamma_r = alpha/r, which the paper proves is the wrong exponent: adapters are rank-stabilized (forward moments and backward gradients both Theta_r(1)) iff gamma_r ∈ Theta_r(1/sqrt(r)). The empirics land on both sides of the theorem — gradient norms collapsing two orders of magnitude across ranks 4→2048 under LoRA and staying banded under rsLoRA, monotone gains with rank only in the corrected version, replicated on GPT-J 6B / GSM8k / Adafactor and under SGD, with 1/r^(1/4) unstable and 1/r^2 collapsing harder as predicted. Two controls matter: an lr sweep proves it is capacity rather than a step-size boost, and Appendix B.3 proves initialization cannot substitute — which closes a door on the map's own init thread, since adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling and quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor both locate the lever in where the adapter starts, and this shows parameterization is a different lever with a different failure mode. Status holds contested, and deliberately: this does not favour either side, it says the measurement instrument was miscalibrated for one of them. Also worth flagging as a rare clean case of the vault's own rule biting both ways — perplexity is the right evidence for the gradient-collapse claim (training health) and the wrong evidence for the better-model claim (model selection), and the paper offers only perplexity for both. Caveats: single author, industry lab, no venue, ~600 steps per run, no downstream benchmark of any kind, nothing over a quantized base, and rank 2048 is far outside practice while the 8–64 range people actually use is not separately reported. See the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank.
  • 2026-07-26 — interaction edge from sublinear-memory-gradient-checkpointing (T1, primary map distributed-training): the axis this map's readers cannot avoid finally has a primary source and a price. The cross-cutting argument has been that PEFT deletes most of the memory ceiling — sharding, tensor parallelism and CPU offload all attack terms proportional to trainable parameters, and LoRA on a 4-bit base has almost none — but activations do not scale that way, so recomputation is the one technique PEFT cannot render moot (rematerialization-trades-compute-for-the-activation-memory-nothing-else-shrinks). The price is now graded: O(n/k) + O(k) memory, one extra forward pass at k = sqrt(n), ~30% runtime, and — the part that matters for anyone comparing fine-tunes — the gradient is unchanged, so unlike sharded optimizer math it never enters the experimental configuration. See activation-memory-is-a-tunable-continuum-not-a-fixed-cost, recomputation-is-the-one-memory-technique-that-is-exact.
  • 2026-07-25 — mechanism edge from lima-less-is-more-for-alignment (T1, primary map data-curation): this map's central contest gets its missing "why", from the data side and without a single adapter in sight. Since 2026-07-18 the map has held a conditional it could state but not explain — low-rank adaptation matches full fine-tuning on task adaptation and underperforms on domain acquisition (low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new) — and a revised default that reads as a wager: 4-bit LoRA is the right opening move and an explicit bet that your target is a task, not a body of knowledge. The Superficial Alignment Hypothesis is that wager restated as a claim about where things live: capabilities and knowledge are acquired almost entirely in pretraining, and alignment only selects which subdistribution of response formats to use. If that is right, then a low-rank update is exactly the right shape of intervention for a selection problem, and no amount of adapter rank substitutes for pretraining when the target genuinely needs new knowledge. The evidence is that 1,000 curated examples on a 65B base — no RLHF, no reward model — reach 43% equal-or-better against GPT-4 and beat the same base trained on 52× more distilled data. Two findings, one about parameters and one about data, arriving from opposite directions at the same boundary, neither citing the other. That yields a testable prediction this map should carry: the targets where LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning ought to be the same targets where curated instruction data underperforms continued pretraining — and if that correlation holds, it is the a-priori test for "does this target need a high-rank update" that the index has called the sharpest open item across the vault. Nobody has checked, and checking is cheap. Bound: LIMA runs full fine-tuning, so this is an argument about the shape of the problem rather than a PEFT measurement; its baselines are 2023-vintage and retired. See alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition.
  • 2026-07-24 — interaction edge from gpipe-micro-batch-pipeline-parallelism (T1, primary map distributed-training): there is one memory term LoRA does not shrink, and nobody in-vault has priced it. The vault's memory-wall comparison lists four axes — shard it, split the layer, demote it to CPU, shrink what you train — and has flagged that PEFT may render offload moot, since LoRA on a 4-bit base has almost no fp32 optimizer state left to demote. That argument generalizes: parameters, gradients and optimizer states all scale with the number of trainable parameters, so freezing 99.99% of the model shrinks all three at once. Activations do not. The forward activations of a 70B model are a function of batch size, sequence length and depth — identical whether you are updating every weight or none of them. So re-materialization / gradient checkpointing is the one memory technique in this vault that PEFT cannot make redundant, and by elimination the one most likely to bind in the LoRA-on-one-card regime this map's premise assumes. GPipe's isolated measurement of the lever — 3.9× more parameters on one unchanged accelerator on a 2018 8GB card, before any distribution — shows the size of the effect, though not at this map's scale or precision. This is the vault's inference, not the paper's, and it names the cheapest unrun experiment shared between the two maps: peak memory for a 4-bit LoRA fine-tune with and without gradient checkpointing at a realistic sequence length — which matters more now that context-length-is-a-cheap-post-hoc-edit-not-a-pretraining-property makes long sequences cheap to acquire and expensive to train through. See rematerialization-trades-compute-for-the-activation-memory-nothing-else-shrinks.
  • 2026-07-22 — ingest of loftq-quantization-aware-lora-init (Li, Yu, Liang et al., Georgia Tech + Microsoft Azure, ICLR 2024, T1): the second LoRA-init source and the one that turns a single result into a replicated finding — quantize and initialize jointly (alternate quantization with SVD of the residual) rather than quantizing then zero-initializing. Shipped in Hugging Face PEFT as init_lora_weights='loftq', one-time per checkpoint, reusable across tasks, with memory and mergeability unchanged. Partly serves the map's "rsLoRA/AdaLoRA/LoftQ/OLoRA unsourced" item, and sharpens the most-wanted one: the missing source is no longer "a head-to-head of the init family" in the abstract but specifically PiSSA vs LoftQ on one base, one rank, one bit-width — each paper is conspicuously silent about the other. Ripple to post-training-quantization: part of the apparent sub-3-bit cliff was an artifact of the fine-tuning method, not the weights. Also a small echo of lora-regularizes-more-than-it-adapts — 4-bit LoftQ beats 16-bit LoRA on 13B GSM8K (45.0 vs 43.1), and the authors test the regularization explanation themselves by adding weight decay to the 16-bit baseline, which closes most of the gap (45.3). See quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor.
  • 2026-07-22 — interaction edge from sparse-upcycling-moe-from-dense-checkpoints (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts): a third move on the same axis as this map's, priced in a different currency. Where PEFT shrinks what you train to fit a budget, upcycling grows the base model's capacity by continued training from a dense checkpoint (~50% of dense pretraining cost as extra compute) — orders of magnitude past LoRA-on-one-card, but far short of pretraining. Now the sharpest untested cell across both maps: upcycle a dense base into an MoE, then LoRA the dense skeleton (sparse-models-overfit-when-finetuned) — nobody has tested upcycling with adapters instead of full updates, or at fine-tuning-scale extra compute. See dense-checkpoints-can-be-upcycled-into-moe.
  • 2026-07-21 — interaction edge from st-moe-stable-transferable (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts, coined this run): a parameter-subset fine-tuning result from the architecture side. On a sparse expert model, updating only the non-expert dense skeleton matches updating all parameters (≈86 SuperGLUE either way), while updating only the experts — ~80% of the model — drops ~3.5 points. An 80%-frozen model matching a full update rhymes with this map's thesis (most of the useful update lives in a small, well-chosen subset), but note what it is not: full-rank updates on the dense subset, task fine-tuning only, no adapters tested. How LoRA composes with an MoE base — which subset to adapt, whether the rank findings transfer, what routing does under fine-tuning — is now a named open cell in both maps.   • 2026-07-21 — ingest of pissa-principal-singular-adaptation (Meng, Wang & Zhang, Peking Univ., NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight, T1): the first LoRA-init variant in-vault, and a partial push-back on the pessimism the map went contested over — a chunk of LoRA's shortfall is an initialization artifact, not a rank ceiling. Open questions:

  • The contest's central comparison may need re-running under a corrected scaling factor (new 2026-07-26). This is now the cheapest experiment that could move this map's status, and it is cheaper than any other item here. Both push-back sources — low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new and intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting — configure LoRA under gamma_r = alpha/r, which the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank shows damps adapters harder as rank rises. So the two load-bearing questions are: does the domain-acquisition gap close, narrow, or hold when LoRA is run at high rank under alpha/sqrt(r); and do intruder dimensions appear at all in a rank-stabilized high-rank adapter, or are they a signature of an under-driven low-rank one? Nobody has checked either, use_rslora ships in Hugging Face PEFT, and both are a rank sweep on one base model. Until they are run, the map's contested status should be read as "unresolved and possibly mis-measured," not merely unresolved.
  • The four levers have never been varied together, and one pair is actively self-cancelling (new 2026-07-27). Placement, initialization, parameterization and rank each have a source; no source varies more than one. Two specific holes follow. First, the map's most-wanted comparison grows another dimension: it was PiSSA vs LoftQ vs plain init under alpha/r and alpha/sqrt(r) across a rank sweep, and placement is now a fifth axis on the same grid — nobody knows whether the initialization methods' differences survive being pointed at the right matrices. Second, and cheaper: AdaLoRA reallocates rank per matrix while inheriting alpha/r, which damps a matrix exactly in proportion to the capacity the allocator just gave it. That is this vault's inference rather than either paper's claim, and it is the single place in the PEFT literature where the scaling bug should bite hardest — which makes "AdaLoRA under alpha/sqrt(r)" the most informative cheap run available, since both pieces already ship in Hugging Face PEFT. rsLoRA's author conjectures the improvement and tests nothing.
  • Every placement result in-vault is on a 183M encoder (new 2026-07-27). The 10.7-point spread above says FFN matrices and upper layers are where adapter budget pays — on DeBERTaV3-base, on GLUE. Whether that ordering survives a decoder-only 7B–70B, an instruction-tuning target, or a domain-acquisition target is untested, and it is exactly the regime where the answer would change what a practitioner types. Worth pairing with the observation from shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts that the precision-sensitivity ordering runs the other way — attention, embeddings and gates cost far more per parameter when degraded than FFN weights do. Both can hold: the shared path is where error compounds, the FFN is where specialization lives. If that pairing survives at scale it is a usable rule — precision on the shared path, adaptation on the specializing part — and nobody has tested it as one.
  • Where exactly is the task-adaptation / domain-acquisition boundary? The map now has both sides but no crisp diagnostic. What a practitioner needs is an a priori test — given a target dataset, predict whether the required update is low-rank — rather than discovering it from a disappointing eval. Unsourced.
  • Does DoRA's decomposition change the contested picture? DoRA recovers part of the LoRA→full-FT gap on commonsense reasoning (weight-decomposition-recovers-lora-gap), but was never tested on the distant-domain / continued-pretraining regime where the gap is now known to be largest, nor checked for intruder dimensions. Two unanswered questions that would move this map's status.
  • Does the push-back survive quantization and scale? Both contested T1s run at 7B/13B (and RoBERTa-scale for the spectral analysis) over full-precision bases. QPiSSA (2026-07-21) is a data point near the operating point — a 4-bit 70B base, fine-tuned and beating QLoRA — but on task benchmarks, so it says the low-rank ceiling can be raised at 4-bit/70B, not whether the domain-acquisition gap or intruder dimensions persist there. The 65B-on-one-card × new-domain × 4-bit combination is still the map's most decision-relevant untested cell.
  • Is the spectral fix practical? Post-hoc scaling of intruder dimensions is demonstrated but productized nowhere; no library ships it, and nobody has checked it over merged or quantized adapters.
  • Rank/placement/hyperparameter practice has moved since 2021 (which modules, what rank) — and rank is now a first-order decision rather than a tuning detail. The variant zoo is four-sourced (revised 2026-07-26): DoRA (decomposition), PiSSA and LoftQ (init), and rsLoRA landed 2026-07-26 (parameterization). Still unsourced: AdaLoRA — which the rsLoRA author flags as inheriting LoRA's scaling factor and conjectures would improve when rebuilt on alpha/sqrt(r), untested — and OLoRA. The most-wanted missing source is now specific and one dimension larger than it was: not "a neutral head-to-head of the init family" but PiSSA vs LoftQ vs plain init, each under alpha/r and alpha/sqrt(r), on one base at one bit-width across a rank sweep. All three init papers are author-evaluated against vanilla LoRA/QLoRA only, they disagree about which subspace the adapter should hold, their reported gains sit on non-overlapping scales (LoftQ tops out at 13B, QPiSSA runs to 70B), none cites the others — and whether their differences grow or vanish in the high-rank regime rsLoRA unlocks is the part that would actually settle which lever matters.
  • Nobody has looked for intruder dimensions in an init-method spectrum. PiSSA seeds the adapter on the base's principal singular directions; LoftQ seeds it on the quantization-error subspace. Both are exactly the kind of intervention that would plant or prevent intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting — and neither paper runs the SVD analysis that would show it. Since the whole contest in this map turns on what LoRA does outside the target task, a spectral check on a LoftQ- or PiSSA-initialized fine-tune is the cheapest experiment that could move the status, and it is unrun. LoftQ's 2-bit recovery is also reported at rank 64, so some of it may be rank rather than initialization.
  • Eval-hygiene ripple (2026-07-16, sharpened 2026-07-18): this map's claims are benchmark deltas, and lora-vs-full-ft-illusion-of-equivalence now shows a second way that class of number misleads — not contamination this time but construct mismatch, equal in-domain scores over materially different solutions. Both failure modes get graded through evaluation-contamination.
