Local inference (formats, runtimes, on-device) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 during the seed ingest — first evidence in the "run it locally" half of the craft question. Format and kernel halves covered at seed; the runtime half started filling the same day from the GPU-serving side (vLLM); llama.cpp/Ollama as systems remained the open gap until 2026-07-24.

Status: consolidating (emerging → consolidating 2026-07-24) Status note (2026-07-24): the condition this map set for itself has been met. The 2026-07-22 note said, of a map already holding five T1s, that the status "moves when someone measures the local stack, not when the vault adds another datacenter mechanism" — every mechanism in it had been measured on a datacenter GPU or a TPU. This run lands two independent local-stack measurements, both through llama.cpp, from unrelated groups, on three machines this map's readers actually own: a 13-format sweep of the K-quant ladder with task benchmarks, perplexity and prefill/decode throughput (llama-cpp-quantization-unified-evaluation), and an instrumented MoE-vs-dense comparison on an Apple M2 Pro and a Jetson Orin Nano, with energy per token (moe-on-consumer-and-edge-hardware). The map now has the runtime, the format ladder, and consumer/edge silicon — the three things its own status note named. Honest qualifiers on the move: both new sources are venue-less preprints (graded T1 on released methodology and data, and the quantization sweep is single-author), the quant ladder is one model on one CPU, and the edge study is one MoE model on two devices. What tipped this is coverage of the named half, not weight of evidence — the mechanism claims below remain better-sourced than the local measurements that now anchor them.

What we believe now:

  • The quant ladder is graded, the damage is task-shaped, and perplexity will not choose for you (new 2026-07-24). Measured across 13 llama.cpp formats on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: 8-, 6-, 5- and 4-bit K-quants all sit within about half an aggregate point of FP16 (Q4_K_M 69.15 vs 69.47, at 69.4% smaller). The only rung that moves is 3-bit, and it moves unevenly — at Q3_K_S, GSM8K falls 9.3 points and MMLU 4.2 while HellaSwag falls 0.6. Precision is load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and instruction compliance, near-free for single-step commonsense. Within 3-bit it is a slope rather than a cliff: Q3_K_L recovers most of Q3_K_S's loss for four points of file size. Selection rule with teeth: do not pick from the perplexity column — the four 5-bit formats span 0.03 PPL and 3.4 GSM8K points, so PPL and task quality rank the menu differently. Converting costs 27–43 s, so measuring beats guessing. [T1]
  • Quantization is two speed knobs pointing opposite ways (new 2026-07-24). Decode is bandwidth-bound and gets monotonically faster as the file shrinks (2.83 → 9.91 tok/s, F16 → Q3_K_S, 3.5×). Prefill is compute-bound and does not: throughput peaks mid-ladder (Q4_0 at 97.35 vs F16's 79.57) and then falls to 57.39 at Q3_K_S — 28% below full precision, because dequantizing packed weights costs more than the smaller read saves. So the right quant level depends on your prompt-to-generation ratio, not only your memory budget: long-prefill RAG and long-generation chat should not pick the same file. Every throughput figure in this map now needs a fourth stamp field — which phase was measured — alongside kernel, card, and precision. [T1]
  • On bandwidth-bound hardware, inference cost tracks total parameters, not active ones (new 2026-07-24). The MoE promise to a local user — 1.3B-active costs what 1.3B dense costs — does not survive contact with the devices it is aimed at. OLMoE-1B-7B (6.9B total) at Q4_K_M through llama.cpp is 10% slower than Llama-3.2-1B on an M2 Pro and 31% slower on a Jetson Orin Nano, where it also loses to Qwen2.5-1.5B — a model with more active params — at 2.1× the energy per token and 8.01 GiB peak RSS against an 8 GB ceiling. Node-level timing of the decode graph puts routing under 9% of MoE-block time, so this is not a kernel that will improve: to serve 1.3B of compute you must keep 6.9B parameters resident, and residency is what a bandwidth-bound device is short of. Sparsity buys FLOPs; FLOPs are not the constraint here. [T1]
  • The serving stack has three independent layers, and the two levers you have are anti-correlated (new 2026-07-25). Placement, depth, and admission are separate problems: PagedAttention manages where the KV cache lives, speculative decoding attacks serial depth for one stream, and iteration-level scheduling manages when requests enter and leave. The third is the origin of continuous batching and the observation is specific to generation — a request needs an unpredictable number of iterations, so a request-granularity batch stays frozen until its slowest member finishes, and early finishers cannot return while new arrivals cannot join. Rescheduling after every iteration removes both stalls: 36.9× throughput on GPT-3 175B at a matched 190 ms/token median latency vs FasterTransformer, with the engine alone (scheduler disabled) measured as slightly worse than the baseline — so the win is entirely scheduling, not kernels. Two things this map must carry with it. The numbers do not compose: 36.9× is against FasterTransformer while PagedAttention's 2–4× is against this, so quoting a stacked multiple chains baselines that do not chain. And the regimes are opposite: continuous batching's one clear loss is max_bs=1 and its weak case is low load — precisely single-user local serving — while speculative decoding is measured at batch size 1 and erodes under concurrency. Many users → batch continuously; one user → speculate. Neither paper says this, because each measured only its own regime. [T1]
  • Local distribution standardized on a self-describing single file. GGUF: typed key-value metadata (extensible without breaking changes), mmap loading, parseable without external libraries — built because its predecessors couldn't identify their own architecture. Its quant menu (Q4_0…Q8_0, K-quants Q2_K–Q8_K, IQ series, MXFP4) is the concrete precision-vs-quality dial local users actually turn. [T2]
  • 4-bit weights are an edge enabler, not just a VRAM discount. Generation is memory-bandwidth-bound, so 4× smaller weights are a throughput win — when kernels built for the packed format collect it: TinyChat shows >3× over HuggingFace FP16 and 70B-class models on mobile GPUs. Format and runtime are one decision, not two. [T1]
  • Practical shape of the pipeline, therefore: fine-tune → merge the adapter → convert to GGUF at a chosen quant level → run on llama.cpp/Ollama — one conversion command, because the format is self-describing. [T2]
  • Serving throughput is a memory-management problem. The KV cache — growing with context and concurrency, not model size alone — is what fills the card once weights are quantized; paging it like OS virtual memory (PagedAttention) reaches near-zero cache waste and 2–4× throughput at equal latency vs prior systems. The deploy fork is concrete: vLLM for concurrent GPU serving (mostly not GGUF), llama.cpp/Ollama for single-user on-device — runtime choice and quant-format choice are one decision, now confirmed from both sides. [T1]
  • One lever in this map costs no quality at all, and it is the only one. Every other technique here — 4-bit weights, 2-bit KV, the GGUF quant ladder — buys speed by spending accuracy, and pricing that loss is this map's job. Speculative decoding is the exception: a small draft model proposes γ tokens, the target scores all γ+1 in one parallel pass, and a modified rejection rule (accept with min(1, p/q), resample the first rejection from norm(max(0, p−q))) makes the output provably identical in distribution to ordinary decoding. It spends arithmetic and a second model's memory instead — which is affordable precisely because decode is bandwidth-bound and the ALUs are idle. 2.8–3.4× at batch size 1 on T5-XXL (TPU-v4). The counter-intuitive tuning rule: the best draft is a deliberately weak one — T5-large agrees with the target most often (α=0.82) and is the slowest option (1.7×) because its cost eats the winnings, while T5-small (α=0.75) gives 3.4×. Use this as the map's yardstick: ask any claimed speedup what currency it spends, and note that this one spends the cheapest. [T1]

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-07-25 — ingest of orca-iteration-level-scheduling (Yu, Jeong, Kim, Kim, Chun, SNU / FriendliAI, OSDI 2022, T1): closes half the "prefix caching and continuous-batching variants" gap this map opened at the vLLM ingest and re-flagged on 2026-07-22 — and it turns out to be the origin of continuous batching rather than a variant of it. The map now holds three distinct layers of the serving stack and should stop treating "vLLM" as the name of one idea. Three results outlast the paper. (a) The win is scheduling, provably: with the scheduler disabled the engine is slightly worse than FasterTransformer, so none of the 36.9× is kernels. (b) The relationship to paged-kv-cache-unlocks-throughput is precursor-plus-tension, not duplication — Orca reserves max_tokens KV slots per admitted request to avoid deadlock, which is exactly the over-reservation PagedAttention was written to eliminate. Continuous batching created the fragmentation problem that paging then solved, and this map can finally state that sequence properly, along with the warning that Orca's 36.9× (vs FasterTransformer) and vLLM's 2–4× (vs Orca) do not multiply. (c) The operating-regime split with speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution is the most decision- relevant thing here for this vault's premise, and it is unflattering: Orca's entire win is multi-tenant, its clear loss is max_bs=1, and its weak case is low load — which together describe single-user local serving exactly. Also ripples to transformer-architecture (selective batching rests on attention being the only stateful, parameter-free operator in the block) and distributed-training (pipeline utilization with no micro-batching, so GPipe's bubble partly dissolves at inference for a scheduling reason). Caveats: synthetic Poisson trace with uniform lengths and EOS suppressed, so request duration is a sampled constant rather than content-dependent; one vendor-configured baseline; the 36.9× is one point on a latency–throughput curve; 2022 A100s at 13B–341B with no quantization anywhere. See scheduling-at-iteration-granularity-unblocks-generative-serving, attention-is-the-one-op-that-cannot-be-batched.
  • 2026-07-25 — advisory edge from alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts): the MoE residency problem this map measured on 2026-07-24 now has a compression answer, and it partly softens the edge verdict. If every expert must be resident and most are idle per token, they need not all cost the same bits — Qwen1.5-MoE at an average 3.5 bits matches its BF16 accuracy at over 4× weight-memory compression, with the gain over uniform precision largest on fine-grained MoEs (13.2 points at 2.5 bits vs Mixtral's 2.9). Since moe-inference-cost-tracks-total-not-active-params-on-edge traced the edge deficit to residency, and residency is exactly what uneven bit allocation attacks, this is the most promising un-run local experiment on this map: an unevenly-quantized MoE on the M2 Pro / Jetson setup. Two reasons not to over-read it — the source reports no latency, throughput or energy at all, so it addresses footprint and not the bandwidth bound that actually caused the deficit; and llama.cpp's K-quant menu does not currently express per-layer mixed precision of this kind. See moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity.
  • 2026-07-24 — ingest of llama-cpp-quantization-unified-evaluation (Uygar Kurt, arXiv preprint, T1): the map's founding gap closes and the status moves. Thirteen llama.cpp formats, one model, five task benchmarks plus perplexity, throughput and conversion time — the first time this vault can say which GGUF quant level loses what instead of repeating folklore. Three results outlast the table. (a) The 4-bit default translates: K-quants are a different construction from AWQ/GPTQ/QLoRA, and nobody in-vault had checked they land in the same place — they do, which extends post-training-quantization's headline into the format people download. (b) The damage is task-shaped, so a quant level that is free for chat costs a tenth of a math benchmark, and this map's advice must be conditional on workload. (c) The prefill/decode inversion is attention-is-memory-bound-not-compute-bound made visible on one machine — the same format change that triples decode throughput cuts prefill by 28%, which no in-vault source had said. Also lands a fifth construct-mismatch instance in evaluation-contamination: perplexity does not rank the formats. Provenance is the weak point — single-author, no venue, one model, CPU-only — and the crossover point in (c) is a property of that machine's arithmetic-to-bandwidth ratio, not a law. See the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff, quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill, perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats.
  • 2026-07-24 — ingest of moe-on-consumer-and-edge-hardware (Alfarizy et al., UNB / NRC Canada, arXiv preprint v3, T1; primary map mixture-of-experts): the second local-stack measurement this run, and the one that supplies what this map most lacked — an Apple-silicon number, a Jetson number, and energy per token. It closes the MoE × local inference cell opened at the mixture-of-experts coining and carried in the index since 2026-07-21: what llama.cpp does with routed FFNs on consumer hardware is now served, and the answer is that it runs them correctly and slower than the active-parameter count promises. The durable half is the instrumented one — routing under 9% of MoE-block time means the deficit is structural rather than a kernel awaiting optimization, which is the distinction this map's expiry rule (kernel-performance-expires-with-the-gpu-generation) exists to draw. It is also the clearest in-vault statement of where the memory-wall answers stop composing: sparsity is an answer denominated in FLOPs, and a bandwidth-bound device is not short of FLOPs. Caveat that the configuration measures naive full-residency serving — -ngl 99, no expert offloading — so the offload/caching literature it does not test is the open question it leaves. See moe-inference-cost-tracks-total-not-active-params-on-edge, routing-is-not-what-makes-moe-slow-on-device.
  • 2026-07-22 — ingest of speculative-decoding-fast-inference (Leviathan, Kalman & Matias, Google Research, ICML 2023 Oral, T1): closes the "the serving field's later moves — speculative decoding/prefix caching/continuous batching — are not in-vault" gap this map opened at the vLLM ingest. Two properties make it unusual here. It is the only quality-free lever in the map (exact distribution, by construction — there is no eval to discount and no knob to tune), and it is measured at batch size 1, the single-user latency regime that is this map's actual objective and the one place paged-kv-cache-unlocks-throughput's batched wins do not apply. The two are complementary: paging manages KV placement under concurrency, speculation attacks serial depth for one stream. It also takes attention-is-memory-bound-not-compute-bound constructively — the other sources in that thread treat memory-boundness as a constraint to route around; this one treats it as a budget to spend. Ripple to distillation-small-models: a distilled student has a second use where its quality gap is priced automatically — agree with your teacher 75% of the time and you are a 3× accelerator for the teacher, with disagreement costing latency rather than correctness. Did not tip this map's status; see the status note below. See speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution.
  • 2026-07-22 — edge-hardware edge from qwen25-vl-technical-report (T2, primary map vision-language-models): the first in-vault model sized for the edge half of the multi-modal group — Qwen2.5-VL-3B at DocVQA 93.9 / MMMU 53.1, open weights. Its encoder uses window attention in all but 4 layers to make vision cost linear rather than quadratic in patch count, which is the same play as GQA (sharing-kv-heads-buys-decode-speed-at-near-zero-quality-cost): buy back a cost curve with an architectural restriction rather than a kernel. Still unanswered here — what the GGUF/llama.cpp path does with a vision tower, and whether native-dynamic-resolution token counts (which vary per image) interact badly with a paged or preallocated KV cache.   • 2026-07-21 — interaction edge from the new mixture-of-experts: MoE inverts this map's budget — compute like the active count, RAM like the total count.   • 2026-07-20 — advisory edge from flashattention-3-asynchrony-low-precision (T1, primary map transformer-architecture): the expiry warning added here 2026-07-19 is now a measurement, and the stamp grows a third field.   • 2026-07-20 — interaction edge from yarn-context-window-extension (T1, primary map context-extension): the cheapest capability upgrade in the vault is also the one that breaks this map's memory budget. This map holds three levers on that budget (GQA architecture, KV precision, paging); a long window is what makes all three bind at once, and no in-vault source measures extension together with any of them.   • 2026-07-19 — advisory edge from flashattention-2-work-partitioning (T1, primary map transformer-architecture): an expiry date on every throughput number in this map. Open questions:

  • llama.cpp is no longer unsourced, but it is sourced as a measuring instrument, not as a system (revised 2026-07-24). Two sources now run it — across the K-quant ladder on CPU, and across Metal and CUDA backends on edge silicon — so this map finally has local numbers. What neither describes is llama.cpp's design: the ggml graph, the CPU/Metal kernel split, threading and batching behaviour, mmap and memory-mapping strategy, or Ollama's serving layer above it. The gap has narrowed from "no local evidence" to "no local architecture source," and the second is the harder one to close from papers, since the primary documentation is the repository.
  • Continuous batching landed 2026-07-25; prefix caching did not (orca-iteration-level-scheduling). What replaces the closed half is narrower and points at this map's actual target: every continuous-batching number in-vault is datacenter, multi-tenant, unquantized, and the technique's own evidence shows it degenerating at max_bs=1. Whether iteration-level scheduling buys anything for a single-user llama.cpp/Ollama session — where the answer is plausibly "nothing, and the scheduler is overhead" — is unmeasured, and it is the form of the question this map cares about. Still absent entirely: prefix caching, chunked prefill (which would interact directly with the prefill/decode inversion measured 2026-07-24), and disaggregated prefill/decode serving.
  • The quant ladder is measured on one axis only (new 2026-07-24). Thirteen formats, but one model, one size, one CPU. Three extrapolations the map is now tempted to make and cannot: whether the 3-bit task-shaped damage holds at 70B (larger models are widely believed to tolerate aggressive quantization better, and nothing in-vault tests it), whether it holds on an MoE — where per-expert calibration coverage is already flagged as unsourced — and where the prefill/decode crossover sits on Apple silicon or a consumer GPU, whose arithmetic-to-bandwidth ratios differ sharply from a 96-core Xeon's. The IQ series and MXFP4 rungs of the menu remain unmeasured entirely.
  • Nobody has measured what quantization does to the KV cache in the local stack (sharpened 2026-07-24). The new ladder evidence is weight-only, at short context. This map holds that the KV budget is a product of three levers (architecture, precision, placement) and that a long window makes all three bind at once — and the one measurement it just gained deliberately avoids that regime. llama.cpp's own KV-quantization options are still ungraded here.
  • What an offload-aware runtime would recover on edge MoE (new 2026-07-24). The edge result measures full residency, and its own decomposition stops at "routing is not the problem" without separating weight traffic from expert-dispatch gather/scatter from KV pressure. Only the first is irreducible. Whether measured route locality (~28% consecutive-token expert reuse) is enough for an expert cache to close a 31% gap is the actionable unknown, and the offloading literature that attacks it is un-ingested.
  • What speculative decoding costs on the hardware this map is about, nobody has measured. Three specific unknowns, all pointing the same way: (a) batching erodes the win — concurrency already fills the arithmetic headroom speculation spends, and the crossover batch size is unreported; (b) the draft model competes for VRAM with the target's weights and KV on exactly the device where memory is the binding constraint, and no in-vault source prices that trade; (c) α is measured on full-precision pairs, so whether a quantized draft and a quantized target still agree at 0.6–0.8 — the only pairing a llama.cpp user would ever run — is unknown, and it is this map's sharpest missing datum now. A fourth, smaller constraint: draft and target must share a tokenizer.
  • KV-cache quantization — shrinking the entries PagedAttention places — now has a first source (KIVI, 2-bit KV; post-training-quantization). Unserved: whether llama.cpp/Ollama's local KV-quant options match it, and a measured paging+precision combined result on one runtime.
  • The KV budget is now known to be a product of three levers — architecture (GQA, fewer heads), precision (KV quantization, fewer bits), placement (paging, less waste) — and no in-vault source measures more than one at a time. Since the models people actually run are already GQA, the headline numbers from the other two levers are upper bounds on what they add. The combined measurement is this map's sharpest missing datum.
  • Which GGUF quant level loses what, measured — RESOLVED 2026-07-24 by llama-cpp-quantization-unified-evaluation for the K-quant/legacy 3–8-bit menu on one 8B model; the tutor can now teach the trade from graded evidence rather than folklore. What replaces it is narrower and listed above: the IQ/MXFP4 rungs, other scales, and MoE bases.
  • Every in-vault throughput number needs a hardware stamp (new 2026-07-19). The kernel evidence now shows tuning does not transfer across GPU generations, yet this map's figures (2–4× from paging, >3× from 4-bit kernels, 2.35–3.47× from 2-bit KV) come from different papers on different cards and are read here as if commensurable. They are not. A single-runtime, single-card measurement of the stacked levers would fix both this and the KV-budget gap above.
  • Edge constraints beyond the mobile-GPU demo (thermals, memory bandwidth on SBCs/NPUs, the compress→convert→deploy pipeline as Track E teaches it) — future sources.
  • Note for the tutor bridge: the atlas-tutor stack itself runs on Ollama Cloud — the learner's daily tooling is this map's subject matter.
