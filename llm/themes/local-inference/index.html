Local inference (formats, runtimes, on-device) — map

Coined 2026-07-16 during the seed ingest — first evidence in the "run it locally" half of the craft question. Format and kernel halves covered at seed; the runtime half started filling the same day from the GPU-serving side (vLLM); llama.cpp/Ollama as systems remained the open gap until 2026-07-24.

Status: consolidating (emerging → consolidating 2026-07-24) Status note (2026-07-24): the condition this map set for itself has been met. The 2026-07-22 note said, of a map already holding five T1s, that the status "moves when someone measures the local stack, not when the vault adds another datacenter mechanism" — every mechanism in it had been measured on a datacenter GPU or a TPU. This run lands two independent local-stack measurements, both through llama.cpp, from unrelated groups, on three machines this map's readers actually own: a 13-format sweep of the K-quant ladder with task benchmarks, perplexity and prefill/decode throughput (llama-cpp-quantization-unified-evaluation), and an instrumented MoE-vs-dense comparison on an Apple M2 Pro and a Jetson Orin Nano, with energy per token (moe-on-consumer-and-edge-hardware). The map now has the runtime, the format ladder, and consumer/edge silicon — the three things its own status note named. Honest qualifiers on the move: both new sources are venue-less preprints (graded T1 on released methodology and data, and the quantization sweep is single-author), the quant ladder is one model on one CPU, and the edge study is one MoE model on two devices. What tipped this is coverage of the named half, not weight of evidence — the mechanism claims below remain better-sourced than the local measurements that now anchor them.

What we believe now:

  • The quant ladder is graded, the damage is task-shaped, and perplexity will not choose for you (new 2026-07-24). Measured across 13 llama.cpp formats on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: 8-, 6-, 5- and 4-bit K-quants all sit within about half an aggregate point of FP16 (Q4_K_M 69.15 vs 69.47, at 69.4% smaller). The only rung that moves is 3-bit, and it moves unevenly — at Q3_K_S, GSM8K falls 9.3 points and MMLU 4.2 while HellaSwag falls 0.6. Precision is load-bearing for multi-step arithmetic and instruction compliance, near-free for single-step commonsense. Within 3-bit it is a slope rather than a cliff: Q3_K_L recovers most of Q3_K_S's loss for four points of file size. Selection rule with teeth: do not pick from the perplexity column — the four 5-bit formats span 0.03 PPL and 3.4 GSM8K points, so PPL and task quality rank the menu differently. Converting costs 27–43 s, so measuring beats guessing. [T1]
  • Quantization is two speed knobs pointing opposite ways (new 2026-07-24). Decode is bandwidth-bound and gets monotonically faster as the file shrinks (2.83 → 9.91 tok/s, F16 → Q3_K_S, 3.5×). Prefill is compute-bound and does not: throughput peaks mid-ladder (Q4_0 at 97.35 vs F16's 79.57) and then falls to 57.39 at Q3_K_S — 28% below full precision, because dequantizing packed weights costs more than the smaller read saves. So the right quant level depends on your prompt-to-generation ratio, not only your memory budget: long-prefill RAG and long-generation chat should not pick the same file. Every throughput figure in this map now needs a fourth stamp field — which phase was measured — alongside kernel, card, and precision. [T1]
  • ...unless you stop keeping them resident, and then sparsity is exactly what saves you (new 2026-07-26). The bullet below is measured with every weight in memory, which is the configuration that makes the active/total distinction invisible. Offload the experts to host RAM and the same property inverts: Mixtral-8×7B is 96.6% expert parameters and decode touches two of eight per layer, so an LRU expert cache (k=2 at 12 GB, k=4 at 16 GB) plus speculative expert prefetch — run layer L+1's gating function on layer L's hidden states, recall ~0.8–0.9 one layer ahead — sustains 2–3 tok/s at batch size 1 on a free-tier Colab T4, an RTX 3060 and an RTX 3080 Mobile, against 0.58–1.06 for naive layer-wise offloading. The 12 GB 3060 nearly matches the 16 GB 3080 Mobile because both are bottlenecked by host-to-device PCIe, not the GPU. So the practitioner's rule is conditional on fit, and the two halves are complements rather than rivals: if the model fits, prefer dense at matched active params; if it does not, an MoE is the easier thing to offload, because a dense model of the same total has no idle 75% to leave in RAM. Note the prefetch is a different kind of speculation from the map's quality-free lever below — it guesses which weights, not which tokens, so it needs no verification and a wrong guess costs only the load you already owed. Bounded: a venue-less two-author report, one model family, quality evidence perplexity and 5-shot MMLU only at ~2.6 bits per expert parameter, and 2–3 tok/s is readable rather than comfortable. [T1]
  • On bandwidth-bound hardware with everything resident, inference cost tracks total parameters, not active ones (new 2026-07-24; scoped 2026-07-26). The MoE promise to a local user — 1.3B-active costs what 1.3B dense costs — does not survive contact with the devices it is aimed at. OLMoE-1B-7B (6.9B total) at Q4_K_M through llama.cpp is 10% slower than Llama-3.2-1B on an M2 Pro and 31% slower on a Jetson Orin Nano, where it also loses to Qwen2.5-1.5B — a model with more active params — at 2.1× the energy per token and 8.01 GiB peak RSS against an 8 GB ceiling. Node-level timing of the decode graph puts routing under 9% of MoE-block time, so this is not a kernel that will improve: to serve 1.3B of compute you must keep 6.9B parameters resident, and residency is what a bandwidth-bound device is short of. Sparsity buys FLOPs; FLOPs are not the constraint here. [T1]
  • The prefill/decode split is a property of the computation, and the admission layer needed a second generation to respect it (new 2026-07-27). Two facts, and the second follows from the first. The workload has two opposite bottlenecks. Prefill runs the whole prompt through the linear layers at once, so the cost of hauling each weight matrix out of memory is amortized over thousands of tokens: it is compute-bound, and batching more requests buys nothing because one request already saturates the arithmetic units. Decode runs one token per request through the same matrices, so it is memory-bound, and batching helps almost linearly. The gap has a memorable size — one decode token costs about what 128 prefill tokens cost — and two consequences: prefill throughput saturates around 512 tokens, so a prompt is not an indivisible unit of work, and a decode batch has compute slack that prompt work can be poured into nearly free. This is the same partition the map already held from llama.cpp measurement on a CPU (decode speeds up down the quant ladder, prefill slows), now derived from arithmetic intensity on A100s under vLLM — two unrelated stacks, one structure. Therefore iteration-level scheduling had a second defect. If a request can join mid-flight, its prompt executes mid-flight, freezing every running decode for the duration — a generation stall measured at several seconds in vLLM. Chunked prefills (split the prompt across iterations) plus stall-free batching (fill each iteration with running decodes first, then top up to a token budget with prefill chunks) bound it: naive coalescing inflates an iteration up to 28.3× over decode-only, chunked it is 3.7×, and the penalty shrinks as batch and context grow. Worth 2.6–6.3× more sustainable load at a fixed P99 time-between-tokens target across 7B–180B. Two riders. The token budget is the knob, not the batch size — vLLM's capacity here is "largely identical" at max batch 32, 64 and 128, because under a latency target the scheduler stalls before the memory manager runs out, so paging enabled batches the admission policy could not use. And the chunk size is not a free parameter: 512 costs at most ~25% extra prefill work and 2048 nearly none, but tile quantization makes a chunk of 257 cost 32% more than 256, a one-token cliff. Scope this honestly — it is a multi-tenant technique, and with one user there are no incumbents to protect; what transfers to the local stack is the token-budget idea, which llama.cpp already exposes as a physical batch size, and the tile-divisibility warning. That transfer is unmeasured. [T1]
  • The serving stack has four layers, they stack in a dependency order, and only one of them pays for a single user (revised 2026-07-26). The fourth is reuse: every engine throws away a request's KV cache when it finishes, though the next request usually shares a prefix — a system prompt, a few-shot block, an image, a chat history. RadixAttention keeps them all in a radix tree with LRU eviction on leaves, reference-counts nodes against the running batch, holds no preallocated cache pool (cache and running requests share one pool, so a full cache is evicted for a bigger batch), and schedules longest-shared-prefix first — provably the optimal order, since it is a depth-first traversal, and measured at 96% of optimal hit rate. Worth up to 6.4× throughput and 3.7× lower latency vs vLLM v0.2.5 on Llama-7B, holding at 70B and on Mixtral under tensor parallelism. Two structural facts neither this nor any other paper here states. First the layers compose in one direction: RadixAttention needs the per-token page layout (paging), needs reference counting against a running batch (continuous batching), and its no-preallocation policy presumes iteration-level admission — each layer builds the substrate the next one needs. Second, and this is what corrects the rule below: reuse is driven by prefix overlap, not by concurrency, and the most reliable source of prefix overlap is one user having a conversation. A month of production service on Chatbot Arena, one worker per model, measured 74.1% hit rate on Vicuna-33B and 52.4% on LLaVA-NeXT-34B, cutting first-token latency 1.7×. So the map's rule gains a third clause — many users → batch continuously; one user, single-shot → speculate; one user, multi-turn → cache the prefix — and reuse is free when it does not apply (0.2 s of 74.3 s, under 0.3%, on a workload with no reuse to find). Its own null result is honest and matters: long outputs with no cross-session sharing show almost no speedup, because reuse cuts prefill and nothing else. [T1]
  • The serving stack's first three layers (new 2026-07-25). Placement, depth, and admission are separate problems: PagedAttention manages where the KV cache lives, speculative decoding attacks serial depth for one stream, and iteration-level scheduling manages when requests enter and leave. The third is the origin of continuous batching and the observation is specific to generation — a request needs an unpredictable number of iterations, so a request-granularity batch stays frozen until its slowest member finishes, and early finishers cannot return while new arrivals cannot join. Rescheduling after every iteration removes both stalls: 36.9× throughput on GPT-3 175B at a matched 190 ms/token median latency vs FasterTransformer, with the engine alone (scheduler disabled) measured as slightly worse than the baseline — so the win is entirely scheduling, not kernels. Two things this map must carry with it. The numbers do not compose: 36.9× is against FasterTransformer while PagedAttention's 2–4× is against this, so quoting a stacked multiple chains baselines that do not chain. And the regimes are opposite: continuous batching's one clear loss is max_bs=1 and its weak case is low load — precisely single-user local serving — while speculative decoding is measured at batch size 1 and erodes under concurrency. Many users → batch continuously; one user → speculate. Neither paper says this, because each measured only its own regime. (Superseded 2026-07-26 by the four-layer bullet above, which adds the third clause and the composition order.) [T1]
  • Local distribution standardized on a self-describing single file. GGUF: typed key-value metadata (extensible without breaking changes), mmap loading, parseable without external libraries — built because its predecessors couldn't identify their own architecture. Its quant menu (Q4_0…Q8_0, K-quants Q2_K–Q8_K, IQ series, MXFP4) is the concrete precision-vs-quality dial local users actually turn. [T2]
  • 4-bit weights are an edge enabler, not just a VRAM discount. Generation is memory-bandwidth-bound, so 4× smaller weights are a throughput win — when kernels built for the packed format collect it: TinyChat shows >3× over HuggingFace FP16 and 70B-class models on mobile GPUs. Format and runtime are one decision, not two. [T1]
  • Practical shape of the pipeline, therefore: fine-tune → merge the adapter → convert to GGUF at a chosen quant level → run on llama.cpp/Ollama — one conversion command, because the format is self-describing. [T2]
  • Serving throughput is a memory-management problem. The KV cache — growing with context and concurrency, not model size alone — is what fills the card once weights are quantized; paging it like OS virtual memory (PagedAttention) reaches near-zero cache waste and 2–4× throughput at equal latency vs prior systems. The deploy fork is concrete: vLLM for concurrent GPU serving (mostly not GGUF), llama.cpp/Ollama for single-user on-device — runtime choice and quant-format choice are one decision, now confirmed from both sides. [T1]
  • One lever in this map costs no quality at all, and it is the only one. Every other technique here — 4-bit weights, 2-bit KV, the GGUF quant ladder — buys speed by spending accuracy, and pricing that loss is this map's job. Speculative decoding is the exception: a small draft model proposes γ tokens, the target scores all γ+1 in one parallel pass, and a modified rejection rule (accept with min(1, p/q), resample the first rejection from norm(max(0, p−q))) makes the output provably identical in distribution to ordinary decoding. It spends arithmetic and a second model's memory instead — which is affordable precisely because decode is bandwidth-bound and the ALUs are idle. 2.8–3.4× at batch size 1 on T5-XXL (TPU-v4). The counter-intuitive tuning rule: the best draft is a deliberately weak one — T5-large agrees with the target most often (α=0.82) and is the slowest option (1.7×) because its cost eats the winnings, while T5-small (α=0.75) gives 3.4×. Use this as the map's yardstick: ask any claimed speedup what currency it spends, and note that this one spends the cheapest. [T1]

  • 2026-07-26 — ingest of sglang-radixattention-prefix-caching (Zheng, Yin, Xie, Sun et al., UC Berkeley / Stanford / SJTU / Texas A&M, arXiv 2023 v2, T1): closes the prefix caching half of the gap this map has carried since 2026-07-24, and completes the serving stack into four layers — placement, admission, serial depth, and now reuse. The technique is a radix tree over the KV cache with LRU eviction, reference counting against the running batch, and a cache-aware scheduler that runs longest-shared-prefix first (equivalent to a DFS traversal, provably optimal, measured at 96% of optimal). Up to 6.4× throughput / 3.7× latency vs vLLM v0.2.5 on Llama-7B across eleven workloads; hit rates 50–99%; multi-modal LLaVA-v1.5-7B 0.18 → 1.15 images/s by hashing the image and keying the tree on it. Two things this map should state that the paper does not. (a) The four layers stack in a dependency order — reuse needs paging's per-token pages and continuous batching's reference counts, and its no-preallocated-pool policy presumes iteration-level admission. That is friendlier than the 2026-07-25 finding that continuous batching created the fragmentation paging was written to fix, but it is the same kind of structural relation. (b) It breaks the map's own regime rule in a useful direction: reuse tracks prefix overlap, not concurrency, so it is the only one of the four that pays at batch size 1 without changing the arithmetic — and the evidence for that is production rather than benchmark, 74.1% hit rate and 1.7× faster first token on Vicuna-33B over a month on Chatbot Arena with one worker per model. It also marks the exactness boundary cleanly: identical-prefix reuse is bit-exact and default-on (under 0.3% overhead where no reuse exists), while non-prefix "modular" reuse is characterized as costing up to a 43% accuracy drop. Ripples to transformer-architecture (the KV cache as a shared data structure across requests, not per-request scratch) and vision-language-models (the vault's first source about serving a VLM at all). Caveats: the headline is against vLLM v0.2.5 and the paper's own footnote concedes RadixAttention was subsequently absorbed into vLLM, so 6.4× prices the technique rather than a current project gap; all hardware is datacenter (A10G, A100) with no CPU, Apple-silicon or llama.cpp measurement; ten of eleven workloads have prefix sharing by construction; cache-aware scheduling can starve requests, acknowledged and unquantified. See prefix-reuse-attacks-prefill-and-needs-no-concurrency, kv-reuse-must-be-exact-prefix-to-stay-lossless.

Ablation decomposes the T4 2-bit column — pinned contiguous buffers 0.661 → 1.168, LRU cache → 1.567, prefetch → 2.092 — so all three parts earn their place.   • 2026-07-25 — ingest of orca-iteration-level-scheduling (Yu, Jeong, Kim, Kim, Chun, SNU / FriendliAI, OSDI 2022, T1): closes half the "prefix caching and continuous-batching variants" gap this map opened at the vLLM ingest and re-flagged on 2026-07-22 — and it turns out to be the origin of continuous batching rather than a variant of it. The map now holds three distinct layers of the serving stack and should stop treating "vLLM" as the name of one idea. Three results outlast the paper. (a) The win is scheduling, provably: with the scheduler disabled the engine is slightly worse than FasterTransformer, so none of the 36.9× is kernels. (b) The relationship to paged-kv-cache-unlocks-throughput is precursor-plus-tension, not duplication — Orca reserves max_tokens KV slots per admitted request to avoid deadlock, which is exactly the over-reservation PagedAttention was written to eliminate. Continuous batching created the fragmentation problem that paging then solved, and this map can finally state that sequence properly, along with the warning that Orca's 36.9× (vs FasterTransformer) and vLLM's 2–4× (vs Orca) do not multiply. (c) The operating-regime split with speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution is the most decision- relevant thing here for this vault's premise, and it is unflattering: Orca's entire win is multi-tenant, its clear loss is max_bs=1, and its weak case is low load — which together describe single-user local serving exactly. Also ripples to transformer-architecture (selective batching rests on attention being the only stateful, parameter-free operator in the block) and distributed-training (pipeline utilization with no micro-batching, so GPipe's bubble partly dissolves at inference for a scheduling reason). Caveats: synthetic Poisson trace with uniform lengths and EOS suppressed, so request duration is a sampled constant rather than content-dependent; one vendor-configured baseline; the 36.9× is one point on a latency–throughput curve; 2022 A100s at 13B–341B with no quantization anywhere. See scheduling-at-iteration-granularity-unblocks-generative-serving, attention-is-the-one-op-that-cannot-be-batched.
  • 2026-07-25 — advisory edge from alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts): the MoE residency problem this map measured on 2026-07-24 now has a compression answer, and it partly softens the edge verdict. Open questions:

  • llama.cpp is no longer unsourced, but it is sourced as a measuring instrument, not as a system (revised 2026-07-24). Two sources now run it — across the K-quant ladder on CPU, and across Metal and CUDA backends on edge silicon — so this map finally has local numbers. What neither describes is llama.cpp's design: the ggml graph, the CPU/Metal kernel split, threading and batching behaviour, mmap and memory-mapping strategy, or Ollama's serving layer above it. The gap has narrowed from "no local evidence" to "no local architecture source," and the second is the harder one to close from papers, since the primary documentation is the repository.
  • Continuous batching landed 2026-07-25 and prefix caching 2026-07-26; the local half of both is still missing (revised 2026-07-26). Every serving number in-vault is datacenter, multi-tenant, unquantized — A100s, A10Gs, V100s — and continuous batching's own evidence shows it degenerating at max_bs=1. Prefix caching is the one layer whose mechanism predicts a single-user win, and the production Chatbot Arena hit rates (74.1% on Vicuna-33B) are the closest thing in-vault to evidence for it, but that is still one-worker datacenter serving rather than a laptop. The measurement this map wants is now very specific and nobody has published it: llama.cpp / Ollama, one user, a multi-turn conversation, with and without prompt-prefix reuse, at a GGUF quant level — because the two effects interact in opposite directions (reuse cuts prefill; quantization slows prefill and speeds decode, quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill), and nobody has measured them together. Still absent entirely: chunked prefill, disaggregated prefill/decode serving, and any measurement of KV-cache reuse across process restarts, which is the form a local user would actually want.
  • The quant ladder is measured on one axis only (new 2026-07-24). Thirteen formats, but one model, one size, one CPU. Three extrapolations the map is now tempted to make and cannot: whether the 3-bit task-shaped damage holds at 70B (larger models are widely believed to tolerate aggressive quantization better, and nothing in-vault tests it), whether it holds on an MoE — where per-expert calibration coverage is already flagged as unsourced — and where the prefill/decode crossover sits on Apple silicon or a consumer GPU, whose arithmetic-to-bandwidth ratios differ sharply from a 96-core Xeon's. The IQ series and MXFP4 rungs of the menu remain unmeasured entirely.
  • Nobody has measured what quantization does to the KV cache in the local stack (sharpened 2026-07-24). The new ladder evidence is weight-only, at short context. This map holds that the KV budget is a product of three levers (architecture, precision, placement) and that a long window makes all three bind at once — and the one measurement it just gained deliberately avoids that regime. llama.cpp's own KV-quantization options are still ungraded here.
  • What an offload-aware runtime would recover on edge MoE (new 2026-07-24). The edge result measures full residency, and its own decomposition stops at "routing is not the problem" without separating weight traffic from expert-dispatch gather/scatter from KV pressure. Only the first is irreducible. Whether measured route locality (~28% consecutive-token expert reuse) is enough for an expert cache to close a 31% gap is the actionable unknown, and the offloading literature that attacks it is un-ingested.
  • What speculative decoding costs on the hardware this map is about, nobody has measured. Three specific unknowns, all pointing the same way: (a) batching erodes the win — concurrency already fills the arithmetic headroom speculation spends, and the crossover batch size is unreported; (b) the draft model competes for VRAM with the target's weights and KV on exactly the device where memory is the binding constraint, and no in-vault source prices that trade; (c) α is measured on full-precision pairs, so whether a quantized draft and a quantized target still agree at 0.6–0.8 — the only pairing a llama.cpp user would ever run — is unknown, and it is this map's sharpest missing datum now. A fourth, smaller constraint: draft and target must share a tokenizer.
  • KV-cache quantization — shrinking the entries PagedAttention places — now has a first source (KIVI, 2-bit KV; post-training-quantization). Unserved: whether llama.cpp/Ollama's local KV-quant options match it, and a measured paging+precision combined result on one runtime.
  • The KV budget is now known to be a product of three levers — architecture (GQA, fewer heads), precision (KV quantization, fewer bits), placement (paging, less waste) — and no in-vault source measures more than one at a time. Since the models people actually run are already GQA, the headline numbers from the other two levers are upper bounds on what they add. The combined measurement is this map's sharpest missing datum.
  • Which GGUF quant level loses what, measured — RESOLVED 2026-07-24 by llama-cpp-quantization-unified-evaluation for the K-quant/legacy 3–8-bit menu on one 8B model; the tutor can now teach the trade from graded evidence rather than folklore. What replaces it is narrower and listed above: the IQ/MXFP4 rungs, other scales, and MoE bases.
  • Every in-vault throughput number needs a hardware stamp (new 2026-07-19). The kernel evidence now shows tuning does not transfer across GPU generations, yet this map's figures (2–4× from paging, >3× from 4-bit kernels, 2.35–3.47× from 2-bit KV) come from different papers on different cards and are read here as if commensurable. They are not. A single-runtime, single-card measurement of the stacked levers would fix both this and the KV-budget gap above.
  • Edge constraints beyond the mobile-GPU demo (thermals, memory bandwidth on SBCs/NPUs, the compress→convert→deploy pipeline as Track E teaches it) — future sources.
