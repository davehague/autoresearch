Context extension (making a trained model handle longer sequences) — map

Coined 2026-07-20 (auto run) at emerging — first evidence in the thread transformer-architecture has flagged since RoPE landed on 2026-07-19 as "the lever RoPE created and the vault has not sourced," and which vault/index.md has carried as a named open gap. Anchored on one strong T1 (YaRN) with released checkpoints and code. Auto-coined; flagged for David's review — the alternative was folding it into the architecture map, but this is a distinct operation: it modifies a released checkpoint after training, which makes it a fine-tuning-craft lever rather than a design decision, and the vault's premise is about exactly those levers.

Status: contested (emerging → contested 2026-07-28) Status note (2026-07-28): the measurement this map asked for three times has arrived, and it moves the map down rather than up. The 2026-07-25 note named the missing experiment exactly — "a needle-in-a-haystack or long-form reasoning eval on a YaRN-class cheaply-extended checkpoint." The nearest thing published is ruler-real-context-size-long-context (NVIDIA, COLM 2024, T1): seventeen models, thirteen synthetic tasks across retrieval-with-distractors, multi-hop tracing, aggregation and distractor-padded QA, at 4K–128K, 500 examples per cell. Two findings tip the status, and neither is a clean refutation, which is why this is contested and not fading. First, the three cheaply-extended small checkpoints in the study are its three worst models: LongChat-7B (linear RoPE interpolation plus a brief fine-tune — the Position Interpolation method YaRN's own paper reports beating) and LongAlpaca-13B (LongLoRA) both claim 32K, and both have effective lengths below 4K, meaning they never beat an unextended Llama-2-7B at any length tested. No YaRN checkpoint appears, so this is the family and not the method — and LongAlpaca scoring 60.6 at 4K shows the extension procedure also cost general capability, which confounds window handling with base quality. Second and sharper, LWM-7B — trained to a 1M window on the blockwise ring-attention substrate, i.e. the scaled-up successor of the very model whose ~0.9 line-retrieval figure at 500K is this map's only non-perplexity evidence — also lands at effective <4K. Both of this map's sources have now had their strongest evidence discounted by the same mechanism: line retrieval is a needle test, and RULER's whole thesis is that needle tests are passed by models that cannot do anything else at length. What survives is a conditional, and it is real: Qwen2-72B, trained at 32K and extended at inference time, reaches an effective 32K and sits among the top open models. Across the table what predicts effective length is model size and RoPE base frequency, not whether the window was trained in. So the honest restatement is that extending the window is cheap, and extending it on a small model and getting usable context out is undemonstrated — which is precisely the half of the claim this vault's premise depends on. Status note (2026-07-25, superseded above but retained): held at emerging deliberately. This run lands a second T1 from a different group (ring-attention-blockwise-near-infinite-context) and with it the map's first non-perplexity long-context evaluation — a LLaMA-13B fine-tuned to 512K holding ~0.9 line-retrieval accuracy out to 500K tokens, which is exactly the class of evidence flagged as absent at coining and escalated on 2026-07-24. Two reasons that is not enough to tip. First, the retrieval result is one figure, one model, no ablation, reported in a paper whose subject is systems throughput, with the authors noting they capped at 512K on budget grounds. Second and more importantly, it does not replicate this map's actual claim. The headline here is that extension is cheap — ~600 steps on <0.1% of pretraining data; Ring Attention's extension used 32 A100s. So the map gains a second source and a better kind of evidence while the "cheap" half still rests on one paper. What would tip it is a needle-in-a-haystack or long-form reasoning eval on a YaRN-class cheaply-extended checkpoint — the same measurement, on the budget this map is about. Status note (2026-07-30): the successor experiment named above has been run, and the map holds contested — with the belief now conditional on size rather than on evidence type. jet-long-dynamic-bifocal-rope (T1) scores RULER on Qwen3 1.7B / 4B / 8B with YaRN as a baseline, and the YaRN column reads 52.99 / 70.11 / 78.49 against unextended bases of 60.93 / 69.94 / 73.13 — i.e. −7.94 at 1.7B, +0.17 at 4B, +5.36 at 8B. At the smallest size, rescaling the frequencies is nearly eight points worse than doing nothing. That converts RULER's cross-model regression (size and RoPE base frequency predict effective length) into a controlled within-family ablation, from a group whose purpose was to beat YaRN rather than audit it. Two reasons it does not push the map to fading. The YaRN here is zero-shot — applied at inference with no fine-tuning — where the map's central claim is specifically about YaRN with ~600 fine-tuning steps; so what is established is that the free version of YaRN fails on small models, and whether 600 steps rescues a 1.7B model is still unmeasured. And the failure is family-specific, not size-specific: DNTK, DCA and Self-Extend all beat the base model at 1.7B, and only YaRN does not. The map's most-wanted source is therefore now smaller and sharper than it was — run YaRN's own fine-tuning recipe on Qwen3-1.7B and score it on RULER. Everything needed is public.

Sub-threads: none — one contested question (can a cheaply-extended small model deliver usable, not just low-perplexity, long context, or is usable length gated by scale?); every finding characterizes that single contest [reviewed 2026-08-01 @ 13 bullets]

What we believe now:

  • The training context window is a third axis, it has an interior optimum, and neither capacity nor positional encoding rescues it (new 2026-08-16). Every source here argues about the deployed window — how to stretch it, whether the stretch is usable, whether usable length is gated by scale. Sweep the pretraining window instead, with the token budget, update count, data, seed and optimizer all held fixed, and performance rises and then falls: SuperGLUE and closed-book multiple-choice knowledge peak around 2048 tokens, language-modeling loss bottoms around 8192, and past those points more training context makes the model worse at both. The two obvious escapes are closed — the same inverted U appears at all four model scales tested (20M–750M), and it appears under RoPE, ALiBi and LongRoPE alike, which matters because this map's entire extension literature is a positional-encoding argument. The mechanism offered is economic and worth carrying in the map's own terms: context and weights are alternative carriers of task information, so a longer window lowers the minimum the weights must hold, and the gradient trace agrees — the feed-forward-to-attention gradient-norm ratio falls as the window grows. The fine-tuning half is the actionable one and it is a causal result: holding the document budget fixed at eight and varying only how many are task-relevant, going from zero to eight relevant documents improves accuracy with supporting context while significantly reducing no-context accuracy and significantly increasing vulnerability to conflicting context, with the supporting-minus-conflicting gap opening from 22.1 to over 42 points across Qwen3 0.6B–14B — and tuning feed-forward modules alone versus attention alone reproduces each half of the trade on demand. What this does to the map's contest: the live question is whether a cheaply-extended small model can deliver usable long context or whether usable length is gated by scale, and this adds a possibility neither side priced — that usable length is partly set by the window the model was trained at, independent of both the extension method and the parameter count. The scope condition is the headline caveat: the controlled sweep runs 20M–750M, so "capacity does not rescue it" holds within a small band and not at 8B; the corroborating off-the-shelf comparison (Phi-3 128K under Phi-3 4K, OLMo 3 65K under OLMo 3 8K at both 7B and 32B) is confounded by post-training differences; the fine-tuning arm is LoRA rather than full training; and nothing here measures long-context task performance at all. Status holds contested — this widens the contest rather than settling it. [T1] the-pretraining-context-window-has-an-interior-optimum, task-relevant-training-context-trades-recall-for-context-dependence
  • Cheap extension is size-gated, and the gate sits between 4B and 8B — but the failure belongs to the frequency-rescaling family, not to small models (new 2026-07-30, and this is the sharpest thing this map has ever been able to say). On RULER across 13 tasks × 7 lengths, Qwen3 base checkpoints (32K native) evaluated to 128K: YaRN reads 52.99 / 70.11 / 78.49 at 1.7B / 4B / 8B against bases of 60.93 / 69.94 / 73.13. Below the gate, rescaling is worse than leaving the model alone. Read the same table by column, though, and the picture changes: DNTK (69.14 / 79.75 / 83.54), DCA (67.80 / 80.19 / 81.08) and Self-Extend (67.86 / 80.84 / 84.71) all beat the base model at every size, and a position-remapping scheme beats everything (73.93 / 83.02 / 86.74, tuning-free). So the map's problem was never that small models cannot be extended — it is that the one family this map had sourced is the fragile one. That distinction was previously undrawable here, and it reinforces the map's own suspicion about RoPE base frequency as the operative variable: rescaling manipulates that frequency directly and breaks; remapping leaves it alone and moves the indices instead (f(x) = ⌊x/G⌋, G = max(1, ⌈L/w_pretrained⌉) recomputed from the current length, so remote rotation angles always land inside the trained distribution and no per-model constant is fitted). Bounded: RULER is synthetic, and on the one natural long-context benchmark reported (HELMET-RAG) the methods sit within a point of each other and the remapping scheme loses by 0.73 at 1.7B — so the six-point synthetic gap does not reproduce on retrieval work. One model family, a 4× extension ratio, and no fine-tuned baseline of any kind. [T1]
  • The live contest, stated first because it conditions everything below (new 2026-07-28). A model's claimed context length and the length at which it still works are different numbers, and the gap is the norm: of seventeen long-context models that score near-perfectly on the standard needle-in-a-haystack test, only about half hold their performance at 32K, against claims of 32K to 1M. Measured against an operational bar — the longest length still beating an unextended Llama-2-7B's 85.6% at 4K, across thirteen tasks — Yi-34B claims 200K and holds 32K, Mixtral-8×22B claims 64K and holds 32K, DBRX claims 32K and holds 8K, and three cheaply-extended checkpoints hold nothing at all (LongChat-7B, LongAlpaca-13B and the 1M-trained LWM-7B all read effective <4K). The failure modes are specific and invisible to a needle test: ~40 points lost to distractor needles with the errors being near misses (values retrieved from the target's vicinity, so the range is matched coarsely and the key is not located); ~15 points lost by raising the number of queries from one to eight; over 80% of aggregation output at 128K copied verbatim from the single in-context example; and in QA, enough distracting paragraphs drive performance toward the model's no-context baseline — it stops reading. Two ablations name what actually buys effective length, and it is not the advertised window: training length helps non-monotonically (a 1M-trained checkpoint scores worse at 256K than the same model trained to 512K), while model size helps reliably — holding data and training length fixed, 34B beats 9B beats 6B. This does not refute the bullet below; it says the bullet's evidence type was never the right one, and that the two attempts to buy usable context cheaply on a small model both failed. [T1]
  • A model's context window is a post-hoc, cheap edit — not a property fixed at pretraining. Because RoPE stores position in a rotation rather than a learned table, the frequencies can be rescaled after the fact and briefly fine-tuned back into alignment. YaRN takes LLaMA 2 7B/13B to 128k context with ~600 fine-tuning steps on <0.1% of the original pretraining data — 10× fewer tokens and 2.5× fewer steps than prior extension methods. This is the most budget-asymmetric lever in the vault: a capability normally associated with a pretraining run, bought for a fine-tuning afternoon. [T1]
  • Position is a frequency bank whose dimensions do different jobs, and uniform scaling is what breaks. Position Interpolation squeezes every RoPE dimension by the same factor, destroying the high-frequency dimensions that carry local token order. YaRN's NTK-by-parts interpolation decides per dimension by how many rotations it completes over the training context (r = L/λ): long-wavelength dimensions (r < α=1) encode absolute position and are fully interpolated; short-wavelength ones (r > β=32) encode relative offset and are left alone; a ramp blends between. The generalizable rule: treat the position encoding as heterogeneous, or lose locality. [T1]
  • An extension method can generalize past its own fine-tuning length. YaRN's s=32 model was trained only at 64k yet evaluated cleanly at 128k (ppl 2.37 at 131,072 tokens for 7B; 2.24 for 13B), which suggests the fine-tune is teaching a scaling relationship rather than memorizing a length. A Dynamic-YaRN variant sets s = max(1, l'/L) at inference for >2× extension with no fine-tuning at all, degrading gracefully instead of falling off a cliff. [T1]
  • Attention entropy grows with sequence length, and the correction is a scalar. YaRN's attention temperature term (√(1/t) = 0.1·ln(s) + 1) is folded into the embedding scale rather than the attention code — a uniform perplexity improvement across the window for no architectural change and no extra parameters. [T1]

  • 2026-08-08 — edge in from kv-skill-external-factorized-operators (T1, primary map peft-methods): this map has priced the window and never priced what practitioners voluntarily put in it. Map holds contested; nothing here touches the effective-length question. Pasting an authored procedural skill into the system prompt inflates a prompt from 184 to 12,040 tokens on LongHealth (65×) and 150 to 5,848 on QuALITY (39×), at 671 tokens per query on LiveMath — re-encoded and re-attended on every request, occupying the same window the task needs. Set against this map's position that the window is cheap and the reading is not (claimed-context-length-is-not-effective-context-length, context-length-is-a-cheap-post-hoc-edit-not-a-pretraining-property), that is the bill from the other side, and it is now measurable against an alternative: the same knowledge carried as a positionless per-layer operator costs 1.0–1.8% of prefill and 6.5% of decode, with overhead flat from 128 to 8,192 tokens because the operator is not in the attention cache and does not grow with the context. The sharpest datum is that the tax is not always buying anything — on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B the text skill scores below the unskilled base (26.6 vs 31.2) while consuming its positions. Practitioner form: a system prompt is per-request compute, not configuration — count its tokens, multiply by request rate, and compare against moving it somewhere positionless. For a local single-user setup the arithmetic will often still favour the prompt, since the alternative needs a per-backbone interface trained first at a cost the paper never states.   • 2026-08-03 — two arrivals, and between them they say this map has been arguing inside a presupposition. Map holds contested; neither source resolves the small-model question, and one of them declines to have it. (a) kimi-k3-open-frontier-intelligence (T1, primary map mixture-of-experts) ships a production 1M-token window with No Position Encoding — no RoPE on any query or key — carrying position implicitly in the recurrent gating and channel-wise decay of a hybrid linear-attention stack, and states that the model "extrapolates directly to 1M-token contexts without any positional-encoding modification, such as RoPE rescaling or interpolation." This map's whole contest — rescale the frequency bank (YaRN, and the family Jet found fragile at 1.7B) against remap the indices — is a contest inside one architectural choice, and here is a frontier model that declines to make it. Three reasons it does not resolve anything. It is not the cheap operation this map is about: the window comes from a four-stage pretraining and cooldown curriculum (8K → 64K → 256K → 1M) on a 2.78T model, which is evidence about how to build long context, not how to buy it. There is no controlled comparison — no RoPE arm, no YaRN arm — and, pointedly, no RULER, so the one instrument that downgraded this map on 2026-07-28 is not run by the report that most challenges its framing. What it does land is this map's first claim about long-context training data, and it is transferable: "length alone does not confer long-range capability," so genuinely long documents are deduplicated (exact, fuzzy, and perceptual hashing over video frames), upsampled against short text, and synthetically augmented by permuting and concatenating documents so embedded tasks can only be solved by attending across the full window. That is a data-curation move applied to a capability rather than to a corpus. (b) comem-depth-division-unbounded-context (T1, primary map kv-cache-management) attacks the same demand from the cost side and supplies a caveat this map should adopt as a reading rule. Its headline — RULER 97.05 against a full-context reference's 78.80 at 128k — is measured on a Qwen3-8B that is not position-extended (native window 40,960), and the authors say outright that everything above that window is "an unextended stress reference, not a substitute for a length-extended model," explicitly declining to claim superiority over a YaRN-enabled full context. Read carefully, that is a paper refusing to make the comparison this map keeps asking for, and being right to. The rule to carry: a long-context result against an unextended baseline measures the baseline's window, not the method's reach — which is the same discipline evaluation-contamination applies to leakage, one level up.   • 2026-08-01 — KV-budget edge, and it is the third lever on the cost side of this map's benefit, from counter-causal-surprise-kv-eviction (T1, primary map local-inference): this map has carried since coining that "the interaction with quantization and KV budget is unmapped," and that extending the window is the move that makes the KV wall bind hardest. The vault now holds three levers on that budget — grouped-query attention, KV precision (a-data-free-rotation-puts-the-kv-cache-at-three-and-a-half-bits, 3.5 bits at parity) and now eviction. The criterion is a good one: evict the cached tokens that later tokens can already predict, scored under a transposed attention mask, with no training. What this map should take from it is mostly a caution, because the source measures the wrong axis for this map's purposes. The quality bill against a full cache is 5.0–5.6 points on MATH500 and 13.3 on AIME at 25% retention, so a compressed 128K window is not a free 128K window — and the one efficiency table, measured on an RTX 4090, shows peak memory 0.2–0.6 GB higher than not compressing, because at J=512 on a 7B model the cache is negligible beside the weights. There is no memory-versus-context-length curve anywhere in the source, which is precisely the curve this map needs to know what a long window costs after eviction. So the third lever exists and remains unpriced at length, and still nothing measures any two of the three together. One genuinely encouraging detail for this map's evidence standards: the benchmarks here (MATH500, AIME, LoCoMo, Qasper) compress a reasoning trace in flight rather than running a needle sweep, which is the evidence type claimed-context-length-is-not-effective-context-length taught this map to demand.

  • 2026-07-31 — an interaction edge, and it is the first thing in-vault to price a long window on the training side rather than the inference side, from on-policy-distillation-dynamics (T1, primary map distillation-small-models): this map's central claim is that context is a cheap post-hoc edit — acquire a 128K window for ~600 fine-tuning steps on <0.1% of the pretraining data. Everything the map has argued about since then concerns whether the model can use the window (RULER's effective-length collapse, the YaRN-at-1.7B result). Here is a different question the map has never asked: whether a training signal survives out to the lengths the window makes available. In on-policy distillation it does not — the teacher's continuation advantage falls from +0.37 at a 1K-token prefix to +0.02 at 16K, the usable response-length window is 3K–7K, and by 10K–15K performance plateaus or declines, with instability propagating back-to-front from the end of the response. So a cheaply-extended model can hold 128K and still be untrainable past ~7K by at least one standard post-training method, for reasons that have nothing to do with position encoding. That is a third distinct sense of "effective context" the vault should keep separate from the two it already has — perplexity-effective (what YaRN measures), retrieval-effective (what RULER measures), and now supervision-effective (how far into a sequence a training signal remains informative). Nobody has measured the third for any other method, and it is not obvious that the ordering between the three is the same for any two models. Bounded hard: one method, mathematics only, 1.5B–1.7B students, and the authors' proposed explanation for the decay is explicitly unverified — so this is an existence proof that the question exists, not an answer to it. See dense-token-rewards-decay-with-trajectory-depth.

The construction is what makes it usable: thirteen fully synthetic tasks so context length is a dial and parametric knowledge cannot substitute for reading, in four families that go beyond retrieval — needle variants hardened along needle type, hard distractors and multi-match recall; variable tracking (a value is bound, a chain of assignments is scattered through noise, return every name bound to it — a minimal coreference proxy); aggregation (top-K frequent words from a synthetic list, so the answer depends on the whole context); and QA padded with distracting paragraphs from the same dataset. (b) The cheap-extension family is the bottom of the table: two 32K-claiming post-hoc extensions of Llama-2 and one 1M-trained model all fail to beat an unextended Llama-2-7B at any length.   • 2026-07-25 — ingest of ring-attention-blockwise-near-infinite-context (Liu, Zaharia, Abbeel, UC Berkeley, T1; primary map distributed-training): the other road to a long window, and the first answer to this map's founding caveat. Everything here so far edits the position encoding so a trained model tolerates more tokens. Ring Attention does not touch the model at all — it partitions the sequence across a ring of hosts and rotates the KV blocks, making per-host activation memory a function of block size rather than sequence length, so context scales linearly with device count (3B to 16M tokens on TPUv4-1024). Two consequences for this map. (a) Context length has stopped being a model property in two different ways: YaRN says the window is a cheap post-hoc edit to the position encoding, this says the window is however many devices you can put in a ring. Neither is architectural — a third instance of the vault's recurring finding that capability headlines are systems progress on an unchanged block. (b) The line-retrieval result (LLaMA-13B fine-tuned to 512K on 32 A100s, ~0.9 accuracy sustained to 500K, where Vicuna-13B-16K collapses to 0.6 at 16K) is the first evidence in this map that is not perplexity, and it answers the escalation the 2026-07-24 edge made — but see the status note: it is a single unablated figure from a paper about something else, and the extension it validates cost 32 A100s rather than YaRN's 600 steps, so it does not replicate the affordability claim that is this map's actual point. The honest summary is that the map's evidence type improved and its evidence weight on the central claim did not. Also worth carrying as a boundary: the technique's precondition (c ≥ F/B) makes it a datacenter method, so it is not an alternative a practitioner at this vault's scale can choose — post-hoc extension remains the only affordable road, which raises rather than lowers the value of measuring it properly.   • 2026-07-24 — advisory edge from llama-cpp-quantization-unified-evaluation (T1, primary map local-inference): the perplexity caveat this map was coined with has been upgraded from suspicion to measurement — on a neighbouring task. At coining, the loud flag was that every number here is perplexity, which does not establish usable retrieval or reasoning across the window. That was an argument. This run supplies a demonstration of the proxy failing in a setting where both the proxy and the downstream tasks were measured on the same artifacts: four llama.cpp quantization formats span 0.03 perplexity and 3.4 GSM8K points, so perplexity ranks them differently from the capability they are chosen for. The mechanism transfers directly — a token-averaged likelihood is dominated by many easy tokens and blind to the few hard ones, and long-context use (retrieval of one fact at position 90k, reasoning that chains across the window) is exactly a few-hard-tokens problem, arguably more so than 8-bit-vs-5-bit weights are. Nothing here measures a long window, so this does not falsify YaRN; it does mean the vault should stop reading "perplexity stays flat to 128k" as weak evidence for capability and start reading it as evidence about a different construct.   • Perplexity is not usable context — RESOLVED 2026-07-28, and the answer is the reason this map is now contested. At coining the loudest absence was that low perplexity at 131k tokens shows only that the model is not confused. That absence is closed: a thirteen-task suite across 17 models establishes that needle-style evidence and long-context capability come apart almost everywhere, and that the cheaply-extended small checkpoints in it have effective lengths below their own base model's. What replaces the gap is more specific and still unrun, and it is now the single experiment that would settle this map in either direction: the same suite on a YaRN-class checkpoint. The three failing models used Position Interpolation, LongLoRA and 1M-token training respectively — not the per-dimension NTK-by-parts scheme this map is actually built on, whose own paper reports beating the first of those. Nobody has published RULER (or an equivalent) on a YaRN-extended model, the benchmark is open source, and YaRN checkpoints are released. Until someone does, this map holds a T1 affordability claim and a T1 capability refutation of its neighbours, which is exactly what contested means. HALF-ANSWERED 2026-07-30 (jet-long-dynamic-bifocal-rope): RULER has now been run with YaRN as a baseline on Qwen3 1.7B / 4B / 8B, and YaRN comes in 7.94 points below the unextended base at 1.7B, level at 4B, ahead only at 8B. So the suite has met the scheme, and the answer is size-gated. The half still missing is the one that matters most here and it is now stated exactly: that YaRN was applied zero-shot, with no fine-tuning at all, where this map's claim is about ~600 steps on <0.1% of the pretraining data. Run YaRN's own fine-tuning recipe on Qwen3-1.7B and score it on RULER — one small model, one published recipe, one open benchmark, a few hundred GPU-hours. That single run would move this map off contested in one direction or the other, and it is the cheapest status-deciding experiment in the vault.

  • What predicts effective length appears to be size, and this vault runs small models (new 2026-07-28). The size ablation holds training data and training context fixed and finds 34B clearly better than 9B and 6B, on both absolute score and rate of degradation; every model with an effective length of 32K or more in the study is either large or frontier-closed. If usable long context is a scale phenomenon, then the premise this vault is organized around — a small open model on one or two consumer cards — may not be able to buy it at any price, and the extension literature would be answering the wrong question for this audience. One counterweight, and it is the strongest reason not to over-read this: Llama3.1-8B reaches an effective 32K, so 8B is not disqualified — what fails is 7B–13B models extended cheaply after the fact. Nothing in-vault separates "small" from "small and cheaply extended," and that is the discriminating experiment.

  • Nothing above 13B, and nothing outside the LLaMA family. α=1 / β=32 are empirical constants, not derived; whether the ramp thresholds transfer across RoPE base frequencies (θ=10000 vs the much larger bases modern long-context models ship with) is untested here.

  • The modern default may already be elsewhere. Since 2023 the field has shifted toward training long-context in from the start, plus RoPE-base scaling and long-context continued pretraining at much larger budgets. Whether YaRN-style post-hoc extension is still the practitioner's best move in 2026, or a superseded technique, is unsourced — and this map's status should not firm until a current source settles it. Date everything here.

  • The interaction with quantization and KV budget is unmapped. A 128k window multiplies the KV cache by the same factor the context grew; post-training-quantization and local-inference hold three levers on that budget (GQA, KV precision, paging), and no in-vault source measures extension with them. Extending context is the move that makes the KV wall bind hardest, so this is the practical blocker for the local/edge half of the premise.

  • How extension interacts with task fine-tuning is open. If a practitioner YaRN-extends and then LoRA-tunes (or vice versa), nobody in-vault has measured whether the extension survives — a concrete instance of the forgetting question peft-methods carries.
