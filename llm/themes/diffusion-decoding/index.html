Diffusion decoding (parallel denoising, exactness, serving consistency) — map

Coined 2026-08-10 (auto run) — auto-coined at emerging; flagged for David's review. Two sources landed in one night on the same object and neither had a home. A discrete-diffusion language model does not decode one token at a time: it iteratively denoises a block of masked positions in parallel and commits them together. That is a different generation algorithm, not a different deployment shape and not a different architecture, and the three maps that could have absorbed it each hold a piece and can state none of the trade. speculative-decoding owns "more than one token per target forward pass" but is organized around a draft-and-verify bargain with an exactness guarantee that diffusion decoding does not have and cannot break. local-inference is organized by deployment shape and would file a sampler as a runtime detail. kv-cache-management owns the cache but not what writes into it. The case for a map is that the questions this literature asks — how many tokens can be committed in parallel, what the parallelism costs in accuracy, and whether the accelerated model still says what the unaccelerated one would — are the same questions on every substrate and are not answerable from any of those three. The alternative considered and rejected was folding both sources into speculative-decoding, which would have put a method with no target distribution inside a map whose founding claim is that the target distribution is preserved.

Status: emerging (coined at emerging 2026-08-10; one T2 vendor technical report with released open weights and one T1 measurement study, on different models, with no independent replication of either)

What we believe now:

  • The exactness trade this map was coined on belongs to letting the diffusion model emit the final text, not to parallel denoising (new 2026-08-12, and it narrows the map's founding framing two days after coining). The map opened by placing parallel emission on "a curve with an exchange rate," speculation at the zero-quality-cost end and diffusion decoding at the many-tokens-per-pass end. That framing carried an unstated assumption — that the diffusion model produces the output. Put it in the drafter slot behind a verifying target and the block-parallel generation survives while the output distribution becomes the target's, exactly: lossless, with formal guarantees, at 2.1×–3.5× typical over autoregressive decoding (up to 8.49× best cell) across six targets. The reason it works is the same mechanism that made the sampled regime's bill so uneven — committing many positions in parallel bets they are independent, worst on long dependency chains, which are precisely the tokens a verifier rejects. The failure mode and the rejection criterion select for the same thing, so the accuracy cost converts into a throughput cost. So there are three configurations where this map recorded two, and the honest restatement is that a decoder with no target distribution has none to preserve — but this literature is not obliged to run the diffusion model as the decoder. Every measurement below stands; the axis framing does not. It also gives the map's reporting standard a bright-line exception: under verification, agreement with the target is 1.0 by construction, making the drafted configuration the only one here whose speedup number is self-interpreting. [T1] parallel-generation-and-exactness-stop-competing-when-the-generator-drafts
  • A decoding regime is a cheap post-hoc conversion of an existing checkpoint, not a pretraining commitment (new 2026-08-10, and it is the map's most premise-relevant claim). DiffusionGemma is not a from-scratch diffusion model: it is the shipped autoregressive Gemma 4 26B A4B mixture-of-experts checkpoint (25.2B total, 3.8B active) converted by supervised fine-tuning for bidirectional denoising and then reinforcement learning combined with sampler distillation, for fewer than 10% of the original model's total training token budget — and it retains autoregressive decoding with only minor degradation afterwards. That makes three structural properties this vault has now watched turn out to be fine-tunes rather than architecture: sparsity (dense-checkpoints-can-be-upcycled-into-moe, ~50% of sunk pretraining cost), window length (context-length-is-a-cheap-post-hoc-edit-not-a-pretraining-property, <0.1% of the pretraining data), and now the sampler itself. [T2] a-decoding-regime-is-a-fine-tune-not-an-architecture
  • Parallel denoising buys tokens-per-forward-pass by having no exactness guarantee to keep, and the accuracy bill lands on long dependency chains (new 2026-08-10). At a 256-token canvas the model commits 15–20 tokens per forward pass — an order of magnitude past what acceptance-rate work on speculative-decoding reports — at roughly 1,500 output tok/s on one H100 (the model card says ">1,100 tok/s at low batch size, FP8"). Speculation pays for its extra tokens in engineering and costs quality zero by construction; this pays in measured accuracy, and unevenly: against the parent it was converted from, MMLU Pro 77.6 vs 82.6 (−5.0) but AIME 2026 69.1 vs 88.3 (−19.2), BigBench Extra Hard 47.6 vs 64.8 (−17.2) and MMMU Pro 54.3 vs 73.8 (−19.5). Committing 256 positions in parallel is structurally a bet that they do not depend on each other, and reasoning is where that bet is worst — the same damage profile aggressive quantization produces by an unrelated mechanism (a-longer-reasoning-trace-absorbs-approximation-error), which points at sequential dependency as the shared cause. [T2] parallel-denoising-buys-tokens-per-pass-by-abandoning-exactness
  • The advertised speedup and the speedup at reproducible output are different numbers, by an order of magnitude (new 2026-08-10, and it is the map's only independent measurement). A training-free accelerator on an 8B diffusion multimodal model runs 10–12× faster (72.9s → 6.1s) at its default setting and produces zero of fifty outputs identical to what the same model says unaccelerated (lexical agreement 0.420). Buying agreement back is monotone and expensive: 0.463 at 8.12s, 0.572 at 17.53s, 0.666 at 30.11s, and 0.987 with 40 of 50 exact at 55.29s — which is 1.3× the unaccelerated 71.90s. Half of fifty audited low-agreement pairs are genuine content substitution, not paraphrase. A blinded two-annotator factuality study finds no difference (0.00, 95% CI [−0.17, +0.17], κ = 0.18) — a null that detects nothing rather than one that establishes equivalence, exactly as the authors say. [T1] reproducing-your-own-output-costs-most-of-the-speedup
  • The knob these systems expose is not the knob that controls their drift (new 2026-08-10). Sweeping the documented confidence threshold across its full range (0.1 → 0.9) changes committed tokens per step from 1.055 to 1.254 and changes agreement by +0.0056 — a null with a tight interval (Wilcoxon p = 0.75, Cliff's δ = 0.017). What controls drift is the KV-cache refresh interval, an internal constant. The mechanism is stale cached state, with the visual half larger than the generated-text half (0.498 vs 0.454 at interval 4), and the causal check is decisive: under the default schedule you can swap the input image and get a byte-identical description back, 10 times out of 10. Twelve cleverer schedules across five families — adaptive budgets, confidence decay, attention decay, learned gating — all fail to beat a fixed interval at matched compute. Read together they define the map's reporting standard on night one: a diffusion-decoding speedup must be quoted with the agreement it was measured at, and neither source's headline number satisfies that on its own. Three edges out. Against speculative-decoding this is the first serious challenge to that map's framing rather than to any of its results: parallel emission now has two prices, one paid in engineering with a preserved output distribution and one paid in accuracy with no distribution to preserve, and nobody has run them head to head at matched quality — which the DiffusionGemma release uniquely enables, since the same weights still decode autoregressively. Against mixture-of-experts the artifact is a 25.2B/3.8B sparse model whose canvas step activates the expert union over 256 positions at once, the same union inflation speculation-multiplies-the-expert-union-not-just-the-tokens found under speculation at far larger width, and no offloaded measurement of it exists. Against kv-cache-management the drift study contributes a failure mode nothing there covers — a cache that is neither compressed nor evicted but simply refreshed too rarely. Bounded on both sides: the vendor report and its own model card disagree about the headline throughput (~1,500 vs >1,100 tok/s), and the measurement study's causal work rests on one base model (LLaDA-V, 8B) with one implementation that exposes the refresh knob at all.

Open questions:

  • What does a diffusion sampler cost on consumer hardware? Nothing in this map is measured below an H100. DiffusionGemma ships GGUF builds and is roughly 13 GB of 4-bit weights — over a 12 GB card, under a 24 GB one — so the offloaded case is reachable and untested, and moe-sparsity-becomes-a-transfer-budget-when-weights-are-offloaded predicts a canvas-wide expert union is exactly where the throughput claim breaks. A tokens/second and peak-memory measurement on one consumer card would be the single most useful number this map could acquire.
  • Diffusion versus speculation at matched quality. The comparison everyone will want and nobody has run. It is unusually cheap here because one checkpoint supports both regimes: score the same task suite under a tuned speculative pipeline and under the diffusion sampler on the same base weights, and report tokens per forward pass at equal accuracy rather than at equal effort.
  • Does the accuracy bill fall with more conversion compute, or is it structural? Under 10% of the original token budget bought a 5-point general-knowledge loss and a ~19-point reasoning loss. Nobody has reported a scaling curve for the conversion, so it is unknown whether the reasoning gap is a training budget the lab chose not to spend or a property of committing dependent tokens in parallel.
  • Is there a consistency-preserving accelerator at all? Twelve variants across five families lost to a fixed refresh interval at matched compute, and a learned gate reached cross-validated R² ≈ 0.04. Worse, re-running the same input at the same average refresh rate with different timing moves agreement by 0.11 on average and up to 0.53 — so the schedule's realization, not its rate, is doing the work, and nobody has explained why.
  • What is the memory profile of a 256K context under canvas decoding? The encoder-decoder split builds the prompt cache autoregressively and appends each finished canvas, so the cache grows as it would under autoregressive decoding, plus bidirectional attention over a canvas rewritten every step. Neither source reports a memory figure.
