Tokenization & vocabulary (the tensor sized at training time and paid at inference) — map

Coined 2026-08-14 (auto run) — the vault has been accumulating results about the embedding/unembedding pair for two weeks under three different maps and could not state the trade between them. an-unused-vocabulary-is-deletable-weight and weight-tying-decides-whether-vocabulary-pruning-is-free sat on local-inference as deployment edits; the-lm-head-is-the-tensor-four-bit-quantization-skips sat on post-training-quantization as a bit-allocation gap. Both treat V as storage. Tonight's arrival measures it as bandwidth and shows the optimum moves 16× with a parameter — the serving batch size — that nobody knows when the tokenizer is trained. That is one object with three cost currencies and a regime boundary, which is not a facet of any of the three parent maps: local-inference is organized by deployment shape, post-training-quantization by bit budget, transformer-architecture by component. None of them can ask "how big should V be, and who decided?" Auto-coined at emerging; flagged for David's review — the case against is that it is three sources deep and one of them is six pages; the case for is that the alternative was adding a fourth home for the same tensor.

Status: emerging (coined at emerging 2026-08-14 — one T1 measures the cost curve, one T1 measures the deletion lever, and no independent group has replicated either; the roofline argument is closed-form and the empirical support behind it is thin)

What we believe now:

  • A different tokenizer is a small tax on cross-model transfer, not a wall — because two tokenizers built independently already agree on where 90% of their boundaries fall (new 2026-08-17, and it is the map's first finding about V as an interface rather than as a cost). The vault's working assumption, visible in every distillation source here, is that token-level transfer between two models requires a shared vocabulary, with projection matrices or learned mappings as the workaround. Distil in shared text space instead and supervise only at positions where the student's token and the teacher's token cover identical character spans, leaving the rest unsupervised: the alignable fraction starts near 0.90 and rises to 0.96–0.98 during training, across three students from three families against a Qwen-family byte-level BPE teacher. No projection, no mapping, no surgery. The cost scales with segmentation algorithm rather than vocabulary size — GLM's differently-vocabularied BPE gains on both benchmarks, Gemma's SentencePiece gains on the proof benchmark and loses the answer benchmark. The consequence for this map's other findings is that V stops being a commitment to a model family: if the best teacher for your student is a question about capability rather than about vocabulary, then the tokenizer you inherit constrains your costs (bytes, bits, bandwidth) without constraining who you can learn from. [T1, vendor-self-evaluated] See cross-tokenizer-distillation-works-on-shared-text-spans.
  • The vocabulary is a serving parameter frozen before serving exists, and its optimum is a function of the batch size (new 2026-08-14, the map's founding claim). The unembedding matmul's arithmetic intensity is I = 2BVd / (V·d·bpp) — in fp16 that is exactly B, and V cancels. So the batch decides whether you are bandwidth-bound and the vocabulary decides only how much you move. At B=1, I=1 against ridge points of ≈117 (A10G) and ≈183 (A100): the full V×d matrix streams from memory on every decoded token, at 15.1 μs for V=8k, 68.4 at 65k, 202.7 at 262k, 760.8 at 1M on an A100. At B=4096 the same reads cost 0.04 / 0.34 / 1.37 / 5.31 μs — amortization of 377× / 201× / 148× / 143×. The predicted crossover lands where it is measured (128–256 on A10G, 256–512 on A100), which is what makes this a measurement rather than an argument. Composed into a lifecycle cost C(V,B,λ) = C_train(V) + λ·C_infer(V,B), the optimum moves 16×: 32k at B=1 for any inference volume at all, 65k at B=16, 131k at B=64, 262k at B ≥ 256 — against a training-only optimum of 16k everywhere. [T1, one study] See the-optimal-vocabulary-is-a-function-of-batch-size, vocabulary-size-is-a-bandwidth-term-at-batch-one.
  • Quality is flat enough across the range that this is a systems decision, with one metric caveat that does most of the work (new 2026-08-14). BPB spread is 1.8% from V=8k to V=262k at 100M scale and 0.9% across 16k/65k/262k at 1.3–2.3B, with the quality optimum moving right with scale (16k → 65k, read as V* ∝ √N). So there is no quality/throughput trade to argue about inside the measured range. But bits-per-byte is deliberately tokenizer-neutral — that is what makes it the right instrument for comparing across vocabularies — and the price is blindness to the two failures vocabulary edits are known for: rare entities, which weight-tying-decides-whether-vocabulary-pruning-is-free found break silently and permanently, and non-English scripts, which quantization-damage-has-a-language-term established three days ago as the general form of "measured in English, therefore an English result." Everything here is English FineWeb-Edu, ≤2.3B, step-matched rather than FLOP-matched. [T1, scoped] See vocabulary-quality-is-flat-across-a-thirty-two-fold-range.
  • For a single-user local deployment the vocabulary is oversized in bytes, in bits and in time — three independent measurements pointing at one tensor (new 2026-08-14, composing two prior arrivals with tonight's). In bytes: most of a 128K–256K multilingual vocabulary is dead rows for a single-language or single-domain deployment, and deleting them cuts the embedding layer ~60% with no quality loss after a short fine-tune — and on two of three architectures with no fine-tune at all. In bits: the LM head is the largest tensor production 4-bit backends leave in BF16 — 1,188 MB on Qwen3-8B under both AWQ and bitsandbytes — and a head-specific compressor recovers 3.9× of it at +0.007 cross-entropy. In time: the same tensor is re-read from memory once per decoded token at B=1, so V is a throughput term and not only a footprint term. Shipped open-weights vocabularies go the other way — Qwen3 at 151,936, Gemma at 262,144 — chosen for multilingual coverage and for the batch sizes their labs serve at. Both are defensible reasons; neither transfers to a laptop. The vocabulary you inherit is someone else's optimum. [T1 ×3] See an-unused-vocabulary-is-deletable-weight, the-lm-head-is-the-tensor-four-bit-quantization-skips, vocabulary-size-is-a-bandwidth-term-at-batch-one.
  • Whether the vocabulary can be edited at all is a checkable property of the checkpoint, not a research question (inherited 2026-08-10). Pruning-without-retraining is quality-neutral on NLLB-200 (26.43 vs 26.82 BLEU) and M2M100 (24.95 vs 24.86) and collapses on mBART-50 (13.65), which the authors attribute to tight coupling between the decoder embedding and the LM head. Weight tying is inspectable before you start. The other precondition is a data one: which tokens survive is decided by a sampling corpus, so anything absent from the sample becomes unreachable by construction rather than merely degraded — data-curation's regime argument arriving in the deployment stack. [T1, one study, translation models] See weight-tying-decides-whether-vocabulary-pruning-is-free.

Through-line (newest first):

  • 2026-08-17 — edge from simpleopd-tokenizer-agnostic-on-policy-distillation (T1, primary map distillation-small-models; Shanghai AI Lab, seven students across five families): the map gets its first result about the tokenizer as a boundary between models rather than as a cost inside one. Everything here so far treats V as a tensor with three bills — bytes, bits, bandwidth. This treats it as an interface, and finds it far more permeable than the vault assumed. Distilling across different tokenizers by aligning only positions whose tokens cover identical text spans — no vocabulary projection, no learned mapping, unaligned positions simply unsupervised — retains ~90% of positions at the start of training, rising to 96–98% as the student's outputs move toward the teacher's, measured on three students from three families. That reframes a constraint the vault had been assuming rather than measuring: tokenizer mismatch costs a few percent of supervision, not the transfer. The boundary is named by the paper's one failure — the cost tracks segmentation algorithm rather than vocabulary size, with a differently-vocabularied BPE student gaining on both benchmarks and the SentencePiece student gaining on one and losing the other. Read against this map's newest claim from yesterday (a-vocabulary-can-be-extended-in-place-on-a-trained-backbone), the position is now that V is editable in both directions and porous at its edges, which is three independent literatures in four days saying a structural hyperparameter everyone treats as frozen is not. Graded with the primary map's discount — teacher and flagship student are the authors' own models, benchmarks are judge-scored — and one gap specific to this map: no accounting of which positions fail to align. Whether the unsupervised 2–10% is scattered uniformly or concentrated on numerals, symbols and formatting is the difference between a small tax and a targeted one, and a proof benchmark is exactly where that would matter. See cross-tokenizer-distillation-works-on-shared-text-spans.
  • 2026-08-16 — edge from lfm25-vl-3b-edge-vision-language (T2, primary map vlm-on-device): the vocabulary turns out to be editable in the growth direction too, and a shipping team did it in public. This map was coined two days ago holding V as a design-time parameter that a deployment can afterwards only cut — the roofline curve says how big it should have been, and an-unused-vocabulary-is-deletable-weight says a single-language deployment can delete most of the rows it got. A vendor shipping a 3B edge model expanded its trained backbone's vocabulary to 128,000 tokens for non-Latin script coverage by in-place tokenizer extension rather than retraining. Combined with the deletion result the map's position becomes symmetric and much more useful: vocabulary size is a post-hoc editable property in both directions, which puts it alongside context length (context-length-is-a-cheap-post-hoc-edit-not-a-pretraining-property) rather than among the things fixed at initialization — two independent literatures now saying the same thing about two structural hyperparameters that were assumed frozen. The consequence for this map's cost findings is that they become negotiable per deployment: extend for the scripts you need, delete the rows you never reach, and the output head shrinks in exactly the currency vocabulary-size-is-a-bandwidth-term-at-batch-one says binds at B=1. Nobody has run that composition and it is the obvious next experiment. Status holds emerging and the grade is deliberately low: the vendor states the mechanism in a single clause with no ablation, no before/after quality comparison, no accounting of the added embedding and head bytes, and no statement of how the new embeddings were initialized — which is the detail deciding whether in-place extension is cheap or merely fast. The claim to carry is existence; the open question is the price. [T2, single-study] a-vocabulary-can-be-extended-in-place-on-a-trained-backbone
  • 2026-08-14 — The map is coined, and its founding source measures the tensor rather than editing it. lifecycle-optimal-tokenization (T1, six pages, A10G + A100, V swept 8k→1M at two model scales) closes the loop the vault opened four days ago from the opposite end: the pruning and bit-allocation results attack an already-trained model's storage; this prices the choice that made the tensor that size, and finds the answer depends on a deployment parameter fixed long afterwards. Two transferable generalizations. An efficiency parameter frozen at training time is a bet on a serving regime — the same shape as local-inference's currency test, one stage earlier in the pipeline, and with the regime boundary computable from a spec sheet instead of discovered empirically. And the open-weights local practitioner is always at the end of the axis nobody publishes: every recommendation in the paper's own table for B=1 points at a vocabulary a quarter to an eighth of what actually ships.
  • 2026-08-14 — The vocabulary term shows up in an unexpected place: a fine-tuning method's serving bill. weightless-fine-tuning-logit-space-transport applies a V×V transport operator to the logits at decode time, never materializing it but paying an O(V)-scale operation per token per personalized request. Its headline is a training-budget saving (<7% of SFT); its unstated cost lives on this map. Filed here as a cross-link rather than a bullet with its own belief, because the source reports no wall-clock number of any kind. See a-supervised-update-can-be-applied-in-logit-space.
  • 2026-08-11 — In bits: the LM head is the tensor 4-bit quantization skips (inherited from post-training-quantization, where the bit-allocation framing keeps it). Checkpoint inspection under bitsandbytes NF4 and AWQ finds lm_head still a BF16 torch.nn.Linear at [151936, 4096] — 1,188 MB on Qwen3-8B-Base, 1,280 MB on Gemma-4-E4B. A head-specific residual compressor recovers 3.87–3.91× at delta-CE +0.007 against group-INT4's +0.140 at matched storage, and composes with block quantizers for +0.006/+0.007.   • 2026-08-10 — In bytes: an unused vocabulary is deletable weight (inherited from local-inference, which keeps the deployment framing). Corpus-driven pruning from 128K+ to ~10K–26K tokens across M2M100, NLLB-200 and mBART-50 shrinks the embedding layer ~60%; fine-tuned English→Arabic M2M100 reaches 42.04 BLEU / 0.8730 COMET against a dedicated bilingual OPUS-MT's 44.59 / 0.7911. The trimmed-but-not-fine-tuned arm is the transferable one and the authors underplay it. The 60% is a parameter-count reduction on a 418M encoder-decoder, not a measured footprint on a decoder-only LLM — the vault deflates it to "roughly a gigabyte-scale constant, once." Open questions:

  • Nobody has composed the three levers on one model. Row deletion, head-specific bit allocation and batch-matched vocabulary sizing are orthogonal by construction and have never been run together — or even pairwise — on a decoder-only checkpoint. The obvious experiment is a Qwen3-8B pruned to a code-only vocabulary, ARCHead-compressed, benchmarked at B=1 for tokens per second and peak RSS. Every piece exists; nobody has assembled it.
  • The entire map is measured on the wrong models for its own audience. The pruning evidence is 418M–600M encoder-decoder translation models; the cost-curve evidence is ≤2.3B decoder-only GPT trained for 5k steps; the bit-allocation evidence is the only one on a modern 8B open-weights checkpoint. There is no measurement of vocabulary cost on a model anyone actually runs locally.
  • What does V cost in int4? The bandwidth term is V·d·bpp / BW, so quantizing the head changes the numerator directly and should move the B=1 optimum rightward. The founding source extends its model to int4 analytically and never measures it, which is unfortunate, because int4 is the regime this vault's premise operates in and it is the one cell of the table that matters most here.
  • Where do the unalignable positions live? (new 2026-08-17.) Span-based cross-tokenizer alignment leaves 2–10% of a student's generated tokens unsupervised, and nobody has characterized them. If they are scattered uniformly the mechanism is a small uniform tax; if they concentrate on numerals, operators, LaTeX and formatting — precisely where two tokenizers are most likely to segment differently and precisely what a mathematical proof is made of — then the method is silently declining to supervise the hardest positions, and the reported 96–98% overlap is measuring the wrong thing. The analysis costs one pass over an existing training log.
  • Does the flat-quality result survive a non-English corpus? Compression differs by script and the authors say so. If per-character compression is worse for non-Latin text, every curve in the paper moves, and the multilingual vocabularies that look oversized at B=1 may be correctly sized for the languages they were built for. Untested in either direction.
