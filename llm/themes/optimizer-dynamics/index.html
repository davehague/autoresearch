Optimizer dynamics (pretraining optimizer choice and its downstream consequences) — map

Coined 2026-11-03 (auto run) — flagged for David's review. This vault's training-pipeline evidence has split pretraining-loss results (distributed-training's memory/parallelism accounting) from RL-specific training dynamics (post-training-rl) and has a growing but separate AdamW-numerics thread inside training-time-quantization (a denominator-floor precision stall on power-law-skewed problems; a zero-sum LM-head gradient structure amplified by bf16 quantization error) — both about why AdamW's own arithmetic misbehaves during training. Nothing until now asked a different, adjacent question: does the choice of pretraining optimizer (not its numerical precision) change how well the resulting checkpoint later fine-tunes? This map exists to hold that question as its own thread, since the answer ("it depends on data scale, and the dependence reverses") is neither a quantization finding nor an RL finding and does not read naturally as an extension of either existing map.

Status: emerging Status note (2026-11-03, at coining): emerging. One source anchors the map — a single T1 (Carnegie Mellon) with ~600 pretraining runs, ~29,000 fine-tuning runs, and a supporting proof in a toy linear model. No independent replication yet attempted, and the largest model tested is 600M parameters, well below the scale where Muon pretraining is actually being adopted. The map moves to consolidating the first time an independent group reproduces the crossover (in either direction) at a different model family or a materially larger scale.

Sub-threads: none — one question (does pretraining optimizer choice change fine-tuning outcomes, and if so how) with one source and one mechanism answering it; no separable strands yet at this bullet count [reviewed 2026-11-03 @ 1 bullets]

What we believe now:

  • Muon's well-known pretraining-loss advantage over AdamW does not carry over to which optimizer's checkpoint fine-tunes better — and which one wins flips as pretraining data scale grows, with the crossover point shrinking toward smaller, more realistic token-to-parameter ratios as the model gets bigger (new 2026-11-03). Across OLMo-style models from 30M to 600M parameters and 150M-154B DCLM pretraining tokens, Muon pretraining gives a better fine-tuning learning-vs-forgetting tradeoff at small data budgets (GSM8K, StackMathQA, Alpaca, MusicPile fine-tuning targets), but AdamW overtakes it past a crossover token-to-parameter ratio (TPR) that falls from ~320 TPR at 30M params to ~10 TPR at 600M params on StackMathQA — bracketing the field's own compute-optimal reference point (TPR=20, Hoffmann et al.) from both sides depending on model size. Muon's pretraining loss stays lower than AdamW's at every budget tested regardless — the reversal is specifically about what the checkpoint does after fine-tuning, not about which optimizer pretrains better in the metric everyone already tracks. The explanatory mechanism (a "margin-to-spread ratio" that favors Muon early and AdamW late) has a matching closed-form proof in a toy linear associative-memory model, which is why this is scored strong rather than suggestive despite the modest model scale: the empirical crossover and the theoretical account point the same direction independently. The vault's first source separating "which optimizer pretrains to a lower loss" from "which optimizer's checkpoint fine-tunes better," and finding they are not the same question.

Open questions:

  • Does the crossover reproduce at a larger model scale, or on a different pretraining corpus? The largest model tested is 600M parameters; the paper argues the crossover-shrinks-with-size trend should continue but does not test anywhere near the scale where Muon pretraining is now seeing real adoption. Independent replication at larger scale (or a null result there) is what would move this map toward consolidating or straight to contested.
  • How does this interact with training-time-quantization's AdamW-numerics thread? That thread's two sources (a denominator-floor precision stall; a zero-sum LM-head gradient structure amplified by bf16 quantization error) are about AdamW's own arithmetic misbehaving during training; this source is about which checkpoint (Muon- or AdamW-pretrained) generalizes better after fine-tuning. Both implicate AdamW, by different mechanisms, and nobody has asked whether they compound — e.g. does an AdamW-pretrained checkpoint that already sits near this paper's crossover become more fragile under the quantization-error interaction the other thread describes?
  • Does the reversal hold for RLVR/preference fine-tuning, not just supervised fine-tuning? All four fine-tuning targets here (GSM8K, StackMathQA, Alpaca, MusicPile) are standard SFT; post-training-rl's entire thread is RL-based post-training, and whether the pretraining-optimizer crossover shows up the same way under an RL objective is untested.
