Post-training shifts the features contamination detectors read

evaluation-contamination carries membership inference as the branch a practitioner can actually run, anchored on pretraining-membership-leaves-a-detectable-tail at AUC 0.72 — already discounted here as 2022–2023 vintage on models ≤65B. This adds a discount of a different kind, and it is not about age.

Feature-based detectors — the family that now carries the strong numbers — derive membership features jointly from the input text and the model's output. Post-training changes model outputs. Instruction tuning, preference optimization and reasoning-oriented training each "alter model outputs and shift the corresponding membership features, thereby reducing the separability between members and non-members." Every open-weights checkpoint a reader would want to audit has been through at least one of them; the base checkpoints these detectors were validated on are a regime nobody deploys.

The size of the discount is not measured here, and it matters to say so precisely. What is measured is how much a calibration wrapper can win back: up to 7.0% AUC and 15.0% TPR@5%FPR across two base detectors, four benchmarks, and three targets deliberately chosen to span the three post-training types. Recovery is a lower bound on damage — a correction that recovers 7 points establishes that at least 7 points were available to recover, not that the total loss was 7. So the operational consequence is about how the vault quotes its own number: 0.72 is a base-model figure, and the post-trained figure is lower by an unknown margin.

Graded pushes-back rather than extends because it contradicts a scope condition this map has been treating as settled, not because it refutes a measurement. The mechanism generalizes beyond membership inference and this map should say so: a detector's reported AUC is a property of the checkpoint stage it was calibrated at. That is the fourth independent appearance in three weeks of one shape — data-curation's downgrade turned on a filtering ablation being a fixed-compute experimental condition rather than a data property, this map's flagged recipe-leakage mode is the same thing about training recipes, and a serving configuration was recently found to be an undisclosed preparation step. The general form is worth stating plainly: a measurement is valid only in the preparation regime it was calibrated in, and the preparation regime is the thing least often reported. Here it is the instrument itself that carries the defect, which is the most awkward place for it to sit.

One edge worth carrying to distillation-small-models. The weakest reported cell is DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B at 54.3% base AUC — near chance — and that is a distilled reasoning model, the exact family this vault's distillation map is built on. If membership signal is hardest to recover from a distilled student, there is a plausible untested reason: a student trained on teacher traces never saw the pretraining text in the form the detector probes for. Neither literature has said which corpus a distilled student's membership is even a claim about — the teacher's pretraining set, the trace set, or both — and that ambiguity sits underneath every contamination claim about a distilled small model.

Companion finding from the same source, and the two should be read together: the-strong-contamination-detectors-need-labeled-non-members — the detectors this shift damages most are also the ones a practitioner cannot run, so the branch's weakness and its inaccessibility land on the same half of it.

Graded strong: two independent base detectors, four benchmarks, three targets spanning three post-training regimes, and reporting TPR at a fixed low FPR alongside AUC, which is the operationally honest membership-inference metric that AUC-only reporting hides. Bounded: all targets are 7–8B, and the base numbers range from 54.3% to 88.8% AUC across cells, so "up to 7.0%" is a best case over a heterogeneous grid.
