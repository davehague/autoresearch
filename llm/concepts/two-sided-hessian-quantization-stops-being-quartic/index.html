Two-sided Hessian quantization stops being quartic

GPTQ's curvature model is one-sided: H ≈ E[xx^T] ⊗ I_m. It reads the geometry off the input activations and treats every output coordinate as independent. That approximation is why second-order-reconstruction-quantizes-at-scale could run at 175B in a few GPU-hours, and it is the reason GPTQ became a default — not because it was the most faithful objective, but because it was the affordable one.

Two-sided Kronecker-factored approximations (BoA, YAQA) drop the independence assumption and additionally capture correlations across output coordinates. They quantize better and they cost O(m²n²), which is quartic in the matrix dimension where GPTQ is cubic. Every practical deployment therefore kept the worse objective.

BaKron is a solver, not a new objective, and its argument is that the quartic term was an arrangement rather than a requirement. Two independent structural facts do the work. Anti-diagonal parallelism: entries on the same anti-diagonal carry no mutual dependency under the two-sided update, so they round in parallel and the sequential depth falls from O(mn) to O(m+n). Recursive divide-and-conquer: split, quantize the first half, propagate the induced error into the second, quantize that — which brings total work to O(mn(m+n)). BoA is the naive corner of that design space and YAQA is the anti-diagonal-only corner; BaKron is the one with both, at 60× on an 8192×8192 matrix.

For post-training-quantization this is a different kind of entry than anything else on the map. The map's spine is that low-bit damage is an allocation problem — which channels, layers, or experts get the bits (salient-channels-carry-quantization-error, expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer, rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free). BaKron does not touch allocation. It changes what a fixed allocation costs to compute, which is the axis that decided the incumbent in the first place.

Hold it as suggestive rather than strong, and for a specific reason the authors state themselves: at today's calibration sizes the excluded terms — Cholesky, Hessian accumulation, calibration-data propagation — dominate end-to-end quantization wall-clock, so the 60× is a speedup of a component that is not currently the bottleneck at 8B. And the quality comparison that would make this a recommendation — two-sided against one-sided at matched bit-width, at 4 bits and below, on models this vault cares about — is appendix material here rather than the paper's claim. The correct entry is: the barrier moved and nobody has yet walked through it.
