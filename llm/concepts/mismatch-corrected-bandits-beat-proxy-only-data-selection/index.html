The proxy's ranking of data choices is often wrong for the target — and the fix is to audit it rarely, but on a schedule

This map's proxy-model data-selection thread (LESS/DSIR gradient-alignment scoring, and the practice of training identical proxy models on each data variant to infer which filtering/dedup choice helps) has generally treated a small model's ranking of data interventions as a cheap stand-in for the target model's, with transfer assumed rather than measured in-run. This source measures the assumption directly on a real closed-loop task and finds it breaks often enough to matter: about a third of per-round reward signs disagree between proxy and target, despite an overall positive correlation. Framing data allocation as a multi-fidelity bandit — proxy reward observed every round, target reward revealed only on scheduled "audits" of the same update — lets the method estimate an arm-wise (per-intervention) correction from paired proxy/target observations, without assuming the mismatch is a constant offset or that proxy rankings are even directionally preserved. The paper proves a minimum audit count is necessary (proxy-only allocation incurs linear regret) and, separately, that when audits are spent matters as much as how many: spending the audit budget early loses to spreading it across training under drifting reward conditions, with a sliding-window policy achieving provably sublinear regret.

What this does and does not establish. It establishes, on one real (not simulated) closed-loop proxy/target pair and one classification-curriculum task, that mismatch-aware correction with paced auditing beats both proxy-only allocation and naive audit scheduling, with a theoretical backing (regret lower bounds, necessary audit count) rather than only an empirical result. It does not establish that the mechanism transfers to LLM-scale pretraining data curation — the tested pair (28.8M proxy, 335M target) and task (CivilComments-WILDS worst-group accuracy) are far smaller and more narrowly scoped than this map's usual billion-token generative-pretraining setting, and the sliding-window regret bound itself is validated only in the paper's synthetic experiments, not on the BERT/CivilComments task. Directly relevant to this map's own open gap (curation-choice rankings validated only at small proxy scale, with target-scale transfer unconfirmed): this is the first source on this map that treats that gap as something to measure and correct in-run, rather than an unresolved risk.
