A parseable answer, a healthy score, and nothing behind it

evaluation-contamination mostly catalogues benchmarks that measure something other than their name — a suite average hiding its weakest dimension, a commonsense set covering a narrow slice of commonsense, a win that is mostly answer formatting. This one is different in an uncomfortable way: the benchmark is behaving correctly and the model's failure is hidden by the benchmark's label distribution.

BoolQ's gold labels here are roughly 70% yes. A compressed model that stops discriminating and starts emitting a constant will therefore score near 70% if it constants to yes and near 30% if it constants to no — and which one it does is a property of the checkpoint, not of how badly it was damaged. Both happen in the same paper, on two models from the same family. Qwen3.5 0.8B at 5% pruning collapses to no and scores 31, which looks like the disaster it is. Qwen2.5 1.5B at 3% pruning collapses to yes and scores 53 strict / 67.9% on parsed responses, which looks like a working model. Balanced accuracy puts them the other way round (37.9 versus 50.7). Strict accuracy does not merely understate the damage; it inverts the ranking of two broken configurations.

The part that should change practice is what happens next. LoRA recovery is the standard repair after pruning, and on this pathology it is worse than nothing: Qwen2.5 1.5B recovered at 1% pruning answers yes on 97 of 100 samples, reports 71% strict accuracy, and reads 52.6% balanced accuracy — chance. The adapter restored the prior. Anyone reading the strict column would conclude the repair worked, and would ship a model that answers yes to everything.

There is a second, cruder version of the same trap in the same tables, and it is worth flagging because it is easier to hit: pruned Gemma variants parse on 1–4 of 100 prompts, so a reported BoolQ accuracy of 100 means one correct parseable response out of a hundred attempts. A score computed over parseable outputs, without the parse rate beside it, is not a score.

The general rule this supports is one the vault can apply immediately and cheaply. Report parse rate, prediction skew and balanced accuracy alongside any accuracy number computed on a compressed model over an imbalanced label set. It is three extra columns and it is the difference between detecting a collapse and shipping one. The same discipline is what averaging-a-suite-hides-its-weakest-dimension asks for at the suite level and a-detector-needs-a-no-verdict-branch asks for at the decision level.

It also composes with the quantization map's damage catalogue in a way neither side has stated. quantization-collapses-agency-toward-inaction found compressed models drifting toward a default behaviour rather than losing capability uniformly; quantization-multiplies-the-decision-margin gives the mechanism for why a near-tie resolves the same way every time once the representation coarsens. A collapse to a constant class is that mechanism at its endpoint — a decision whose margin has gone to zero, resolved by the prior. The novelty here is not the collapse; it is that the standard metric rewards it whenever the prior happens to match the label distribution.

Bounds: one benchmark (BoolQ), one 100-sample subset, Q4_K_M on GPU, models at 0.8–1.5B, and the damage is from low-ratio structured pruning rather than from quantization alone — no quantization-only configuration in this study collapses. Whether four-bit weights alone can induce this on a larger model is untested and is the obvious next run.
