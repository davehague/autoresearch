3:T498, Parallel speculative decoders propose an entire block of draft tokens at once rather than one at a time, but a single early wrong candidate can cause the target model to reject the whole remaining suffix, since nothing in the draft step let later candidates react to earlier ones. Corrective Prefix Attention (CPA) gives each candidate token attention over the other candidates already chosen in the block — made cheap by fixing the candidate shortlist in advance so all pairwise interactions can be precomputed as table lookups rather than additional model passes — and adds an auxiliary autoregressive loss trained on clean (non-speculative) prefixes, which needs no extra inference pass either. On eight math, code, and dialogue benchmarks with Qwen3-4B/8B targets on Ascend NPUs, CPA commits 13-15% more tokens per verification round than DSpark and 25-26% more than DFlash (two other parallel drafters), for mean speedups of 3.35x/3.37x under greedy decoding and 2.86x under sampling.
