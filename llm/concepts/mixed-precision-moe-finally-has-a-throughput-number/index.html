The number the technique has been missing

When moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity landed on 2026-07-25, this vault recorded the gap in its own note and again in the map: weight-only and memory-only. No latency, no throughput, no energy figure appeared anywhere in that source, and the reason it mattered is that quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill had already established that footprint and speed move independently. "Four times smaller" said nothing about tokens per second on the devices local-inference cares about. The gap sat open for eight days.

It is closed, and in the favourable direction. On a single H100 at batch size 1 — the single-user configuration this vault's premise cares about, not a throughput-serving benchmark — mixed-precision expert quantization is 1.4× to 1.6× faster to decode than an FP16 baseline with torch.compile enabled, and it gets faster as the bit budget falls. This is the expected sign once decode is understood as bandwidth-bound: fewer bytes per expert means fewer bytes moved per token, and the dequantization arithmetic is paid out of headroom that was idle. It is the same shape as four-bit-weights-put-llms-on-device on dense models, now measured on the routed case.

The Mixtral column is the one to remember for the premise. Full-precision Mixtral-8×7B is over 90 GB and does not fit one H100 at all; the 1.5-bit build is 12.0 GB at 88.6 tok/s. That is inside a 12 GB RTX 3060 — the exact card mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference used to demonstrate that Mixtral could be run at 2–3 tok/s by offloading experts and caching them. If a mixed-precision build fits outright, the entire offload apparatus becomes unnecessary for that model, and the comparison a 12 GB owner faces changes shape: not "offloaded MoE versus a dense model that fits," but "an aggressively quantized MoE that fits versus a dense model that fits." Nobody has run that head-to-head, and it is now the sharper question.

Two things bound the enthusiasm, and the second is real.

The kernel is custom — a fused Triton path for 1-, 2- and 3-bit dequantization plus grouped GEMM, written for this paper. None of it is reachable from llama.cpp or Ollama today, whose quantization menus cannot express per-expert bit assignment at all. So this is a demonstration that the speed exists, not a thing a local practitioner can run, which is the same standing complaint the vault registered against the allocation result itself.

And the throughput is measured on an H100 with every expert resident. There is no offload configuration anywhere in the paper. For DeepSeekV2-Lite at 5.2 GB the point is moot. For a larger MoE that still exceeds a consumer card after quantization, how a 1.5-bit expert behaves under streaming — the regime moe-sparsity-becomes-a-transfer-budget-when-weights-are-offloaded describes, where the win comes from moving fewer experts, not smaller ones — is unmeasured, and the two effects should compound rather than compete.
