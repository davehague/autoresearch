More calibration text, not more calibration passes

Standard practice fixes the calibration set at 128 or 256 sequences and treats it as sufficient. This source sweeps it over six doublings and finds accuracy still climbing at 16 M tokens — Math-500 rising 20.90 → 66.20, GSM8K 15.84 → 70.96, with no visible plateau at the largest budget the authors ran.

The controlled part is what makes it a finding rather than a curve. More tokens means more gradient steps unless you hold iterations fixed, so the obvious objection is that this measures optimization, not data. The paper separates them: at fixed 256 K tokens, quadrupling optimization iterations buys +5.49% average across five tasks; at fixed iterations, going 256 K → 2 M tokens buys +13.86%. The signal is in the diversity of what the quantizer sees, not in how long it looks.

Two connections make this more than a hyperparameter note.

It re-frames what a calibration set is for. The vault's existing model of calibration is a contamination model — quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model: a small sample imprints its domain, and the risk is that the imprint does not match deployment. This adds a coverage model on top: the sample is also too small to cover the activation regimes a reasoning workload visits, and the fix is more of it. The two are the same underlying fact seen from either end — a 128-sequence sample is simultaneously biased and thin — and only the coverage half has a knob attached.

And it sits uncomfortably beside this vault's best-replicated data finding. finetuning-data-quality-and-diversity-beat-quantity holds that at fine-tuning scale, scaling example count 16× moves nothing while selection moves everything, replicated at three pipeline stages (data-curation-choices-are-ablatable-and-non-monotonic, LIMA, filtering-a-generated-corpus-outperforms-generating-more-of-it). Here quantity moves a great deal — 64× the tokens for +45 Math-500 points. The two are compatible and the boundary is worth naming: selection dominates when the objective is to teach a behaviour the weights can already express; volume dominates when the objective is to reconstruct a function over its whole input domain. Calibration is the second kind. That is a testable distinction and nobody has stated it, which makes it this note's most useful export.

The practical ceiling is compute, not data: calibrating at these budgets takes 4–240 hours on 8×A100-80GB. Two cheaper levers in the same paper have better ratios — extending the calibration sequence length 2,048 → 4,096 moves Math-500 58.40 → 68.60 within the same token budget, and adding target-task training samples inside a fixed 4 M budget moves GSM8K 61.56 → 70.45.

Scope: one model at one bit-width for the sweep; every point is weight-only ternary with 16-bit activations; no sweep exists at 4 bits, where calibration-content-is-the-dominant-term-below-four-bits says the whole family of effects is small.
