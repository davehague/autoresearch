Stop asking whether to speculate; change what the speculation routes to

This vault has measured the same failure four times now. offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass, speculation-multiplies-the-expert-union-not-just-the-tokens and verified-tokens-and-activated-experts-are-different-costs all say that verifying B draft tokens against a sparse model loads the union of every expert any candidate routes to, so the cost of speculating grows with route dispersion rather than with token count — and that the combination can be a net loss. Every response so far has been a scheduling policy: decide whether to speculate (routing-can-be-decided-per-prompt-instead-of-per-token in spirit, Cascade in practice), or how long a draft to run.

The move here is different and, in retrospect, obvious. The union is not a fact about the batch; it is a function of decisions the system is already making, and both of them can be re-aimed at it.

Price the candidate by what it adds. Expected benefit is the usual prefix confidence times a saved decode step. Cost is the draft's own compute plus |E_i \ ∪_{j∈S} E_j| · T_exp — the experts this candidate brings that nothing already admitted brings, converted into a measured latency unit. Admit while utility ≥ 1. The crucial detail is that draft-time routing is available before verification and predicts target routing well enough to serve as the proxy; the resulting model tracks real verification latency at R² = 0.943–0.999. Confidence-only pruning cannot do this: draft confidence piles up near 1.0 for retained tokens, so it barely separates them, while two equally-confident candidates can differ several-fold in expert cost.

Bias the routing toward what is already in flight. Aggregate confidence-weighted expert logits across the draft batch, take a bounded globally-preferred set, and add a per-token bias proportional to the gap between the top-1 expert and the (k+1)-th. High-confidence tokens keep their choices; low-confidence ones drift toward the shared set. This exploits the skew experts-want-purity-and-routers-want-contrast and fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy describe from the training side — beyond the top few, many experts carry comparable scores and are functionally interchangeable.

The measured baselines are the confirmation half of this note and they are stark. On a Jetson Orin NX 16 GB with experts on SSD, expert-sparsity self-speculation runs at 0.61× and layer-sparsity at 0.20× of plain autoregressive decoding on OLMoE/HumanEval. Speculating actively hurts. The same model, same device, same draft construction, plus these two mechanisms and a shared cache: 5.25×.

Two scope conditions. The mechanism is expert traffic, so the benefit is a function of how tight the memory budget is — the same cell falls from 3.76× to 1.65× when the device has 64 GB and nothing needs offloading. And the routing bias is not free; it is the subject of reuse-aware-gating-edits-the-target-model-not-the-draft, and it is the reason this technique cannot be described as a pure scheduling win.
