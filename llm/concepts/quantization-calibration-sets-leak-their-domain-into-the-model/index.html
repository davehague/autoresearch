A fifth way a number misleads, and it happens before the evaluation starts

post-training-quantization has carried, since the seed ingest, the claim that "no-backprop calibration generalizes — AWQ's no-reconstruction design keeps calibration cheap and avoids overfitting the calibration set." That has been an assertion by one method's authors about their competitors, sitting in the map beside the standing gap "a neutral AWQ-vs-GPTQ head-to-head." This is the first in-vault measurement of the effect it points at, and the effect is larger than the rhetoric implied.

Hold the model, the method and the bit budget fixed; vary only the calibration corpus:

Calibration setMMLUHumanEvalGSM8K
C455.829.337.7
MATH47.413.741.0
GitHub-Code45.025.129.6

HumanEval moves by a factor of 2.1. And note what the table does not show: this is not a tidy "calibrate on your target domain" story. GitHub-Code calibration produces a worse HumanEval score than generic C4, and MATH calibration costs 8.4 MMLU points while buying only 3.3 on GSM8K. There is no simple corrective rule available — the reliable statement is the more unsettling one, that an undisclosed preprocessing choice moves published scores by margins larger than most papers' claimed improvements.

This does not fit any of the four failure modes evaluation-contamination names. Leakage, construct mismatch, infrastructure non-equivalence and judge bias (benchmark-scores-can-be-memorization, intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting, sharded-optimizer-math-is-not-locally-equivalent, llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias) are all properties of the evaluation. This is a property of an undisclosed model-preparation choice: two people can publish "Mixtral-8×7B, 2.5-bit" and differ by 16 HumanEval points, both honestly, both reproducibly. Call it recipe leakage — the compression pipeline's own hyperparameters silently determining the score. It also compounds with the vault's existing warning, because perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats would not have caught it: the WikiText2 numbers across calibration variants are far closer than the task numbers.

Operating consequence, stated as a rule the vault can apply: a quantized model's benchmark score is only interpretable alongside its calibration corpus, and almost nobody reports it. Every 4-bit comparison in post-training-quantization that involves GPTQ-family reconstruction inherits this uncertainty.

Bound: measured on MoE bit allocation, where input-dependent routing makes calibration coverage a structurally harder problem than in a dense model — an unrepresentative corpus can fail to activate whole experts, which has no dense analogue. So the magnitude here is an upper bound on what the same effect does to dense quantization, and it is one paper, one model, one method, one budget. It does not vindicate AWQ over GPTQ; AlphaQ's own pipeline uses GPTQ with 128 WikiText2 samples underneath, and the authors scope "calibration-free" (footnote 1) to the bit-allocation stage only. Graded as a strong flag rather than a fifth pillar until a second group measures it.
