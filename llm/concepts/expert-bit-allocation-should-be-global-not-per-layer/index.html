The budget should not be handed out one layer at a time

moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity closed this map's MoE × quantization cell on 2026-07-25 and drew a structural conclusion along the way: because the up, gate and down projections inside one expert score differently on a spectral training-sufficiency criterion, the natural unit of allocation is the layer, not the expert. That conclusion arrived with a companion — that any fixed per-block budget leaves quality on the table, so the program must be global — and the two have sat together in this vault without anyone noticing they pull in different directions.

An ICML 2026 result, with code, resolves the tension in favour of the second and against the first. Measuring expert-weight sensitivity as the trace of the empirical Fisher information matrix, it finds sensitivity "varies not only within a layer but also across layers" — so the layer-local linear program that every prior expert-level method solves, which forces each layer to spend an identical budget, is optimizing under a constraint the data does not support. Replace it with one binary linear program over every expert in the model, using a second-order loss-increase estimate taken on the MoE layer output rather than on individual expert outputs — which folds the routing probabilities in and, more importantly, makes the resulting ΔL̃_ij comparable across layers, which per-layer reconstruction objectives are not. Solved in seconds, no hand-tuned weighting coefficients.

Global allocation wins perplexity in all twelve model-budget cells, and the margin widens as the budget tightens — on Qwen3-30B-A3B at 1.5 bits it is 12.16 against 20.85. The paper also ablates the obvious cheaper alternatives and both fail: naively lifting the layer-local coefficients into a global program "suffers significant degradation, as the resulting expert importance is not comparable across layers," and a two-step scheme that assigns per-layer budgets first and experts second beats that but still trails true global allocation.

Two qualifications keep this from being a clean overturn.

The accuracy story is weaker than the perplexity story, and the exception is instructive. On Mixtral at 2.5 bits, the uniform baseline posts the best zero-shot average of the three methods (65.49 against global's 65.13), and at 2.0 bits the layer-local method edges global. Mixed precision's advantage on the coarse-grained model only becomes decisive at 1.5 bits. That is the same architecture-shaped pattern the earlier source reported — vanilla MoEs with few large experts have little exploitable variance, fine-grained ones have a lot — so the two sources agree about where allocation pays even while disagreeing about what to allocate over, and this vault should read "perplexity moves in every cell, accuracy moves where the architecture is fine-grained."

The two allocation signals are not comparable on cost. The earlier method's selling point was that its criterion is calibration-free — read off the weight spectra, no data at all — which sidestepped the confound in quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model rather than resolving it. This one needs 128 calibration sequences for gradients, and its own results show that dependence biting hard. So the honest form of the disagreement is: the allocation unit should be the expert and the scope should be global, and the best-performing way to find that allocation currently costs you the calibration independence the previous best method had. Nobody has tried the obvious hybrid — a global program over experts scored by the calibration-free spectral criterion — and it is a cheap run, since both halves are published.

Bound: four models, one quantizer (GPTQ, group size 128), attention fixed at 4 bits throughout, seven commonsense zero-shot tasks that this vault has repeatedly measured as the least quantization- sensitive family. Peak GPU memory during quantization is 191 GB against the layer-local method's 32 GB, because of the gradient computation — an offline cost, but one the authors flag as potentially exceeding single-GPU capacity for larger MoEs.
