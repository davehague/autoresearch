The 4-bit backbone charges rent in wall-clock, and some of it is refundable

peft-methods has priced quantized adaptation almost entirely in memory — low-rank-adapters-match-full-finetuning, the QLoRA line, energy-reorders-the-peft-methods-that-memory-ranks. The time axis has been an aside. This makes it the subject: QLoRA dequantizes every NF4 block back to half precision on each forward and backward pass, which several independent studies put at 0.7–0.8× the training throughput of plain fp16 LoRA. So the practitioner who quantizes in order to fit at all pays twice.

The mechanism is one sentence: an fp16 layer inside an otherwise-quantized model skips dequantization, so every protected layer converts a little memory into a little speed. The recipe is a single calibration-free CPU pass that ranks layers and keeps the top K in fp16 under a budget — seconds, no calibration data, no search, where ILP-based joint allocation needs repeated passes over calibration data.

Two things make this worth carrying rather than filing as another QLoRA variant.

The scale story is the vault's own premise. At 1.4B fp16 LoRA fits everywhere and quantization is optional; the methods compete in a band best read as parity. At 7B, fp16 LoRA's 19.1 GiB already crowds a 24 GB consumer card. At 14B its weights alone are 27.5 GiB — past a 24 GB card before a single activation — while every quantized method loads the same base in 9–11 GiB. In that regime quantization stops being an optimization and becomes a precondition, and the only remaining question is which quantized method. That is precisely the regime peft-methods exists to arbitrate.

And the author states the case against his own method. Against LoftQ the two are tied on accuracy (74.84 over three seeds vs 74.78 over six, inside either spread), LoftQ uses less memory (3.83 vs 4.05 GiB peak), and AQLoRA-q wins only on step time — "behind or level on two of the three axes a practitioner weighs, and its case rests on step time alone. The spread of that gain is the size of the gain." A reader who does not care about training time should prefer LoftQ. On Phi-3-mini the three main 4-bit methods span 0.07 points (87.0 / 87.0 / 87.0, p=.73), which is peft-methods's recurring finding restated: the differences reported in this literature are smaller than the spread between repeated runs of the same configuration, the same complaint a-fifteen-point-baseline-gap-is-a-claim-about-the-baseline makes about the whole method race.

The decomposition matters for anyone porting the idea. Early backward stopping — a known trick, and the author says so rather than claiming it — is about 6% of the 11%. With gradient checkpointing off, the gap grows to 42% at 23% less memory, because stopping the backward early also drops the lower-block activations. So the headline number is measured in the configuration that suppresses the mechanism most.

Limits that bound this hard. Every number is on one V100-SXM2-32GB, a pre-Hopper datacenter card; the author explicitly targets smaller commodity and edge GPUs, does not run on them, and offers only a bandwidth-scaled feasibility projection. The budget is specified as a fraction of layers, which the author names as a defect of his own design: grouped-query attention shrinks k/v projections below q, and fused qkv architectures leave four kinds of linear layer where Llama has seven, so the same nominal budget buys 4.3% of the weights on one model and 16.3% on another — a factor of fourteen in the memory it costs. Cross-family comparisons at equal budget are comparisons at unequal memory. Base perplexity is also shown to be a poor guide to the budget: it improves monotonically with protection while downstream accuracy stays flat (0.6 points of range against a 0.39 seed spread), which is perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats arriving on the fine-tuning side.
