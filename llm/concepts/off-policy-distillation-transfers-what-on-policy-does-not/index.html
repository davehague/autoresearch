Off-policy distillation transfers what on-policy does not

The control arm is the reason on-policy-distillation-buys-sampling-efficiency-not-capability is a finding about a stage rather than about distillation. Run off-policy distillation on the same students, the same teachers and the same budgets, and the pass@K curves do not cross: the off-policy student beats its base at small and large K alike. Whatever the on-policy objective is doing, distillation in general is not doing it.

That gives distillation-small-models's two-stage ordering a mechanism instead of a heuristic. distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank arrived at "off-policy SFT cold start before on-policy distillation" from a novelty argument — a teacher that looks like the student has nothing to transfer — and noted that Qwen3 already ships that order. This says why the order is not interchangeable: the two stages do different kinds of work. Off-policy training exposes the student to teacher trajectories it would not have produced, which is the only way its support can widen. On-policy training scores the student's own samples, so it can reweight what the student already reaches and cannot introduce what it does not. Run on-policy alone and you get exactly the observed signature — better averages, worse boundary.

The perplexity check supports the reading directly: on-policy trajectories become more likely under the teacher while staying closer to the base distribution under the base scorer. The student moved toward the teacher's preferred region of its own output space without leaving that space.

Practitioner form, and it is an ordering constraint rather than a preference: the off-policy stage is where new capability enters, so a budget-constrained run should spend on it first and treat the on-policy stage as sharpening. Skipping the cold start is not a smaller version of the recipe — it is a different intervention with a different result.

Held at suggestive rather than strong for one specific reason: the off-policy arm is a control, not the paper's subject. Its exact configuration, tuning effort and compute against the on-policy arm are not laid out to the standard the headline result is, so a matched-cost claim is not available here. Add the paper's general bounds — competition mathematics only, three configurations, 1.5–1.7B students, no code release — and what this supports is the ordering, not a quantified split of where the gains come from. The matched-cost version is the run this map still wants: one student, one teacher, equal GPU-hours spent off-policy only, on-policy only, and split, all scored at pass@1 and pass@1024.
