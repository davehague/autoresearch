OPD's dense teacher signal is cheap to exploit only when the teacher's distribution already sits where the student can express it -- which a self-GRPO checkpoint satisfies by construction and an external teacher does not

TL;DR: This map's OPD strand has accumulated six levers on "which prefix, which teacher, which divergence" without ever testing the more basic question this source asks: is OPD's dense per-token supervision actually cheaper than GRPO's sparse outcome reward, in training FLOPs to reach a target accuracy? The answer is conditional rather than general. Against an externally-trained, already post-trained teacher, OPD loses to GRPO in accuracy-per-FLOP even when that teacher is further GRPO-trained, and OPD tends to inflate response length as a side effect. Against a self-GRPO teacher -- the student's own prior GRPO checkpoint -- OPD recovers that checkpoint's accuracy at 50x less compute than the original GRPO run cost, replicating with a larger student, on code, and in a second model family. The practical payoff is multi-teacher OPD: train N GRPO specialists per domain, then distill them into one student for 5.2-8.0% of the specialist-training cost, beating joint GRPO's total compute by 42.0% on a two-domain mix.

What it says. This reframes the strand's standing assumption -- that dense supervision is intrinsically more sample/compute-efficient than RL's sparse reward -- as false in general and true in one specific, checkable condition: teacher-student distributional closeness, which a self-GRPO checkpoint has by construction (same base, same training trajectory up to that point) and an external teacher does not have by default. None of this map's six existing OPD-composition levers (SPTA, ESC, MTA-OPSD, Shuffled-OPSD, CoRD, O²PD) test against a self-GRPO teacher specifically, and all of them implicitly assume OPD's efficiency advantage holds — this source is the first to measure the FLOPs side of that assumption directly rather than only accuracy. The multi-teacher-consolidation result also gives post-training-rl a concrete alternative to joint multi-domain GRPO training: train specialists separately (simpler optimization, no cross-domain interference during RL) and merge cheaply afterward, rather than fighting for a single RL run to cover every domain.

Caveats. All comparisons are the authors' own harness; no third-party replication. The 50x figure is specifically measured on math; "a second model family" and "code" extend the direction but the paper does not report the exact multiplier replicating at 50x in every setting. Response-length inflation under vanilla OPD against an external teacher is noted qualitatively, with no FLOPs-equivalent penalty assigned to it in the headline comparison. No code release stated at ingest time.
