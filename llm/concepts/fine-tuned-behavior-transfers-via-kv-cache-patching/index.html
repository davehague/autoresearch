The KV cache is not just a compute/memory artifact of a fine-tuned model -- for conventions and dispositions (not capabilities), it is causally sufficient to carry the fine-tune's behavior into a model that never saw the fine-tuning weights at all

TL;DR: This map's sources treat the KV cache as something to spend a budget on -- evict it, quantize it, share it across a loop, offload it -- and have not asked what the cache's contents causally determine beyond the next-token distribution of the model that wrote them. This source answers a different question directly: if you take the keys/values a LoRA fine-tune computed while reading a prompt, and let the unmodified base model's own weights decode conditioned on that cache instead of its own, does the fine-tune's behavior come along? For conventions (language choice, formatting, refusal, reasoning style) and dispositions (misalignment framings, sycophancy) the answer is substantially yes in one direction and reversible in the other; for capabilities it is mostly no. That asymmetry -- behavior lives partly in the cache, skills mostly don't -- is new information for this map, and it is localized to a handful of positions (system-prompt and chat-template tokens) rather than spread uniformly, which is the kind of claim this map's eviction and allocation literature could in principle act on (a cache-compression scheme that protects "early, template-adjacent" positions is untested) but has never measured.

What it says. Patching is directional: swap source model's cached keys/values for some prefix of the sequence into the target model, then let the target model's own weights continue decoding. ft->base patching (implanting fine-tuned behavior into the untouched base) transfers conventions and dispositions substantially (German 78-97%, emergent-misalignment-medical 4.8% against the real fine-tune's 7.2% and base's 0.0%) but not most of the tested capabilities. base->ft patching (erasing fine-tuned behavior from the fine-tune itself, using only the base model's cache) removes most convention/disposition behavior back out, though less completely at the default learning rate for German specifically than the forward direction suggests should be possible -- it takes a higher fine-tuning learning rate (1e-4, 2e-4) before base-cache patching meaningfully fails to suppress German. The effect is not spread evenly across the prompt: a gradual, position-by-position sweep shows the KL-based proximity-to-target score shifts almost entirely at the last few template tokens, and LoRA rank controls how much the effect concentrates versus spreads (rank 1: 68-70% transfer; rank 16+: 94-99%). The clean capability/convention split -- JSON extraction, semantic parsing and G2P mostly resist cache transfer, while forcing the swap on semantic parsing produces outright repetition-loop degeneration in 17-41% of completions rather than a recovered or degraded-but-coherent capability -- is the paper's cleanest result and the one with no counterpart anywhere else in this map: nothing here has previously distinguished "what the cache stores" by behavior type.

Caveats. Only 4-8B instruction-tuned models and twelve hand-picked tasks; nothing says this holds at larger scale or on a broader task sample. Everything above is LoRA at mostly one learning rate -- the authors' own note that higher learning rates and full fine-tuning "appear to rely less on the KV cache" is observed only via the German learning-rate sweep, not tested systematically across tasks. Qwen3.5's linear-attention layers store a fixed-size recurrent state rather than per-position KV and are outside this method's applicability as stated. The gradual-patching proximity measure is distributional (KL-based), not a direct behavioral read, so it complements but does not substitute for the free-generation transfer rates. The paper's own flagged implications -- that serving several LoRA fine-tunes over one shared prompt cache could leak behavior across them, and that safety training wanting to localize where a model stores a change would have a concrete target in these few positions -- are untested in this paper and open for this map and peft-methods (LoRA rank's role here is a direct, if narrow, echo of that map's own rank-vs-capacity argument) and adapter-objects-and-serving (shared prompt caches across adapters).
