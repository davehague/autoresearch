Linear-time in the exponent, slower on the clock

The case for a state-space model on constrained hardware is asymptotic: O(N) sequence scaling against attention's O(N²), and a constant-size recurrent state instead of a KV cache that grows with context. This vault has recorded the second half approvingly — a-recurrent-state-removes-the-position-encoding-problem, and Kimi K3's KDA context parallelism turning a fixed-size state into a fixed-size all-gather.

Measured on one RTX 4090 at the sequence lengths a personalization fine-tune actually uses, the first half does not hold, and the reason is that the asymptotics never engage. At 128 tokens (GLUE) and 512 (LaMP), the quadratic attention term is small, so there is nothing for linear scaling to beat; meanwhile the sequential selective scan is memory-access-bound and cannot saturate tensor cores the way a dense matmul does. Mamba-1.4B takes 49.4 minutes on SST-2 where the larger Qwen3-1.7B takes 22.3, and 147.9 on QNLI against 104.2, at roughly 18% fewer TFLOPs. Serving is worse: 484 seconds for a LaMP-2 inference pass against TinyLlama-1.1B's 75, because beam-search decoding must copy and reorder the recurrent state at every step through kernels that are less optimized than the transformer path. Mamba-2's matmul-based scan is a real improvement (31.7 vs 49.4 min on SST-2) and does not close the gap on the generative tasks.

The second finding is the one that binds a plan. There is no QLoRA for these models. The study's 4-bit arm simply does not exist for either Mamba, because no available library supports their native quantization — the authors attribute this to selective-scan feature maps being highly sensitive and to large activation outliers not present in transformer attention modules. Read against activation-outliers-are-the-barrier-to-w8a8 and rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free, this is a familiar obstacle in an unfamiliar place, and the transformer's standard remedy — rotate to delete the outlier basis — has never been tried on a scan. Until it is, the single most reliable memory lever this vault has does not apply to this architecture family.

The honest counterweight, because it is the reason anyone would still choose one: peak VRAM does not follow parameter count, and the SSMs often win it. Mamba-2-1.3B uses 7,680 MB on LaMP-1 against Qwen3-1.7B's 19,098 MB, and on STS-B the smallest model in the study, TinyLlama-1.1B, peaks highest at 24,209 MB against Mamba-1.4B's 19,158 and Mamba-2's 16,998. So in a hard memory ceiling an SSM can be the model that fits — it will simply take longer and cost more energy to get there, and you cannot quantize it.

Scope: two Mamba checkpoints, sub-2 B, one card, short sequences, an immature software ecosystem the authors flag explicitly as a cause. Every number here is a statement about kernels and libraries in 2026-08, not about the architecture, and it is the kind of claim this vault expects to expire.
