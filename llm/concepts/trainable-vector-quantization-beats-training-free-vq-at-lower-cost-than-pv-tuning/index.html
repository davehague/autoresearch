Vector quantization's codebook-and-index assignment step does not have to stay training-free just because it is discrete -- a straight-through estimator makes it trainable against a distillation objective, and that buys most VQ schemes a real accuracy gain at a fraction of the one existing training-based method's cost, except on the one model size (8B) a local deployment actually wants

TL;DR: This map's vector-quantization lineage (AQLM, QuIP#, VPTQ, QTIP) has optimized a layer-wise reconstruction objective because the codebook/index step is discrete and therefore not naturally differentiable; PV-tuning is the map's one prior training-based exception, at very high cost (1,500+ A100-hours for a single 70B model). This source generalizes the training-based approach to any existing codebook scheme via a straight-through estimator, and the result both extends and complicates this map's allocation-vs-training-based-correction story: TVQ+QTIP's win on Llama3-70B (72.8 vs. VPTQ's 70.9, narrowing the FP16 gap from 4.5 to 2.6 points) is a genuine improvement over the best prior training-based number, at roughly a third of PV-tuning's cost -- but on Llama3-8B, the model size closest to what a local/consumer setup would actually quantize, training-free QTIP alone (66.9) beats TVQ+QTIP (65.0). That is the first result in this map where a training-based correction loses to its own training-free starting point, on exactly the scale this vault's local-inference premise cares most about, and the paper reports it without resolving why.

What it says. TVQ keeps continuous latent weights W and a codebook C, recomputing discrete index assignments from them on a schedule (full recomputation every step is the dominant training cost) and passing gradients through the discretization via straight-through estimation. Training is two-stage: block-wise (each quantized transformer block trained to reproduce the original block's own output -- local distillation) then end-to-end (a smaller parameter subset -- codebooks, norms, LM head -- fine-tuned against a KL-divergence loss on model outputs). This generalizes across the map's existing codebook families -- BasicVQ (AQLM/VPTQ-style, unconstrained), QuIP#'s fixed E8P codebook, QTIP's bitshift-trellis HYB codebooks -- rather than introducing a new codebook of its own, so the contribution is specifically "make the assignment step trainable," isolable from "which codebook." At ~2 bits, TVQ+QTIP's Llama3-70B result is the map's new best training-based number; TVQ+BasicVQ's Llama2-70B result (69.5 at 2.07 bits) is close to the paper's own "near-lossless"/"~1% drop" framing, more so than the QTIP row on the same model (68.8, nearer a 1.4-point drop) -- an internal inconsistency this digest flags rather than resolves. Cost scales with which codebook TVQ is paired with (QuIP# ~104 A100-hours, BasicVQ ~262, QTIP ~440 for Llama2-70B at 2-bit) and stays far below PV-tuning's reported 1,500+ for its Stage 2 alone. The method keeps the underlying codebook structure, so it contributes no new inference kernel and existing QTIP/AQLM CUDA kernels apply unchanged at serving time -- a genuine "free" compatibility win this map has not had from a training-based method before.

Caveats. Only ~2-bit settings (2.00-2.07 bits) are tested; nothing here establishes the pattern at 3-4 bits, where training-free methods already approach lossless and a training-based correction may have much less to buy. The Llama3-8B loss against plain QTIP is the paper's one clean counter-example to its own headline and is reported, not explained -- this map should read TVQ's gain as model-size- and codebook-dependent, not as a uniform improvement, until that result is understood. Evaluation is zero-shot QA plus perplexity only (no reasoning, code, or batch-1 throughput measurement), and perplexity is explicitly noted by the paper's own authors to diverge from downstream accuracy in at least one case (QuIP# on Llama3-70B) -- a caution this map has independently accumulated before. No code release is stated. Untested against this map's other 2026-10/11 arrivals that target the same "where does the optimization objective get its signal" question via a different mechanism (Hessian-guided joint range/transform/rounding choice) -- whether a Hessian-guided transform choice composes with or is subsumed by TVQ's trainable-assignment approach is open.
