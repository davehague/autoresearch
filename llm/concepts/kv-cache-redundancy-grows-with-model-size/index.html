The bigger the model, the cheaper its cache is to compress

AnchorKV reports its headline at three sizes and does not comment on the trend, which is the most interesting thing in the table. At a fixed 20× compression ratio, the fraction of full-cache quality retained reads 93.5% at 8B, 95.3% at 24B, 99.3% at 70B. LongBench moves the same way (94.1% → 98.4% across the two Llamas). At 70B the compressed model is effectively indistinguishable from the uncompressed one on the benchmark that most reliably punishes cache damage.

If this is real rather than a property of one method, it inverts the intuition a local practitioner runs on. Every other compression lever in this vault gets harder on bigger models or, at best, stays flat: silent-failures-quantized-reasoning and four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer describe weight quantization's damage concentrating on the reasoning tasks big models are chosen for, and the-lm-head-is-the-tensor-four-bit-quantization-skips describes a tensor that has to be exempted. A cache method that gets safer with scale would compose unusually well with at-a-fixed-footprint-take-the-larger-quantized-model — the standing rule on local-inference that at a fixed VRAM budget the larger, more aggressively quantized model usually wins. If the same holds for the cache, the two levers point the same direction, and the long-context configuration a 24 GB owner can reach gets meaningfully better than a naive extrapolation from 8B numbers suggests.

The mechanism would be geometric and is not measured here: a wider model has more KV heads and higher head dimension, so a fixed number of anchor directions spans a proportionally smaller subspace, and residual energy after projection is a smaller fraction of the whole. That is a hypothesis with an obvious test — hold the method and ratio fixed and sweep size within one family — and it is cheap.

Graded as an open question, not a finding. Three points across two families at one ratio, from a paper that is not making this claim, with no controlled scaling arm and no error bars. Compression quality here is also task accuracy throughout, the instrument accuracy-is-an-asymmetric-diagnostic-for-a-compressed-cache holds to be inverted on reasoning workloads — so the trend could be a scale trend in answer recoverability rather than in cache fidelity, which would make it much less useful than it looks.
