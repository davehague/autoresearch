Two groups, one unreported knob

quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model was flagged on 2026-07-25 as a candidate fifth failure mode for evaluation-contamination — recipe leakage, where the number is honest, the protocol is reproducible, and an undisclosed model preparation step makes two published results incomparable. It was held at one paper in one setting, which was the right call and the reason it was never promoted to a mode.

It now has a second measurement, from an unrelated group, at ICML, on the same model family, in the same direction, and larger. Fix Mixtral-8×7B, fix the quantizer, fix the bit budget, and change only the calibration mix from 128 C4 sequences to 128 C4 plus 128 MATH sequences: GSM8K moves 31.77 → 42.30 at 2.5 bits per expert and 12.89 → 23.12 at 2.0. The competing method moves too (33.06 → 41.93), which matters — this is a property of calibration-based quantization, not of one method's sensitivity.

Two details make this the stronger of the two measurements.

It is not an ablation the authors were forced into; it is one they volunteered against their own interest. The C4-only column is where their method loses to the layer-local baseline on GSM8K (31.77 against 33.06), and they say why: their formulation "does not heavily rely on heuristic regularization," so it has less to fall back on when the calibration distribution misses the target domain. Publishing the condition under which your method is worse is the behaviour this map's positive-exemplar entry (open-vlms-are-mostly-distilled-closed-vlms cites Molmo for the same reason) exists to reward.

And the effect is on a task benchmark, not perplexity. The original measurement already established that perplexity does not catch it. Here the perplexity columns barely move under the calibration swap (WikiText2 5.10 → 5.20 for the baseline, 5.03 → 5.18 for the new method) while GSM8K moves ten points — so the two instruments disagree by an order of magnitude on the same intervention. That is a fifth independent failure of perplexity as a selection criterion, this time not against a format choice (perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats) but against a preparation choice.

What this changes. The operating rule the map stated on 2026-07-27 — "a decontamination claim absent its method is uninformative" — has a sibling that can now be stated with two independent legs under it: a quantized model's benchmark score is uninterpretable without its calibration corpus. Almost nobody reports one. That covers every GPTQ-family number in post-training-quantization, including the 4-bit results the map is built on, which were produced with a corpus that was usually WikiText2 or C4 and rarely named as a condition.

It also sharpens the general form the map flagged on 2026-07-30, when recipe leakage reached three instances across three literatures (quantization calibration, an ablation's compute budget, a baseline's learning rate). This is a fourth, and the first that is a direct replication of an earlier instance rather than a new literature — which is what the flagged mode needed to stop being a candidate. The open question is unchanged and now more pressing: whether this is a fifth failure mode alongside leakage, construct mismatch, infrastructure non-equivalence and judge bias, or a meta-rule sitting above all four — a comparison absent its preparation protocol is uninformative.

Bound: one model family (Mixtral-8×7B), one quantizer, one target benchmark (GSM8K) in the calibration table, and the "fix" is domain-matched calibration data, which is only available if you know the target domain in advance — so this measures the size of the confound without supplying a general remedy.
