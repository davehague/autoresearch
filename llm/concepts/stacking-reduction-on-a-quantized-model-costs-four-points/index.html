Two free things are not a free thing

local-inference has a standing finding that local decode levers contend for the same headroom — speculation-and-quantization-compete-for-the-same-headroom is the sharpest version, and speculations-transient-memory-evicts-the-prefix-cache the most mundane. Both are about memory contention. This is the same shape in the quality currency, and it is the cheapest demonstration of it in the vault.

Attention-side arithmetic reduction at retention 0.8 is close to free on an unquantized model: it holds the dense summarization baseline at every scale from 1B to 70B and moves RULER long-context scores by at most 0.6 points. Apply the identical intervention to an INT8 LLaMA 3.1 8B and COPA falls 81.40 → 77.40. Four points, for a lever that was measured as roughly free one table earlier. At retention 0.5 it is 8.4.

The mechanism is the one quantization-damage-has-a-task-term-not-just-a-weight-term describes. A quantized model has already spent part of its signal-to-noise margin; the second intervention removes activation energy from what is left, and the two errors do not add in the currency either was measured in. quantization-multiplies-the-decision-margin says the same thing in the form that predicts this outcome — compression damage is multiplicative in the margin, so a model with a thinner margin loses more from an identical perturbation. small-models-amplify-quantization-error-large-ones-attenuate-it is the scale version of the same asymmetry.

The practitioner rule is a measurement rule, and this vault should apply it to every stacking claim it holds. The headroom for a second compression is a property of the already-compressed model. Nothing in this vault's 4-bit default, KV-quantization ladder or sparsity results was measured on top of another lever, so every "these compose" statement in the local stack — 4-bit weights plus KV quantization plus speculative decoding plus vision-embedding caching — is currently an inference from single-lever measurements. The one place a composition was measured directly, it cost four points on a benchmark where the single lever cost nothing.

Graded single-study and it should be read as a flag rather than a number: one benchmark (COPA, which is small and noisy), one model, one format (INT8, not the 4-bit width this map is organized around), attention side only, and the paper does not report the unquantized COPA arm — so the 4.0 points are measured against the INT8 baseline rather than decomposed into what each lever cost. The run that would settle it is a 2×2: dense and INT4, each with and without reduction, on a task with enough items to carry an interval.
