Training into the deployment format beats choosing a better one

post-training-quantization has spent a month recording ways to spend a bit budget better — where the bits go (channels, layers, experts, the head), which solver computes the allocation, which calibration set to use. Every one of those is an operation on a finished checkpoint. quantization-can-be-a-training-decision-not-a-post-hoc-one was the exception, and it closed with a complaint: Kimi K3's MXFP4 quantization-aware training is a microscaling block format with datacenter hardware support, "unreachable from the local stack."

This is the same move landed at the other end of the hardware range. The deployment format is Q4_0 GGUF — the oldest, simplest entry on llama.cpp's menu, the one everyone is told to skip. The deployment targets are a MacBook, a mini-PC, a Galaxy S26 Ultra and a Raspberry Pi 5. The artifacts are ordinary GGUF files, no custom kernel and no runtime patch. And the four models retain 96.5–97.4% of their BF16 in-format ceiling.

The consequence for practice is a small inversion, and it is the reason this note is graded pushes-back rather than extends. the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff established the ladder a local user actually chooses from, and the rule that fell out of it — take the highest rung that fits — is what this vault has been carrying. That rule is a statement about post-training quantization. Once the model has been trained inside Q4_0's grid, the extra bits in Q5_K_M buy nothing measurable, and Q4_0's better-optimised dot-product kernels are free throughput: 4–33% at 230M–350M, 3–14% at 1.2B–2.6B. The ladder ranks formats for a fixed training run. Change the training run and the ranking changes.

Two limits keep this from moving the map's status.

The method is not disclosed. "A high-precision teacher model is distilled into a quantized student model" is the whole recipe as published — no loss, no teacher identity, no token budget, no statement of how the 4-bit grid is simulated during training, no tensor list, no block size. The benchmark and throughput tables are images, so only the four retention percentages and the two throughput ranges are quotable, and "within evaluation variance" carries no interval.

And the scale is the one where the effect should be largest. small-models-amplify-quantization-error-large-ones-attenuate-it is among this map's better-replicated findings: sub-billion models are exactly where 4-bit hurts most, which is exactly where a training-time repair has the most room to show a win. Every model here is between 230M and 2.6B. Nothing establishes that quantization-aware distillation is worth its training cost at 8B or 30B, where the post-training gap it repairs is already inside benchmark noise — which is where most local users sit.

What it does establish, and what the map did not have before: quantization-aware training is now reachable from the local stack, in a format a llama.cpp user already runs.
