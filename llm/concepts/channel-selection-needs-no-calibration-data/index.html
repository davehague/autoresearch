A second independent instance of "calibration data isn't needed for MoE routing/execution decisions" — this time for which intermediate channels of a routed expert to compute, not which experts or how many bits — and the reason calibration fails is a general one: offline-fit thresholds don't track per-token, per-length variation in what a decode step actually needs

TL;DR: This map's standing finding that MoE bit-allocation doesn't need calibration data (AlphaQ, which ranks layers by weight-spectrum power-law tail exponent with zero calibration sequences) gets an independent confirmation in a different sub-problem: KAIST/DeepAuto.ai show that channel selection — deciding which of a routed expert's intermediate-dimension channels are worth computing for a given token, the lever this map's edge-I/O-bottleneck thread has not previously addressed at this granularity — also doesn't need it, and shows concretely why calibration fails here: a threshold calibrated to hit a target read budget on offline data does not actually control how many channels a live token reads, because that count is itself input- and length-dependent in a way a fixed cutoff cannot track.

What it says.

The failure mode. Channel selectors score each candidate channel's expected contribution to the output and keep those above a threshold calibrated offline (on UltraChat) to hit a target read budget. The paper measures, isolating the keep rule from the score, that the number of channels clearing a fixed threshold drifts with input: on held-out chat the middle 90% of Qwen3-Thinking tokens keep 9.0-16.2% of channels under a threshold calibrated for ~12%, and on the model's own benchmark answers the median kept count falls further as the answer gets longer — to 71% (Qwen3-Thinking), 92% (Gemma 4) and 88% (Qwen3.5) of the calibrated count by ~6,000 tokens into generation. Across 72 benchmark runs made in this paper, both a prior calibrated selector (Prox) and the paper's own calibration-free reader (run with calibrated per-layer thresholds for comparison) read below their calibrated target in every single run.

The comparison. Isolating the keep rule (threshold vs. top-k) on the same underlying channel-importance score, across four MoE models and three channel scores, top-k ties or beats the calibrated-threshold version in 40 of 43 paired comparisons, with no model favoring the threshold version when results are pooled. The sharpest instance: on Gemma 4 at a 13% read budget, calibrated thresholds trigger 32,768-token repetition loops in generation (145/792 to 674/792 answers hitting the length limit depending on selector), which top-k on the identical score avoids entirely — a collapse that looks like a cliff (accuracy drops sharply once error sparsity crosses a model-dependent level) rather than a gradual degradation, and is invisible to a deployer at calibration time because the threshold's actual reads at deployment are unknown in advance.

The calibration-free reader. Having established top-k as the better keep rule, the paper builds a selector that is calibration-free in the stronger sense of fitting nothing to the target model at all: a rotated ternary sketch (randomized Hadamard rotation in 128-coordinate blocks, then ternary {-1,0,1} codes plus an FP16 scale, ~7% memory overhead) screens every candidate channel; the kept channels' scores are computed exactly from the native weights, in output units (weighted by router weight and down-projection norm) so channels from different experts are directly comparable; skipped channels' contribution is recovered via a rank-32 low-rank map built once from each layer's down-projection columns. All three settings (block size, screen width, recovery rank) were chosen once on OLMoE and reused unchanged on every other model. Result: 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads, the most accurate of six tested selectors at both of the two sparsity levels evaluated, and MoE-block decoding up to 2.69x faster than optimized vLLM.

Caveats. Whole-model speedup (1.15-1.88x) trails the block-level speedup (1.60-2.69x) because fixed per-step costs (kernel launch) don't shrink with fewer reads — this map's recurring lesson that footprint and measured speed are different claims, now reconfirmed on a channel-level lever rather than a bit-level one. Tested on MoE models up to 35B parameters; the partial-screen coordinate-selection rule was chosen once (on OLMoE) and validated on only one other model (Gemma 4). No composition tested against this map's quantization levers or against CRISP's combination-aware pruning (crisp-combination-aware-pruning-plus-scheduling-for-edge-moe, same night) — the two attack an overlapping I/O bottleneck by different mechanisms (per-token channel selection vs. per-combination structural pruning) and neither paper cites the other. Code released: github.com/AidenGeunGeun/calibration-free-moe.
