A repair pass that doesn't need a better importance score, because it isn't scoring importance at all

Given attention-magnitude-does-not-predict-a-tokens-causal-contribution's finding that attention magnitude barely correlates with what actually matters for the answer, the obvious next move is a better importance score. TwinKV takes a different one: it skips scoring importance and instead scores redundancy — is this key's information duplicated elsewhere in the cache? A key with no duplicate (orphan) is protected; a key whose information survives via a near-duplicate elsewhere (redundant donor) is a safe swap target. Both judgments are made without touching attention weights at all (training-free, attention-free), and the pass composes with whatever eviction policy already decided the token budget — it repairs the policy's choices in place rather than proposing a competing policy.

That composability is the useful design choice for this map. Every other repair-style entry here (residual reconstruction, an adapter that repairs what eviction deleted) also layers onto an existing policy rather than replacing it — an-adapter-can-repair-what-eviction-deleted, what-eviction-deletes-is-two-sums-not-a-set-of-tokens — so TwinKV joins a real, if small, family of "fix it after the eviction policy runs" methods, distinguished from this family by needing no training and no attention scores at all, which is cheaper to deploy than an adapter and orthogonal to the-attention-backend-decides-whether-cache-compression-pays's finding that attention-scored policies fight optimized kernels — a redundancy signal computed from the keys themselves sidesteps that fight entirely.

The results are honestly mixed, and that is the useful part. Gains concentrate on Llama-3.2-1B and on RULER; on Qwen3-4B the benefit depends heavily on which base eviction policy is being repaired, and one policy already near its ceiling has little slack left for a repair pass to recover — the same "near- optimal top-k leaves little headroom" argument keeping-the-largest-attention-weights-is-already-near-optimal made about ranking quality applies here to repair quality. Few-shot classification shows no benefit at all, which is the clean negative result: a repair pass has nothing to contribute when the eviction decision it's patching was never the task's bottleneck.

Discounts. Abstract-level digest — exact per-configuration numbers, which specific attention-score eviction baselines were repaired, and MMLU-Pro's role are not yet pulled from the PDF. Graded suggestive pending that follow-up pass; the mixed, policy-dependent result pattern is itself well-supported even without the missing numbers.
