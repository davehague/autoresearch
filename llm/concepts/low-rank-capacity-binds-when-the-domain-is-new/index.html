The low-rank budget is a real ceiling — it binds exactly when the domain is new

The first controlled LoRA-vs-full-fine-tuning comparison in the vault, and it splits the map's founding claim along a seam nobody had measured: which regime the fine-tune is in. Biderman et al. hold model (Llama-2 7B/13B), domain (programming, mathematics), and data regime (instruction tuning ~100K pairs; continued pretraining ~20B tokens) fixed and vary only the adaptation method. LoRA in standard low-rank settings loses on the target domain, and it loses most where the target is farthest from what the base model already knows — code more than math, continued pretraining more than instruction tuning.

The proposed mechanism makes the boundary predictable rather than empirical: full fine-tuning learns perturbations of rank 10–100× greater than the ranks people actually configure. LoRA's original result rests on the observation that task-adaptation updates are rank-deficient (low-rank-adapters-match-full-finetuning) — and that observation is true for task adaptation. It does not transfer to acquiring a body of knowledge, because that update is not rank-deficient.

This pushes back on the map's headline without refuting the source it came from. Both claims survive under a scope restriction: LoRA matches full FT on task adaptation; it does not on domain acquisition. The map's practical ordering survives too, but conditionally — the 4-bit-base-LoRA-on-one-card default (quantized-bases-preserve-finetune-quality) is the right starting point when you are teaching a task interface, and the first thing to question when you are teaching new knowledge. The authors do sweep rank and find higher ranks recover some of the gap, so the honest reading is a statement about configurations in common use, not an impossibility result.

Companion finding: lora-regularizes-more-than-it-adapts — the same constraint that costs capacity buys forgetting resistance. Mechanistic companion from an independent group: intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting.

Rebutted by: the-minor-singular-directions-are-where-new-knowledge-fits — reports that a low-rank update confined to the weight's minor singular directions beats both LoRA and full fine-tuning at domain acquisition (39.2% vs 29.4% vs 30.4% on a 102-question held-out quiz over an injected history corpus), which would make the binding constraint the subspace the update occupies rather than its rank. Weak evidence — small unreleased eval sets, one scale, a single full-fine-tuning row — so this narrows to "rank may not be the operative variable," not a reversal.
