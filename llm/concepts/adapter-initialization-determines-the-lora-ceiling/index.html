Adapter initialization determines the LoRA ceiling — part of the gap is a warm-start problem

LoRA starts its adapter as a no-op: A Gaussian, B zero, and the frozen full-rank W carries everything while gradient descent hunts for a useful low-rank direction. PiSSA keeps LoRA's exact architecture but changes what the adapter starts as. Take the SVD W = UΣVᵀ, load the top-r principal components into the trainable A,B, and freeze the residual (W_res, the low-energy tail) rather than freezing all of W. The adapter now trains on the directions that already dominate the layer's function, from a warm, well-conditioned start — so it converges faster and lands higher. Mistral-7B GSM8K 72.86% vs LoRA's 67.7% (+5.16), consistent across 12 models (184M–70B), at identical param count and inference cost.

The reason this matters to peft-methods is precisely where it lands. The map went contested (2026-07-18) on two T1s showing low-rank adapters structurally underfit genuine domain acquisition — LoRA learns far smaller-rank perturbations and reaches a different solution (intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting, low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new). PiSSA answers the first half: a chunk of LoRA's shortfall is a warm-start artifact, removable for free. It does not answer the second half — it is still rank-r, still can't manufacture a rank-100 update, and it is evaluated on task-adaptation benchmarks (GSM8K/MATH/HumanEval), the exact regime where LoRA already matched full FT (low-rank-adapters-match-full-finetuning). No forgetting or new-domain measurement. So the net for the vault: if you use LoRA, initialize with PiSSA — it's free — but the contested finding about domain acquisition stands untouched.

Rebutted by: the-minor-singular-directions-are-where-new-knowledge-fits — argues the exact opposite subspace. PiSSA puts the adapter on the weight's principal singular components; MiCA puts it on the minor ones and reports better domain acquisition (39.2% vs 29.4% for LoRA on a held-out injected-corpus quiz). Both cannot be the right slice for the same target, and the two claims are evaluated on different halves of peft-methods's contested axis — PiSSA on task adaptation, MiCA on domain acquisition — so this may be a scope split rather than a contradiction. Nobody has run them head to head.
