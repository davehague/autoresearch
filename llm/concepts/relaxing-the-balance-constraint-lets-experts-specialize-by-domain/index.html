The routers weren't refusing to specialize; they were being told not to

experts-specialize-by-syntax-not-semantics is one of the better-supported beliefs in mixture-of-experts — two independent groups, one analysing expert assignment across Pile domains and one analysing token types, both finding routing keyed on syntax and position with near-uniform selection across content domains. There is no maths expert to extract and none to protect, which the map has used to explain everything from why per-expert mixed precision allocates by layer rather than by expert (moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity) to why route locality is short-range and predictable one layer ahead (expert-routing-is-predictable-one-layer-ahead).

Both of those groups trained with auxiliary balance losses. Balance losses of the usual sequence-wise kind require each sequence to spread its tokens evenly across experts, and a sequence is usually one domain — so the constraint directly forbids the specialization the analyses went looking for.

Remove it and it appears. Expert-load heat maps over three Pile domains — Wikipedia, Github and DM Mathematics — at two layers of a 16B model show the auxiliary-loss-based model near-flat across all three, reproducing the existing belief, and the model balanced by bias terms over the batch (balancing-scope-not-the-auxiliary-loss-is-what-costs-moe-quality) showing distinct per-domain peaks up to about ten times the balanced load, most sharply on maths.

How much this should move the belief is a genuine judgement call, and the honest answer is: reframe it, do not retire it. The evidence is qualitative heat maps at two of many layers over three domains, from a vendor report, against quantitative domain-level analyses from two prior groups. There is no lesion test showing the specialized experts are individually necessary — the standard this map already applies via fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy, where disabling the shared expert moves loss from 1.808 to 2.414 — and no measurement that specialization is what produces the quality gain rather than a side effect of it. What the vault can say is that the belief was stated unconditionally and is not unconditional: the degree of semantic specialization depends on the balancing scheme, and the two labs that measured its absence were measuring models trained under the constraint that suppresses it.

The interaction worth chasing is with local-inference. Expert offload and caching (moe-sparsity-becomes-a-transfer-budget-when-weights-are-offloaded) work because routing is locally predictable, and the mechanism was assumed to be syntactic — runs of a few tokens, forecastable one layer ahead, unstable over longer spans. Routes that key on domain would have a different and probably better locality profile for a single-user workload, because a conversation stays in a domain for far longer than it stays in a syntactic construction. Whether an aux-loss-free-trained MoE is easier to cache than an aux-loss-trained one is unmeasured and cheap to test.
