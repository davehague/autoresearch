The transport operator is the method, not the residual

The interesting thing about a-supervised-update-can-be-applied-in-logit-space is not that residuals help. Residuals are free — they fall out of one forward pass over the author's own writing, and shoving them onto the current prompt's logits is a two-line intervention that anyone could have tried. The paper's ablation says that version mostly does not work: strip the operator M and the average drops from 0.335 to 0.277, further than any compute or data reduction they test, and on the news-headline task the score roughly halves.

So the content of the method is the claim that a residual observed at one context tells you something about the logits at a different context, and that the relationship is estimable. That is the cross-prefix NTK, and the estimator — dropout-induced cross-covariance, M̂ = Σ_{t,s}(Σ_{s,s} + λI)^{-1} — is what converts an intuition about style transfer into an operator with a stated ridge parameter.

The ablation shape matters for reading the rest of the map. Halving the offline steps costs 0.024 average R-1; halving the author's data costs 0.026; removing M costs 0.058. A method whose gradient of performance with respect to compute and data is that flat, and with respect to one structural component that steep, is a method whose reported budget advantage is probably real and whose reported quality advantage probably comes from a single mechanism. Against energy-reorders-the-peft-methods-that-memory-ranks, which found the PEFT literature's rankings flipping depending on which cost you denominate in, this is the cleaner situation: there is one thing to price.

It also sets the bound on the accompanying theory. The estimator carries ridge λ for stability, and the authors state that convergence to the true cross-prefix NTK needs λ → 0 — which fights the finite-sample estimate that makes it work at all. Multi-step application additionally assumes the operator is time-homogeneous while the logits it acts on are changing. Both are approximations the ablation cannot separate from the operator's contribution, so "the operator matters" is well supported and "the operator is the NTK" is not.

Bounds. One ablation, one base (Qwen3-8B), three tasks in one family. No ablation of K (the dropout-pass count) or λ is reported, which are the two knobs the estimator is actually built from — so the study isolates whether the operator matters without isolating what makes it a good estimate.
