KV-cache approximation error should scale with the model's own decoding-step uncertainty, not stay fixed — and reasoning traces are redundant enough that a cheap recency-based penalty recovers much of what that costs

TL;DR: Behnam et al. first establish a mechanism this vault's kv-cache-management map had evidence for but not a measured cause of: when a reasoning model's KV cache is compressed by an attention-score-approximating method (RocketKV, which this map already knows as a hybrid sparse-attention baseline), the selection error stays about the same size at every decoding step, but how much that error actually damages the next-token distribution depends strongly on how uncertain the model already is at that step — nearly flat on confident steps, an 8x larger total-variation distance and a 65% flip rate on the most uncertain ones. They also show long reasoning traces are disproportionately the wrong ones: tail probability of generation length stays high for incorrect answers and drops fast for correct ones on GSM8K and AIME25, meaning a lot of cache budget on long traces is being spent on unproductive looping. RoCoKV responds with two training-free, nearly-free additions layered on RocketKV's existing attention-score approximation: an uncertainty-guided control that widens the retained-token budget when the smoothed entropy of the previous step's output distribution is high and tightens it when low (no extra forward pass needed), and a redundancy-aware score that multiplicatively discounts a cached token's selection score by its cosine similarity to a running summary vector of the most recent cached keys (cheaper than R-KV's full pairwise-similarity computation). Both pieces are fused into a three-phase CUDA decode pipeline — approximate scoring, top-k selection, exact sparse attention over the selected set — integrated into vLLM as a drop-in plugin that keeps the engine's existing paged KV layout, scheduler and CUDA-graph capture untouched, with the adaptive per-step budgets resolved entirely on-device so the pipeline never breaks graph capture for a CPU round-trip. Across GSM8K, MATH500 and AIME25 on two DeepSeek-R1-Distill models, RoCoKV beats RocketKV, RaaS, R-KV and SkipKV at every tested budget, with the largest margins on the hardest, longest-reasoning benchmarks.

Why it matters here. kv-cache-management already carries the 2026-08-06 lesson that a scored eviction method's accuracy rank can invert relative to its trace-fidelity rank, and separately tracks "reasoning traces are self-redundant" as a recurring theme (R-KV, RaaS, SkipKV). This source is the first here to turn the model's own output-distribution uncertainty into the compression-budget control variable rather than relying only on attention scores or recency, and to measure directly (via the paired exact-vs-approximate forward pass) that approximation error's harm, not its magnitude, is what tracks uncertainty. It's also one of relatively few sources on this map reporting accuracy and an end-to-end deployed-kernel throughput/latency number together, which the map's standing 2026-07-31 open item flagged as rare.

Caveats. Measured on an NVIDIA A40 (datacenter card), not a consumer GPU, though both tested models (7-8B) are small enough to run on one; no consumer-hardware number given. Both models are from the same DeepSeek-R1-Distill family; no non-reasoning or non-GQA model tested, so reliance on reasoning-specific trace redundancy is untested outside reasoning workloads. Code is released only upon acceptance, so not yet independently reproducible. Hyperparameters (uncertainty thresholds, redundancy penalty/window) are hand-set rather than learned; the ablation shows the method is fairly but not perfectly robust to getting the pruning direction wrong.
