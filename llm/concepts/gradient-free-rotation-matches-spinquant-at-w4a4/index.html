The map's rotation mechanism gets a gradient-free instance that matches its gradient-trained exemplar — evidence that what a rotation buys at W4A4 is explainable well enough to derive, not just tune

This map already organizes its allocation strand around rotation as a recurring mechanism: SpinQuant trains a rotation with gradient descent against a calibration objective, and the 2026-09-17 entry (structured-dct-transform-fixes-quip-family-numerical-instability) showed a structured-but-full-width transform (DCT) can replace a dense random rotation at lower asymptotic cost while preserving the same numerical behavior, because what matters is the rotation's mixing support, not its structure. This source adds a third axis to the same mechanism: whether the rotation needs to be learned at all. By decomposing the quantization error a rotation leaves behind into a piece recoverable by activation-guided weight compensation and a residual that is provably orthogonal to it, the paper derives closed-form bounds on the residual from outlier-channel statistics and uses those bounds to pick a randomized-sign Hadamard rotation plus an L2-derived per-channel scale — no calibration-objective gradient descent required — and the result matches or beats SpinQuant's own gradient-trained rotation on WikiText-2 perplexity across every one of eight tested models.

The mechanism-level claim worth carrying: SpinQuant's gradient training was previously the map's standing reason a rotation needs an optimization loop at all. This source's decomposition argues that loop was closing a gap that closed-form outlier statistics can close directly — the "activation-guided" half of the compensation names the same outlier-channel structure AWQ/SmoothQuant have managed empirically, and the "orthogonal residual" half is where a rotation's mixing-support argument (this map's 2026-09-17 finding) actually does its work. If this replicates, the practical upshot for a local practitioner is a W4A4 rotation-based quantizer that needs no gradient-descent calibration pass at all — cheaper to run than SpinQuant for the same or better fidelity.

Graded extends: this does not contest SpinQuant's numbers or this map's rotation-mechanism framing, it adds a gradient-free instance that matches the gradient-trained exemplar and explains why via the same-family error decomposition. Graded strong: eight models tested, a stated closed-form derivation rather than a tuned heuristic, and a consistent direction of improvement across every model — but discounted for no accuracy/downstream-task numbers (perplexity only in what was fetched), no code/weights release found, and no coverage of MoE or non-Llama/Mistral backbones, activation-quantization ablations isolating the compensation term from the residual term, or bit-widths beyond W4A4.

Rebutted by: none — this extends the map's rotation-mechanism narrative with a gradient-free instance rather than contesting a standing claim.
