The filter is a compression trick for a compute budget you can't afford

This map's position is selection dominates scale, replicated at three pipeline stages by three groups: data-curation-choices-are-ablatable-and-non-monotonic (FineWeb, 15T pretraining tokens), finetuning-data-quality-and-diversity-beat-quantity (LIMA, 1,000 instruction examples), filtering-a-generated-corpus-outperforms-generating-more-of-it (CoT-Self-Instruct, an RL prompt pool). None of those measurements is challenged here. What is challenged is what they mean.

The study fixes a Common Crawl pool and then spends more compute than one epoch over it — the high-compute, data-scarce regime, pushed to 121.6 epochs — across five model sizes from 15M to 7B, at a cost of over 20,000 H100-hours. Against five real filters, including DCLM-Baseline, which keeps one token in fifty.

In every configuration they can run, there is a crossover. The unfiltered pool starts behind and ends ahead. At 1B parameters on the 670M-token pool it reaches loss 3.37 and beats every filter once given enough steps. Fit the crossing points, extrapolate, and unfiltered data overtakes RefinedWeb at roughly 1e30 FLOPs.

That number is why the paper's title is a trap for a careless reader. 1e30 FLOPs is about 2,000× the largest training run in existence. At every scale anyone reading this vault will ever touch, filtering still wins, and this paper's own curves are the evidence. So the finding is not "stop filtering." It is that filtering is not correcting a defect — it is buying sample efficiency with a compute budget too small to extract the signal directly. A filter is a compression technique whose value falls as compute rises, and reframing it that way is more useful than the belief it replaces, because it predicts when filtering stops paying instead of asserting that it always does.

What makes this a genuine narrowing rather than a footnote is the methodological consequence. An ablation is by definition run at a fixed compute budget. If the ranking of filters depends on that budget, then every filtering result in this map is a measurement at one point on a curve the paper shows has a crossing on it — including FineWeb's, whose ablation models are the map's foundation. That is the same shape as the recipe leakage mode flagged on evaluation-contamination: an undisclosed preparation choice silently setting the result. A filter comparison run to a fixed step count is an experimental condition, not a fact about the data, and no filtering paper in this vault reports where on the crossover curve its ablations sit.

Bounds worth holding. The headline is an extrapolation three orders of magnitude past the largest experiment, which the authors call "speculative and large." The primary metric is loss on three corpora — the QA benchmarks in Appendix B (PIQA, ARC-Easy, SocialIQA) agree directionally but are "much noisier" — which collides with this vault's own rule that validation-perplexity-does-not-track-generation-quality, and consistency requires saying so even when the source is otherwise persuasive. Pretraining only: nothing here touches instruction tuning, preference data, or synthetic-data filtering, which is where two of this map's three sources live. Pools cap at 10B tokens, so scarcity is enforced by construction. And it is dense transformers with no curriculum and no data weighting, with mixture-of-experts and synthetic data named as unexplored.
