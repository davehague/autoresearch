The drafter-shape strand gets a control signal read from the target model itself, not from the draft — and it beats a black-box exploration policy trying to solve the same selection problem from outside

This map's founding equation is accepted length against draft cost plus verify cost, and its context-based-copying branch (Prompt Lookup Decoding, CopySpec) has always had one known defect: n-gram surface overlap is a noisy proxy for whether the model actually intends to reproduce a span, so a context-copy drafter regularly pays full drafting-plus-verification cost for a zero-token accept on "accidental repetitions" (the paper measures these as over half of all copy triggers in reasoning-heavy tasks). SwitchSD's contribution is locating a better proxy: copy-intent is a first-order, linearly readable feature of the target model's own residual stream (best read after the attention sublayer, peaking at an intermediate layer, degrading near the output as representations rotate toward vocabulary logits) — evidence read as an attention-driven induction-head mechanism rather than a feed-forward one. Reading that signal directly, instead of inferring intent from text-level heuristics, converts a brittle "speculate on any n-gram match" policy into a high-precision gate.

The comparison against BanditSpec is the map's sharpest new data point on the "how do you choose between drafting strategies" question strand 3 tracks. BanditSpec treats strategy selection as a black-box multi-armed bandit over observed throughput, which means it must explore — and on the one setting reported here that exploration is catastrophic: it over-selects copying 91% of iterations on a task where copying mostly fails (mean acceptance 0.28), collapsing to no speedup at all (0.99×). SwitchSD's white-box probe needs no exploration phase because it reads the internal state before drafting rather than inferring strategy value from past reward, and is correspondingly far more selective (8.9% of iterations) while landing far higher yield per trigger (4.45 mean acceptance) — a 1.48× speedup on the identical task. The general lesson for this map: when the choice is between draft strategies, a signal computed from the target model's forward pass it is already running is cheaper and more reliable than a policy that has to learn strategy value from throughput feedback over time.

Graded extends: this refines the context-copying branch of the map's drafter-shape equation with a better trigger signal — it does not contradict any standing claim, including CopySpec's own heuristic switch, which it is a strict precision upgrade over. Graded strong: three model families up to 70B, three benchmark domains, 9 reported configurations, both a neural-draft-agnostic design (SPS and EAGLE3 both tested as the fallback path) and a direct statistical-baseline comparison (BanditSpec) under matched conditions. Discounted for scope: coding/math/summarization only, no agentic or open-ended chat workload; the probe's own training data is itself LLM-generated (Claude Sonnet 4.5), which could bias what "copy-intent" it learns to detect; per-model layer/threshold re-fitting is required (not a transferable probe across model families); no code-release statement found in the sections read, unlike several of this map's other recent entries.

Rebutted by: none — sharpens the context-copying branch's trigger mechanism rather than contesting an existing claim.
