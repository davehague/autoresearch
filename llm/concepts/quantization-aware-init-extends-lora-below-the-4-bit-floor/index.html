Quantization-aware initialization extends LoRA below the 4-bit floor

QLoRA quantizes W → Q, attaches a zero-initialized adapter, and absorbs the full error ‖W − Q‖. Below 3 bits that displacement is large enough that fine-tuning starts from a broken model — and QLoRA simply fails to converge. LoftQ solves for the base and the adapter jointly, minimizing ‖W − Q − ABᵀ‖_F by alternating quantization and SVD of the residual, and hands LoRA that pair as its starting point. Memory, parameter count, and mergeability are unchanged (adapters-can-merge-away-inference-latency); it is a one-time preprocessing pass on the checkpoint, reusable across tasks, and T = 1 already captures most of the gain.

This is the second in-vault source for one claim, reached from the opposite direction, and the agreement is what matters. QPiSSA (quantizing-only-the-residual-shrinks-qlora-error) takes the SVD of W first, puts the principal components in the full-precision adapter, and quantizes the low-energy residual. LoftQ quantizes first and puts the SVD of the quantization error in the adapter. Two independent groups, months apart, land on the same load-bearing finding: the quantization error is not a fixed tax on the base — it depends on which slice of the weight you keep in full precision, and the adapter is that slice. Treat that as replicated (adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling).

They disagree about which slice, and nobody has run them head to head. QPiSSA says the adapter should hold what is hardest to quantize; LoftQ says it should hold what the quantizer actually got wrong. Those coincide only if quantization error concentrates in the top singular directions, which neither paper establishes — and their reported gains sit on different bases, tasks, and scales (QPiSSA at 70B, LoftQ at 13B), so the numbers are not comparable.

The consequence for post-training-quantization: at 2.5-bit the same base is either 19.39 ppl or 5.22 ppl depending only on how the adapter was initialized, so "QLoRA fails below 3-bit" was evidence about QLoRA, not about bit-widths. This does not overturn the 4-bit default — LoftQ's own 2-bit numbers degrade sharply (7.85 vs 5.24 ppl; GSM8K 20.9 vs 35.0), so sub-3-bit remains a genuine quality loss — but it moves the floor of quantize-then-fine-tune below where the vault had it.

Same limit as every other init result: evaluated on task adaptation only — no forgetting test, no distant-domain test, and no check for intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting in a LoftQ-initialized spectrum, even though initializing on the quantization-error subspace is exactly the intervention that might plant or prevent them. It raises the low-rank ceiling; it does not resolve peft-methods's contest (low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new).
