A drafter's acceptance rate is a claim about the temperature you ran it at

speculative-decoding has spent two weeks on the verification rule — lossless, truncated, blended, metered as a per-request budget. DBLAST points at the other side of the transaction and finds a variable the map has been holding fixed without noticing: the decoding temperature.

Block drafters (the DFlash / block-diffusion family) predict b positions in a single pass by factorizing them as independent marginals, q(x_{1:b}|y) = ∏ q_i(x_i|y). Verification does not work that way: it walks the block, accepting each token conditioned on the ones already accepted. Under greedy decoding this barely matters, because there is essentially one continuation and the marginals concentrate on it. Under sampling — which is how anyone runs a chat, an agent or a creative workload — the target is deliberately stochastic, several continuations are live, and an independent draft produces a blend of them that survives verification less often. Measured directly: accepted length degrades as target entropy rises, and a diagnostic binned by how concentrated the target's early block distribution is shows the deficit growing monotonically as determinism falls (+1.8% → +4.2% → +7.0% recoverable across the bins).

The fix is architectural and cheap: a categorical latent z gives q(x_{1:b}|y) = Σ_z q(z|y) ∏_i q_i(x_i|y,z), a low-rank mixture that correlates positions without serializing them, at +1.4% to +3.4% per-iteration latency and 5–7% more drafter parameters.

The finding that outlives the method is the underspecification. Every acceptance rate and speedup in this vault is quoted without its sampling configuration, and this says the number moves by ~10% across a temperature range people actually use. That is the same species of claim as cache-compression-must-be-scored-query-agnostic (a compression number is a claim about when you compressed) and the-attention-backend-decides-whether-cache-compression-pays (a throughput number is a claim about which kernel) — the third unreported-configuration finding on this axis in two weeks, and the first about the workload rather than the runtime. It also gives a-downloaded-drafter-is-a-bet-on-its-training-distribution a second dimension: a downloaded drafter is a bet on someone else's data and on someone else's temperature.

Note what it does not touch. The verification rule is unchanged, so this buys throughput without moving the output distribution — the property speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution makes load-bearing for every stacking argument on the map. Against lossy-verification-silently-replaces-the-target-distribution that yields a clean ordering: fix the drafter's distribution before relaxing the verifier's, because only one of those has a quality bill.

Discounted: no code, no stated affiliation or venue, one model family, and the headline metric is accepted length rather than wall-clock — latency appears only as an overhead table, and the hardware is described as "consumer-level GPU" with no model named, which is a wasted opportunity on the map whose longest-standing open question is what speculation costs on exactly such a card.
