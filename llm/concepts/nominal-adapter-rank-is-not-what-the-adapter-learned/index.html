Look inside the object instead of scoring what it does

peft-methods has known for a long time that rank barely matters, and every route it took to that conclusion went through training. lora-rank-has-a-matching-lower-bound derives it, adapter-rank-behaviour-is-small-real-and-method-specific measures ~1-point rank-dependent differences after learning-rate tuning, a-random-non-uniform-rank-profile-already-beats-a-flat-one finds a random allocation beating a uniform one at matched budget. All of those train something and score it.

This is a different instrument entirely: it opens adapters other people trained and published, reconstructs each module's spectrum, and asks how many components a null built from that adapter's own factor channels cannot explain. The answer across 684 modules is a median of one.

The rank sweep is the cleanest single result, because it varies only the nominal rank inside one model and one task: nominal 1, 8, 16, 64 → calibrated 0, 1, 1, 2.5. Sixty-four times the declared capacity yields two and a half statistically surprising directions. And the corpus is not selectively harsh — ten independently trained FLAN-T5-large GLUE adapters at nominal rank 16 all land at exactly 1.0, and one published RoBERTa text-classification adapter at nominal rank 4 retains nothing at all.

If that is what is inside a trained adapter, then the flatness this map has been documenting from the outside is not a phenomenon requiring explanation — it is the expected shadow of extra capacity that was never filled. The two literatures fit together: over-ranking-is-an-estimator-defect-not-a-lora-defect proves that a nuclear-norm-regularized estimator makes over-ranking harmless in theory; this shows that in the checkpoints people actually shipped, the surplus components are indistinguishable from noise anyway. Neither paper cites the other, and they were derived from unrelated mathematics.

The scope condition has to travel with it, because the tempting inference is not licensed. This is 26 checkpoints of weights, not 26 fine-tuning runs. Nobody retrained at a lower rank to see whether it landed in the same place. The paper's only task-level measurement is 24 GLUE-RTE examples, which its authors explicitly disown as a utility study. So the correct use is diagnostic: your rank-64 adapter probably contains rank-2.5 of signal, which is a strong prior for running the cheap experiment. It is not evidence that the rank-2.5 adapter would have trained there — and this map holds several reasons the trajectory is not recoverable from the destination, including adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling and the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank, where the rank you declare changes the effective step size that gets you anywhere at all.
