A failure with a location, which is why a cheap fix exists

The useful thing about the repetition failure in pruning-doubles-repetition-while-perplexity-and-accuracy-hold-still is not its size but its structure. Degeneration is not the model being uniformly worse at every position; it is a trajectory falling into a small recurrent set of n-gram contexts and failing to leave. That distinction has a consequence anyone can act on.

The decomposition: with L the repetition-prone region, R_T = P(τ_L ≤ T) the horizon-T entry risk and ρ̄ = sup_{c∈L} P(c_{t+1} ∈ L | c_t = c) the worst-case one-step persistence,

P(Coverage ≥ τ) ≤ R_T · ρ̄^{ℓ_τ}

ℓ_τ is how many consecutive in-loop steps are needed before a repeated n-gram covers enough of the sequence to count as degenerate. Entry enters linearly; persistence enters raised to ℓ_τ. So the leverage is entirely on the persistence side: you do not have to prevent the model from ever wandering into a loop, you have to make it 10–20% more likely to step out on any given token, and the compounding does the rest.

The two-token experiment is the direct test and the result is the number to remember. Locate the first loop onset, replace only the first two tokens with an alternative drawn from the model's own top-2 at that same prefix — so the continuation stays high-probability and reads as plausible, no sampling hack, no penalty — regenerate, re-score. CREP 5.9% → 0.7% under sampling; 26.6% → 10.8% greedy. Under nucleus sampling this is governed by escape mass: how the nucleus set S_p(c) divides between loop-continuing tokens and the plausible alternatives that leave the region. A model that has lost its escape mass loops; one that keeps a little does not.

Three reasons this matters beyond the pruning map it sits on.

It explains why token-level supervision beats sentence-level. The paper's own comparison has DPO and DITTO — both of which construct sentence-scale repetition data — reducing repetition only partially (CREP 7.3 → 5.27 for DITTO), while the onset-localized margin objective reaches 2.23 alone and 0.57 composed. If the failure is concentrated at a handful of onset positions, a sequence-level preference signal spends most of its gradient on positions that were never the problem.

It is a decoding-time result before it is a training-time one. The two-token edit involves no training at all. That leaves an obvious and unrun experiment: whether a runtime-side intervention — detect onset, resample from the second candidate — recovers most of the benefit without a recovery fine-tune. The paper does not test it, and for a reader running a compressed model locally that is the cheapest version of this fix by a wide margin.

It gives the greedy/sampling gap a mechanism. Greedy decoding sits at 26.6% degeneration even unpruned, and the two-token intervention only takes it to 10.8% against sampling's 0.7%. Greedy has no escape mass by construction — it cannot take the second candidate — so persistence is near 1 inside the loop region and the exponent works against you. This is the quantitative form of the folk knowledge that greedy decoding loops, and it argues that any evaluation of a compressed model run greedily is measuring a decoding artifact on top of the compression.

Scope. One decoding configuration per arm, no sweep over p, temperature or repetition penalty. The escape-mass quantity is not measured directly — it is the interpretation attached to an intervention that worked. And the bound is an upper bound: it shows why the intervention could be this effective, not that persistence is the only term that moved.
