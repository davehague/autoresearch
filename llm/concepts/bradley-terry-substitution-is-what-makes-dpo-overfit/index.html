The Bradley-Terry substitution is what makes DPO overfit

Every prior push-back on DPO in this vault was empirical and judged. This one is a derivation, and it names a defect in the objective rather than a shortfall on a benchmark.

The ΨPO framework writes preference learning as max_π E[Ψ(p*(y ≻ y'))] − τ·D_KL(π ‖ π_ref) for any non-decreasing Ψ. Both RLHF and DPO are Ψ(q) = log(q/(1−q)) — the Bradley-Terry logit — which is where the pointwise-reward substitution enters and where the damage is. That transform is unbounded: as a preference probability approaches 1, the implied reward gap diverges, and substituting into the closed-form optimum gives π*(y')/π*(y) = 0 for every τ. The KL term is not weakened by determinism; it is overridden by it.

The finite-sample version is the one that bites in practice, and it is worse. Preference probabilities in a dataset are empirical: a pair whose true preference is 0.8 but which was sampled three times in the same direction has p̂ = 1, and the objective treats it as certain. In a large action space with a small dataset — every real preference dataset — some pairs are sampled once or never, so this is the modal case rather than a corner case. The bandit demonstration is blunt: on a dataset where one pair is never observed, DPO drives that action's probability to zero anyway, at every regularization strength tested.

Operating consequence, which changes advice this map already gives: β/τ does not do what practitioners think it does. It is not a smooth dial from "stay near the reference model" to "follow the preference data" — under {0,1}-valued empirical preferences it is inert, and the early stopping the field treats as hygiene is performing the regularization the objective was supposed to provide.

The counter-intuitive corollary is worth carrying because it inverts the map's founding move. preference-tuning-reduces-to-classification records DPO's deletion of the reward model as pure simplification. This says the reward model was doing unacknowledged work: under {0,1} preferences the optimal reward is infinite, so a fitted reward model underfits, and that underfitting is a regularizer. Classical RLHF is more robust here precisely because its intermediate stage is bad at its job.

Scope, and it is a real limit: the theory is general, the demonstration is three actions, no context, no neural network, no language. Nothing here shows the failure at LLM scale, and the vault should not record "IPO beats DPO" as an empirical finding. The failure is also conditional on determinism — genuine annotator disagreement keeps preference probabilities away from {0,1} and the problem never binds. Practice has partly caught up independently (β-tuning, label smoothing, conservative DPO variants), none of it compared here.
