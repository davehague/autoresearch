Experts specialize by syntax, not semantics

The intuitive mental model — a "math expert," a "French expert," a "biology expert" — is what two independent analyses fail to find. Mixtral's routing across The Pile is near-uniform per domain (only synthetic DM-Mathematics diverges, mostly at first/last layers); the structure that does exist is syntactic and positional — indentation tokens, multi-token words, and consecutive tokens routed together (first-choice repetition ~28% vs 12.5% random at depth). ST-MoE, on encoder-decoders, found the same shape earlier: encoder experts specialize visibly on sentinel tokens, punctuation, verbs, proper names — token-type work, not topics — decoder experts barely specialize at all, and multilingual models grow no per-language experts (st-moe-stable-transferable).

Why it matters beyond curiosity: (1) it kills the folk theory that you can carve an MoE up by domain — e.g. extract "the code expert" for a smaller deployment, or fine-tune one expert for your domain; nothing in the routing supports that granularity. (2) The temporal locality is an engineering fact with two faces — exploitable for expert caching/offload in local serving (local-inference), and a load-balancing hazard for expert parallelism (distributed-training), since bursts of same-expert tokens oversubscribe one device. (3) It pushes back on reading fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy's "specialization" as semantic — the lesion evidence there shows experts become less mutually redundant, not that they align with human topic categories.

Caveat: both analyses are pretraining-corpus routing on the trained model; nobody in-vault has looked at how routing shifts under fine-tuning — the question this vault's premise would ask next.
