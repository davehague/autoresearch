Grouped-query attention leaves V spectrally unstructured

Among the five recurring patterns this source names across eleven models, two are about which projection holds what: "Q dominates spectrally" and "V is least structured under GQA." The numbers on LLaMA-2-7B put Q and K at roughly 88% of spectral energy in their outliers (1519 and 1517 of them) against V and O at 79% (1392 and 1393) — and V is singled out as lacking a clean signal/noise separation at all, rather than merely having a weaker one.

transformer-architecture holds that the KV cache is an architectural choice rather than a fixed cost, on GQA's evidence: sharing key/value heads across query-head groups buys 5.4× decode speed for 0.1 points. This suggests part of what that sharing does is push V into a regime where the spectral separation degrades — plausible on its face, since V is the projection being shared hardest.

The consequence points at kv-cache-management, and it is an inference this vault is drawing rather than a claim either literature makes. Every KV compression method in this vault treats K and V as one object: the same bit-width under quantization, the same eviction policy under token dropping, the same budget under low-rank compression. shrink-the-cache-by-bits-before-you-shrink-it-by-tokens argues about which axis to compress along and still compresses both tensors alike. If K carries cleanly separable structure and V does not, the two should not be equally compressible, and the per-tensor asymmetry is a free lever nobody has pulled. It would also give the map's central question — given a fixed budget, what do you keep — an answer on an axis orthogonal to both token selection and head partitioning (head-function-is-readable-from-the-frozen-query-key-spectrum).

Graded open-question and suggestive on purpose, because the gap between what is shown and what would follow is wide. The paper measures spectra and runs no compression experiment at all — the asymmetry is observed in the weights, and the claim that it should change a cache budget is unproven. There is an obvious confound the source cannot rule out: V's lower separability may be a property of what V is — a value-carrying projection whose output is a weighted average rather than a matching operator — rather than a consequence of GQA sharing, and distinguishing those needs an MHA-versus-GQA comparison within one family, which is not run here. The direction of the practical implication is not even fixed: lower spectral structure could mean V tolerates more compression (less to destroy) or less (no low-rank basis to exploit), and nothing here decides which.

The experiment is cheap and specific, which is why this is worth recording as an open question rather than discarding: quantize K and V at different bit-widths on one long-context benchmark, and check whether the optimal split tracks the spectral asymmetry. The infrastructure exists in every runtime that already supports KV quantization, and a negative result would be as informative as a positive one.
