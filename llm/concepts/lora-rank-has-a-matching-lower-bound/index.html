The rank you declare is a bill, and now it has a price

peft-methods has had four accounts of what rank does and no arithmetic. LoRA's own Table 6 said rank 64 was no better than rank 4. the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank said that measurement ran through a divisor that damps the adapter harder as rank rises, so the ceiling was a denominator. low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new said full fine-tuning learns perturbations of rank 10–100× greater than configured LoRA ranks, so rank is the binding constraint on new domains. adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact swept r=4→256 with the damping removed and reported only "subtle rank-dependent behaviors."

None of those says how much rank costs. This does: Θ̃(rd/n), with d the adapted dimension and n the number of training examples. The upper bound comes from a localized Rademacher argument under a local quadratic assumption on the population risk; the lower bound — the part the literature was missing — comes from a Fano-type packing of the rank-r subspace and applies to any estimator constrained to that class. Together they say the rate is a property of low-rank adaptation itself, not of a particular algorithm or a slack proof.

Read as a practitioner's rule, the shape is what matters. Excess risk has two terms: an approximation floor Θ(Σ_{i>r} σ_i²) from the singular directions you refused to model, and an estimation term Θ(rd/n) from the parameters you asked a finite dataset to fit. The first falls to zero once r reaches the intrinsic rank r*; the second keeps climbing. So validation loss is U-shaped in rank, and all three real fine-tunes show the U — DistilBERT/SST-2 minimizing at r=8, DistilBERT/MRPC and RoBERTa/SST-2 at r=32, each rising materially by r=128.

The consequence the vault should carry is a regime split that reconciles this with rsLoRA rather than contradicting it. rd/n is small when n is large: a 7B model fine-tuned on a real corpus, which is rsLoRA's setting, sits where extra rank is nearly free and the binding question is whether the adapter receives gradient at all. rd/n is large when n is small: 500 training examples, which is this paper's most significant cell and is also roughly the regime alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition showed is enough to align a 65B model. Extra rank is cheap on a big fine-tuning set and expensive on a curated one — and a practitioner with one card and a thousand hand-written examples is squarely in the second.

Bounds on the bound: the theory is well-specified (realizability assumed, misspecification named as unexplored), needs a locally quadratic landscape, and its lower bound is proved for squared loss rather than cross-entropy. The empirics are encoder-only at 66M and 125M on two GLUE tasks with a fixed learning rate at every rank, which is exactly the confound adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact identified three days earlier — so the U-shape is established at one step size, and whether it survives a per-rank sweep is unrun.
