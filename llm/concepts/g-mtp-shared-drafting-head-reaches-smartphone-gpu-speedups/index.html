A shared Multi-Token-Prediction head that reads the target model's KV cache directly, rather than paying for its own prefill, reaches speculative-decoding speedups on a smartphone GPU

TL;DR: G-MTP extends the Gemma 4 backbone with a single shared drafting head rather than a separate draft model, combining three architectural choices: target activation injection anchors the draft head to the target model's own hidden states; zero-copy KV-cache cross-attention lets the drafter read the target's existing KV cache in place, avoiding a second prefill pass and its memory cost; and a clustered vocabulary projection reduces the cost of the full-vocabulary softmax at draft time. The resulting speedups scale from up to 3.1x in large server-side deployments down to up to 2.3x on a single H100 for smaller models and up to 2.2x on a smartphone GPU — the first speculative-decoding drafter result in this vault measured on phone-class hardware. Separately, the authors supply a parameter-efficient fine-tuning recipe that keeps the drafter aligned with the backbone as the backbone's data distribution shifts, needing up to 1.83x fewer forward passes through the (expensive) target model than fine-tuning the backbone itself would require to reach equivalent alignment.

Why it matters here. speculative-decoding's hardware-portability thread has so far measured laptop-class and datacenter silicon; this is the first drafter evaluated on a smartphone GPU, landing directly on this vault's standing question of what runs acceptably on one or two consumer-grade devices. The zero-copy KV-cache cross-attention mechanism is also new to kv-cache-management's shared concern with prefill cost: rather than giving the drafter a separate KV cache (and a separate prefill bill) or quantizing an existing draft cache, it reads the target's cache directly in place, sidestepping the cost altogether. The PEFT-based drafter-realignment recipe is a new maintenance-cost result for peft-methods: parameter-efficient fine-tuning applied to keeping a draft head calibrated to a backbone that itself keeps changing, rather than to the backbone or a downstream task.

Caveats. No institutional affiliation or code/weight release is stated on the arXiv page, though the author list and Gemma 4 backbone are consistent with an internal Google evaluation; no independent replication is possible without a release. All headline numbers are "up to" figures for best-case configurations, not full sweeps across concurrency or output entropy — exactly the axes this map's other hardware-portability sources (e.g. LiLiCorr) show a headline multiplier is least portable along. No acceptance-rate or accuracy-distribution detail accompanies the speedup figures in the account available here, and the smartphone-GPU result covers only Gemma 4's smaller variants.
