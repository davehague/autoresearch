A third scoring signal for which parts of long-context attention matter — not attention weights, not lexical overlap, but how much a tile's local vectors change — cuts prefill cost near-losslessly and is itself cheaper to compute than its block-sparse-attention rivals

TL;DR: DTS (Delta-based Tile Scoring) is a training-free, content-adaptive method for block/tile-sparse attention at long-context prefill: it scores query/key tiles by how much their local vectors change ("delta") rather than by an attention-derived signal, computing these scores via boundedly-parallel chunk scans (parallel delta packing) instead of a full attention-like pass — addressing the fact that computing which tiles matter can itself be expensive, which is XAttention's (the main baseline's) own cost. Tiles selected as important are then attended to with their original, unmodified vectors. On LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, DTS keeps average RULER (to 128K tokens) and LongBench scores within 0.10 percentage points of full dense attention, cuts the scoring pipeline's own latency 38% versus XAttention at 128K tokens, and delivers a 3.3x prefill speedup versus a FlashAttention-2 PyTorch baseline at 512K tokens on four H100 GPUs.

Why it matters here. This map already holds HiSA (hisa-hidden-dimension-sparse-rope-attention, ingested 2026-11-20), a trained-from-scratch sparse-attention method that drops along the hidden/feature dimension of a RoPE dot product by a fixed positional rule. DTS attacks the orthogonal axis this map's convention assigns here too (per structured-pruning's own framing note, "attention sparsity lives under transformer-architecture"): which query/key tiles (token-axis blocks) get computed at all, training-free and applied post hoc to an existing dense checkpoint rather than trained in. The two compose in principle — HiSA computes a fraction of the dot product for every kept tile; DTS decides which tiles get computed — and neither paper tests the other's axis, so the combination is untested but structurally plausible rather than contested. DTS also gives kv-cache-management's standing "coverage beats precision, and selection signals are a live design question" theme a third scoring-signal family at the prefill stage: that map's eviction literature already contrasts attention-derived scores against a lexical retriever (BM25, which an oracle ties and attention-based eviction loses to on retrieval-shaped work) — DTS's delta/local-vector-change signal is neither, a genuinely new third candidate for "what should decide which part of a long context is worth full computation," untested against either of the other two on the same benchmark. Relevant to local-inference's standing complaint that prefill is compute-bound and under-optimized for consumer hardware, though DTS's own hardware (4xH100) does not close that gap.
