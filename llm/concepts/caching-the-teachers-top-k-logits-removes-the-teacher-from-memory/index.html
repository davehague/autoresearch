The teacher's job finishes before training starts

Knowledge distillation is normally run online: teacher and student both resident, both forward-passing every batch, the student's loss computed against the teacher's live distribution. That makes the teacher's parameters a permanent tenant of the training GPU, and it is the single largest reason distillation reads as out of reach for someone with one card — an 8B teacher plus a 3B student plus optimizer state plus activations, before any of the terms this vault normally counts.

The observation is that the teacher is a pure function of the input in the off-policy case. If the training data is fixed, its distribution over every token is knowable in advance, so it can be computed once and stored. Retain the top-K = 100 logits per token and train against the cache. The teacher never enters the training loop.

Measured on a single H200 at 8K context: peak memory 103 GB → 78 GB, iteration time 25.9s → 18.5s (~29% faster), throughput 237 → 331 TFLOP/s (~41%). Memory and speed move together because they have the same cause — a whole model's forward pass left the loop.

This is the lever the vault predicted three days earlier and nobody had taken. on-policy-distillation-runs-on-a-shared-high-probability-token-set measured that the overlapping top-k tokens carry 97–99% of both models' probability mass, and this vault noted at the time that "the tail is mostly wasted compute… a straightforward efficiency lever nobody has taken." Here it is taken — by a group with no connection to that work, for a systems reason rather than a dynamics one, and at a much wider retention (K=100 against that paper's k=16). The two are consistent, and they jointly point at an experiment neither ran: how far can K fall before quality moves? If 16 suffices for the learning signal, a 6× smaller cache may suffice for the storage, and that is the number that decides whether this is affordable on a consumer disk.

The cost it does not report is the one that decides its usefulness. Top-100 logits plus token indices, for every token of a full training corpus, is a real disk footprint and a real I/O bandwidth demand traded against the GPU memory saved. The paper never quantifies it — a conspicuous gap in a caching paper — and on a machine where the constraint is a consumer SSD rather than a datacenter filesystem, that trade could invert.

And the win is structurally confined to the off-policy stage. A cached teacher cannot go on-policy: the student's own rollouts are not in the cache, and generating them is exactly what would require the teacher back. strong-to-weak-distillation-is-the-small-model-build-path records the shipped recipe as two stages — off-policy cold start, then on-policy — so this accelerates the first and cannot touch the second. That is still worth having, since the same overlap paper found the cold start raises the initial overlap ratio by 15–20 points and lifts the final ceiling, but it means "distillation now fits on one card" would be an overstatement of what was shown.

Graded suggestive rather than strong on the quality claim specifically. "Near-identical loss" is a training-health signal, and this vault's most-replicated methodological rule — perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats, now failing in four unrelated literatures — is that a loss-shaped proxy does not license a model-selection conclusion. The systems measurements are strong and directly reported; the equivalence claim rests on the proxy the vault trusts least. Everything is also one teacher–student pair on 141 GB datacenter hardware, which the authors state.
