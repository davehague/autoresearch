Strong-to-weak distillation is the production build path for small open-weights models

Qwen3 runs its full four-stage post-training pipeline (long-CoT cold start → reasoning RL → thinking-mode fusion → general RL) only on the flagship teachers (235B-A22B, 32B), then strong-to-weak distills into the lightweight students: off-policy distillation on teacher outputs across thinking and non-thinking modes, plus on-policy distillation matching student samples to teacher logits. The report claims this "leads to better immediate performance… higher Pass@1… and also improves the model's ability of exploration… improved Pass@64," at "only 1/10 of the GPU hours compared to the four-stage training method."

This confirms the map's central result (distilled-reasoners-beat-direct-rl) from a second lab and a different base family, and crucially outside competition-math: where DeepSeek-R1 showed distillation > direct RL as a controlled ablation on AIME, Qwen3 reports the same direction as the routine way it builds an entire model family, with a concrete efficiency figure attached. Strength is suggestive, not strong: it is a T2 vendor report, the 1/10 figure is the team's own and relative to their own recipe, and distillation quality stays bounded by teacher quality and teacher-trace/logit access — the same leverage-and-limit R1 names. The thinking-budget / mode-fusion mechanism is an adjacent test-time-compute lever (post-training-rl) the vault has not yet mapped.

Rebutted by: distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank — the on-policy half of this recipe transfers distributional novelty, not teacher quality: a stronger same-family teacher produces a student indistinguishable from one taught by a weaker checkpoint, so "distill from the strongest teacher you can serve" does not follow from the result. Note the recipe itself survives — the paper's recommended repair (off-policy cold start before on-policy distillation) is the ordering shipped here, independently derived. Also dense-token-rewards-decay-with-trajectory-depth — the on-policy signal's per-token quality falls from +0.37 at a 1K prefix to +0.02 at 16K, so the technique has a horizon ceiling this report's scale does not reveal. Also on-policy-distillation-buys-sampling-efficiency-not-capability — this report's distinguishing claim is higher Pass@1 and Pass@64, and pass@64 is exactly where a controlled sweep to K=1024 finds the distilled student overtaken by its own base checkpoint (53.3% vs 70.0% at pass@1024 on AIME2024). Scope condition rather than contradiction: the number here is a pipeline result (off-policy cold start then on-policy logit matching) and the crossover is measured on the on-policy stage alone, which this report never separates — so it is not evidence about on-policy distillation specifically, and has been read here as if it were. See off-policy-distillation-transfers-what-on-policy-does-not.

Rebutted by: a-from-scratch-small-model-still-needs-a-teachers-output — a 1B model trained from scratch with no teacher in the gradient path beats the open 1B field by 18–32 points on its target languages, so the build path is not a training-procedure requirement. The scope condition that survives: 11.08% of that model's corpus is data generated by a 31B model, so the requirement is on where the information comes from, not on whether a distillation objective moves it.

Rebutted by: a-weight-merge-is-the-baseline-multi-teacher-distillation-must-beat — holds for compressing a parent into a smaller child, not for consolidating same-size peers. On a controlled three-domain benchmark, training-free task-arithmetic weight merging recovers 71.7% of the available headroom against naive multi-teacher on-policy distillation's 35.6%, and a fully repaired three-mechanism distillation recipe beats the free merge by only 0.78 total points after 300 steps on eight A100s.
