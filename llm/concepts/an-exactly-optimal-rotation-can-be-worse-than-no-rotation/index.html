An exactly optimal rotation can be worse than no rotation

post-training-quantization treats rotation as its most dependable mechanism. rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free gives the reason — the outlier basis is an artifact of the coordinate system, so change the coordinate system — and every result since has been about where to spend that freedom. This is the first source to ask whether the transform should respect the architecture it is rotating, and it answers by building the best possible version of that idea and watching it lose to nothing at all.

The construction is deliberately airtight. RoPE partitions each head into two-dimensional frequency pairs, and the per-pair planar rotations are the only single-head orthogonal maps that commute with it — the paper proves the converse, so there is no other candidate in the family. For the head-shared parameterisation it then derives the closed-form angle ϕ*ₖ minimising the larger channel variance under a stated surrogate and verifies numerically that the implementation attains that minimum. That separates "failed to optimise" from "optimised and it didn't help," and the result is the second: pairwise-only at ϕ*ₖ reads 10.6499 where the identity transform reads 10.5764. Doing the math perfectly is worse than doing nothing.

The diagnosis is a mismatch between three quantities, and it is the part that travels. The theory minimises a position-averaged second moment within each pair. The deployed INT4 quantiser sets its step from a tokenwise max−min range over groups spanning multiple pairs. A quadratic moment averaged over RoPE phases can fall while Δₜ,g does not move, because the two statistics apply different operations over different supports. The certificate is real and binds on the wrong thing. There is a conditioning problem underneath it too: at L = 2048, 17 of 64 frequency pairs have a near-isotropic surrogate covariance, so many angles are nearly optimal and the selected representative is decided by a small finite-sum remainder — a mean offset of 0.75 radians from a criterion that barely discriminates.

This is the third independent instance of one pattern, and three is enough to state it as a rule. perplexity-and-accuracy-disagree-about-where-to-repair found a cost-matched sweep where five weight-space repairs, including a random control, all lost to spending one more bit. a-route-flip-is-detectable-and-its-sign-is-not found a selective route repair whose oracle beat a rate-matched random control by +0.4% [−1.8, +2.8]. Now an exactly optimal rotation loses to the identity. Three mechanisms, three groups, one shape: a quantization intervention routed through a derived importance or optimality signal keeps losing to simply giving the quantiser more range. The derived signal is not fake in any of the three cases. It is measured against a statistic the quantiser never consults.

The practical residue is short. Do not accept an optimality argument for a quantization transform without checking what the quantiser's scale-setting statistic is — group size, per-token vs per-channel, dynamic vs static. And note the one place the ordering moves: an exploratory static per-channel control gives lower short-context perplexity for pairwise-only, reported single-seed in an appendix. If the transform is competitive under static scaling and hopeless under dynamic tokenwise-group scaling, the finding is about the quantiser rather than about RoPE, which is exactly what the surrogate-mismatch diagnosis predicts and nobody has tested.

Bounds. Perplexity-primary; the generation evaluations corroborate weakly and two of them do not separate the configurations. Four checkpoints from two pretraining families, and the surrogate optimum is evaluated on one of them. The head-shared restriction is the paper's own — a per-head angle was never run, so this rules out the evaluated parameterisation rather than the whole commuting family. Composing the pairwise rotation with the Hadamard is harmless (inside a pre-selected ±0.05-PPL interval on every checkpoint); it is substitution that costs.
