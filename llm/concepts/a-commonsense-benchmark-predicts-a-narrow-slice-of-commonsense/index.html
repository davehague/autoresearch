The scoreboard everyone reports is a narrow instrument with an unread label

An eight-benchmark commonsense average is the default headline of the parameter-efficient-fine-tuning literature, the mixture-of-experts literature, and most quantization papers that want a cheap quality check. It is treated as a general capability reading — a model that moves it has got broadly better.

This measures the assumption directly, using the one design that can: leave-one-family-out cross-validation. Train the predictor on five model families and predict the sixth, so the result is transfer rather than within-family curve fitting, with three non-commonsense benchmarks (BLiMP, MMLU-Redux, GPQA Diamond) as controls so "everything predicts everything" is detectable.

The picture is narrow and uneven. False Beliefs is predicted well (R² .483/.695, improving on controls by .766/.695) and so is TRIP (.451/.397). Presupposition is weakly positive on both metrics. Implicature and Indirect Requests are positive on macro-F1 only; TimeDial on accuracy only. CEI and Sarcasm are negative for every model. After FDR correction, no positive correlation survives for CEI, Presupposition, TimeDial or Indirect Requests.

The part that does the most damage is not the narrowness but the mismatch. "Transfer to particular tasks does not reliably follow the domains implied by benchmark labels." Social IQA does not preferentially predict social downstream tasks; Physical IQA does not own the physical ones. So the common practice of reading a per-benchmark breakdown as a capability profile — "this model improved on the social reasoning benchmark, therefore it reasons better socially" — has no support in the transfer data. The average is a partial instrument, and the components are not the sub-instruments their names suggest.

Graded pushes-back because it contradicts an assumption every source in this vault that reports a commonsense average relies on, including two ingested the same night. expert-capacity-can-be-decoupled-from-adapter-storage reports its entire headline as an eight-benchmark commonsense average with margins of +0.6 to +1.9 over its strongest baseline. alphaq-calibration-free-moe-bit-allocation was already flagged on 2026-07-25 for being "evaluated on the commonsense benchmarks least sensitive to quantization damage" — an insensitivity argument this now joins from a second direction, since a benchmark that predicts little downstream is not a good place to demonstrate that nothing was lost.

The rule this map should carry: a commonsense-average delta is evidence about that average. Converting it into a claim about downstream capability requires either a downstream measurement or an argument, and the vault has seen neither in any source that reports one.

Read against the vault's other proxy failures, this leaves the evaluation ladder with a hole in the middle rather than at the bottom. The standing rule that perplexity is a training-health signal and not a model-selection criterion has always had a "use a real benchmark instead" clause. This says the real benchmark, for this family of claims, predicts a narrow slice — so the honest ladder is loss → benchmark → downstream task, and the second rung is weaker than assumed while the third is measured by almost nobody.

Bounds worth holding. This is about commonsense benchmarks, not GSM8K, HumanEval or MMLU. Scoring is option log-likelihood, not generation. Several downstream tasks are small (CEI n=31, TimeDial n=60), so their R² estimates carry intervals the paper does not print, and the two clearest positives (False Beliefs n≈192, TRIP n≈100) are the larger ones — which is either reassuring or a power artifact, and the study cannot tell you which.
