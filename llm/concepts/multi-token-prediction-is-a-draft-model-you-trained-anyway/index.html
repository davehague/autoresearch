The cheapest draft model is one you get as a training side effect

speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution is the only quality-free lever in local-inference: a small draft proposes γ tokens, the target verifies all γ+1 in one pass, and a modified rejection rule makes the output provably identical in distribution to ordinary decoding. What decides the win is the acceptance rate, and the in-vault figures sit at 0.75–0.82 for a separately trained draft, with the counter-intuitive tuning rule that the weakest usable draft wins because a strong one's own cost eats the winnings.

The map lists three standing objections to running this locally: batching erodes the win, the draft competes for VRAM with the target's weights and cache on exactly the device where memory binds, and draft and target must share a tokenizer.

Multi-token prediction removes two of them by producing the draft as a byproduct of pretraining. D sequential modules — each a shared embedding, one transformer block, a linear projection and a shared output head — predict the 2nd, 3rd, … next token while keeping the full causal chain at each depth, rather than predicting them independently in parallel. Trained with an averaged extra cross-entropy term, they are discarded at inference by default, so the trained model's serving cost is unchanged — or kept and used as the draft. The draft then shares the target's tokenizer by construction, shares its weights lineage, and costs one transformer block rather than a second model.

Measured second-token acceptance is 85–90%, above every separately-trained draft in the vault, for 1.8× tokens per second. That figure is the reason to record this: the map's sharpest open question about speculation is whether acceptance holds up in the configurations a local user runs, and this is the first in-vault evidence that the architecture of the draft can push acceptance up rather than the usual trade of accepting a weaker draft for a cheaper one.

The training-side result is a genuine bonus and independent of the speculation use. At two scales, with the modules discarded so inference cost is identical between arms, MTP improves most benchmarks and improves HumanEval by 6.1 and 9.2 points — a lever that costs nothing at serving time. Both scales also show one regression (NaturalQuestions at small scale, MMLU at large), which the report's "consistently enhances performance" summary glosses over.

Two things this does not settle, and they are the ones that matter for the premise. Acceptance is measured on a 671B FP8 frontier MoE served across hundreds of GPUs, not on a quantized 7B on one card — and the map's sharpest missing datum is precisely whether a quantized draft still agrees with a quantized target at the 0.6–0.8 acceptance speculation needs. Nothing here is quantized in the deployment sense. And MTP is a pretraining-time decision: you cannot add it to a checkpoint you downloaded, so for a practitioner it is a reason to prefer models trained with it rather than a lever they can pull.
