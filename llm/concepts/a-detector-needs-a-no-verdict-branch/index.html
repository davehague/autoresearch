A detector needs a no-verdict branch

Most of this map is about detectors that answer. This is about one that declines, and the decline is the most useful thing it does.

Two of six audits returned "no verdict, Requirement E fails." They were the two with the largest raw statistics the protocol measured — WikiMIA at +0.0498 and +0.0610 — and they were refused because a model-blind classifier separated those item sets at 0.855, so the exchangeability precondition the whole method rests on does not hold. A detector without a refusal branch converts that same input into its strongest positive. The refusal is not caution; it is the only correct answer available, and almost nothing else in this literature can produce it.

The paired discipline is on the negative side: a null must be reported with its detection floor. The instrument's power crosses 0.8 near a planted effect of ε ≈ 1.5 and it is underpowered below ε ≈ 1, so the four Pile arms that came back null mean "no depth-dependent familiarity signal is detectable at this sensitivity," not "none exists." The paper is explicit that no calibration exists to convert ε into a duplication count, which means the floor can be stated in the instrument's own units and not yet in the units a practitioner cares about. Saying so is the point.

This gives contamination-detection-is-a-prior-not-a-verdict a mechanism. That note says a detector's output should update a belief rather than settle a question; this shows what a protocol looks like when the claim is built into its output type — three outcomes, a precondition gate, a stated floor, and a four-cell table separating "seen but not exploited" from "inflation without memory" (exposure below the floor, format overfitting, or difficulty mismatch). The last of those cells matters beyond contamination: a benchmark score can be wrong with no leakage at all, which is the reading alignment-is-format-selection-not-knowledge-acquisition supports from the post-training side, and which this vault ingested a live instance of in the same run — see training-on-the-answer-format-is-most-of-a-benchmark-win.

The reason to believe the discipline is affordable is the cost line. The full control battery — the placebo split, the permutation null, the nuisance audit — costs 6.51 s plus 2.4 s for 2000 permutations, because the smoother is label-free and can be refit implicitly. Any probe without that property projects to 1.9 hours, a factor of ~2919, "and it is why the battery is mandatory here rather than encouraged." Rigour that is cheap gets adopted; rigour that costs two hours per audit does not.

Graded suggestive rather than strong: the reporting discipline is demonstrated on six small audits, not established as improving outcomes across a literature, and the "no verdict" branch is only as good as the precondition it gates on. Bounds: single author, no venue; 160M–410M scales; RSCP cannot run at all on a benchmark with no exchangeable twin, "which today is most of them," so the third outcome will fire far more often than the other two; and the whole protocol is white-box and defeated by an adversary willing to null out a linear direction while keeping the behaviour.
