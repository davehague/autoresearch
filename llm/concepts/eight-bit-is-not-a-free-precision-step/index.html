The free step was free because nobody ran the matched test

Eight-bit weight quantization occupies a comfortable position in this vault: the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff puts it at the top of the ladder where quality is indistinguishable from the source, and post-training-quantization recorded on 2026-08-14 that a whole FP16 → INT3 sweep costs 1.58 points overall with "INT8 a wash." Both statements were derived from unpaired comparisons of aggregate benchmark scores, and both remain true as statements about aggregate benchmark scores.

This source runs the same comparison with the design that a precision sweep actually admits. Because the trial sequence — subject, distractor values, order — is held byte-identical across FP16, INT8 and INT4 for a given seed, every comparison is paired at the trial level, and the appropriately matched test is McNemar's on the discordant pairs rather than Fisher's on the marginal counts. On the identical data, the unpaired test says INT8 is indistinguishable from FP16 in all three models (p = 0.837 / 0.410 / 0.197). The paired test says it is indistinguishable in one of three (Qwen, p = 0.629) and carries a real penalty in the other two (Mistral OR 1.84, p = 0.018; Phi OR 2.50, p = 0.004).

The paper's own framing changed as a result: it had described a clean cliff — FP16 ≈ INT8 ≫ INT4 — and revised it to a graded picture. That revision is the finding.

Two reasons to hold this as suggestive rather than strong, despite the significance levels. It is one task family (semantic proactive interference), where the same paper shows four-bit damage is unusually concentrated — so INT8's small penalty may be the tail of a task-specific effect rather than a general property. And it is bitsandbytes' LLM.int8() specifically, a mixed-precision decomposition scheme with its own outlier handling, not INT8 in general and not the GGUF Q8_0 this vault's local stack actually ships. Q8_0 is a different format with a different error profile, and nothing here measures it.

What survives regardless: the ordering is intact and the gap is large. INT4's odds ratios at the same operating points run 2.58–20.00 against INT8's 1.43–2.50, and every summary statistic in the paper is monotone in precision. Eight bits is still the conservative choice. It is just no longer the choice you make without measuring, and "INT8 is safe by default" should be retired as a phrase even where it survives as a practice.

This also sharpens a methodological point the vault should carry beyond quantization. The reason the field believed INT8 was free is not that anyone measured it and found zero — it is that the standard evaluation discards the pairing that a precision comparison naturally has. Every quantization sweep runs the same prompts through the same model at different bit-widths; that is a matched design, and scoring it with unmatched statistics throws away most of the power. It is the same shape as a-benchmark-score-is-one-draw-from-an-unreported-distribution — an evaluation practice hiding a real effect behind avoidable variance — and it is cheaper to fix than any of the methods in this map.

Rebuts: the "INT8 is a wash" reading attached to the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff, narrowly — on interference-heavy retrieval under bitsandbytes, in two of three models. The ladder's shape and its top rung's ranking are unchanged.
