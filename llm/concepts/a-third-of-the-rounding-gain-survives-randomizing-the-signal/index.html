The control that prices the prior

post-training-quantization recorded a rule on 2026-08-15 after its third independent instance: a quantization intervention routed through a derived importance or optimality signal keeps losing to simply giving the quantiser more range. The three legs are a cost-matched repair sweep in which a random-weight control tied five principled interventions (perplexity-and-accuracy-disagree-about-where-to-repair), an oracle route-repair gate beating a rate-matched random gate by 0.4% with an interval spanning zero (a-route-flip-is-detectable-and-its-sign-is-not), and a rotation proved to attain its own analytic optimum and measured worse than applying no rotation at all (an-exactly-optimal-rotation-can-be-worse-than-no-rotation).

revising-the-rounding-buys-a-point-without-calibration-data is the first intervention in this family to beat its matched baseline outright. This note is the discount, and it comes from the paper's own ablation rather than from a critic: matched random flips — the same number of rounding assignments changed, chosen at random, averaged over ten seeds — move group-wise RTN from 58.1 to 58.5 where the full method reaches 59.4. Roughly a third of the gain is available with no signal whatsoever.

Two readings, and both are useful.

The first is that the map's rule survives in weakened form. A learned prior over the model's own weights is worth about 0.9 of a 1.3-point gain — real, and about twice what randomness gets, which is a far better showing than the random-weight control that tied five weight-space repairs. The signal is not noise. But this is now the fourth time in this map that a random control has taken a substantial share of a result its authors attributed to their scoring function, and the pattern is consistent enough to be a methodological demand rather than an observation: any future rounding-revision, importance-scoring or repair result in this map should be read as uninterpretable until a matched random control is reported. ReRound's authors ran theirs, which is why this note grades strong rather than speculative.

The second reading is about the object being perturbed. If randomly reassigning midpoint-ambiguous weights helps, then the round-to-nearest pattern is not a local optimum at the matrix level — which is exactly the paper's stated premise, and the ablation confirms it from the other direction. That connects to a-random-non-uniform-rank-profile-already-beats-a-flat-one in peft-methods, where a random non-uniform allocation captured most of a learned allocator's gain, and to attention-splits-into-a-random-bulk-and-a-thin-learned-signal: three unrelated literatures in this vault have now found that a randomized version of a structured intervention captures a large fraction of it.

The other two ablation arms rule out the cheap explanations. Replacing the spectral candidate-selection criterion with element-wise weight MSE costs 0.8 points at 3 bits (54.3 against 55.1) — so it is not merely the candidate sweep doing the work — and replacing the position-dependent tolerance with a flat midpoint window also loses. Every component is load-bearing; the question is only how much of the total is signal.

Bounded the same way its parent note is: one model (OLMo 2 1B) for the random control, under 3B, four zero-shot multiple-choice tasks, no seed variance reported anywhere except in this ablation.
