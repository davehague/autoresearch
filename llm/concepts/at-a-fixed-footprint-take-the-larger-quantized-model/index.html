Spend the budget on parameters, then quantize

vision-language-models has carried "the edge half of this group is still empty" as a named gap since it was coined — quantizing and deploying a VLM had no sources. The gap partly closed on 2026-07-31 with lqa-quantized-adaptive-vlm-edge, which priced the encoder and left the generative half open. This closes the generative half, and it does it on the substitution a practitioner actually faces.

The framing is the contribution. A fixed memory budget does not let you choose model size and precision independently; it gives you a curve, and the three points on it are: a small model at full precision, the same small model quantized, and a larger model quantized into the same footprint. Measured on identical inputs, the third dominates:

2B fp162B 4-bit7B 4-bit
Accuracy0.9040.8880.935
Error-detection AUROC0.950.800.98
Risk–coverage area (lower better)0.0170.0480.008

Not a trade — a dominance. The larger quantized model is better at answering, better at knowing when it has failed to answer, and better at holding a deferral threshold calibrated on clean inputs when the inputs degrade.

This is the second in-vault source pointing the same way about the interaction between size and precision, and the two arrive in different currencies. vision-language-models recorded from LQA that a ~150M CLIP encoder falls off a cliff below 8 bits (60.59 / 42.54 / 3.02% at 3/2/1 bits) where an 8B language model shows a graded descent (the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff), and hypothesized small models quantize worse — which reads as bad news for edge deployment. Here the 2B loses 15 AUROC points to 4-bit where the 7B sits above the 2B's own full-precision signal. Same direction, generative model, confidence rather than accuracy. The hypothesis now has two independent supports.

The practical inversion is the useful part. If small-and-quantized is the bad cell, the answer is not to avoid quantization — it is to stop shrinking twice. The vault's existing claims (four-bit-weights-put-llms-on-device, the graded quant ladder) are about quality at a fixed model; this is the first measurement of quality at a fixed memory budget, which is the question someone with a 12 GB or 16 GB card is really asking. The answer, on this evidence: buy parameters, then pay for them in bits.

Bounds: two scale points is not a scaling law and the author says so; the 7B fp16 arm was never run, so the 7B's quantization robustness is inferred rather than measured; the task is a 100-item four-way classification with n ≈ 100 per condition and undefined AUROC wherever accuracy hits ceiling; one model family; single author, no venue. The ordering is a within-study comparison on identical inputs and is the durable part. The magnitudes are not.

Rebutted by: a-four-bit-replica-with-exact-rescoring-outscores-an-exact-scan — not on the capacity trade itself, but on the premise underneath it that full precision is the quality ceiling you are spending down from. Where the binding constraint is how much of the working set fits in the fast path rather than representational fidelity, a 4-bit path with exact rescoring returns higher recall than an exhaustive full-precision scan, and the crossover arrives earlier the smaller the machine.
