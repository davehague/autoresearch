One dial with an exponent on it, not five methods

This map has been treating adapter initialization as a menu. PiSSA takes the base weight's principal singular components (adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling). LoftQ takes the quantization error (quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor). MiCA takes the minor singular directions (the-minor-singular-directions-are-where-new-knowledge-fits). A third group of methods — LoRA-GA, LoRA-One, CG-LoRA — takes the subspace from the downstream loss gradient, and it is this group that turns out to be one method.

Under the Kronecker-factored curvature approximation F ≈ T ⊗ S, write the preconditioned gradient as P_{α,β} = T^{−α/2}[d_T^{β/2} ⊙ ∇_W L ⊙ d_S^{β/2}] S^{−α/2}. Then α is how hard curvature whitening rescales the gradient's eigen-directions and β is Adam-like per-feature normalization, and the existing methods are coordinates: LoRA-GA and LoRA-One are (0, 0), plain gradient; CG-LoRA is (1, 0), inverse-square-root whitening; the K-FAC natural gradient is (2, 0); Shampoo's quarter-power is (0.5, 0). Initialize the adapter from the top-r subspace of P_{α,β} and the whole family is one implementation with two knobs.

Sweeping it makes the design question empirical, and the answer is that neither published endpoint is right in most settings. On one 7B backbone the optimum moves from the raw-gradient corner on GSM8K to near-full whitening with diagonal correction on HumanEval to an interior point on MMLU. On the encoder, the selected α clusters at moderate whitening with β > 0, and three of five GLUE tasks land away from CG-LoRA's point.

Two things make this the most trustworthy initialization result in the vault. First, the protocol: every method and every grid point runs under a full learning-rate search — the discipline adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact demanded and none of the initialization papers this subsumes applied. Second, the paper reports the oracle and the deployable variant separately rather than quoting the oracle. ULoRA-Auto sets the exponents per layer and per Kronecker side from statistics the initialization pass has already computed — effective rank and condition number of the Ritz spectrum for α, off-diagonal energy of the projected covariance for β — at zero search cost, and it is the single best method on MMLU (45.43), where it beats its own grid-searched parent because the grid was constrained to one global exponent while Auto can vary per layer.

The accuracy landscape is the practitioner-facing part. Over α, β ∈ [−3, +3], negative α — which amplifies high-curvature directions instead of suppressing them — costs up to 15 points, while anything past α > 0.5 sits on a broad plateau where β matters mildly. So the operating rule is asymmetric: getting the exponent roughly right is easy and getting it wrong in the specific direction of amplifying curvature is catastrophic. That is a different shape of risk from the one this map has been reasoning about, where initialization choices were assumed to trade points against each other.

Bounds. No quantized base, so LoftQ's claim — that initialization decides whether 2-bit LoRA converges at all — stays untested here as everywhere else, and it is the one setting where this vault has evidence that initialization matters enormously. No domain acquisition: GLUE, GSM8K, HumanEval and MMLU are all targets the base was trained to do, which is the half of the contest where low-rank adaptation already worked. Rank fixed at 8, one epoch, models to 7B. The largest single gain (MRPC, 83.5 → 87.0) sits on the smallest and noisiest task in the suite, where vanilla LoRA's own standard deviation is ±1.6.
