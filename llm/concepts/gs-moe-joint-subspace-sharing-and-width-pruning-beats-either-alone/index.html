Two MoE expert-compression axes — shared subspace across experts and pruned width within each expert — are complementary when co-designed under one budget, not just stackable as separate post-hoc passes

TL;DR: GS-MoE (Florida State/UPenn/Notre Dame, code released) compresses MoE expert parameters along two axes at once — a groupwise low-rank subspace shared across experts in a group (cross-expert redundancy) and a Nyström-based intermediate-width reduction within each expert (within-expert redundancy) — with layer-level and group-level budgets both set by gradient-free importance/leverage scores rather than uniform splits. At 60% parameter reduction on DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base, it retains ~90% of uncompressed reasoning performance against 73% for the prior shared-subspace method (D2-MoE), with 20% lower out-of-domain perplexity, and a 3.32x decode throughput advantage on Mixtral-8x7B.

What it says.

The setup. mixture-of-experts's existing compression finding (composable-moe-compression-is-not-the-sum-of-its-levers, 2026-08-26) is about stacking three different pipeline stages — expert pruning, weight quantization, and KV-cache compression — sequentially, and finding the combination's damage unpredictable from any stage measured alone. GS-MoE's two axes are a different scope: both act on expert weights specifically, and both are designed jointly within one reconstruction objective rather than bolted together post-hoc. That distinction matters — this is not a test of whether independently-published levers compose; it's a test of whether two expert-weight-compression mechanisms, co-designed to share a budget, produce complementary gains. The two prior baselines it targets, MoE-SVD (one shared subspace, fixed) and D2-MoE (shared base plus compressed per-expert residuals), are each a single-axis answer — a fixed shared subspace restricts where expert-specific variation can live; width reduction alone still stores a full per-expert representation for retained neurons.

The method. Groupwise low-rank representations let the shared subspace vary across expert groups (not one global subspace), with eigenvalues and leverage scores from that reconstruction-error objective guiding which experts group together and how rank is distributed across groups. Separately, Nyström-based neuron compression reduces each expert's intermediate width. A gradient-free, per-layer importance score sets each layer's overall compression budget first; within a layer, eigenvalues and leverage distribute that budget as non-uniform ranks across groups and non-uniform widths across experts. No training or gradient computation anywhere in the pipeline — calibration only (512 Alpaca samples, 2048 tokens).

The result. On DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base (a fine-grained, many-small-experts architecture) at 60% total-parameter reduction: ~90% of uncompressed average reasoning performance across seven zero-shot tasks, against 73% for D2-MoE at the same budget, plus 20% lower out-of-domain perplexity. On Qwen2-57B-A14B (64 experts, top-8) at 60% compression, GS-MoE reaches lossless accuracy against baselines. On Mixtral-8x7B (coarse-grained, 8 experts, top-2) at 60% compression, decode throughput is 374.77 tok/s at group count G=1 vs. D2-MoE's 112.84 tok/s — a 3.32x advantage — though raising G from 1 to 8 trades some of that throughput back (226.81 tok/s on DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base at G=8), naming a real knob between compression granularity and serving speed that the paper does not fully resolve. Ablations (Table 2/3) isolate the contribution of each axis: grouped expert assignment alone drops WikiText perplexity from 29.49 to 25.23 on DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base; adding group-rank allocation on top improves it by a further 0.57 average across three datasets — evidence the two levers add rather than substitute, at least within this single framework's own ablation.

How this sits on the map. Reads as a finer-grained instance of this map's compression theme than the pipeline-stacking question MoExBench posed: here, within one expert-weight-compression objective, two redundancy types (across-expert and within-expert) are shown complementary when jointly budgeted — which is a narrower and more favorable claim than "compression levers compose," since the levers aren't independent here, they're two terms of one reconstruction loss. Whether GS-MoE's own combination would itself compose cleanly with weight quantization or KV-cache compression in the MoExBench sense is untested.

Caveats. D2-MoE and MoE-SVD are not independently sourced in this vault — their comparison numbers come only from GS-MoE's own paper. "Fine-grained" advantage is demonstrated on DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base specifically; the group-count/throughput trade-off (G=1 vs. G=8) is reported but not fully characterized as a Pareto frontier. No test against a speculative-decoding or offloading combination, and no frontier-scale (100B+ total parameter) MoE tested.
