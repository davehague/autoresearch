The modality is a vocabulary

transformer-architecture's through-line, now replicated across several literatures, is that much of what reads as architecture progress is systems progress on a component stack that has not changed. This is the strongest instance the vault has, because the thing left unchanged is being asked to do something it was not designed for.

An 8B early-fusion multimodal model that generates images, generates video, and answers questions about both is, structurally, a Llama-2 decoder: 32 layers, hidden 4096, grouped-query attention with 32 query heads and 8 KV heads, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, rotary position embeddings, QKV biases removed. Every one of those is a component this vault has a graded note on already (sharing-kv-heads-buys-decode-speed-at-near-zero-quality-cost, position-can-be-a-rotation-not-an-addition). The only structural difference from a text model is that the embedding table has 184,622 entries instead of ~150,000 — the text vocabulary, plus a 32,768-entry vision codebook, plus five special tokens.

Everything modality-specific lives outside the transformer, in two places.

The tokenizer. A vector-quantized autoencoder (SBER-MoVQGAN-270M with added 3D-convolution temporal residual layers) maps a 512×512 image, or a 4-frame clip, to 4,096 discrete indices at 4× temporal and 8×8 spatial compression. Once that runs, an image is a sequence of vocabulary items and there is nothing left to fuse. The entire apparatus the late-fusion literature builds — a CLIP encoder, a trainable projection, a decision about what to freeze, a separate diffusion decoder, an external text encoder to carry the conditioning — has no place to attach.

The document format. Conditioning is positional rather than architectural: [BOS] {caption} [SOV] {meta text} [SOT] {vision tokens} [EOV] [EOS], with resolution, frame rate and duration written into the meta text as plain text. Move the caption after [EOV] and the same example becomes an understanding example instead of a generation one. There is no conditioning mechanism; there is only what came earlier in the sequence.

Two costs make this a trade rather than a free simplification, and both are worth stating because they are the parts a practitioner would hit.

Sequence length is the bill. 4,096 tokens per image is why pretraining's second stage runs at a 131,072-token context, and why the training uses tensor, context and data parallelism together. Late fusion spends compute in a vision encoder once and hands the language model a short token sequence; early fusion spends nothing on an encoder and pays in context forever after — through the KV cache, through attention cost, through everything context-extension and distributed-training price. Adding a modality this way is cheap in architecture and expensive in the exact resource local inference is short of.

Balance has to be imposed by hand. Vision tokens vastly outnumber text tokens in the mixed corpus, so they carry an explicit loss weight of 0.5, alongside dropout 0.1 added "to improve training stability." That is a small echo of the stability tax the map already records for mixed-modal training (mixed-modal-training-needs-qk-norm-to-stay-stable) — a second group finding that one loss over two modalities needs a thumb on the scale.

The lossy step is worth naming too: a 32,768-entry codebook reconstructs at LPIPS ~0.1 and PSNR 21.6–24.3, so there is a fine-detail ceiling built into the interface before any model sees anything. No benchmark here isolates what that ceiling costs.
