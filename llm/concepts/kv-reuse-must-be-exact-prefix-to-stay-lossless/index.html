The line between a free lever and an expensive one

The serving optimizations this vault trusts most share a property: they are provably output-preserving. Speculative decoding samples from the exact target distribution (speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution). Paging relocates KV blocks without touching their values. Prefix reuse joins that set for a simple reason — attention's KV for position i depends only on tokens 0..i, so two requests sharing a token prefix have bit-identical keys and values for it. There is nothing to evaluate, which is why the paper reports no quality numbers and why the authors can say "we can turn on RadixAttention by default."

The boundary is where it gets interesting. The obvious generalization is to reuse KV for a shared block that is not a prefix — a document that appears in the middle of two different prompts, a few-shot example after a varying preamble. That is a much bigger cache-hit surface, and it is not exact, because the block's keys and values were computed against a different left context. The paper characterizes prior work in this direction (PromptCache's modular attention reuse) as something that "can impact accuracy by up to a 43% drop."

So the vault's ledger of serving levers now sorts cleanly by exactness rather than by mechanism:

  • Exact, enable freely: paging, iteration-level scheduling, prefix reuse, speculative decoding, activation recomputation on the training side (recomputation-is-the-one-memory-technique-that-is-exact).
  • Approximate, measure before trusting: weight and KV-cache quantization, fuzzy or non-prefix KV reuse, and anything that reorders numerics.

The practical consequence for anyone running locally is that the exact levers should all be on before any lossy one is considered, because their combined win is free and their combined risk is zero — and this vault has spent considerable effort establishing how hard the lossy ones are to evaluate honestly (perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats).

Graded suggestive, not strong: the 43% figure is the authors' characterization of a competing system, cited in related work rather than reproduced, and it is a single number without the task or setting attached in this text. The exactness of prefix reuse itself is an argument from the structure of causal attention, which is sound, but nothing here measures a fuzzy-reuse trade-off curve — so the right reading is that exact-prefix is a safe default and non-prefix reuse is an open question with one alarming data point, not that fuzzy reuse is settled as bad.
