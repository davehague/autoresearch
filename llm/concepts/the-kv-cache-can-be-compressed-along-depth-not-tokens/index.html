Every method on this map compresses the same axis, and it is not the only one

The KV cache grows in two dimensions: tokens × layers. Everything else on this map operates on the first — evict tokens (H2O, SnapKV, TOVA, counter-causal surprise), reconstruct what eviction omitted (what-eviction-deletes-is-two-sums-not-a-set-of-tokens), merge tokens, quantize per-token entries (KIVI, TurboQuant), or sparsify a view of them for drafting (a-recallable-sparse-cache-is-a-free-drafter).

This source operates on the second, and the ratio is large. Given semantic-content-is-assembled-by-mid-depth, cache one intermediate residual tensor h_j per token rather than keys and values at every layer. On Qwen3-8B at j=12 of 36, h_j is 8 KB against full bf16 KV's 144 KB — 1/18 — and the general form is |h_j|/|KV| = n_q/(2Ln_kv), so the ratio improves with depth and with grouped-query attention's KV-head count, both of which are moving in the helpful direction.

The structural claim is stronger than the ratio, though, and it is the one worth arguing about. Write once, streaming, through layers [0:j] with chunk-local RoPE restarted at zero (which is what makes a cached chunk reusable at any document offset). Then per query, select a bounded top-k of chunks — plain BM25, no training — pack them with fresh contiguous positions and recompute layers [j:L] with full cross-chunk attention. For fixed k and chunk size c, the read is sink + k·c + query ≈ 6.7k tokens regardless of how much context is stored. Not sublinear: constant. At 128k that is 18.26 GB against a full context's 89.36 GB and a 7.83× prefill speedup.

Three controls make the design non-obvious, and each is a finding in its own right.

Retrieved chunks must be allowed to interact. Replacing full cross-chunk attention with block-diagonal reuse — each chunk attending only within itself and to the query — costs 36 and 62 points on 8k/16k multi-key RULER and 19/7 on BABILong qa2. So this is not retrieval-augmented generation with cached activations; the joint recomputation over the pack is where multi-fact disambiguation happens.

More retrieval is not better. A wider ~17k-token pack beats the ~6.7k default at 8k and loses from 16k onward, consistent with a larger read admitting distractor chains. The bounded read is a quality choice, not only a cost one.

And depth is a trade rather than a free win. j=0 — retrieval with no layer reuse — already reaches 41.59 on LoCoMo against full-context's 34.59, so bounded selection is doing part of the work before any depth reuse; deeper splits then cut per-query read cost (j=12 lowers Read time 29% relative to j=0) while sacrificing frozen readout fidelity, which is what the self-distilled adapter repairs.

Graded suggestive rather than strong, for two reasons the source states and one this vault adds. Qwen3-8B is not position-extended: its native window is 40,960 tokens, so every number above that compares against a model that is failing for a reason this method does not fix. The headline reads "bounded reads beat a model past its window," not "bounded reads beat long context" — and the authors explicitly decline to claim superiority over a YaRN-enabled full-context model. The LongBench macro F1 is a tie (12.15 vs 12.17), which they also say outright: "a bounded read, not uniform accuracy dominance." And the flagship configuration costs 4,000 steps on 8 GPUs of self-distillation, which is outside the reach of the single-card reader this vault is written for — though the artifact it produces is a 0.25 GB rank-32 LoRA that anyone could download.

One more caveat the headline hides: the external store still grows linearly in stored context. What is bounded is the model-side online read. That is the right thing to bound for a serving stack, and the wrong thing to quote if the constraint is disk.
