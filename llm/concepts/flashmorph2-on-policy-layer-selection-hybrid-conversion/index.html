Converting a pretrained Transformer into a hybrid full/linear-attention model is a few GPU-hours of post-hoc fine-tuning, joining sparse upcycling and diffusion-conversion as a fourth instance of this vault's "architecture is a cheap fine-tune, not a pretraining commitment" pattern — and the published layer-selection method for doing it scores candidates the wrong way

TL;DR: FlashMorph2 fixes two "off-policy" mismatches in FlashMorph's method for choosing which layers of a pretrained, full-attention Transformer to convert to linear attention under a fixed full-attention budget: FlashMorph scores candidate layer-selections using continuous interpolation between full and linear attention with teacher-forced hidden states (not how the real, discrete hybrid actually computes once deployed), and it selects layers with the backbone weights frozen (ignoring that the weights will be further adapted afterward). FlashMorph2 instead evaluates sampled discrete hybrid architectures end-to-end and jointly optimizes the selection scores with the model's weights under the real post-conversion recovery objective. Across Qwen3 scales (0.6B-8B) and several linear-attention variants, it consistently beats FlashMorph (+2.42 RULER, +1.08 LongBench, +0.76 on recall-intensive tasks), and the whole selection-plus-conversion procedure costs only 0.72-3.57 GPU-hours.

Why it matters here. This map already carries three instances of a structural architectural property turning out to be a cheap post-hoc fine-tune rather than a pretraining-time commitment: sparse upcycling (mixture-of-experts, dense→MoE at ~50% of sunk pretraining cost), window-length extension (YaRN, <0.1% of pretraining data), and decoding regime (DiffusionGemma, autoregressive→diffusion at <10% of the token budget, diffusion-decoding). FlashMorph2 is a fourth: full-attention→hybrid-attention conversion, at 0.72-3.57 GPU-hours — cheaper than any of the three predecessors in absolute terms, though on far smaller models (0.6B-8B vs. the 8B-30B+ scale of the other three). It also sits directly beside physics-of-language-models-4-2-canon-layers (ingested 2026-11-21, same map): that source trains hybrid Transformer/linear-attention architectures from scratch and finds Canon layers close most of the gap between gated linear attention and Gated DeltaNet/Mamba2 at 1-2T tokens; FlashMorph2 instead converts an already-pretrained dense Transformer into a hybrid post hoc. The two give a from-scratch-vs-converted comparison this map has never had for the hybrid-attention design space, though neither paper tests the other's regime (Physics of LM 4.2 does not convert a pretrained checkpoint; FlashMorph2 does not compare against a from-scratch hybrid at matched budget). Also relevant to kv-cache-management and speculative-decoding: both maps hold standing findings about hybrid models' KV-cache and verification costs (e.g. a recurrent layer's state is path-dependent, unlike append-only attention KV), and a cheap, on-policy conversion path makes those costs reachable by converting an existing dense checkpoint rather than requiring a hybrid pretraining run — lowering the bar to testing those findings on a wider range of base models.

Caveats. No institutional affiliation or code-release link stated on the abstract page. The GPU-hour figures are for the selection-plus-conversion procedure only, not full recovery training to convergence (unclear from the abstract-level account whether recovery fine-tuning cost is included). No comparison against a from-scratch hybrid-pretrained model at matched final-model budget — so "cheap" is a claim about the conversion step, not about matching from-scratch hybrid quality outright. No evaluation beyond RULER/LongBench/recall-intensive tasks reported here; no quantization, no consumer-hardware serving numbers, and no comparison against Canon-layer-augmented architectures (physics-of-language-models-4-2-canon-layers).
