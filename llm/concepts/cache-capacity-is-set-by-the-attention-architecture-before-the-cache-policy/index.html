The checkpoint you picked already spent most of the budget

The authors give this result in the negative reading, and it is the right one: "a method that halves the rate cannot compensate for a checkpoint that requires four times more per token." The 3B model reaches larger contexts than the 8B under every single policy, including no compression at all, because it carries two KV heads where the 8B carries eight — 36.0 against 144.0 KiB per token.

Inside one model the policy ratios are clean and behave exactly as a rate account predicts (2.44× for the 2-bit ladder over KIVI-4, identical at both checkpoints). Across models they are swamped. So the practitioner's first lever on "how much context fits on my card" is which checkpoint, and every cache-policy comparison in the literature is a second-order argument conducted inside a first-order choice nobody reports as a variable. This is the same shape as the-attention-backend-decides-whether-cache-compression-pays — the thing the method is measured against turns out to dominate the method — and it belongs on this map's founding question as a precondition, not a footnote.

The second half is sharper. Geodesia-KV's graded-precision ladder and StreamingLLM's window eviction land on exactly the same single-sequence capacity: 590.3k and 590.3k on the 8B, 3.21M and 3.21M on the 3B. A policy that keeps every token at a demoted precision and a policy that throws most of them away reach the identical byte budget. What separates them is not bytes at all: on Needle-In-A-Haystack across all depths and in multi-turn dialogue, the graded policy retrieves at 100.0% and the eviction policy at 0%, because an evicted token cannot address a later query.

That is kv-cache-management's "keep everything, badly" thesis given its cleanest possible statement — and it retires capacity as the axis on which to prefer eviction. Eviction's remaining case has to be made on throughput or implementation simplicity, because on the memory axis, at equal aggression, it now ties.

Where compression genuinely beats both is concurrency: 53 concurrent 8B sequences at the native window against Full-KV's 6 and KIVI-4's 21, and 16 against 2 and 6 at 131,072. Those are multiplicative ratios, unlike the sub-percent perplexity margins the same paper reports and disowns. But concurrency is the multi-tenant strand of this map, not the one-card strand — the standing complaint on local-inference applies here too, and the honest single-user reading of this table is that the 2-bit ladder buys 7.8× the context of Full-KV on one 16 GiB card at zero measured quality cost, which is the number worth carrying.

Caveats on the table itself. Only the Full-KV limit performs real decode steps at every bisection iteration; for the compressed representations the search allocates buffers of the computed size, and KIVI and Quest per-token costs are estimated from the rate account rather than measured, because only this method has a resident pack in the implementation. The authors point to their end-to-end vLLM benchmarks as the verification, and those are one model on one card.

Rebutted by: pruning-tolerance-is-architecture-governed-not-scale-governed — no contradiction; the two are the same finding measured on different objects. Attention design constrains what compression can do to a checkpoint more than the compression method does, whether the compression is a cache policy (here) or expert pruning (there).
