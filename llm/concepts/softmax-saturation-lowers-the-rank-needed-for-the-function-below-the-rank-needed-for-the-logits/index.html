softmax(T, 0) and softmax(2T, 0) both go to (1, 0). The logits diverge; the distributions agree. That observation is trivial on its own, and this source turns it into a rank statement: the rank required to match an attention function and the rank required to match the scores producing it are different quantities, and the first can be strictly smaller.

Formalized as the relative softmax closure rank — the smallest r for which a sequence of rank-≤r score matrices, with every column inside the score space a query-only LoRA can actually reach through the fixed key matrix, converges columnwise to the target distribution. The restriction to the reachable space is not a technicality; it is what makes this a statement about LoRA rather than about matrices.

The separation is real and bounded. For every k ≥ 3 there is a Walsh-attention family in which every exact finite-logit realization has update rank exactly k, while the closure rank of the limiting target is k − ⌊k/3⌋. A second linear-token construction reaches k − max{⌊k/3⌋, 3⌊k/7⌋} with vanishing KL, a 4/7 ratio on complete seven-context blocks. So saturation buys roughly a third off, or a bit better on a favourable family.

And it is bounded from below on purpose. The construction's lower-bound argument forces every limiting triple block to keep rank at least two, so saturation cannot collapse the rank to o(k) in this family. That is the more disciplined half of the result: it is a constant-factor saving, not an escape hatch, and anyone tempted to argue that attention's nonlinearity means low rank is always enough has an explicit counter-family.

It is a second, independent reason a rank sweep does not transfer. the-rank-a-lora-needs-is-set-by-the-part-of-the-update-the-task-activates gives one — the task reweights the spectrum. This gives another that operates even at fixed task: how saturated the target attention is decides how much of the logit spectrum you actually have to reproduce. A task whose attention is peaked (long context, sharp retrieval heads) sits near the boundary where the saving applies; one whose attention is diffuse does not. Both effects push the same way, which is that the number r in a config file is not a property of the method.

It sharpens what a "capacity ceiling" means. low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new records low rank as a genuine ceiling on unfamiliar domains. This says part of what looks like a ceiling in logit space may not be one in function space — but only by a constant factor, so it narrows the gap rather than closing it, and the ceiling stays a ceiling.

Scope, and it is the tight kind. These are explicit constructions, and the author says so plainly in the limitations: they "isolate a genuine nonlinear effect but are explicit constructions rather than models of typical language data." Walsh families with a designed isolated-triple structure are not what a fine-tuning corpus produces. The exact equality k − ⌊k/3⌋ is proved for that construction only, and other target families can have smaller closure rank; the 4/7 family is a different construction whose minimum closure rank is not identified. Nothing here is measured on a trained head, and the whole result lives in attention-probability space rather than task loss. Treat it as an existence result about the kind of gap that exists between logit rank and function rank, not as a factor to apply to a config.
