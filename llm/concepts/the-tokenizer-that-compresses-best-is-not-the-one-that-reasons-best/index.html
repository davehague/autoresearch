Two axes, and the default heuristic only sees one

tokenization-and-vocabulary has, until now, been a map about V the number: what a bigger vocabulary costs in bandwidth (vocabulary-size-is-a-bandwidth-term-at-batch-one), where its optimum sits (the-optimal-vocabulary-is-a-function-of-batch-size), how flat quality is across its range (vocabulary-quality-is-flat-across-a-thirty-two-fold-range), what you can delete (an-unused-vocabulary-is-deletable-weight).

This is the other axis. Vocabulary size is pinned at ~128K across all 46 configurations and the things that vary are algorithm, pretokenization rules, and tokenizer training mixture — how the text gets cut, not how many pieces exist. The models are trained from scratch with nothing else changing.

The result separates cleanly enough to state as a rule. Information-theoretic metrics predict language modeling. Structural metrics predict task accuracy. Neither predicts the other.

Rényi efficiency at α=2 is the single best correlate of bits-per-byte at ρ = −0.80, and four other entropy- and compression-family metrics join it as significant on the same target. None of them significantly predicts MBPP pass@1, BLiMP, or code bits-per-byte. Going the other way, the only significant predictor of code BPB is digit-boundary F1 (ρ = −0.62), and the only significant predictor of MBPP is AST boundary alignment (ρ = +0.61) — a metric that asks whether token boundaries land on syntactic leaf nodes.

Compression rate is the specific casualty and it is the one everybody uses. It is a significant predictor of bits-per-byte in the per-language regression (β = −0.010 per SD) and not a significant predictor of MultiBLiMP accuracy, and its aggregate correlations with the two task-accuracy targets are 0.26 and −0.14. This corroborates two prior results the paper cites — that minimizing corpus token count does not reliably improve downstream performance, and that Rényi efficiency can be raised arbitrarily while degrading the model — but does it inside a controlled grid rather than by construction.

The practitioner form is short. If your workload is math or code, evaluate the tokenizer on how it cuts digits and line breaks, not on how few tokens it produces. A right-aligned digit pretokenizer that groups numbers by place value costs compression and buys structure; prior work has measured roughly 20 accuracy points on math from digit-tokenization strategy alone.

Two honest limits, both raised by the author. The BLiMP correlation with digit boundary F1 coincides with an algorithm split rather than a gradient — the three UnigramLM tokenizers share one digit-F1 value and sit high on BLiMP, while the tokenizer with the panel's best digit F1 sits mid-range — and removing a single tokenizer pushes it below significance. The MBPP/AST correlation is attributed to line-break handling that AST alignment partly reflects, i.e. a pretokenizer difference. So the division of labour between the two metric families is the robust claim; the individual structural correlations are group differences with n = 29 behind them.

And the scale bound cuts both ways. These are 1.27B models, which may overstate the effects because larger models compensate — and understates nothing for a reader of this vault, whose models are frequently in exactly this size class.
