Pin the integer kernel and behaviour crosses the ISA boundary

two-int8-kernels-of-the-same-model-agree-on-no-sequence is the sharpest reproducibility result this vault holds: hold the checkpoint, the card, the engine, the container digest and the decoding fixed, swap only which INT8 GEMM kernel is selected, and two deterministic machines agree on zero of 64 sequences. the-divergence-is-in-the-scale-not-the-accumulator localized it to scale application and output rounding downstream of a provably exact accumulator. What neither could say is what the positive version looks like — what you have to control for behaviour to transfer.

This is that control, run in the hardest available direction: across instruction sets. The same files serve on aarch64 and x86-64 with the integer kernel arms pinned bitwise-identical, established by exact lane-sum equivalence between the sdot and lane-folded vpdpbusd formulations under a shared float schedule. All five behavioural fixtures reproduce exactly on x86, including the cell sitting on a decision knife-edge — the one whose step-0 argmax is known to flip under within-process history and ULP-level build changes. That fixture is the sensitive instrument, and it agrees.

Meanwhile the model around the kernels does not agree: end-to-end perplexity drifts 1.1% relative (4.44 against 4.39 at 512 tokens, 0.0113 nats per token) from accumulated rounding in architecture-specific attention and normalization schedules. So model-level bit-exactness is unavailable and behaviour transfers anyway.

The author's one-line summary is the transferable form and it deserves to be the note's conclusion: "kernel-level yes, transformer-stack level no, behavior in practice yes." Read against the integer-alibi result, the pair localizes the earlier finding more precisely than either does alone. Leave the integer kernel free and behaviour does not survive a kernel swap on one card; pin it and behaviour survives an instruction-set change. That is a much more actionable statement than "quantized inference is nondeterministic," and it names the thing to put in a method section.

The scope is narrow: one engine, one model, five fixtures, two machines. And the fixtures are argmax-agreement checks against a hosted API, so this establishes that these decisions transfer, not that the distributions do — the 1.1% perplexity drift says the distributions demonstrably do not.
