The rotation matrix inside incoherence processing doesn't have to be dense and random to work — a structured DCT is cheaper per iteration and, on the models where QuIP's own LDL decomposition NaNs out, the difference is the method working at all

This map's allocation strand already treats "rotation" as one of the recurring mechanisms quantizers reach for to spread outlier mass before rounding (alongside per-channel/per-layer/per-expert allocation). This source narrows what "rotation" needs to be: QuIP-family incoherence processing has been using a dense random orthogonal matrix, which costs O(N²) per iteration and — on Pythia-6.9B and Mistral-7B specifically — leaves QuIP and QuIP-RG at perplexities in the thousands and forces QuIP#'s own LDL decomposition to NaN on Mistral-7B. Swapping the dense rotation for a sign-randomized Discrete Cosine Transform (O(N log N)) inside a Kashin-decomposition quantizer, paired with a new greedy alternating-update algorithm that provably reaches the four-peak weight distribution 2-bit clustering requires (removing the multi-restart k-means prior versions needed), produces a quantizer that stays within ~2.5 points of FP16 on the models that break the QuIP family outright, while landing between OPTQ and QuIP# — not ahead of either — on the four standard models where nothing breaks.

Graded extends, strong, direction: mixed (deliberately not improves-outcomes): the paper's own numbers are honest that this is not a best-in-class accuracy method on well-behaved models — it is a robustness fix for a failure mode this map had not previously named as belonging to the rotation step itself rather than to the model or the bit-width. The failure-avoidance case is quantified (thousands of PPL vs ~20, NaN vs a finite number), not asserted, which earns strong despite the mixed direction. Discounted for scope: weight-only, post-training, exactly 4-bit-per-channel — no activation quantization, no comparison to activation-aware baselines (AWQ, OmniQuant) or other rotation-based methods despite rotation being the map's own recurring mechanism, and no fine-tuning-recovery comparison (GPTQ+LoRA-style). All models are dense decoder-only transformers; nothing here touches this map's MoE-quantization or KV-cache-quantization cells. In absolute wall-clock this is the slowest of the four methods compared at matched size (only its scaling is favorable), which matters for anyone running quantization on a single consumer card rather than an H100.

Rebutted by: none.
