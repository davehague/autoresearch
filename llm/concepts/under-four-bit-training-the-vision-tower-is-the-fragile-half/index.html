The map's one allocation rule now has a regime where it inverts

This map's most actionable single line is vlm-precision-should-be-allocated-by-modality: vision at 4 bits, text at 8, with a couple of projection and layer-norm layers per tower kept at float16. It came from one T1 on a quantized CLIP ViT-B/16 adapting at test time, and it was the third independent partition in this vault to conclude that a quantization budget is an allocation problem rather than a global setting. That framing survives. The direction of the allocation does not survive unconditionally.

Under quantization-aware training at four bits, on two multimodal models, with vision and language pathways quantized in the same numerical formats so the difference is attributable, the vision side is the fragile one. Both models. In Wan2.2 the severe degradation is in the WanDiT visual backbone while the UMT5 text encoder holds; in Qwen3-VL the vision encoder is the sensitive component, though the language backbone is not spared either.

The paper names the disagreement itself, which is what makes this a scope condition rather than a contradiction to litigate: "In prior work such as MBQ, where vision tokens are generally less sensitive than language tokens under post-training quantization, we observe a reverse trend in our QAT setting." Two candidate boundaries are on the table, and the source separates neither cleanly:

PTQ versus QAT. The incumbent measurements are post-training; this one is a 5-billion-token quantization-aware fine-tune. Outliers that a calibration set sees as static are, in this setting, evolving during training and concentrating in different components in different architectures — which is the source's own stated reason PTQ methods do not transfer here.

Weight-only versus activation-inclusive. The incumbent rule allocates weight precision on an encoder. This result is driven by activations (activations-not-weights-are-what-four-bit-costs-a-multimodal-model), and the mechanism is specifically that vision-related layers carry the largest activation outliers. If the fragility lives in the activation distributions, then a weight-only regime — the one a single card actually runs — may never encounter it, and the incumbent rule may be perfectly safe exactly where this vault's readers operate.

There is a third difference nobody has controlled: the incumbent evidence is a CLIP encoder, a contrastive discriminative model, and this evidence is a generative video model and a generative VLM. Those are not the same object and the map has said so before.

What this map should state now, and what it should stop stating. State: the precision budget is allocated by modality, and which modality gets the cheap bits depends on the regime — weight-only post-training quantization of an encoder is not the same problem as activation-inclusive low-precision training of a generative VLM, and the answer flips between them. Stop stating: "vision at 4 bits, text at 8" as an unqualified rule. It was carried on one source and one model class, and there is now a T1 running the other way on two models.

Discounts on this side too, and they are real. Two models, no seeds, no intervals, no code. It is a training-regime result that costs a 5B-token QAT run to reproduce, on formats (MXFP4, HiF4) that consumer hardware does not support today, so nobody reading this vault can check it on their own machine. The vision-versus-language comparison is reported qualitatively — heavier outliers, larger drops — with the quantitative table given per format rather than per modality, so the size of the asymmetry is not stated as a number anywhere. That is enough to reopen the map's rule and not enough to replace it with the opposite one.
