The pipeline that never drains, and what it costs to keep it full

The vault filed pipeline parallelism as a memory axis when GPipe landed — the fifth answer to one memory ceiling, and the only one attacking activations. PipeDream shows that framing came from one branch of the literature. Its motivation is bandwidth: data-parallel BSP all-reduces every parameter every step, and on a commodity cluster that is 70–90% of training time for VGG16, AlexNet and S2VT. Split the layers into stages and each machine ships one layer's activations instead of the whole parameter set — up to 95% less traffic.

The design decision that separates the two branches is the flush. GPipe drains the pipeline at every step so the gradient is exact and identical to data-parallel training, paying a bubble of O((K-1)/(M+K-1)). PipeDream never drains: a 1F1B steady state where each worker alternates a forward and a backward pass belonging to different minibatches, indefinitely. No bubble, and no synchronized weight version either — stage 1's forward pass for a minibatch runs many updates before its backward pass does.

Weight stashing is the repair, and it is partial by design. Keep one weight version per in-flight minibatch and reuse the stashed copy on the backward pass, giving

w^(t+1) = w^(t) - v * grad f(w_1^(t-n+1), w_2^(t-n+2), ..., w_n^(t))

— every stage at a different staleness. Without it, the paper states, "the weight update is not a valid gradient of the loss function f for any weight vector." A second mechanism, vertical sync, would make every stage use one version and is "semantically the same as data parallelism with BSP synchronization" — and it is off by default, because its metadata cost is real and its measured effect was negligible on the three models tested.

Two consequences the vault should carry. First, this axis costs memory: NOAM in-flight minibatches of intermediate state plus a weight version each, statically allocated. It is not a substitute for rematerialization-trades-compute-for-the-activation-memory-nothing-else-shrinks — it is the opposite trade. Second, the measured driver gets worse over time: across five models and three GPU generations, faster GPUs raise communication's share of training time, and PipeDream's advantage over BSP grew from 2.99× to 5.12× moving to newer cards on a slower network. That is kernel-performance-expires-with-the-gpu-generation observed on the interconnect rather than in the kernel, and four years earlier.

Bound: no transformers and no language models — VGG16, Inception-v3, S2VT, ResNet-50, AlexNet on at most 16 GPUs. Convergence evidence is time-to-advertised-accuracy on three models, which shows stashing sufficed there, not that staleness is generally safe; the paper's own flagged asymmetry (deeper stages see fewer interleaved updates) is never quantified.
