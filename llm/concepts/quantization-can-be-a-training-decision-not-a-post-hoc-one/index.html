The strongest open model does not quantize after training; it never trains in full precision

post-training-quantization is, by construction, a map about a post-hoc operation: take a trained model, spend calibration data, recover the quality. Every source on it — GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, QuaRot, AlphaQ, GEMQ — accepts a finished checkpoint as input. QLoRA and LoftQ complicate that by training through a frozen quantized base, but the base is still quantized after someone else finished it.

Kimi K3 does something this map has no slot for. MoE expert weights are held in MXFP4 with MXFP8 activations, and quantization-aware training runs from the SFT stage onward through the whole of RL. Quantization is not applied to the artifact; the artifact is trained inside it. The released weights are the 4-bit weights.

Three things follow, in descending order of confidence.

The tensor-class partition replicates, from the largest instance available. K3 quantizes expert weights and holds attention projections, latent-MoE projections, shared experts and routers in higher precision. That is the same partition GEMQ arrived at independently (attention held at 4 bits while experts take 1–3) and the same shape as the Mixtral-offloading result this map records — 2-bit experts cost 3.85 MMLU points, 2-bit attention/embeddings/gates cost 11.5 from a thirtieth of the parameters. Three groups, three methods, one conclusion: experts are the cheap parameters and the shared path is not.

The RL detail is the genuinely new one. During RL, "rollout and training share the same quantization scheme, eliminating the train–inference mismatch." This vault has a name for the failure that avoids: evaluation-contamination's infrastructure non-equivalence ladder — cases where the number a system reports was produced by arithmetic that is not the arithmetic it claims. An RL loop whose rollout policy is a quantized model and whose gradient step is a full-precision one is optimizing a policy it will not deploy, silently. Making the two identical is a correctness argument, not a memory argument, and it belongs on that ladder as the first entry where a practitioner-visible fix exists.

And the map's central question is left unanswered by the same source that raises it. There is no BF16 control. K3 reports no arm in which the same recipe ran at full precision, so the quality cost of MXFP4-with-QAT is unmeasured — the report treats it as a deployment decision, not an experiment. Graded pushes-back and suggestive on exactly that basis: it pushes back on the framing that quantization is a post-training stage, with the strongest possible existence proof (someone shipped a frontier model this way), while supplying no effect size at all. What it establishes is that the choice is available and survives contact with a 2.8T RL pipeline. What it cannot establish is whether it is better than quantizing afterwards.

The practical distance is worth stating plainly: MXFP4 is a microscaling block format with hardware support on recent datacenter accelerators, not a llama.cpp K-quant. As with AlphaQ's per-layer allocation and GEMQ's Triton kernels, the finding is currently unreachable from the local stack — a third consecutive quantization result this map cannot act on.
