A third entrant to the drafter-training-loss contest, arriving by diagnosing a shared bias in the surrogate-objective class the map's existing D-PAL/RCSD/WTV sources all build on

TL;DR: This map's open question (new 2026-10-22) asks whether D-PAL's distributional matching or RCSD's outcome-only preference training wins at drafter-loss design, or whether they compose. REAL adds a third answer that cuts across both: it names a covariance-gap bias specific to the block-level EAL-surrogate objective class (which includes WTV, and structurally resembles the averaging approach D-PAL's distributional matching could share) -- averaging marginal next-step acceptance over target-sampled continuations discards which branches actually survive verification, producing a bias term, Cov(path weight, next-step acceptance), that the paper measures as positive throughout training and capable of reversing the gradient. REAL's fix is a change to the loss alone (unbiased path-weighted branch estimator, Rao-Blackwellized for variance), with no drafter architecture or inference-procedure change, beating WTV/VSD/DBLast/static-LK by roughly 1% acceptance length and cutting gradient variance 52-58%.

What it says. Neither distributional-acceptance-loss-beats-point-mass-draft-training (D-PAL) nor rcsd-preference-pairs-from-accept-reject-traffic-beat-distribution-matching (RCSD) tests for this covariance-gap bias in its own training objective, so REAL's diagnosis is a standing, untested question about whether either of those sources' reported gains partly reflect gradient-direction noise this bias introduces rather than the mechanism each paper credits (distributional fidelity for D-PAL, outcome-only preference signal for RCSD). This is the map's first source to attack the loss construction itself as a shared defect across a class of methods, rather than proposing a new training signal (imitate the distribution more faithfully vs. train on acceptance outcomes directly) -- a third, orthogonal axis: fix the bias in whichever signal you already chose. The ablation that REAL-without-Rao-Blackwellization matches full REAL's acceptance length at T=1 but with higher gradient variance and worse temperature generalization is itself a data point for this map's standing temperature-confound open question (new 2026-08-09): training-loss variance under one sampling temperature need not transfer its stability to another.

Caveats. Single-author work (University of Hong Kong), evaluated on one target/drafter pair (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MoE target, Domino drafter) with no cross-backbone replication, and the comparison is a controlled loss-level swap rather than each baseline's own paper's reported setup -- so this is the first place any of WTV, D-PAL, RCSD, REAL have been measured against each other on one fixed backbone, but it is one backbone, not a sweep. The acceptance-length gain over the best baseline is small in absolute terms (~1%), and no tokens/sec or wall-clock serving number accompanies it -- acceptance length remains this map's standing proxy for speedup, not speedup itself, per the map's own measured-cost open question (new 2026-08-09). No test of whether REAL's unbiased objective composes with D-PAL's distributional pretraining or RCSD's preference-pair fine-tuning stage, despite all three now sharing this map's open composition question.
