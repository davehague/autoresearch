Whether a skill document can be read back out of a LoRA adapter depends on whether the adapter's behavior is guessable from the base model alone -- when it is, a reward loop without the adapter works just as well, which bears directly on what this map's adapter-as-substrate sources actually prove

TL;DR: This map's strand-1 question — what is the adapter object, and what can it be converted into or read out of — gains a new data point from an unexpected direction: can the text that trained a LoRA be recovered from the trained weights? Reading weights directly recovers almost nothing (a learned weights-to-text map passes on "about one bit" of the document); running the adapted model and training a writer on its behavior ("Executing Writer") recovers 39-44% of key instructions when the training process stored the document, against 1.3-1.5% for a mismatched-adapter control, and restores 85-91% of the original task-performance gain across 41 external skills. The sharper finding is the boundary condition: for adapters trained on behavior alone (no document memorized), trained writers collapse to 1-2% recovery, and the fix that works instead — an agent that runs base and adapted models and writes a skill from where they differ, with no adapter weights needed as training signal — recovers 58-71% of the LoRA's gain on ALFWorld and transfers the resulting skill document to models that cannot even load the original LoRA, lifting Llama-3.1-8B from 5% to 55%.

What it says. This bears directly on this map's construct-validity caution, shared with peft-methods's lora-adapters-mostly-measure-shared-reading-not-content finding: when a LoRA's learned behavior is itself inferable from the gap between base and adapted outputs, a method that never touches the adapter's weights at all (the difference agent) recovers an equally good skill document — meaning the adapter was not the irreplaceable source of the capability, only a convenient compiled form of something the base model's own comparison already contains. The adapter only becomes the sole source of information when its behavior genuinely cannot be guessed that way (new knowledge, not a small nudge on an already-capable base) — and that is exactly the regime where the document-recovery numbers are weakest (1-2%). Read against this map's own substrate question — what does task knowledge actually live in, and is a LoRA weight delta a privileged place to find it — this source's own boundary condition suggests the answer may split the same way the knowledge-vs-skill distinction already splits on distillation-small-models (reverse-kl-opd-transfers-skill-not-knowledge-forward-kl-restores-it): a LoRA that encodes "skill" (nudging an already-capable base) is recoverable without the adapter at all; one that encodes genuinely new "knowledge" is not recoverable without it, and recovering it well requires the document to have been memorized during training in the first place.

Caveats. The strongest recovery numbers depend on a condition (training stored the document) the paper can verify after the fact but an adapter author in the wild cannot easily disclose or may not know holds. Weights-only and learned-weights-to-text baselines are near-floor by design, so the contribution is "run the model, don't just read the weights" rather than a general LoRA-interpretability method. No code release stated at ingest time. The ALFWorld transfer result (55% on Llama-3.1-8B) is one benchmark, one target model.
