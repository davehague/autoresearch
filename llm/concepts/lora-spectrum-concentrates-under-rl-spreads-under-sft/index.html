A LoRA update's own singular spectrum behaves differently under SFT and RL -- RL concentrates it and rotates its subspaces faster, traced to GRPO's group-mean baseline subtraction -- a second, distinct geometric lens on the SFT-vs-RL contrast this map already holds through the base weight's own principal directions

TL;DR: This map already carries a geometric account of why RL forgets less than SFT (SST, 2026-10-16): SFT concentrates updates along the base weight's principal singular directions, where prior capability is encoded, and RL empirically avoids them. Liu, Wang, Dong & Lei (NYU/ISTA) study a different object entirely -- not where the LoRA update lands relative to the frozen base weight's spectrum, but the shape of the update's own singular spectrum and how fast its own singular subspaces rotate -- on math-reasoning fine-tunes across Llama/Qwen models, holding optimizer settings fixed between SFT and RL (GRPO). RL concentrates the update's spectral energy onto fewer directions at every learning rate tested; SFT spreads it at moderate learning rates; RL also rotates singular subspaces faster than SFT at small learning rates. Interpolating between SFT and RL one ingredient at a time isolates the cause to GRPO's group-mean baseline subtraction, which cancels the gradient component shared across a group's same-prompt responses (the part that drives coherent fitting along pretrained-aligned directions) while preserving the within-group contrastive signal -- shifting the balance toward spectral concentration and faster rotation, and lowering the risk-favorable learning rate in the process (consistent with RL's standard practice of small learning rates).

What it says. A quadratic multi-index toy model, trained by SGD on correlated input pairs modeling a GRPO group, reproduces the mechanism analytically: coherent fitting (linear in learning rate, concentrates spectral energy on strongly-responding directions) competes with gradient-noise feedback (quadratic in learning rate, redistributes spectral energy more evenly); group-mean baseline subtraction cancels most of the shared component that anchors updates to pretrained-aligned directions while damping task-irrelevant gradient noise, so coherent fitting persists through the contrast while the redistributing force weakens -- producing both effects under RL, at the cost of a lower risk-favorable learning rate (subtracting the shared component makes coherent fitting less efficient at reducing risk). Guided by this, the authors propose stage-specific LoRA updates combining Muon-style norm-preserving spectral flattening with a per-factor SGD/Adam choice: flatten the full update's spectrum for SFT (spreads adaptation across directions); flatten only the component orthogonal to the current LoRA subspace for RL (promotes subspace rotation while preserving what the within-subspace component has already learned). These configurations win in most evaluated model/task settings.

Caveats. extends this map's SFT-vs-RL forgetting strand with a second, methodologically distinct geometric lens -- subspace-tuning-protects-99-percent-of-weight-space-and-nearly-eliminates-forgetting measures alignment with the base weight's principal directions; this source measures the update's own spectral concentration and subspace rotation speed. Neither paper tests against the other, so whether the two accounts are compatible, redundant, or in tension is this vault's open question, not either source's claim. Math reasoning only (GSM8K, MATH); generality to non-reasoning SFT/RL (instruction-following, preference optimization) is untested. Exact models, full benchmark numbers, and the complete baseline list were not confirmed on this read -- the paper refers to "multiple models" and "the compared methods" generally. The causal mechanism rests on a toy quadratic multi-index model plus an empirical interpolation study, not a full-scale controlled ablation at the production sizes the recipe is evaluated on.
