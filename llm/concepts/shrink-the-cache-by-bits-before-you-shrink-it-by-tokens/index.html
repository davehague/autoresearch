Coverage beats precision when the cache holds a derivation

kv-cache-management was coined to compare levers acting on one budget and has complained since that nobody runs two of them together. This is not a composition either — but it is the first time the two families have been measured on one axis under identical conditions, and the gap is not close.

KIVI 2-bit quantization reduces the precision of cached keys and values while keeping every token in the cache. Token eviction keeps some tokens at full precision and deletes the rest. At a comparable memory reduction on Qwen3-8B, restricted to cases where the compressed model returns the same correct answer as the uncompressed one and the uncompressed chain was judged valid, the compressed chain becomes invalid in 4.2% of cases under quantization and 47.9–64.8% under the eviction family. On final accuracy KIVI matches Full-KV exactly on AIME (67.4 vs 67.4) where the best eviction method reaches 50.0.

The authors' reading is the durable part, and it names a mechanism rather than a winner: the answer–evidence gap "is not an inevitable consequence of reducing KV memory" — it is tied to losing access to parts of the reasoning trace. Preserving broad coverage of evidential states matters more for faithfulness than preserving any of them at full precision. Every step in a derivation is load-bearing for the steps after it; a slightly noisy record of all of them supports a chain, an exact record of the salient third does not.

That gives this vault a practitioner ordering it could not previously state. Given a fixed KV budget on a reasoning workload, spend bits before you spend tokens. Both halves are supported here and only one of them is new: a-data-free-rotation-puts-the-kv-cache-at-three-and-a-half-bits already put quantized KV at 3.5 bits with no calibration data at all, and kv-eviction-is-a-quality-spending-lever-and-its-own-numbers-say-so already found eviction's measured benefit on a consumer card to be a 4% speedup at higher peak memory. The ordering follows.

Two honest limits before adopting it as a rule. The comparison is not budget-matched in bytes — a 2-bit cache over all tokens and a 16-bit cache over 256 tokens are different memory footprints, and the paper does not equalize them, so the fair statement is that quantization achieves this quality at some real saving rather than at a matched one. And it remains true that nobody has composed them: 2-bit quantization applied to a token-evicted cache is the obvious next run and the errors have no reason to be independent — this map's founding complaint, still unaddressed, now with a reason to expect the composition to be interesting rather than additive.
