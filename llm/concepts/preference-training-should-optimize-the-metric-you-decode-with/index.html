Preference training should optimize the metric you actually decode with

SimPO's argument is a mismatch argument, and the mismatch is the durable part. DPO's implicit reward is β log π(y|x)/π_ref(y|x) summed over tokens; at decode time, generation is governed by average log-likelihood. Those are different objects, and the paper shows the DPO reward frequently disagrees with the actual likelihood ranking of the pair it was trained on. SimPO makes the training reward be the decoding metric — length-normalized average log-prob — drops π_ref entirely, and adds a target reward margin γ to the Bradley-Terry objective. Result: +6.4 over DPO on AlpacaEval 2, +7.5 on Arena-Hard; Gemma-2-9B-it at 72.4% LC win rate and 1st on Chatbot Arena under 10B.

Two side effects worth naming. Length normalization directly attacks length exploitation — models discovering that longer answers win. And dropping the reference model removes a full model copy plus a forward pass per step, which is the same deletion orpo-monolithic-preference-optimization made on a different argument. That convergence is the map-level signal: two independent groups reached "the reference model is optional" by different routes, which replicates the direction (post-training-rl's successive-deletion trajectory) even though neither validates the other's specific objective.

The caveat is unusually sharp this run. AlpacaEval 2, Arena-Hard, and MT-Bench are all GPT-4-family judged, and llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias — graded in the same ingest — documents verbosity bias in exactly those judges. SimPO's headline gain is measured on an instrument that rewards length, using a method whose distinguishing feature is length normalization; "length-controlled win rate" is a harness correction, not an escape from the judge. Also: γ and β are per-setup hyperparameters found by search, and nothing anchors a reference-free policy to the base model, so off-distribution degradation is unmeasured here.
