Three handles, and one of them needs nothing but a temperature flag

one-test-set-replica-beats-infinite-clean-compute is the bad news. This is the part that a person holding a downloaded checkpoint can act on, which is rare for this map — its four failure modes are all things to be warned about, and these are things to do.

Fresh tokens dilute the dose. Overtraining past compute-optimal with uncontaminated data produces an interaction, not a uniform effect: below ~10 replicas test cross-entropy keeps falling (generalization winning), above ~10 it rises, because the memorization is being washed out. The threshold moves down with model size — 32 replicas at 34M, 10 at 63M, 1 at 93M — which is the uncomfortable direction, since it implies larger models sit in the memorization-dominated regime at lower doses.

Fine-tuning can lower a contaminated model's score, and this is a new failure mode. Supervised fine-tuning on the MATH training split lowers test loss below 10 replicas, does nothing at ~10, and raises test loss above it — the model both learns to generalize and forgets what it memorized, and past the threshold the forgetting costs more. So a practitioner fine-tuning a contaminated base and watching the benchmark drop would naturally blame their own recipe. The correct inference may be that the base was contaminated and the fine-tune is working. Nothing else in this map predicts that.

Temperature is a contamination probe that needs no corpus and no log-probs. Math-Verify scores are flat from greedy decoding through τ ≤ 0.56, then fall off; at 1000 replicas, going from τ=0 to τ=1.0 costs ~40×, collapsing the contaminated model back to roughly uncontaminated performance, while clean models are nearly flat. You sample the same benchmark twice at two temperatures and read the gap. That matters against this map's sharpest structural complaint: the reliable rephrasing detector needs the training corpus (supply side) while pretraining-membership-leaves-a-detectable-tail runs at AUC 0.72 on the model alone (demand side), so everyone fine-tuning a checkpoint they did not pretrain has been stuck with the weak tool. This is a third demand-side signal, cheaper than Min-K% Prob and orthogonal to it — memorization brittleness rather than a log-prob tail. contamination-detection-is-a-prior-not-a-verdict still governs: a temperature-sensitivity gap is evidence, not a verdict.

Long solutions resist memorization exponentially. The probability of emitting a length-T solution sorts into three regimes — exponential decoherence P(T) ~ e^(−E·T) for clean models, a stretched-exponential "brittle memorization" band, and "deterministic lock-in" at high dose — with temperature scaling the exponent as α_eff(τ) ≈ α/τ, so low-temperature decoding makes brittle memorization look like lock-in. This is the map's first positive benchmark-design result, an argument for what to trust rather than a list of what to distrust, and it is a mechanism: prefer long-form generative benchmarks, because memorizing them is exponentially harder than memorizing a multiple-choice letter. It is also the cleanest statement of why the discriminative-evaluation literature did not already answer this — a two-token answer has no length to decay over.
