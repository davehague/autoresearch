A quantization that shrinks the file and slows the model

The allocation strand of this map asks where the bits should go and answers it in units of quality per bit. This is a cheap reminder that there is a second currency, that the two do not rank tensors the same way, and that ignoring the second one produces configurations that are strictly worse than doing nothing.

Quantizing only attention tensors on Gemma 3 1B — attn_v, attn_k, or both — costs 62.3–64.4 tok/s against an F16 baseline of 67.2, while shrinking the file by 8–15 MB out of 2,007. The dequantization tax is paid on every token and the bytes saved were never the bottleneck, so the trade is negative on both axes that matter at batch one: slower, and not meaningfully smaller. Meanwhile FFN-only quantization reaches 80.6 tok/s and beats the everything-but-the-FFN configuration (72.3) by more than eight tokens per second, on a larger file.

This map already carries the shape of this result on the vocabulary side — vocabulary-size-is-a-bandwidth-term-at-batch-one — and here it appears for the FFN and the embedding matrix together, with the embedding's importance underwritten by a size fact the author cites rather than measures: it is 52% of LLaMA-3.2-1B's parameters. At sub-2B scale the tensors that dominate the bytes read per token are the feed-forward projections and the embedding/output matrix, and attention is a rounding error on the memory bus even though it is the part of the architecture everyone thinks about.

The quality side ranks them differently, which is the point. At Q8 per-channel the embedding's output SQNR is 45.61 dB and the FFN's worst projection, W_down, is 29.70 dB — the FFN is both the most valuable target for speed and the more fragile one for quality, with sensitivity concentrated in the middle layers and rising again toward the output. So the allocation question on a small model is not "which layers can I afford to damage" but the two-sided one this vault keeps rediscovering: a bit budget is an allocation problem, and the ordering depends on which of the two currencies you are spending.

Practical form, for the reader converting a small model with llama-quantize tonight: do not quantize attention tensors in isolation on a sub-2B model — the configuration exists in the tooling, saves almost nothing and costs throughput. Put the bits into the FFN first and the embedding second, and check W_down if quality moves.

Scope, and it is tight. One model for the profiling table, one T4, one runtime (llama.cpp), Q8 only, short-to-100-token contexts, and three or more runs per cell without intervals reported. The mechanism — bandwidth-bound decode, dequantization overhead on tensors that are not the bottleneck — is architecture-general and should hold wherever weight reads dominate, but the specific crossovers are 1B-scale numbers on one card and there is no reason to expect the same ordering at 7B, where attention's KV traffic grows with context and the FFN's share falls.
