The adapter moved the model somewhere, and not back

The standard pipeline for structured pruning ends with a recovery fine-tune, usually LoRA, and the literature that established it — LLM-Pruner and its descendants — asks a single question: does aggregate accuracy come back? Reported per task, the answer stops being a scalar.

On one pruned TinyLlama, LoRA lifts extractive QA from 9.55 to 36.59 F1 and drops binary QA from 26 to 11. On a pruned Qwen3.5 at the same ratio the trade runs the other way: binary QA recovers 57 → 72 while extractive QA falls 64.56 → 51.27 and open-domain QA falls 8.78 → 5.16. At a heavier pruning ratio the same model recovers both of the first two while the third still degrades. Nothing returns to the dense baseline anywhere.

The natural reading is that a small recovery run on a damaged model is re-specialization, not repair. The adapter has a low-rank budget and a recovery corpus, and it spends both on whatever the corpus emphasizes; capability that was already fragile after pruning and is not represented in that corpus keeps sliding. This is the pruning-side echo of intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting, which found that LoRA-trained updates introduce singular directions orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum and that these causally drive forgetting — the mechanism there predicts exactly this here, from a study that does not cite it.

The compounding hazard is that the metric most likely to look recovered is the one most likely to be lying. The same paper's clearest example is a LoRA-recovered variant reading 71% strict binary-QA accuracy while answering yes on 97 of 100 items (a-compressed-model-can-hold-its-score-by-answering-one-class). Averaged with a genuinely recovered extractive-QA score, that produces a healthy aggregate for a model that has learned a label prior.

The operational form is a reporting rule rather than a method claim: report recovery per task, against the dense baseline, with the pre-recovery pruned score beside it. A single recovery percentage is not interpretable, and — as the paper's own tables show — its sign varies by task within one checkpoint.

Held as suggestive rather than strong deliberately. The pruning method is one width pruner at low ratios; the recovery configuration (rank, corpus, steps) is fixed and never swept, so "LoRA recovery is selective" could in principle be "this particular recovery corpus was selective"; the models are sub-1.5B, where capability is thin enough that any redistribution shows up loudly; and the task set is three QA families. What would settle it is cheap and this vault should want it: one pruned 7B, one recovery corpus, scored on four task families before and after, at two adapter ranks. where-the-adapter-budget-goes-matters-more-than-how-much-there-is predicts rank would not be the lever; the corpus composition would.
