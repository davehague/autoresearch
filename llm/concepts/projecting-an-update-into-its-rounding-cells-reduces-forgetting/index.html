The box is a trust region, not only a tax

The natural objection to in-cell learning is that it is unnecessary: if you want the released artifact preserved, serve the adapter unmerged. That costs nothing, preserves the checkpoint trivially by never touching it, and is what adapter-objects-and-serving and peft-methods have treated as the conservative default. The experiment that tests it is a clean single-intervention design — identical training, identical adapter, projection into the cells the only variable — and it comes out the other way.

On held-out LAMBADA, the projected model is better in every one of the nine runs that converged, spanning four model sizes, two families and both softmax and gated-linear attention, by 0.97 to 271.3 perplexity points. And the effect is largest exactly where drift is worst: a rank-16 adapter at learning rate 10⁻³ whose unmerged version reaches LAMBADA 311.1 lands at 39.8 after projection, against a 28.67 anchor. It is self-strengthening when it is most needed.

The mechanism is the useful part. The rounding cell is a per-weight trust region whose radii the quantization grid supplies for free, and clipping pulls the update back toward the anchor along precisely the coordinates that moved farthest — which is what a trust region is for. The conservative-looking option is the one that lets the update drift unchecked; the constraint that looked like a tax is regularization you were already paying for when you quantized.

The boundary is reported and it is a real one. On two runs whose unmerged optimization had already diverged past 100× its anchor, projection is not stable: it removes more than half the damage on Mistral (7512 → 3166) and makes matters far worse on a LoRA at lr 3×10⁻³ (6420 → 115,459), because clipping 70.2% of the weights of an incoherent update leaves it incoherent and truncated at 18.2% of its norm. Projection is a trust region, not a repair — it constrains a converging optimization and cannot rescue one that has already failed.

This does not rebut the result in a-swappable-adapter-beats-a-fused-one-when-the-trajectory-needs-both, which is about composition across skills mid-trajectory and is a different question. It rebuts the reading that keeping the adapter separate is safe for the base model's general ability. Measured on the served function rather than on the stored artifact, it is not.
