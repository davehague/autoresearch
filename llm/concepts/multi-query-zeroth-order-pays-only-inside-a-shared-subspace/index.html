A hundred perturbations, a hundredth as many steps, forty-one points

The multi-query paradox is a real published result and the reason nobody bothered: averaging K independent perturbations cuts per-step gradient variance by K, but under a fixed forward-pass budget it also cuts the number of optimization steps by K, and the two cancel exactly. More queries buy nothing.

The claim here is that the cancellation depends on the queries being independent in direction as well as in draw. Restrict every perturbation in a step to the same per-layer low-rank subspace — one fixed pair of column-orthonormal projection matrices, redrawn only every F steps — and the K estimates are expressed in a common basis. Averaging then reinforces the signal components that lie in that basis while cancelling the noise orthogonal to it, rather than averaging K estimates that disagree about which directions exist.

The measurement is the right one to have asked for, because the budget is held fixed at 30,000 forward passes and the step count falls as K rises. SST-2 goes 50.9% at K=1 — indistinguishable from guessing — to 92.2% at K=100. That is not a variance-reduction improvement; the single-query configuration is not learning at all, and K ≤ 10 sits within 5–7 points of random on all three evaluated tasks.

Two features of the sweep are more informative than the headline and support reading this as suggestive rather than settled.

The optima are interior and task-dependent. RTE peaks at K=50 and falls to 57.0 by K=100 as the step count starves; MultiRC barely moves across the entire range, spanning 52.3 to 61.1. That is the shape of a genuine trade with a task-specific balance point, not a monotone win — and it means K joins the learning rate as a knob that has to be found rather than defaulted, which partially offsets the robustness this method buys elsewhere (zeroth-order-fine-tuning-is-brittle-in-the-learning-rate-not-the-loss).

The isolating experiment is missing. The ablation varies K inside the subspace method only. There is no matched full-space multi-query arm at the same budget, so the data are consistent with the shared subspace breaking the paradox and equally consistent with the low-rank restriction doing all the work and multi-query merely riding along. The mechanism is plausible and stated clearly; it is not demonstrated against its own null.

What the result does establish without ambiguity is a downstream consequence the paper exploits well: a clean enough estimate makes per-layer curvature legible. The multi-query gradient norm ‖Z̄_i‖_F varies by an order of magnitude across layers and becomes a usable preconditioning signal, which is why adaptive optimization works here after prior zeroth-order work found Adam offering no advantage over SGD. That whole chain — more queries, cleaner norms, working Adam — costs nothing in memory, because the moment buffers live in r × r (backprop-free-fine-tuning-removes-a-different-memory-term-than-peft-does). It relates to no-fixed-low-dimensional-subspace-survives-a-task-change in an unresolved way: that finding says no fixed subspace carries across tasks, and this method sidesteps it by redrawing the subspace every F steps rather than learning one, which is also the paper's own stated limitation.
