A null is an estimate, not a zero

evaluation-contamination is a checklist of ways a benchmark delta can be manufactured: leakage, construct mismatch, infrastructure non-equivalence, judge bias, the sampling budget the score was read at. Each entry says rule this out with the appropriate control. This adds the layer above the checklist, and it is the one that makes the rest fragile: the control is itself a measurement, and it has a variance nobody measures.

The demonstration is clean enough to state in three lines. The expansion statistic ER_m — problems the base model never solved where the trained model now gets m or more correct out of k — is how the field argues a model acquired a capability rather than sharpened one. At m=1 it fires on a frozen, untrained model 28% of the time (7/25 on an AIME control; 0.176 pooled over 110 frozen comparisons). The obvious fix is m=2, and at m=2 the null looks like zero. Estimated across replicates, it is 0.058 [0.038, 0.078].

So the corrected threshold was validated against a null that had been sampled once, and the single sample happened to be zero. A repair chosen by looking at one realization of the null inherits that realization's noise as a claim. That is a general defect, not a property of expansion statistics.

The replacement is cheap and worth adopting as a habit: pool every independent baseline evaluation the study already owns into one denominator — on AIME here, 11 evaluations giving 1,408 draws per problem — and run a per-problem one-sided exact test under false-discovery-rate control instead of thresholding a difference. Its validation is the right shape: it returns zero detections on 11 held-out replicates, and between-arm conclusions are unchanged under Bonferroni, under different error rates, and under different pool sizes. The authors are explicit that the nulls "cost no new experiments" because the baseline replicates are already present in any multi-arm design — "though not from as few as most possess."

The six other failures in the same audit are worth carrying as a list because several are standard practice: a single greedy decode as the ledger (see greedy-decoding-is-nondeterministic-under-batching); a fixed token cap that scores verbose methods as destructive; transition statistics needing roughly 10× more data than accuracy statistics for the same power; results flipping across training seeds (majority-vote CLR spanning 0.55–1.53); and underpowered safety probes producing effects that replication does not confirm.

The general form for this map: the finer the instrument, the larger the control it needs. Moving from mean accuracy to per-problem transitions is a resolution increase, and it multiplies the data requirement rather than merely reorganising the same numbers. A study that could support a claim about a mean cannot automatically support a claim about which items moved.

Bounded: one model family (Qwen3-8B), three rounds of rank-32 LoRA, expansion evidence resting on 22 low-base problems on one benchmark. The statistical argument, however, does not depend on the subject matter — it depends only on the null having a variance, which it always does.
