On-policy distillation buys sampling efficiency, not capability

distillation-small-models is built on the claim that distillation transfers capability, and its replication leg is a vendor report of "higher Pass@1 and Pass@64." Push the sampling budget to K = 1024 and the curves cross. On AIME2024 the distilled 1.7B student reads pass@32 30.5% against the base's 28.2%, pass@64 33.6% against 35.0%, and pass@1024 53.3% against 70.0% — sixteen points below the checkpoint it was trained from, on the benchmark it was trained for.

Average accuracy moves the other way at every budget (avg@1024: 12.0% against 4.2%), which is the whole problem. Every metric a practitioner reports — pass@1, an averaged score, a leaderboard row — improves, and the one that measures the capability boundary does not. The authors call this "illusory distillation."

The problem-level decomposition says what actually happened. Partition problems by pass@1024 solvability into retained, learned and forgotten: retained dominates, mean accuracy on retained problems roughly doubles (13.5% → 25.9% on Qwen3), and forgotten consistently exceeds learned at large K across all three student–teacher pairs. The student became reliable at what it could already do and lost the tail it used to reach occasionally.

This does not refute distillation-small-models's headline so much as narrow it to a stage. The vault's replication evidence is a pipeline result — off-policy SFT cold start followed by on-policy logit matching — and nothing in that vendor report separates the stages. What this measures is the on-policy stage alone. The map has been reading a pipeline number as evidence about a stage, and it should stop.

It also converges on distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank from a completely different instrument. That source found a teacher distributionally indistinguishable from the student transferring almost nothing, with the reward carried by on-policy-distillation-runs-on-a-shared-high-probability-token-set. This one finds, from a perplexity analysis, that on-policy trajectories score lower under the teacher while staying closer to the base distribution under the base scorer. Two groups, no cross-citation, one statement in different coordinates: the on-policy objective redistributes probability mass inside the student's existing support.

For a reader running one or two cards, the direction is worth stating carefully rather than as a warning. Local deployment samples at K = 1, so the sampling-efficiency gain is real value and the capability loss is invisible — until the workload acquires a retry loop, a best-of-N sampler or a verifier, all of which are standard in agentic use, at which point the base model would have been better.

Bounds: mathematics only (AMC/AIME), three student–teacher configurations, one decoding temperature, students at 1.5–1.7B, no code release, and no limitations section. Whether forgetting still exceeds learning at the 7B–14B sizes a local reader actually distils into is untested and is one training run per size.
