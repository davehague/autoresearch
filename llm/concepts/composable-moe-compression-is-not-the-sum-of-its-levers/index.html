The pipeline is the unit of evaluation, and the field has been measuring its parts

mixture-of-experts has been closing its compression cells one lever at a time — moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity for bit allocation, bit-width-can-be-assigned-by-architectural-role for the coarse version of it, moe-sparsity-becomes-a-transfer-budget-when-weights-are-offloaded for residency. Every one of those holds the other levers fixed. Deployment does not: a practitioner fitting a 30B-A3B model on a card prunes experts, quantizes weights, and compresses the cache, in that order, and this is the first systematic measurement of what the stack does.

The headline is a non-result about a shortcut. Composite quality cannot be predicted from the standalone techniques, and the compression rate does not predict the loss — two configurations at the same ~75–80% compression land in different places, with the more aggressive weight format the worse one. So the standard practice of picking a target ratio and choosing whichever combination reaches it is unsound; the combination has to be measured.

The ordering is stable, though, and useful. Expert pruning dominates the degradation; low-bit weight precision is second; KV compression contributes mainly memory and changes perplexity only marginally. That gives a clean spending order for a practitioner with a memory target: take it out of the cache first, then precision, then experts last — the reverse of the order in which the levers are usually reached for, since expert pruning is the one that looks free because the experts are idle most of the time.

KV precision has a knee at 8 bits. KV-q8 preserves short-context accuracy in every configuration tested, and q4 or TurboQuant-style variants cost measurably more — with the degradation more pronounced in already-pruned models, which is itself a composition effect: the same cache compression is more expensive on a model that has already lost experts. That is an interaction kv-cache-management has no way to see, because everything on that map is measured on dense checkpoints.

Average accuracy hides the failures. Prune+Quant scores 96.35 on average retention and 88.91 on workload robustness. The drops concentrate on MMLU and HumanEval while GSM8K, MuSR and RULER stay stable, and coder-oriented variants inflate perplexity more than instruction-tuned ones under comparable pruning. This is evaluation-contamination's standing complaint arriving on the compression side: a single averaged number is the wrong instrument for a damaged model, and the damage is capability-shaped.

Two method riders worth carrying. The calibration dataset materially changes which capabilities survive pruning — the same dependence quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model and calibration-corpus-choice-swings-a-quantized-models-math-score-again established for quantization, now measured for expert selection. And a quantization-aware pruning score that knows the packed MoE layout it will be composed with beats a module-level one, which is a small argument that the levers should be co-designed rather than merely sequenced.

Limits. Perplexity plus seven benchmarks is the whole quality instrument — no reasoning-trace-length or generation-quality measurement of the kind this vault has repeatedly found to invert compression rankings. Expert pruning is REAP throughout, so "pruning dominates" is a claim about one method's damage profile. Models start at 30B total parameters; the smallest configuration measured (17.1 GB peak) only just fits a 24 GB card, and nothing here is measured at a size that fits 8–16 GB.
