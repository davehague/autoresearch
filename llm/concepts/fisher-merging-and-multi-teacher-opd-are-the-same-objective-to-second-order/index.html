Strand 2's same-base closed-form fusion result generalizes from single-domain fusion to gated multi-teacher combination, and the cheap merge keeps beating the expensive training loop it approximates

This map's 2026-10-16 entry (closed-form-forward-pass-fusion-of-same-base-model-specialists) showed that fusing same-base-model specialists reduces to an exact per-layer least-squares solve from forward-pass statistics — no backward pass, no rollouts. That result was framed as a restricted special case: it works because the specialists share a frozen base, and it said nothing about why a merge should approximate a training-based combination method at all. This paper supplies the missing theory and extends the practical result from single-domain fusion to multi-teacher, multi-domain combination with a gating mechanism.

The theoretical move. Near a shared basin of related specialists, multi-teacher OPD's training objective is, to second order, the Fisher-merging objective — so the expensive thing (sample rollouts, score against teachers, take gradient steps, repeatedly, across teachers) and the cheap thing (solve a Fisher-weighted merge) are approximating the same target, not two different ones. That reframes this map's standing question about OPD's transfer mechanism: at least in the same-basin regime, OPD's benefit over a merge is a second-order correction, not a different kind of learning.

The practical result beats its own baseline. The cheap Phase-1 merge alone (32.52) outperforms a fully-trained multi-teacher OPD student (30.93) that cost 144 GPU-hours to produce — the approximation beats the thing it approximates, on this benchmark. RGBM's Phase 2 (per-domain gating: passivate, isolate for branch training, or admit with calibrated shrinkage) adds a further +0.51, and the gating is load-bearing: every un-gated write-back alternative tested (TIES, DARE, uniform distillation from the merge) scores below the Phase-1 merge, meaning naive write-back is a net loss and the paper's gating mechanism is what converts a residual training signal into a net gain rather than a regression.

Scope, matching this map's existing restriction. Same-basin, same-base-model specialists only — identical to the restriction the 2026-10-16 fusion result carries. Two model families (SmolLM3-3B, Qwen3-4B), two-to-three specialists. No independent replication of the central equivalence claim yet.

What's still open. No wall-clock/GPU-hour figure is given for RGBM Phase 2 beyond "zero extra joint-training steps," so there is still no direct cost comparison at a more heavily-trained OPD baseline than the 600-step student used here. Whether the Fisher-geometric argument extends past same-basin specialists — to teachers that diverged further from a common base, which is the regime this map's single-teacher OPD entries already operate in — is untested by this paper and remains this strand's open question.
