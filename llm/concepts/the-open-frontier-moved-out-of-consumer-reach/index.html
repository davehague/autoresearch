The open frontier is now datacenter-only, and the sparsity dial is why

mixture-of-experts has held since 2026-07-21 that sparse routing decouples parameter count from per-token compute, and since 2026-07-24 that on bandwidth-bound hardware cost tracks total parameters, not active ones. Kimi K3 is what happens when a frontier lab spends the first claim as hard as it can: 2.78T total against 104.2B active, 896 routed experts with 16 live per token. The decoupling worked — 104B of arithmetic buys behaviour that ranks fourth of 580 models on an independent index and first of 99 on WebDev Arena.

It also puts the artifact 1.4 TB of weights away from anything on this map. That is not a criticism of the model; it is the boundary condition this map exists to state. A 12 GB card owner reading "the first open 3T-class model" should read "the first open model that a fleet can serve." The report's own serving section is about scheduling across clusters — cache-aware affinity routing that pins a session to a primary and secondary cluster, and budget-based admission control because per-request cost spans three orders of magnitude. There is no VRAM number, no batch-size-1 tokens/s, no offload configuration, and no discussion of a partially-resident deployment anywhere in 34 pages.

Two things keep this from being simply bad news for this map.

The economics moved in the right direction even though the hardware did not. K3 posts the best BrowseComp score at $2.03 per task, half of GPT-5.6 Sol's and an order of magnitude below the Claude models at maximum effort; on the vendor's own coding suite it is 4.0 points behind the leader at 38% of the cost, and at high effort matches Claude Opus 4.8's maximum-effort score at roughly a third. For the reader who cannot run it, that is still the number that changes what they would do — rent it, don't run it.

And every lever this vault tracks appears in it, decided. Expert granularity, load balancing, activation stability, position encoding, quantization timing, speculative decoding, expert parallelism, activation offload: a single team made a call on each and published which way. That is worth more to this vault than a model it could deploy, because the calls are portable and the model is not. See quantization-can-be-a-training-decision-not-a-post-hoc-one, a-recurrent-state-removes-the-position-encoding-problem, expanding-the-expert-pool-needs-a-latent-width-not-a-full-one.
