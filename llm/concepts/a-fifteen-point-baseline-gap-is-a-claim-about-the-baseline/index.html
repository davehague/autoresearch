A fifteen-point baseline gap is a claim about the baseline

adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact is the most load-bearing methodological result on peft-methods. Ten LoRA variants, four base models, up to sixteen learning rates each: tuned per method, the whole field collapses to a 0.43–1.75% spread, and what the variants genuinely differ in is which step size they want — up to 18.2× apart, because a clever reparameterization moves the largest Hessian eigenvalue and the optimum moves inversely with curvature.

That result is a filter, and it should be applied to every margin this map is offered. AuroOFT reports qoft beating QLoRA by 15.5 Macro-6 points on Qwen2.5-3B math benchmarks, with no learning-rate sweep and no disclosed rates. Orthogonal fine-tuning changes the parameterization more aggressively than any variant in that ten-method study — it learns a rotation of activations rather than an additive low-rank update — so it is precisely the case where a shared or lightly-tuned rate would be expected to favour the new method heavily. And 15.5 points is an order of magnitude beyond anything a parameterization has ever bought here.

Two readings survive the paper. Either the QLoRA arm is undertrained, or exam-style mathematics at 1.5–3B on an NF4 base is a regime where QLoRA genuinely collapses — which would itself be a significant finding, and would deserve to be the paper's headline rather than an unremarked column. The study cannot separate them because it never sweeps. This is the fourth consecutive adapter paper ingested here to run without a sweep, two weeks after the confound was documented.

The salvage is straightforward and the vault should apply it as a habit: carry the matched-branch delta and discard the cross-family one. AuroOFT versus qoft is 1.30–2.70 points, same rotation branch, same protocol, same NF4 base, differing only in the added nonlinear residual — a plausible magnitude for what an added component buys, and consistent with everything else on this map. AuroOFT versus QLoRA at 6.52–10.62 points rides entirely on the suspect column and should not be quoted.

The paper's own honesty about SAT Math — kept in "to expose protocol sensitivity, not to serve as a standalone structural claim" — shows the authors understand that small exam benchmarks are configuration-sensitive. The same scepticism applied one column left would have caught this.
