The best teacher signal is not the most informative one — it is the one the student could have produced

Self-distillation with privileged information is the cheapest thing on this map: no second model, no larger teacher, just the same weights conditioned on something the student will not have at inference. This map has priced multi-teacher setups (multi-teacher-distillation-fails-on-token-accounting-not-teacher-conflict) and on-policy dynamics (off-policy-distillation-transfers-what-on-policy-does-not). It has never had a statement of what to condition the teacher on, and this review's is counter-intuitive enough to be worth carrying even at T3.

The criterion is reconstructibility at test time, imported from robotics: privileged information helps only if the student could plausibly have arrived at it. That inverts the naive ordering. Handing the teacher the final answer is the riskiest option, because an oracle stops deliberating and the student learns to skip the deliberation. A full worked solution is next. Error feedback — which tells the teacher only that something went wrong — is the safest, because it is conditional on the student's own attempt rather than substituting for it.

The quoted margins are consistent with the ordering and none of them are large: step-wise hints beat oracle answers 71.3 versus 59.5 on C-Eval, error-aligned critique beats a reference solution by +5.27 avg@12, and full demonstrations cost up to −17% avg@16 on thinking models. The last is the one to remember — the strongest available signal is not weakly worse, it is actively harmful, and specifically on the models most worth distilling.

This is distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank appearing on a different axis. There, what the student gained tracked how novel the teacher's output was rather than how strong the teacher was. Here, what the student gains tracks how reachable the teacher's advantage is rather than how large. Both say the transferable quantity is a relationship between teacher and student, not a property of the teacher.

Two honest gaps in the source. It does not adjudicate the direct conflict it names — SDFT reporting that self-distillation reduces forgetting against "Denser ≠ Better" reporting the opposite in continual learning — and that conflict sits directly on this vault's question of what a small model loses when derived from a larger one. And the settled/disputed line it draws is drawn among preprints less than six months old, mostly on Qwen at ≤8B, on mathematical reasoning only.

Graded T3. No new experiments; every number is quoted. Useful as a decision procedure to test, not as evidence that the procedure is right.
