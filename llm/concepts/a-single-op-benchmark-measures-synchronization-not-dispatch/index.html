A single-op benchmark measures synchronization, not dispatch

The methodological half of this source is more portable than its WebGPU result. Time one GPU operation the obvious way — encode, submit, wait for completion — and you read 497 μs. Roughly 450 μs of that is the synchronization; the operation itself costs 24–36 μs. The naive figure overestimates per-operation cost by about 20×.

The reason it is an error rather than a conservative estimate is a mismatch of denominators. Autoregressive decoding synchronizes once per token, after a few hundred operations have been queued — so charging one synchronization to one operation prices a cost the workload pays once as though it were paid every time. The corrected method is trivial: submit N identical dispatches with a single synchronization at the end and divide by N, which isolates the marginal cost.

This vault has one rule of this kind already. attention-kernels-are-tuned-to-a-gpu-generation holds that a throughput figure is a claim about a kernel-and-card pair on a date. Add a second: a per-operation cost measured with a synchronization inside the timing loop is a measurement of the synchronization. Both are instrument warnings that change how existing numbers should be read rather than producing new ones.

The uncomfortable consequence for local-inference is that the vault cannot check itself here. None of the map's llama.cpp, MLX or vLLM evidence states its synchronization discipline, and micro-level attributions built on per-operation timings — which is how profiling tables that assign percentages to attention, expert loading or dequantization are usually produced — inherit whatever discipline the profiler used. A profile that reports "expert loading is 87% of decode time" is trustworthy at the aggregate level and unverifiable at the per-op level, and this source gives the specific reason to care.

Graded strong on the claim because it is a decomposition validated against the method it corrects, on released code, at a peer-reviewed venue — and narrow, because it is demonstrated on one API where synchronization happens to be unusually expensive. Whether the same 20× gap appears under CUDA, Metal natively, or Vulkan outside a browser sandbox is not measured here, and the size of the error is certainly API-specific even where its direction is not.
