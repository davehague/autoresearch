It bounds the number; it does not order the list

Evaluating a tokenizer properly costs a pretraining run. Evaluating it intrinsically — from the tokenizer and a text corpus, no model — costs seconds. The whole question is how much of the first the second can replace, and this study answers it with the experiment that matters: fit predictors on a panel, hold out two tokenizers whose models were never in any fit, and see what the fits say about them.

The point predictions hold. Six of eight land inside a 90% interval calibrated on the cross-validation errors, and the cross-validation is leave-one-family-out — a family being a set of tokenizers sharing algorithm, pretokenization strategy and training mixture — so no tokenizer is ever predicted by a fit that saw a sibling. That is a stricter protocol than leave-one-out and it is the reason the result is worth anything.

The ranks do not. The validation-BPB fit places LLaMA-3's tokenizer at predicted rank 1 among forty; its observed value ranks 21st. The prediction interval was wide enough to contain the truth and the ordering inside that interval was noise.

The operational consequence is a two-stage procedure rather than a replacement. Screen the design space intrinsically, discard configurations whose predicted bits-per-byte is clearly outside what you can accept, and then train the survivors — because nothing here will tell you which of three plausible candidates wins. The author states it plainly: the practical use is screening, not selection.

This vault keeps meeting the same shape and should start naming it. an-int4-proxy-ranks-channels-it-cannot-compute found a cheap proxy that identifies which channels matter without being able to compute what they do. a-frozen-weight-spectrum-can-replace-a-calibration-set found a data-free signal standing in for a data-dependent one. In both, and here, the cheap measurement is calibrated but not discriminative — good for a threshold, bad for a ranking — and the failure mode is a practitioner reading a strong aggregate correlation as license to pick the top of the list.

The magnitude of that failure mode is quantified in the same paper, twice over. The strongest correlation in the entire study is ρ = −0.80 between Rényi efficiency and bits-per-byte, which is a strong relationship and still leaves the ordering of nearby candidates unrecoverable. And the aggregate correlations are themselves fragile: seven of ninety-eight change significance status when the metrics are recomputed on a different corpus (a-tokenizer-metric-changes-meaning-with-the-corpus-it-is-measured-on), and several are one-tokenizer-removal away from crossing the corrected threshold.

The bound to attach is statistical power. With n = 29 in the primary panel, the author is explicit that non-significant entries mean "not detected" rather than "absent," and that includes the possibility that a better-powered study finds the ranking signal that this one could not.
