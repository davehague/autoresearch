Selection beats scale at both ends of the pipeline

data-curation-choices-are-ablatable-and-non-monotonic established two things at pretraining scale: curation choices can be ablated rather than argued about, and the intuitive lever inverts — aggressive global dedup hurts, and a cheap learned quality filter beats raw scale. This is the same finding at the fine-tuning stage, from a different group, using the same instrument.

Three variables, separated:

  • Quality is worth +0.50. Two thousand Stack Exchange examples with the paper's quality and style filters score 3.83; the same two thousand unfiltered score 3.33. The filters are unglamorous and specific — answer score ≥10, length between 1200 and 4096 characters, drop first-person or cross-referencing answers, strip links and images — and they are worth half a point on their own.
  • Diversity is worth +0.34. Filtered Stack Exchange (heterogeneous prompts, excellent responses) scores 3.83; wikiHow (equally excellent responses, but every prompt is a "how to" question) scores 3.49. Holding response quality fixed, prompt heterogeneity is independently valuable.
  • Quantity is worth nothing. Exponentially scaling quality-filtered Stack Exchange from 2K to 32K — a 16-fold increase — leaves generation quality flat at roughly 3.8.

The practitioner consequence is direct and is the reason this matters for the vault's premise: if you have a fixed annotation or curation budget, the evidence says spend it on filtering what you have and broadening the prompt distribution, not on collecting more of the same. That is the opposite of the default instinct, and it is now the second time controlled ablations have inverted a data default in this vault.

Read together with the pretraining result, the generalization is stronger than either alone: the same qualitative ranking — quality and diversity dominate, volume saturates — holds at 15 trillion pretraining tokens and at a thousand instruction examples. Neither paper cites the other and they share no methodology beyond "train the variants and measure." That is a principle-level replication rather than a method one.

Bound: the quantity ablation samples only from Stack Exchange, so "more data doesn't help" is really "more data from the same source under the same filter doesn't help" — it does not test adding a genuinely new distribution, which is the move a practitioner would actually make and the one where the diversity result predicts a gain. All three ablations are run at 7B while the paper's headline is 65B, and all are graded by GPT-3.5-turbo on a 1–6 Likert scale, so llm-judges-match-human-agreement-and-carry-systematic-bias applies to precisely the most load-bearing numbers here — the deltas are 0.50 and 0.34 points on a scale scored by the weakest judge in the vault. Still nobody compares teacher synthesis against human collection at matched cost.
