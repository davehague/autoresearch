Buy the accuracy, don't buy the experts

mixture-of-experts closed its expert-offload cell on 2026-07-26 with the rule that guessing which weights you need is free to be wrong, unlike speculative decoding, which guesses tokens and must verify them. This source is what happens when you try to do both at once, and the first thing it finds is that the naive combination is a net loss.

The natural MoE self-drafter needs no training: run the shared expert plus the router's top-r routed experts and skip the rest. Raising r raises acceptance — 22% → 31% → 42% for r = 1, 2, 3 — and raises what the draft must stream, so draft speedup over the full target collapses 4.7× → 2.7× → 2.0×. Even the most expensive arm stays under 50% acceptance. End to end it is worse than no speculation at all: 1.19 tok/s against 2.19 on a 4090, 4.80 against 10.18 on a phone. The drafter pays expert movement for candidates that get rejected.

What breaks the trade is noticing that the two quantities are only coupled if the extra accuracy has to come from extra experts. Add one trainable expert per MoE layer, accelerator-resident, and draft with shared + top-1 + draft expert. Train the draft expert to reproduce the residual the top-1 path omits — the summed contribution of the routed experts you chose not to load — by self-distillation from the frozen target, on unlabeled text, with no separate teacher model. Acceptance goes to 85.0% at 88 unique experts touched against top-1's 84. The whole gain is bought in resident parameters instead of in transfers.

Three things make this more interesting than a speedup number.

It is exact. Every emitted token is still verified by the frozen full MoE, so this sits with speculation-trades-arithmetic-for-latency-without-moving-the-distribution and not with lossy-verification-silently-replaces-the-target-distribution. Truncation and prefetching change only which candidates are proposed and when weights move, never what is accepted.

It is the right shape of intervention for the offload regime. local-inference's standing complaint is that its levers do not compose because they bid for the same idle arithmetic (speculation-and-quantization-compete-for-the-same-headroom). This one spends a third currency — a small amount of resident memory and an offline training run — to buy back transfer volume, which is the scarce resource under offload. It is the second lever this vault has seen that pays in a different denomination, after ffn-activation-sparsity-is-a-training-free-decode-lever.

The phone number is the one to remember. Under Flash→NPU staging the autoregressive baseline is 10.18 tok/s and the resident-memory reference — all experts in RAM, no staging at all — is about 14.36 tok/s. DraftExpert reaches 15.47. The paper does not build a claim on this and reports the resident figure as a reference rather than a baseline, which is the honest framing, but both are measured under the same Q4_0 llama.cpp runtime, and it means the offload penalty is not merely recovered.

The cost this vault has to flag because the paper does not: the added parameter and memory footprint of the draft experts is never stated. One extra expert across 27 layers is real accelerator-resident memory on a device chosen because memory is scarce, and "fixed-footprint" is the paper's own framing, so the number is load-bearing and absent. The distillation compute is also unpriced, which leaves the comparison a 12 GB owner actually faces — this, versus running a dense model that fits — unanswerable, the same gap mixtral-offloading-moe-consumer-inference left open.

Bound: single author, no venue, no code, two models of the same MoE shape, one GPU and one phone, and the two platforms run at different precisions (BF16 on the 4090, Q4_0 on the NPU), so the two speedups are not measured under a common configuration.
