A pruner misses the compression ratio you asked for

The reason to prune at all is that a model does not fit — a memory ceiling, an accelerator, an edge device. So a pruner's interface is a promise about size, and this is the first source to check whether the promise is kept. It is not. Six published structured pruners miss their target compression ratio by up to 33%, averaging a CRAFT of 0.78 and reaching 0.58 at worst, and the misses run toward under-pruning — the model comes back bigger than requested, which is the direction that fails the deployment the pruning was for.

Two things make this worse than a rounding complaint. The deviation is not disclosed in the methods that have it, so a practitioner reads "25% compression" in a table and gets something else on disk. And the workaround the field has been using without naming it is hit-and-trial: sweep targets until the artifact fits. That is a search over an expensive procedure — importance estimation is ~96% of a ~27-minute pruning run — done blind, because nothing reports the miss.

The fix is structural rather than clever. A single-granularity pruner cannot hit an arbitrary budget: depth pruners remove whole blocks, so their reachable sizes are quantised to layer boundaries and they share a fixed deviation per target by construction. Adding a fine-grained second stage whose only job is to fill the residual budget takes CRAFT to 0.98 and holds it across five target ratios and four architectures. The discretisation the knapsack solver needs turns out to be nearly free: α from 8 to 32,768 yields identical pruned models with runtime falling from 22.18 s to 0.01 s, and only collapses when large and small components land in the same bucket.

The generalisable form is a reporting rule, and this vault already has the pattern under two other names. claimed-context-length-is-not-effective-context-length and a-benchmark-score-is-one-draw-from-an-unreported-distribution are the same defect in different literatures: a number in the abstract that a reader will treat as a specification and that nothing in the evaluation verifies. Ask a compression method what it produced, not what it was configured to produce — and for pruning specifically, ask before trusting any retention number, because a method that under-prunes by 33% is being scored at a gentler dose than its label claims.

Bounds. CRAFT is measured on Qwen3-8B across the target range with the per-method table in the appendix; the retention results themselves cover only 25% and 35% (plus a 50% transfer arm). The critique is partly definitional for depth pruners, which cannot hit arbitrary budgets and are being faulted for a property of their granularity — though that is precisely the point, since they ship the same interface. Single source, no independent replication of the CRAFT measurement.

Rebutted by: no-fixed-low-dimensional-subspace-survives-a-task-change — the calibration robustness measured here (structural importance moving 0.85 points across Slim Orca, Alpaca and C4) holds for a structural signal computed over the architecture, and does not extend to an activation-derived feature subspace, which a second T1 finds does not transfer across tasks at all; read the 0.85 as a property of layer-level importance rather than of importance estimation in general.
