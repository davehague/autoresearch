Standard benchmarks can miss the pretraining-scale architecture comparisons that actually matter, and miss basic reasoning failures that survive at any scale

TL;DR: Allen-Zhu's controlled pretraining study finds that whether a Transformer beats a linear-attention model (or vice versa) on standard benchmarks depends heavily on how much the comparison is scaled: at 1.3B parameters and 100B tokens, careful per-architecture tuning erases most of the difference; at 1-2T tokens the differences reappear and are large enough to matter in practice. The clearest reappearing difference is that Canon layers — a cheap local-token-mixing addition bolted onto an otherwise-ordinary Transformer block — save roughly 30% of the pretraining tokens needed to hit a given MMLU accuracy at 8B parameters. Two new variants, GLA5 and GDN2, add low-rank output gates to gated linear attention and Gated DeltaNet respectively; with Canon layers added on top, the simplest of the linear-attention family (gated linear attention) matches Gated DeltaNet's benchmark scores and beats Mamba2's, at 8B parameters and 1T tokens — implying that Mamba2's and Gated DeltaNet's additional architectural machinery over plain GLA contributes little once local token mixing is supplied separately. Underneath all of this, the paper reports a harder finding: even the highest-scoring 8B/1T models of any architecture, Transformer included, fail to reliably retrieve information from a short 500-token context and do not beat random guessing on the simplest possible two-hop question. Small synthetic playgrounds under 100M parameters correctly predicted the Canon/GLA5/GDN2 differences the large runs confirm, which the paper uses as its argument for building and trusting such playgrounds over standard-benchmark scores alone.

Why it matters here. transformer-architecture has tracked a linear-attention/hybrid-reasoning lineage (Gated DeltaNet → Kimi Linear → GDN-2 → Muir) entirely through comparisons at ≤1.34B parameters. This source is the first to test whether such comparisons survive scaling to 1-2T tokens at all, and the answer is a qualified no: differences that look settled at academic scale can flip or vanish by 8B/1T, which retroactively weakens the confidence this map should place in any smaller-scale ranking within that lineage until it is re-tested at this scale. Canon layers, in particular, are nearly free relative to Gated DeltaNet's or Mamba2's added parameterization, which bears directly on the map's recurring question of which components of a hybrid architecture are load-bearing versus decorative. The two-hop-reasoning failure, present even in the Transformer and even at the scale where Canon's advantage is clearest, is new evidence that this vault's standard-benchmark-based architecture rankings in general may not detect basic in-context reasoning failures.

Caveats. Single-author study; the benchmarks used to demonstrate "standard benchmarks miss this" (500-token retrieval, two-hop QA) are the author's own constructed synthetic probes, not independently validated instruments. All models are trained from scratch by one group on one data source (Nemotron-CC) — no comparison to a released frontier model at these scales, and no test of whether Canon layers or GLA5/GDN2 compose with any released model's existing architecture. Code and results are public, but no independent replication of the 1-2T-token runs (at ~$100k each) exists yet.
