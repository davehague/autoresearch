What is left to repair depends on who quantized it

The headline is that a 49 MB sidecar closes 88% of a round-to-nearest checkpoint's perplexity gap to BF16. The finding is the row underneath it: the same sidecar does essentially nothing for AWQ.

Read that as a statement about the parents rather than about the codec. AWSRC fits its residual under an activation-weighted objective — it repairs the error coordinates that actually move layer outputs under the model's real input distribution. Round-to-nearest, which never looks at activations, leaves exactly that kind of error lying around in quantity. AWQ and GPTQ spend their calibration budget removing it. What survives their optimization is error the same objective cannot see, so a codec built on that objective has nothing to grip.

For post-training-quantization this sharpens what "repair" is worth. The map holds several results about the allocation of bits (expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer, bit-width-can-be-assigned-by-architectural-role, the-lm-head-is-the-tensor-four-bit-quantization-skips) and several about the procedure (the-ptq-optimizer-is-a-separate-lever-from-the-objective, below-three-bits-the-quantization-procedure-outweighs-the-format). This adds a composition rule between them: a post-hoc corrector and a calibrated quantizer are substitutes, not complements — they are competing for the same structured error, and whichever runs first takes it.

The practitioner form is narrow and useful. If you have a calibrated 4-bit build, this buys you nothing. If you quantized cheaply — on the device, without a calibration corpus, in a build step that could not afford GPTQ's sequential pass — a detachable sidecar recovers most of the difference at under a fifth of a bit per weight, and can be removed later without touching the backbone format.

Two limits before generalizing. The strong-parent arm is a clean-room reimplementation of QAM-W whose absolute numbers differ from the published ones, so only the matched pairs support the claim; the authors say so. And every number is WikiText-2-calibrated and WikiText-2-measured — calibration-content-is-the-dominant-term-below-four-bits says that is a first-order term at this bit width, and nothing here varies it.
