The proxy has a floor and you can push straight through it

Almost every post-training quantization method minimizes the same thing: the output-reconstruction error of a linear layer, ‖WX − W_ref X‖²_F, weighted by the activation Gram matrix. It is a surrogate for what anyone actually wants, which is the end model's loss, and the field has treated it as a surrogate that is monotone enough to optimize freely.

This study contains two independent demonstrations that it is not.

Across optimizers. Holding the objective exactly fixed and installing a strictly better discrete optimizer, the effect on the end model changes sign with bit width: at 2 bits perplexity falls 258.96 → 81.95 and accuracy rises 11.88 points; at 3 bits perplexity rises 15.79 → 21.26 and accuracy moves −0.12; at 4 bits perplexity rises again and accuracy moves +0.08. The optimizer is better at its stated job in every case. The model is only better at 2 bits.

Within one optimizer. The refinement-count sweep is the cleaner half, because the local objective is monotone non-increasing in R by construction. Perplexity improves 70.46 → 20.54 from R=0 to R=4 — a 49.92 gain — and then does nothing at all: 21.41, 21.19, 21.21 across R = 8, 16, 32, a total spread of 0.87, non-monotone. Four sweeps extract everything the surrogate has to say; the next twenty-eight fit it more tightly for free and buy nothing.

The reading that transfers is a regime boundary, not a rejection of the surrogate. At 2 bits, search error dominates: the grid is coarse enough that a better discrete assignment is a genuinely different model, and reconstruction error and end quality still move together. At 3–4 bits, the grid is fine enough that nearest-level rounding is nearly optimal already, the remaining reconstruction error is small, and driving it down further is fitting the calibration set's activation statistics rather than improving the model. That is the same failure this vault records from the data side in quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model and calibration-content-is-the-dominant-term-below-four-bits, arriving through the optimizer instead.

It also gives a mechanism to perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats, which this vault has held as an empirical warning. Here the disagreement is three-way and visible inside one paper's tables: local reconstruction loss, perplexity, and six-task zero-shot accuracy each rank the same configurations differently, with cells where perplexity prefers GPTQ and accuracy prefers the alternative by over two points. Three metrics, three orderings, one set of weights.

The practitioner rule is short. Below three bits, a better optimizer is worth real money. At four bits, stop optimizing the surrogate — pick your method on task accuracy or not at all. And when a quantization paper reports only perplexity at 4 bits, that number is measuring something the end model does not follow.
