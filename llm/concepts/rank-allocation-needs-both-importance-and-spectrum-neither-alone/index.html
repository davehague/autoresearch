Rank allocation needs both importance and spectrum, neither alone

where-the-adapter-budget-goes-matters-more-than-how-much-there-is established on this map that placement dominates size — a fixed rank budget spent well beats a larger one spent evenly. It left open the question of what "well" is scored by, and two published methods answer it differently: GoRA allocates on sensitivity (a layer's marginal contribution to the downstream loss), RaLoRA on intrinsic dimensionality derived from the gradient's effective rank.

The argument that neither is sufficient is clean enough to state without the experiment. A layer can be highly sensitive to the loss while its gradient is nearly one-dimensional — it matters, but one direction captures it, and extra rank is wasted. A layer can have a broad gradient spectrum while being irrelevant to the downstream task — many directions, none of them useful. Sensitivity answers whether a layer matters; effective rank answers how many independent directions its gradient needs. Neither implies the other, so a criterion using one is systematically wrong on the layers where they disagree.

Multiplying normalised sensitivity by normalised effective rank grants capacity only when both hold. On Llama-3.1-8B across three seeds, the ablation separates cleanly: sensitivity-only 72.98, effective-rank-only 73.51, the product 73.94, and no adaptive allocation at all 72.40.

Two readings of the size of that gap, and the second matters more.

The generous one: each single criterion is beaten by margins (0.96 and 0.43 points) comparable to the paper's headline margin over its strongest published baseline (1.03), so this is not a rounding effect within the paper's own frame of reference.

The deflationary one, which is this map's house style: those margins are also comparable to the reported standard deviations on several rows of the same table (effective-rank-only carries ±0.51, no-allocation ±1.32), and the whole spread from worst to best allocation criterion — 72.40 to 73.94 — is 1.54 points against a plain-LoRA-to-full-fine-tuning gap of 5.91. Allocation criteria are arguing over a quarter of the gap they are all trying to close. That is worth knowing before treating the choice of criterion as important.

One structural caveat the authors name themselves: the score uses a single global exponent λ across all layers and all tasks, with no reported sensitivity analysis in the main text. A free scalar tuned once and then held fixed is exactly the kind of parameter the learning-rate critique on this map has taught it to look for, and this one is unswept.
