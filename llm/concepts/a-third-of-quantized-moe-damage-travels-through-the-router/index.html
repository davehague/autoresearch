A third of quantized-MoE damage travels through the router

moe-inference-cost-tracks-total-not-active-params-on-edge settled that routing is not where the time goes: node-level decode-graph timing put routing under 9% of MoE-block cost, which is why mixture-of-experts treats the sparsity deficit on edge hardware as structural rather than as a kernel awaiting optimization. This measures a different quantity in a different currency. Under compression, roughly a third of the extra loss goes through the routing decision — 0.31 with a 95% interval of [0.20, 0.41], replicating at 0.429 on a pre-registered held-out split.

The apparatus is what earns the grade. Four runs on the same tokens: clean compute with a free route, quantized compute with the clean route pinned, quantized compute with a free route, and clean compute with the quantized route transplanted. Route-mediated excess is the gap between quantized-free and quantized-pinned, which is a counterfactual rather than an association. An INT8 arm reads −0.00006, so the effect is dose-dependent and not an artifact of the measurement chain.

The mechanism this exposes is specific to where the compression is applied, and it is the reason this result has no in-vault precedent. Every MoE-quantization source here — moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity, expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer, bit-width-can-be-assigned-by-architectural-role — compresses expert weights and leaves the router in full precision, so the routing decision is disturbed only downstream. Quantizing the KV cache perturbs the hidden states the router reads, which is the shortest possible path to a flipped route. The consequence is uncomfortable for the map: an expert-quantization result measured against a full-precision cache is measuring a system whose routing is intact, and every one of them was.

The two paths also fail to separate. The compute-by-route interaction is +0.0083 nats [+0.0039, +0.0124], excluding zero — so "a third routing, two thirds arithmetic" is a decomposition at this dose and not a budget you can carry to another one. The paper's own real-kernel check makes the point: at an aggressive int2 dose the measured route share falls below its predicted band, because an optimized kernel loads more damage onto the direct channel.

Held at suggestive despite the design. The primary disturbance is a simulated fake-quantizer, with the real int4 anchor at 0.194 [−0.111, 0.394] — compatible but including zero.
