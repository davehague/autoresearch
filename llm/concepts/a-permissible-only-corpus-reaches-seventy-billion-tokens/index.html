A permissible-only corpus reaches seventy billion tokens

Every data finding in data-curation is about selection — data-filtering-is-a-compute-limited-strategy-not-a-data-property, filtering-a-generated-corpus-outperforms-generating-more-of-it, finetuning-data-quality-and-diversity-beat-quantity — and every one of them presupposes a pool you are legally free to select from. This is the first source here to treat that pool as the binding constraint and to measure what respecting it costs.

The answer is: less than expected, and the reason is regeneration rather than restraint. The corpus admits only openly licensed data, data supplied under agreement, and data covered by the EU's text-and-data-mining exception for research institutions. Where a needed dataset failed that test, the authors did not drop it — they synthetically recreated it, generating replacement ("transplant") variants with a 31B model and auditing them, at acceptance rates ranging "from single digit percentages to high nineties." That accounts for 11.08% of 70.48B tokens, and the report states the synthetic alternatives "achieve comparable or superior performance without compromising data rights." The model trains from scratch on 8 B200s in under three weeks and beats every open model in its class on its target language by 20 points, and two 8–9B models by 11.

The reusable claim is that a legal filter is a curation constraint like any other, and it behaves like one. It removes mass from specific regions of the distribution, and the removed mass can be partially reconstructed from a permissible generator — which is the same operation synthetic-instructions-from-a-blind-teacher-bootstrap-a-corpus and filtering-a-generated-corpus-outperforms-generating-more-of-it describe, applied to a rights boundary instead of a quality boundary. For anyone whose data pool is bounded by policy rather than by budget — which is a large and growing population — that is the finding: the compliant corpus is buildable, and the gap is closed by generation plus audit rather than by acquisition.

The composition is where this vault should hold back. The corpus is extremely concentrated: ten datasets are 66.5% of it, three are 38.1%, and a single Danish instruction set is repeated 4× to become 11.8% of all tokens, with eight more repeated 10× and one 20×. That is exactly the regime where data-curation-choices-are-ablatable-and-non-monotonic says the mixture ratios are the experiment — and no ablation is run. Whether the result is evidence about permissibility, about the HRM architecture, about repetition schedules, or about the Danish-heavy mixture is not separable from this report. The one clean contrast it does contain is the same-architecture, same-scale baseline: against HRM-Text 1B, Mimir gains +35.1 points on Danish and +36.7% on math and code with the data mixture as the only difference.

Graded T2 / suggestive: technical report with open weights and a released training framework, corpus enumerated to the token, self-evaluated with no independent replication and no ablation isolating any variable. Bounds: one architecture, one scale, two target languages, and licensing that is not yet fully open — a small agreement-supplied corpus (DBC, Lex.dk) cannot be shared, which the report discloses. The English-side benchmark comparisons should be read through training-on-the-answer-format-is-most-of-a-benchmark-win before being quoted; the data-rights result and the Danish results do not depend on them.
