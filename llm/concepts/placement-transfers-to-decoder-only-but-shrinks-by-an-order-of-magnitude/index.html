The fourth knob works on a decoder, and it is a small knob

where-the-adapter-budget-goes-matters-more-than-how-much-there-is named placement as a fourth independent lever on a low-rank update and measured it once: hold the trainable budget at 0.28M on a 183M encoder and MNLI moves 10.7 points depending only on which matrices and which layers get the adapter. The note also named the bound in the same paragraph — no decoder-only model appeared anywhere in that source — and peft-methods has carried "placement is the only lever with a large unexplained effect" ever since.

The experiment has now been run on decoders, and the honest summary is: the direction survives, the magnitude does not. Three backbones at 8B and 14B, three seeds, total rank budget pinned, ranks assigned once before training. Every cell favours non-uniform allocation, and the average gain is +1.13 GSM8K points — real against ±0.3–0.9 seed variance, and an order of magnitude below the encoder spread.

Two structural reasons the gap is unsurprising, and both are worth stating because they say where the encoder number should and should not be trusted. The encoder measurement was a spread across extremes — best placement against worst placement, including a pathological "adapt only layers 1–3" arm — while this is a good allocation against the flat default, which is nobody's worst case. And an 8B decoder has far more redundant capacity to route an update through than a 183M encoder, so a misplaced budget has more ways to recover. The two numbers are not measuring the same quantity, and the vault should stop citing 10.7 as "what placement is worth" without that qualifier.

Where the budget wants to go replicates cleanly across the two sources, which is the part that generalizes: mid-to-late feed-forward modules, with shallow layers compressed to the floor. That matches the encoder finding that the two FFN matrices outscore every attention projection and that the top layer band outscores the bottom by ten points, and it lands against tooling defaults from opposite directions — the original LoRA paper's W_q/W_v targeting is the wrong end of both sweeps, and "adapt every linear module" is flat by construction.

One number the paper does not comment on and this map should. On Qwen3-14B, doubling the uniform LoRA rank budget from 4 to 8 makes it worse (84.84 → 84.69), while the allocated version improves (85.44 → 85.67). That is the symptom the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank predicts — extra rank buying nothing under alpha/r — showing up at ranks far below the ones rsLoRA studied, and it is one datum, unreplicated, at a rank step of two.

The reason to hold this at suggestive is the protocol. Every method in every table runs at a fixed learning rate of 1e-4 with no sweep. adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact, published two weeks earlier, showed that a shared default learning rate is exactly the confound that manufactured this literature's previous round of wins, and that adapter parameterizations want optima up to 18× apart. A non-uniform rank profile changes per-module effective step sizes, so it is precisely the kind of intervention that confound catches. Nobody has re-run this under a per-method sweep, and until someone does, "+1.13 points" is an upper bound on a possibly smaller number.
