Bit-width can be assigned by architectural role

post-training-quantization holds two mixed-precision allocators for sparse models and they disagree about the unit. moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity scores layers from weight spectra with no calibration data at all. expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer scores experts from 128 calibration sequences and wins on perplexity in every cell. Both maps carry the same complaint about both: neither is reachable from the local stack, because llama.cpp's K-quant menu cannot express a per-layer or per-expert assignment.

This is a third unit, and it is the coarsest one that still buys something: the role a tensor plays in the architecture. Dense layers get 4 bits. Routed experts get 2. The shared expert — the one every token passes through — gets 8. There is no search, no calibration pass and no sensitivity estimator; the allocation falls out of a single measured statistic, the shared expert's activation kurtosis at 10.1 against 0.41 for the routed specialists. Heavy-tailed activations are what activation-outliers-are-the-barrier-to-w8a8 identifies as the thing aggressive quantization cannot absorb, so the tensor with the outliers is the tensor that keeps its bits.

The reason this matters more than its quality delta is granularity. Per-layer and per-expert allocation need a representation that assigns a bit-width to an arbitrary tensor index. Per-role needs a three-way partition of tensor names — dense, routed expert, shared expert — which is exactly what GGUF-side per-tensor overrides already operate on. So this is the first mixed-precision sparse allocation in the vault whose granularity the local stack could represent today, and nobody has checked whether the 4/2/8 split reproduces under K-quants rather than under the paper's own Metal kernels.

Two things keep this at suggestive. There is no ablation isolating the allocation: the paper reports whole-system footprints and a pass/fail quality gate, never the same models at uniform 4-bit or with the shared expert dropped to 2, so how much of the win belongs to the role split rather than to the cache compression and the offload is unmeasured. And a role partition is by construction blind to the variation the other two allocators exist to exploit — moe-experts-differ-in-quantization-sensitivity found that fine-grained MoEs have far more exploitable variance than vanilla ones, and a scheme with three buckets cannot spend it. What this trades is headroom for reachability, and the trade has not been priced.
