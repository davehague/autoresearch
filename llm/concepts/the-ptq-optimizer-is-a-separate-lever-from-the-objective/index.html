GPTQ's decision rule was never the clever part

The thing worth noticing about GPTQ is what the curvature is actually used for. It visits weights along the input-column axis, rounds each one to the nearest representable level, and then propagates the resulting error into the weights it has not reached yet through an inverse Hessian. The Hessian shapes the repair. The decision is round-to-nearest — which is why the whole procedure is provably Babai's nearest-plane algorithm on the lattice defined by XXᵀ.

Almost all subsequent work changed what is being minimized: asymmetric calibration, propagated activation error, residual-path reconstruction, end-loss gradients. This source holds the objective exactly equal to GPTQ's and changes only how the integer assignment is searched, which is the experiment that prices the neglected half.

Two changes. When a group is quantized, the already-quantized prefix is fixed and the suffix is still continuous, so part of any error the group introduces will be absorbed downstream. Eliminating the suffix's optimal continuous response analytically leaves an exact reduced quadratic whose curvature is the Schur complement S = G_cc − G_cr G_rr⁻¹ G_rc — provably no larger than the raw block G_cc that prior coordinate-descent methods optimized against. And within the group, the scale and zero-point are refit in closed form rather than frozen at initialization, alternating with coordinate descent over the integer codes.

The 2×2 ablation is what makes this a finding rather than a method paper, because it separates the conditioning from the two things it could be confused with:

Grid refitting is the larger single lever, which is consistent with what this vault already holds in the-quantization-parameterization-outranks-the-rounding-rule. But the two are not substitutes: conditioning buys another 37% relative on top of refitting, and neither coordinate descent nor refitting alone produces the 2-bit result. revising-the-rounding-buys-a-point-without-calibration-data found roughly a point from a better rounding rule with no calibration; this is the calibrated, curvature-aware version of the same idea, and at 2 bits it is worth twelve.

Where it pays is the part to remember. The full system's margin over the strongest baseline is +9.65 points at 2 bits, +2.16 at 3, and +0.68 at 4 — the advantage is a function of how coarse the grid is, because on a fine grid a better discrete assignment and a nearest-level one increasingly agree. Nobody quantizing to 4 bits should reach for this. It is also worth noting what it does not change: the output is the same scalar per-row group format GPTQ emits, so no inference kernel is affected (two-bit-recovery-can-be-bought-offline-without-touching-the-kernel).

The bound is that above 2 bits this same optimizer makes perplexity worse while barely moving accuracy — see tighter-layer-reconstruction-stops-paying-above-two-bits, which is the same study's other half and the reason the paper had to bolt an augmented objective onto its optimizer to stay ahead at 3 and 4 bits.
