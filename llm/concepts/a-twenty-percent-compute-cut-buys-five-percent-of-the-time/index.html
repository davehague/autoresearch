The arithmetic you deleted was not the time you were spending

local-inference's most-repeated result is that a saving on paper is not a saving on a machine. at-batch-size-one-the-bottleneck-is-dispatch-count found the CPU path spending its time issuing work rather than doing it; a-single-op-benchmark-measures-synchronization-not-dispatch found the standard microbenchmark measuring the wrong thing entirely; cache-compression-charges-throughput-and-pays-only-under-pressure found a memory saving that costs throughput until the memory is actually scarce. This is the same lesson from a fourth direction, and it is unusually clean because the authors report both halves.

Cut 20% of the contraction axis out of every attention matrix product — a genuine 20% arithmetic reduction — and measure it end to end on an A100 at batch size 1. At 1024 tokens you get 1.05×. The kernels themselves are doing much better than that (PV alone is 1.67× at the same length, with the top-k selection overhead already charged against it), so the gap is everything the kernels are not: the dense MLP that was left alone, the launch and framework overhead, the parts of the step that do not scale with the saving. Push the sequence to 4096 and the same intervention reads 1.40×, because the reduced work grows with length while the fixed costs do not.

The practitioner form is a question to ask of any efficiency claim: at what sequence length, and against what measured baseline? A 20%-FLOP-reduction headline is compatible with a 5% speedup, and this vault has now recorded that conversion loss for KV compression, for CPU dispatch, for single-op microbenchmarks and now for matrix-product reduction. reproducing-your-own-output-costs-most-of-the-speedup and accepted-length-can-be-anti-correlated-with-speedup are the speculative-decoding versions of the same disease. The rule this map keeps re-deriving is that the only number worth anything is the one from your own hardware, your own runtime and your own sequence length — the-same-four-bit-weights-run-faster-on-mlx-than-on-llama-cpp and kernel-performance-expires-with-the-gpu-generation are the two sharpest statements of it here.

Two riders on how far to carry these particular numbers. The baseline is HuggingFace generate with SDPA, not a tuned serving stack, so the 1.05× is a comparison against a fairly generous reference — against llama.cpp or vLLM the conversion would likely be worse, not better. And all of it is one A100, a datacenter card, at batch size 1; the standing complaint on this map is that the results that matter here are the ones measured on 12–24 GB consumer hardware, and this is not one of them. What transfers is the shape of the curve, not the multipliers.

The direction it points is nevertheless useful for a single-card reader: this is a long-context lever, not a general one. If your workload is short prompts, the arithmetic you delete is not the time you are spending, and attention-is-memory-bound-not-compute-bound already said why.
