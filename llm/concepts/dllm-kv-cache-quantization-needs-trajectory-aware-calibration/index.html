A cache-quantization lever this vault has treated as substrate-agnostic is not: diffusion language models reuse KV states across a bidirectional denoising process that drifts before any quantizer touches it, and the first dLLM-specific quantizer only partially, not uniformly, beats quantizers designed for exact autoregressive caches

TL;DR: Every KV-cache quantization entry on this map so far (eviction, rotation, MLA role-split, learned codecs) assumes the thing being quantized is a cached state fixed once computed under a causal mask — true for autoregressive decoding, not true for diffusion language models (dLLMs), whose Fast-dLLM-style inference reuses cached key/value states across iterative bidirectional denoising steps even though the underlying representations keep shifting as more masked positions resolve. This paper is the map's first KV-cache quantizer built for that substrate. It names the gap (a reused dLLM cache is already an approximation of a full recomputation, before quantization adds a second, independent source of error) and answers it with two pieces: TRACE, which reconstructs a plausible per-token reveal-order trajectory from a prompt-answer pair (iterative masked-state rollouts scored by answer-token log-probability, resolved by min-cost max-flow) to produce calibration states that match how masked decoding actually unfolds rather than assuming a fixed schedule; and dLLM-KVQ, which uses those trajectories to keep a sensitivity-selected subset of per-layer key-cache entries at high precision while quantizing the rest of K and all of V with a standard low-bit path borrowed from KIVI/KVQuant. Tested on LLaDA-Instruct and Dream-Base-7B across GSM8K/HumanEval/MATH/MBPP at 4-bit and 2-bit, prefix and dual cache-reuse modes, dLLM-KVQ beats both AR-adapted baselines' average score in six of eight cells — but loses to KVQuant specifically on Dream-Base-7B dual-cache GSM8K at both bit-widths, so the gain is real but not a clean sweep.

What it says.

Why AR cache quantizers don't transfer cleanly. KIVI and KVQuant calibrate and quantize on the premise that a cached key/value state, once written under a causal mask, never changes — only the quantization step distorts it. A dLLM under Fast-dLLM-style prefix/dual cache reuse violates that premise structurally: its representations are bidirectional and shift as more of a masked block is revealed across denoising steps, so the reused cache already differs from a freshly recomputed one, independent of any quantization. Applying AR quantizers to this setting means calibrating against a moving target.

TRACE. Given a prompt-answer pair, assign each answer token to one legal reveal step within its block's budget window; start from a schedule-consistent feasible assignment; repeatedly roll out the masked states under the current assignment and score every (token, step) pairing by that token's log-probability under the model; resolve the next assignment via min-cost max-flow; repeat to convergence or a round limit. Only the prompt, answer, and final reveal assignment are retained — calibration needs no persisted logits or caches beyond that schedule, recomputing on demand.

dLLM-KVQ. Using TRACE-derived trajectories, select per-layer K-cache entries to keep at high precision by output-level and attention-level sensitivity scores, quantize the remainder of K and all of V via a standard low-bit QDQ path. Calibration uses eight trajectory samples per (model, cache-mode, bit-width) combination.

Results. Across LLaDA-Instruct and Dream-Base-7B, 4-bit and 2-bit, prefix and dual cache: dLLM-KVQ's average beats both KIVI and KVQuant on LLaDA at both bit-widths and both cache modes, and on Dream prefix-cache at both bit-widths — but trails KVQuant specifically on Dream dual-cache at both bit-widths (most visibly on GSM8K). Per-task wins split across methods rather than one method sweeping every benchmark. The mixed-precision K selection spends slightly above its nominal bit budget (effective 4.77 bits prefix, 4.60 dual) where the sensitivity selector retains entries at full precision.

Caveats. No throughput, latency, or memory measurement anywhere — the authors state these need an optimized fused low-bit attention kernel they have not built, so every reported number is accuracy/bit-accounting, not a serving-cost claim. Single runs, no seed variation reported. Two dLLMs, two cache modes, four tasks, two bit-widths — no sequence-length or batch-size sweep, no comparison to any dLLM-native cache method (none exists in this vault yet), and the V-cache path is an unmodified AR low-bit policy the authors themselves flag as a likely place for further improvement. No code or policy-file release stated.
