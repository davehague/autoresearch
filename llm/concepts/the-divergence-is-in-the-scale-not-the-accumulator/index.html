The divergence is in the scale, not the accumulator

The reflex explanation for two GPU kernels producing different numbers is accumulation order: floating-point addition is not associative, different reduction trees give different sums, and there is nothing to be done. That explanation is unavailable for INT8, and proving it unavailable is what turns a reproducibility complaint into a localization.

For shared INT8 operands, if no dot product overflows INT32, the accumulator value is an exact integer, independent of reduction order. The paper verifies the operative bound 16256·K < 2³¹ — reduction depth K ≤ 132,104 — for every measured layer, with 2.82–3.36 bits of headroom to the 2²⁴ threshold below which the result also enters float32 losslessly. So the accumulator is eliminated by argument, and whatever differs must happen after it.

What happens after it is scale application and output rounding. The test is direct: round weight scales to the nearest power of two, making the scale multiply exactly representable, feed both kernels identical operands from every linear layer, and the outputs become bit-identical — 196/196 layers at 1.7B (pre-registered), 252/252 at 8B. Under the checkpoints' real, non-power-of-two scales the difference reappears but is bounded: no element across 448 layers exceeds one bfloat16 spacing.

The intervention closes the loop at the sequence level. Applied as a probe checkpoint, power-of-two scaling restores end-to-end bitwise agreement, 8/8 and 16/16 — so the layer-level cause and the total sequence disagreement of two-int8-kernels-of-the-same-model-agree-on-no-sequence are linked by an intervention rather than by argument.

The FP8 contrast is the scope line, and it matters more than the fix. Sweep reduction depth K from 512 to 32,768. INT8 produces 18 differing elements in total, 1.9–7.6 parts per million, with no detectable growth in K — consistent with a bounded rounding step applied once. FP8's divergence fraction climbs 8.2% → 52.7%, with magnitude growing too, fitted by a saturation model with exponent α ≈ 0.518. Under FP8 the accumulator is implicated, the alibi does not hold, and the same audit would return a different verdict.

That distinction is the durable content for post-training-quantization. As this map's allocation sub-thread migrates toward low-precision floating-point formats — FP8, MXFP4, NVFP4 — it is migrating from a regime where cross-implementation differences are a one-ULP rounding artifact that a scale change removes, into one where they accumulate with the depth of the reduction and no such fix is offered. The map has been treating INT and FP low-precision formats as points on one bit-width ladder; on the reproducibility axis they are different objects.

The practical residue is the conformance procedure: seven auditable checks, five with falsifiable outcomes rather than tunable thresholds — shared operands, INT32 no-overflow, lossless float32 entry, exact accumulator, power-of-two scale identity. The power-of-two check is the strongest because it converts a rounding argument into a required equality, which either holds or does not.

The honest caveats are the author's own: the probe is not a validated mitigation (no accuracy, calibration or throughput evaluation was run on it), the checks have never been executed against a deliberately faulty kernel so their sensitivity is unmeasured, the alibi covers only the INT8 GEMM (attention, normalization and the unquantized head are outside it), and the FP8 signature rests on seven grid points from one implementation pair.
