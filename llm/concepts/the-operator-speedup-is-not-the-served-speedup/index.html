The operator speedup is not the served speedup

local-inference reads a lot of kernel numbers. This is the clearest single-paper demonstration of how far one travels before it becomes something a request experiences, and it is useful precisely because the authors publish every intermediate step rather than the largest one.

Four measurements of one change to block-sparse prefill attention, at 128K context on an H20:

What is measuredFP8BF16
Attention operator vs FlashAttention-247.26×27.19×
Attention operator vs an FA3/4-aligned dense kernel30.49×17.54×
End-to-end time-to-first-token via SGLang3.66–4.83×2.14–3.40×
Same, with chunked prefill at 8K chunks—1.2–1.6×

Each division is a different mechanism, and each is legitimate.

The first removes a confound rather than a cost. FlashAttention-2 is an old dense baseline; comparing a Hopper-native sparse kernel against it measures sparsity plus three years of kernel engineering. The FA3/4-aligned dense control is the honest denominator for a claim about sparsity, and it costs the headline about a third.

The second is Amdahl's law, and the paper states it: end-to-end speedup "is bounded by the attention share of total prefill compute." Attention is a fraction of prefill; prefill is a fraction of a request. Ten-and-a-half more×'s disappear here.

The third is the one this vault had no example of. Chunked prefill is not an optional extra — it is the standard mechanism production engines use to stop long prompts from stalling concurrent decode, and this vault already carries it as a good trade (chunked-prefill-converts-a-memory-ceiling-into-a-throughput-tax). Turning it on erodes the sparse win, because block-index selection re-runs on every chunk and the mandatory tail blocks raise the effective density of short chunks. The interaction is not additive and it is not small: 2.2× becomes 1.2× on Qwen3-30B-A3B. Two independently-beneficial optimizations partially cancel, and only the paper that measures them together can tell you.

The last row of the table is the one to carry into any local reading. At 4K context in BF16 the method is at parity — 1.01–1.09× — because measured attention density there is 70–76% and the index stage costs what the sparsity saves.

The scope: this is one method, on one workload family, on datacenter accelerators. What generalizes is not the ratio but the discipline — when a paper reports a kernel speedup, the questions are (1) what is the dense baseline actually running, (2) what fraction of the request is this kernel, and (3) what scheduling feature will already be on when it ships. evaluation-contamination classifies this as construct mismatch: the number is correct and answers a question adjacent to the one being asked.
