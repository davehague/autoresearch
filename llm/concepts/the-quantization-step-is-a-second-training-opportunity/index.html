The 4-bit checkpoint came out better than the 16-bit one

post-training-quantization is organized around a premise so basic it is rarely stated: quantization is a lossy step, the compressed model approximates the uncompressed one, and every method on the map is a better approximation under a budget. This is a counter-instance, and the mechanism is not exotic.

Under compound compression — structural compression to a smaller architecture, then 4-bit quantization — the intermediate bfloat16 checkpoint was never independently trained. It is a distillation-recovered approximation of the original, carrying the compressed architecture's capacity ceiling. So if you are going to run gradient steps at quantization time anyway, the teacher is a free variable, and the recovered checkpoint is the weakest available choice. Distil the 4-bit student from the original uncompressed model instead — teacher and student no longer share an architecture, which distillation never required — and the deployable artifact matches or beats its own bfloat16 source on 7 of 9 benchmarks, gaining 7.4 points on long-context reasoning and 5.6 on AIME, at roughly a quarter of the weight memory and half the teacher's parameters.

The claim to carry forward is the authors' own, which is narrower than the headline: this does not show 4-bit representations are inherently superior. It shows that given the extra distillation pass performed at quantization time, the deployable 4-bit object is at least as capable as the 16-bit one it came from. The step stopped being postprocessing to minimize and became a place to add capability.

This complicates rather than overturns the map. quantization-can-be-a-training-decision-not-a-post-hoc-one and the-four-bit-penalty-is-a-training-decision-you-can-decline already hold that training into the format beats converting into it; what is new is that the comparison object changes. Every result on this map of the form "X costs Y points against bfloat16" presumes the bfloat16 arm is the ceiling. In a multi-stage compression pipeline it is not — it is just another approximation, and possibly a worse-supervised one.

The honest weakness is severe and self-declared: the direct comparison this argument rests on was never run. No head-to-head against standard quantization-aware distillation from the recovered checkpoint at matched configuration. Every number is a single run. One model family, one format, one data mixture, and a proprietary compression operator. Read as a well-motivated recipe with one demonstration behind it.

The claim it pushes against — that a quantized model is bounded above by its full-precision source — is the reading behind quantized-bases-preserve-finetune-quality, which now carries the back-edge. The scope condition is compound compression, where the full-precision source is itself derived rather than trained.
