The levers on this map are factors of one distortion

kv-cache-management was coined on the complaint that five levers act on one budget and no source measures any two of them together. This is the first source to say what the levers are, and it does so by deriving them rather than by running the composition it was asked for.

Treat quantization error on cached keys and values as additive white noise. The expected squared error at the attention output — not in the cache, which is what every method here minimizes — separates into a key term and a value term, and each factors into three independent pieces: a per-token weight from the attention distribution, a per-channel weight from statistics the cache cannot see (the query energy q_c² for keys, the output projection's column norm for values), and the quantizer's own per-channel distortion σ_c² 2^{-2b_c}. Cross-terms vanish to first order.

Every family on this map is now readable as an assignment to those factors. Token eviction zeroes the per-token weight for the tokens it deletes. Low-rank projection zeroes the per-channel weight for the dimensions it discards — and falls out of the derivation for free, because the classical reverse water-filling solution assigns zero bits to channels below the water level. Per-channel and per-token scaling and codebooks — KIVI's per-channel keys with per-token values, KVQuant's Fisher-weighted non-uniform codebook — attack the third factor. Several of the heuristics turn out to be partial derivations of the same objective; the paper observes that the output projection enters KVQuant's Fisher sensitivity only implicitly.

That is a reframe rather than a result. The map's founding question was "given a fixed budget, what do you keep and in what form?" — a question that implicitly assumes the answers compete. If they are factors of one product, the sharper question is which factor is cheapest to spend on this model, and the composition question becomes a prediction: two levers acting on the same factor should interfere, two acting on different factors should be closer to additive. Nobody has tested it. The paper's own limitations section names interaction with token eviction as unstudied, which is the map's founding complaint restated by a source that had the theory to predict the answer.

Two honest limits before treating the decomposition as settled. The theory assumes tokens are independent, which the authors concede natural language is not, and the guarantee is first-order in the perturbation — it is an approximation to the error, not a bound on it. And the empirical support is two model families, five benchmark suites and no timing of any kind: the implementation "lacks a dedicated CUDA implementation, which would be required before the method could be used in production," so the online cost of a per-channel bit-width lookup plus a whitening projection and a reconstruction per attention call is unpriced. qevict-recoverable-quantized-kv-eviction is this map's standing evidence that exactly that class of per-step reconstruction can cost 59% of throughput under a fused kernel.
