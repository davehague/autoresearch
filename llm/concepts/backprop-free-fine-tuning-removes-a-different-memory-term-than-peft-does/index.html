A second way to not pay for training memory

Every method on peft-methods works the same way at the memory level: freeze most of the weights, train a small number of new ones, and run backpropagation through the frozen base to reach them. LoRA and its initialization family, DoRA, rank allocation schemes, quantized bases — all of them shrink the trainable parameter term and leave the backward pass intact.

Zeroth-order optimization does the opposite. It keeps every parameter trainable and removes the backward pass entirely, estimating the gradient from loss differences under random perturbations: perturb by εz, evaluate the loss, and scale z by the difference. Two forward passes replace one forward and one backward, and with a random-seed trick that regenerates perturbations instead of storing them, peak memory falls toward inference levels. The term that disappears is activations and their graph — the one activation-memory exists to describe, and the one rematerialization-trades-compute-for-the-activation-memory-nothing-else-shrinks says nothing else touches.

So the two are orthogonal and they compose; this study runs its zeroth-order method under LoRA and it works. But the memory table contains a scope condition sharper than the paper's framing, and it is the part to carry:

Under LoRA, every zeroth-order variant measures identically. MeZO, SubZero and SubZero+ report the same peak memory on all four evaluated tasks, because once the trainable set is a rank-8 adapter, the adapter dominates and the choice of estimator stops mattering. The 3.8× spread between ZO configurations — 25.89 GB to 97.24 GB on OPT-13B — exists only under full-parameter tuning.

That inverts the usual reason to reach for a technique. ZO's memory argument is strongest exactly where PEFT's is weakest, and nearly absent where PEFT already works. peft-does-not-reliably-reduce-peak-memory established the first half of that from the other direction: adapters do not shrink peak memory the way practitioners assume, because activations dominate. ZO is the tool that attacks the activation term, and it is worth reaching for when you want to move all the weights on a card that cannot hold the graph — not when a rank-8 adapter would have done.

The price is quality and it does not close. At a matched forward-pass budget the best zeroth-order result trails first-order Adam by 4.5 points on OPT-13B, 5.2 on OPT-1.3B, and 1.0 on LLaMA3.1-8B across nine SuperGLUE tasks. The gap narrows on the strongest model, which is suggestive and is one data point. There is also a cost the paper never prices — 40,000 forward passes is a lot of wall-clock, and this vault has found repeatedly that memory techniques relocate cost into time and energy rather than removing it (four-bit-fine-tuning-buys-vram-with-time-and-energy, energy-reorders-the-peft-methods-that-memory-ranks). Treat backprop-free tuning as a ceiling-raising technique, not a cheaper one.
