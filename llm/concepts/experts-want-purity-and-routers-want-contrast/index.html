A mixture of adapters has two learnable objects, and only one of them wants clean data

Two framing notes before the finding, because this vault has a specific hole this source could be mistaken for filling.

This is not LoRA on a mixture-of-experts model. mixture-of-experts has carried "MoE × LoRA" as an open cell since 2026-07-24, meaning fine-tuning a pretrained sparse model with adapters. This is a mixture of LoRA adapters over a frozen dense backbone — Llama3.2-3B and Gemma-2-2B — which shares vocabulary with sparse pretraining and not mechanism. The cell stays open.

And the federated setting is scaffolding, not subject. What makes the experiment possible is that federation forces an explicit choice about what unit of data an update is computed over. The finding transfers to any multi-adapter setup where that choice exists.

The finding: the two learnable objects want different data.

  • Experts need purity. An expert improves when its updates come from pattern-coherent batches. Mix incompatible patterns into one update and the adaptation signal loses the coherence that specialization requires — measured as off-pattern routed contamination χ_m(θ_m), which the paper's Theorem 1 makes the term controlling how far the expert's stationary point sits from its clean optimum.
  • Routers need contrast. A router learns a selection rule, which requires observing patterns side by side. Give it pattern-pure updates and its trajectory fragments into per-bucket segments that must then be averaged, incurring a consensus residual the persistent-trajectory schedule avoids (Theorem 2).

The ablation is the reason to believe it, because it holds everything else fixed and varies only the two aggregation granularities:

Both symmetric configurations lose, in opposite directions. Going finer on everything is worse than going coarse on everything. It is the mismatch between the two, not the resolution of either, that is the lever — and the routing map confirms the mechanism visually, with a distinct dominant expert per task where the closest baseline reuses one expert for two.

Why this belongs on peft-methods rather than filed as a federated-learning curiosity: the map's 2026-07-27 frame says a low-rank update is four independent choices — placement, initialization, parameterization, rank. This is a candidate fifth, orthogonal to all of them: what data unit an adapter's update is computed over. And it is the only one of the five that has no meaning for a single adapter, which is why nothing in the map's existing literature could have found it. As multi-adapter serving becomes ordinary, the question generalizes past federation — a router over task-specific adapters trained on shuffled batches is making the same mistake this ablation measures.

Discounted to suggestive, on four grounds. The effects are small relative to the reported spread — 0.0199 macro-average against per-task standard deviations of 0.005–0.02 — and the simplest baseline, one shared LoRA under sample-weighted averaging, still wins one of the four tasks outright. Every method runs at a fixed 1e-4 with no sweep, the third source in eight days to do so, four days after this map recorded that a shared default learning rate is exactly what manufactured the last round of adapter-variant wins (adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact); a rank-8-per-expert mixture and a rank-32 single adapter have no reason to share an optimum. The "patterns" are four benchmarks, so the latent structure the method discovers is close to benchmark identity, which makes 0.95 alignment purity less surprising than it reads. And 2B–3B backbones only, by the authors' own statement of their compute limit.

One detail worth keeping regardless of the headline: the whole study runs on one RTX 4090, which makes it among the few sources in this map measured on hardware its readers own.
