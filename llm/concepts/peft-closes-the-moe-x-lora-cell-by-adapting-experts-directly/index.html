This map and mixture-of-experts both held one cell open since 2026-07-21 — "train an adapter over a routed expert layer of a sparse base" — and the answer that closes it is procedural, not diagnostic

Three prior arrivals on this exact gap (MoEGen's hypernetwork-generated adapter, SALT's domain-basis-plus-residual split, VANE's reader/writer expert router) all declined it the same way: each put the mixture inside the adapter, tested on a frozen dense backbone, and stated the sparse-base case as out of scope. The gap's sharpened wording from 2026-08-05 asked for exactly this run: "train an adapter over a routed expert layer of a sparse base and report per-expert gradient coverage alongside the task score." This paper runs the first half of that — three real MoE backbones, adapter placed on the base model's own routed experts — across a systematic sweep of placement (attention/experts/router), granularity, PEFT method, and the load-balancing coefficient.

The headline is a trade, not a dominant strategy. Adapting only the native experts wins on raw downstream accuracy (68.6% vs. 61.7% for attention-only, vs. 67.0%/68.1% for the mixed placements) but pays for it in general-capability retention (MMLU-Pro −3.3, IFEval −3.6) relative to attention+experts (−1.8, −1.0). A practitioner choosing "adapt the experts" is buying downstream performance with retained capability, exactly the kind of trade this map's own strand 1 (the contest — rank/init/placement/subspace) has characterized for dense backbones; this is the first time that trade is measured with the base model's own routing in the loop rather than a frozen dense substrate.

LoRA and DoRA are statistically tied (68.5% each) at this scale — a result that sits inside this map's standing finding that most LoRA variants cluster within a point or two of each other once the learning-rate confound is controlled, now replicated on a sparse substrate.

The load-balancing-loss null is the cleanest finding. No nonzero coefficient beats zero on the Pareto front, on any backbone tested — the loss term MoE pretraining leans on to keep expert usage even does not transfer as a useful lever once you are fine-tuning with PEFT rather than training from scratch. That is new information for both maps: neither held a prior belief about whether load-balancing even applies during adaptation.

What stays open. The paper does not report per-expert gradient coverage — the second half of the gap's 2026-08-05 wording — so mixture-of-experts's uneven-visitation concern (an adapter's effective per-expert training-set size differing by whatever the frozen router's routing distribution happens to be) is answered procedurally here (adapt the native experts and accept the retention cost) rather than diagnostically (show which experts actually got trained how much). No code or model release is stated. All three backbones are 20B-30B total parameters — nothing here runs on one consumer card un-offloaded, and the MoE²-LoRA headline numbers are reported on a single backbone (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base), not replicated across the other two.
