A reused rollout makes the student half of a dense reward stale

This one is not about multiple teachers, and that is why it is filed here rather than on the distillation map.

Any policy-gradient loop that generates a batch of rollouts and then takes K inner updates on it — the standard PPO throughput arrangement — computes its rewards once, at rollout time. When the reward is dense and token-level, it has two terms: a teacher term, which depends only on the teacher and the sampled tokens, and a student term, which depends on the current student's log-probabilities. The teacher term is genuinely constant across the K updates. The student term is not: after the first inner update the student has moved, and the reward being optimised was computed against a policy that no longer exists. By the K-th update it is stale by K−1 steps.

The fix is smaller than the bug. PPO's actor forward already computes current-student log-probabilities on every inner update, because the ratio needs them. Reading the dense reward's student term from that forward instead of from the saved rollout-time values requires no additional forward pass of any kind — not of the student, and certainly not of the teacher, whose log-probs stay prefilled once per rollout batch.

The measured cost is consistent with that account rather than merely asserted. Dense-reward computation is 2.2% of one outer step; at K = 4 it takes 27.3 s with refresh and 27.8 s without, and the full outer step 1298 s against 1313 s — inside step-to-step variation, and nominally faster with the fix, which is the signature of noise rather than a saving. The gain is +0.81 total points on the paper's benchmark, on top of two other mechanisms.

The reason to record this on post-training-rl rather than leave it inside a multi-teacher distillation paper: the argument makes no reference to teachers, domains or routing. Any on-policy loop with K > 1 and a student-dependent dense reward has this staleness, including single-teacher on-policy distillation, and including reward-model setups where the reward depends on the policy's own probabilities. This vault has read several such recipes and none of them mention it.

Graded suggestive: the effect is isolated in one cumulative ablation on one 3B student at one value of K, and +0.81 points is not separable from the other two mechanisms it stacks on in any reported single-mechanism arm at K = 4. The claim that it is free is well-supported; the claim about its size is one measurement. What makes it worth carrying anyway is the asymmetry — a fix costing nothing needs much less evidence to justify adopting than one costing a training run.
