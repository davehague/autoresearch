A third independent route to "the early rollout prefix is where OPD's teacher-student mismatch concentrates" -- this one from token-level supervision-reliability measurement and divergence construction, not from teacher retraining or rollout coupling

TL;DR: O²PD names two failure modes in standard top-k on-policy distillation -- Teacher-support Uncovering (the teacher's top-k support excludes mass the off-policy student rollout needs) and Dominant-Mode Mismatch (top-k restriction forces single-mode collapse) -- and finds, by directly measuring supervision reliability across rollout position, that reliability is highest on the first 20% of tokens and decays afterward. Training only on that prefix, with an overlap-gated α-divergence that pools excluded mass when teacher/student support diverges (Reset Support) and narrows to the agreed intersection when it converges (Support Convergence), beats standard top-k OPD by up to 5.29 points average across seven benchmarks.

What it says. This is the map's third independent arrival at "the early rollout prefix is the load-bearing region for OPD's teacher-student mismatch," each from a structurally different mechanism: prefix-adapted-teacher-beats-task-adapted-teacher-opd (SPTA) showed the teacher's own accuracy collapses when forced to continue a long, student-supplied off-distribution prefix, and fixed it by RLVR-adapting the teacher to continue from exactly those prefixes; cord-couples-rollouts-to-close-opd-blind-spot (CoRD) showed the student's rollout sampler rarely visits teacher-preferred states and fixed it with maximal-coupling sampling, leaving the teacher untouched; O²PD now shows that measured supervision reliability itself, token-by-token, peaks early and decays — and fixes it purely by restricting training scope (first 20% of tokens) plus a divergence-construction change (overlap-gated α between Reset Support and Support Convergence), touching neither the teacher's weights nor the student's sampler. Three papers, three objects (teacher accuracy, rollout sampling, divergence construction), one converging diagnosis. None of the three tests against either of the other two, so the map's standing composition question now carries a sixth untested lever, but this one is the first to measure where in the rollout the mismatch concentrates directly rather than inferring it from an intervention's effect.

Caveats. No code or checkpoint release stated — a method built from two new mechanisms (position-based training cutoff, overlap-gated divergence) is unusually hard to verify from prose alone without one. The 20% prefix cutoff is reported as the authors' measured reliability curve for their specific model/benchmark setup, not derived from a theoretical account of why reliability decays where it does, so whether 20% transfers to other student/teacher pairings or task types (math vs. code vs. general reasoning) is untested. No head-to-head against SPTA or CoRD exists, despite all three converging on the same structural diagnosis — which lever (teacher adaptation, sampler coupling, or scope-plus-divergence restriction) buys the most for the least overhead, or whether they compose, remains the map's open question.
