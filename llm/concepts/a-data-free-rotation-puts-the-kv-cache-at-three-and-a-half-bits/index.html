Destroy the structure, then quantize each coordinate on its own

kv-cache-error-is-directionally-structured is this vault's incumbent KV-cache finding, from KIVI: key-cache error concentrates per channel and value-cache error per token, so an asymmetric scheme that quantizes keys along one axis and values along the other wins. That is a method built by modelling the structure.

This is the opposite move and it lands better. Apply a random rotation first. In high dimension that induces a concentrated Beta distribution on the coordinates and makes distinct coordinates near-independent — at which point the optimal vector quantizer collapses into applying the optimal scalar quantizer per coordinate, which is cheap, vectorizable and needs no data. The structure KIVI had to model is simply gone, spread uniformly by the rotation. The result is provably near-optimal: within a constant ≈2.7× of the information-theoretic distortion floor at every bit-width and dimension, against proved lower bounds the paper also supplies.

The second stage matters for a reason specific to attention. An MSE-optimal quantizer is biased for inner-product estimation, and the bias grows with the true inner product — which is exactly the wrong failure mode when attention is an inner product, because it means the error is systematic and query-dependent rather than noise. The fix is to run the MSE quantizer at b−1 bits and spend the last bit on a Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss sketch of the residual, yielding an unbiased inner-product estimator.

Three things this changes for the vault.

The number. 3.5 bits per channel with a LongBench-E average identical to the full 16-bit cache; 2.5 bits at −0.62. The non-integer widths come from splitting channels into outliers and non-outliers and running two instances — in the 2.5-bit setup, 32 channels at 3 bits and 96 at 2. And unlike KIVI and PolarQuant, which leave generated tokens unquantized, this quantizes during streaming generation too, so it wins from a harder starting position.

Data-free is the property that matters here, and it is the second source in five days to have it. quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model established that the calibration corpus silently determines the score — a 2.1× HumanEval swing from calibration choice alone — and AlphaQ's answer was a data-free allocation signal. This is a data-free quantizer. Neither resolves the calibration problem; both step around it, and for a practitioner who cannot audit what corpus a downloaded quant was tuned on, stepping around it is the more useful outcome. It also shows in the cost: five orders of magnitude faster than Product Quantization, because there is no codebook to fit.

The damage is task-shaped, and the shape is the one the vault keeps seeing. At 2.5 bits the loss is concentrated in summarization (−1.75) while QA, few-shot and code stay flat; on Ministral, single-document QA actually rises. A single average would have hidden that, exactly as perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats warns.

Bounded hard: footprint and quality only — the paper reports no latency, throughput or kernel timing anywhere, so this closes the KV-precision cell for memory and leaves it open for speed. All experiments are a single A100; nothing on consumer silicon, and llama.cpp's K-quant menu has no way to express a 2.5-bit outlier split. Two ~7–8B instruct models, full-precision weights underneath, so the compounding question — grouped-query attention × weight precision × KV precision — is untouched. And the exact tie at 3.5 bits is one decimal on one average: KIVI at 5 bits also exceeds the full cache (50.16 vs 50.06), which is the clearest available evidence that ±0.2 here is noise.
