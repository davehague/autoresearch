One byte layout from the disk to the kernel

moe-inference-cost-tracks-total-not-active-params-on-edge closed the MoE-times-consumer-hardware cell unfavourably: on bandwidth-bound machines the bill tracks total parameters, and sparsity buys FLOPs that a small device is not short of. This is the first source on mixture-of-experts that takes that verdict as its design premise rather than as a disappointment.

If bytes-moved-per-token is the governing quantity, the format's job is two things: minimize bytes, and make sure the bytes never need touching between tiers. Tying PTQTP's two free per-group scales at a ratio of three collapses the two ternary planes into a uniform nine-level quantizer, nine levels fold into one 4-bit code plane, and that plane becomes the persistent representation — tq2_0_fx4, a 520-byte block over 4 columns × 256 elements at 4.0625 bits/weight, identical on disk, in the expert-cache slab, and at the integer kernel's input. No transcode at any tier. A per-projection miss is therefore one contiguous read by construction, and a block-granular NVMe tier can stripe hot expert prefixes over the primary file.

The measured returns are almost exactly proportional to the bit ratio, which is the sign that the mechanism is the one claimed: −10.6% bytes read, −9% file size, +10.8% experts pinned in the same RAM, 6.7% faster decode. The byte and pin figures are identical in every round of every session across two machines and two instruction sets — the mechanical advantage is substrate-invariant even where the absolute rates are not.

This confirms a-packed-expert-pool-turns-a-cache-miss-into-a-copy from an independent direction and extends it by one tier. That result packed experts once, offline, into the kernel's native format in pinned host memory on a GPU, so a miss became a memcpy. Here the same principle reaches disk, on a CPU, with the SSD as the backing store — which is the tier that matters when the model is 284B and the machine has 64 GB. Two groups, two substrates, one finding: pay the packing once and never pay it again, and make "once" include the file on disk.

The speed comparison does not causally separate format, kernel, cache and workload — the arms generate different continuations, so their routed-expert workloads differ — and the author says so. The byte-and-pin decomposition is the part that is clean.
