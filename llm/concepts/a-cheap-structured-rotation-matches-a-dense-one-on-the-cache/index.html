A cheap structured rotation matches a dense one on the cache

Rotation is the recurring mechanism in this vault's quantization literature. rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free establishes why — a rotation redistributes outlier energy across coordinates so a uniform grid stops wasting range on a few channels — and a-data-free-rotation-puts-the-kv-cache-at-three-and-a-half-bits applies it to the cache with no calibration data, reaching parity with a 16-bit cache at 3.5 bits per channel.

What this adds is that the rotation can be almost free and still work better. Instead of a dense d×d transform, compose the normalized Walsh–Hadamard matrix with block-diagonal SO(4) rotations on 4-dimensional blocks. At d = 128 that is 896 fused multiply-adds for the Hadamard butterfly plus 512 for the blocks — 1,408 against 16,384, and 256 stored parameters per head against 16,384. The quality goes the other way from what a 64× parameter reduction would predict: at 3 bits the structured rotation costs +0.0009 perplexity on Qwen3-30B-A3B where the dense data-free method costs +0.0405.

The reading that makes sense of both halves is that isotropization is a coarse requirement. What the quantizer needs is that no coordinate carries disproportionate energy; it does not need the specific basis that minimizes some reconstruction objective. A Hadamard transform spreads energy maximally by construction, and the SO(4) blocks supply just enough learned freedom to correct what the fixed transform leaves behind. That is the same lesson an-int4-proxy-ranks-channels-it-cannot-compute teaches from the other direction — a deliberately coarse signal can be sufficient for a decision that looks like it needs precision.

The rotation is not decorative and the paper proves it with the cleanest ablation available: skip the inverse rotation on the aggregated attention output and perplexity goes from 7.05 to 15,369. The compressed space is a real basis change, not a storage trick.

Two bounds. The main fidelity table is at 2,048 tokens, with only one model checked at 32K — where it does hold at 0.024% divergence with 100% needle retrieval, but a needle test is the instrument context-extension already discounts. And the method cannot be applied to multi-head latent attention at all: DeepSeek-V2-style models split KV into content and RoPE sub-spaces and the implementation does not perform the split, which excludes an architecture family that is growing rather than shrinking.

Rebutted by: a-rotations-value-is-its-mixing-support-not-its-structure — read as "small blocks are enough," this note is false: on a controlled block-size interpolation under dynamic W4A4KV4, b = 4 still costs +0.226 PPL and the paired intervals do not reach the full-head baseline until b = 64 out of 128, so the composition with a full-width Hadamard is load-bearing rather than incidental, and the claim holds for Hadamard-plus-SO(4) but not for structured rotations generally. Also an-exactly-optimal-rotation-can-be-worse-than-no-rotation — a structured rotation derived in closed form and verified to attain its own optimum lands below the identity transform, so "cheap and structured" is not by itself evidence that a rotation is doing useful work.
