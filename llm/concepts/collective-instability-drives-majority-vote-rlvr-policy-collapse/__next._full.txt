13:T92e, Each training problem, in isolation, has a stable individual drift under reward-for-majority-agreement: F(z) = p(1-p)(2p-1), sharpening toward whichever answer the model currently favors and freezing there — this alone predicts no collapse. The collapse appears only once problems are coupled through a shared parameter direction (e.g. a shared output bias), and only for the subset of problems still undecided (logit magnitude below z* ≈ 1.32): there, the population-averaged slope of individual drift is positive, so the shared direction's own small perturbation is amplified rather than damped — a paramagnetic-type collective instability, mathematically distinct from any individual problem's dynamics. Because the collective mode's growth rate scales as λ² independent of how many problems there are, while each problem's own stabilizing drift is diluted by a factor of 1/N across N parameter-sharing problems, there is a derived critical training-set size N_c ≃ ln(1/δ)/(λ²·ln[F*/F(a)]) past which the collective mode wins the race and the whole population collapses to a single shared answer — fit on the toy model to N_c ∝ λ^(-2.2) with one calibrated constant (Λc≈2.6). Lower initial model confidence (higher entropy) widens the undecided window and lowers N_c, so less-confident models collapse at smaller training-set sizes; confident models (above the undecided threshold) are predicted to never collapse in the homogeneous case.
