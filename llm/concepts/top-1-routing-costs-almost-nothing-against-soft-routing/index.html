Train dense over the experts, serve sparse, and the bill is a rounding error

Training a mixture of adapters wants soft routing: every expert receives a weighted share of every example, which keeps the router differentiable and — the point this source's whole design turns on — supplies comparative supervision, so a non-anchor expert still receives signal while early assignments are uncertain (experts-want-purity-and-routers-want-contrast). Serving wants the opposite: activate one adapter, skip the rest.

Measured on the same trained checkpoints over three seeds, the gap between the two is negligible:

Inference modeMacro avgMean latency
Soft (all experts)0.58833,177 ms
Top-20.58902,587 ms
Top-10.58722,147 ms

32.4% of the latency for 0.0011 of the score — and top-2 is nominally the best of the three, which is well inside seed noise and is best read as "the three are indistinguishable."

Two reasons to keep this rather than treat it as an implementation note.

It confirms a claim this vault has held only for sparse pretraining, in a setting with a different mechanism. mixture-of-experts has carried since Switch Transformers that top-1 routing suffices, established for models pretrained with sparse routing where the experts were shaped by the routing from step one. Here the experts are LoRA adapters over a frozen dense backbone, trained under a full soft mixture, and top-1 still holds. Different substrate, different training regime, same conclusion — which is worth a little more than another instance of the original claim.

And the paper supplies a condition rather than just a number. The top-1 risk bound is gap-calibrated: excess risk over the oracle expert scales with 1/c_gap, the calibration constant relating router-probability margins to expert-loss gaps. Read as an engineering rule, top-1 is safe exactly when the router is confident and the experts are genuinely different, and degrades when experts overlap — which the routing map here supports, since this method assigns a distinct dominant expert per task while its closest baseline reuses one expert across two.

Bounded: 2B–3B backbones, four tasks, small absolute effects, and latency measured without an end-to-end wall-clock or peak-memory profile (the authors say the parameter counts they do report "do not replace end-to-end wall-clock and peak-memory profiling"). The 32.4% latency figure is also a soft-versus-sparse comparison within one implementation, not a comparison against a single merged adapter — which is the baseline a practitioner deciding whether to run a mixture at all would want, and which nobody reports.
