Preference alignment collapses into a single stage

The trajectory of this thread is one of successive deletions. RLHF needed a reward model, a reference model, and a sampling loop. DPO deleted the reward model and the sampling loop (preference-tuning-reduces-to-classification). ORPO deletes the reference model and the stage boundary: L_ORPO = L_SFT + λ·L_OR, where L_OR = −log σ(log[odds(y_w|x)/odds(y_l|x)]) and λ is small (0.1–0.25). One model, one loss, one run.

The design argument is why odds ratio rather than probability ratio. A probability ratio is too sharp — it produces extreme discrimination that fights the SFT term and destabilizes a shared objective. The odds ratio has a wider dynamic range and applies what the paper calls a "minor penalty": enough downward pressure to suppress the disfavored style (cross-entropy-sft-raises-the-odds-of-rejected-text), not enough to take over the loss. The resource consequence is concrete and is the real appeal on one card — no frozen reference copy resident in memory, half the forward passes per batch, and no second training run.

Grade this below DPO, and the reason is in the paper's own tables. Where the comparison is genuinely apples-to-apples — same bases, same data, small scale — ORPO's win rate against DPO is 41.7% at OPT-125M, 49.4% at 350M, and only reaches 70.9% at 1.3B: a loss, a tie, then a win. The headline 7B numbers are stronger but weaker evidence, resting on a single dataset for a single epoch, with nothing tested above 7B (explicitly deferred), only DPO and PPO as baselines, and comparison figures for Zephyr-β and Llama-2-Chat lifted from leaderboards rather than re-run alongside. And three of the four headline benchmarks are GPT-4-family judged — AlpacaEval 1.0, AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench — leaving IFEval as the only judge-free number, which is exactly the discount evaluation-contamination demands. The direction is credible and consistent with the thread; the margin is not established.
