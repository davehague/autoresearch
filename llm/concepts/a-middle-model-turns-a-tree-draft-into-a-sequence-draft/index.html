Push the tree down to the model that can afford it

Once speculation-and-quantization-compete-for-the-same-headroom establishes that the binding cost on a 4-bit target is tree verification, the fix follows from the diagnosis rather than from tuning. Tree verification is compute-heavy and the small model has compute to spare; sequence verification is memory-light and the big 4-bit model is exactly what wants that. So put a mid-sized model between them and let each stage do the thing it is cheap at.

Concretely: a W4A16 Llama-3-8B sits between EAGLE-2's single-layer draft head and a W4A16 Llama-3-70B target. EAGLE-2 tree-drafts at depth 3 or 4 against the 8B, repeating until the 8B has emitted a flat draft of length 6 or 7; the 70B verifies that sequence in one pass. The tree never touches the model where a tree is unaffordable. Result: 98.19 tok/s, 2.78× over autoregressive decoding, 1.31× over the best EAGLE-2 configuration — and, notably, better than either component alone, where EAGLE-2 tops out at 2.12× and plain sequential speculation at 2.31×.

Two things generalize past this one framework.

Speculative decoding is a hierarchy, not a pair. The field's framing is draft model + target model, with the design question being how small the draft can be. This says the interesting variable is how many levels and what each level's arithmetic-intensity profile is. Every level converts its predecessor's output into a shape the next one can verify cheaply, and the shape — tree versus sequence — is as important as the model size. That reframes a lever this vault had filed as a fixed technique.

A 4-bit draft agrees with a 4-bit target better than the vault expected, and this partly answers a question local-inference has named as its sharpest missing datum. The paper cites prior work finding that GPTQ-quantizing a draft wrecks the acceptance rate, and keeps its EAGLE-2 head at FP16 for that reason. But its own intermediate model is a W4A16 8B drafting for a W4A16 70B, and the resulting average accepted length is 5.28 — above every FP16-headed EAGLE-2 configuration it replaces (3.25–3.81) and above vanilla sequential speculation with the same 4-bit 8B draft (4.72). So the fragility reported elsewhere belongs to quantizing a single-transformer-layer draft head, which has no redundancy to give up, not to quantizing a real model. The vault's open question should be rewritten at that grain rather than closed.

The cost is honest and unpriced: HierSpec keeps an 8B, a 70B and a draft head resident, so it buys latency with memory. On a card where the target barely fits, that trade may not be available at all — and the paper never measures it.
