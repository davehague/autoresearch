Move the indices, not the frequencies

Every context-extension source this map held belonged to one family: change the frequencies of the rotary position encoding so old angles cover new distances. YaRN rescales them non-uniformly (context-length-is-a-cheap-post-hoc-edit-not-a-pretraining-property), NTK-style methods scale the base. zero-shot-yarn-helps-only-at-8b-and-hurts-at-1-7b is what that family does on a small model applied zero-shot: worse than nothing.

The other road leaves the frequencies untouched and remaps the position indices. Two windows over the same sequence: a local, RoPE-faithful window where positions within w₀ behave exactly as the base model expects, and a remote window where positions are compressed by f(x) = ⌊x/G⌋ with

G = max(1, ⌈L / w_pretrained⌉)

The load-bearing detail is that G is recomputed from the current sequence length L. Remote rotation angles therefore always land inside the distribution the model was trained on, at the finest positional resolution available for that length — and there is no per-model constant to fit and no target length to choose, which is exactly what YaRN's fixed rescaling factor requires. Nothing is trained.

On RULER it wins at every size — 73.93 / 83.02 / 86.74 at 1.7B / 4B / 8B, beating the best baseline by +4.79 / +2.18 / +2.03 — and on PG-19 it holds 11.41 / 9.62 / 8.51 at 128K where the unextended bases collapse to 71.00 / 104.66 / 79.37.

Two readings the map should carry beyond the leaderboard.

The family distinction is the durable content. Three of four methods beat the base model at 1.7B and only YaRN fails, so the map's problem was never "extension needs scale" — it was that the one family it had sourced is the fragile one. That is a distinction the map could not previously draw.

And this is the vault's perplexity rule demonstrated in a fourth literature. On PG-19 the methods are nearly identical — Jet-Long 11.71 against DCA 11.77 at 1.7B, a 0.5% difference — which RULER renders as 73.93 vs 67.80, six points. Same ordering, wildly different apparent magnitude, exactly what validation-perplexity-does-not-track-generation-quality predicts. Worth noting for evaluation-contamination that the ordering survived here even though the magnitude did not, which is a slightly kinder result for perplexity than the quantization-format case (perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats) where the ordering itself was wrong.

The throughput numbers make a familiar point. The method as written is a 7–11% prefill slowdown against FlashAttention-2; fusing three FlashAttention calls plus the LogSumExp merge into a single CuTe kernel turns that into 1.28×–1.39×. The mathematics did not change between those two measurements — another instance of attention-is-memory-bound-not-compute-bound and of the vault's recurring lesson that the win lives in the kernel. Single-batch decode overhead is ≤4%, which is the number a local user would care about; it is measured on an H100, and neither the remapping nor the fused kernel exists in llama.cpp.

Bounds: RULER is synthetic, and the one natural long-context benchmark reported (HELMET-RAG) is a wash — Jet-Long loses by 0.73 at 1.7B — so the six-point synthetic gap does not reproduce on retrieval work. One model family, so RoPE base frequency is held at whatever Qwen chose. A 4× extension (32K → 128K), far short of YaRN's headline ratio. No fine-tuned baseline of any kind. And the authors note the position-aliasing advantage diminishes at 128K, meaning the method's edge is weakest at the longest length it advertises.

Rebutted by: a-recurrent-state-removes-the-position-encoding-problem — not on the finding, which stands, but on its scope: a production 1M-token window was reached with no position encoding at all, so rescale-versus-remap is a contest inside one architectural family rather than a fact about long context. Holds wherever a rotary encoding exists to manipulate; there is a frontier model where none does.
