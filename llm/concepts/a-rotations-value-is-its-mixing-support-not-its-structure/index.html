A rotation's value is its mixing support, not its structure

The useful half of a negative result. Having established that a RoPE-respecting per-pair rotation loses to a full-head Hadamard, this source asks why along the one axis that separates them, and the answer is arithmetic rather than empirical.

A norm-preserving orthogonal map that distributes one peak with equal magnitude across b channels leaves a residual peak of at least |x|/√b. That is the whole mechanism. A pairwise rotation has b = 2; a full-head Hadamard at dₕ = 128 has b = 128; the bounds differ by roughly 8×, and no choice of angle inside the two-channel subspace can close that. The per-pair family was doomed before any experiment was run, and the same one-line check screens every future structured-transform proposal: read off its mixing support first.

The interpolation prices it. Sweeping block size through a pair-interleaved block-Hadamard family, mean K range falls 14.82 → 9.64, relative K quantisation error falls 0.02306 → 0.00954, and the perplexity gap falls +0.4158 → +0.0039 — all three monotone in b. The paired intervals do not cross zero until b = 64, and at b = 128 the quantisation error matches full-head to the reported precision while mean K range differs by 0.0011. Three different quantities, one ordering, one driver.

This puts a scope condition on a-cheap-structured-rotation-matches-a-dense-one-on-the-cache, which is the vault's closest claim and reads more permissively than the evidence supports. That note (RotaryQuant) established that a Walsh–Hadamard composed with block-diagonal SO(4) rotations beats a dense data-free KV quantizer at 1,408 fused multiply-adds against 16,384. Nothing here contradicts it — but the composition is load-bearing in a way the phrasing hides. The full-width Hadamard supplies the mixing; the SO(4) blocks supply refinement on top of it. Read as "small blocks are enough," the claim is false in this setting by a wide margin: b = 4 alone still costs +0.226 PPL. The two results are compatible and the distinction decides whether a practitioner can drop the wide transform, so it is recorded on both notes.

It also reframes what rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free actually established. That result is usually read as "the outlier basis is arbitrary, so change it." The sharper reading this supports is "an outlier is a peak concentrated in few coordinates, and a rotation helps exactly in proportion to how many coordinates it can spread that peak over" — which is why Hadamard, the maximally-mixing cheap transform, keeps winning, and why every attempt to make the transform smarter or more structured has been paying for meaning the quantiser cannot see.

Bounds, stated by the paper and worth keeping. The interpolation covers one checkpoint, one corpus, one context length and one transformation path, and it leaves the RoPE-commuting family for b > 2 — so support size and block structure move together and the evidence supports association rather than isolated causal attribution. The |x|/√b inequality is a bound on an outlier-range proxy and does not bound perplexity; it predicts the ordering here, it does not guarantee one elsewhere. And this is measured under a dynamic tokenwise-group quantiser; under static per-channel scaling the paper's own single-seed control moves the ordering, which is unresolved.
