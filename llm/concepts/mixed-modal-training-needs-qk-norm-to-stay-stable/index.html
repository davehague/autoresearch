Mixed-modal training needs QK-norm to stay stable

Chameleon's real cost is not architecture, it's stability. Feeding one transformer two token distributions with very different entropy and activation-norm statistics — image codebook tokens and text tokens — makes attention logits blow up from the higher-norm modality, and a stock LLaMA block diverges past ~7B. The fixes are all normalization: QK-normalization (layernorm on the query and key projections, capping attention-logit growth), reordered layernorm in the block, and dropout. Without them the 34B run does not stay stable.

This is the same genre of result as Megatron's finding that post-LN BERT degrades at scale and moving normalization onto the identity path fixes it (layernorm-placement-gates-large-model-stability). Two independent regimes — pure scale (Megatron, 2019) and modality mixing (Chameleon, 2024) — reach the same conclusion: the block's normalization details are what gate whether a large model trains at all, and each new stressor tends to demand a new normalization tweak rather than a new architecture. For vision-language-models this is the fine print under early fusion (early-fusion-tokenizes-images-into-the-text-vocabulary): the reason the field mostly stayed with encoder+projection is not that early fusion doesn't work, but that it is harder to train, and this paper is where that cost gets named.

Suggestive rather than strong: the paper reports the stabilizers as necessary but the excerpt gives no isolating ablation table, and it is a single (large, careful) training report. It connects the VLM map to transformer-architecture's normalization thread and to distributed-training's "training at scale is mostly stability engineering" lesson.
