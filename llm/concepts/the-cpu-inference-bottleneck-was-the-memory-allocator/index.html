A 2.3× win that has nothing to do with the model

This vault's local-inference evidence is a stack of comparisons: llama.cpp against vLLM, MLX against llama.cpp, a K-quant ladder against itself, an MoE against a dense model at matched active parameters. Every one of them is a measurement of a runtime as much as of a method, and this source is a warning about how far down that goes.

Arm's year of vLLM CPU work names four optimizations. Three are the expected kind — an OpenMP runtime built against Large System Extensions atomics (9% throughput, 15% lower time-per-output-token), oneDNN BF16 weight prepacking into a blocked layout at warmup (16% throughput, 60% lower TPOT at low concurrency), and custom paged-attention GEMM kernels using BFMMLA plus a vectorized polynomial approximation for the softmax exponential (up to 4× on the kernel, 12% end to end). The fourth is replacing glibc's malloc with mimalloc as PyTorch's default allocator on Arm, worth 2.3× offline throughput and about 7× at low concurrency — so far outside the others' range that the post leaves it out of its own charts.

Two things follow.

Comparative local-inference numbers have a confound nobody reports. This vault holds the-same-four-bit-weights-run-faster-on-mlx-than-on-llama-cpp and a llama.cpp K-quant sweep, neither of which states its allocator, its OpenMP build or its threading configuration. On a CPU backend those are not second-order details; here one of them was worth more than every kernel change combined. The rule to carry is the same shape as perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats and the query-agnostic construction rule on kv-cache-management — a runtime comparison absent its runtime configuration is uninformative — and it now has a number attached.

And the pattern of where the wins land corroborates the decode-side accounting. The allocator is worth 7× at low concurrency against 2.3× offline; weight prepacking is worth 60% TPOT at low concurrency; the post says W4A8's advantage is most pronounced in "low-concurrency, memory-bound scenarios." Single-stream decode is the regime where there is no other work to hide latency behind, so anything that reduces memory-system pressure pays disproportionately — the same conclusion four-bit-weights-put-llms-on-device rests on, reached through host-side allocation behaviour rather than VRAM bandwidth.

Graded T2, suggestive: this is the vLLM project's official blog authored by the silicon vendor, with named instruction sets, named libraries and per-optimization deltas, but no released harness, no quality evaluation of any kind, and a single primary benchmark model. The hardware is Neoverse V2 server cores, not a laptop or a phone, so the kernel work plausibly travels down-market on shared ISA features while the core-count-dependent scheduling results plausibly do not.
