TokenGate decides what enters the KV cache before the cache exists, rather than evicting from one already built

kv-cache-management's strand 1 ("given a fixed budget, what do you keep and in what form") has mostly answered that question post hoc — eviction, quantization, anchor-plus-residual, depth partitioning all operate on a cache that already exists. AbridgeKV (2026-10-18) was this strand's first trained, non-retrofittable mechanism, re-encoding a finished generation block into learned pause tokens. TokenGate is the complementary case: a zero-initialized MLP scores every prunable token at a chosen layer from its post-attention residual; only the top-K survive into upper layers, so the pruned tokens' KV entries are never computed in the first place, not computed-then-evicted. The gate itself is MoE-routing-inspired (sparsity over tokens rather than over parameters) and trains through ordinary next-token cross-entropy — the top-K selection is non-differentiable, but a softmax-over-kept-tokens attention-reweighting term carries gradient back to the scorer, and zero-initialization means training starts exactly equivalent to standard top-K attention. Beats every tested training-free pruning method, the same qualitative result AbridgeKV delivered against training-free eviction.

What stays open. TokenGate and AbridgeKV act at different points on the generation timeline (before prefill vs. after a block finishes) and plausibly compose, but neither paper tests the other, and TokenGate has no head-to-head against this map's eviction or quantization baselines on a shared benchmark. Per-task exact accuracy figures live only in plots in what was read, not the paper's text.
