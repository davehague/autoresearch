More supervision per response can hurt on-policy distillation, and the fix is to downweight tokens where teacher and student already silently agree there is nothing there

This map's existing transfer-mechanism sub-thread asks what actually moves from teacher to student and under what conditions — on-policy-ness, distributional novelty, pass@K collapse. DIAL-OPD adds a different axis inside the same sub-thread: given an on-policy rollout, which tokens in it should carry the distillation signal. The motivating result is counterintuitive on its face — randomly keeping only 40-90% of tokens consistently beats training on all of them (0.6B student, 4B teacher: 80% retention reaches 38.4% on MATH-500 vs 30.2% for full-token training) — and the obvious fix (keep the tokens with the biggest teacher/student disagreement) makes things worse at aggressive retention, because the biggest-looking reward magnitudes are dominated by "low-low" tokens where both models assign near-zero probability to everything, which is numerically large disagreement carrying no real signal.

The proposed score, s_beta = L(p,q)^beta * |reward| (L = logarithmic mean of student and teacher probability), has a proven fixed point: Proposition 2 shows the score diverges for beta<1 on one-sided-low tokens, is the unique finite-nonzero case at beta=1, and vanishes for beta>1 — exactly matching the empirical peak in a beta sweep (0.6B student: 35.2 at beta=0 -> 44.8 at beta=1.2, roughly flat near beta=1; 1.7B student peaks at 61.8 exactly at beta=1). This is one of the cleaner theory-matches-measurement results this map has seen for a selection heuristic.

Cross-link to training-time-quantization: this paper and opd-teaches-skills-not-knowledge (ingested the same week) both land on this map's on-policy-distillation thread but address orthogonal axes — KL direction (which divergence to train against) versus token selection (which tokens in a rollout to train against at all). online-distillation-on-student-rollouts-recovers-ultra-low-bit-qat-modestly, the training-time-quantization strand's on-policy QAT recovery result, uses reverse-KL recovery and explicitly flags that whether its W2A16 weakness is a quantization ceiling or a reverse-KL limitation is unanswered from either paper alone — DIAL-OPD's token-selection axis is a third, untested lever on the same recovery stage.

Caveats carried from the source. The random-retention anomaly that motivates the whole method is reported by the authors to vanish when student and teacher are matched in size — the effect is tied to a capacity gap, not universal. Tested only on mathematical reasoning (7 math benchmarks); no factual-QA or open-ended generation test, which is exactly where this map's KL-direction finding shows on-policy distillation behaves very differently by task. Excessive beta (tested to 2) degrades performance as theory predicts, so the method has a real tuning dimension. Code released.
