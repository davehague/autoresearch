A model suite can be one nested model trained once

Every open-weights family a reader downloads — 0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B — is normally several training runs and several artifacts. Nesting makes it one: the 500M model is a subset of the 1.5B, which is a subset of the 3B, trained end-to-end together. The suite's total parameter count falls because the small models are not stored separately, and the largest sub-model "distill[s] to all smaller sub-models at every training step" instead of in a separate post-hoc stage. Result: parity with independently trained baselines at 36% less training compute.

For distillation-small-models this changes the play's cost structure without touching its logic. The map's fixed costs have been the teacher's residency and the loss tensor (caching-the-teachers-top-k-logits-removes-the-teacher-from-memory, the-kl-loss-not-the-model-caps-distillation-context-length). Nesting deletes the first outright — you cannot pay to hold the teacher in memory separately when the teacher is a superset of the student in the same forward pass — and it deletes trace generation too, since there are no traces.

But note where it lands relative to this vault's premise, because the direction is unusual. Every other result on this map takes an existing strong checkpoint and transfers from it, which is a move a reader with their own compute can make. This one requires training the suite from scratch. It is a finding about how labs should build the small models we download, not about what we can do to them after downloading — the first entry on this map that is advice for the producer rather than the consumer. What it changes for a reader is what to expect on the shelf: if nesting is adopted, the 1B and the 8B in a family stop being independent artifacts, and a claim measured on one carries over to the other in a way it previously did not.

It is also a real test of distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank, and the outcome is genuinely ambiguous. That claim says transfer follows distributional novelty rather than teacher quality — too-distant teachers transfer nothing, and teachers indistinguishable from the student transfer nothing. A nested teacher is the low-novelty extreme: it shares parameters with the student. The suite still reaches parity at 36% less compute. Two readings the paper does not separate: either the novelty account fails at this end of the range, or the nested teacher contributes no transfer at all and the entire win is the shared training compute — which parity is fully consistent with, since parity is precisely what a zero-transfer control would predict. The architectural ablations are where this would be settled and a no-distillation nested arm is not reported in the abstract.

Graded single-study deliberately. One suite, one size ladder (500M/1.5B/3B), nothing above 3B, two authors, no independent replication, and no stated code or weight release. "On par" is a parity claim: the compute saving is the finding, and a compute saving reported by the group that designed the training framework is the kind of number this vault waits for a second lab on.

Open and consequential for a local reader: nobody has quantized a nested suite. The sub-models share weights, so a per-tensor scale fitted for the largest is imposed on the smallest whether it suits it or not — and small-models-amplify-quantization-error-large-ones-attenuate-it says the small end is where quantization error is amplified rather than damped. Whether the parameter saving survives to a GGUF on a consumer card is untested and is the question that decides whether this matters here at all.
