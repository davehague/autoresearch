Layernorm placement gates large-model stability — a detail that is actually the ceiling

A recurring lesson in this vault is that much of what reads as architecture is really about keeping training stable at scale. Megatron's easily-missed second contribution is a clean instance: the original BERT's post-LN block (layernorm after the residual add) degrades as the model grows past a few hundred million parameters — bigger is worse, not better. Rearranging the connections so layernorm sits on the identity path (the pre-LN-style fix) is what lets their 3.9B BERT keep improving. A systems paper quietly reporting an architecture-stability result.

The point generalizes beyond one paper. The same genre of fix reappears whenever a new regime stresses the block: chameleon-early-fusion-mixed-modal found that training a single transformer on mixed image+text tokens diverges past ~7B with a stock LLaMA block, and the cure was again a normalization change — QK-normalization and reordered layernorm (mixed-modal-training-needs-qk-norm-to-stay-stable). Two independent regimes (scale, modality mixing), same conclusion: the block's normalization details, not its headline shape, are what gate whether a large model trains at all.

This is suggestive rather than strong — Megatron reports the direction without an ablation table isolating layernorm placement from the other changes, and it's a 2019 BERT-era result. But it's the first note in transformer-architecture to name normalization placement as a first-class scaling variable, complementing the position (position-can-be-a-rotation-not-an-addition) and attention-cost (attention-is-memory-bound-not-compute-bound) threads.
