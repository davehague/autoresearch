On-policy distillation's feedback loop, decomposed: a local log-probability-gap-to-update conversion, and a tangent-kernel broadcast that reshapes untouched prefixes -- explaining why students respond weakly to disagreement, why locally-agreeable updates can still degenerate generation, and why offline prep before OPD pays off later rather than immediately

TL;DR: Hong, Motwani, Chen et al. (Oxford/EPFL/City University of Hong Kong) give a mechanistic account of on-policy distillation (OPD): each update both changes the student's parameters and the distribution of states its own future rollouts will visit, closing a feedback loop. They split this into a local stage (the teacher-student log-probability difference at a supervised prefix converts into a weight update, scaled by how much that difference overlaps directions the student's current parameters can express) and a broadcast stage (formalized via the neural tangent kernel between prefixes, through which an update at one supervised state reshapes predictions at other, unsupervised states the student's own rollouts will subsequently reach). Chaining broadcast effects across training steps explains how a sequence of locally-reasonable updates can still drift the rollout distribution toward states the teacher never scored well.

What it says. The framework explains two standing puzzles this map has observed without a mechanism: why a student can respond weakly to OPD despite large measured teacher-student disagreement (the signal doesn't overlap an expressible parameter direction), and why updates that improve local teacher agreement can simultaneously raise the probability of unsuccessful continuations elsewhere (broadcast reshapes predictions at prefixes the update never touched, feeding the student its own less-reliable rollouts at the next step) -- a candidate mechanism for the kind of pass@K degeneration on-policy-distillation-illusory-pass-at-k measured empirically without explaining. Separately: offline (teacher-forced) distillation run before OPD does not substantially move immediate held-out accuracy on its own, but changes the student's starting geometry such that a subsequent OPD run -- which can degrade generation from an unprepared start -- instead yields substantial accuracy gains, independently converging with this map's established two-stage (offline-cold-start-then-on-policy) staging requirement via a wholly different, theoretical route.

Caveats. Framed as general theory (log-probability gap, tangent-kernel broadcast) rather than tied to rollout position specifically -- so while the mechanism is consistent with this strand's three independently-measured "early rollout prefix carries the load-bearing supervision signal" findings (prefix-adapted-teacher-beats-task-adapted-teacher-opd SPTA, cord-couples-rollouts-to-close-opd-blind-spot CoRD, o2pd-restricts-opd-to-the-rollout-prefix-and-gates-the-divergence-by-overlap O²PD), the paper does not test or claim that specific link; it would need to show the broadcast kernel's effect concentrates early in a rollout to close that gap. No benchmark table (model scale, task, specific accuracy deltas for the offline-then-OPD staging result) was extracted on this read. No code release stated.
