Every branch needs its own correction, not the chain's

The cheap way to combine causal correction with a draft tree is to run the correction once along the most likely chain and then build the tree from the corrected distribution. It is fast, it is the obvious composition, and it leaves up to 16.4% of the accepted length on the floor — at essentially identical round time (28.3 ms against 28.5 ms on GSM8K), so the loss is pure.

The reason is that correction is what supplies the intra-block dependency a block-parallel drafter lacks. A diffusion drafter emits Π qᵢ(yᵢ|x₁:t): every position conditioned on the verified prefix, none conditioned on the realized earlier draft tokens. A chain correction restores that dependency for one path. Alternative branches then inherit a correction computed against a token history they do not have, which is a mismatch of exactly the kind the correction exists to remove. Correcting each branch against its own prefix is not a refinement of the chain approach; it is the thing the chain approach was approximating.

So causal correction and tree coverage are one quantity rather than two features. This is the same non-separability speculative-decoding declared when it argued its apparent sub-threads are the terms of a single equation — accepted length against draft cost plus verify cost — and it is now visible inside a single ablation table: coverage without per-branch conditioning is worth less than coverage with it, at the same price. The acceptance-position curves show where the difference lands. The tree method wins at early positions, the chain-correction method reaches later ones, and the joint method does both, accepting nearly the entire 16-token draft block in almost half of all GSM8K rounds.

The transfer result is the part that makes this a claim about drafters rather than about one method. The construction is applied untrained to a second, architecturally different correction head — DSpark's Markov head in place of Domino's RNN head — and improves both metrics in all six dataset–temperature settings, acceptance by 14.6–40.6% and speedup by up to 34.3%. A per-branch scoring discipline that helps two unrelated correction architectures is a property of the drafting problem, not a tuned pairing. That matters for anyone assembling a pipeline from released parts, which is the normal case: this is a training-free wrapper around correction heads other people trained.

Read against a-downloaded-drafter-is-a-bet-on-its-training-distribution, the transfer is the reassuring direction — the wrapper survives a change of drafter where the drafter's own distributional bet does not. Read against a-greedy-draft-head-is-an-under-queried-conditional, it is the same diagnosis one level up: a draft head asked for a marginal when the target wants a conditional gives back less than it knows, and the fix in both cases is to ask it more times, conditioned properly, rather than to make it bigger.

Bounds. The 16.4% figure is one model (Qwen3-4B) at one temperature and one verification budget; the transfer study is one additional correction head on the same base model. Batch size one, one GPU, no quality evaluation (verification is unchanged, so the speedup is lossless by construction). Both compared constructions are the paper's own implementations rather than the original authors', which is the standard caveat on a re-implemented baseline.
