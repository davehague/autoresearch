The open VLMs were largely closed VLMs, laundered

The paper's framing is an indictment of the category: "the strongest open-weight models rely heavily on synthetic data from proprietary VLMs to achieve good performance, effectively distilling these closed models into open ones," so "the scientific community is still missing foundational knowledge about how to build performant VLMs from scratch." Open weights, closed provenance. What is reproducible is the architecture; what actually produced the capability — the supervision — came out of a model nobody outside the vendor can inspect, license cleanly, or improve on.

The demonstration is that the dependency was not necessary. With zero VLM-generated training data, a conventional architecture (CLIP ViT-L/14 336px, MLP connector, open LLM backbone) and a deliberately plain two-stage recipe — caption pretraining, then SFT, all parameters updating, no RLHF — Molmo-72B posts the top score in its 11-benchmark table at 81.2, above GPT-4o's 78.5 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 76.7, and ranks second on human-preference Elo behind only GPT-4o.

This is the push-back that moves vision-language-models off n=1 in two directions at once. It replicates the architecture of a-projection-layer-is-enough-to-add-a-modality from an independent group 18 months later at frontier-competitive quality — the adapter-shaped bridge holds. And it directly contests the data half, synthetic-instructions-from-a-blind-teacher-bootstrap-a-corpus, whose 158K corpus came from a blind GPT-4 given captions and boxes: Molmo names that entire lineage as the field's blind spot and routes around it (speech-sourced-captions-replace-the-teacher-vlm). The map's settled claim is now the architecture; the live tension is where supervision comes from.

Read the 81.2 as a suite average, not a capability verdict. The wins concentrate in perception and counting — Flickr Count 85.2 vs GPT-4o's 59.6, CountBenchQA 91.2 vs 87.9, the pointing data paying off — while knowledge-heavy reasoning trails badly: MMMU 54.1 against GPT-4o's 69.1, MathVista 58.6 vs 63.8. A model can top an average while sitting 15 points below on multi-discipline reasoning. The openness claim also has a seam: the flagship's backbone is Qwen2-72B, open-weights but not open-data, so "from scratch" holds for the multimodal training and its data, not for the language model underneath.
