Recovery-stage on-policy distillation inherits the pruning stage's calibration choice, and reweighting that choice toward what the teacher would actually miss closes much of the gap recovery alone leaves open

This map already carries two findings about recovery fine-tuning that this source sits directly between: "recovery fine-tuning redistributes capability rather than restoring it" and "recovery fine-tuning is not optional and does not close the gap" (both pre-dating this ingest). ReCal does not change the recovery procedure (still SFT then on-policy distillation) — it changes what the pruning stage was calibrated to protect in the first place, and that upstream change measurably improves what recovery can achieve downstream. The mechanism is specific: forward-KL divergence between an unpruned teacher and a pruned probe identifies which teacher-supported predictions a pruning decision is about to disrupt, and calibration statistics get reweighted (not the pruning criterion itself — Wanda, FLAP, Minitron, and LLM-Pruner all run unchanged on top of the reweighted statistics) to protect those predictions.

Direct interaction with yesterday's calibration-sensitivity finding. sparsedecoding-autoregressive-calibration-for-decode-pruning (ingested 2026-10-09) showed that calibrating a pruner on fixed natural text (C4) targets a decode-time activation distribution the model never actually visits, and fixed that by calibrating on the model's own self-generated decode activations instead — a distributional calibration fix, aimed at matching what decoding looks like. ReCal calibrates on a different axis entirely: not which activation distribution to sample from, but which specific predictions, within whatever distribution is sampled, the teacher would most object to losing. Both are calibration-stage fixes for structured pruning published within 24 hours of each other, by different groups, targeting different failure modes (distributional mismatch vs. teacher-disagreement localization), neither tested against or combined with the other — a concrete, cheap open question this map did not have before this week: do they compose, or do they fight over the same calibration budget?

Where the gain concentrates is diagnostic, not incidental. On the heaviest-damage quintile of tokens, post-recovery KL-to-teacher falls from 0.627 (base pruner + OPD) to 0.470 (ReCal + OPD) — a 25.1% further reduction on top of what recovery alone achieves — and that quintile carries 81-83% of a response's total within-response KL damage. This directly names where this map's existing "recovery fine-tuning does not close the gap" finding was leaving the most value on the table: the worst-damaged tokens, not an even spread.

Uneven gains, and a counter-example. IFBench declines in all 8 model-criterion pairs tested — the gain is concentrated in reasoning benchmarks (AIME, LiveCodeBench), not instruction-following. One pair (LLM-Pruner on Qwen3-8B) is net-negative on math even with ReCal (12.4%→9.2% AIME), so the fix is not uniform across pruning criteria.

Cross-linked to distillation-small-models, whose recovery-stage findings concern on-policy distillation generally; this is a pruning-specific instance of making OPD recovery more effective by changing what the model was damaged against before OPD ever runs, which that map's existing OPD-mechanism entries (teacher proximity, KL direction, token selection) do not address — none of them touch the pruning/calibration stage that precedes recovery in this pipeline.

Caveats carried from the source. FFN-width pruning only, two Qwen3 models, one fixed SFT+OPD recovery pipeline. The full ReCal procedure is evaluated as a package — trajectory choice, KL direction, and token weighting are not individually ablated. One model-criterion pair is net-negative; IFBench declines uniformly. No code/repo link found.
