Checkpoint averaging helps PTQ for a reason, finally: it's a lag-vs-noise trade, and quantization grids of different coarseness want different amounts of each

This map's damage-measurement strand has so far been about fixes applied at or after the quantization step itself — a reconstruction-loss objective (rmse-fixes-ptq-optimization-imbalance-for-free), a rounding mode (stochastic-rounding-halves-low-precision-inference-degradation). This source moves one stage upstream, into the pretraining optimizer, and gives a mechanism for an effect that was previously just an empirical correlation practitioners reported as "inconsistent and fragile": averaging checkpoints during pretraining (rather than relying on learning-rate decay to a single final point) tends to produce models that quantize better.

The mechanism: decompose an averaged checkpoint's distance from an idealized fully-converged model into temporal lag (how much genuine training progress is sacrificed by pulling in older checkpoints) and spatial noise (how much stochastic per-checkpoint fluctuation survives the average). These trade off against each other as the averaging window and weighting change, and the paper derives a continuous kernel family (LNWA) tracing the resulting Pareto frontier, recovering LAWA (uniform averaging, long history, low noise, high lag), "Stable" (most-recent-checkpoint, the opposite extreme), WMA, and EMA all as points on or near that frontier rather than as unrelated heuristics.

The quantization-specific claim is the sharper one: which point on the frontier a model should sit at depends on how coarse the eventual quantization grid is, because PTQ error's sensitivity to local weight noise scales with grid coarseness. Coarse grids (2-bit) are predicted — and the authors report confirmed on OPT-125M/350M/1.3B — to prefer less spatial noise even at the cost of more lag; fine grids (4-bit) prefer the opposite. That is a genuinely new axis for this map: every other damage-measurement or allocation source here treats the pretrained checkpoint as a fixed input and intervenes at or after quantization time; this one says the checkpoint itself should be produced differently depending on which bit-width it is destined for, which is a planning decision that has to be made before the quantization method is even chosen.

Caveats, and why this is held at suggestive rather than strong. The specific perplexity numbers behind "LNWA beats both decay baselines" and "largest improvement at W2" live in figures that did not convert to extractable text in this ingest's read — the mechanism and kernel derivation are directly confirmed from the paper's appendix text, but the magnitude of the empirical win is not independently verified here and needs a follow-up read of the archived PDF. Models tested (125M-1.3B) are well below the 1-8B range this vault's local-deployment premise cares about; whether the lag-noise trade and its bit-width reversal hold at that scale, or compose with this map's existing reconstruction-loss and rounding-mode fixes, is untested. The theory's covariance and bounded-progress-variation assumptions are stated as empirically checked by the authors but are modeling assumptions outside the tested regime.
