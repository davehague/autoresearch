LLM judges match human agreement — and carry three biases that don't average out

The paper that both legitimized LLM-as-judge and named its failure modes, and the vault has been leaning on the first half without ever grading the second. GPT-4's agreement with human preference exceeds 80% — the same rate humans agree with each other, which reframes the problem: judges are not approximating a clean ground truth, because there isn't one, only a noisy human signal at the same noise floor.

Against that: position bias (which response is shown first), verbosity bias (longer preferred regardless of quality), self-enhancement bias (GPT-4 favors its own outputs by ~10% win rate; Claude-v1 by ~25%), and weak grading on math/logic. These are systematic and directional, so more samples do not wash them out. Position bias is mitigable by swapping order and requiring consistency; verbosity and self-enhancement are not cleanly fixable, and the paper says so. Its own recommendation is the one to carry: judge evals and traditional benchmarks are complementary.

Filed pushes-back because of where it lands. Every judge-ranked result in the vault inherits this: the whole preference thread in post-training-rl (DPO, ORPO, and SimPO all report AlpacaEval/Arena-Hard/MT-Bench — and see preference-training-should-optimize-the-metric-you-decode-with, where a length-normalizing method is scored by a length-biased judge), LLaVA's GPT-4 evaluation in vision-language-models, and the vendor deltas in distillation-small-models. The self-enhancement figure is the sharpest: a GPT-4 judge inflating GPT-4-family outputs by ~10% is the measurement behind the "closed teacher loop" vision-language-models flagged on suspicion on 2026-07-18, since LLaVA's training data and its evaluator were both GPT-4.

This names the fourth distinct way an in-vault number misleads, alongside leakage (benchmark-scores-can-be-memorization), construct mismatch, and infrastructure non-equivalence.

Caveats: 2023 vintage — GPT-4-0314 and Claude-v1 are retired, so the magnitudes are stale even if the failure modes are structural, and nobody has re-measured them on current judges. The authors (LMSYS) operate the platform they are validating. The 80% figure is on open-ended chat, not the technical or domain-specific outputs this vault's premise targets.
