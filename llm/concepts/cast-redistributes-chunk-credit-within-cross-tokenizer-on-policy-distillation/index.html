The chunk-to-token credit problem this map's cross-tokenizer entry left open, with a solution that reuses this map's own OPD-stabilization trick

simpleopd-tokenizer-agnostic-on-policy-distillation established two separate things on this map: that cross-tokenizer distillation works by aligning only positions whose tokens cover identical text spans (90% of positions at the start of training, rising to 96-98%), and — unrelated to tokenization — that a KL penalty anchoring the student to its own initial policy fixes OPD's length/repetition degeneration where masking special tokens alone does not. Neither finding addressed what happens inside an aligned chunk: decoded-text alignment gives a chunk-level correspondence, but a chunk can span several student tokens, and nothing in that correspondence says how a chunk-level advantage signal should be distributed across them, or what should happen to student tokens that have no aligned teacher span at all.

CAST answers exactly this, and it is a direct beneficiary of both of SimpleOPD's findings: it is built on top of chunk-level decoded-text alignment (the same mechanism SimpleOPD validated), and its fix for unaligned positions — a KL penalty anchoring to the student's own SFT-initialization policy — is structurally identical to SimpleOPD's student-reference KL fix, independently arrived at in a different paper for a different symptom (SimpleOPD was fixing runaway length/repetition; CAST is bounding drift at positions with no teacher signal at all). That two independent groups converged on the same anchoring mechanism, for different proximate problems within the same cross-tokenizer-OPD setting, is suggestive that "anchor to the student's own initialization" is a generically useful stabilizer for this class of training, not a fix narrowly tailored to one failure mode.

What's genuinely new. The "shift" half — redistributing a chunk's advantage by predictive uncertainty times local teacher-signal smoothness, with weights that exactly solve a stated KL-regularized allocation problem while conserving the chunk's total advantage — has no precedent on this map. It is also validated in the shared-tokenizer setting (where chunking is punctuation-defined or fixed-length rather than forced by tokenizer mismatch), beating GKD/MiniLLM/DistiLLM, which places it as a general token-credit mechanism for on-policy distillation rather than a tokenizer-mismatch patch specifically.

Caveats carried from the source. Evaluation covers a limited set of model families (Qwen3, Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) on mainly reasoning-oriented training data. Training compute is not matched across all baselines. Most comparisons are single runs; three-seed confirmation covers only four Mistral and two Llama configurations. A teacher-scaling check found the effect is student-size-dependent rather than monotonic: a larger (30B-A3B) teacher beats an 8B teacher for Qwen3-4B, but the ordering reverses for Qwen3-1.7B. The authors flag their own benchmark-based checkpoint selection as a source of optimism in the reported averages, and note AIME-style subsets are small enough (15-30 problems) that each problem moves a score by 3.3-6.7 points.
