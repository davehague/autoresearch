The kernel that stopped waiting on memory

Nearly every quantization claim in this vault rests on one premise: single-stream decode is memory-bound, so fewer weight bytes means more tokens per second, and the advantage narrows as batching turns the workload compute-bound. attention-is-memory-bound-not-compute-bound states the mechanism, quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill states the asymmetry it produces, and the batch-size expiry date is folklore everyone repeats.

This source builds a kernel where the premise no longer holds, and — unusually — supplies the profiler evidence rather than asserting it. FluxBin reports >99.8% GPU busy time against 67–87% for FP16, with low memory-bandwidth utilization: the kernel is limited by on-chip lookup and compute throughput, not by HBM stalls. Two design choices get it there. Weights are stored as binary bases and the products are read out of a lookup table rather than dequantized to FP16 before a GEMM, so the dequantization work that quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill identifies as the cost that eventually eats the saving is not performed at all. And the column scale is fused into the table itself (X ⊙ α_c), removing those floating-point operations from the GEMV loop entirely.

The observable consequence is the one to check any future LUT claim against. The speedup does not fade with batch size. Conventional weight-only quantization's advantage shrinks as the batch grows because the win was bandwidth and the workload stops being bandwidth-bound; here the computation reduction is µ/K_b, a property of the sub-vector size and base count rather than of the memory system, so it is batch-size independent, and the batch dimension is tiled per thread block at fixed shared-memory cost. Decode-only speedup is 6.3–6.6× at batch 1 and the gap persists upward.

The energy figure follows from the same mechanism and is the part most relevant to anything running on a battery or a fan: average board power falls from ~225 W to ~115 W on 13B, because FP16 fused multiply-adds are replaced by additions and SRAM lookups. SRAM bandwidth is independent of HBM, so the trade moves work off a contended resource without worsening the memory bottleneck. Combined with the speedup that is the paper's 10.19× energy claim. This vault has been accumulating evidence that energy and latency reorder differently — energy-reorders-the-peft-methods-that-memory-ranks, and last night's finding that idle leakage can exceed transfer energy — and this is a third instance from a fourth direction.

Two limits on how far to carry it. The authors add the correct caveat themselves: GPU busy time reflects occupancy, not arithmetic-unit saturation, so "compute-bound" here is inferred from two indicators rather than measured directly. And the regime claim is one kernel on one datacenter GPU. What this licenses is a scope condition, not a reversal: the memory-bound decode premise holds for dequantize-then-GEMM kernels, and a LUT kernel is a different machine. Every "quantization is a bandwidth play" sentence in this vault should now carry that qualifier.

The scope condition also cuts the other way, and it is worth stating because it is the local-stack answer: llama.cpp, MLX and every runtime a reader of local-inference actually runs are dequantize-then-GEMM. None of this is reachable from them today.
