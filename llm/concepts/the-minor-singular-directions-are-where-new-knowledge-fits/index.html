Adapt where the weight is quiet

peft-methods has been contested since 2026-07-18 on one axis: low-rank adapters match full fine-tuning on task adaptation and fall short on domain acquisition, because full fine-tuning learns perturbations of 10–100× higher rank (low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new). Every source since has characterized the gap. This is the first to propose that the gap is about direction, not dimension.

Take the pretrained weight's SVD, W = UΣVᵀ, and initialize the adapter's frozen factor on the last r left singular vectors — the least-energetic directions — then train only the other factor. The update lives entirely in the subspace that carries the least of what the model already does. This is the exact mirror of PiSSA (adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling), which initializes on the principal components.

On a ~100,000-token German history book injected into Llama-2-7B-chat and scored on 102 held-out questions about it: baseline 27.4%, full fine-tuning 30.4%, LoRA 29.4%, this method 39.2%. The gain over baseline is +11.8 points against LoRA's +2.0 — the ratio the paper's abstract advertises as "5.9×". Retention is untouched (HellaSwag 57.8 → 57.8, where full fine-tuning drops 0.5). On short blog corpora the margin is smaller (+3.05 and +1.76 over LoRA) and the gap widens with corpus size, which is the direction a capacity story predicts and a noise story does not.

The subspace ablation is the load-bearing evidence, because it holds everything else fixed and varies only which singular directions the adapter spans: minor 75.63 > major 74.21 > random 73.75 > untouched 72.91. Random beats major. If dominant-subspace adaptation were merely neutral you would expect the opposite ordering.

There is a mechanism the vault can assemble that neither paper states. intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting found that LoRA introduces new high-ranking singular vectors absent from full fine-tuning's solutions, and that these drive forgetting. If adapting in the dominant subspace is what manufactures them, adapting in the subdominant one should both leave more room for genuinely new content and disturb less of what is there — which is what the accuracy and retention columns respectively show. The paper never runs the intruder-dimension SVD check, and that is the cheap confirmation this claim needs.

Held at suggestive, deliberately, and the discount is specific. The evaluation sets are 300 and 102 multiple-choice items, authored by the same people as the method and unreleased; the headline is a mean over four runs against a mean over two, with no variance reported on either. The full-fine-tuning arm exists in one cell and ran for 1 epoch against the adapters' 8. And MiCA's optimal learning rate (2e-3) is the top of the swept range while LoRA's optimum (5e-4) sits inside it, which is uncomfortably close to the confound adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact identified — though note that paper swept MiLoRA, the same minor-component idea, and found it null on task adaptation, which is the other half of this map. The two results are compatible as a scope condition: among adapters, on task adaptation, initialization is a tuning artifact; on domain acquisition, one initialization has now been reported to matter, once.

The status-moving experiment is exact and cheap: run this against full fine-tuning on the domain-acquisition setup from lora-vs-full-ft-illusion-of-equivalence, with the intruder-dimension check on both. Everything needed is public.
