One expert per token is enough to scale

The 2017 paper conjectured k≥2 was necessary for routing gradients; Switch falsifies it. Top-1 routing (gate value = softmax probability of the argmax expert, which keeps the router differentiable) preserves quality, and simplification is the point: less router compute, half the expert capacity requirement, cheaper all-to-all. At fixed FLOPs per token, expert count becomes a fourth scaling axis — 7× wall-clock to T5-Base quality, gains monotone from 2 to 256 experts, surviving fine-tuning (+4.4 SuperGLUE) and multilingual transfer (all 101 languages).

The load-bearing machinery is expert capacity: static tensor shapes force each expert to accept (tokens/N) × capacity factor; overflow tokens are dropped through the residual. The single simplified aux loss α·N·Σ fᵢPᵢ (α=10⁻²) keeps drops <1%, and lower capacity factors (1.0–1.25) win the speed-quality trade. Sparse models are also numerically fragile in specific, cheaply-fixed ways: cast only the router to float32 (bfloat16-elsewhere speed, float32 stability — pure bfloat16 diverges), init ×0.1 (seed variance 0.68 → 0.01), and fine-tune with expert dropout 0.4 against small-task overfitting. The successor stability story is router-z-loss-buys-moe-stability-for-free.

Also quietly opened here: sparse→dense distillation — initialize the student from the teacher's non-expert weights, 0.75/0.25 hard/soft loss, keep ~30% of the sparse gain at 95–99% compression — the compress-the-architecture direction of distillation-small-models, four years before reasoning-trace distillation.

Caveat: encoder-decoder T5 on TPUs, Google's own baselines throughout; the decoder-only replication is moe-buys-dense-quality-at-a-fraction-of-active-params.
