The instrument this vault retired is still the field's headline

On 2026-07-28 this vault moved context-extension to contested on the strength of claimed-context-length-is-not-effective-context-length: across 17 models and 13 synthetic tasks, near-perfect needle-in-a-haystack scores turned out to be compatible with an effective context below 4K. Needle retrieval is the easiest possible long-context test — one fact, verbatim, no distractors, no aggregation, no multi-hop — and models pass it while failing everything harder.

One day later, the vault ingests a KV-cache quantizer whose long-context section is a needle sweep: 4k to 104k tokens, every method held to a quarter of the full cache, TurboQuant scoring 0.997 against full precision's 0.997. Presented as the demonstration that the method is safe for long context.

This is not misconduct and the paper's headline claim survives — but it survives on its other evaluation, and the ordering is the finding. LongBench-E is real long-context work (multi-document QA, summarization, few-shot, code completion, deliberately uniform length distribution), and the 3.5-bit row matches the full cache on it. That is the evidence. The needle plot is decoration on top of it.

Three things follow, and they are about the field rather than this paper.

The construct-mismatch failure mode evaluation-contamination catalogued for models applies unchanged to compression methods. A KV compressor that preserved only the tokens needed to find one inserted sentence would score 0.997 and be useless. Nothing about a needle test can distinguish "the cache still supports reasoning across the window" from "the cache still supports exact retrieval of a salient string," and those are exactly the two hypotheses a compression paper needs to separate. The reading rule for the vault is direct: when a KV-compression result leads with a needle plot, read the LongBench column instead.

The needle test does establish one thing cleanly, and it is worth keeping. It separates families: the two token-eviction methods (SnapKV 0.858, PyramidKV 0.895) fall well below the two theoretically grounded quantizers (0.995, 0.997). A method that discards tokens can discard the needle; a method that compresses all of them cannot. So the test is diagnostic for "did you throw information away" and uninformative for "is the remaining information usable" — which is a sharper statement of its role than either this source or RULER makes.

Timing is the point. The vault held the instrument critique for eight days before meeting a source that depends on the instrument. That is the connect layer doing what it exists for: a claim graded on one map arriving in time to condition how the next map reads its own evidence. It also means this vault should expect to keep applying it — needle plots are cheap, standard, and appear in nearly every long-context systems paper, so the discount is a standing one rather than a note about this source.
