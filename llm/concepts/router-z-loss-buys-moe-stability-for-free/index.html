Router z-loss buys MoE stability for free

The systematic study of why sparse models diverge and what the fixes cost. Almost everything that stabilizes — tight update clipping, dropout, input-jitter, removing multiplicative interactions (GEGLU, RMSNorm scale params) — pays in quality, sometimes catastrophically (clipping: −1.755 → −4.206). The exception: L_z = mean((log Σ e^logits)²) at c_z = 0.001 on the router logits — 3/3 seeds stable and slightly better quality than baseline.

The mechanism is numerics, not optimization: routers add exponential functions, and bfloat16's roundoff (up to 65,536× float32's) doesn't reorder a softmax but does flip top-k threshold decisions and perturb the gate value scaling expert outputs. Clipping logits acts after the roundoff; the z-loss teaches the model to emit logits small enough to be represented accurately. Two corollaries worth keeping: multiplicative components boost quality while worsening stability (keep them, stabilize around them), and this is another instance of the pattern layernorm-placement-gates-large-model-stability and mixed-modal-training-needs-qk-norm-to-stay-stable established — each new architectural stressor (scale, modality mixing, now routing) demands its own numerics/normalization fix as the price of training at all (transformer-architecture's stability thread).

Caveat: encoder-decoder models, Adafactor, TPUs; the authors observed (but did not show) decoder-only models to be more stable. Capstone validation: ST-MoE-32B (269B params, 32B-dense FLOPs) — the first sparse SOTA across transfer benchmarks (SuperGLUE test 91.2; ANLI R3 53.4 → 74.7).
