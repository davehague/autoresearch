Everything is 4 bits now, and the bill scales inversely with model size

The vault's headline that four-bit-weights-put-llms-on-device has always been a weight-only claim: the weights are stored in 4 bits and upcast for a matmul that still runs in FP16. That buys memory and bandwidth, which is why decode gets faster, and it explicitly does not buy compute — quantization-speeds-decode-and-can-slow-prefill records the sting, measured on llama.cpp, that prefill gets up to 28% slower down the quant ladder because dequantizing packed weights costs more than the smaller read saves.

Removing the outliers by rotation (rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free) makes the activations quantizable too, and once both operands are INT4 the multiplication happens on 4-bit tensor cores with an INT32 accumulator. There is no dequantize step in the inner loop, and prefill goes the other way: 1.97×–2.16× faster on a 7B at batch 1–64, up to 3.33× on 70B, with peak decode memory down 3.6×–3.9×. The measurements are on RTX 3090s, chosen by the authors because they "target consumer-type GPUs" — which makes this the vault's first evidence that the compute-bound half of the workload is reachable at all on hardware its readers own. The map's rule therefore needs a qualifier it did not have: weight-only quantization slows prefill, weight-and-activation quantization speeds it up.

The accuracy story is where the vault should be careful, because the framing everyone quotes is the 70B number. Averaged over six commonsense zero-shot tasks, W4A4KV4 costs 1.09 points at 70B, 2.80 at 13B and 4.18 at 7B. "Retains 99% of zero-shot performance" is true of the 70B and of nothing else in the paper. The gradient runs the wrong way for this vault's premise — the models a local user can actually run are the ones that pay most — and the same size-dependence shows up in the calibration story: without any calibration data at all, round-to-nearest weight quantization is lossless at INT8 on both 7B and 70B, while at INT4 it costs 2.27 perplexity on the 7B against 0.34 on the 70B. Second-order reconstruction (second-order-reconstruction-quantizes-at-scale) earns its two GPU-hours mainly at small scale.

Two boundaries on how much to believe the 7B figure in either direction. Finer quantization groups recover a useful fraction — per-column 6.10 perplexity, group-256 5.98, group-128 5.93, group-64 5.88 against a 5.47 baseline — so the headline is a per-column result and the ladder is graded, exactly as the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff found for weights. And the evaluation is perplexity plus six commonsense tasks with no GSM8K, no MMLU and no reasoning benchmark anywhere, which by this vault's own 2026-07-24 finding is the family where quantization damage is least visible. A 4-point drop on the easy half is not a bound on what happens to multi-step arithmetic; it is a reason to expect worse and an observation that nobody has measured it.
