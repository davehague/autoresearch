LoRA+'s asymmetric learning rate is not free of a hyperparameter of its own — the B/A ratio itself can collapse training under standard initialization, and the fix is a different initialization for A, not a smaller ratio

TL;DR: peft-methods already treats LoRA+ (16x learning rate on B vs. A) as close to a free win — "the free version of a learning-rate sweep" (a-differential-learning-rate-is-the-free-version-of-the-sweep), winning 18-19 of 24 model-task pairs under a fixed LR. This paper (University of Waterloo/Vector Institute, NeurIPS 2026) names a failure mode the map hasn't captured: under standard LoRA initialization, pushing the B/A learning-rate ratio higher can cause unstable training and ratio-dependent performance collapse, traced to a non-vanishing initialization-noise term in the update dynamics. The fix — Init[A²], scaling A's initialization variance as n⁻² instead of the standard n⁻¹ while keeping B zero-initialized — suppresses that noise by an extra factor of 1/n and substantially widens the range of B/A ratios that train stably, without changing LoRA's zero-initial-output guarantee.

What it says.

The setup. LoRA's standard initialization (Init[A]) sets B₀=0 and draws A₀ with variance scaling as β²n⁻¹ (width n), so the adapter starts at zero output. LoRA+ assigns separate learning rates η_A, η_B to the two factors, with η_B > η_A; the ratio η_B/η_A is itself an unavoidable extra hyperparameter, and recent empirical work found that large ratios can both improve adaptation and cause instability, depending on initialization scale and rank. This paper derives why via a signal-noise decomposition of the LoRA update: under Init[A], the update carries an init-induced noise term whose magnitude is affected by η_B, the init scale β, and rank r — a noise floor that doesn't vanish as training proceeds, and that caps how much benefit an aggressive η_B/η_A ratio can actually deliver before it destabilizes training.

The method. Init[A²] keeps B₀=0 (preserving the zero-initial-output property every LoRA variant relies on) but scales A's initialization variance as n⁻² rather than n⁻¹. Under matched rank and training time, this reduces the initialization-noise energy by an additional factor of 1/n relative to standard Init[A] — a width-dependent correction, so the fix matters more as the adapted layer gets wider.

The result. Across a toy MLP (controlled, feature-norm trajectories), encoder-only RoBERTa-base on GLUE, and decoder-only Llama 3-8B/Llama 3.2-1B on arithmetic (~100k examples) and commonsense (~170k examples) generation benchmarks, Init[A²] widens the stable learning-rate region across initialization scales, adapter ranks, and LoRA update multipliers; prevents the high-ratio collapse standard Init[A] exhibits as β or r grows; and reduces best-ratio drift relative to both standard Init[A] and an alternative Init[AB] scaling. The practical upshot the paper argues for is that the width-based guideline η_B ≈ n·η_A becomes a useful starting point for a finite pretrained model under Init[A²], where it was not reliable under standard init.

How this sits on the map. This doesn't contradict a-differential-learning-rate-is-the-free-version-of-the-sweep's headline (LoRA+'s fixed 16x ratio won 18-19/24 pairs with no reported instability in-vault) — that ratio may simply sit inside the stable region this paper maps out. What it adds is the boundary and a mechanism: the map's "LoRA+ is a free lunch" framing has an asterisk at high ratios, scoped by width and init scale, and this paper supplies both the diagnosis (a noise term standard init never eliminates) and a one-line fix. It is also a second entry, from a different angle, in this map's running argument that initialization scale interacts with rank and learning rate rather than being a separable choice — the same shape of interaction this map has tracked across rsLoRA (α/r vs. α/√r), PiSSA/LoftQ's rival initialization subspaces, and AdaLoRA's rank-reallocation-inherits-α/r finding.

Caveats. No single headline accuracy table — the empirical claims are shown via LR-sweep stability curves and training trajectories, not one comparison number. No quantized-base experiment (QLoRA-style), so whether the noise term interacts with quantization error the way LoftQ's joint quantize-and-init result would predict is untested. Only two decoder-only model sizes (1B, 8B) and two task families (arithmetic, commonsense); no reasoning-benchmark or instruction-tuning evaluation, and no test against this map's own rank floor (r=1) finding. Two-author paper, one institution pair.
