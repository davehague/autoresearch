A second, independent looped-transformer architecture reaches real benchmark scale and ships four checkpoints — and it pays, unaddressed, the same per-recurrence KV-cache tax kv-cache-management's newest entry just showed how to remove

TL;DR: Accordion (Tsinghua/Baidu) is a mixture-of-experts transformer that serves five inference-depth budgets from one set of weights by looping a fixed prefix window of layers (7-18 of 24) a variable number of times, trained with budget-sampled joint pretraining at 3.6T tokens and carried through SFT and RL via parameter-group merging and budget-matched multi-teacher on-policy distillation. Increasing depth lifts the macro-average across 37 benchmarks by 3.01 points, and the released model line (Base, Base-32K, Instruct, RL) surpasses each single-domain specialist it distills from on that specialist's own task. The paper also reports, without addressing, that its second attention pass maintains its own KV cache — storage scales 1.25x-1.5x with loop depth — the exact cost benice-kv-sharing-collapses-looped-model-recurrence-memory (ingested one day earlier) showed is removable for a related looped/recurrent-depth architecture family.

What it says.

The setup. This is a different point in the looped-transformer design space from BeNice's object: BeNice repeats one entire block N times (recurrent-depth, parameter count fixed independent of depth); Accordion repeats a fixed contiguous prefix window of an otherwise-normal-depth MoE transformer (layers 7-18 of a 24-layer model) a variable number of additional times, with the minimum budget being the plain unlooped model and four additional budgets layering on more repetitions of that same window. Both are "loop a shared block to vary effective depth," and both are new to transformer-architecture's existing block vocabulary, but they are not the same mechanism — Accordion's prefix-window choice and its MoE substrate (top-8 routing, 128,000-entry reduced-vocabulary DeepSeek-V3 tokenizer, sigmoid router scores) are specific design choices this paper's own ablations justify (which layers to repeat, how many budgets to train, whether to share the router across passes, whether each budget gets its own normalization scale).

The method. Joint pretraining samples one of five budgets per micro-batch and optimizes only that budget's next-token loss — no explicit cross-budget consistency term. Post-training (SFT, RL) preserves the five budgets through two devices: parameter-group merging (combining budget-specific parameter updates) and budget-matched multi-teacher on-policy distillation, which integrates three separately-trained domain experts (math, code, and a third domain) into the single elastic-depth model.

The result. After 3.6T tokens of 8K-context pretraining, Accordion-6B-A0.5B's five-category macro-average across 37 benchmarks rises 3.01 points from lowest to highest budget, with the middle budget already capturing 89% of that total gain at half the additional layer executions of the top budget — a sharply diminishing-but-real return to looping further. The post-trained model (after RL) beats each individual domain specialist on that specialist's own task-and-budget average while holding strong instruction-following across all five budgets, which is the paper's evidence that the single elastic model isn't a compromise relative to training five separate models. Depth-dependent gains persist specifically in mathematical reasoning and code generation after RL, where the paper names the budget-matched distillation step as what prevents those gains from washing out during alignment training.

The unaddressed cost. Buried in the architecture section, not in the results: "During autoregressive inference, the second attention pass maintains a separate key-value (KV) cache built from its own activations. Consequently, for our equal-shaped layers, KV storage scales as (L+k)/L relative to k=0: 1.25x at k=6 and 1.5x at k=12." The paper treats this as an accepted analytical cost (Table 44) and does not test or propose a fix — no cache-sharing mechanism analogous to BeNice's broadcast-and-adapt stage appears anywhere in the design-choice ablations (§2.2). This is a second, independent, concurrent (one day apart, un-cross-citing) data point that the looped/recurrent-depth architecture family's KV-cache-multiplication problem is real and recurring across genuinely different looping schemes — and that at least one live, benchmark-validated, multi-checkpoint release of the family ships without BeNice's fix.

Caveats. Model-weight release status is not confirmed in this read (abstract names four checkpoints and "Project website" but the archived PDF text does not state a license or hosting link explicitly). KV-cache-scaling figures are analytical (derived from the equal-shaped-layer assumption), not a measured memory/latency number under the paper's own reported runtime benchmarks. No test of whether Accordion's prefix-window looping is compatible with BeNice's cache-sharing mechanism (different loop structure — a contiguous window repeated variably vs. a single block repeated uniformly — so compatibility is not obvious either way).
