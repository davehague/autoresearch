On-device SLM decoding needs its own DVFS governor — generic mobile or cloud-tuned frequency scaling leaves real energy and latency-QoS on the table, and the fix is a one-time-profiled, workload-aware estimator rather than a heavier runtime policy

Running small language models locally on mobile edge devices (Jetson-class boards) exposes a DVFS problem neither built-in governors nor the prior learned state of the art (GearDVFS) were designed for. Two specific effects: (1) autoregressive token generation has phase-dependent frequency/power/latency interdependencies that differ from prefill and from generic mobile workloads; (2) GPU, CPU and EMC (memory controller) frequencies interact opaquely during collaborative execution, so a governor tuned on one processor alone misses cross-processor effects.

DVFSLM addresses both with a one-time offline profiling step per device-model pair — sampling ~5% of context lengths (50 of 1024) across GPU-EMC frequency combinations, with CPU held at a separately profiled fixed threshold since its interaction effect is small (under 1% latency once fixed) — to train XGBoost-based estimators correlating matrix-operation scale with power/latency. At runtime, a governor uses those estimators to jointly set GPU/EMC frequencies to minimize energy per token subject to a configurable token-generation-rate deadline.

Across seven SLMs (GPT2-small/medium/large/xl, Qwen2.5-0.5B, TinyLLaMA-1.1B, OLMoE-1B-7B MoE) on Jetson AGX Orin and Orin NX running llama.cpp (q4_0), DVFSLM cuts energy per token by up to 12.4% versus the latest built-in rule-based governors and up to 8.4% versus GearDVFS (an RL-based DVFS method, here adapted with an added deadline-compliance reward term), while improving latency QoS (meeting the target token rate) by up to 93.12% and 69.14% respectively. A worked example makes the mechanism concrete: at a 20 tokens/s deadline on GPT2-large, GearDVFS runs greedily at high frequencies (403.6 mJ/token), the rule-based baseline is conservative enough to miss the deadline outright (423.4 mJ/token), and DVFSLM co-optimizes GPU/EMC to meet the deadline at 372.9 mJ/token. Device temperature stayed under 43°C across all tested SLMs at a 40 tokens/s deadline and 25°C ambient, even without an explicit thermal constraint. The profiling cost is real and disclosed rather than hidden: roughly 21-27 hours wall-clock, under 0.33 kWh, and under 0.61 GB storage per device-model pair.

Graded extends on local-inference's on-device single-user inference sub-thread: this is a hardware-scheduling lever orthogonal to the map's existing quantization/format and KV-cache-budget results — it sits underneath whatever quantized model and runtime is already chosen — and it is measured on genuinely edge/mobile hardware (Jetson AGX Orin/NX) rather than the datacenter-class GPUs (L4/A100/H100) this map's recent quantization/serving comparisons use.

Caveats: no public code release found for DVFSLM itself. GearDVFS is compared as an adapted reimplementation (deadline-compliance reward term added, policy retrained) rather than its originally published objective. All evaluation is on two Jetson SoC variants (Orin family) — generalization to other edge/mobile silicon is untested. Quantization is fixed to q4_0 GGUF throughout; interaction with other quantization formats or KV-cache configurations is unexplored. The headline percentages are best-case ("up to") figures across the tested sweep, not a single representative average.
