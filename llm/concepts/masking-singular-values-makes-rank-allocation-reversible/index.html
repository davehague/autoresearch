Why the adapter is written as an SVD

Once you accept that adapter budget should be distributed unevenly (where-the-adapter-budget-goes-matters-more-than-how-much-there-is), the question becomes how to move it during training without wrecking the run. The obvious implementation is to prune LoRA's existing structure: Δ = BA is a sum of rank-one outer products, so drop the weak ones. This source argues that the obvious implementation is the reason people have not been doing this, and then measures the difference.

The alternative is to write the update as a pseudo-SVD: Δ = PΛQ, with Λ a diagonal matrix of singular values stored as a vector, Λ initialized to zero so the adapter starts as a no-op, and P, Q regularized toward orthogonality with an explicit penalty ‖PᵀP − I‖²_F + ‖QQᵀ − I‖²_F rather than enforced structurally. Nobody computes an actual SVD; that would cost O(min(d1,d2)·d1·d2) per step per matrix.

Two properties follow, and both are about what happens when the allocator is wrong.

Reversibility. Pruning masks a singular value to zero while leaving its left and right vectors alive and trainable. A component the importance score misjudges at step 3,000 can come back at step 3,100 — the paper prunes every ~100 steps precisely to allow this. Deleting a LoRA doublet zeroes all its entries and stops training them, so a mistake is permanent. When the signal driving the decision is a first-order sensitivity estimated on a sampled minibatch — noisy by construction — the difference between a recoverable and an unrecoverable mistake is the whole design.

Smaller perturbation per removal. LoRA's A and B are not orthogonal, so their rank-one terms are not independent; deleting one moves the resulting matrix further from the original than truncating the smallest singular value of an (approximately) orthogonal decomposition does. The authors report this as training instability that "even hurts generalization," not merely as a worse final score.

The controlled comparison holds the importance score, the budget scheduler and the training setup fixed and varies only the pruning target: AdaLoRA beats structured LoRA pruning in every cell at every budget level tested (RTE at 0.16% of parameters: 87.73 against 86.15).

The importance signal itself is a third design choice that carries measurable weight. Each rank-one component is scored by its singular value's sensitivity plus the mean sensitivity of its two vectors — mean rather than sum, so the score does not reward components simply for having more parameters attached. The per-entry sensitivity |w · ∇_w L| (a first-order estimate of the loss change from zeroing w) is smoothed by an exponential moving average and multiplied by an uncertainty term measuring how much that estimate has been moving. Substituting raw sensitivity, or the plain magnitude of the singular value, costs up to 0.9% — comparable to the entire margin the method wins by, which is a useful calibration of how much of the result is the parameterization and how much is the scoring.

The general lesson travels past this method: when an automated allocator runs on a noisy signal, make its decisions reversible. The same shape appears in activation-memory-is-a-tunable-continuum-not-a-fixed-cost and in the-parallelism-configuration-is-searchable-not-a-dial — a budget search over a continuum, where the design that survives is the one that can back out.

Bounded by the same scope as the rest of this source: a 183M encoder and an encoder-decoder, GLUE and SQuAD and XSum, no decoder-only LLM and no quantized base. And the cost side is unreported — computing sensitivity for every entry of P, Λ and Q plus two moving averages is strictly more per-step work than LoRA, and the paper gives no wall-clock or memory figure for it.
