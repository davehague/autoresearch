The drafter already knew how to branch

DSpark-style semi-autoregressive drafters produce a whole B-token block from one backbone pass, then refine it position by position with a lightweight Markov head: z_d = L_d + Markov(y_{d−1}), take the argmax, move on. The head is explicitly a conditional — it was trained to bias the shared logits by the previous token — and greedy decoding queries it at exactly one previous token. PCTree's observation is that this leaves the conditional structure unexploited: re-score the same head at each concrete parent and you get parent-specific children for free, because parents at one depth share L_d and differ only by a bias vector. Branching costs a matrix-add, not a forward pass.

The mechanism-isolation experiment is what makes this a finding rather than a system. Three variants on one checkpoint: the chain reads τ = 9.41; a tree that reuses one distribution per depth reads 10.23; the parent-conditioned tree reads 11.16. So roughly half the gain is "trees beat chains," which the tree-speculation literature already knew, and the other half — +9.1% — is specifically the re-conditioning. Rounds per sample fall 26.81 → 22.63, which is where the wall-clock comes from.

The generalisable form: a drafter trained with any conditional structure is being under-queried if you decode it greedily. This is the same shape as ffn-activation-sparsity-is-a-training-free-decode-lever and remapping-positions-beats-rescaling-frequencies-for-context-extension — capacity idle inside a shipped artifact, recoverable by changing an inference policy rather than by training anything. It is also the cheapest entry on speculative-decoding: every other recent arrival trains a draft expert, trains a dependent block head, or relaxes the verification rule.

The cost accounting is the part a local reader should keep. Draft-side latency goes 1.09 ms → 5.24 ms per round while the backbone stays at 3.55 ms, so the drafter stops being a rounding error and becomes the larger half of the step. Under speculation-length-has-a-closed-form-inversion-point, that is γ = C_d/C_v rising sharply, and the closed form says a higher γ shortens the horizon over which speculating pays. PCTree wins because acceptance rises faster than γ does — on an H20 running an unquantized 4B. Quantize the verifier, shrink the target, or move to a card where verification is cheap, and the same arithmetic can flip. The paper says as much in its own last limitation.

Bounded: no code link, one datacenter card at batch 1, one model family and one drafter architecture, several cells single-run, and the comparison is against DSpark rather than against the EAGLE-family trees that dominate the literature.
