Native small models are not automatically the more sustainable edge choice -- an optimized quantized large model can beat every small model on a joint capability/energy/safety score, but format-vs-model-family interactions mean no single quantization scheme wins uniformly

Edge deployment choices between "small model, full precision" and "large model, quantized" are usually argued on one metric at a time -- accuracy, or latency, or memory. The author scores 30 configurations (5 BF16 small language models; 5 LLM families each run under BF16/INT8/NF4-4bit/GPTQ-4bit/GGUF-Q4) on A100-80GB hardware using a Holistic Sustainability Score: equal-weighted economic (capability + latency/throughput/VRAM efficiency), environmental (measured GPU energy per token) and social (five-prompt harmful-prompt attack-success-rate proxy, reverse-scored) pillars, min-max normalized within a stated comparison pool.

In the SLM-only pool, Llama-3.2-3B/BF16 ranks first (HSS 92.42) despite lower raw capability than Phi-4-mini (0.556 vs. 0.591), on the strength of its latency/throughput/energy balance. In the full 30-case pool, Qwen3-30B-A3B/GGUF Q4 ranks first overall (93.38) and Mistral-Small-24B/GGUF Q4 second (92.40); the best-ranked small model (Phi-4-mini/BF16) places third (89.49) -- so the hypothesis that native SLMs are necessarily the more sustainable edge choice is not supported in this pool, though small models stay competitive specifically because lower VRAM/energy partly offsets a capability gap, not because they win outright.

Averaged across the five LLM families, GGUF Q4 (executed via llama.cpp rather than the Hugging-Face/Transformers path the other formats use) nearly preserves mean capability (0.524 vs. 0.525 BF16) while quadrupling mean throughput (22.74 -> 95.50 tok/s), cutting mean peak VRAM by three-quarters (56.35 -> 13.64 GB) and mean energy per token by more than half (12.10 -> 5.28 J/token). But the paper is explicit this is a joint backend+format effect, not "four bits are simply better": within a fixed quantization scheme the best model varies (gpt-oss-20B wins BF16/INT8/NF4, Mistral wins GPTQ, Qwen3 wins GGUF), Mistral's INT8 case is slower and more energy-intensive than its own BF16 case, and Gemma-3-27B under GPTQ is the single worst configuration observed in the entire study -- 958.71s latency, 0.33 tok/s, 85.01 GB peak VRAM, 493.02 J/token, an order of magnitude worse than its own BF16 case, attributed to kernel support and conversion-quality issues rather than bit-width.

Graded extends on local-inference: this is a direct empirical answer, on this atlas's own standing question of what runs well on one or two consumer cards, to whether native small models or quantized large models make the better edge deployment -- scored jointly rather than on accuracy or speed alone. Cross-linked to post-training-quantization because it grades five formats head-to-head on the same models; its single worst-case result (a format collapsing to under 1 tok/s on one model while working normally on others) is a second independent instance of this map's "a format clearing one check can fail badly on another" pattern, first seen in the naive-FP8-KV-cache null result. direction: mixed because the finding cuts both ways -- quantized LLMs can win, but the specific winner depends on model family and format in a way the paper shows is not reducible to bit-width alone.

Caveats: single author, no stated peer review, no code repository found (workbooks/notebooks referenced but not linked). All measurement is on A100-80GB datacenter GPUs, not literal consumer/prosumer cards -- the paper's "edge AI" framing outruns the hardware actually used. The social pillar is a five-prompt lexical refusal proxy the author explicitly calls unsuitable as a complete safety assessment and "especially misleading" for a non-aligned base model -- graded suggestive in part because of this weak third pillar. Min-max normalization is sensitive to the Gemma-3-27B/GPTQ outlier, which compresses apparent differences among the other configurations in the same pool; HSS values are stated to be valid only within their named comparison pool. Equal pillar weighting is a stated value judgment, not a derived optimum.
