The map's named experiment, run by people who wanted YaRN to look strong

This map went contested on 2026-07-28 and then named its successor experiment with unusual precision: the same suite on a YaRN-class checkpoint. RULER (claimed-context-length-is-not-effective-context-length) contained no YaRN model — the cheaply extended checkpoints that read effective <4K used Position Interpolation and LongLoRA (cheap-context-extension-does-not-survive-a-hard-long-context-test) — so the map's own claim, context-length-is-a-cheap-post-hoc-edit-not-a-pretraining-property, had never faced the instrument that discounted its neighbours.

Here is the column, from a paper whose purpose was to beat YaRN rather than to audit it:

RULER average1.7B4B8B
Base (32K native, unextended)60.9369.9473.13
YaRN52.9970.1178.49
Relative to base−7.94+0.17+5.36

At 1.7B, rescaling the frequencies is nearly eight points worse than leaving the model alone. At 4B it is noise. At 8B it works.

What makes this stronger than RULER's own evidence is the design. RULER's finding that effective length tracks model size and RoPE base frequency came from a regression across 17 heterogeneous models, where a 34B and a 7B differ in far more than size. This is a controlled within-family ablation — one method, one model family, one data blend, three sizes — and the effect is monotone and large. And the authors had every incentive to make their baseline look good.

Cheap context extension is size-gated, and the gate is between 4B and 8B. That is the sharpest statement this map has been able to make, and it lands badly for this vault's premise: the models a person runs on one or two consumer cards sit at or below the gate.

Two things keep this from closing the map. First, the YaRN here is zero-shot — applied at inference, no fine-tuning — where the original YaRN did ~600 steps on <0.1% of the pretraining data. That is a legitimate deployment mode (the paper argues zero-shot extension is now dominant for open-weight releases, since one checkpoint must serve arbitrary lengths) but it is not the configuration the map's central claim describes. The honest reading is narrower than the headline: the free version of YaRN does not work on small models, and whether 600 fine-tuning steps rescues a 1.7B model remains unmeasured. So the map holds contested rather than fading, and its most-wanted source is now smaller and sharper: run YaRN's own fine-tuning recipe on Qwen3-1.7B and score it on RULER. Everything needed is public.

Second, this is not "small models can't be extended." Read the table by column: every other method beats the base model at all three sizes, and three of four beat it at 1.7B. YaRN is the only one that does not — see remapping-positions-beats-rescaling-frequencies-for-context-extension. The failure is specific to the frequency-rescaling family, which is the family every extension source this map held belonged to, and it reinforces the map's existing suspicion about RoPE base frequency as the operative variable: rescaling manipulates that frequency directly and breaks; remapping leaves it alone and does not.
