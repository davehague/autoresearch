A pruned multimodal KV cache does not have to mean permanently discarded context -- deferring the pruned tokens' prefill into spare decode-time compute, rather than dropping them, recovers most of the quality a hard-pruned cache lost, though the benefit shrinks sharply once deferred content is large and sequential (video) rather than a few images

TL;DR: This map's budget-spending strand is organized around what to keep, in what form, and at what retention ratio when a cache must be compressed. This source reframes "pruned" as a temporary rather than permanent state: instead of evicting unselected multimodal tokens outright, keep them and prefill them later, during the model's own decode steps and in post-response scheduling gaps, using spare compute that would otherwise sit idle. Layered on four existing pruning methods at 30% initial retention, this recovers 77.7%/60.4% of the quality hard pruning lost on two image-dialogue benchmarks, while still capturing most of hard pruning's first-token-latency win (up to 24.1%/17.9% TTFT reduction vs. full prefill) at a small per-token-latency cost. This is a cache-population-timing mechanism, not a new compression format or eviction policy — it composes with, rather than competes against, whatever pruning/retention method decides what gets deferred in the first place.

What it says. The mechanism has two parts that matter for how a cache gets written, which is this map's territory: attention-visibility control lets a full-attention model (Qwen3-VL-8B) insert newly-prefilled deferred tokens into the KV cache after already-processed suffix tokens, without violating the causal structure the model was trained under; an affine state-mapping correction lets a hybrid-recurrent model (Qwen3.5-9B's Gated DeltaNet layers, which have no explicit per-token KV cache to simply append to) approximate the effect of having prefilled those tokens all along, by correcting the running recurrent state with a recorded transition matrix — explicitly an approximation of a full rerun, not an exact equivalent. Prefill-decode fusion (batching deferred-token prefill into the same forward pass as the ongoing decode step) is the throughput trick that keeps this nearly free in the image-dialogue regime (MultiVerse: +0.13-0.47% TPOT). The video result is the more important number for this map's own skepticism about headline speedups: once the deferred content is large and sequential rather than a handful of images, the "spare compute" budget this method relies on is no longer enough to absorb the deferral cheaply (TPOT overhead rises to +14.9-19.9%, and 42-69% of dialogues still need post-response completion, meaning the "free" recovery is not actually free, or not actually recovered within the response window, for a meaningful fraction of cases) — a scope boundary this map should track whenever a technique's headline number comes from image-dialogue rather than video/long-sequence benchmarks.

Caveats. All latency numbers are measured on one NVIDIA H20 GPU per worker via SGLang — a datacenter accelerator; no consumer-GPU or edge measurement is reported, which matters for this vault's own standing "what runs on one or two consumer cards" question. Concurrency (multiple simultaneous requests competing for the same "spare" decode-time compute this method spends) is explicitly left untested by the authors, and the hybrid-model state-mapping approximation's error over a long multi-turn session has no reported bound. On MultiVerse, the paired confidence interval for Raincheck's TTFT win specifically over hard pruning (rather than over full prefill) includes zero — the clean win is against doing full prefill every turn, not necessarily against a well-tuned pruning baseline alone. Quality can regress on individual turns, and deferred prefill does not reproduce full prefill's exact generation trajectory. Code release is not confirmed from the text read.
