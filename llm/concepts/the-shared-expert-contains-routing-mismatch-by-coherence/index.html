The shared expert is also a routing stabilizer

fine-grained-plus-shared-experts-reduce-expert-redundancy records why the always-on shared expert was introduced: put what every token needs in one place so the routed experts are free to specialize. quantize-the-routed-experts-and-leave-the-shared-path-alone and shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts give it a second property, that it is the precision-sensitive part. This adds a third, and it is the first dynamic one on mixture-of-experts.

The intervention is graded rather than a knockout, which is what makes it readable. Multiply the shared output by α ∈ {1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0} at each block and propagate the full forward pass, leaving the router to respond only to the resulting drift. Removing backbone mass raises the routing mismatch's exposure to the residual stream monotonically — 0.024 → 0.040 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, 0.040 → 0.077 on Gravity.

The mechanism is separated from the obvious alternative by two controls, and this is the part worth trusting. A frozen-numerator null — change only the denominator, i.e. test whether shrinking the residual is doing the work — is nearly flat. What actually moves is the numerator: the routing term's own norm rises to 1.57× and 1.69× baseline as routing coherence between the two passes falls. Then a common-mode ladder confirms it is mass and not content: at matched input drift, replacing the shared content while preserving its mass decoheres routing less than a matched-norm off-manifold perturbation, which decoheres less than removing the mass — three-way ordering preserved in 96% and 98% of resamples.

So the always-on path contains mismatch by keeping the two passes routing the same way, not by drowning it out. For anyone running same-weight self-distillation, teacher-forced replay, prefix-cache experiments or any protocol where the same weights see two different conditionings, presence or absence of a shared expert is a design variable with a measured effect on how much of that conditioning difference reaches the output. That is a reason to prefer shared-expert architectures for these protocols that has nothing to do with parameter efficiency.

Two confirmatory checkpoints only, and the α = 0 knockout is out of distribution — the authors rest the mechanistic claim on the graded trajectory and the common-mode ladder rather than on full removal, and note that the content-preserving arm is near the no-op on one checkpoint and reacts on the other, so complete content-irrelevance is not claimed.
