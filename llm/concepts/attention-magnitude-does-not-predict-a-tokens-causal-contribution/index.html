The signal nearly every eviction method scores tokens by is uncorrelated with what it's supposed to predict

This map already carries two entries narrowing what raw attention score is worth as an importance signal: rank-cached-tokens-by-their-effect-on-the-attention-output found attention-output-utility beats raw attention score in a controlled ablation (Llama-3.1-8B, RULER-32K), and keeping-the-largest-attention-weights-is-already-near-optimal found that top-k-by-attention-weight is already within 2-5% of an oracle best subset for reproducing full attention — a ceiling argument, not a defense of raw score as causally meaningful. TwinKV adds a different, stronger kind of pushback: it measures attention magnitude against a token's actual causal contribution to the answer — not to attention-output fidelity, a different ground truth — and finds essentially no relationship at all (Spearman ρ = −0.004).

Why this is not simply the same finding restated. ContourKV's near-optimality result is about how close top-k-by-attention gets to the best achievable subset under the same objective attention weight is already proxying for (reproducing full-attention behavior). TwinKV's correlation is against a different and arguably more decision-relevant ground truth — whether the token actually mattered for the answer that got produced. A signal can be near-optimal for one objective (matching full attention) while being uncorrelated with another (causal contribution to the output), and that gap is exactly the tension worth carrying: this map's ranking methods have mostly been validated against the first objective, and this result says the second, arguably more important one, is not what attention magnitude tracks at all.

What TwinKV does about it is the extending half of this source — see pairwise-key-redundancy-is-a-training-free-repair-that-composes-with-existing-eviction-policies — a training-free, attention-free redundancy signal that composes with whatever eviction policy is already running rather than replacing its scoring rule.
