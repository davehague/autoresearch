Near-optimal at one objective, uninformative at another — and the eviction family only checked the first one

keeping-the-largest-attention-weights-is-already-near-optimal measured attention-magnitude ranking against an oracle best subset for reproducing full attention and found it close to optimal (median 2.1–4.7% gap). This paper measures the same ranking against a different ground truth — a token's actual causal contribution to the answer, established by ablation — and finds essentially no relationship: Spearman ρ = −0.004.

The two results do not contradict each other; they score different objectives. A signal can be near-optimal for reconstructing a distribution while carrying almost no information about which inputs that distribution's output actually depended on. But the practical consequence is real: every eviction policy on kv-cache-management that ranks-then-keeps by attention magnitude has been validated against "does this reproduce full attention," not "does this keep the tokens the answer needed," and this is the first measurement against the second standard. It does not move the map's Status — the near-optimality claim stands, scoped to its own objective — but it removes the implicit license to read attention-weight ranking as a proxy for causal importance, which is the assumption most of this map's eviction family quietly rests on.

Rebutted by: none — this note is itself the pushback. See keeping-the-largest-attention-weights-is-already-near-optimal for the back-edge.
