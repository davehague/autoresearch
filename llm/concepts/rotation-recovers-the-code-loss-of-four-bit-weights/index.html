The cheapest thing you can add to a 4-bit recipe

rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free is QuaRot's claim: because a transformer is computationally invariant to an orthogonal transform inserted and undone around its residual stream, a Hadamard rotation can spread the outlier channels that make low-bit quantization hard, at no cost to the function computed. It has been a single-source claim in this vault, argued by the group that proposed it, in a paper about pushing to W4A4.

This is a third-party reproduction of the mechanism in the most boring and therefore most convincing way possible: a paper about something else entirely (the interaction of speculative decoding with quantization) bolts rotation onto its baseline GPTQ recipe, reports both rows in the same table, and moves on. Same model, same group size, same calibration, one extra preprocessing step — HumanEval 53.05 → 57.93, recovering 4.88 of the 8.54 points that 4-bit weights cost against FP16.

Two readings, and the second is the more useful one.

Rotation is becoming a general-purpose preprocessing primitive for quantization, not a technique inside one method. This vault has now seen the same operation do the same job in three unrelated places: on weights and activations (QuaRot), bolted onto GPTQ here, and — in the other ingest of the same run — as the load-bearing first step of a KV-cache quantizer, where randomly rotating the vector concentrates its coordinates onto a Beta distribution and makes the optimal vector quantizer collapse into per-coordinate scalar quantizers (a-data-free-rotation-puts-the-kv-cache-at-three-and-a-half-bits). The unifying statement is that rotation removes the structure that makes a quantizer's job hard — outliers, channel concentration, coordinate dependence — and it is close to free because orthogonal transforms preserve the geometry everything downstream depends on. That is a synthesis across four sources and no single one of them says it.

Perplexity ranked these two configurations almost identically and the capabilities differ by five points. perplexity-does-not-rank-quantization-formats was established on the llama.cpp K-quant ladder — 0.03 perplexity spanning 3.4 GSM8K points. The spread here is wider and in a different codebase: across one 8B quantization ladder, 0.56 perplexity against 10.98 HumanEval points, with the rotated variant better on perplexity by a margin far too small to predict its 4.88-point code advantage. Code generation is again where the damage concentrates — W8A8 loses 3.05 HumanEval points while gaining 0.38 on GSM8K, which is also a good reminder of how much of a small benchmark delta is noise. The rule for the vault: if a quantization result reports only perplexity, it has reported nothing about the thing you are quantizing for.
