Perplexity and accuracy disagree about where to repair

post-training-quantization has assembled a case that a compressed model's aggregate scores go quiet while something breaks — quantization-multiplies-the-decision-margin on decision margins, quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation on reasoning traces, four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer on confidence. Every one of those is a benchmark hiding damage. This is the mirror case, and it is arguably more actionable: an optimization target pointing the wrong way.

The setup is an accident of a good ablation. Quantization-error hotspots turn out to sit in different places by model family — layers 18–30 of 36 on Qwen, layers 7–14 of 28 on Llama — so the author tested whether concentrating the repair on the hotspot half beats spreading it uniformly. On Llama it does, decisively, on perplexity: bottom-half placement beats uniform by 10 points and top-half by 43 points of perplexity-gap recovery, using half the parameters. Then the same configuration recovers 9.5% of the accuracy gap against uniform's 17% — the reverse ordering, at the placement perplexity most prefers.

The proposed mechanism is worth carrying because it predicts when this will happen. A late-layer error distorts the final next-token logits, which is what perplexity reads directly and what a local correction can cancel. An early-layer error distorts intermediate representations that feed every downstream attention and MLP call, so repairing it locally restores the token-level likelihood without restoring the computation that likelihood was supposed to summarize. On the family whose errors are early, perplexity becomes a measure of how well you patched the symptom.

This turns post-training-quantization's standing open question — is weight-space reconstruction error the wrong objective for everything? — from a suspicion into an observed failure. That question was raised by three literatures finding the loss should be measured where the consumer reads it: the output head, cross-model cache transfer, feed-forward sparsity (an-int4-proxy-ranks-channels-it-cannot-compute). All three were arguments that a better metric exists. This is a case where the conventional metric selected a worse configuration, on a downstream benchmark, in one paper's own tables.

Held at suggestive: the inversion appears on one model family — Qwen's late-error profile shows no such disagreement — with one benchmark on the accuracy side, at one bit-width, from a single author. The confirming run is cheap and would matter to every method in this map: take one repair or one quantizer, fit it under a perplexity objective and under a downstream-accuracy objective, and report both metrics for both fits. If the inversion holds on a second family, every perplexity-tuned allocation in this vault needs re-reading.
