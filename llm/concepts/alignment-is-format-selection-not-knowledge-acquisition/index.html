If alignment is format selection, the cheap levers are the right shape

The Superficial Alignment Hypothesis is a claim about where things live: capabilities and knowledge come from pretraining, and alignment teaches only which subdistribution of formats to use when talking to a user. LIMA is built as a test of its corollary — that a small, carefully chosen set of examples should suffice — and the corollary survives. 1,000 examples, ~750,000 tokens, plain cross-entropy, no reward model and no sampling loop, and the result is equal-or-preferred to GPT-4 in 43% of head-to-heads and to an RLHF-trained DaVinci003 in 65%.

The reason this belongs in this vault rather than only in an alignment reading list is that it supplies the missing why for peft-methods's live contest. low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new found that LoRA matches full fine-tuning on task adaptation and underperforms on genuine domain acquisition, and the map's revised default reads "4-bit LoRA is the right opening move and an explicit bet that your target is a task, not a body of knowledge." The Superficial Alignment Hypothesis is that bet restated as a claim about the pretraining/adaptation division of labour. If alignment is selecting among behaviours the model already has, then a low-rank update is exactly the right shape of intervention for it; and if your target genuinely needs new knowledge, neither adapter rank nor curated instruction data is the lever, because pretraining is.

Two findings arrive from opposite directions — one about parameters, one about data — and agree. Neither cites the other. That is the strongest account the vault has of when the cheap levers work, and it makes a testable prediction: the tasks where LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning should be the same tasks where curated instruction data underperforms continued pretraining. Nobody has checked.

The multi-turn result is the sharpest single demonstration of the hypothesis. A model trained on zero dialogue examples can hold a conversation at all — which is only explicable if the ability was already present — and 30 hand-written chains take it from 45.2% to 76.1% excellent. The appendix reports that removing 6 examples of a task can break structured generation entirely. These are the numbers of a capability being invoked, not installed.

Bound: 2023-vintage baselines (DaVinci003, Bard, Claude-v1, GPT-4-April), all retired, so the win rates are historical. LIMA still loses outright to Claude (54%) and GPT-4 (57%), so the honest reading bounds the marginal value of preference optimization rather than eliminating it — see preference-tuning-reduces-to-classification and the alignment-loss family in post-training-rl. 230 of 300 test prompts were written by the same authors who wrote 200 training examples, and the paper concedes "significant contact between the groups" produced shared priors. Twelve percent of outputs still fail outright.

Rebutted by: distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank — supplies the distillation-side counterpart from an unrelated group: a teacher that is only aligned is distributionally close to its student and transfers little, which is why 1,000 curated examples beat 52× more data from text-davinci-003. Compatible with this claim rather than against it, and it converts the observation into a mechanism.
