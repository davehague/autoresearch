Most of the missing table, minus the column that decides it

vlm-on-device's first open question, carried since the fork, asks for one thing: prefill and decode throughput, peak VRAM, and a real benchmark for a 3B–8B VLM at 4 bits on a 12–24 GB card, with and without vision-embedding caching. Every source on that map prices a component instead — a CLIP encoder's precision budget, an MLX engine's embedding cache, a datacenter serving stack's prefix reuse, one generative model's calibration at 4 bits on a 100-item task.

This is the first artifact to answer most of it. A 3.1B generative VLM, in about 3 GB, decoding at 228 tok/s on an M5 Max, 116 on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395 and — the number that matters most, because nothing in this vault had one — 20 tok/s on a phone. Twenty tokens per second is above reading speed, which makes this the first entry here that says a vision-language model on a handset is a usable thing rather than a demonstration. Day-one GGUF, ONNX and MLX-8bit checkpoints answer a second standing question directly: vlm-on-device has asked since the fork what llama.cpp actually does with a vision tower, and the answer is now "there is a GGUF build, shipped by the vendor, on release day."

The column that is missing is the one that decides how to read the rest. The post never states the precision of any throughput or footprint number. 3.1B parameters is roughly 6.2 GB at BF16 and 3.1 GB at 8 bits, so "about 3 GB" implies an 8-bit checkpoint — but that is arithmetic this note is doing, not something the vendor says, and the gap between BF16, MLX-8bit and a GGUF K-quant is larger than the gap between two of the devices in the table. There is also no quality-versus-precision comparison anywhere: the quantized checkpoints ship with benchmark numbers reported only for the BF16 model. So the artifact demonstrates that an edge VLM runs, and supplies nothing about what the quantization it runs at costs — which is the other half of this map's founding question ("what does compression take from it?").

That gap is exactly where this map's live tension sits. Its third open question asks whether small models quantize worse is a law or a coincidence, with three directional supports and no controlled test; a 3B VLM shipping in four precisions with no measured quality delta is a fourth data point that could have been the experiment and is not. And four-bit-costs-the-confidence-signal-not-the-answer is the specific reason to distrust a vendor's silence here: a 2B VLM lost 1.6 accuracy points and 15 AUROC points at 4-bit, so an accuracy-only report — which is all this is — would have called that free too.

Two supporting details worth keeping. The build path confirms rather than challenges open-vlms-are-mostly-distilled-closed-vlms: post-training stage one is explicitly "supervised fine-tuning with knowledge distillation from larger teacher models," followed by multi-reward RL. And the throughput claim has a serving half — ~11K output tok/s at high concurrency, roughly 2× the 4B class — which is the vendor's own framing and is a datacenter number, not a local one.

Graded T2 / single-study: a vendor engineering post plus its own model card, every benchmark self-reported with vendor-chosen baselines, no independent replication, and a custom lfm1.0 licence rather than a standard open one. What it establishes is existence and order of magnitude, which this map did not have; what it does not establish is any number in the accuracy-per-bit currency the map is organized around.
