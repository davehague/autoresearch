A frozen weight spectrum can replace a calibration set

post-training-quantization's most persistent nuisance finding is that the calibration corpus is often the largest single term in the result. calibration-content-is-the-dominant-term-below-four-bits puts it at +2.2 points at W4A16 and +58 at ternary; quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model shows the domain leaking into the compressed model; calibration-corpus-choice-swings-a-quantized-models-math-score-again is the replication. Every method that needs to know "what is this weight matrix for" pays that tax, and no amount of careful calibration-set construction removes it — it moves it.

Three methods in this vault now decline to pay it, each by reading a spectral statistic of the frozen weights where the obvious design would have read data:

  • Head function — the entropy-based effective rank of W_K^⊤ W_Q separates retrieval heads from streaming heads (head-function-is-readable-from-the-frozen-query-key-spectrum).
  • Bit budget — the power-law tail exponent of each layer's weight spectrum ranks layers for mixed-precision allocation with no calibration data at all (expert-bit-allocation-should-be-global-not-per-layer).
  • Outliers — a data-free rotation removes the activation outliers that block low-bit quantization (rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free).

Different labs, different literatures, no cross-citation, one move. The design principle worth naming: a spectrum cannot leak a domain. Where a method's decision is a classification of a weight matrix's role rather than an estimate of its input distribution, the trained parameters may already encode the answer, and reading it there is both cheaper and immune to the corpus problem — which data-curation would predict is where a filter's measured advantage comes from in the first place.

Graded suggestive rather than strong, and the reason is a real limit. Each of the three is individually well-evidenced, but nobody has tested the general claim — that spectral statistics substitute for calibration across allocation problems — and there are obvious places it should fail. A spectrum says what a matrix is shaped to do, not what your workload will ask it to do; activation-outlier magnitude, which activation-outliers-are-the-barrier-to-w8a8 shows is the binding constraint at W8A8, is a property of inputs and has no weight-space proxy here. The honest form is a heuristic with a scope condition: try the spectrum first when the decision is about role; keep calibration when the decision is about range.
