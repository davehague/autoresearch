Task knowledge has a third place to live

Everything on peft-methods has assumed a binary. Put the knowledge in the prompt — modular, editable, shareable, and re-interpreted from scratch on every single query at a cost in context. Or put it in the weights — free at inference once merged (adapters-can-merge-away-inference-latency), and welded to the checkpoint in a way that makes it hard to load, remove or hand to someone else. Two decades of adapter work is a search inside the second option.

KV-Skill proposes a third. At each injection layer ℓ an operator M_s = W_s U_s^T is read from the residual stream — Read(M, q) = (1/√m_s)·W_s(U_s^T q), with q a normalized function of the hidden state — and the response is added back through a shared, per-backbone interface with per-layer gates and gains. The backbone is frozen. There is no softmax across slots, the slots are not entries in the attention KV cache despite the name, and they do not grow with the context. Nothing is added to the prompt.

What makes it a substrate rather than another adapter variant is that the three properties come apart. Storage is per-task and tiny (67 KiB compact, 20 KiB at rank 1, against LoRA's 10 MiB at r=8). Loading is a pointer swap — 0 ms for a cached operator. And skills compose: three registered sequentially into one interface end at 80.9 / 78.2 / 40.6 against single-skill baselines of 78.3 / 78.2 / 40.3, with mixed replay carrying the composition and the no-replay arm losing 2.4 points. That is the property intruder-dimensions-are-the-mechanism-of-lora-forgetting said the adapter-per-task workflow does not have.

The cost side is honest and incomplete. Every backbone needs its own interface — 13.3M parameters on a 4B model — trained once before any skill can be loaded, and the paper never states what training it costs. Reading the operator costs 6.5% of decode on an H100. The text-derived form is built in one model's residual space and cross-backbone transfer is not evaluated.

The head-to-head against LoRA is the number to hold, and it splits by task rather than uniformly. LoRA wins LiveMath at 4B (80.7 vs 79.0) and loses SearchQA by ten points, then loses both at 35B. The suggestive reading — untested by anyone — is that the operator is the better substrate where the knowledge is a procedure for reading and the adapter is better where it is closer to a learned function. That maps onto this map's contested axis without settling it: no arm here runs full fine-tuning, none runs a quantized base, and none sweeps the learning rate, which is the confound adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact showed can manufacture exactly this size of gap.
