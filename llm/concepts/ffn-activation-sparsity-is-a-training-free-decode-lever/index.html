The first lever in this map measured in composition, and it composes

local-inference's central deflationary finding is that its levers do not stack, because most of them spend the same thing. Speculative decoding and weight quantization both convert idle arithmetic into saved memory traffic, a decode step has a finite amount of idle arithmetic to sell, and quantization spends it first — which is why applying EAGLE-2 to a 4-bit model returns 1.3× where the fp16 model gets 2.2×, and why the one cell in that whole grid where integration loses is a 4-bit 8B model on an RTX 3090 (speculation-and-quantization-compete-for-the-same-headroom).

FFN activation sparsity spends a different currency. It buys bandwidth with memory (about 12% extra weight storage for the INT4 proxies) and with quality. It does not need spare arithmetic; it needs spare capacity to hold a second, smaller copy of the weights. That is why it can sit on top of a W4A16 model and on top of KV-cache compression simultaneously and still return 2.62× at 32K context, which is the first time anything in this map has been benchmarked in combination with two other techniques rather than alone.

The mechanism is worth stating plainly because it is the reason the sparsity is free of training. Feed-forward networks dominate a decode step's weight traffic. If you knew which SwiGLU intermediate channels mattered for this token you could skip reading the rest of the matrix — but the signal that tells you, the intermediate state itself, is the dense computation you were trying to avoid. Prox resolves the circularity by observing that sparse execution needs only the mask: a cheap approximation built from INT4 proxy weights on a magnitude-sparsified input gives the right channel ranking, and the selected channels are then computed exactly at full precision. The ordering is approximated; the arithmetic is not.

The quality bill is real and rank-ordered by sparsity level. At 50% the cost is under 1.5 points of aggregate benchmark on the 8B and 14B Qwen3 models — cheap. At 70%, where the 1.82× throughput headline sits, Qwen3-8B loses 7.5 aggregate points and Llama-3-8B loses 6.7, which is a different proposition entirely and is not mentioned alongside the speedup. The honest operating point for someone who cares about answer quality is 50–60%, where the speedup is smaller than advertised.

Scope: single-batch autoregressive decoding only — the authors name large-batch serving as the primary limitation, which is the regime this map cares least about but does bound the claim. Every throughput number is an A6000, A100 or RTX 3090; there is no Apple silicon, no CPU, and nothing in llama.cpp, so like AlphaQ and TurboQuant before it this is a technique the local stack cannot currently express. Thresholds are calibrated on Alpaca instruction text with domain-shift stability stated as not extensively explored, which is the fourth appearance in this vault of a calibration corpus quietly entering a quantization-adjacent pipeline (quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model).
