Nesting the drafter inside the verifier removes drafter alignment

Drafter provenance is in speculative-decoding's title and is its least-evidenced dimension. The drafters this map has recorded are separately trained, self-speculative, tree-shaped, diffusion-based or hardware-co-designed, and each carries the same latent problem: acceptance rate is a property of how well the drafter matches this particular target, which is the whole reason train-the-drafter-on-accepted-length-not-likelihood is a finding rather than a triviality.

A nested drafter has no alignment problem by construction. It is a subset of the verifier's own weights, trained jointly with it, on the same data, in the same run. There is no separate checkpoint to obtain, no separate memory to allocate, and no distributional gap to close. That is the ceiling of this axis — you cannot align a drafter to a target more tightly than by making it part of one.

The number is what makes this interesting: 14–26%. Against 2–3× for ordinary lossless speculation on a bandwidth-bound decode, a structurally perfect drafter buys a modest increment. The reading this map should take is not that nesting is disappointing but that it localizes the bottleneck: if removing drafter-target mismatch entirely is worth a quarter at most, mismatch was not what was limiting the setups here, and the constraint lives on the verification side. That is consistent with where this map's recent evidence has actually moved — verification-cost-is-a-property-of-the-backbones-memory-structure (verification is not one forward pass once the backbone stops being an append-only cache), offloaded-moe-verification-is-not-one-forward-pass (nor once the weights are offloaded), and the-verification-budget-has-an-interior-optimum (the budget has an interior optimum nobody's default sits at). Three of the map's four newest findings are about checking, not drafting, and this one supplies the complementary bound from the drafting side.

There is a second, cheaper implication. A nested drafter costs no extra resident memory, because its weights are already in the verifier. Every other drafter in this map is a second model a local reader must fit alongside the first, which on a 12 GB card is often the reason speculation is not attempted at all. The throughput gain is modest; the memory footprint is the part that would matter on consumer hardware, and it is not measured anywhere here.

Graded single-study: one nested suite topping out at 3B, no replication, and the throughput figure carries neither the sampling temperature nor the attention backend it was measured at. This map established both as load-bearing within the last two weeks — a-drafters-acceptance-rate-depends-on-the-temperature-you-decode-at for the first, the-attention-backend-decides-whether-cache-compression-pays for the general form of the second — making this the third consecutive unreported-configuration gap on the axis. Take 14–26% as an order of magnitude for the effect, not a figure to plan against.
