On-policy distillation may not be distilling anything from the teacher at all

This map already carries distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank — on-policy distillation transfers distributional novelty rather than teacher quality, since a stronger same-family teacher produces a student indistinguishable from a weaker one. This paper extends that claim from a coarser to a finer instrument: not just teacher rank, but teacher signal accuracy fails to move the outcome. Comparing teacher token-level advantages against ground-truth rollouts, it finds high error rates throughout generation — and, sharper still, scaling the teacher makes the noise worse, not better. The diagnostic that clinches it: student performance under OPD is essentially unchanged whether the teacher signal is accurate or deliberately scrambled. If supervision correctness doesn't move the outcome, the outcome isn't coming from the supervision in the way the framing assumes. The paper's decomposition attributes the real gain to suppressing low-probability tokens — an entropy-control regularization effect distinct from transfer — and a teacher-free version of that mechanism (OPSA) beats OPD outright.

Graded extends rather than pushes-back: it sharpens an already-recorded map claim (teacher quality doesn't predict the gain) into a mechanism (why: it isn't transfer, it's entropy suppression), rather than contradicting a settled claim this map held. It is a mechanism-level challenge to what "distillation" means in the RLVR-adjacent, on-policy setting specifically, distinct from this map's pretraining-corpus distillation results (teacher rank, novelty, contamination).

Bound: two authors, no venue beyond arXiv, no code/weights indicated, AIME24 is a narrow math-competition benchmark, and "gains are entropy suppression, not transfer" is the paper's own reading of a correlational decomposition rather than a causal ablation that isolates supervision quality from the entropy mechanism.
