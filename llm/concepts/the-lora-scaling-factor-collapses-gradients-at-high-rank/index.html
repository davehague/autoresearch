A confound under the contest

peft-methods has been contested since 2026-07-18 because two independent T1s found LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning on genuine domain acquisition, with low-rank-capacity-binds-when-the-domain-is-new reporting that full fine-tuning learns perturbations of rank 10–100× higher than adapters use. The obvious response — raise the rank — has a standing answer from LoRA's own ablation, where rank 64 was no better than rank 4.

This paper argues that answer was measured through a broken instrument. A LoRA submodule computes (W + gamma_r * B A) x, and practiced LoRA sets gamma_r = alpha/r. Theorem 3.2 shows that the adapter preserves both forward moments and backward gradients at Theta_r(1) — the paper's definition of rank-stabilized — if and only if gamma_r is in Theta_r(1/sqrt(r)). Dividing by r is therefore overly aggressive: it suppresses the adapter harder the more capacity you give it, which slows learning rather than diverging it, so the symptom is exactly "extra rank does nothing."

The empirics match the theorem on both sides. LoRA's gradient norms fall through two orders of magnitude across a rank-4-to-2048 sweep while rsLoRA's stay in one band; LoRA's perplexity curves overlap into a single line while rsLoRA's separate monotonically. It replicates on a second model, optimizer and dataset (GPT-J 6B, GSM8k, Adafactor) and under plain SGD, ruling out AdamW as the stabilizer. And it is not a disguised learning-rate change: a two-decade lr sweep for rank-4 LoRA cannot beat rsLoRA at rank 2048 with the default lr. Exponents on either side of 1/sqrt(r) fail as predicted — 1/r^(1/4) is unstable, 1/r^2 collapses harder.

This does not resolve the contest; it confounds it. Every LoRA-versus-full-fine-tuning comparison in this vault was run under alpha/r, so the vault cannot currently say whether the rank ceiling it has been reasoning about is a property of low-rank adaptation or of a denominator. Naming the confound sharpens the map's most-wanted experiment rather than answering it.

It also closes a door on the adapter-initialization thread. adapter-initialization-determines-the-lora-ceiling and quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor both locate the lever in where the adapter starts; Appendix B.3 shows that rank-correcting the initialization instead of the scaling factor does not substitute — it is unstable at rank 2048, and combined with 1/r it reproduces LoRA's suboptimal behaviour exactly. Parameterization and initialization are different levers with different failure modes.

Graded suggestive, deliberately, and the split is the point. The gradient-collapse mechanism is a training-health finding backed by a theorem, which is what gradient norms and training perplexity are valid evidence for. The "higher rank gives a better model" claim is a model-selection claim resting on fine-tuning perplexity — this vault's most-repeated bad proxy, failed three times already (validation-perplexity-does-not-track-generation-quality) — with no downstream task benchmark anywhere in the paper. Single author, industry lab, no venue, ~600 steps per run, nothing over a quantized base, and whether the fix changes anything at the ranks people actually use (8–64, far from the asymptotic regime the theorem addresses) is not separately reported.
