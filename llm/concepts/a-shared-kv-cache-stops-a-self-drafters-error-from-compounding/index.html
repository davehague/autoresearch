A state-management bug that reads as a drafter-quality problem

This vault has treated draft-target alignment as something you buy. train-the-drafter-on-accepted-length-not-likelihood buys it with a training objective. a-downloaded-drafter-is-a-bet-on-its-training-distribution warns what happens when you inherit it instead. nesting-the-drafter-inside-the-verifier-removes-drafter-alignment removes the problem architecturally by making the drafter part of the verifier. a-distilled-draft-expert-buys-acceptance-without-buying-transfers buys it with a distillation pass.

This source points at a slice of the same symptom that costs nothing at all. In self-speculative decoding the draft is derived from the target — by sparsifying experts, skipping layers, or quantizing — so its parameters are already aligned by construction. The remaining divergence is not parametric. It is that the draft, running as a separate execution, was keeping its own KV cache, so every small approximation error entered the conditioning context and shaped the next prediction. Next-token prediction is P(y_t | x, y_<t); corrupt y_<t even slightly and the corruption compounds. The measured KL between draft and target grows steeply over decoding steps under this arrangement.

The fix is bookkeeping. The target processes the prompt and owns the cache. The draft reads that cache directly instead of maintaining a history. Draft-side KV writes are treated as temporary and rolled back before verification, and only accepted tokens commit. Each approximation error is then confined to the speculative step that made it and cannot reach the next one.

The gain is large and unevenly distributed in an informative way: +23% to +79% acceptance, with the biggest jumps on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (OLMoE on GSM8K nearly doubles). That is the signature you would expect if the mechanism is error accumulation over long dependent generations — short answers give the drift less room to develop.

Two reasons this deserves attention beyond its own paper. First, it is the only distribution-preserving component of the system it appears in; the other two mechanisms trade exactness or need per-platform profiling (reuse-aware-gating-edits-the-target-model-not-the-draft, the-expert-union-is-a-decision-variable-not-a-cost-to-schedule-around). Second, it is not MoE-specific at all. The failure is a property of any self-speculative arrangement where the draft runs its own state, which includes layer-skipping and quantized-draft schemes (a-four-bit-replica-with-exact-rescoring-outscores-an-exact-scan is a neighbour here). The measurements happen to be on sparse models because that is what the paper is about; nothing in the mechanism requires it.

The bound is that the study never isolates this against a non-self-speculative setup, so the size of the effect on an externally-trained drafter — which has no shared parameterization to preserve in the first place — is untested.
