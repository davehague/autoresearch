Bitwise alignment costs a quarter of throughput and has not yet bought a measured gain

The engineering is real and the accounting should be stated plainly, because this vault's job is to price things rather than admire them.

What it costs: 25.3% end-to-end slowdown — generation +31.3% (591.3s → 776.6s), policy training +18.6% (498.6s → 591.3s). A quarter of the RL budget.

What it buys, numerically: the rollout-versus-training logprob gap collapses by roughly four orders of magnitude at the tail — average per-step maximum 5.073 → <10⁻⁴ — with mean and standard deviation falling similarly. The mismatch described in rollout-and-training-are-two-different-policies is genuinely eliminated, not merely reduced.

What it buys, on the metric anyone trains for: nothing measured. The authors' own sentence: over the 50-step window, "we did not observe a meaningful reward improvement from eliminating contract-covered train–inference mismatch."

Two readings, and the difference between them is what a follow-up should resolve.

The generous reading is that the experiment is powered to demonstrate a fix and not to demonstrate its value. The motivating failures are instabilities, not slow drifts — the cited Fireworks GLM-5.2 collapse happened around step 20 on a different model, and instabilities are stochastic. Fifty steps on one model, one dataset and one seed is nowhere near enough to see a collapse that may or may not have occurred, so a null here is close to uninformative about the risk case.

The disciplined reading, which is what goes on post-training-rl until someone runs the missing arm, is that the vault has a precise cost and an absent benefit. The arm that would settle it is obvious and cheap relative to what has already been built: take a configuration known to collapse under mismatched execution and show it surviving under the contract. Nothing in the post reports it.

The general shape is one this vault keeps meeting, and it is worth naming as a pattern rather than as a complaint about this post specifically. A correctness intervention justified by reasoning about a mechanism, priced in throughput, and validated on the mechanism rather than on the outcome, is a plausible purchase and not yet a demonstrated one. reproducing-your-own-output-costs-most-of-the-speedup is the mirror image from the acceleration side — there the vault insisted an advertised speedup be re-priced at the setting where output is preserved. The same discipline applies pointing the other way: a correctness cost should be re-priced at the setting where the outcome improves.

For a practitioner today the operational read is narrow but usable. If your RL run is stable and your clip fraction is low, this buys you reproducibility and costs you a quarter of your throughput. If your clip fraction is high — the Fireworks incident reports ~45% of tokens discarded — mismatch is a live suspect and the contract is worth its cost as a diagnostic even before it is worth it as a default.

T2, self-reported, one model, one dataset, one node, 50 steps, no rescue arm.
