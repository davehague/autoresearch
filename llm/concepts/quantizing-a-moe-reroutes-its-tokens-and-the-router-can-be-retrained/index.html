The experts moved and nobody told the router

Every quantization result in this vault treats the model as a fixed function whose weights get coarser. For a mixture of experts that is wrong in a specific way, and the error compounds: the router is a learned classifier over expert outputs, and quantizing the experts changes the thing it was trained to discriminate between. Measured directly, over 40% of tokens are routed to different experts after 1.5-bit expert quantization of Mixtral-8×7B.

Two consequences, and the second is the one worth carrying.

First: the router is a rounding error in the parameter budget and a first-order term in the quality budget. Routers are under 0.04% of total parameters. Freezing everything else and updating only them against cross-entropy task loss on 128 calibration sequences — one epoch, under a minute on three H100s, 3.5% of the GPTQ quantization time — moves Mixtral at 1.5 bits from 9.66 to 7.69 WikiText2 perplexity and 23.53 to 17.18 on C4. No inference-time cost. Nothing else in the paper's ablation ladder buys that much.

Second, and this is the finding: matching the full-precision router is the wrong objective. The paper runs the obvious alternative — replace the quantized model's router logits with the recorded full-precision ones, which is what prior work on routing shift does — and it is worth almost nothing: 9.66 → 9.29, against 7.69 for task-loss fine-tuning. The interpretation is clean and slightly uncomfortable. After the experts have been quantized, the full-precision routing is no longer the routing that minimizes loss, because it was optimal for a different set of experts. Preserving the original routing decisions preserves a decision that has stopped being correct. The rule generalizes past MoE: when you perturb a model's components, do not pin the parts that select among them to their pre-perturbation behaviour — re-fit them to the perturbed components.

The same logic drives the paper's third component. Estimating which experts deserve bits requires a Taylor expansion of the loss around the weights, and at 1.5 bits the perturbation is far too large for that to be accurate from the full-precision model. So estimate importance from a previously quantized and router-fine-tuned model at a nearer bit budget and step down 2.5 → 2.0 → 1.5. That lowers the expert-selection change ratio from 41.31% to 34.69% and the perplexity from 9.66 to 9.01 — smaller than the router-tuning effect, and the same idea applied one level up.

This is a genuine interaction with post-training-quantization that no source in either map had stated: quantization damage in a routed architecture is not confined to the weights that were quantized. It propagates into a discrete decision made by weights that were not. The nearest thing this vault already holds is shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts — where the router is one of the parameters worth protecting because every token passes through it — and this refines that from "keep the router at high precision" to "keep the router at high precision and re-fit it, because even an untouched router is now mis-specified."

The honest wrinkle: taking a gradient step on 0.04% of the parameters means this is not post-training quantization in the strict sense. It is a very cheap fine-tune, and every calibration-only protocol in this map is measured under a different rule. That is a small departure — no labels, no target data, one epoch — but it should be stated when comparing numbers across the map.

Bound: measured on Mixtral-8×7B at the lowest bit budget in the paper, with GPTQ as the underlying quantizer and attention held at 4 bits. The router-shift percentage at 2.5 and 2.0 bits is not reported separately, so how quickly rerouting sets in is unknown.

Rebutted by: router-movement-is-not-a-measure-of-downstream-influence — on the inferential step, not the result. The ">40% of tokens change expert" figure is a router statistic, and router movement varies independently of how much of it reaches the residual stream (1.6x span against 3.2x, running opposite for the most fine-grained router measured). What establishes that routing is the lever here is the retraining outcome (perplexity 9.66 → 7.69), not the reroute rate; quote the second, not the first.
