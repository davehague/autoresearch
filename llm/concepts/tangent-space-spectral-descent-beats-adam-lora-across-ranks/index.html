The LoRA optimizer's geometry is a fourth, previously-untested axis in the "does low-rank match full fine-tuning" contest

peft-methods's central contest — does a low-rank update match full fine-tuning, and which of rank, init, placement, or learning rate explains the gap — has so far been argued entirely under a fixed optimizer (Adam applied independently to the A and B factors). LoRA-TSD is the map's first source to vary the optimizer's geometry instead: it treats the LoRA step as a tangent vector of the fixed-rank matrix manifold the BA product actually lives on, applies spectral (Muon-style) descent in that tangent space, then retracts — correcting for the fact that independent per-factor Adam steps don't respect the manifold BA lives on, so a step that looks small in (A, B) parameter space can be an arbitrarily different size in BA space depending on current factor magnitudes.

This bears directly on the map's existing learning-rate-artifact and rank-allocation findings (adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact, a-random-non-uniform-rank-profile-already-beats-a-flat-one if present) — both were measured under Adam, and LoRA-TSD's result that its margin over competing optimizers holds across ranks where other methods' advantage narrows suggests some of what the map has been attributing to rank/init/placement choices may instead (or additionally) be optimizer-geometry sensitive. The map has never separated "which factorization/init/placement wins" from "under which optimizer," and this source is the first data point on that missing axis.

Caveat carried from the source: all six tasks are commonsense reasoning or NLI — none are generative or domain-acquisition tasks, which is exactly where the map's contest is least settled (initialization-leverage-vanishes-where-tuned-lora-saturates names domain acquisition as the untested frontier). LoRA-TSD's advantage on the map's most-contested terrain (domain acquisition, generative tasks) is therefore still open.

Graded extends: opens a new optimizer-geometry axis without overturning any existing placement/init/rank finding. strength: suggestive — quantified but single-paper, no independent replication, and "outperforms every competing optimizer" is against the baselines the paper selected rather than an exhaustive PEFT-optimizer sweep.
