Length inflation in on-policy distillation has a specific, fixable cause hiding under "aligned" stopping sets — and a second cause the fix doesn't reach

This map's transfer-mechanism strand already carries two accounts of what moves during on-policy distillation: entropy suppression (on-policy-distillation-gains-are-mostly-entropy-suppression-not-transfer) and probability transport toward teacher-preferred targets (routed-transport-of-teacher-student-disagreement-beats-sampled-reverse-kl-in-on-policy-distillation). This source names a distinct, previously-unattributed failure mode on the same mechanism side of the ledger: OPD students sometimes grow responses toward the generation budget instead of terminating, and the paper traces a concrete, measurable cause — student and teacher can declare an identical stopping-token set and still disagree about which member of that set each one actually uses. Qwen3's base student places ~0.8 probability on <|endoftext|> at rollout-end; its post-trained teacher places ~10⁻¹¹ on that same token, favoring <|im_end|> instead. Aligning the declared sets does nothing here, because both tokens were already members of both sets — the mismatch is about probability allocation within a shared set, not set membership.

The fix treats all functionally-equivalent EOS tokens as one semantic "stop" action: sum each model's probability across its equivalent-EOS tokens into a single aggregate, compute the OPD KL signal against that aggregate rather than any one surface token, and register every equivalent token as a valid stop during rollout. This lets student and teacher keep different individual-token preferences — Gemma-3-4B's student keeps using <eos> while the teacher uses <end_of_turn> — while the underlying stopping decision transfers. Results hold across three model families with the effect cleanest on Qwen3 (near-complete recovery) and more gradual on Llama (residual length gap persists).

What earns this strong rather than suggestive: the mechanism is named, measured at the probability level (not just inferred from output length), and the fix is validated with matched before/after numbers across three independent model families rather than one. What keeps this honest rather than a closed case: the paper's own K2-Horizon stage-wise analysis finds a second length-inflation mechanism — termination probability collapses again late in training (~step 300+) and length re-inflates toward the budget, despite the semantic-EOS fix already being applied. The authors state directly they cannot fully explain this phase-3 behavior; an appendix offers a "plausible" (their word) contributor involving omitted future mismatch in local OPD, not a demonstrated mechanism. Starting OPD from a mid-training/SFT checkpoint avoids the severe early collapse but not the late-stage re-inflation, meaning "warm-start the teacher relationship" is not a substitute fix for the underlying open mechanism.

Graded extends: this adds a mechanism and a partial fix to the map's transfer-mechanism strand without contradicting the entropy-suppression or probability-transport accounts — length inflation and OPD's accuracy gains are different observables, and nothing here claims the entropy-suppression account is wrong about what drives accuracy. Discounted for scope: single-turn mathematical reasoning only; multi-turn/agentic settings may need context-dependent termination classes rather than one "stop" action, since special tokens there can mark distinct events (message completion vs. tool handoff); template sensitivity documented only for Qwen3.

Rebutted by: none — extends the transfer-mechanism strand with a distinct, complementary failure mode rather than contesting an existing claim.
