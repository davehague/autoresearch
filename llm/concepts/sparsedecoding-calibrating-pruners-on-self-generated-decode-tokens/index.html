A pruner calibrated on text nobody will ever decode is leaving quality on the table for a reason that has nothing to do with sparsity itself -- and the fix also exposes that the standard 2:4 sparse kernel actively slows decode down

This map's central claim -- at matched footprint, quantize rather than prune, by roughly an order of magnitude -- was measured under standard (C4-style) calibration. This paper isolates a confound inside that accounting: Hessian-guided pruning calibrates its reconstruction target on fixed natural text, but decoding runs the model on its own previously-generated tokens, a distribution the calibration set never samples. Appendix A's discrepancy measurement (median activation-distribution ratio 1.81-3.26x across the majority of prunable modules) confirms this is not a hypothetical mismatch. Fixing it -- calibrate on activations collected while the dense model autoregressively generates, discarding prefill -- improves every generation-task result tested, with the largest relative gains exactly where the sparsity budget is tightest (2:4), which is the regime where this map's own compression-trinity source measured pruning's worst damage. This narrows, but does not close, this map's order-of-magnitude finding: some of what looked like an intrinsic cost of removing weights was a calibration artifact in how that cost was measured, specifically for generation-quality tasks.

The kernel half reframes this map's speed/accuracy tradeoff. The map already holds "structure costs accuracy and keeps its speed; precision costs speed and keeps its accuracy" from the MoE-pruning cross-link. This paper shows that claim is contingent on which kernel implements the structure: the standard 2:4 Sparse Tensor Core path is measured here at 0.85-0.87x of dense decode speed -- slower than dense, inverting the "keeps its speed" half entirely -- while a bitmask-indexed SpMV kernel built specifically for the memory-bound decode operation (not the SpMM operation sparse kernels usually target) restores and exceeds dense speed (1.34-1.48x). "Sparsity buys decode speed" is therefore a kernel-engineering claim, not an architectural guarantee of N:M patterns.

Cross-link to post-training-quantization's calibration thread: this is the first in-vault source showing a calibration-sensitivity result for structural pruning rather than precision reduction, and it sits in tension with this map's own flagged anomaly that structural importance appeared calibration- insensitive (0.85 points across three corpora, from the SNIP-style source) -- the two findings measure different objects (layer-importance robustness vs. a Hessian reconstruction target's distributional match), and reconciling them is now a concrete open question.

Caveats carried from the source. Speed measured only on A100 GPUs, batch size 1, context 512 -- no CPU/Apple-silicon/llama.cpp measurement, which is this map's standing local-deployment gap. Most sparse configurations remain well below dense even with the fix (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B at 2:4 reaches only 4.0% ClassEval vs 24.0% dense) -- the paper narrows a gap, it does not close it. WritingBench quality depends on an LLM judge (DeepSeek-V4-Flash), not human evaluation; ClassEval's 100 tasks make percentages noisy. No stated code/kernel release.
