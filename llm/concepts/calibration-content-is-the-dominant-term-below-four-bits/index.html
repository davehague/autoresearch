The calibration set is not a nuisance parameter

Post-training quantization fits a compressed model to reproduce a full-precision one's behaviour on a small sample of text. The sample is treated everywhere as a formality — 128 or 256 sequences of C4 or WikiText2, chosen by convention and rarely reported.

This is the third independent measurement in this vault of what that choice is worth, and it is the largest by an order of magnitude. Fix the model (Qwen3-4B), the quantizer (CAT-Q), the bit budget (W1.58A16) and the token count (4 M). Change only what the text is, and Math-500 goes from 0.00 to 58.40, HumanEval+ from 0.00 to 53.98. The prior instances were quantization-calibration-sets-leak-their-domain-into-the-model (HumanEval 13.7 → 29.3) and calibration-corpus-choice-swings-a-quantized-models-math-score-again (GSM8K 31.77 → 42.30). Three labs, three quantizers, one conclusion, with the effect growing as the format tightens.

The ladder shows the effect has structure. Generic web text gives zero. Domain-relevant question-answer pairs give 24.20. Question-answer pairs plus a chain-of-thought trace give 58.40. The step that matters is not topical relevance — it is whether the calibration text contains the multi-step derivation the model produces at inference. A reconstruction objective can only preserve activation patterns it observes, and web text never elicits the long dependency chains that break first under precision loss (quantization-preserves-the-answer-and-changes-the-derivation).

And the effect is bit-width-indexed, which is the scope condition the earlier instances could not supply. The same intervention under a second quantizer: +2.2 points at W4A16, +58.8 at W2A16, +58.4 at ternary. So this vault's four-bit default is comparatively robust to the confound, and the practitioner warning is sharp rather than universal — below four bits, the calibration corpus is a larger term than the method.

That also adds a second non-bit-width cause to the sub-4-bit cliff. quantization-aware-init-extends-lora-below-the-4-bit-floor already showed part of the cliff is an initialization property. This shows part of it is a calibration-content property. Neither abolishes the floor — Qwen3-4B still gives up 38 Math-500 points at ternary against BF16 — but two of the cliff's apparent causes have now turned out to be recipe rather than physics.

Practitioner form, and it is free: when you quantize something you intend to reason with, build the calibration set out of the model's own outputs on the domain you will use it in, at the longest sequence length you can afford. The paper measures the length term separately — 2,048 → 4,096 tokens moves Math-500 58.40 → 68.60, with the largest gains on long-form reasoning, because a longer window captures a complete trace rather than a truncated one.

Scope: weight-only quantization with 16-bit activations throughout; one model family plus one distilled Llama; reasoning and coding benchmarks; calibration itself costs 4–240 hours on 8×A100-80GB, so the procedure is out of reach locally even though the rule is not.
