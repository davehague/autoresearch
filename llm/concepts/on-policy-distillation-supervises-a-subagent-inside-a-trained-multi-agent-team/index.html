The teacher-student pair this map has studied as one model training another turns out to compose: a teacher trained on a simpler workflow can supervise one role inside a more complex one

Every on-policy-distillation (OPD) entry on this map so far is about a single student learning from a single teacher's token-level feedback on the student's own rollouts — the mechanism this map calls strand 2's transfer question. MultiAgent-OPD does not change that mechanism; it changes what counts as the student. Here the "student" receiving OPD supervision is the Searcher role inside a three-role hierarchical agent system (Leader assigns sub-tasks, Searchers gather evidence, Answerer produces the final response), and the "teacher" is not a larger frontier model but a frozen, already on-policy-distilled-or-RL-trained simpler two-role system (Searcher-Answerer, no Leader) — trained first on plain outcome rewards, then frozen and reused as the process-supervision signal for the harder, three-role system's Searchers.

Why this needed a mechanism beyond outcome reward. The paper's own diagnosis is specific: a Searcher's contribution is only indirectly visible in the team's final answer (a useful report can still go uncredited if the Leader or Answerer later mishandles it), and scoring Searcher quality directly with an LLM judge is unreliable because the judge's score reflects its own opinion of a "good report" rather than measured usefulness. On-policy distillation sidesteps both problems the way it has elsewhere on this map: it scores the Searcher's own sampled trajectories against what a demonstrably competent policy (the frozen Teacher) would have done, rather than relying on a judge's held opinion or a diluted team-level signal.

What the ablations isolate, and what they don't. Swapping the task-trained Teacher for the untrained base model collapses most of the gain (avg@4 29.50% vs. 38.68%), and removing direct Searcher supervision entirely collapses it by a similar margin (29.82%) — two separately-necessary pieces, not one dominant one. Neither ablation, however, tests whether the Teacher needs to be trained on the same simpler workflow it is distilling into, or whether any sufficiently competent Searcher-Answerer policy would do; the paper's design conflates "task-trained" with "trained on this specific two-role precursor task."

Scope, and why this is graded suggestive. This is an agent-orchestration result, evaluated on one base model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) and one domain (English open-domain multi-hop QA with a fixed retriever) — no cross-model or cross-domain replication, and the five compared systems in the paper's main table all share this same base and benchmark set. It extends the map's on-policy-distillation mechanism to a new structural setting (supervising a role inside a larger trained system) rather than reporting a new distillation algorithm, which is why it belongs here rather than opening new ground on the build-path-ordering strand. Reverse-KL-style training, used for the Searcher signal, is noted by the authors as capable of reducing exploration — a caveat this map's existing strand-2 entries have also flagged for other OPD variants, unresolved here too.
