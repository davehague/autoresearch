The learning-forgetting trade-off this map has measured repeatedly has a nameable source — unused configured rank — and a cheap loss-term fix that composes with three existing low-rank parameterizations

TL;DR: This map's strand (1) contest has accumulated results on which configuration (rank, init, placement, learning rate) narrows the LoRA-vs-full-fine-tuning gap without asking why a given rank's capacity goes unused. Sawada & Goyal name the mechanism directly: a trained LoRA update's Rényi-2 effective rank — not its configured rank — is the real capacity ceiling, and standard LoRA leaves roughly half of it on the table at high ranks (145.0/265.0 effective rank at r=256/512 on Qwen2.5-0.5B, i.e. 57%/52% utilization). Their fix doesn't touch the adapter parameterization at all: an SVD-free, differentiable Rényi-2 effective-rank penalty added to the existing training loss. At λ=0.01 it lowers held-out Wiki perplexity at every tested rank ≥16 across three models (Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) with training perplexity essentially unchanged — the penalty reclaims capacity rather than trading it against fit quality. On mixed-domain fine-tuning at r=896, the gap to dense FT's Wiki PPL (16.56) narrows from standard LoRA's 19.6 to the penalized run's 14.6, though dense FT still holds a code-retention edge (StarCoder PPL 3.64 vs. the penalized runs' 4.10-4.14) — the trade-off narrows, it does not disappear.

What it says. The penalty is parameterization-agnostic: applied to PiSSA (GSM8K: erank 829.0→895.7, Wiki PPL 23.173→19.706) and LoRA-XS (erank 565.0→895.0, Wiki PPL 25.479→20.121), it reclaims capacity in both of this map's cited low-rank variants using the same loss-term mechanism — the capacity gap this paper names is a property of low-rank training in general, not a defect specific to vanilla LoRA's parameterization. A norm-bounded alternative (NB-LoRA) also raises effective rank but the authors report optimization instability and cannot cleanly separate its gains from norm control alone, leaving the Rényi-2 penalty as the paper's preferred mechanism. This source supplies a candidate causal account for why this map's existing capacity-adjacent findings (intruder dimensions, spectral mismatch between a LoRA update and the base weight's principal directions) show up at all: configured rank was never the binding constraint, effective rank was, and it is cheaply trainable. Untested against malora-margin-lora-merging-forgetting's theoretical account of the same forgetting-vs-adaptation trade-off (margin-based, near-orthogonal-task linear classification) or deletion-tuning-sparse-mask-matches-full-finetuning's causal weight-change-geometry account of RL forgetting specifically — three independent mechanisms for the same headline trade-off arrived within one week, with no composition or head-to-head test among them.

Caveats. Two-author Georgia Tech preprint, no stated peer venue; code released but several results are single runs, and the authors themselves flag one excluded PiSSA run for apparent data replay. λ needs per-task tuning (the authors note it can hurt when the goal is deliberate underfitting or narrow retention), and at r=1 the effective-rank term is constant — there is no capacity to reclaim at the lowest ranks, where this map's own rank-cliff findings already live. No RL or on-policy-distillation setting tested (SFT/language-modeling loss only), so whether the penalty helps or competes with deletion-tuning-sparse-mask-matches-full-finetuning's RL-specific geometric forgetting-resistance is open. No merging experiment — whether a higher-effective-rank adapter merges more or less cleanly than a low-utilization one (this map's other headline LoRA property, per malora-margin-lora-merging-forgetting) is untested.
