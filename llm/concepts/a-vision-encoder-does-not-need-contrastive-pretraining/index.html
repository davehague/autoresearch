Nobody needed CLIP; they needed a warm start, and at scale the warm start costs stability

vision-language-models has framed its architecture contest as two families: late fusion (a pretrained vision encoder, a trainable bridge, an LLM — LLaVA, Molmo, Qwen2.5-VL) against early fusion (no encoder at all, images tokenized into the text vocabulary — Chameleon, Emu3). The map's encoder sub-question has three branches, two measured: frozen off-the-shelf CLIP, a ViT trained from scratch at native resolution, and unfreezing an existing CLIP.

Kimi K3 occupies a cell the map does not have. Architecturally it is late fusion — MoonViT-V2 encodes images and video, a lightweight MLP projector maps features into the shared embedding space. Procedurally it is early fusion: "language and vision are jointly optimized from the start of training, rather than grafting a vision encoder onto a pre-trained language model through a post-hoc alignment stage," with visual and textual tokens interleaved inside one next-token objective. So the map's dichotomy conflates two independent choices — whether the modality boundary exists in the architecture and whether it exists in the training schedule — and this is an existence proof that you can take one without the other.

The specific finding worth grading is narrower and more useful. Prior practice, including this team's own previous model, initializes the vision tower from a contrastively pretrained encoder such as SigLIP, "under the premise that pre-trained visual knowledge gives the model a head start." K3 trains MoonViT-V2 entirely from scratch with next-token prediction, and reports two things: it matches the SigLIP-initialized baseline across vision evaluations, and the reason they switched was training stability — the SigLIP-initialized tower "shows persistently higher gradient norms with frequent spikes" under joint optimization while the from-scratch tower stays smooth. Their reading is that next-token prediction lets the encoder's representations be shaped by the language-modelling objective directly, "rather than by a contrastive loss that favors global semantics over fine-grained textual and structural cues."

This pushes back on the map's cheapest and most-replicated assumption. The frozen CLIP prior is what makes late-fusion adaptation cost about a node-day — LLaVA's economics, which this map explicitly credits as what puts VLM work within a practitioner's reach. If contrastive pretraining is unnecessary at scale, the prior was buying convenience rather than capability, and the map's practitioner rule needs a scale condition attached: import CLIP because you cannot afford to train a tower, not because the contrastive objective contributes something a language objective cannot.

Two reasons this is suggestive rather than strong, and the second is the one that constrains it.

The comparison is a gradient-norm figure and a sentence. No table of vision benchmarks compares MoonViT-V2 against MoonViT-3D. "Matches the SigLIP-initialized baseline" is asserted with a stability plot as the visible evidence; the ablation exists inside the lab and not on the page.

And "at scale" is doing enormous work. This is a 401M encoder trained jointly with a 2.78T backbone on a corpus that includes programmatically generated multimodal data — SVG, 3D assets, webpages, games, CAD schematics coupled with the code that produced them — plus coordinate supervision in both absolute and normalized formats. The claim that a language objective can substitute for a contrastive one is a claim about a training budget nobody on this map's practitioner side has. It says nothing about whether a 7B late-fusion model fine-tuned on one node should replace its frozen CLIP, and the honest prediction is that it should not.

One portable detail regardless of scale: a 2×2 pixel-shuffle downsample before projection cuts the visual token count fourfold, which is what keeps up to 3584×3584 inputs affordable. That is the same lever this map recorded for Qwen2.5-VL's window attention — spend fewer tokens per unit of image — and it lands on the bill vlm-on-device cares about, where 1024×1024 already costs 156 MB of cached embeddings.
