A gradient-signal proxy explains why on-policy distillation stalls with distant teachers while self-RL teachers recover almost fully — with the actual cause (representation adaptation) named but not yet tested

This map's transfer-mechanism strand has converged on what moves under on-policy distillation (KL direction selects skill vs. facts — reverse-kl-opd-transfers-skill-not-knowledge-forward-kl-restores-it — and distributional novelty, not teacher quality — distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank, does-on-policy-distillation-really-distill). This source attacks a different, narrower question: why does OPD sometimes barely move the loss at all, independent of what it transfers when it works. The answer it offers is a training-dynamics diagnosis, not a transfer-content one: a logged learning-signal proxy collapses an order of magnitude early in larger-scale-teacher runs while most of the loss is still unaccounted for — a premature plateau, not a floor. Self-RL teachers (same model family, RL-trained, close in parameter space to the student) show gradual signal decline alongside near-total loss reduction.

What the theorem does and does not claim. Theorem 5.2 proves a sufficient local-recovery bound for teachers within a bounded parameter-distance ρ of the student — it explains why self-RL teachers (small ρ) recover, but larger-scale teachers have different parameter dimensions entirely, so the theorem is silent on whether they could in principle recover by some other path. This is a narrower and more honest claim than "OPD fails with distant teachers" — it is "this specific sufficient condition for recovery doesn't apply to distant teachers," which is compatible with distant-teacher OPD working for reasons the paper's model doesn't capture.

The open mechanism. The authors' own candidate explanation — post-OPD students show almost no parameter movement (0.025-0.098%) and near-identical representations (CKA >=0.985) to their pre-OPD selves, suggesting limited representation adaptation starves the learning signal — is stated as a hypothesis the paper's own measurements "do not establish." Self-RL teachers show roughly 5x more self-displacement from their own base than OPD students show from their pre-OPD state, which is at least consistent with the story, but displacement magnitude alone does not separate stalled from successful runs in their data.

Why this belongs on this map rather than training-time-quantization or post-training-rl. The object is on-policy distillation's own training dynamics (loss decay rate, gradient-signal proxy), not a recovery procedure for a downstream compression (contrast training-time-quantization's on-policy-QAT-recovery entries) or an RL-specific forgetting fix (contrast post-training-rl). It sits most naturally next to this map's teacher-selection findings — distillation-gain-tracks-novelty-not-teacher-rank found teacher quality doesn't predict OPD gain; this source finds teacher parameter-distance predicts whether OPD's training signal survives at all, which is a different axis (where the teacher sits relative to the student's optimization landscape, not what the teacher knows) and the two have not been tested against each other on the same teacher pool.

Caveats carried from the source. Idealized continuous-time gradient-flow model against real noisy AdamW updates; the logged μ̂ proxy is explicitly not a direct measurement of the gradient norm the theorem concerns. One learning rate, one training budget, no seeds. Seven runs across two model families is a small sample for a self-RL-vs-larger-scale split. Code released.
