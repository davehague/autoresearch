The router moved. That is not evidence of anything yet.

Router divergence is the quantity the mixture-of-experts literature measures when it wants to say something has gone wrong — after quantization, after conditioning changes, during self-distillation — and this vault has repeated the habit. It measures the wrong thing, or rather it measures a thing whose relationship to the output is not the one assumed.

An exact blockwise decomposition separates the routing term (gates move at fixed content) from the dense-like content term (content shifts at fixed gates), and then asks the question that decides whether either matters: how much of it reaches the residual stream. Across seven checkpoints and two domains, the routing term spans only 1.6× as a fraction of block output while its exposure spans 3.2×, and exposure is ordered by the routed block's share of the residual — Spearman 0.96 and 1.00 — not by how fine-grained the router is. The clean demonstration: Qwen3-30B, top-8-of-128, has one of the largest routing factors (0.133) and the smallest exposure (0.019); OLMoE shows the reverse (0.124 and 0.056). Routing more, in the sense everybody measures, and mattering less.

The content term is also larger than the routing term on every checkpoint, by 1.6–4.1×, which is the first thing a debugging effort should be told.

This does not overturn results that measured the mediated path rather than the gate. a-third-of-quantized-moe-damage-travels-through-the-router ran a causal decomposition and found route-mediated damage of 0.31 with a confidence interval — that is exactly the instrument this paper prescribes, and the two agree on method. What it pushes back on is the inferential step taken elsewhere: quantizing-a-moe-reroutes-its-tokens-and-the-router-can-be-retrained reports that over 40% of tokens change expert after 1.5-bit quantization, and that number is routinely read as establishing where the damage is. It does not establish that; the retraining result (perplexity 9.66 → 7.69) does. The scope condition is clean: a reroute rate is a router statistic, and a router statistic needs an exposure measurement before it becomes a claim about behaviour.

Boundaries stated by the authors and worth keeping. The regime is single-step with frozen weights — teacher and student passes compared before any update — so accumulated training drift is out of scope. The seven checkpoints are family-correlated, so the observed range is an empirical regularity rather than a population law. And the range has a scale boundary that does not replicate: Qwen3 at 235B pushes the routing factor to 0.188/0.200, above the observed range, while exposure holds at 0.030 — and DeepSeek-V2 at 236B stays inside on both.
