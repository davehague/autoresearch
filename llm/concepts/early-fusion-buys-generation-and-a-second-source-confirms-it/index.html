The second early-fusion source

vision-language-models went contested on 2026-07-21 when early-fusion-tokenizes-images-into-the-text-vocabulary showed that the late-fusion recipe — CLIP encoder, trainable projection, open LLM backbone, replicated by three groups — is not the only working design. The map has carried two qualifiers on that ever since: early fusion is n=1, and the one capability it claims over the adapter stack, native image generation, shipped disabled in the source that claimed it. So the trade on offer was a demonstrated cost (no CLIP prior, much harder training, QK-norm required to avoid divergence) against an undemonstrated benefit.

This closes the second qualifier and halves the first. A single 8B decoder-only transformer, trained from scratch on interleaved text, image and video tokens with one cross-entropy loss, generates images that beat SDXL in human preference (70.0 vs 66.9), more than doubles Chameleon's own GenEval score (0.66 vs 0.39), reaches 81.60 on DPG-Bench where SDXL is 74.65 — and then does the same thing for video, topping OpenSora 1.2 on VBench (80.96 vs 79.76) by predicting the next token in a video sequence, and extending a clip by continuing it. Video is the sharper part: the adapter stack has no story for generating a modality at all, let alone a temporal one, and this needs no new mechanism to get it — a video is a longer sequence of the same tokens.

What this does not do is resolve the contest, and the map should hold contested deliberately.

The perception side is where the headline is thinnest. "Surpasses LLaVA-1.6" is an average of 62.1 against 61.8 across twelve benchmarks. Split by the paper's own asterisk — defined as the images of related training datasets were observed during training — the marked benchmarks are where Emu3 wins big (ScienceQA 89.2, ChartQA 68.6, DocVQA 76.3, InfoVQA 43.8, AI2D 70.0) and the unmarked ones are mostly losses: MMBench 58.5 vs 67.4, MMMU 31.6 vs 35.1, MM-Vet 37.2 vs 43.9. It wins two unmarked cells — SEEDBench-Img 68.2 vs 64.7 and OCRBench 687 vs 532 — which is a genuine result and a much narrower one than the average suggests. This is worth carrying on evaluation-contamination as its own finding: the authors disclosed the exposure and the summary statistic erased the disclosure.

Both families are still lab-scale, and neither is priced for a consumer card. No latency, no throughput, no memory footprint and no quantized variant appears anywhere. Early fusion's structural cost is legible even so: one 512×512 image is 4,096 tokens, which is why this model needs a 131,072-token context to hold video at all. Late fusion spends a vision encoder's compute once and hands the language model a short sequence; early fusion spends nothing on an encoder and pays in sequence length forever after. That is the trade stated in the currency that matters for local inference, and nobody has measured either side of it on hardware this vault's readers own.

The count is now three late-fusion sources against two early-fusion ones, both families demonstrating what they claim. The comparison the map has wanted since the contest opened — matched training cost, matched data, one evaluation — remains unrun, and this source makes it more valuable rather than less.
