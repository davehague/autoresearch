Data-aware hashing needs a tenth the bits of random projections

Locality-sensitive hashing picks its hyperplanes at random and is therefore correct in expectation and wasteful in practice: MagicPIG needs more than a thousand bits per key to select reliably. Learned hashing fixes the waste by training a nonlinear map, and costs about eight GPU-hours per model variant. BinaryPC takes the third option — compute the binary principal components of the key matrix itself, greedily, with no gradients — and reaches the same selection quality at 64 bits.

This is the third time this vault has recorded that shape, and the other two are on the neighbouring map. rotating-a-transformer-deletes-its-outliers-for-free uses a computational-invariance rotation to remove the activation outliers that block 4-bit quantization — a fixed linear map, no learning. a-data-free-rotation-puts-the-kv-cache-at-three-and-a-half-bits does the same for the cache with no calibration data at all. Now the same move on key selection. Across weights, cache values and cache addresses, the recurring finding is that the geometry of these tensors is structured enough that a well-chosen linear map recovers most of what a trained one buys, and the structure is available cheaply — from an SVD, a Hadamard rotation, or a greedy binary decomposition.

The practical form is a bit budget with an elbow in it: 32-bit codes degrade at 104K context, 64 and 128 behave identically, so 64 is the operating point. That is a smaller, more concrete number than the map usually gets, and it comes with a safeguard that turns out to be load-bearing — retaining the ~10% of tokens with the largest reconstruction error, without which passkey retrieval in the online mode falls to 64%.

One thing to hold against it, in the week this vault started counting undisclosed settings. The offline projection is calibrated on 180 samples: 60 each from PG19, ProofPile and CodeParrot — books, mathematics, code. calibration-corpus-choice-swings-a-quantized-models-math-score-again and calibration-content-is-the-dominant-term-below-four-bits both established that which text a compression method calibrates on is a load-bearing and usually unreported choice, and no ablation here varies it. That the online mode, which calibrates on the actual prompt, performs nearly as well is weak evidence the choice does not matter much for this method — and it is not the experiment.
