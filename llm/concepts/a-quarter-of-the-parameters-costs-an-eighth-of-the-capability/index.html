A quarter of the parameters costs an eighth of the capability

The number that decides which compression lever a practitioner should reach for first. Cut 25% of the parameters with the best available structured pruner and run recovery fine-tuning, and you keep 87.34% of base performance on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and 82.64% on Qwen3-8B. Cut 35% and it is 77.26% and 75.13%. Without recovery fine-tuning, subtract another 8–30 points. This is measured on an 18-task suite spanning generation, world understanding, domain knowledge, NLU and safety — chosen explicitly because narrow pruning evaluations hide where the damage lands.

Set that against the quantization ledger this vault has been keeping. pruning-costs-trustworthiness-where-quantization-does-not measured the full INT8 → INT3 sweep at 1.58 points on Qwen2.5-1.5B, with INT8 a wash and INT4-GPTQ occasionally raising the score. the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff and four-bit-weights-put-llms-on-device say the same thing in the local practitioner's currency. Roughly a point or two for a 4× footprint reduction, against thirteen points for a 1.33× one. At matched memory, take the bits.

The two measurements are independent and they agree, which is why this note is confirms rather than another data point. The earlier one ran unstructured and N:M sparsity against GPTQ and AWQ on a four-dimension trustworthiness benchmark. This runs structured depth-plus-width pruning against six baselines on a capability suite across four architectures including a fused-MLP model and an MoE. Different sparsity families, different instruments, different models, same order of magnitude. It also answers the obvious objection the earlier study could not: recovery fine-tuning was the missing variable, and it narrows the gap without closing it.

Two details that change how the headline should be read. First, the averages hide the shape: task-wise standard deviation runs 8.8% to 30.4% across methods, and SliceGPT posts ~50% retention at 30% spread — a model that has not degraded uniformly but has had particular capabilities destroyed. averaging-a-suite-hides-its-weakest-dimension is the standing warning and pruning is the strongest instance of it in the vault, which is why the paper's decision to report Std RP alongside Avg RP is the more useful half of its metric contribution. Second, the methods are not interchangeable at the tail: every baseline has at least one configuration where it disintegrates, and mean ranks spread from 1.25 to 6.50. A pruning result quoted from one model at one ratio says almost nothing about the next.

Bounds. Two compression ratios with full results (25%, 35%), four architectures, one paper, no independent replication of these specific numbers. Recovery fine-tuning is applied uniformly but its recipe and compute are not compared across methods, so some of the spread at the top may be absorbing RFT sensitivity rather than pruning quality. The comparison to quantization is cross-source and cross-benchmark — the retention percentages here and the TrustLLM points there are not the same scale, so the "order of magnitude" is a comparison of relative damage at comparable footprint reduction, not a single controlled experiment. Nobody has run pruning and quantization against each other at matched footprint on one suite, and until someone does, that is the shape of the evidence.
