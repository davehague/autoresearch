"Ask a different provider's judge" is not a reliable check on an LLM-judged conclusion

TL;DR: When no human labels exist, practitioners commonly re-judge a system comparison with a model from a different provider, expecting that providers' differing biases will not agree by accident. Han & Kim preregister four hypotheses (H1-H4) and test them on 4,000 Chatbot Arena battles (9 systems, 33 pairs, both response orders, every battle human-voted) re-judged by 5 models from 3 providers.

What it says. The core replication hypothesis (H1) failed: verdict-level agreement with humans was low for every judge and system-ranking agreement with the human ranking varied from essentially uncorrelated to strong depending which judge was used (tau 0.17-0.89) -- a system ranking is not stable across a judge swap. The one supported precision gain (H2) had a narrow mechanism: it came entirely from excluding one poorly-aligned judge from the replication set, not from independently-biased judges converging on a shared truth; requiring that excluded judge's agreement collapsed usable coverage from 100% to 35% of comparisons. Sample-efficiency (H3) held for 4 of 5 judges (no more verdicts needed than human votes, once converged) but 3 judges failed to converge at all on a quarter or more of system pairs even at 100 verdicts per pair. Judges' errors against the human vote co-occur more than independence predicts (H4) -- judges share correlated bias rather than contributing independent noise, which is the structural reason cross-provider swapping doesn't automatically cancel error.

Caveats. Small study (9 systems, 33 pairs, 3 low-cost judges in the cross-provider analyses); affiliation (CEO Business School) has no track record training or serving models at scale, a gap relative to this map's other T1/T2 sources. Preregistered design with disclosed deviations (one of six registered judges dropped), public fixed-revision Chatbot Arena data, and released battle IDs/verdicts/preregistration/analysis code as ancillary files partly offset the affiliation gap. Results reported separately for English-only battles; non-English battles are included in judging but not separately validated against human votes. Directly bears on this map's judge-bias checklist entry and its existing mitigation ("swap order, require consistency") -- adds the sharper finding that provider-swapping, the field's other common mitigation, fails for the same underlying reason (correlated bias, not independent noise) that batching failed in batch-membership-destabilizes-llm-judge-verdicts on 2026-11-01.
