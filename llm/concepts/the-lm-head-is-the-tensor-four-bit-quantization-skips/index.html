A gigabyte of unquantized weights hiding in a 4-bit model

post-training-quantization is organized around allocating a bit budget, and holds four independent partitionings of that budget: per layer (AlphaQ's spectral integer program), per tensor class (shared-weights-tolerate-quantization-worse-than-experts), per modality (vlm-precision-should-be-allocated-by-modality), and by what the output is consumed as (an-int4-proxy-ranks-channels-it-cannot-compute). All four assume the tensor under discussion is being quantized at all.

This source checks, and it is not. Loading a bitsandbytes NF4 checkpoint and an AWQ checkpoint and inspecting the modules, lm_head is in both cases a plain torch.nn.Linear holding BF16 weights of shape [151936, 4096]. On Qwen3-8B-Base that is 1.19 GB — after the blocks have been crushed to 4 bits, one of the largest dense tensors left in the file. Vocabularies have grown (Gemma-4-E4B carries 262,144 entries at 1.28 GB) while hidden sizes have not, so the head's share of a quantized model is rising.

For a reader fitting an 8B model onto a 12 GB card, that is not a rounding error, and no source in this vault had priced it.

The recovery is the ordinary part and it works: a quantized rank-10 core, group-wise INT4 residuals, and a rank-6 correction fitted in an activation-derived metric, giving 3.7–3.9× compression at relative perplexity 1.007 where storage-matched naive INT4 reads 1.151. It is a drop-in replacement for the retained BF16 head — composed with AWQ blocks it adds 0.006 cross-entropy, with NF4 blocks 0.007 — and throughput moves under 2%. Applied to the head, it also beats GPTQ at matched calibration (1.0062 ± 0.0004 against 1.0105 ± 0.0007, three seeds) with a 2.36× faster build.

Three qualifiers this map should carry.

The benefit is model-dependent and the paper says so: on Gemma-4-E4B naive INT4 is already at 1.013 against ARCHead's 1.010, which is inside the reporting precision. The head-specific machinery earns its keep where naive INT4 fails, and on that model it does not fail.

The calibration confound is untested here. The activation metric is fitted on WikiText-103 and evaluated on held-out WikiText-103, with no corpus swap. calibration-content-is-the-dominant-term-below-four-bits puts the expected effect at this bit range at only +2.2 points, so the risk is small — but the head is the one tensor whose output is the vocabulary distribution, and a metric fitted on general web text is a strange choice for a code or math deployment. One missing arm on an otherwise careful paper.

And the saving is a constant, not a lever. The paper's own limitations note that peak GPU memory is dominated by logits, activations, workspaces and the KV cache. On a long-context workload the budget kv-cache-management tracks binds first, and 884 MB is a one-time win that does not scale with the workload.
