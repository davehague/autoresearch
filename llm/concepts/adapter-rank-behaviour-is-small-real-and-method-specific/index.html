What survives the sweep is about a point, and it changes sign with rank

adapter-variant-gains-are-a-learning-rate-artifact is the headline: tune the learning rate and the LoRA variant zoo collapses into a 1–2% band. This is the residue, and the residue is more interesting than a flat null would have been, because it is structured by rank and the structure is method-specific:

MethodLow rankHigh rank
PiSSAup to −1.67% vs LoRA at r ≤ 32−0.11% at 64, +0.22% at 128, +0.33% at 256
MiLoRAahead of LoRAadvantage erodes
DoRA+1.1% math, +0.95% code at r=8washes out

PiSSA and MiLoRA inverting each other is the coherent part: PiSSA initializes on the weight's principal singular components and MiLoRA on the minor ones, so a budget too small to hold the principal energy favours the method that isn't trying to. Read together with the null, the picture is that initialization determines where a small budget is spent, not how good the eventual solution is — which matters only while the budget binds.

DoRA's low-rank-only gain is the one with practical reach, because r=8 on one card is where a memory-bound local fine-tune actually sits. It is also a point per benchmark, which is the honest size.

The detail the paper does not draw out is the most useful thing here. The authors hold alpha = r in every experiment, noting this makes γ_r = 1 for all r. That is not a neutral convention — it deletes exactly the mechanism the-lora-scaling-factor-collapses-gradients-at-high-rank identified, where LoRA's default alpha/r divisor damps the adapter harder as rank grows, so gradient norms fall two orders of magnitude from r=4 to r=2048 and extra rank buys nothing. This study sweeps r=4→256 with the damping removed by construction and finds only "subtle rank-dependent behaviors" — no dramatic ceiling to explain.

Those two results are consistent, and together stronger than either alone: rsLoRA says the rank ceiling is a denominator artifact; this paper, having removed the denominator, finds no such ceiling. The vault should record it as a de facto replication of rsLoRA's mechanism by a group that never cites it, arrived at through a hyperparameter convention rather than an argument — which is the most credible kind of replication available.

It also relocates the map's cheapest status-moving experiment. The interesting run is no longer "rsLoRA versus LoRA" but whether any variant gain survives a corrected divisor and a tuned learning rate simultaneously, since this pair of results suggests two of the three knobs the field has been turning were compensating for the third.
