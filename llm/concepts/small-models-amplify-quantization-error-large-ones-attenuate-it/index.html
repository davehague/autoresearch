Small models amplify quantization error; large ones attenuate it

Quantization damage is usually reasoned about one layer at a time — this tensor's error, that tensor's sensitivity. The second half of the signal-noise account is about what happens after the error is made. Errors "can be attenuated, preserved, or amplified as they pass across layers," and over depth the propagated term dominates the freshly-introduced one. So a network is not just a sum of local roundings; it is a gain stage applied to them.

Scale is what sets the gain, and the evidence is a within-family sweep rather than a cross-model regression: across Qwen3 from 0.6B to 14B, "larger models consistently exhibit both lower source error and accumulated error across most layers," with smaller models showing sustained amplification where larger ones "largely preserve or mildly attenuate."

Two disadvantages, at two different stages, pointing the same way. A small model starts with a noisier source — consistent with quantization-damage-has-a-task-term-not-just-a-weight-term, since fewer parameters means less headroom in M — and then multiplies it instead of damping it.

This cuts against this vault's premise, which is why it belongs on the map rather than in a footnote. local-inference exists because a reader runs open weights on hardware they own, and quantization is the lever that makes the model fit. The models that most need the lever are the ones the lever damages most, and the vault has been quoting a single quant ladder across sizes. the-gguf-quant-ladder-is-graded-not-a-cliff established that the ladder has rungs rather than a cliff; this says the rungs are at different heights for different model sizes, and that the 3-bit band — the one the same source finds "varies markedly with task type, quantization method and model scale" — is where the size dependence bites. A 14B at Q3 and a 1B at Q3 are not the same decision, and no in-vault source has priced the difference.

It also gives the margin account a second reason its constants do not transfer. c was already task-dependent; the propagation gain makes it model-scale-dependent through a separate mechanism, which is consistent with borrowing a fitted c costing far more at 3 bits (18–33pp) than at 4 (0.2–8.5pp) — 3 bits is where amplification has something to amplify.

Graded suggestive, not strong, and the reasons are specific. The scale claim rests on one model family (Qwen3) — the sweep is clean and within-family, which is its strength, but a single lineage confounds size with every other choice that family scaled along. The paper reports error statistics rather than accuracy-at-matched-quality per size, so "small models are damaged more" is established in the error metric and inferred in the metric a practitioner cares about. And it is weight-only PTQ, with the authors explicitly declining extrapolation to activation quantization, QAT or combined compression.

The experiment that would settle it is cheap and reachable: the same task suite at the same bit-widths across one open family's size ladder, scored with a paired margin set (a-paired-margin-set-prices-quantization-damage-without-generation) rather than with benchmark accuracy — which would convert this from an error-propagation claim into a per-size quant-ladder recommendation, the thing a local reader actually needs.

Rebutted by: at-two-bits-model-family-outranks-model-size — an approximation of that very experiment arrived 2026-08-19 and does not support the claim at 2 bits: eight checkpoints from three families under one method and one codebase show damage growing with size inside Llama3.x (−15.30 → −21.14 → −26.08 points from 1B to 8B) and Qwen3 (−10.00 → −19.67 → −23.78 from 0.6B to 8B), with only Llama2 running the predicted way. Normalizing against the collapse floor, retained headroom separates by family (Llama2 57–70%, Qwen3 34–41%, Llama3.x 29–32%) and barely by size. The claim survives as a within-family statement and is dominated at the extreme low-bit end, most plausibly by training-token density — untested here.

Rebutted by: training-into-the-deployment-format-beats-choosing-a-better-one — narrowly, and only where a training-time pass is available. This note's amplification holds for post-training quantization, which is how every source establishing it measured. Quantization-aware distillation into the same 4-bit grid retains 96.5–97.4% of BF16 across 230M–2.6B — flat in model size across the range where amplification predicts a widening loss — so the size dependence is a property of compressing a finished checkpoint rather than of the bit width itself. Untested above 2.6B, where the gap being repaired is already inside benchmark noise.
