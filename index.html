Every chemical limit in the world is a dose divided by uncertainty factors, one of which covers the fact that people differ. For the *toxicodynamic* half of that, the convention is 10^0.5 = 3.16 — a chosen number, never measured per chemical. This study ran 53 substances against 131 human lymphoblastoid cell lines from named 1000 Genomes donors (CEU, GBR, TSI, YRI; sex-balanced) and computed, per chemical, the ratio of the population's median sensitivity to its 5th-percentile sensitivity. Median 3.8. Range 1 to 46.2. (1) ⚠️ The abstract and the results section describe the same data at different sizes. The abstract: *"largely consistent with default assumptions."* The results: *"About half of these chemicals exceeded the default TDVF05 of 10^1/2."* For one substance in two the assumed margin is too small; doxorubicin's real spread is 46-fold, and two chemicals exceeded even the combined factor of 10 meant to cover both handling *and* response. For anyone setting a limit those readings have opposite consequences. A genome-wide association analysis locates the cause in *"transporter and metabolism genes"* — a mechanism, not noise. (2) Loop: `closed-loop-ready` on arrival — automated liquid handling, resazurin fluorescence on a FLIPR Tetra, a scripted EC10-and-percentile derivation, no human anywhere in the call. ⚠️ And it qualifies the tier's definition for the third time in five days: its central estimator is a Markov chain Monte Carlo sampler, which works by drawing random samples, so the rule is *fixed* without being *deterministic* — two runs on identical plate data differ unless the seed is pinned, and TDVF05 is a ratio of two *percentiles* of a distribution of such estimates. This is the concrete instance of the open question the vault logged against GL 497 Part III's Bayesian rule on 2026-07-19, arriving one layer lower than expected. Nothing demotes. The remedy is trivial and exactly the kind of thing an optimiser strips as overhead: pin the seed, version the sampler, log both.
