European law requires a pesticide to be tested for 90 days in rats and 90 days in dogs. Of 461 approved active substances the dog drove a health-based guidance value in 288 cases (107 = 23% consumer limit, 183 = 40% operator limit); re-examined, 97% (consumer) and 95% (operator) of those apparent dog-specific sensitivities were experimental and biological variability, dose spacing, study-design differences or body-size scaling — most inside a threefold no-effect-level ratio, i.e. ordinary scatter. EFSA's sentence: *"selecting two species for toxicity testing does not necessarily reduce uncertainty regarding potential human health risks."* ⚠ That is a measured noise floor for the reference standard every method in this vault is scored against, and it is this vault's correlated-blind-spot rail arriving from the *animal* side: two measurements of the same uncertain thing are not two votes. The second half is the flowchart — two preliminary phases plus a decision tree, consuming in-vitro comparative metabolism, in-vitro human Caco-2 permeability, in-vivo rat ADME and PBK modelling of internal exposure; branch conditions are stated and one branch converts a missing study into an explicit extra uncertainty factor rather than a refusal. ⚠ It is still a *"tiered, weight-of-evidence"* approach and no branch carries a number — how dissimilar a metabolite profile must be, and how large the compensating factor should be, are left to context. Same shape as the developmental-neurotoxicity battery's decision path on 2026-07-30. ⚠ And the model layer is given a job and denied another, in writing: PBK may decide whether a species is informative, and *"not for the derivation of HBGVs. The application of standard regulatory uncertainty factors remains unchanged."* A model may say which experiment to skip; it may not say what dose is safe. Tested on four substances, three medium-to-high confidence; the denominator is substances that were *approved*, so any candidate a dog study killed is invisible by construction. Reduction, not replacement — the substitute for the dog is the rat.   • 2026-07-31 — tumorigenicity-anchorage-independent-growth → consolidating (🚩 auto-coined — the vault's 68th map, and a new context of use: cell and gene therapy products) · validates + loop-move (Dorsheimer et al. 2026, *Gene Therapy*, PMID 42527625, DOI 10.1038/s41434-026-00635-z — open access, full text read; HESI Global's CGT-TRACS committee; `funding_flag: [industry]` and load-bearing: all nineteen experimental authors work for Sanofi, AstraZeneca, Bristol Myers Squibb, Novartis or Bayer, the parties who would otherwise pay for a twelve-month mouse study, and no independent validation body took part) · Four laboratories agreed, and the thing they agreed on was read by an imager. Two assays for anchorage-independent growth — the ability of a cell to grow attached to nothing, a classic signature of a cancer-like state — run on a shared spike-in model (MCF10A breast cells seeded with a controlled fraction carrying a PTPN12 tumour-suppressor knockout). Limit of detection 0.8% edited cells at most sites, about one transformed cell in 125. Soft agar colony formation: Spearman rank correlation 0.988–1 between laboratories, 0.952–1 within, against the authors' own honest statement that random data at this sample size lands anywhere in −0.64 to 0.64; ~200-fold dynamic range against the ATP assay's ~15-fold. The ATP assay's agreement was below the noise threshold until saturating concentrations above 12.5% were excluded. ⚠ Reproducibility is not predictivity — one test article, no negatives, no reference set, and whether the assay correctly classifies an unknown product was never tested. ⚠ The mouse disagreed and the authors sided with the dish: the same edited cells produced no tumours in NSG mice, read by the authors as the animal model failing to manifest a real event, on documented grounds (human-cell engraftment in mice is unreliable) — logged as an open question, because the symmetric reading is that anchorage-independent growth over-calls transformation a body would clear, and this study cannot distinguish them. ⚠ Two rail rows — between this and carcinogenicity-cell-transformation-assay (both read a transformation phenotype in a cultured cell line; neither has an immune system, stroma, blood supply or time), and *inside* the map (both assays measure one property in one cell line). ⚠ And a hand-tuned step inside the automated readout: *"the imaging settings used herein need to be re-evaluated for each experiment."* → four-laboratories-agreed-and-an-imager-did-the-reading, the-dish-fired-the-mouse-did-not-and-the-mouse-was-called-wrong, the-imaging-settings-were-retuned-for-each-experiment

Nineteen pesticides in two human cell lines, whole-transcriptome dose-response, benchmark-dose curve fitting, then conversion to a swallowed human dose. Potency estimates obtained for all 19, spanning 0.032–880 µM — four and a half orders of magnitude, so it ranks rather than flags. Against chronic animal studies, ~50% (gut cells) and ~70% (liver cells) within one order of magnitude — restated, about half the gut-cell numbers and three in ten of the liver-cell numbers are off by more than tenfold, with the in-vitro values tending to run conservative. The loop finding: every step from sequencer output to final dose is a published algorithm — curve fitting, model selection, gene filtering, summarisation, extrapolation — so unlike nineteen other maps here, the interpretation *procedure* exists. What does not exist is the criterion. Nothing published states how far below this number a human exposure must sit, or what concordance would qualify it to replace a chronic study: the performance is reported against a target that does not exist, and the blocker moves from *"nobody wrote the rule"* to *"the rule runs and nobody said what it licenses."* ⚠ Unexplained: the *more* concordant cell line is HepG2, whose weak drug-metabolising capacity the vault already holds as a limitation, in a chemical class where bioactivation is common.
