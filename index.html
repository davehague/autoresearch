(1) ⚠️ The hardest version of "can an AI run a lab" gets asked, and the answer is 3.3%. A real 45-workstation robotic chemistry facility exposed every station as a machine-readable skill and scored 4,608 agent-written workflows on whether the lab could physically execute them: 3.3% overall, 28.1% for the best system, and only three executable workflows exceeded 30 operations. Every other automation number this vault holds — ProtoPilot's 88.24% instrument pass rate, AEGIS's F1 0.97 plan check, Pipette's 37–72% manipulation — answers an easier question, because it hands the agent the plan. And the update leg of the loop was finally watched: across five rounds of real experimental feedback, agents made *"local adjustments but no workflow-level replanning or analytical-method redesign."* They turned knobs. That is an empirical foot under the correlated-blind-spot rail — the correct response to a metabolically incompetent battery missing a bioactivation-dependent compound is method redesign, the exact category observed zero times; the parameter-level response produces a cleaner, more confident, equally wrong negative (Guo et al. 2026, arXiv 2607.23045, preprint, `funding_flag: [method-developer]` — the group owns the exam; no human baseline). (2) ⚠️ And the vault's oldest soft finding — agenda drift — turns out to be a theorem about the scoring function. For every lookahead depth there is a discovery problem on which a planner scoring by expected information gain has an unbounded approximation ratio, and one it never solves — because acquiring a capability (an instrument, an assay, a pipeline) returns no information inside the horizon and is therefore *observationally indistinguishable from paying for a null action.* Developing a non-animal method for an endpoint that has none IS that action. Read the vault's two `bench-bound` maps against it — reproductive-fertility-multigenerational-nams (no method exists at all) and immunotoxicity-myelotoxicity-cfu-gm (fully quantitative, bench-bound only because its scaling model consumes a mouse dose) — and both are precisely what an information-maximising loop will never fund, while it spends happily on the plate-reader endpoints. An autonomous testing system optimising information gain gets better and better at the assays that already exist and never builds the ones that do not (Hassoon & Dredze 2026, arXiv 2608.05085, preprint, proof plus author-built synthetic testbed; the remedy is not established and the authors say the gap *"appears only under gating"*). (3) The vault's dominant blocker turns up in a new LOCATION: between two loopable assays. Fourteen consumer-product ingredients through a three-tier estrogen strategy — 13 of 14 met the positivity rule of OECD Test Guideline 455 (the adopted glowing-cell estrogen-receptor test), four advanced to Test Guideline 456 (the adopted hormone-synthesis assay), selected *"due to strong ERα responses"* — a phrase with no published threshold. Both assays are `closed-loop-ready` with fixed arithmetic rules; the rule routing chemicals between them does not exist, because an IATA is by construction the expert-judgement structure. And a 93%-positive tier-1 screen has done almost none of the discriminating, so essentially all the selectivity lives in the undocumented step. The computational tier meanwhile showed *"limited agreement across platforms"* — models converging on what they *propose* and diverging on what they *judge*. Reassuring result said plainly: binary mixtures were predominantly additive, no large synergy (Lee et al. 2026, *Toxicol. Appl. Pharmacol.* 117978, PMID 42543081, confirmed subscription — abstract-only ingest). (4) ⚠️ An adopted guideline's unexplained false positives get a candidate mechanism, from a group that was not looking at it. A redox-profiling platform saw salicylic acid switch on Nrf2 and read it as *"antioxidant-response engagement rather than skin-sensitization classification"* — the identical event, in the identical cell type, that OECD Test Guideline 442D and all five of its adopted methods convert into a skin-allergy call. That predicts a structural, directional, perfectly reproducible over-call on redox-active chemicals behind the guideline's ~76% specificity (84/110 non-sensitisers) — invisible to reproducibility, and doubled rather than checked by a lab running two KE2 methods for confidence. Nothing demotes; a `domain_gate` was considered and DECLINED as one anecdote plus a plausible mechanism (Armouch et al. 2026, *NAM J.* 100117, PMID 42542580, four materials, `funding_flag: [method-developer]`). The oxygen argument that came with it — every wet method in this vault runs at ~19 kPa O₂ while human tissue sits at 1–6 kPa — was deliberately NOT fired as a rail flag: no side-by-side comparator, no chemical class named, direction of effect unknown.
