(a) The night's headline, and it is the same sentence said four times by people who do not read each other. chaudhry-2026-nams-conundrum-evidence-hierarchy (*Archives of Toxicology* guest editorial, published today, eight European risk-assessment scientists, `funding_flag: none`, no primary data) states that there are *"no harmonised principles for incorporating NAM data into regulatory frameworks"* and that the three questions assessors actually face — when non-animal data can resolve conflicting evidence, when it alone suffices, how uncertainty compares across data types — are *"often resolved through expert judgement, introducing variability and reducing reproducibility."* Run that sentence through this vault's own `loop_readiness` ladder and step 3 returns `human-in-the-loop` for the integration layer. A pipeline is as loopable as its least loopable step, so every `closed-loop-ready` badge here describes a segment of a chain that terminates in a human. (b) ⚠ And a blocker no axis in this vault could express. The same editorial describes regulatory practice as ranking evidence *types* — human, then animal, then in-vitro, then in-chemico/omics, then in-silico — *"deeply embedded in legislation and guidance (e.g. REACH)"*, with animal data *"generally outweigh[ing] in vitro results."* Their word for it: "NAMs are expected to replace animal testing, yet they are positioned in a way that prevents them from doing so." A perfect closed loop returning a clean negative still loses to a rodent study, and automating the assay does not move it up the list. Recorded as a standing finding, not a fourth axis. (c) The same blocker at the FRONT of the pipeline, and the vault's first blind spot caught forwards. stevens-2026-disease-aop-mapping-bioassay-panel (*Environment International*, Helmholtz-funded, ⚠ abstract-only — CC BY licensed and the publisher's host refused every rung of the fetch ladder) curated 487 adverse outcome pathways to 266, mapped them onto six disease clusters, and found the three most-recurring key events are cytotoxicity (6/6 clusters), oxidative stress (5/6) and mitochondrial dysfunction (4/6) — proposing a 15-assay panel on that basis. It is the first machine-executable answer to *"why these assays and not others?"*, and frequency and independence are opposite selection criteria: the events that recur most are the ones that discriminate least. New `correlated_blind_spot` row, the rail's first attributable to a selection rule rather than a shared biological gap. (d) The one place a loop's output met a real outside adjudicator — and it needed three rounds of repair. yu-2026-ai-engineer-certifiable-design-aip (arXiv 2608.21976, preprint, `funding_flag: [industry]`) reports a closed loop designing a 20-megawatt floating wind foundation that received Approval in Principle from the China Classification Society, at 8.1% less steel and 8.1% lower unit cost than the human benchmark. The methods add what the abstract omits: the panel *"raised three rounds of formal clarification requests"* and the certificate issued only *"after iterative exchanges and design refinements."* A loop produced a strong first draft; a human-mediated review cycle finished it. Its internal termination gate was validated by one submission in the confirming direction only — nothing the gate rejected was ever shown to the certifier — which is precisely the one-sided audit chen-2026-auditing-discovery-claims-two-sided built a two-sided criterion against, ingested here yesterday. (e) The deciding leg, measured, and it crosses zero. rajwal-2026-logit-energy-hypothesis-ranking (arXiv 2608.17270, preprint, Oak Ridge National Laboratory, `funding_flag: none`): across 1,323 papers in 12 disciplines, GPT-5 asked in plain language to rank a real hypothesis against 15 wrong ones put the real one ahead 37.4% of the time — random ordering scores 50%. Reading a model's own internal confidence instead scored 68.8–82.2%, best of all in a 1-billion-parameter model. And the rail again at the opposite pole: seven models agreed on the right answer 3 times in 1,323 under the best-performing scorer, leaving the authors unable to say *"whether the models share a common signal tracking scientific correctness."* ⚠ The authors volunteer the confound that would explain their own win — the right answers were written by experts and may simply read better.
